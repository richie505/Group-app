### Oldest and earliest — inscriptions, coins and texts
## 1. Inscriptions, coins and texts
- Sohgaura Copper Plate: The earliest known copper plate (copper-plate record) — the earliest royal (famine-related) order related to famine relief
- Satavahana inscriptions: The earliest (religious) land-grant epigraphic record
- Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription: The earliest specimen of the Kavya style; the earliest perfect Sanskrit inscription
- Prakrit: The language of the earliest stone inscription
- Silver punch-marked coins: The earliest extant coins of India
- Ashtadhyayi of Panini: The earliest known Sanskrit grammar
- Fourteen Purvas: The earliest Jain holy book; Ardha-Magadhi — the language of the earliest Jain literature
- Dashpur inscription: The oldest advertisement tradition in India
- Dastan-i-Amir Hamza: The earliest Mughal illustrated manuscript
- Annamacharya: The earliest known Telugu song-composer (Padakavita Pitamaha)

## Earliest references to Andhra

- Puranas — name Pulomavi III (not Gautamiputra Satakarni) as the last Satavahana

- V. S. Sukthankar — Andhras and Satavahanas not the same people; tied to the Karnataka theory
### Key inscriptions
## Kharavela — Hathigumpha
- ★ Hathigumpha inscription — Kharavela of Kalinga (now Odisha); records his wars, including against Satakarni, and names Tamil kingdoms

## Rudradaman — Junagadh

### Foreign accounts in order
## Order of visitors

## Greek and Roman writers
- Megasthenes — sent by Seleucus Nikator to Chandragupta Maurya; lived at Pataliputra
- Indica (Megasthenes) — first foreign work to mention the Andhras; not by Aryabhata (who wrote the Aryabhatiya)

- Megasthenes wrote of Brahmins and Shramanas
- Pliny — Natural History in Latin; complained of Roman gold flowing to India (about 50 million sesterces a year) for spices, pearls, muslin
- Ptolemy — Geography; Masulia/Maisolia/Masolia = Machilipatnam, a big trade centre (for textiles), not a Roman army headquarters
- Ptolemy's Ben Ra (Vengi) — a trade centre
- Periplus — by an anonymous Greek sailor
## Chinese pilgrims
- Fa-Hien — during Chandragupta II; wrote of Buddhist pilgrimage

- Hiuen Tsang — studied at Nalanda; wrote of Mahayana
- I-Tsing — 671–695 CE; landed at Tamralipti; studied at Nalanda, wrote on Buddhist monk life and relics
## Arab writer
- Al-Biruni — Kitab-ul-Hind in Arabic (not Persian); wrote on Hindu religion, Puranas, Sanskrit, Yoga
