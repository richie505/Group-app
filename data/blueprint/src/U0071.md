## 2. Odd-one-out lists
- A later Pratihara king was also called Devapala (c. 948-954), besides the Pala Devapala
- ⚠ Nagabhata I = founder, Arabs; Nagabhata II = Kannauj capital; Vatsaraja lost to Dhruva, Nagabhata II to Govinda III
### Mihira Bhoja → title, parentage, capital, extent
- Mihir Bhoja: Captured Kannauj around 836 AD and made it his capital
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say Nagabhata II made Kannauj the capital
- Titles: Adivaraha; Prabhasa — followed Vaishnavism
- Title: Adivaraha (UPPSC 2017) - devotee of Vishnu; Adivaraha legend on his coins
- Parentage: Grandson of Nagabhata II (son of Ramabhadra) - not son (CGPSC 2022)
- Capital: Kannauj (Mahodaya); Barah copper plate mentions a skandhavara at Mahodaya
- Extent:
- Early defeats by Palas, Rashtrakutas, Kalachuris, then a comeback
- Checked by Sankaravarman of Kashmir, Rashtrakuta Krishna II and Pala Devapala
### Arab travellers → what they said of the Pratiharas
- Suleman identified him (Bhoja) as the ruler; Al-Masudi called the kingdom Al-Juzr and Bhoja Baura
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say it was Sulaiman who called Bhoja "Juzr"/"Al-Juzr"
| Traveller | Period | Statement |
| Sulaiman | Mid-9th c. | Called Bhoja "Juzr"/"Al-Juzr"; praised army, wealth and security; best cavalry in India (RPSC 2023: Sulaiman came in Bhoja I's reign) |
| Al-Masudi | - | Called him "Baura" |
- Comparative note: Sulaiman praised Pratihara cavalry but named Amoghavarsha (Rashtrakuta) one of the four great kings of the world
- ⚠ Juzr = Sulaiman's name; Baura = Al-Masudi's
### Rajashekhara → patrons and works

### Decline → raids and successor Rajput states
- Raids: Rashtrakuta Indra III's raid on Kannauj; Mahmud of Ghazni attacked Kannauj in 1018
- Breakaway feudatories: Chandelas (Khajuraho), Chauhans, Paramaras, Chaulukyas
### Universal angles
- Arab invasion of Sindh:
- Muhammad-bin-Qasim: Targeted and conquered Sindh in 712 AD — also given as 711–12 CE; an Arab invasion (conquest)
- Dahir was defeated and killed by him
- Arab expansion — his conquest — remained largely limited (confined) to Sindh and Multan
- Significance: Main power that kept Arab rule confined to Sindh
# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:70
### Name & origin
- Pratihara = door-keeper; claimed descent from Lakshmana, Rama's door-keeper brother
- Agnikula story (Prithviraj Raso): Pratiharas, Chaulukyas, Paramaras, Chauhans born from the fire-pit at Mount Abu
### Capitals
- ★ Order: Vatsaraja (775–800) → Nagabhatta II (800–833) → Mahendrapala I (885–910) → Mahipala (912–944)
### Rulers
- Nagabhata I — founder; fought Arab expansion
- Vatsaraja — took Kannauj, defeated Dharmapala; defeated by Rashtrakuta Dhruva (start of the tripartite struggle)
- Nagabhata II — removed Chakrayudha, defeated Dharmapala near Monghyr, made Kannauj the capital
- Mihira Bhoja — stopped by Sankaravarman of Kashmir, Rashtrakuta Krishna II and Pala Devapala
- c. 916 CE — Indra III defeated Mahipala I and looted Kannauj (not the reverse)
- Rajashekhara — court poet of Mahendrapala I; also at Mahipala I's court
- Order: Vatsaraja → Nagabhata II → Mihir Bhoja → Mahendrapala I → Mahipala
### Mihira Bhoja
- Son of Ramabhadra; grandson of Nagabhata II
- Title Adivaraha — devotion to Vishnu; legend on his coins; a Vaishnava
- Captured Kannauj c. 836 AD, made it the capital (629 AD = Hiuen Tsang's journey)
- Pratiharas ruled first from Ujjain, later Kannauj
### Arab travellers
- Sulaiman (mid-9th c.) called Mihira Bhoja Juzr (Al-Juzr)
- Al-Masudi called him Baura
### Rajashekhara's works
- Rajashekhara: Kavyamimamsa, Karpuramanjari, Viddhasalabhanjika
- Prabandhachintamani — by Merutunga, not Rajashekhara
### Arabs & decline
- Mahmud of Ghazni attacked Kannauj in 1018; with Indra III's raid and feudatories leaving, weakened the Pratiharas
- Pratiharas kept Arab rule limited to Sindh
- Muhammad bin Qasim defeated and killed Raja Dahir of Sindh; conquest stayed within Sindh and Multan
### Exam corner
- Sulaiman praised the Pratihara cavalry
- Sulaiman named Rashtrakuta Amoghavarsha I one of the four great kings of the world
### Pratiharas → name, origin and capitals
- ★ Founder: Nagabhatta I
- ★ Rajputs of the Agni clan; "Gurjara" from link with Gurjars
- ★ Gurjars first mentioned in the Aihole inscription of Pulakesin II and Harshacharita of Banabhatta
### Pratihara rulers → period and achievement
- ★ Bharatrabhatta I — not a Gurjara-Pratihara ruler
- ★ Pratiharas ruled much of northern India mid-8th to 11th c.; first at Ujjain, later Kannauj
### Mihira Bhoja → title, parentage, capital, extent
- ★ Mihir Bhoja (836–885 AD) — the greatest Pratihara king; follower of Vaishnavism
- ★ Titles Aadi Varaha and Prabhas
- ★ Others: Dharmapala — Pala (770–810); Mahendrapala I — Pratihara (885–910); Harsha — Pushyabhuti (606–647)
### Arab travellers → what they said of the Pratiharas
- ★ Suleman visited during the reign of Bhoja I (Mihir Bhoja)
- ★ Mihir Bhoj — son of Ramabhadra and queen Appa Devi (not son of Nagabhatta II)
- ★ Capital Kannauj (seized c. 836 AD); Al-Masudi called him King Baura
### Rajashekhara → patrons and works
- ★ Rajashekhara — Sanskrit poet and dramatist at the court of Mahendrapala I
