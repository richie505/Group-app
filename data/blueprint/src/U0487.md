### AMRUT and AMRUT 2.0 — mission cities, thrust areas, coverage
## 1. AMRUT
- Launch: June 2015; initially 500 cities, 15 merged → 485
- Coverage: ~60% of urban population (some notes say ~65%)
- Mission cities:
- All ULBs with 1 lakh+ population
- Other State/UT capitals; all HRIDAY cities
- Cities on stems of main rivers; hill-State, island and tourist cities
- PYQ: Dausa was not a Rajasthan mission city
- Outlay: Rs 1 lakh crore incl. Rs 50,000 crore central (2015-16 to 2019-20); extended to Sept 2025
- Thrust areas: water supply; sewerage and septage; storm-water drains; green spaces and parks; non-motorised urban transport; capacity building
## 2. AMRUT 2.0
- AMRUT 2.0 (2021, till 2025-26):
- Universal water supply in all statutory towns
- 100% sewerage/septage in the 500 AMRUT cities
- Combined progress: Projects worth Rs 2.79 lakh crore sanctioned under AMRUT + AMRUT 2.0; urban tap connectivity up 64% since 2011
- ⚠ AMRUT = 500 cities (now 485); Smart Cities = 100 cities
### Smart Cities Mission — selection, structure, financing, progress
## 1. Launch & selection
- Launch: June 2015, MoHUA; 100 cities via competitive challenge
- Selection: Two-stage; four rounds from Jan 2016; last city June 2018
- State shares: Equal weight to urban population and number of statutory towns of each State
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Area-Based Development | Retrofitting/redevelopment of min. 50 acres |
| Pan-City | Tech solutions; Integrated Command and Control Centres |
| SPV | One per city → company under Companies Act 2013; 50:50 equity of State/UT and ULB |
| CITIIS 2.0 (2023) | 18 smart cities, with AFD, KfW, EU and NIUA → integrated waste management |
## 3. Financing & data
- Centre: Rs 48,000 crore (Rs 100 crore per city per year, ~Rs 500 crore per city), matched by State/ULB
- Other sources: ULB own funds + Finance Commission grants, municipal bonds, PPP, loans, convergence
- Progress (11 Jul 2025):
- Rs 47,459 crore (99%) of central share claimed
- Extension: Mission extended to 2025-26 to finish pending works
- Not in your sources: Smart Cities Mission logo description.
### HRIDAY — heritage cities (places)
- Full form: National Heritage City Development and Augmentation Yojana, 2015, MoHUA
- Aim: Heritage-sensitive planning of cultural, natural, living and built heritage
- 12 cities: Ajmer, Amaravati (AP), Amritsar, Badami, Dwarka, Gaya, Kanchipuram, Mathura, Puri, Varanasi, Velankanni, Warangal
- ⚠ HRIDAY cities from the Telugu states = Amaravati and Warangal

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:43
### Urban missions in order
## Swachh Bharat Mission
- ★ Swachh Bharat Mission launched 2014 (Phase 1 from October 2014); goal ODF and Clean India by 2 October 2019 (Gandhi's 150th birth anniversary)
- Two components: Rural (Grameen) and Urban (Shahari)
- ★ Focuses on collective behavioural change of the whole community; for many, open defecation is part of the usual early morning walk (mainly rural India)
## Order of launch
| Scheme | Launch |
| JNNURM | December 2005 |
| SBM-Urban | 2 October 2014 |
| AMRUT | June 2015 |
## Other missions
- AMRUT thrust areas: water supply, sewerage and septage, storm-water drains, green spaces and parks, non-motorised transport, capacity building (not crop procurement)
- AMRUT 2.0 (2021 to 2025-26): universal water supply in all statutory towns
- Smart Cities shared among States with equal weight to urban population and number of statutory towns (not districts)
- CITIIS 2.0 (2023, 18 Smart Cities): integrated waste management; partners AFD, KfW, EU, NIUA
- HRIDAY cities include Ajmer, Amritsar, Badami, Dwarka
- PMAY-U 2.0 (2024): 1 crore more urban houses
- DAY-NULM: self-employment, skilling, shelters, street vendors among the urban poor
- Rurban Mission: rural-urban clusters with urban amenities; rural focus
### AMRUT vs Smart Cities
- AMRUT: 500 cities initially; 15 merged, leaving 485
- AMRUT covers ULBs with population 1 lakh or more, besides capitals, HRIDAY cities and certain other categories
- Smart Cities Area-Based Development (paired with Pan-City solutions): retrofitting or redevelopment of at least 50 acres
### PMAY-U verticals
- BSUP and IHSDP were components of JNNURM; CLSS and ISSR belong to PMAY-U
- ISSR, AHP, BLC = Centrally Sponsored Schemes; CLSS = Central Sector scheme
- PMAY-U 2.0 verticals: BLC, AHP, Affordable Rental Housing, Interest Subsidy Scheme; ISSR not in 2.0 (earlier structure only)
### Other tools
## Smart Cities and HRIDAY
- Smart City SPV: company under Companies Act, 2013; equity 50:50 between State/UT and ULB
- AP's Smart Cities: Visakhapatnam, Kakinada, Tirupati, Amaravati
- HRIDAY = National Heritage City Development and Augmentation Yojana; launched 2015 by MoHUA
- Amaravati: AP city among the 12 HRIDAY cities
## Housing and funds
- GHTC-India (MoHUA): mainstream innovative and disaster-resilient construction technologies
- UIDF (Budget 2023-24, Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities): managed by National Housing Bank (not NABARD)
- UIDF: about Rs 10,000 crore a year, paid from banks' priority sector lending shortfall (not G-Sec bonds)
- Urban Challenge Fund: Centre funds 25% of project cost if the city raises at least 50% via bonds, loans or PPPs
### JNNURM reforms
- JNNURM: 25% of municipal budget kept for the urban poor (and 25% of developed land for EWS/LIG)
### Andhra Pradesh
- Anna Canteens (AP): meals to the urban poor at Rs. 5
- AP has four Smart Cities; Amaravati is an HRIDAY city
- AP's urban-affairs MoU signed with UN-Habitat (not UNESCO)
### Exam corner
- ★ Indore: cleanest city (population over 10 lakh), MoHUA, 31 December 2019
- Dausa: not an AMRUT city in Rajasthan; Jaipur (State capital), Ajmer (HRIDAY city), Jodhpur (1 lakh+) qualified
