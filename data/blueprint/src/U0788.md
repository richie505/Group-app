### Pollution — types, classification and terms
## 1. Pollution type → source → impact
| Pollution type | Major primary source (APPSC 2025 key) | Key impacts |
| Air | Industrial emissions (SO2, NOx, PM); vehicle exhaust | Respiratory and heart disease; acid rain, poor visibility |
| Water | Sewage discharge; industrial effluents | Water-borne disease, eutrophication, fish kills |
| Soil | Chemical fertilisers and pesticides; industrial waste | Loss of fertility, toxic crops, soil fauna imbalance |
- Air pollution deaths: ~7 million a year (WHO); household air pollution ~3.8 million
## 2. Pollutant classification
| Basis | Types | Examples |
| Form / persistence | Primary (as emitted) vs secondary (formed by reaction) | Plastics vs ozone, peroxyacyl nitrate |
| Existence in nature | Quantitative vs qualitative | CO2 vs insecticides, herbicides |
| Natural disposal | Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable | Sewage, heat vs plastics, DDT [GK] |
| Source geometry | Point (chimney) / line (vehicles) / area / diffuse (non-point) | Thermal plant; highway; mining area; farm runoff |
### Pollutants → effect; terms and matched pairs
## 1. Pollutant → effect
| Pollutant | Effect |

### Index and organisations — CEPI
## 1. Comprehensive Environmental Pollution Index (CEPI)
- Developed by: CPCB with IIT Delhi (2009)
- Scoring: Industrial clusters scored 0-100 for air, water and land
- Three factors = pollutant, pathway, receptor (exposed people)
| CEPI score | Category |
| ≥70 | critically polluted |
| 60-70 | severely polluted |
- 2009 assessment: 43 of 88 clusters critically polluted
- AP clusters: Visakhapatnam assessed; Patancheru-Bollaram (then AP, now Telangana) critically polluted
- Not in your sources: latest CEPI scores of AP clusters (CPCB revised assessment).

### Classifying pollutants
- Secondary pollutants form by reactions in the air — ozone, PAN; plastics are primary
- Qualitative pollutants — insecticides, herbicides; quantitative — CO2
- By source shape: chimney — point; highway — line; mining area — area; farm runoff — diffuse (non-point)
- DDT — non-biodegradable; persists and builds up in food chains
