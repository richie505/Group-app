### Air masses — nature, types, Bergeron code → source region
## 1. Nature
- An air mass: A large body of air with relatively uniform temperature and humidity
- Forms over a source region with uniform surface properties — the source region may be land or ocean
- Classified on the basis of the temperature and moisture content of their source regions
- Control temperature, humidity, stability and precipitation
- Acquired by staying over a homogeneous source region (hot desert, warm ocean, snow-covered plain)
- Classification (Bergeron):
- First letter = moisture → c continental = dry; m maritime = moist
- Second letter = thermal source → T tropical, P polar, A arctic, E equatorial
## 2. Types
| Source region | Properties | Designation |
| Tropical oceans (tropical/subtropical oceans) | Warm and moist | mT — maritime Tropical |
| Tropical land areas (subtropical hot deserts: Sahara, Arabia) | Warm and dry | cT — continental Tropical |
| Polar oceans (high-latitude oceans) | Cold and moist | mP — maritime Polar |
| Polar land areas (snow-covered high-latitude continents: Siberia, Canada) | Cold and dry | cP — continental Polar |
| Arctic and Antarctic (Arctic basin, Greenland ice) | Extremely cold and dry | cA or cAA |
| Equatorial oceans | Hot, very humid | mE |
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say cT is "Hot, dry", mP "Cool, moist", cP "Cold, dry, stable" and cA "Very cold, very dry"
- ⚠ c = dry (continental), m = moist (maritime) - first letter is moisture, not temperature
- Polar anticyclones: Cold, dense cP/cA air → cold-core highs, strongest near the surface
- Siberian High, Canadian High, Antarctic High
- Weather: clear skies, fog, frost, sharp night cooling (Jul 2025)
### Air mass modification → weather; stability; nuclei
## 1. Modification
- An air mass travels away from its source region and gets modified — it loses its original characteristics
| Passes over | Change |
| A warm surface | Warmer and more unstable |
| A cold surface | Cooler and more stable |
| An ocean | Gains moisture |
| Land | Loses moisture — becomes drier |
- Vertical mixing alters the temperature and humidity profile
- Modification: Air mass changes as it leaves its source → heated or cooled from below; gains or loses moisture
| Situation | Result | Weather |
| Cold air moves over warmer surface | Heated from below, unstable | Convection, cumulus, showers [GK] |
| Warm air moves over colder surface | Cooled from below, stable | Fog, stratus, drizzle [GK] |
## 2. Dust, nuclei and stability
- Dust particles act as condensation nuclei; dust and moisture contribute to cloud formation
- Hygroscopic nuclei: Dust and salt particles → nuclei for condensation
- Stability: Water vapour (up to 4% by volume in the humid tropics) controls stability/instability of air
- Arctic amplification: Meandering jet stream carries warm air masses into the Arctic → Arctic amplification (UPSC Env p.220)
### Elements of climate → defined
## 1. The elements defined
- Temperature: Measures the degree of heat — includes daily and annual ranges; controlled by latitude and altitude
- Atmospheric pressure: The force exerted by air — varies with temperature and altitude
- Wind: The horizontal movement of air — moves from high pressure toward low pressure
- Humidity: The water vapour present in the atmosphere
- Cloudiness: Condensed water or ice particles in the atmosphere
- Precipitation: Water falling from the atmosphere — rain, snow, sleet and hail
- Temperature range: The difference between maximum and minimum temperature
### Fronts — nature and types → weather
## 1. Nature
- A front: A boundary zone between two contrasting air masses — forms when two air masses with different temperatures and humidity meet
- Lighter, warmer air is forced to rise over denser, colder air
- Mid-latitude cyclones form along fronts; frontal uplift produces cyclonic or frontal rainfall
- Front: Sloping boundary zone between two air masses of different temperature and density
- Cold air always forms a wedge under the warm air
## 2. Types of front
| Front | Formation | Uplift | Weather |
| Warm front | Warm air advances over cold air; warm air rides up over retreating cold air | Gradual (gentle) | Widespread light to moderate rain; wide belt of layered cloud, prolonged light-moderate rain ahead of the front [GK] |
| Cold front | Cold air undercuts warm air; cold air advances and lifts warm air sharply | Steep | Intense short-duration rain and thunderstorms; cumulonimbus, heavy short showers, thunder, squalls, sudden temperature fall [GK] |
| Occluded front | A cold front overtakes a warm front; warm air lifted off the ground | — | Complex weather — often prolonged precipitation; mixed cloud and rain; last stage of a temperate cyclone [GK] |
| Stationary front | Neither air mass advances; winds blow parallel to the front | — | Prolonged cloudy and rainy conditions; cloud and rain can persist for days [GK] |
- ⚠ Cold front = steep, short heavy showers; warm front = gentle, prolonged light rain
- Arcus (shelf/roll) clouds: Form along the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts → mark the warm/cool air boundary (Jul 2025)
- Polar anticyclones + polar fronts: Form cold fronts → storms and temperature drops
### Fronts → terms, passage sequence; Bjerknes polar front theory
## 1. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Frontogenesis | Formation or sharpening of a front where contrasting air masses converge |
| Frontolysis | Weakening and dissolution of a front as the temperature contrast is lost |
## 2. Cloud and rain sequence at passage of a front
| Stage | Clouds | Weather |
| Approaching warm front | Cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus | Pressure falls; steady, widespread rain [GK] |
| Warm sector | Clearing, some stratus | Warmer, humid, rain eases [GK] |
| Cold front passes | Cumulonimbus | Heavy showers, thunder, wind shift; then cold, clear air [GK] |
## 3. Proponents → polar front theory
- Proponents: Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes (Bergen School, Norway), around the end of World War I
- Core idea: Temperate (wave) cyclones form along the polar front → cold polar air meets warm westerly air
- Life cycle: Wave on the front → warm and cold fronts → occlusion → dissipation
- India link: Western disturbances reach north-west India at the occluded-front stage
- ⚠ Bjerknes = polar front theory; Bergeron = air-mass classification
### Air masses affecting India; climatic effects; relief
## 1. Climatic effects
- A new air mass can cause a sudden rise or fall in temperature
- Seasonal shifts in air masses produce seasonal climatic changes — e.g. monsoon winds
- Persistent air-mass influence creates characteristic regional climates — e.g. maritime and continental climates
- Air masses and fronts can produce cold waves, heat waves, thunderstorms and blizzards
## 2. Relief and rainfall
- Windward slopes receive orographic rainfall; leeward slopes form rain shadows
- The Western Ghats produce a strong rainfall contrast across their slopes
- The Himalayas block cold continental winds and influence the monsoon
## 3. Season → source → effect
| Season | Source / air | Effect |
| Winter | Cold, dry continental air from Central Asia | Blocked by the Himalayas: north India is 3-8 degC warmer than places on the same latitude (UPSC Geo p.42; IYB) |
| Winter | Anticyclonic cell of sinking air over India | Clear skies, light northerly winds, low humidity, large daily temperature range (IYB p.19; UPSC Geo p.41) |
| Summer / monsoon | Moist maritime air from the Indian Ocean, fed by the low-level Somali Jet | South-west monsoon rain (UPSC Geo p.41) |
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Antarctic high reaching unusually far north → snow in Chile's Atacama Desert, first time in over a decade (Jul 2025)
- Stalled high-pressure heat dome → record early heatwave in Western Europe (27 May 2026)
- AP angle: Dual influence
- SW monsoon = summer maritime air
