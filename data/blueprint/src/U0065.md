### Women — marriage, property, education; earliest sati inscription
- Marriage: Pre-puberty marriage advocated by Dharmashastras
- Svayamvara largely gave way to marriages arranged by parents
- Widows: Expected to stay celibate
- Property: No general property rights, but control over stridhana (marriage gifts)
- Education: Most women denied education; treated as property
- FIRST - earliest sati inscription: Eran, 510 CE (wife of Goparaja, general of Bhanugupta); sati rare under the Guptas
- ⚠ Eran 510 CE = Goparaja, general of Bhanugupta (not a king's wife)

## 2. Features of guilds
- Autonomy: Guilds (shreni) were autonomous associations of artisans and merchants → production units
- ⚠ Trap: "Non-autonomous bodies" is always the wrong option (APPSC-G1 2022, 2023 key)
- Rule-making: Framed their own rules and laws; had executive committees/heads and their own seals; regulated trade; settled internal disputes
- Banking: Received public deposits and gave loans
- Military role: Some guilds maintained their own armed bodies for protection (APPSC-G1 2023 key)
- Charity: Temples, rest houses, assembly halls, gardens

### Trade decline and urban decay — causes, effects, timing
## 1. Causes → effect
| Factor | Effect |
| Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire | Eastern Roman Empire trade in silk and spices continued until about 550 CE; fell once the Romans learnt to rear silkworms → Indian silk exports suffered |
| Western Rome | Collapse of the West Roman Empire = standard cause of North India's trade decline (7th-10th c.) (APPSC-G2 2017 key) |
| Sassanid decline | After the rise of Islam → hurt overland trade |
| Hunas | Hunas disrupted the north-western route + loss of Western India → cut trade revenue; less gold and silver inflow |
## 2. New directions
- Shift to Southeast Asia: Trade with South-East Asia grew via Tamralipti → did not revive the northern economy
- China: Trade grew; Chinese silk = 'Chinansuka'
## 3. Urban decay and timing
- Urban decay (R.S. Sharma): Old commercial towns shrank; economy turned rural and self-sufficient; gold coins became scarce
- Decline of towns: Sites → Sanghol, Hastinapur, Mathura, Shravasti, Kaushambi (APPSC-GS 2023 key)
- Timing:
- Decline visible in the post-Gupta period / 7th century
- c. 750-1000 CE = stagnation in North India
- Recovery with the Arab empire from the 10th century
- Exam angle: statement-type questions on the post-Gupta decline of trade and towns.
