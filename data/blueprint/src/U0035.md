- Identification: Sandrocottus = Chandragupta first made by Sir William Jones (1793)
- Fixes early Indian chronology: Chandragupta contemporary of Alexander, c. 322 BCE
- ⚠ Amitraghata (Sanskrit) = Amitrochates (Greek) = Bindusara; Sandrocottus = Chandragupta
### Chandragupta Maurya → origin, Chanakya and the overthrow of the Nandas
- Takshashila: Also known as Taxila — located in Gandhara; an ancient centre of learning
- Associated with Chanakya, Chandragupta Maurya, Charaka and Jivaka
## 1. Founder and origin
- Founder: Maurya dynasty (324/321-297 BCE); Mauryas = first pan-Indian empire (BPSC 2020, 2024 keys)
- ⚠ Trap: some notes call the Nandas the first empire-builders - exam answer for "first empire" is still Chandragupta Maurya
- Origin debate:
- Brahmanical: "Maurya" from a Shudra woman Mura
- Buddhist/Jain sources = Kshatriya (Moriya clan)
- Traditions: Mudrarakshasa, Devi Chandraguptam; Vamsatthapakasini (10th c.) on the Chanakya-Chandragupta legend
## 2. Chanakya
- Office: Chandragupta's chief minister/guide; taught at Taxila
- Under Bindusara: Per Taranath and Jain legend, also served as Bindusara's minister
## 3. Overthrow of the Nandas
- First eliminated the Greek satraps
- Besieged Pataliputra → Chandragupta took the Magadhan throne c. 321 BC from Dhana Nanda
- Story told in Mudrarakshasa
### Chandragupta's conquests → army, regions, governors
## 1. Sudarshan Lake
- The Junagadh Inscription is important for the history of Sudarshan Lake
- Originally constructed by Pushyagupta — governor of Chandragupta Maurya (Sudarshana lake, Girnar, Saurashtra)
- Canals constructed by Tushasp — governor of Ashoka (Yavanaraja Tushaspha)
- Repaired by Rudradaman — without forced labour; later also by Skandagupta
## 2. Conquests
- Army: 600,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry
- Justin called it a "dacoits' gang" / "gang of dacoits" (recruited from outlaws and frontier tribes)
| Region | Conquest / fact |
| North-west | Expelled Greek satraps; won Seleucus's four provinces up to Afghanistan |
| Western India | Saurashtra under governor Pushyagupta, who built the Sudarshana lake |
| Deccan and south | Jain tradition places him at Shravanabelagola (Karnataka); Tamil poet Mamulanar refers to Mauryan expansion southward |
- Sudarshana lake - source:
- Known from the Junagadh inscription of Rudradaman (UKPSC 2024: lake built in Chandragupta Maurya's reign)
- ⚠ Lake built - Pushyagupta; canals - Tushaspha; repaired - Rudradaman I, Skandagupta
### Treaty with Seleucus Nicator, c. 305 BCE → provisions
- Seleucus I Nicator: Alexander's general; tried to recover the north-west c. 305-303 BCE
- Outcome: Defeated by Chandragupta (some notes: 305 BC)
| Provision | Detail |
| Territory ceded to Chandragupta | Aria (Herat), Arachosia (Kandahar), Paropanisadai (Kabul valley), Gedrosia (Balochistan); also spelt Paropamisadae (Kabul) |
| Matrimonial alliance | Seleucus's daughter (Helena per CDI) married to Chandragupta |
| Chandragupta gave | 500 war elephants |
| Ambassador | Megasthenes sent to Pataliputra |
### Chandragupta and Jainism → Bhadrabahu, famine, Sallekhana
- Guru: Disciple of Bhadrabahu (sixth Jain Thera)
- Famine: 12-year famine in Magadha (South Bihar) → Bhadrabahu migrated south; Chandragupta went with him
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC G2 2003 key = Bhadrabahu, not Sthulabhadra
- End: Abdicated, became an ascetic, died by Sallekhana (fasting to death) at Shravanabelagola, Karnataka
- Source: Parishishtaparvan of Hemachandra
- Consequences:
- Famine → Digambara (Bhadrabahu) - Svetambara (Sthulabhadra) split
### Bindusara → reign, family, foreign contacts, revolts
## 1. Reign and family
- Reign: 297-273 BCE; succeeded in 297 BC
- Parents: Son of Chandragupta and Durdhara
- Son: Ashoka by queen Subhadrangi (Dharma)
## 2. Foreign contacts
- Antiochus I: Asked for sweet wine, dried figs and a philosopher
- Antiochus sent the first two; a philosopher could not be sold
- Envoys received: Deimachus and Dionysius
## 3. Religion
- Religion: Patron/follower of the Ajivikas = standard answer
## 4. Revolts at Taxila - princes sent
| Reign | Revolt and prince sent |
| Under Bindusara | Revolt at Taxila against corrupt/oppressive officials (dushtamatyas); Susima (eldest prince) failed → Ashoka sent to quell it (Divyavadana) |
| Under Ashoka | Another Taxila revolt handled by Kunala |
### Greek envoys → sent by and court; Megasthenes' Indica
| Envoy | Sent by | To the court of |
| Megasthenes (c. 302 BCE) | Seleucus Nicator (Seleucid) | Chandragupta Maurya - wrote Indica |
| Deimachus (Daimachos) | Antiochus I (Syria) | Bindusara |
| Dionysius | Ptolemy II Philadelphus (Egypt) | Bindusara (per CDI, APP) |
- Indica: Survives only in quotations (Strabo, Arrian, Diodorus); chief foreign account of Mauryan administration, economy and city life
- Palibothra = Pataliputra:
- Run by a 30-member municipal body in six boards
- Society: Seven classes - philosophers, farmers, herdsmen, artisans, soldiers, overseers (spies), councillors
- Slavery: Claimed no slaves - slavery did exist (Arthashastra, Ashokan edicts)
- Exam angle: "Who first mentioned Andhra forts?" = Megasthenes (APPSC G2 2002).
### Mauryan rulers → religion; Ajivikas and Barabar caves
## 1. Ajivikas
- Makkhali Gosala: A founder or leader of the Ajivikas — the Ajivika tradition
- Patronised by Ashoka and Dasharatha; Barabar and Nagarjuni Caves were associated with Ajivika monks
## 2. Mauryan caves — Bihar
- Barabar Caves: Located in Bihar — rock-cut caves of the Mauryan period; associated with the Ajivika religion and Ashoka; contain Ashokan inscriptions
- Oldest surviving rock-cut caves, granite; dedicated to Ajivikas by Ashoka and Dasharatha
- Lomas Rishi Cave: Located in the Barabar Hills of Bihar — Ajivika religion; Mauryan period; famous for its rock-cut façade
## 3. Rulers and religion
| Ruler | Religion |
| Chandragupta | Jainism (Bhadrabahu) |
| Bindusara | Ajivika (Ajivika astrologer at court) [GK] |
| Ashoka | Buddhism (after Kalinga; Upagupta/Moggaliputta Tissa) |
| Dasharatha | Ajivika patron - Nagarjuni caves; personally counted a Buddhist (CGPSC 2013 key) [GK] |
| Samprati | Jainism - "Ashoka of Jainism" |
### Extent of the empire → southern limits and Andhra sites
| Area / site | Fact |
| Extent | Afghanistan to Bengal; south to Karnataka (Brahmagiri, Siddapura, Jatinga-Rameshwara minor edicts) |
| Outside | Tamil kingdoms (Chola, Pandya, Satyaputra, Keralaputra) |
| Andhra edicts | Yerragudi (major + minor) + Rajula Mandagiri (minor), Kurnool district |
| Rock Edict XIII (c. 256 BCE) | Names the Andhras among peoples following Ashoka's Dhamma within his domain |
- Satavahanas: Began as Mauryan feudatories; Andhras already Buddhist by RE XIII
### Universal angles
- FIRST/LAST:
- Chandragupta = first Mauryan ruler + first to rule a pan-Indian empire
- First Greek envoy to an Indian court = Megasthenes
- Significance: First empire uniting most of the subcontinent (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, parts of Afghanistan); Mauryan rule reached the Deccan
- AP angle: Megasthenes on Andhra strength; Ashoka's Yerragudi edicts; Samprati spread Jainism in Andhra
