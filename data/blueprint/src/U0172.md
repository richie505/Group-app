# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:171
### Title changes & firsts
- ★ Regulating Act, 1773 — created the office of Governor-General of Bengal (Fort William, supervisory powers); first holder Warren Hastings
- William Bentinck — first Governor-General of India, under the Charter Act of 1833; Canning — first Viceroy
- Title order: Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal (1773) → Governor-General of India (1833) → Viceroy (1858)
### GGs in order
- Order: Wellesley → Cornwallis (second term) → Sir George Barlow → Minto I
- ★ Cornwallis — GG 1786-1793 and again from 30 July 1805; died 5 October 1805 at Ghazipur (tomb there)
- Known for Istamarari (Permanent) Settlement, judicial code, administrative services
- William Bentinck — 1828-1835; Governor-General of India from 1833
- ★ Freedom of the press (Bentinck era, PYQ key) — 1835
- Surendranath Banerjee — first Indian journalist jailed (2 April 1883, article in 'The Bengalee')
- Vellore Mutiny (1806) — Sir George Barlow
- Charter Act of 1813 — transition between Minto I and Marquess Hastings
### Measure → year → GG
| Measure | Year | GG |
| Regulating Act, Ring Fence policy | 1773 | Warren Hastings |
| District Judge post; Code separating revenue from justice | 1793 | Cornwallis |
| College of Fort William | 1800 | Wellesley |
| Infanticide measure | 1802 | Wellesley |
| ★ Sati declared illegal | 1829 | William Bentinck |
| Treaty of perpetual friendship with Ranjit Singh | 1831 | Bentinck |
| ★ Act V of 1843 — slavery illegal (Anti-Slavery Act) | 1843 | Ellenborough (1842-44) |
| Anti-Human-Sacrifice Act | 1845 | Hardinge I |
| Treaty of Lahore (not Ellenborough) | 1846 | Hardinge I |
| Widow Remarriage Act | — | Dalhousie |

- Charter of 1833 asked to end slavery soon; in 1843 all slaves freed
- Impeachment charges against Warren Hastings — Rohilla War, Chait Singh, plunder of the Begums of Awadh, judicial murder of Nand Kumar
- Clive — Plassey; Cornwallis — Permanent Settlement; Wellesley — Subsidiary Alliance
### Other measures
- Warren Hastings — abolished the Dual Government (1772); had the Code of Gentoo Laws compiled
- Wellesley — College of Fort William (1800)
- Sir Charles Metcalfe — liberated the press
- Bentinck — suppressed Thuggee; Dalhousie — PWD
- Ellenborough — Sindh annexed
### Impeachment of Warren Hastings
- Impeached by Burke and the Whigs (1787); trial 1788-1795; ended in acquittal
### Titles & real names
- William Bentinck — Father of Modern Western Education in India
- Robert Clive — founder of British power in India; 'heaven-born general'
- Wellesley — called himself the 'Bengal Tiger'
### Exam corner
- First universities (1857) — Lord Canning, not Bentinck
- Eased entry of British investors and traders — William Bentinck (APPSC-GS 2025 key)
- Warren Hastings — Dual Government abolished, Ring Fence; Wellesley — Subsidiary Alliance; Ellenborough — Sindh, Anti-Slavery Act
### Acts, treaties and institutions → year → GG
- ★ Bentinck reforms — abolition of Sati, suppression of female infanticide; universities were not his (came 1857 under Canning)
- ★ Vellore Mutiny (1806) — Sir George Barlow; sepoys opposed interference in social-religious practices, raised the Mysore ruler's flag
### Governors-General → other measures and policies
## William Bentinck (from July 1828)
- ★ Sati abolished (declared a crime 1829)
- ★ Captain (William Henry) Sleeman — suppressed the Thugs; appointed 1835 under Bentinck
- Thugs (Hindu and Muslim) worshipped Kali, Durga, Bhavani
- ★ Took over Mysore's administration for alleged maladministration
- ★ Social reforms per key: abolition of Sati, abolition of slavery, suppression of Thugs (not removal of disabilities from change of religion)
## Cornwallis
- ★ Reorganised the civil services ("Public Service"); created the Covenanted Civil Service (later ICS)
### Europeans → associated with
- Albuquerque — captured Goa (1510); Almeida's Blue Water Policy — earlier, separate strategy
### Impeachment of Warren Hastings — charges and outcome
- ★ Warren Hastings — impeached by the British Parliament (1788-1795) for unjust, arbitrary acts; all charges dropped
