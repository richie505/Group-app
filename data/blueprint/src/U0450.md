### Terms → meaning: Hindu rate of growth and ICOR
## 1. Hindu Rate of Growth
- Coined by economist Raj Krishna
- Refers to India's slow rate of growth of national income or GDP — around 3.5%
- Variable: annual growth of real GDP (some notes: 3.52% for 1950-1980)
- Per capita GDP grew only 1.37% a year → growth barely above population growth
- Associated with the period from the 1950s to the early 1980s — the relatively slow growth of the pre-reform planning era
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the period was 1950s to 1980, broken in the 1980s (5.1-5.5%)
- 2026 echo: "Bharat rate of growth" of 8%+ through AI-led "Reforms 3.0" (30 Jun 2026)
## 2. Capital-output ratio (ICOR) - low growth despite high savings
- ICOR = change in capital / change in output: extra investment needed for one more unit of GDP
- Lower ICOR = more efficient investment (Jan 2026)
- Harrod-Domar model (First Plan): Growth depends on savings rate + capital-output ratio (g = s/v)
- Second Plan: Raised investment from 7% to 11% of national income
- Heavy, capital-intensive industry with long gestation → high ICOR → growth stayed low
- Now: GFCF ~30% of GDP (2025-26); net household financial savings down to ~5.3% of GDP (Jan 2026)
### Policies, plans and the licence regime, 1944-1991
| Policy / plan | Year | Provision |
| Bombay Plan | 1944 | Industrialists themselves sought state protection → Licence Raj partly a capitalist demand |
| IPR 1956 ("Economic Constitution") | 1956 | Schedule A = 17 industries for the state; Schedule B = 12; rest private under licensing → "Licence-Permit-Quota Raj" |
| Third Plan | 1961-66 | made Import substitution the industrialisation strategy |
| 1980s reforms ("pro-business, not pro-market") | 1980s | Broad-banding of licences + MRTP limit raised to Rs 100 crore + tax cuts + NRI deposits |
| 1991 reforms | 1991 | Public-sector reserved industries cut 17 → 8; delicensing |
- Major focus 1947-1991: Import substitution (APPSC-GS 2023)
- ⚠ Trap: Not export promotion, deregulation or less public investment
- ⚠ 17 = IPR 1956 Schedule A industries; cut to 8 in 1991
