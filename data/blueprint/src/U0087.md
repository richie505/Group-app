### Exam corner
- APPSC-G1 2022, 2023 match: Variyam = committee; Kudumbu = ward; Kudavolai = lottery; Perumakkal = assembly member
- Village autonomy → Cholas (MPPSC 2021, BPSC 2020); Badami Chalukyas = highly centralised
- ⚠ Ur = non-brahmadeya; Sabha = brahmadeya; Nagaram = merchants
- Assemblies could raise loans, levy taxes, auction defaulters' land
- Criticism: elitist; Burton Stein: segmentary state
### Village autonomy — dynasty identification and sources
- Uttaramerur Inscription: Chola dynasty — famous for village administration; mentions the Sabha, village assemblies and local committees — committee selection and working; Chola local self-government
- Thanjavur Inscription: Rajaraja I — military achievements; the defeat of the Chera naval force at Kandallur; Chola overseas expansion
- Rajaraja I and Rajendra I commissioned temple-wall inscriptions recording victories and achievements — extensive temple inscriptions are a major reason Chola history is well documented (an unusually rich historical record)
- Answer / hallmark: Cholas = the dynasty known for village autonomy / elaborate local self-government (MPPSC 2021, BPSC 2020)
- Asked repeatedly against Cheras, Pandyas, Pallavas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas
- Usual distractors: Chalukya, Pallava, Pandya, Chera, Solanki, Parmar
| Dynasty | Local government |
| Cholas | Culmination: elected assemblies, committees, lottery - "grassroots democracy" (Nilakanta Sastri) |
| Pallavas | Sabha and ur assemblies already appear in their inscriptions [GK]; the Cholas built on this base |
| Badami Chalukyas | Highly centralised |
| Rashtrakutas | Allowed village assemblies a significant role in administration and disputes |
- Burton Stein: Segmentary state → local autonomy stayed intact at the periphery
### Assemblies → village type and composition
- Ur: Ordinary villages; Sabha: Brahmana villages; Nagaram: merchant towns
| Assembly | Where | Composition |
| Ur | Ordinary, non-brahmadeya (vellanvagai) villages | General assembly of local residents/landholders; dealt with land sale, gift, tax exemptions; generally fewer than ten members |
| Sabha / Mahasabha | Brahmadeya villages (agraharas, rent-free) | Brahmans only; criteria: property, family, learning, good conduct |
| Nagaram | Market and commercial centres | Assembly of merchants |
- Nattar: Assembly of the nadu; made up of leading peasant (vellala) landholders
- Handled revenue collection + local disputes above village level
- Nagaram: Of the nadu's market town; worked alongside it
- ⚠ Trap: Ur = non-brahmadeya; Sabha = brahmadeya (Brahmans only); Nagaram = merchants
### Uttaramerur inscriptions → ruler, year, qualifications and election
- Uttaramerur: Village self-government — the Kudavolai system; Variyams — specialized village committees
- Ruler: Two inscriptions of Parantaka I, dated 919 and 921 CE
- Location: Uttaramerur, Kanchipuram district
- Importance: Source for Chola village government (APPSC G2 2000, 2003); most detailed record of village administration in ancient India
- ⚠ Trap: Not Kudimiamalai, Gangaikondacholapuram, Silappadikaram, Tolkappiyam or Periya Puranam
| Qualifications | Disqualifications |
| Own at least 1/4 veli of land | Failure to submit accounts |
| Own house (residence) | Committed sins |
| Age 35-70 | Stolen property |
| Knowledge of the Vedas | Served on a committee in the past three years |
- Election procedure - Kudavolai:
- Eligible names written on palm leaves → put in a pot (kudam) → a young boy/girl drew the names
- Term: Members held office for three-year terms; chosen by lot or rotation
- Bar: Those who had served in the last three years were barred
### Variyams → function, and powers of assemblies
| Variyam (committee) | Function |
| Samvatsara variyam | Annual committee - annual works; also justice |
| Eri variyam | Tanks; distribution of water to fields |
| Pon variyam | Gold - finance, currency checking |
| Thotta variyam | Gardens / orchards |
| Panchavara variyam | Standing committee (supervision); exact duty not given in your sources |
- Other committees: Law and order, justice; the Uttaramerur record also covers committees for irrigation, gardens, justice and finance
## 1. Land rights
- Assembly = absolute proprietor of village lands and newly acquired lands
## 2. Finance
- Collected royal dues in cash, kind or both
- Auction land of defaulters
## 3. Other functions
- Tanks (eri variyam)
- Law and order and justice through committees
- Temple services: Rajaraja I directed Cholamandalam Sabhas to serve the Brihadeshvara temple (Brihadeeswara)

### Chola assembly vs 73rd Amendment → Articles
| Point | Chola village assembly | 73rd Amendment PRIs |
| Selection | Lottery (kudavolai) among qualified | Direct election by the Gram Sabha electorate (Art. 243C) |
| Eligibility | Property, age 35-70, Vedic learning | Universal adult franchise; age 21; state-law qualifications |
| Term | 3 years | 5 years (Art. 243E); election within 6 months of dissolution |
| Committees | Variyams | Standing committees under state law |
| Women / quota | Not provided; no quota | 1/3 seats reserved (Art. 243D) and chairperson posts for women; SC/ST reservation |
- Gram Sabha: Art. 243A
- Tiers: 3 tiers; intermediate tier optional below 20 lakh population
- Early model: Chola village administration = early example of the Panchayati Raj System
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: PM Modi cited the Chola Kudavolai "ballot pot" system (Uttaramerur) as proof of India's early democratic traditions (Aug 2025)
- Significance: Nilakanta Sastri → remarkable example of grassroots democracy; often called an early Panchayati Raj
- Criticism & debates:
- Democratic in form but elitist → limited to propertied, Vedic-learned Brahmans in sabhas (Nilakanta Sastri: "educated and propertied elite")
- Burton Stein's "segmentary state" view stresses local autonomy
- Comparative note: Kakatiya and Velanati inscriptions show village mahajana assemblies in Andhra → a weaker parallel
