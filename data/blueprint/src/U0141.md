### Shivaji's career
- Chauth and sardeshmukhi let a regional state become a pan-Indian power without direct administration

- 1646 — Torna, first fort, at 16

- Bargirs = state horses; shiledars = own horses

### Treaties and Mughal grants → year and terms
## 1. Treaty of Purandar
- Treaty of Purandar, 11 June 1665: With the Mughals under Jai Singh
- Ceded 23 of 35 forts
- Son Sambhaji sent to the Mughal court
- To help the Mughals against Bijapur
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes wrongly call Purandar part of Shivaji's "conflicts with Bijapur"
- Exam angle: the 2011 APPSC option "1665 - Raja Jai Singh" is the right pairing.
## 2. Treaty with Golconda
- Treaty with Golconda (AP link): Shivaji visited Hyderabad on 7 March 1677; treaty with Golconda's Abul Hasan Tana Shah, through Akkanna and Madanna, for aid in his southern campaign
- Terms: money for southern conquests; spoils to be shared
- Shivaji refused to share the spoils
## 3. Later grants and settlements
| Grant / treaty | Year | Terms |

### Coronation (1674), titles and FIRSTs
- Place & date: Raigad, 1674 (6 June), Vedic rites
- Priest: Gaga Bhatt of Banaras (Varanasi)
- Title: "Shri Raja Shiva Chhatrapati"; also styled Haindava Dharmoddharak
- New era: Rajyabhisheka shaka - asserted sovereignty against the Mughals and Sultanates
- First conquest: Seized hill forts from declining Bijapur, beginning with Torna (1646), his first fort, at age 16
- ⚠ Shivneri = birth (1630); Torna = first fort (1646); Raigad = coronation (1674)
### Ashtapradhan → minister, Persian title and portfolio
| Minister | Persian / alternative title | Portfolio |
| Peshwa | Mukhya Pradhan | Prime Minister, general administration |
| Amatya | Majumdar | Finance: treasury, accounts audit, revenue |
| Sachiv | Shurnavis (Surnis) | Royal correspondence, records, internal (home) affairs |
| Mantri | Waqia-navis (Waqianavis, Vakeenavis) | Intelligence, the king's diary, royal household and safety |
| Senapati | Sar-i-Naubat | Military commander (commander-in-chief) |
| Sumant | Dabir | Foreign affairs; master of ceremonies who received envoys |
| Nyayadhish | - | Justice (chief justice) |
| Panditrao | Danadhyaksha | Religious affairs and charitable grants |
- Features:
- Posts not hereditary; ministers could be transferred or dismissed at the king's will
- Each reported directly to the Chhatrapati
- All except Panditrao and Nyayadhish had to lead military campaigns
- Later change: Under the later Peshwas the Peshwa post became hereditary and supreme
- PYQ answers:
- Dabir = master of ceremonies (APPSC-G1 2024)
- Sar-i-Naubat = military commander (APPSC-G2 2023)
- ⚠ Sachiv = correspondence; Sumant = foreign affairs; Mantri = intelligence
### Revenue system → terms, units and officials
## 1. Land revenue terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Ryotwari-type direct settlement | State dealt with the cultivator |
| Kathi | Measuring rod; Annaji Datto's survey |
| Jirayat | Dry land |
| Bagayat | Irrigated land |
| Saranjam | Service land grants; returned under Shahu and the Peshwas |
## 2. Units and officials
- Divisions: Kingdom divided into prants
| Unit | Official |
| Prant (province) | Subedar / Sarsubedar [GK] |
| Pargana, Tarf | Deshmukh, Deshpande [GK] |
| Mauza (village) | Patil (head), Kulkarni (accountant) [GK] |
## 3. Grants and hereditary rights
- Jagirs abolished: Officials paid in cash, not land grants
- Deshmukhs: Hereditary deshmukhs and deshpandes / watandars bypassed or regulated, their rights curbed; they resisted
- Big deshmukhs opposed Shivaji as they did not back an independent Maratha state (UPPSC 2024 - A and R true, R explains A)
### Chauth and sardeshmukhi → rate, nature and recognition
| Levy | Rate | Nature / justification |
| Chauth | 25% (one-fourth) of land revenue | Levied on territories outside direct Maratha rule; protection money against third-party invasion, or to be spared Maratha raids |
| Sardeshmukhi | Additional 10% | Claimed as the king's hereditary right as Sardeshmukh (chief headman) of the Deccan |
- Where collected: Lands outside the Maratha realm - Mughal and Deccan Sultanate territory
- Purpose: Financed military expansion + political hegemony without the cost of direct governance
- Recognition:
- Formally conceded by the Mughals in 1719
- By the Nizam at Palkhed; Nizam forced to pay chauth again after the 1795 war
- ⚠ Chauth = 25% (protection); Sardeshmukhi = 10% (hereditary right)
- Exam angle: "Maratha revenue claim for protection" = Chauth (UPPSC 2018).

| Shivrai | Shivaji's copper coin |
| Rajvyavahar Kosh | Replaced Persian official terms with Sanskrit ones |
| Modi script | Cursive script for Marathi administrative records |
| Ganimi Kava | Guerrilla hit-and-run warfare using the Sahyadri terrain |
- Official language: Marathi and Sanskrit replaced Persian
### Military organisation → forts, army and navy
## 1. Forts
- Bases: Raigad, Pratapgad, Sinhagad = bases for defence and expansion
- Fort officers: Three of equal rank = havaldar, sabnis and sarnaubat
- Havaldar = garrison commander
- Sabnis = records
- Sarnobat / sarnaubat = military officer
| Fort type | Examples |
| Giri Durg (hill) | Raigad, Pratapgad, Torna, Rajgad, Sinhagad, Panhala |
| Bhu Durg (land) | Vishalgad |
| Jal Durg (sea) | Sindhudurg, Vijaydurg, Khanderi, Suvarnadurg |
## 2. Army
- Command: Sar-i-Naubat (see Ashtapradhan)
- Cavalry: bargirs (state-equipped) vs shiledars (own horses)
- Pay: Standing army paid in cash, no jagirs - loyalty direct to the state
- Battle of Salher (1672): Mughals beaten in open battle
## 3. Navy
- Navy: Shivaji built a navy
- Sea forts / bases: Sindhudurg, Kolaba, Vijaydurg - guarded the Konkan
- ⚠ Bargirs = state horses; shiledars = own horses
### Marathas → rulers, years and contributions (Shivaji to Baji Rao I)
| Ruler | Period | Contributions / key steps |
| Shivaji | Lived in the 17th century | Founded and organised the Maratha state; established the Ashtapradhan |
| Sambhaji | 1680–1689 (1680-89) | Continued resistance against the Mughals; captured and executed by Aurangzeb (1689), which turned the conflict into a people's war |
| Rajaram | 1689-1700 | Younger son; moved to Gingee (Jinji), directed resistance from there and drew Aurangzeb south; created the post of Pratinidhi |
| Tarabai (for Shivaji II) | 1700-1707 | Rajaram's widow; regent for her young son after Rajaram's death; massive raids into Mughal territory; led Marathas at Aurangzeb's death (1707) |
| Shahu | 1707-1749 | Sambhaji's son, released by Bahadur Shah I (1707); beat Tarabai at Khed (1707); ruled from Satara; appointed Balaji Vishwanath Peshwa (1713) |
| Balaji Vishwanath | 1713–1720 | Consolidated Peshwa authority |
| Baji Rao I | 1720–1740 | Major Maratha expansion in North India |
- Jinji: Became Rajaram's base against Aurangzeb
- Aurangzeb: Died in the Deccan in 1707 without subduing the Marathas
- Battle of Khed (1707): Shahu defeated Tarabai's forces, took the throne
- Balaji Vishwanath: Peshwa 1713-20, appointed by Shahu
- Got the 1719 grants (see treaties page)
- Power shift: From 1713, real power moved from the Chhatrapati at Satara → Peshwa
- Exam angle: order is Sambhaji - Rajaram - Shivaji II - Shahu (UPPSC 2005); "Maratha woman who fought the Mughals from 1700" = Tarabai.
### Universal angles
## 1. Significance & comparison
- Significance:
- State model: Centralised, non-hereditary, cash-paid state
- Mughal finances: Marathas' Deccan war, especially Tarabai's raids, broke them
- Hindavi Swarajya: Marathi and Sanskrit promoted in administration over Persian
- Chauth-sardeshmukhi: Let a regional state become a pan-Indian power without direct administration
- Comparative note:
- Mughal mansabdari rested on jagirs vs Shivaji paid in cash
- Ashtapradhan advisory vs Mughal wazir system hierarchical
## 2. AP angle
- AP angle: Shivaji's Golconda alliance (1677) through Akkanna-Madanna; Tana Shah funded the Karnataka campaign
- Chauth from the Nizam: Taken after the 1795 war
- Pushed the Nizam toward the British Subsidiary Alliance (1798)
## 3. Current relevance
- UNESCO: Maratha Military Landscapes of India inscribed as World Heritage in 2025
- NCERT map row: Removal of a "Maratha Empire" map from revised textbook → PIL in the Bombay HC (20 May 2026)
- Peak extent: Attock to Cuttack; Gulf of Khambhat to beyond the Kaveri
