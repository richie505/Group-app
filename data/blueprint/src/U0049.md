### Stupa features and Buddha symbols → terms

- Structure: 4 ayaka platforms projecting at the 4 cardinal points, each with 5 ayaka pillars = the Pancha Kalyanakas

- Found at: Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda, Ghantasala, Bhattiprolu, Jaggayyapeta

### Amaravati Mahachaitya → patrons, phases, size and themes
## 1. Buddhist structures
- Amaravati Stupa: Built by the Satavahanas (Satavahana period / rule) — Ayaka pillars represent the five key events in Buddha's life
- Chaityams: Built by the Satavahana queens for Buddhist monks
- Vadhika: Satavahana period — built by carpenters
## 2. Phase → patron
| Phase | Patron / act |
| Origin (Ashoka's age) | Mahadeva Bhikshu (by Andhra tradition sent by Ashoka; first Buddhist teacher in Andhra) enshrined the Buddha's bone powder in a copper urn; Naga king Nagashoka helped build it at Dharanikota |
| Enlargement | Enlarged by Vasishthiputra Satakarni → added limestone sculptures + a stone railing (vedika) (APPSC 2001 key) |
| Construction (some notes) | Pulumavi II (his inscription on a gateway pillar) |
| Final enlargement | Under the Ikshvakus |
| Prakara | Acharya Nagarjuna built its prakara (enclosing wall) |
- Site: Satavahana (built under Pulumavi II; enlarged by the Ikshvakus); on the Krishna, Guntur/Palnadu region
- Inscriptions: Pulumavi II; "Nagabu"; stupa of the Chaitika Sangha
## 3. Type, size and themes
- Type: Dhatugarbha (relic) stupa over the Buddha's relics → like Jaggayyapeta, Bhattiprolu, Nagarjunakonda, Salihundam
- Themes: Jatakas, full life of the Buddha, 3 group-dance scenes, Dimikudu the leather-worker offering a Purnakumbha
- ⚠ Railing (vedika) = Vasishthiputra Satakarni; prakara = Nagarjuna; final enlargement = Ikshvakus

### Art schools → region, material, phases and comparison
## 1. Amaravati school — region, period and material
- Region: Developed on its own in the lower Krishna-Godavari valley (Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda)
- Period: 2nd century BCE to the 3rd century CE, under Satavahana patronage
- Final, most mature phase under the Ikshvakus
- Material: white marble-like limestone; Ikshvaku phase also used green stone
## 2. Style and themes
- Style:
- narrative reliefs of the Buddha's life and the Jatakas → movement and emotion
- Themes: Jataka panels, Dharmachakra Parivartana, Buddha at Gaya
- Influence: Amaravati school → Sri Lanka and SE Asia
## 3. Phases of depiction
- Early phase: Buddha shown aniconically, only through symbols (see the Ayaka symbol table) → bodhi tree, empty throne, footprints, wheel, stupa
- Later phase: Buddha in human form, seated on a lotus with dancing Gandharvas and Yakshas
## 4. Ivory and wall paintings
- Ivory:
- Guilds of ivory workers donated at Amaravati
- Ivory carvers of Vidisha carved the Sanchi gateway
## 5. Gandhara, Mathura and Amaravati compared
| Feature | Gandhara | Mathura | Amaravati |
| Patrons | Kushanas | Shungas/Kushanas | Satavahanas, Ikshvakus |
| Material | Grey-blue schist [GK] | Spotted red sandstone [GK] | White limestone |
| Influence | Greco-Roman | Indigenous | Indigenous |
| Sites | Taxila, Hadda | Mathura | Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda |
- Material → school
| Material | School |
| Grey schist / stucco | Gandhara |

### Amaravati school
## Builders and scholars
- Nagashoka (Naga king) — helped build the stupa at Dharanikota
- Mahadeva Bhikshu — sent by Ashoka; first Buddhist teacher in Andhra
- James Fergusson — Tree and Serpent Worship; 'Epitome of Indian architecture'
- Colin Mackenzie identified the stupa (1797); Robert Sewell excavated Amaravati (1877); Alexander Rea excavated Bhattiprolu (1892)
- Nelakondapalli — post-Satavahana mahastupa, sculptor's workshop — V. V. Krishna Sastry
## Sects
- Five Chaitika branches (Purvasaila, Aparasaila, Uttarasaila, Rajagirika, Siddharthika) — of the Mahasanghikas
## Style
- Leather-worker Dimikudu offers a Purnakumbha
- Style marked by movement and emotion; reliefs tell Buddha's life and Jatakas (subject does not explain style)
- Buddha depiction — symbols in early phase (Bodhi tree, empty throne, footprints, wheel, stupa), human form later (seated on lotus, dancing Gandharvas and Yakshas)
- Sanchi gateway — carved by ivory carvers of Vidisha; ivory guilds also donated at Amaravati
- White Palnadu limestone — Amaravati; grey schist then stucco — Gandhara
### Exam corner
- Kielhorn — identified "Sri Parvataswamy" with the Buddha
- Amaravati influence — Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia
- 'Tambaya Danamu' — inscriptional donation term at a Buddhist site in Krishna district
- Satavahana queens' chaityas — for Buddhist monks (kings Brahmanical)
### Stupa features and Buddha symbols → terms
- ★ Stupa — mound; Garbhagriha — where the deity's image is installed; Pradakshina patha — circular path around the stupa
- ★ Wheel with deer — first preaching (at Mrigadava / Sarnath)
- ★ Platform with Ayaka (Aryak) pillars — Amaravati stupa
- ★ Chaitya — place of worship / assembly chamber; Vihara — monks' shelter in the rainy season
### Amaravati Mahachaitya → patrons, phases, size and themes
- ★ Stupa — usually a repository of relics; a votive and commemorative structure in Buddhism
- ★ Stupa tradition predates Buddhism (not Buddhist in origin); Vedic funeral practice pitramedha collected ashes
### Other Andhra sites → features
- ★ Bhattiprolu casket inscriptions (3rd c. BC) — ruler Raja Kubiraka
### Ancient names and Buddhist sects → centres
- ★ Siddharthaka Chaitya — Gudivada
- ★ Purvasaila — Dhanyakataka; Uttarasaila — Jaggayyapeta; Rajagirika — Guntupalli
### Rock-cut caves → site and feature
- ★ Chaitya — place of worship (shrine/prayer hall); Vihara — dwelling of monks (monastery)
- ★ Guntupalli (then West Godavari) — rock-cut Chaityas and Viharas; Eastern Deccan chaityas and viharas usually rock-cut
- ★ Largest, most evolved Hinayana chaitya hall — Karle (Pune district)
### Patrons and donors → gifts
- ★ 'Tambaya Danamu / Dana' — Kotturu (Dhanadibbalu), on the Sarada river near Yelamanchili, Visakhapatnam district
- Nannayabhattu (began Telugu Mahabharata) — patron Rajaraja Narendra (Eastern Chalukya)
- Srinatha — patron Veera Bhadra Reddy
