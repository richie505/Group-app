# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:106 - Khalji dynasty - Jalaluddin; Alauddin Khalji's conquests, Deccan campaigns of Malik Kafur
### Jalaluddin Khalji (1290-1296)
- North: kingdoms annexed; South: kings kept their thrones and paid tribute
- Crowned at Kilokhari, 1290, aged about 70
- Benevolent to Hindus; lenient to Malik Chhajju
- Mongol converts = "New Mussalmans"
- Alauddin's Devagiri raid: first Turkish invasion of the south
- Murdered by Alauddin at Kara (1296)
### Alauddin Khalji (1296-1316)
- Earlier name Ali Gurshasp; Sikandar-i-Sani
- Posts under Jalaluddin: Amir-i-Tuzuk, Ariz-i-Mumalik (army)
- Court poet: Amir Khusrau
- Mongols: Kili (Zafar Khan killed); new capital Siri

### Khalji Sultans → accession, policy, contribution
## 1. Jalaluddin Khalji (1290-1296)
- Origin: Khalji from Turkestan; had fought the Mongols
- Accession: Crowned himself at Kilokhari, 1290, aged about 70
- Policy:
- First Sultan with benevolent attitude to Hindus
- Lenient to rebels, e.g. Malik Chhajju
- End:
- Nephew + son-in-law Alauddin raided Devagiri (1294-96) = first Turkish invasion of the south
- Alauddin murdered Jalaluddin at Kara (1296)
## 2. Alauddin Khalji (1296-1316)
- Earlier name: Ali Gurshasp
- Ambitions: World conqueror like Alexander (Sikandar-i-Sani) + found a new religion
- Advice against both: Kotwal of Delhi Ala-ul-Mulk (Barani's uncle)
- Nobles: Ulugh Khan, Nusrat Khan, Zafar Khan, Ain-ul-Mulk Multani, Malik Kafur, Ghazi Malik
- Court poet: Amir Khusrau

### Posts, titles and terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Amir-i-Tuzuk | Master of ceremonies (Alauddin's post under Jalaluddin) |
| Ariz-i-Mumalik | Army minister (Alauddin's post under Jalaluddin) |
| Sikandar-i-Sani | Second Alexander → Alauddin's ambition to be a world conqueror |
- ⚠ Trap: some notes wrongly call Ariz-i-Mumalik "minister of law"
### Mongol invasions → leaders, battles, defence
## 1. Mongols and Timur
- Genghis Khan: Reached the Indus during Iltutmish's reign — his threat or entry into Indian history in 1221 CE = first Mongol threat (Chengiz Khan, 1221)
- ⚠ Trap: "First Mongol invasion under Jalaluddin" is false (UPSC 2022)
- Alauddin Khalji's reign: Repeated Mongol invasions — contributed to a large standing army, military reforms and revenue reforms
- Ghazi Malik: Defeated the Mongols in 1320 — chased them 29 times; later became Ghiyasuddin Tughluq
- Timur — also known as Taimur: Invaded Delhi in 1398 — "Timur's invasion"; severely weakened the Delhi Sultanate; associated with Nasiruddin Mahmud Tughlaq
## 2. Under Jalaluddin
- Jalaluddin's response: Defeated a Mongol invasion
- Converted + settled Mongols = "New Mussalmans"
- Married his daughter to Ulugh Khan, a descendant of Chengiz Khan
## 3. Under Alauddin
- Battle of Kili: Zafar Khan killed
- Siege of Delhi: Mongol army besieged Delhi → Alauddin sheltered in Siri fort
- Defence:
- Repaired old forts + built new ones on north-west frontier (completing Balban's policy)
- Large standing army
- Built Siri as new capital
- Ghazi Malik made governor of Punjab to hold the frontier

- Policy:
- North → conquered kingdoms annexed
- South → kings kept their thrones + paid tribute
- Exam angle: chronological order (UPPSC 2022: Gujarat, Ranthambore, Chittor, Warangal); statements on Ulugh Khan and Nusrat Khan leading the 1299 Gujarat campaign.
### Chittor and the Padmini legend → text, author, debate
- Chittor (1303): Alauddin defeated Raja Ratan Singh → Rani Padmini + Rajput women performed jauhar

### Successors → end of the Khaljis
- Alauddin's death (1316): Malik Kafur set aside Khizr Khan → enthroned minor Shihabuddin Umar → Kafur then murdered
- Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah (1316-20):
- General Khusrau Khan subdued Warangal again (3rd invasion, 1318)
- End of Khaljis:
- Khusrau Khan (Nasiruddin Khusrau Shah) murdered Mubarak
- Ghazi Malik killed him in 1320 → founded Tughlaq dynasty
- ⚠ Khizr Khan (Alauddin's son, set aside 1316) ≠ Khizr Khan (Sayyid Sultan, 1414-1421)

## Malik Kafur's southern campaigns
- Devagiri (1307–08, first) → Warangal (1309–10) → Dwarasamudra (1310–11) → Madurai (1311)
## Kakatiya–Delhi relations
- 1303 — Kakatiyas defeated a Delhi army at Upparapalli
- Treaty 1309–10; help against the Pandyas 1311; tribute stopped after 1316
### Mongol threat
- Alauddin did not abandon the north-western frontier: repaired and built forts, founded Siri, made Ghazi Malik governor of Punjab
- Mongol threat → a large standing army, paid for by military, revenue and market reforms
- Genghis Khan reached the Indus c. 1221 under Iltutmish; 1398 invasion was by Timur
### End of the Khaljis
- ★ Alauddin's conquests in order: Gujarat / Jaisalmer (1299) → Ranthambhor (1301) → Chittor (1303) → Warangal (1309–10)
- After Alauddin's death (1316), Malik Kafur set aside Khizr Khan and crowned the minor Shihabuddin Umar
- Khusrau Khan defeated Warangal under Mubarak Shah — third invasion, 1318
- Ghazi Malik's victory over Khusrau Khan ended Khalji rule
### Exam corner
- Padmavat — by Malik Muhammad Jayasi, in Awadhi, 1540; tells the Padmini story (some scholars call it legend)
### Khalji Sultans → accession, policy, contribution
- ★ Malik Kafur ("Hazar Dinari", bought for a thousand dinars) — taken by Nusrat Khan in the Gujarat campaign
- ★ Alauddin wanted to start a new religion; dropped it on advice (Ulemas opposed); Sikandar-i-Sani on his coins
- ★ Zafar Khan — Alauddin's commander who died fighting the Mongols
- ★ After 1306, the Ravi was the Sultanate–Mongol boundary; Malik Kafur and Ghazi Malik stopped Mongols under Kabak there
- Bahlol Lodi ruled 1451–1489 (not 1491)
### Mongol invasions → leaders, battles, defence
- ★ Genghis Khan's original name — Temujin (Temuchin)
- ★ Genghis Khan came through the North-West Frontier under Iltutmish, chasing Jalaluddin Mengubirni of Khwarazm, who sought shelter with Iltutmish
### Alauddin's conquests and Malik Kafur's Deccan campaigns → year, ruler defeated, commander
- ★ 1303 — Kakatiya army (Prataparudra II) defeated Alauddin Khalji's army at Warangal; led by Fakhruddin Juna and Malik Chhajju
- ★ Padmini — wife of Rana Ratan Singh (Padmavat, Jayasi, 1540)
