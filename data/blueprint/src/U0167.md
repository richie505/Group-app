### Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan → contributions
## 1. Haidar Ali
- Rise: Under the Wodeyar kingdom of Mysore; Faujdar of Dindigul in 1755 under minister Nanjaraja
- Military: Adopted Western techniques; modern arsenal at Dindigul with French help
- Alliances (1st war): Won over the Marathas + detached the Nizam from the British
## 2. Tipu Sultan - capital, embassies, reforms
- Capital: Seringapatam (Srirangapatna)
- Foreign embassies:
- Ambassadors to France for European technology → built a navy (APPSC-G1 2018 key)
- Envoys to Turkey (Constantinople), Afghanistan (Zaman Shah), Mauritius
- Jacobin Club: Helped French soldiers set up a Jacobin Club at Seringapatam (1797); became a member
- Called himself "Citizen Tipu"; planted a Tree of Liberty
- Reforms:
- Introduced sericulture in Mysore
- New calendar, new coinage, new weights and measures; used war rockets
- Exam angle: statement questions on "French embassy + navy" point to Tipu, not Haidar or the Wodeyars.
## 3. Tipu's titles
- Titles: "Tiger of Mysore"; "Citizen Tipu"; Padshah
- ⚠ Dindigul arsenal = Haidar; French embassy + navy + Jacobin Club = Tipu

### Anglo-Mysore Wars and their battles — year, war, result
## 1. Anglo-Mysore Wars
| War / battle | Year | Key facts / result |
| First Anglo-Mysore War | 1767 to 1769 | The British and Mysore — Haidar Ali led Mysore and was victorious; the Treaty of Madras — concluded in 1769 |
| Battle of Pollilur | 10 September 1780 | Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan (who led the Mysore side) defeated Colonel Baillie — a major British defeat; 2nd war [GK] |
| Second Anglo-Mysore War | 1780 to 1784 | The British and Mysore — Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan; a major Anglo-Mysore conflict; Haidar Ali allied with the Nizam and the Marathas |
| Battle of Porto Novo | July 1781 | British forces under Sir Eyre Coote defeated Haidar Ali — a British victory that reversed the British defeat at Pollilur in 1780; part of the Second Anglo-Mysore War [GK] |
| Seringapatam (1792) | 1792 | 3rd war; Cornwallis's coalition won; Tipu sued for peace |
| Fourth Anglo-Mysore War | 1799 | The British fought Tipu Sultan — Tipu Sultan died in 1799 |
| Seringapatam (1799) | 1799 | 4th war; British won; Tipu killed defending his capital |
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say in the Second war the Nizam was not with the British but was later bought off, and the Marathas had earlier attacked Haidar and made peace with the British at Salbai 1782 (see the Nizam and Marathas page)
- 3rd war:
- 1790: Tipu defeated the British army under General Meadows
- 1791: Cornwallis took Bangalore (March 1791) via Ambur and Vellore
- 4th war - Tipu's death: Fortified Seringapatam; killed on 4 May 1799 defending it
### Treaties of the Mysore wars — year and terms
## 1. Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
- Terms: Tipu ceded about half his territory + ₹3 crore war indemnity + two sons as hostages
| Gainer | Share |
| Nizam | Areas from the Krishna to beyond the Pennar (UPSC). This included half of Rayalaseema: Bellary, Gurramkonda, Gandikota, Kambham, Kadapa (CDX p.367) |
| Marathas | Areas around the Tungabhadra and its tributaries (UPSC) |
| British | Malabar, Coorg (as protectorate), Dindigul and the Bara Mahal (Salem region) [GK]. Bara Mahal is where Read and Munro began the Ryotwari system (APP) |
- Payment: Half the indemnity paid at once; sons returned after the rest was paid
## 2. Settlement of Mysore (1799) and the Ceded Districts (1800)
- Settlement: Mysore partitioned
- Hindu Wodeyar dynasty restored (boy ruler Krishnaraja Wodeyar III) under a Subsidiary Alliance
- Ceded Districts: Nizam's share of Mysore lands → handed to the Company in 1800 to pay for the subsidiary force (Block11)
## 3. Treaty traps
- ⚠ Trap: Treaty of Madras (1769) = with Haidar vs Treaty of Mangalore (1784) = with Tipu
### Nizam and Marathas — side in each war
| War | Nizam | Marathas |
| First | Joined the British, then detached by Haidar (UPSC) | Joined against Haidar, then won over by him (UPSC) |
| Second | Not with the British (anti-British confederacy, later bought off) [GK] | Earlier attacked Haidar; peace with British at Salbai 1782 (CDI) |
| Third | With the British (grand alliance) | With the British |
| Fourth | With the British (subsidiary ally since 1798) | Neutral [GK] (UPSC notes say they "supported" the British) |
- ⚠ Only the Third war had both Nizam and Marathas With the British

### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Third war ended Tipu's supremacy in the south
- Fourth war removed the French-backed threat; brought Mysore + Andhra (via Ceded Districts) under the Company
- AP angle: Ceded Districts (Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary) came to the British in 1800 as a direct result of the Mysore wars (p.367; Block11)

### Haidar & Tipu

- Rose under the Wodeyars; Faujdar of Dindigul (1755) under minister Nanjaraja

## Tipu Sultan
- Jacobin Club at Seringapatam set up 1797; Tipu joined as 'Citizen Tipu', planted a Tree of Liberty
- Used war rockets (did not discontinue them)
- Titles: 'Tiger of Mysore', 'Citizen Tipu', 'Padshah' — 'Lion of Mysore' is not among them
- Sent ambassadors to France and built a navy (APPSC-G1 2018 key) — Tipu, not Haidar or the Wodeyars
- Third Anglo-Mysore War — ended Tipu's supremacy in southern India
- Ceded Districts came to the British in 1800 as a consequence of the Mysore wars (not the Third War's stated significance)
### Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
- Tipu gave up about half his territory, paid ₹3 crore indemnity, two sons as hostages

### Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan → contributions
- ★ Tipu Sultan — ambassadors to France for European technology; tried to build a navy and modernise his army
- ★ Tipu Sultan — set up embassies in foreign countries on modern lines: Ottoman Empire/Istanbul, France, Iran, Afghanistan, Arabia
- ★ Tipu's capital — Srirangapatna; member of the Jacobin Club (founded by French officials); planted the "tree of liberty"
- ★ Haidar Ali — modern armoury at Dindigul (1755)
- Mysore army under Venkata Raya conquered Dindigul in 1742; 18 Palayams under Dindigul Semai
### Other persons → roles in the Mysore wars
