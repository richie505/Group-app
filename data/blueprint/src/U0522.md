### FDI vs FPI
- The 10% line: 10% or more in a listed company = FDI; less than 10% = FPI
| Basis | FDI | FPI / FII |
| Threshold | >=10% listed / any unlisted stake | <10% listed |
| Nature | Long-term, stable, control | Short-term, "hot money" |
| Brings | Capital + technology + management | Financial capital |
| Regulator | DPIIT + RBI | SEBI (FPI Regulations 2019) + RBI |
### Forms of foreign capital
- Foreign capital = FDI, trade credit, NRI deposits, ECBs (APPSC 2019)
- ECB, trade credit, FCCB = debt; ECBs regulated by RBI under FEMA
- ADR/GDR = portfolio investment
- P-Notes: issued by registered FPIs; investor not registered with SEBI
- Greenfield = new facility; brownfield = acquisition
- 10% threshold: Arvind Mayaram Committee

### FDI — nature and what counts as FDI
## 1. Instruments — FDI can be made through
- Equity shares, debentures, preference shares, share warrants
## 2. Nature
- FDI: Foreign direct investment — a largely non-debt-creating capital flow; generally longer-term foreign investment in Indian enterprises
- Associated with capital, technology transfer, management skills, innovation, competition, employment and export competitiveness
## 3. What counts as FDI
- Subsidiaries of foreign companies in India
- Majority foreign equity holdings in Indian companies
- Companies exclusively financed by foreign companies
- ⚠ Portfolio investment does not automatically constitute FDI
## 4. Types of FDI
| Type | Meaning | Example |
| Greenfield | New facility | Hyundai, Sriperumbudur |
| Brownfield | Acquisition | Vodafone-Hutchison Essar |
### FDI vs FPI and FDI vs FII
## 1. FDI vs FPI
| Basis | FDI | FPI (FPI/FII) |
| Type | Direct investment | Portfolio investment |
| Stability | Relatively stable and long-term | More volatile than FDI |
| Brings | Can bring technology and management skills | Primarily financial capital |
| Target | Specific sectors and enterprises | Primarily investment in securities |
| Control | Greater involvement and control | No comparable direct managerial involvement |
| Threshold | >=10% equity in a listed company, or any stake in an unlisted one | <10% in a listed company |
| Nature | Long-term, management control, technology | Short-term, liquid, "hot money" |
| Route/regulator | Automatic/Government route; DPIIT + RBI | SEBI-registered FPI; SEBI + RBI |
- ⚠ 10% line: >=10% = FDI; <10% = FPI
## 2. FDI vs FII
| Basis | FII — Foreign Institutional Investor | FDI |
| Type | Portfolio investment | Direct investment |
| Brings | Primarily financial capital | Capital along with technology and management |
| Term | More short-term; buoyant | Long-term; relatively stable |
| Market | Primarily securities markets | Investment in enterprises or sectors |
| Volatility | More volatile | Less volatile than FII, according to the source |
## 3. SEBI FPI Regulations, 2019
- Issued 2019 — unified FIIs, sub-accounts and QFIs under the FPI framework
- FII vs FPI: Essentially the same; FPI opened in India in 1992
### P-Notes and classification of foreign capital
## 1. P-Notes
- An instrument issued by a registered FII or FPI to an overseas investor
- Enables the overseas investor to obtain exposure to Indian stocks
- ⚠ An overseas investor using a P-Note does not directly register with SEBI
| Basis | Participatory Notes (P-Notes) | Foreign direct investment |
| Exposure | Portfolio-type exposure — linked to Indian securities; enable an overseas investor to obtain market exposure | Direct investment — in an enterprise or business; gives the foreign investor a direct investment |
| Stability | Relatively more mobile and volatile | Relatively more stable and long-term |
| Regulation | The ultimate P-Note investor does not directly register with SEBI | Subject to the applicable FDI framework |
## 2. Classification of foreign capital
- Foreign capital: Includes FDI, trade credit, NRI deposits and ECBs - all four (APPSC-GS 2019 key)
| Form | Classification |
| FDI | non-debt, long-term, management control (>=10%) |
| FPI/FII | non-debt, portfolio (<10%), liquid; FII and FPI are essentially the same |
| ECB, trade credit, FCCB | debt |
| NRI deposits (FCNR(B), NRE) | banking capital (debt) |
| ADR/GDR | equity raised abroad through depository receipts, counted as FPI [GK]; counted as portfolio investment in BoP data |
| Participatory notes | offshore derivative instruments issued by registered FPIs to overseas investors [GK]; issued by registered FIIs/FPIs to overseas investors not registered with SEBI → portfolio channel |
### Acts, committees, policy bodies and Press Note 3
## 1. Laws, committees and bodies
| Act / body | Year | Provision / role |
| Arvind Mayaram Committee | - | Source of the 10% FDI/FPI threshold (FEMA) |
| FEMA, 1999 | 1999 | Governs foreign exchange transactions (replaced FERA, 1973); RBI regulates ECBs under it |
| SEBI (FPI) Regulations 2019 | 2019 | Law for FPIs; SEBI (FPI Regulations, 2019) + RBI = regulators |
| FIPB | Abolished June 2017 | Applications now go to the concerned ministries/departments via the FIF Portal run by DPIIT |
| DPIIT | Consolidated FDI Policy 2020 | Nodal department; under Ministry of Commerce and Industry; nodal for FDI policy and FDI data |
| NSWS | Since August 2022 | National Single Window System - FIF Portal part of it |
- ECBs are regulated by RBI under FEMA
## 2. Press Note 3 (April 2020)
- Press Note 3 (April 2020): All FDI from a country sharing a land border with India (without naming China) needs government approval
- Countries: China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan, Afghanistan
## 3. March 2026 amendment
- Approval: Cabinet; Amended March 2026, effective 1 May 2026
- Beneficial owner: Defined as per the PML Rules, 2005
- Relaxation: Firms with up to 10% Chinese/Hong Kong (land-border) beneficial ownership/shareholding may invest via the automatic route
- Not for entities registered in China, HK or other land-border countries
### Routes and sectoral caps → limit and route — Routes
## 1. Routes — FDI can enter through
| Route | Approval |
| Automatic Route | Does not require prior government approval — subject to applicable conditions; no prior RBI/Government approval; post-facto reporting to RBI |
| Government Route | Requires prior government approval — prior approval of the concerned Ministry via Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIFP, integrated with NSWS since Aug 2022) - defence beyond 74%, multi-brand retail, broadcasting, print media |
- Policy approach: Negative list → FDI up to 100% automatic in most sectors; more than 90% of sectors automatic
