### Articles 29-30 — minority rights and educational institutions
## 1. Cultural and Educational Rights — Articles 29 and 30 (two Articles)
- Article 29: Protects the interests of minorities
- Article 30: Minorities' right to establish and administer educational institutions
## 2. Article 30 - clauses
| Clause | Provision |
| 30(1) | all minorities, religious or linguistic, may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice |
| 30(1A) (44th Amendment, 1978) | on compulsory acquisition of a minority institution's property, the amount fixed must not restrict or abrogate the Art. 30 right |
- 30(1) = the "Charter of Education Rights"; madrasas run under it
- Not absolute: State may regulate standards, but not the minority character
- RTE link: RTE Act's 25% quota does not apply to minority schools (Pramati, 2014)
- Critics: only 8.76% disadvantaged enrolments in minority schools (NCPCR, Sep 2025)
### Right to property — Amendments → year → change
| Stage | Position |
| 1950 | Fundamental Right under Art. 19(1)(f) (acquire, hold, dispose of property) and Art. 31 (no deprivation save by authority of law; acquisition only for public purpose with compensation) |
| 1st Amendment, 1951 | Arts. 31A, 31B and Ninth Schedule inserted to shield land reform laws |
| 4th (1955), 17th (1964), 25th (1971) Amendments | Narrowed compensation; 17th - land under personal cultivation acquired only on paying market value |
| 44th Amendment, 1978 | Deleted Art. 19(1)(f) and Art. 31 from Part III; made it a legal (constitutional) right under Art. 300A, Part XII, Chapter IV |
- FR count: after deletion six Fundamental Rights (originally seven)
- Exam angle: A-R pairs "legal right under 300A in Part XII" + "44th Amendment, 1978".
### Article 300A and LARR Act 2013 — provisions
## 1. Article 300A
- Text: "No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law"
- Available to: all persons (citizens, non-citizens, companies)
- Enforcement: via Art. 226/ordinary suit, not Art. 32
- ⚠ Trap: PYQ G1 2019 (State cannot dispossess except by law) - key given as "Article 300"; correct provision is Art. 300A
## 2. LARR Act, 2013
- LARR Act, 2013: replaced Land Acquisition Act, 1894
- Key provisions:
- Compensation up to 4x market value (rural), 2x (urban)
- Unused land returned after 5 years (May 2026)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:13
### Articles 29-30
- Article 30(1) — right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice for all minorities, religious or linguistic
- State may control standards but may not take away their minority character
- Article 30(1A) (inserted by 44th Amendment) — compulsory acquisition of property of a minority educational institution
- Amount fixed must not limit or cancel the Article 30 right
- Article 350A — teaching in the mother tongue at the primary stage for children of linguistic minority groups
- Six notified religious minorities (NCM): Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis, Jains
- Linguistic minorities are not one of the six notified religious categories
### Who is a minority?
- 'Minority' — used in Articles 29, 30, 350A, 350B but not defined in the Constitution
- NCM Act, 1992 also does not define it
- Unit for deciding a linguistic minority — the State
- Smaller numbers and non-dominant status matter
- Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v. Union of India (2014) — RTE Act's 25 per cent quota does not apply to minority schools
### Right to property: journey
- 17th Amendment (1964) — land under personal cultivation taken only on paying market value
- 44th Amendment, 1978 — deleted Articles 19(1)(f) and 31 from Part III
- Made property a constitutional legal right under Article 300A
### Article 300A & LARR 2013
- Article 300A — no person shall lose his property save by authority of law
- LARR Act, 2013 — replaced the Land Acquisition Act, 1894
- Makes Social Impact Assessment compulsory (a feature, not the reason it replaced the 1894 Act)
- GGR Housing v. State (Andhra Pradesh High Court) — strict statutory due process in land acquisition under Article 300A
### FR or not?
## Legal / statutory rights
- ★ Right to vote — a legal (statutory) right, not a constitutional or fundamental right
- ★ Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2009) — SC held voting is only a statutory/legal right
- Statutory right under Section 62 of the Representation of the People Act
- ★ Right to vote and to be elected — legal rights
- ★ Right to information — a legal right
- Also read into Article 19(1)(a)
- ★ Right to property — a legal right
- Constitutional legal right under Article 300A; available to citizens, non-citizens and companies
- Not a Fundamental Right enforceable under Article 32
## Other classifications
- Right to Work (Article 41) — a Directive Principle, not a Fundamental Right
- Recognised under Article 19(1)(a) though voting is statutory (the 'voting paradox')
- Right to know candidate details, NOTA, secrecy of the ballot
### Exam corner
- ★ 44th Amendment Act, 1978 — deleted right to property from the list of Fundamental Rights (Part III)
- ★ Made it a legal right under Article 300-A in Part XII
- Removed mainly so property rights would not block land reform
- ★ At present, right to property is a legal right
- Fundamental Rights first seven; after removing right to property (Articles 19(1)(f) and 31) — six
### Minorities — meaning, types, Articles and notified minorities
- ★ Article 350-A — every State and local authority to try to provide enough facilities for instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage for children of linguistic minority groups
- ★ Article 30 does not define the term 'minorities'
- ★ All minorities, whether based on religion or language, have the right to establish and administer educational institutions
### Articles 29-30 — minority rights and educational institutions
- ★ Article 30 — all minorities have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice
### Right to property — Amendments → year → change
- ★ Right to Property — not a fundamental right
- ★ Land Acquisition Act, 1894 — made by British rulers; came into effect on 1 March 1894
- ★ Purpose was not land reform, but land acquisition for public institutions, organizations and companies
### Article 300A and LARR Act 2013 — provisions
- ★ Right to property — a legal right available to any person
- ★ First one of the seven FRs under Article 31; one of the most disputed rights
- ★ Removed as a fundamental right by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978
### Fundamental vs constitutional/legal right — classification
- ★ Right to education — a Fundamental right
- ★ Right to vote and right to be elected — legal rights under an Act
- ★ A legal right comes from the ordinary law
- ★ Right to Property — a statutory right
