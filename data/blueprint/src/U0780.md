### Indian Forest Act 1927 → forest categories and sections
## 1. Legal categories of forests
- Reserved forests: The highest restrictions — activities prohibited unless permitted
- Protected forests: More regulated than unclassed forests
- Unclassed forests: Outside the reserved and protected categories
| Category | Protection | Chapter / section |
| Reserved forest | Most strict: felling, grazing and hunting are barred unless expressly permitted. State controls management. | Ch. II, Sec. 3-27 [GK] |
| Village forest | Reserved forest assigned to a village community | Ch. III, Sec. 28 [GK] |
| Protected forest | Activities allowed unless specifically prohibited | Ch. IV, Sec. 29-34 [GK] |
## 2. Sections
| Section | Provision |
| Sec. 26 | Lists acts prohibited in a reserved forest; makes trespass punishable (APPSC 2024 key) |
| Sec. 35 | State may regulate forests that are not government property |
| Sec. 2(7) | definition of "tree" - Bamboo grown outside forest areas removed (2017 amendment) |
## 3. 2017 amendment
- 2017 amendment: Ordinance 2017, Act 2018
- Bamboo on private/non-forest land can be felled and transported without a permit
- Criticism: Seen as hurting forest-dwellers' livelihoods → pushed reform towards community rights
- ⚠ Reserved = barred unless permitted; Protected = allowed unless prohibited
### National Forest Policy 1952 vs 1988 → targets and objectives
## 1. National Forest Policy
| Policy | Features |
| 1952 | Classified forests as national, protected (conserved) and village forests, and tree-lands — ⚠ national parks were not included; one-third of land under forest - 60% in hills, 25% in plains |
| 1988 | Emphasizes ecological stability, forest conservation, people's participation (public and community participation — a major thrust); forest-cover target — one-third (33%) of the country's geographical area; two-thirds (66%) in mountain and hilly areas (Jul 2026) |
| Draft | 2018 |
## 2. NFP 1988 objectives and content
- 1988 objectives: Ecological afforestation, ecological balance, social forestry, biodiversity conservation, soil and water conservation
- 1988 includes: Afforestation and wasteland development; reforestation and replantation; wood substitutes; alternative fuels — ⚠ not promoting wide use of insecticides and pesticides
- Other objectives: Ecological stability; conserving natural heritage; checking soil erosion and desert spread; massive afforestation; fuelwood, fodder and minor forest produce needs of tribal and rural people; Massive people's movement involving women; corridors between protected areas; fuel-efficient chulhas
- Exam angle: the statement "at least two-thirds of the country's land should be under forest" is NOT true (APPSC 2024). Two-thirds applies only to the hills.
- ⚠ 1952 hills = 60%; 1988 hills = two-thirds (66%); national target both ≈ one-third (33%)
### Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 → purpose, structure and sections
## 1. Ownership and hunting
- Section 39(1): Every wild animal is owned by the State Government — an animal hunted in a Sanctuary or National Park declared by the Central Government is owned by the Central Government
- Gharial, Indian wild ass, wild buffalo: Cannot be hunted except as permitted by law
- A protected animal may not be hunted normally — but may be captured or killed when it poses a danger to human life, is diseased, or disabled beyond recovery
## 2. Purpose and sections
- WPA 1972 purpose: Protect wild animals, birds and plants + secure ecological security - not EPA 1986 or FCA 1980
- Structure: 66 sections in 7 chapters
| Section | Provision |
| Sec. 2(37) / Section 2(37) | "wildlife" |
| Sec. 2(1) / Section 2(1) | "animal" = amphibians, birds, mammals, reptiles and their young + eggs of birds and reptiles |
| Section 9 | Hunting of scheduled animals prohibited |
| Section 11 | Hunting allowed: danger to human life or crops |
| Section 12 | Permits for education, research, scientific management, incl. translocation |
| Section 39 | Wild animals, trophies, articles = Government property |
| Sec. 38V(3) | 2006 amendment - Tiger Conservation Plan for every tiger reserve (Aug 2026) |
| Sec. 62 | Centre may declare a wild animal (not in Schedule I) vermin for a specified area and period by notification |
- Mining: Prohibited inside NPs and sanctuaries
- ⚠ Sec. 9 = ban; Sec. 11 = danger exception; Sec. 12 = research/translocation permits; Sec. 39 = Government property
### WPA amendments 2002, 2006 and 2022 → changes
## 1. 2002 amendment
- Conservation Reserves: Government-owned buffers and corridors (e.g. Tsarap Chu, HP; Hesaraghatta, Karnataka)
- Community Reserves: Private or community land, run by a CRMC
- Replaced Indian Board for Wildlife with National Board for Wildlife
## 2. 2022 amendment (Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act 2022)
- In force: April 2023
- Schedules: Cut 6 to 4; separate vermin schedule deleted; rationalised schedules + added invasive alien species
- CITES: Provisions brought into the Act (management and scientific authorities)
- New powers:
- Centre regulates invasive alien species (e.g. African catfish, Lantana camara)
- State Governments may declare conservation reserves
- A Chief Wildlife Warden in every State
- Voluntary surrender of captive animals/products without compensation
- Penalties raised:
- Specially protected animals: minimum fine Rs 25,000 (from Rs 10,000)
- Some notes say "up to Rs 25 lakh / Rs 1 crore"
- ⚠ Conservation Reserve = Government land; Community Reserve = private/community land

### Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 / Van Adhiniyam → provisions and 2023 amendment
## 1. Original Act
- Sec. 2: Prior Central approval needed to de-reserve a forest, use forest land for non-forest purpose, or clear trees
## 2. 2023 amendment
- Renamed: Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980 (w.e.f. 29 Dec 2023)
- Coverage - only two kinds of land:
- Land notified as forest under IFA 1927 or other law
- Land recorded as forest in government records on or after 25 Oct 1980
- Excluded: land converted to non-forest use before 12 Dec 1996
- Exemptions from Central approval:
- Strategic linear projects within 100 km of the IB, LoC or LAC
- Eco-tourism facilities; zoos and safaris under WPA; minor infrastructure
- Compensatory afforestation (CA): Non-forest land of equal area; else twice the area of degraded forest land + Net Present Value (NPV) paid
- Van (S.E.S.) Amendment Rules 2025: Assisted natural regeneration + plantation on leased forest land = "forestry activities"; CA and NPV do not apply (Apr 2026)
## 3. Effect on tribal rights
- FRA: Amendment silent on FRA; no Gram Sabha consent mandated for new exemptions
- ⚠ In force 25 Oct 1980; renamed w.e.f. 29 Dec 2023; cut-off for converted land 12 Dec 1996
### Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act 2023 → provisions
- Decriminalised: Offences → civil penalties
- Exemptions from benefit-sharing: Registered AYUSH practitioners + cultivated medicinal plants (BMC-certified); encourages cultivation
- NBA approval time: 180 to 90 days (IPR applications stay at 180)
- Foreign nationals and foreign-incorporated entities: Still need prior NBA approval (26 Jun 2026)
- Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS):
- Rules 2024: NBA passes 85-90% of proceeds to SBBs
- ABS Regulations, April 2025: 25-40% to identifiable source institutions; 60-75% to local communities via SBBs/UTBCs
