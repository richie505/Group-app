### AP Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill 2026 — Amaravati declared capital
## 1. News
- Lok Sabha passed the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — formally declares Amaravati as capital of AP
- Change: "there shall be a new capital" → "Amaravati shall be the new capital"
- Amaravati defined as the area notified under the AP Capital Region Development Authority (APCRDA) Act, 2014
- Retrospective effect: from 2 June 2024
- AP Legislative Assembly resolution requesting the amendment: 28 March 2026 — reflects cooperative federalism
## 2. Background
- AP Reorganisation Act, 2014 — enacted under Article 3; created Telangana
- Section 5: Hyderabad = common capital for not more than 10 years from 2 June 2014 (i.e., till 2024)
- ⚠ The 2014 Act did not name Amaravati or any city as capital — only said a new capital would be established
## 3. Parliament passage and history
- Parliament passed the Bill — Amaravati = sole and permanent capital; broad support except YSR Congress Party (had pushed three-capital model)
- 2019: then govt proposed three capitals (executive, legislative, judicial functions split)
- 2022: AP High Court ruled the State lacked legislative competence to shift the capital
- Amendment is to Section 5 of the 2014 Act
## 4. Amaravati capital project
- Planned area: ~217 sq. km; developed via Land Pooling Scheme (farmers get developed plots + compensation)
- Governance framework: APCRDA hired a consultant (Mar-Apr 2026), in line with the 73rd/74th Amendments
## 5. Presidential assent and constitutional angle
- President Droupadi Murmu granted assent → AP Reorganisation (Amendment) Act, 2026 — inserts "at Amaravati" in Section 5
- Three-capital model (post-2019): Visakhapatnam (executive), Amaravati (legislative), Kurnool (judicial) — via AP Decentralisation and Inclusive Development of All Regions Act, 2020; ⚠ repealed in 2021, judicial capital never operationalised
- 2022 HC: State cannot arbitrarily change capital — legitimate expectation of Land Pooling farmers; rule of law; judicial review under Articles 32 & 226
- ⚠ The Constitution does not specify how a State capital is determined — statutory law fills the gap
- APCRDA Act, 2014: special planning body — Town Planning Schemes, Betterment Charges; overriding powers over Gram Panchayats in capital region
## 6. Related static GK
- Article 169: create/abolish Legislative Council — Assembly resolution by special majority
- Legislative Council: permanent house; one-third members retire every two years
- Article 174: Governor dissolves Assembly on advice of Council of Ministers headed by CM
- AP Reorganisation Act 2014: Governor of common capital (Hyderabad) had special responsibility for law and order, internal security; ⚠ institutions of national importance stay with the Union
## 7. Gazette notification
- Passed by Parliament on April 1 and 2, 2026; Presidential assent under Article 111; in force after Gazette notification by Ministry of Law; effect from June 2, 2024
- Section 5(2) of 2014 Act amended; future changes to capital area need Parliamentary approval
- AP: formed 1956, reorganised 2014; Governor S. Abdul Nazeer

- Issued by: Director of Animal Husbandry, Andhra Pradesh — seasonal guidelines against heat stress in livestock
## 2. Key advisory points
- Clean drinking water all day; community water troughs in villages
- 30–50 grams of mineral supplements daily; chopped fodder in cooler hours
- Grazing only morning and evening; early morning milking
- Sheds in east–west orientation; fans and sprinklers for crossbred cattle; wet gunny bags around sheds
## 3. Exam angle
- ⚠ Crossbred and high-yield dairy animals are more vulnerable to heat stress than indigenous breeds
- India has the largest cattle population in the world (Livestock Census)

### AP draft Population Management Policy 2026
## 1. News
- CM N. Chandrababu Naidu introduced a draft Population Management Policy — ⚠ first of its kind in India; pro-natalist reversal of family-planning approach
- One-month public consultation; implementation from April 1, 2026
- Objective: raise AP's TFR from 1.5 to replacement level 2.1
## 2. Why needed
- TFR fell from 3.0 (1993) to ~1.5 now
- Ageing: ~23% of population above 60 years by 2047
- Delimitation: Lok Sabha seats based on population — southern states fear losing seats
## 3. Key features
| Measure | Detail |
| Birth incentive | ₹25,000 one-time at birth of second or third child |
| Poshana–Shiksha–Suraksha package (third child) | ₹1,000/month for first 5 years + free education up to 18 years |
| Maternity leave | 12 months for third child |
| Paternity leave | 2 months |
- Female LFPR target: from ~31% to nearly 59%
## 4. Five components (Assembly draft)
| Component | Focus |
| Matrutva | Maternal health |
| Shakti | Women's workforce participation |
| Kshema | Elderly care |
| Naipunyam | Skills, healthcare workers |
| Sanjeevani | Digital public health |
## 5. More measures and data
- Free education till 18 for second and third children in govt institutions; subsidised IVF through PPP
- C-section births: cut from 67.5% to below 40%; train 10,000 healthcare assistants annually
- TFR: AP 2.2 (2003) → 1.5 (2023); India 1.9; Tamil Nadu 1.3, Karnataka 1.5, Kerala 1.5; Bihar 2.8, UP 2.6
- Median age: AP 32.5 years vs India 28.4; 60+ population ~10% now
- ⚠ Shift from "population control" to "population management"
### AP Legislative Assembly passes three Bills (March 4, 2026)
## 1. Public Services (Second Amendment) Bill, 2025
- Full name: AP (Regulation of Appointments to Public Services and Rationalisation of Staff Pattern and Pay Structure) (Second Amendment) Bill, 2025
- Purpose: Junior Assistant job on compassionate grounds to the son of Thota Chandraiah, victim of political violence in Palnadu district
- Change: amends Section 4(2) of the 1994 Act — appointment by special resolution of the Council of Ministers, bypassing APPSC/selection committee/employment exchange
- Governor's assent: under Article 200 — assent, withhold, return (if not a Money Bill), or reserve for President
- ⚠ Article 16 (equality in public employment) — SC treats compassionate appointments as a valid exception
## 2. AP Fire Service (Amendment) Bill, 2026
- Modernises the 26-year-old Fire Service Act; aligns with Centre's Model Bill for Fire & Emergency Service, 2019
- NOC replaced by Fire Safety Certificate (FSC); fire department becomes an "Emergency Service"
- Super High-Rise Buildings in Amaravati; standards per National Building Code (NBC) 2025
- New post: Fire Safety Supervisor
| Penalty | Old | New |
| Standard fine | ₹500 | ₹10,000 |
| Daily default fine | ₹100/day | ₹1,000/day |
- Power to seize buildings violating norms
## 3. AP Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Bill, 2026
- Decriminalises minor offences — jail replaced by monetary penalties; inspired by Centre's Jan Vishwas Act, 2023
- Amends 12+ State Acts — Municipalities Act, CRDA Act, Excise Act, Prohibition Act, Land Reforms Act, Industrial Corridor Development Act, Fire Service Act
- Repeals obsolete drainage cess acts of 1985 and 1986; motto "Minimum Government, Maximum Governance"
