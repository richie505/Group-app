### Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna → categories, purpose and basis
## 1. CPSE categories
- CPSEs are classified as Miniratna, Navratna and Maharatna
- Categorisation is based on factors such as turnover, net worth and net profit
- Basis: Financial performance → autonomy
- Purpose: "Ratna" categories give high-performing CPSEs more financial + operational autonomy
- Invest, form JVs, compete globally without layers of approval
- Granting body: Department of Public Enterprises (DPE)
## 2. Maharatna
- A status or category given to selected Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) — introduced (originated) in 2010
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the scheme came in 2009, first conferred 2010
- Administered by the Department of Public Enterprises (DPE)
- Purpose: Greater autonomy, greater financial and managerial powers to large, high-performing CPSEs — still greater autonomy and power to expand
- Introduced to give greater autonomy to selected Navratna CPSEs and enable them to compete globally
- Factors in categorisation: Turnover, net worth, net profit

- Industrial growth — about 4% in 1965-80, vs 7-8% in 1951-65 and the 1980s
- Rourkela (Odisha, 1 MT) — first integrated public-sector steel plant
- Textiles — largest employer after agriculture; about 4.5 crore direct, 10 crore+ indirect
- ONGC — traditionally the top profit-making CPSE
- Public Enterprises Survey — CPSEs with over 50% GoI equity; excludes departmental undertakings, banks, insurers
## Ratna categories
| Category | Started | Investment without Govt approval |
| Maharatna | 2010 | ₹5,000 crore in one project |
| Navratna | 1997 (with 9 CPSEs) | ₹1,000 crore or 15% of net worth |
| Miniratna-I | — | ₹500 crore |
| Miniratna-II | — | ₹300 crore |
- Maharatna — must already be a Navratna; 3-year averages: turnover > ₹25,000 cr, net worth > ₹15,000 cr, net profit > ₹5,000 cr
- Aim — more autonomy and financial/managerial powers for large CPSEs to compete globally
- Run by Department of Public Enterprises
- Gives more autonomy than Navratna (start years do not explain that)
- Navratna — needs Miniratna-I and Schedule 'A' status and score 60+ on six parameters
- All ratnas — no budgetary support, no Government guarantee, no default on Govt loans
- BHEL became Maharatna in 2013 (not 2010)
### CPSEs & institutions
- ★ HAL — aircraft; HQ Bengaluru; set up as Hindustan Aircraft Ltd on 23 December 1940 by Walchand Hirachand
- CPSE — Central Government or other CPSEs hold 51% or more equity
- CPSEs — 5 at the start of the First Plan, 244 by 1990
- FY 2022–25: rising CPSE dividends boosted non-tax revenue; disinvestment receipts fell
- DPE — CPSEs in four schedules; publishes Public Enterprises Survey; monitors MoUs; now under Ministry of Finance (earlier Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises)
- Arjun Sengupta Committee (1984) — operational and financial autonomy → MoU system
- MoU — applies to all CPSEs incl. subsidiaries; via a High Power Committee
- PESB — selects board-level executives of CPSEs
- As on 1 April 2025 — 14 Maharatna, 26 Navratna
- HAL — 14th Maharatna, October 2024; Oil India 13th (2023); GAIL, BHEL since 2013; Power Grid, PFC, REC also Maharatnas
- Airports Authority of India — Miniratna Category-I; BHEL, IOC, HAL Maharatna
- BEL, MTNL, NALCO — Navratna (BEL is not a Maharatna)
### Exam corner
- ★ Textile industry — maximum workers; 2nd largest employer after agriculture (about 45 million direct, 60 million indirect)
- Founding order: Bombay Dyeing (1879) → Lakshmi Mills (1910) → Raymond (1925) → Grasim (1947)
- Core industries — coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement, electricity (+ iron ore, 2022-23); transport is NOT one

- Ultra-Mega project — investment above ₹5,001 crore
