### Local revenue sources — urban local bodies and panchayats — Urban local bodies
## 1. Urban local bodies
- Property tax: Single most important own-source revenue of urban local bodies
- Chronic under-assessment and under-collection = standard answer to "why are municipalities weak"
- Estimating potential: Economic Survey 2016-17 measured actual vs potential property tax of Bengaluru (and Jaipur) using satellite imagery (APPSC-G1 2017 key)
| Source (ULBs) | Examples |
| Tax revenue | Property tax (most important), professional tax, advertisement, entertainment, water, lighting, pilgrim, market taxes; tolls on new bridges; library/education cesses |
| Non-tax revenue | Rent, fees and fines, royalties, interest, user charges (water, sanitation, sewerage) |
| Grants | Central and State grants for schemes and reforms |
| Devolution | State funds to municipalities on SFC recommendation |
| Loans | From State or financial institutions for capital works; municipal bonds only in a few large cities |
- Octroi: A traditional levy (Block03 Municipal Revenue)
- Reform levers:
- SVAMITVA drone maps → panchayat property-tax baseline
- AMRUT 2.0 incentivises property tax reform + municipal bonds
### Local revenue sources — urban local bodies and panchayats — Panchayats
## 1. Panchayats
- Taxing powers: Gram panchayats levy only State-authorised taxes
- e.g. tax on local fairs, house/property tax, fees and tolls
- ⚠ Trap: Sales tax and land revenue are State levies (UPPSC 2018 key)
- Sources of revenue (UPPSC 2007 key):
- Finance Commission local body grants
- Assistance from Central Cooperative Banks
- Allocations under Centrally Sponsored Schemes
- Allocations from the State Finance Commission
- ⚠ Trap: NABARD is not a direct source
- Ground reality: Gram sabhas spend only 4% of time on revenue generation vs 13% on local issues; panchayats constrained from taxing → grant-dependent
### Central FC local-body grants — size, tied vs untied, conditions
## 1. Size of grants
- 16th FC (2026-31): ₹7,91,493 crore for local bodies
- Urban share up 36% → 45% (urbanisation projected at 41% by 2031)
## 2. Components - tied vs untied (16th FC)
| Component (16th FC) | Share | Tied / untied |
| Basic grant | 80% | 50% tied (sanitation, waste, water), 50% untied |
| Performance grant | 20% | 100% untied; split equally between local and State performance; needs better own-source revenue |
| Special infrastructure (ULB) | ₹56,100 crore | Tied to wastewater management, cities of 10-40 lakh (Census 2011) |
| Urbanisation Premium | ₹10,000 crore | One-time, for merging peri-urban villages + adopting a Rural-to-Urban Transition Policy |
- ⚠ Basic 80% (half tied) vs performance 20% (fully untied)
## 3. Entry conditions
- 16th FC conditions:
- Properly constituted (elected) local bodies
- Audited accounts published online
- Timely constitution of SFCs
- Earlier conditions: ULBs had to publish audited accounts + notify baseline property tax rates
- Tied grants: Steered panchayats to water and sanitation
- Exam angle: tied vs untied percentages; conditions for release.
### Issues — grant dependence, accounting gaps, delayed SFCs; AP
## 1. Challenges
| Factor | Effect |
| Grant dependence | Intergovernmental transfers (IGTs) ≈ 40% of ULB revenue (IIHS study, 80 ULBs, 2012-17); India's IGTs ≈ 0.5% of GDP vs global norm 2-5% |
| Accounting gaps | No accrual accounting + weak balance sheets → no bond-market access |
## 2. AP local body finances
- Expired terms: AP ULBs on 15 March 2026; panchayats on 4 April 2026 → run by Special Officers
- Grant risk: FC grants need elected bodies, audited accounts + SFC compliance → delay puts grants at risk
- Not in your sources: AP-specific figures for devolution of State taxes to panchayats.
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: 16th FC tied local body grants to conditions (elected bodies, accounts, SFCs); critics call it a re-engineering of fiscal federalism
- AP angle: AP's local bodies under Special Officers → 16th FC grants depend on holding elections
