### M.N. Srinivas → concepts and statements
- Sanskritisation: Coined by M.N. Srinivas (APPSC 2019) in Religion and Society among the Coorgs of South India (1952); developed in Social Change in Modern India
- Earlier name: Srinivas first called it "Brahminisation"
- Reference groups: Brahmins + Kshatriya, Vaishya and local dominant caste (a Srinivas concept)
- Westernisation: Changes from 150+ years of British rule
- Levels: technology, institutions, ideology and values
- Ethically neutral (Srinivas's preference) [GK]
- Family: Urbanisation and education change authority patterns but do not end the joint family (Srinivas)
- Secularisation: less "religious" meaning of old practices, differentiation, rationalisation
- Srinivas's set: Secularisation + Sanskritisation + Westernisation + Dominant Caste
- ⚠ "Brahminisation" = Srinivas's earlier name for Sanskritisation

### Change terms → meaning — cultural lag, Sanskritisation, secularisation
| Term | Meaning |
| Cultural lag | Delay in adjustment to change: Material culture (technology) changes faster than non-material culture (beliefs, norms), which lags behind |
| Sanskritisation | A low caste or tribe adopts the customs, rituals and way of life of a higher, often twice-born, caste to claim higher rank |
| De-sanskritisation | Groups reject Sanskritic norms, assert own identity (e.g. non-Brahmin/Dravidian movement) |
| Secularisation (sociological) | Decline of religion's hold on social life |
