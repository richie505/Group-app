- Sec 29A: wilful defaulters cannot bid

### Terms → meaning: stress indicators and causes of NPAs
| Term | Meaning |
| Interest Coverage Ratio | EBIT / interest payable |
| Twin Balance Sheet problem | Over-leveraged corporates + capital-starved banks → stalls private capex, squeezes MSME credit |
| Evergreening | Fresh loans to repay old ones → NPA stays hidden; the AQR exposed this |
| PCR | Provisions / gross NPA; higher PCR → more resilient bank |
- Interest Coverage Ratio: Below 1 → firm cannot service its interest
- Lenders use it to judge a borrower's present and emerging risk
- Other causes: Loan frauds and diversion of funds, weak credit appraisal, slow recovery machinery, corporate over-leverage

### Recovery forums — Lok Adalats and Debt Recovery Tribunals
## 1. Lok Adalats for small NPAs
- Status: Statutory forums under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987
- Feature: Quick, fee-free settlement of small loans by compromise [GK]
- National Lok Adalat (13 Sep 2026): DRT Visakhapatnam bench settled 13 cases worth ₹46.11 crore
## 2. Debt Recovery Tribunals
- Law: Recovery tribunals under the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and FIs Act, 1993 (RDDBFI)[GK]; also used under SARFAESI
- Structure: 39 DRTs and 5 DRATs (Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunals)
- Threshold: Debts above ₹20 lakh
- e-DRT: Launched 1 Oct 2018; e-filing mandatory from 1 Feb 2023
- ⚠ 1987 = Legal Services Authorities Act (Lok Adalats); 1993 = RDDBFI (DRTs); 2002 = SARFAESI
### SARFAESI Act 2002 — sections → provisions, ARCs
- Full name: Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002
- Basis: Narasimham Committee
- Core feature: Banks enforce security on NPAs without court intervention (recovery without court)
| Section | Provision |
| Sec 13(2) | Lenders holding 60% of dues issue a 60-day demand notice |
| Sec 13(8) | Borrower can redeem by paying full dues only until the auction notice is published |
| Sec 17 | Appeal to the DRT within 45 days |
- ARCs: Buy NPAs/bad loans from banks at a discount against security receipts; registered with and regulated by RBI under SARFAESI [GK]
- AP angle: Division bench of the AP High Court clarified Sec 13(8) redemption, auction-stay rules and auction protocols (May 2026)
- ⚠ 60% of lenders + 60-day notice; appeal within 45 days
### Schemes → restructuring, CDR 2001 to Prudential Framework 2019
| Mechanism | Year | Feature |
| Corporate Debt Restructuring (CDR) | 2001 [GK] | Joint restructuring outside court of large multi-bank loans [GK] |
| 5/25 scheme | 2014 [GK] | Infrastructure/core loans amortised over up to 25 years, refinanced every 5 years |
| Strategic Debt Restructuring (SDR) | 2015 [GK] | Lenders convert debt to a majority (51%+) equity stake and change the management |
| S4A - Scheme for Sustainable Structuring of Stressed Assets | 2016 [GK] | RBI scheme to rework the financial structure of stressed firms: split debt into sustainable and unsustainable parts (UPSC 2017 key; UPSC notes) |
| Project Sashakt (Sunil Mehta Committee) - Inter-Creditor Agreement | 2018 [GK] | Lenders agree on faster resolution of stressed assets/consortium loans (UPSC 2019 key); lead bank drives the plan, which binds all once 66% of lenders approve [GK] |
| Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets | 7 June 2019 [GK] | 30-day review after default; ICA mandatory; resolution within 180 days |
### IBC 2016 — definitions, enactment and FIRSTs
## 1. Definitions
| Term | Meaning |
| Insolvency | The state of being unable to pay debts as they fall due (financial state) |
| Bankruptcy | The legal declaration/process for an insolvent individual or firm |
## 2. Enactment - IBC 2016 as structural reform
- Enactment: Assent May 2016; enacted 28 May 2016 on the T.K. Viswanathan Committee (Bankruptcy Law Reforms Committee, 2015)
- PYQ: both statements (Sept assent; 13-member IBBI) were wrong
- Replaced: Scattered laws — SICA 1985/BIFR + scattered Companies Act provisions
- BIFR dissolved 1 Dec 2016; cases sent to NCLT/NCLAT
- IBC 2016: Debtor-in-possession to creditor-in-control; single, time-bound, creditor-in-control process (structural reform)
### Institutions under IBC — NCLT, NCLAT, DRT, IBBI
## NCLT/NCLAT - parent Act, adjudicating authority under IBC, appeal route
| Institution | Role |
| NCLT (Sec 408, Companies Act 2013) | Adjudicating authority for companies and LLPs (APPSC GS 2019 key) and personal guarantors of corporate debtors |
| NCLAT (Sec 410) | Appeals from NCLT (from 1 Jun 2016), IBBI and CCI orders |
| DRT | Adjudicating authority for individuals and partnership firms (LENS Jun 2026); appeals to DRAT [GK] |
| IBBI (2016) | Apex statutory regulator of insolvency professionals, IP agencies, information utilities, registered valuers; frames CIRP/liquidation regulations; 10 members |
- NCLT and NCLAT: Constituted 1 June 2016 (Companies Act 2013, Sec 408/410)
- NCLT replaced the Company Law Board, BIFR and AAIFR
- NCLT: 16 benches, incl. Amaravati and Hyderabad
- NCLAT: Principal Bench New Delhi + a bench at Chennai
- NCLAT hears IBC appeals under Sec 61; further appeal to the Supreme Court
- Related: IBBI (Secs 202 and 211); the CCI from 26 May 2017; the NFRA from 7 May 2018
- ⚠ NCLT = companies and LLPs; DRT = individuals and partnership firms
### IBC provisions — CIRP, timelines, liquidation, pre-packs and the 2026 amendment — CIRP
## 1. CIRP
- Trigger: Financial or operational creditors, on a default of at least ₹1 crore (Jun 2026)
- Committee of Creditors: Financial creditors/lenders vote on resolution plans
- Timeline:
- 180 days (+ 90 days extension); outer limit 330 days incl. litigation (2019 amendment)
- In practice: 713 days on average; 853 days for cases closed in FY25 (ES 2025-26)
- Bidding bar: Wilful defaulters/NPA promoters cannot bid for their own firms (Sec. 29A) — Barred from bidding under IBC (May 2026)
- Liquidation waterfall: Resolution costs, workmen's dues, secured creditors rank above government dues (29 May 2026)
- Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP): For MSMEs, 2021, 120-day limit; only 14 admissions in 4 years (ES 2025-26)
### IBC provisions — CIRP, timelines, liquidation, pre-packs and the 2026 amendment — IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026
## 1. IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026
- Status: IBC Amendment Act 2026 Passed by Lok Sabha (31 Mar 2026; 15 Jun 2026)
- Key changes:
- NCLT must admit or reject a CIRP plea within 14 days; rule on plans within 30 days
- Liquidation cut to 180 + 90 days
- New Creditor-Initiated Insolvency Resolution Process (CIIRP): 150 days, needs 51% by value of notified financial creditors (Secs 54C-54P); hybrid of debtor-in-possession + creditor-in-control
- "may" → "shall" in Sec 7(5)(a), overturning Vidarbha Industries
- Clean-slate protection + cross-border insolvency framework
- Acts in news - provisions, amending Acts: Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025: See judgments
- ⚠ Timelines: CIRP 180 + 90 (outer 330); PPIRP 120; CIIRP 150; admission 14 days
### NARCL and IDRCL — bad bank structure, ownership, funding
- NARCL: India's "bad bank", announced in Budget 2021-22
- Aggregates large stressed loans above ₹500 crore
- Target: about ₹2 lakh crore of fully or partly provided assets, in phases
- IDRCL: Asset-management company that resolves them, incl. by sale to AIFs
| Feature | Fact |
| Ownership | PSBs hold majority in NARCL; IDRCL is majority private |
| Funding | 15% cash + 85% in Security Receipts |
| Govt guarantee | SRs up to ₹30,600 crore for 5 years or until settlement |
- Progress (30 Jun 2025):
- 27 accounts acquired (₹1,57,215 crore exposure)
- Resolution applicant in 2 cases
- ⚠ NARCL aggregates; IDRCL resolves
### Prompt Corrective Action framework — full form, triggers, restrictions
## 1. Basics
- Full form: PCA = Prompt Corrective Action (APPSC-GS 2019 key)
- Introduced: By RBI, introduced 2002, to intervene in banks whose financial health is failing; weak banks placed under watch + restricted until capital and asset quality recover
- Exam angle: APPSC GS 2019 asked the expansion "PCA = Prompt Corrective Action".
## 2. Triggers
- Current triggers (revised Nov 2021, effective 1 Jan 2022): capital, asset quality, leverage (table below)
| Trigger parameter | Indicator |
| Capital | CRAR / Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) [GK] |
| Asset quality | Net NPA ratio[GK] |
| Leverage | Tier 1 leverage ratio [GK] |
- 2017 framework (older notes):
- Threshold 1: CRAR between 7.75% and 10.25%
- Threshold 2: CRAR between 6.25% and 7.75%
- Threshold 3: CET1 below 3.625%
- Net NPA 6-9% = threshold 1; 12% or more = threshold 3
## 3. Restrictions
- Curbs: Lending, branch expansion, risky exposures, growth, dividends, management pay — until capital and asset quality improve
- RBI powers: Order a capital infusion, change of management, or a merger

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:87
### Recovery channels
## Tribunals and forums
- Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs) — set up under the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 1993; also used under SARFAESI
- DRTs and DRATs handle debts above ₹20 lakh
- Lok Adalats — statutory forums under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987
- NCLT replaced the Company Law Board, BIFR and AAIFR (not the Securities Appellate Tribunal)
- NCLT — decides corporate insolvency under IBC 2016; DRT for individuals and partnership firms
## How they differ
- SARFAESI — secured lenders recover without court intervention
- IBC — joint, NCLT-led resolution
- DRTs — tribunal-based recovery
- FY 2024 recoveries: IBC 48% (largest), SARFAESI 32%, DRTs 17%, Lok Adalats 3%
### SARFAESI Act 2002
## SARFAESI
- SARFAESI Act 2002 — based on the Narasimham Committee
- Section 13(2) — demand notice of 60 days
- Section 13(8) — borrower can recover the asset only by paying the full dues (not 25%)
- Section 17 — appeal to the DRT within 45 days
- Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs) — buy NPAs, usually at a discount; regulated by the RBI (not SEBI)
## Related points
- ICR below 1 — firm cannot service its interest from earnings (ICR = EBIT ÷ interest payable)
- NCLAT hears appeals from NCLT, IBBI, CCI (from 26 May 2017) and NFRA (from 7 May 2018)
- IBC waterfall: resolution costs, workmen's dues and secured creditors' claims come above government dues
- PCA: the RBI may limit lending, branch growth, dividends and pay, and order capital infusion, a management change or a merger
- Andhra Pradesh: NCLT bench at Amaravati; DRT bench at Visakhapatnam

### IBC 2016 basics
## Background
- IBC 2016 — based on the T.K. Viswanathan (Bankruptcy Law Reforms) Committee, 2015
- IBC shifted control from debtor-in-possession to creditor-in-control
- Section 29A — stops wilful defaulters and promoters of NPAs from making resolution bids
## CIRP thresholds and timelines
- Minimum default to start CIRP — Rs 1 crore; started by financial or operational creditors
- CIRP normally 180 days with extension; outer limit 330 days including litigation
- Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP) for MSMEs — 2021, 120-day limit
- IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026: liquidation cut to 180 + 90 days; adds hybrid Creditor-Initiated Insolvency Resolution Process (Secs 54C–54P)
## Outcomes and ratios
- Deterrent effect: settlement before admission of 30,310 cases worth ₹13.78 lakh crore
- Higher PCR (provisions ÷ gross NPA) → bank is more resilient
- March 2026 SCB GNPA of 1.93% — a decadal low
### IBC institutions
- NCLT — Section 408, Companies Act 2013
- NCLAT — Section 410, Companies Act 2013; sits at New Delhi and Chennai; hears appeals from NCLT under Section 61 of the IBC
- IBBI regulates insolvency professionals, insolvency professional agencies, information utilities and registered valuers
### Bad bank & 2026 amendment
## NPA decline
- Reasons for the fall in NPAs: 4R strategy, higher provisioning, IBC and NARCL
## IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026
- NCLT must admit or reject a CIRP plea within 14 days
- NCLT must rule on resolution plans within 30 days
- 'may' → 'shall' in Section 7(5)(a): NCLT must admit a financial creditor's plea once default is proved; overturned the Vidarbha Industries ruling (which had read 'may' as NCLT choice)
## NARCL ('bad bank')
- NARCL (National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited) — announced in Budget 2021–22
- Pays 15% in cash, 85% in Security Receipts; Government guarantee of Rs 30,600 crore for 5 years

## Related

### Prompt Corrective Action
- Evergreening — fresh loan used to repay an old loan so the NPA stays hidden
- PCA — Prompt Corrective Action; RBI framework to step in when banks' financial health weakens
- PCA started by the RBI in 2002
- PCA indicator: asset quality → net NPA ratio; Tier-1 leverage ratio → measures leverage
### Exam corner

- ★ Inter-Creditor Agreement (ICA) — signed July 2018 by 33 banks/FIs; faster resolution of stressed assets of ₹50 crore or more under consortium lending
