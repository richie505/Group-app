### Balance of Payments — definition, BoP vs BoT
## 1. Definition
- Balance of Payments: A systematic and complete record of all economic transactions between the residents of a country and the rest of the world during a specified period — normally one year
- Has a current account and a capital account (PYQ 2025; APPSC-G2 2025 key)
- IMF vs RBI:
- IMF's BPM6 = three accounts (current, capital, financial)
- RBI combines the last two as "Capital Account"
## 2. BoP vs BoT
| Basis | Balance of Trade | Balance of Payments |
| Coverage | Goods only — visible trade; records only goods | All international economic transactions — goods, services, transfers and capital transactions |
| Formula / scope | Exports of goods minus imports of goods (merchandise only) | The broader external account |
| Place | Part of the Current Account | Current Account and Capital/financial Account transactions |
- BoT surplus: Goods exports greater than goods imports; BoT deficit: goods imports greater than goods exports
- ⚠ BoT = goods only; current account balance = goods and services + transfers
### Balance of Payments — current and capital account, recording rules
## 1. Current Account
- Includes goods, services, income and transfers
- Visible trade: Exports and imports of goods
- Invisible transactions: Services, transfer payments and net income
- Invisibles = services + primary income + transfers; services = non-factor (shipping, banking, tourism, software) + factor income
- Private remittances: Included in the Current Account (Current Account → Invisibles → Private Transfers)
- Current Account Deficit: Payments exceed receipts — from goods, services, transfers and net income
## 2. Capital Account
- Cross-border capital movements — foreign loans, Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), portfolio investment, international purchases and sales of assets
| Current account | Capital (and financial) account |
| Merchandise exports and imports (visible trade; trade balance) | FDI, FPI/FII, ADRs/GDRs |
| Invisibles: services - software, travel, transport, banking, insurance | ECB (external commercial borrowings), external assistance |
| Invisibles: primary income - interest, dividends, profits, wages | NRI deposits (e.g., FCNR(B)), banking capital |
| Secondary income: remittances, gifts, grants | Short-term trade credit; reserve changes (change in forex reserves) |
## 3. Recording rules
- Capital account entries:
- Purchase of foreign assets = debit
- Sale of Indian assets to foreigners = credit
### Balance of Payments — identities & formulas
## 1. Identities & formulas
- Identity: Current Account + Capital Account + Financial Account + Net Errors & Omissions = 0
- BoP always balances in accounting terms; surplus in one account = deficit in another
- Current account balance: (exports - imports of goods and services) + net primary income + net secondary income
- Balance of current account: Exports minus imports of goods and services + net unilateral transfers (APPSC-GS 2019 key)
- Balance of Trade (BoT): Merchandise exports - merchandise imports only
- BoT = part of the current account; BoP = the whole
## 2. Financing & overall balance → terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Financing a CAD | A CAD must be financed by a capital account surplus (net inflow) or by drawing down reserves; monetary authority = ultimate financier of any BoP deficit |
| Autonomous transactions | Happen for their own sake |
| Accommodating transactions | Fill the gap (reserve changes, official borrowing) |
| Overall BoP | Positive → adds to reserves; negative → draws them down; reserves run short → BoP crisis |

### BoP correction → tools, IMF role, committee threshold — Expenditure-switching vs expenditure-reducing
## 1. Expenditure-switching vs expenditure-reducing
| Expenditure-switching | Expenditure-reducing / others |
| Devaluation/depreciation / exchange depreciation | Tight fiscal policy / fiscal restraint: cut government spending, raise taxes |
| Import tariffs/duties, import quotas, import substitution | Tight monetary policy (deflation): higher interest rates |
| Export incentives, export promotion, tourist incentives | Foreign loans, IMF assistance, encouraging FDI; exchange control |
## 2. Correction tools
- Not a correction measure: Increasing government spending on domestic projects is NOT a correction tool - raises demand and imports (PYQ 2025; APPSC-G2 2025 key)
- Tools used: Devaluation, higher tariffs, attracting FDI
- Devaluation: Classic expenditure-switching policy - home goods become cheaper relative to foreign goods
### BoP correction → IMF role, functions and facilities
## 1. IMF functions
- Monitors global economies and member economies
- Provides financial assistance to members facing balance of payments difficulties
- Last resort: If reserves cannot cover the deficit → IMF as the last resort; IMF finance is the last resort for a BoP crisis and lends to correct BoP disequilibrium
- India: Drew on the IMF in the 1991 crisis
- Provides practical and technical assistance
## 2. IMF facilities
- Reserve tranche: A member's unconditional drawing right on IMF resources — arises from a member's quota subscription and subsequent changes
- Reserve Tranche Position: A reserve position with the IMF — a member's unconditional drawing position; historically called the gold tranche
- Rapid Financing Instrument: Rapid financial assistance through the IMF for urgent balance of payments needs — available to IMF members
- Rapid Credit Facility: Concessional assistance through the IMF for urgent balance of payments needs — available to low-income countries
- Lending windows: Stand-By Arrangement + Extended Fund Facility
- Reserve components: SDRs + Reserve Tranche Position with the IMF, besides FCA and gold
### BoP correction → committee threshold and the May 2026 episode
## 1. Committee → sustainable CAD threshold
- High Level Committee on Balance of Payments (C. Rangarajan, 1991-93):
- Sustainable CAD = about 1.6% of GDP
- Recommended a market-determined exchange rate
## 2. May 2026 episode
- PM's appeal: Cut import-linked consumption (gold, fuel) as the Iran conflict raised fuel and fertiliser prices
- + gold import duty hike = expenditure-switching steps
### Convertibility and Acts → year: FERA to FEMA
## 1. Convertibility → meaning
- Current Account Convertibility: Freedom to convert domestic currency into foreign currency — for current international transactions (trade, income and transfers)
- Capital Account Convertibility: Freedom to conduct investment and financial-asset transactions without capital controls (FDI, portfolio investment, borrowing)
- Steps:
- LERMS (1992-93 Budget, 40% official / 60% market)
- → Unified rate 1 March 1993
- → Full current account convertibility in August 1994 by accepting Article VIII of the IMF
- Sterling delink: Rupee delinked from sterling in September 1975
## 2. FERA to FEMA
| Feature | FERA 1973 | FEMA 1999 |
| Nature | Criminal law | Civil law |
| Approach | Control of outflow | Management and regulation |
| Character | Restrictive | Liberal |
- Timeline:
- FEMA enacted 1999
- ⚠ FEMA enacted 1999 but in force 1 June 2000
