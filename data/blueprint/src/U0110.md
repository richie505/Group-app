### Sayyid and Lodi dynasties → period, founder, succession
## 1. Sayyid dynasty (1414-51)
- Khizr Khan: Defeated Daulat Khan — took Delhi in 1414; connected with the Delhi succession; founded the Sayyid Khan Dynasty
- Founder: Khizr Khan (1414-21) = Timur's viceroy
- Arrested Daulat Khan Lodi
- Ruled as Timur's deputy, without the title of Sultan
- Order: Khizr Khan - Mubarak Shah (1421-34) - Muhammad Shah (1434-45) - Alauddin Alam Shah (UPPSC 2023: 1, 3, 2, 4)
- End: Alam Shah gave up the throne voluntarily to Bahlol Lodi (1451)
## 2. Lodi dynasty (1451-1526)
| Ruler | Reign | Key points |
| Bahlol Lodi | 1451-1489 | Governor of Punjab; received the throne from Alam Shah; conquered Jaunpur; ruled longest of these (BPSC 2025) |
| Sikandar Lodi | 1489-1517 | Agra founded; Gulrukhi; Portuguese arrival in 1498 |
| Ibrahim Lodi | 1517-1526 | Arrogant to nobles; killed at Panipat |
- Order: Bahlol, then Sikandar, then Ibrahim (son follows father each time)
- Kingship: King = "first among equals" among the Afghan nobles
- ⚠ Khizr Khan ruled without the title of Sultan; Bahlol got the throne from Alam Shah without a battle
### Sayyid and Lodi rulers → contributions
## 1. Mubarak Shah (Sayyid, 1421-34)
- Military: Failed against the Khokhars
- Literature and building: Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi written in his reign; Octagonal tomb (see the next page)
## 2. Sikandar Lodi (1489-1517)
- Reign: 1489-1517
- Capital: Founded Agra (1504) → moved capital there from Delhi (CGPSC 2018)
- Measurement unit: Gaz-i-Sikandari = yard used to measure land
- Tax remissions: Abolished several taxes and trade restrictions → necessities cheaper
- Extent: Defeated Bengal rulers + Rajput chiefs → rule from Punjab to Bihar
- Jaunpur: Subdued its ruler
- Pen name: Gulrukhi (Persian poetry)
- Bigotry: Destroyed Jwalamukhi (Nagarkot) images + Mathura temples; imposed jizya
- Translation: Yet had a Sanskrit medical treatise translated into Persian
- Buildings: Moth ki Masjid + royal tombs of the Lodi Gardens
## 3. Ibrahim Lodi (1517-26)
- Plot: Nobles plotted to enthrone his brother Jalal Khan
- Gwalior: Seized Gwalior (had sheltered Jalal Khan) → ruler Vikramajit made a vassal
- Cause of Babur's invitation: Arrogance alienated the Afghan nobles

## 2. First Battle of Panipat, 1526 - details
- Date: 21 April 1526; older notes say 20 April
- Forces: Babur (about 12,000) defeated Ibrahim Lodi (about 1,00,000)
- Tactics:
- Field artillery + muskets = decisive factor
- Tulughma flanking manoeuvre
- Araba carts chained as a defensive line ("Rumi" / Ottoman device)
- Gunners Ustad Ali (Ustad Ali Quli) and Mustafa
- Consequences:
- Ibrahim died on the battlefield = only Delhi Sultan to do so
- Babur captured Delhi and Agra + the Lodi treasure
- End of Delhi Sultanate → start of the Mughal Empire
- ⚠ Khatauli = Ibrahim defeated by Rana Sanga; Panipat = Ibrahim killed by Babur
### Timur's invasion and Khatauli → route, effects
## 1. Timur's invasion, 1398
- Route: Crossed the Indus → Punjab (Multan, Bhatnair) → sacked Delhi in December 1398
- Effects:
- Tughlaq state broke up into regional kingdoms (Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat)
- His nominee Khizr Khan later founded the Sayyid dynasty
## 2. Khatauli (1517-18)
- Rana Sanga: Fought Rana Sanga of Mewar at Khatauli (1517-18) → Ibrahim defeated

### First Battle of Panipat (1526)
## Before Panipat
- Timur's route — crossed the Indus, Punjab via Multan and Bhatnair, sacked Delhi (December 1398)
- Mughal Empire was not an effect of Timur — founded by Babur in 1526
- Khizr Khan (Timur's viceroy) arrested Daulat Khan Lodi, took Delhi in 1414; ruled as deputy, without the title Sultan
- Bahlol Lodi got the throne from Alam Shah, the last Sayyid
- Sikandar Lodi — rule from Punjab to Bihar, took Jaunpur, defeated Bengal rulers and Rajput chiefs
## Invitation to Babur
- ★ Alam Khan — uncle of Ibrahim Lodi and pretender to the Delhi throne; invited Babur
- Daulat Khan Lodi (governor of Punjab) and Alam Khan sought Babur's help against Ibrahim; Rana Sanga too offered to join (per Babur)
## The battle
- ★ Fought 21 April 1526, Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi; Babur won by skilled warfare despite a smaller army
- ★ Artillery first used in India at Panipat; Babur first mentions guns at Bajaur (1519)
- Decisive — field artillery and muskets, handled by Ustad Ali Quli and Mustafa
- Turning point — ended the Delhi Sultanate, began the Mughal Empire; Mughal rule established by Babur (Sher Shah Suri — Sur dynasty)
