### Panchsheel 1954 → five principles and milestones
## 1. Origin and principles
- Source document: Preamble of the Agreement on Trade and Intercourse between India and the Tibet Region of China, 1954
- Signed 29 April 1954, Nehru-Zhou Enlai era
- The five principles:
- Mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty; mutual non-aggression
- Mutual non-interference in internal affairs
- Equality and mutual benefit
- Peaceful co-existence
- ⚠ Trap: Non-alignment is NOT one of the five principles (UPSC 1997)
## 2. Later milestones
| Milestone | Year | What happened |
| Burma | 1954 | Adopted Panchsheel in a joint declaration later in 1954 |
| Bandung | 1955 | Absorbed into Bandung's Ten Principles |
| UNGA resolution | 1957 | Recognised it as a principle of international law |
| Belgrade | 1961 | NAM summit made it a core principle |
- ⚠ Don't confuse: Nehru's Tribal Panchsheel (1952), drafted with Verrier Elwin, on tribal policy
### Non-Aligned Movement → founders, meaning and summits
## 1. Origins
- Bandung (Afro-Asian) Conference, April 1955: Held in Indonesia; adopted the Ten Principles of international peace (include Panchsheel)
- Founding: Belgrade Summit, 1961 (Yugoslavia), at the height of the Cold War
- Meaning: Not neutrality = active engagement + judging each issue on its merits
- ⚠ Bandung 1955 (Indonesia) = Ten Principles; Belgrade 1961 (Yugoslavia) = NAM founded
## 2. Founders ("Initiative of Five")
| Leader | Country |
| Jawaharlal Nehru | India |
| Josip Broz Tito | Yugoslavia (host) |
| Kwame Nkrumah | Ghana |
| Sukarno | Indonesia |
## 3. NAM summits and venues
- 1st-6th: 1st Belgrade 1961 → 2nd Cairo 1964 → 3rd Lusaka 1970 → 4th Algiers 1973 → 5th Colombo 1976 → 6th Havana 1979
- 7th-12th: 7th New Delhi 1983 → 8th Harare 1986 → 9th Belgrade 1989 → 10th Jakarta 1992 → 11th Cartagena 1995 → 12th Durban 1998
- 13th-19th: 13th Kuala Lumpur 2003 (JPSC key) → 14th Havana 2006 → 15th Sharm el-Sheikh 2009 → 16th Tehran 2012 → 17th Margarita (Venezuela) 2016 → 18th Baku 2019 → 19th Kampala 2024
- Exam angle: Bandung is in Indonesia (UPSC 2005); the venue of a particular NAM summit.
### Leaders → doctrines and statements
| Doctrine | Leader | Core idea |
| Nehruvian idealism | Nehru | Non-alignment, Panchsheel, faith in the UN, Asian solidarity |
| Indira Doctrine | Indira Gandhi | South Asia as India's zone; opposes outside interference in the region [GK] |
| Gujral Doctrine (1996) | I.K. Gujral | Non-reciprocal goodwill towards smaller neighbours; non-interference; peaceful settlement [GK] |
| Strategic autonomy | All PMs since 1947 | Freedom to judge each issue on its merits; today expressed as multi-alignment |
- Initiator: Non-alignment initiated by Nehru (UPPSC 2014)
- Commonwealth: Nehru called membership "an agreement by free will to be terminated by free will" (UPPSC 2017)
- Strategic autonomy: Runs through every phase of policy
### Institutions of foreign policy-making → role
## 1. Executive bodies
- MEA: Main body shaping and conducting foreign relations; guards strategic autonomy, security and territorial integrity
- Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS):
- Members: PM + Finance, External Affairs, Home and Defence Ministers
- Invitees: NSA, Cabinet Secretary, service chiefs
- National Security Council: Set up 1998; National Security Adviser = its secretary
- First NSA: Brajesh Mishra
## 2. Parliamentary and advisory bodies
- Parliament's Standing Committee on External Affairs: Departmentally related standing committee (Lok Sabha-serviced); scrutinises MEA budgets and policy
- Indian Council of World Affairs (ICWA):
- Founded 1943 under Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru; based at Sapru House
- Institution of National Importance by the ICWA Act 2001
- Vice-President = ex officio President
- Hosted the Asian Relations Conference, 1947
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Compared to India's non-aligned past; analysts call it strategic autonomy and multi-alignment
- Modi-Xi meeting: China stressed Panchsheel as the frame for ties (Sep 2025)
- Significance: Panchsheel and NAM still underpin India's Global South and South-South outreach
