### Conferences → year, venue, outcome (Stockholm, Rio, Rio+) — Rio Earth Summit, 1992
## 1. Climate and land conventions
- UNFCCC: Adopted at the Rio Earth Summit (1992); in force 21 March 1994 — to stabilise greenhouse-gas concentrations
- UNCCD — Convention to Combat Desertification: Established 1994
- Focus: Desertification and land degradation; links environment with development and sustainable land management

## 2. Global Environment Facility (GEF)
- Pilot GEF agreed in 1990 — established around the Rio Earth Summit
- Initial implementing partners: UNDP, UNEP, the World Bank

### CITES, 1973 — provisions and appendices
## 1. Basics
- Full name: Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora
- Other name: Washington Convention (APPSC G2 2025 key)
- Adoption: 3 March 1973, Washington D.C.; in force 1 July 1975
- Scope: Regulates international trade in wild fauna and flora; about 35,000 species → bans commercial trade for ~3%, regulates the other 97%
- Secretariat: Geneva, administered by UNEP (UNEP in Geneva)
- World Wildlife Day = 3 March: Day CITES was signed (proclaimed by UNGA in 2013)
## 2. Appendices I, II, III - criteria and trade
| Appendix | Listing criterion | Trade |
| I | Threatened with extinction (tiger, snow leopard, rhino) | Commercial trade prohibited[GK]; only exceptional non-commercial (e.g., research) with import and export permits |
| II | Not yet threatened [GK]; not necessarily threatened, but could be without control (corals, many sharks and rays) | Trade controlled by permits[GK]; allowed with export permit |
| III | Listed by one Party seeking others' help to control trade [GK]; protected in at least one country that asks others for help (walrus - Canada) | Allowed with permits/certificates of origin |
- Moving species: Between Appendices only by COP vote
### CITES in India — law, bodies, monitoring
## 1. Accession and law
- Accession: India became a Party in 1976 (PYQ 2018)
- Implementing law: Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972
- 2022 amendment: Added a chapter on CITES + new Schedule IV for specimens listed in the CITES Appendices, with Management and Scientific Authorities
- ⚠ Trap: some notes say Schedule IV lists invasive alien species - wrong; IAS regulated under a separate new section
- National bodies: Management Authority (permits) + Scientific Authority (advice)
- ZSI: Hosts a CITES Cell; authorised wildlife-forensic body → analysed 1,545 confiscated specimens of 105 species in 2025
## 2. Enforcement - Wildlife Crime Control Bureau
- WCCB: Statutory body that enforces CITES + prevents wildlife trafficking
- Set up in 2007 under the WPA (2006 amendment)
- Functions: Helps Customs inspect wildlife consignments under CITES and EXIM policy; builds the wildlife-crime database
## 3. Global monitoring
- CITES Trade Database: Kept by UNEP-WCMC
- TRAFFIC: Wildlife-trade monitoring network; joint programme of WWF and IUCN (1976)
- Global help:
- MIKE tracks elephant poaching

### CBD — adoption, objectives, finance
## 1. Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)
- CBD: Adopted at Rio in 1992 — biodiversity conservation; sustainable use; benefit sharing
- Opened at the Rio Earth Summit, 1992 (legally binding)
- CBD: Came into force on 29 December 1993
- In-situ: In the natural habitat; ex-situ: outside it
- Secretariat: Montreal, Canada
- Three objectives:
- Conservation of biodiversity
- Sustainable use of its components
- Fair and equitable benefit-sharing from genetic resources
- Financial mechanism: Global Environment Facility (GEF) - also for UNFCCC
- India: Ratified in 1994
- COPs: Every two years; first COP Nassau, Bahamas, 1994

### Cartagena and Nagoya Protocols — provisions — Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety
## 1. Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety
- Cartagena Protocol: 2000 — biosafety; living modified organisms (LMOs) and GMOs
- Subject: Biosafety - regulates safe transfer, handling and use of Living Modified Organisms (LMOs) from modern biotechnology [GK] (PYQ 2018 key)
- Timeline: Adopted 2000; in force 2003 [GK]
- Parties: 173 parties incl. India
- Name: After Cartagena, Colombia
- Biosafety Clearing-House (BCH): Online platform for sharing LMO information and decisions
- India:
- Nodal ministry: MoEF&CC
- GM approvals: Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)
### Cartagena and Nagoya Protocols — provisions — Nagoya Protocol on ABS - subject and nature
## 1. Nagoya Protocol on ABS - subject and nature
- Nagoya Protocol: 2010 (2014 in one list) — access and benefit sharing; genetic resources
- Full name: Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization (ABS)
- Nature: Legally binding supplementary agreement to the CBD
- Utilisation of genetic resources: = research and development on their genetic and/or biochemical composition (APPSC 2025 key)
- Coverage: Plant, animal and microbial genetic resources + associated traditional knowledge, excluding human genetic material
- Pillars:
- Prior informed consent
- Mutually Agreed Terms for monetary/non-monetary benefits
- Compliance against illegal acquisition
## 2. Nagoya - timeline
- Adopted: COP10, Nagoya, Japan (2010)
- In force: 2014 (LENS, Mar 2026; APPCA)
- Parties: 137 parties incl. India
- India ratified: 2012
- ⚠ Trap: some notes say 2014 - that is the entry-into-force year
### Cartagena and Nagoya Protocols — provisions — Nagoya - India's law and report

- Rio+5 (1997) — UNGA special session, New York, reviewed Agenda 21

## Rio Conventions and bodies
- UNCCD (1994) — one of three Rio Conventions with UNFCCC and CBD; Secretariat Bonn
- Forest Principles (Rio 1992) — non-binding
- CBD — opened at Rio 1992 (not Stockholm); Secretariat Montreal
- CMS — Bonn Convention; binding treaty under UNEP
- CMS COP-13 (India) — Great Indian Bustard, Asian elephant, Bengal florican added to Appendix I
- GEF — first implementing partners UNDP, UNEP, World Bank; Global Biodiversity Framework Fund under GEF
- Secretariats — World Heritage Paris; IWC Cambridge; Basel Geneva
- IUCN — Red List, field projects (not CITES customs enforcement)

- Indian star tortoise — CITES Appendix I (uplisted at CoP18, 2019, on India's proposal); red sanders, golden gecko, rosewood — Appendix II
### CITES appendices
- Appendix I — generally prohibits commercial trade; e.g. tigers, rhinoceroses, Indian and Chinese pangolins
- Appendix II — corals, most sharks and rays; striped hyena, rosewood (Dalbergia), golden gecko (AP-endemic)
- Appendix III — walrus (protected by Canada)
- Sharks and rays — 54 added to App. II at CoP19; whale shark, oceanic whitetip, manta rays to App. I at CoP20
- Red sanders (Pterocarpus santalinus) — endemic to Seshachalam and southern Eastern Ghats; IUCN Endangered
- CMS Appendix I — endangered migratory species, strict protection; App. II — need cooperation agreements
- KMGBF 2050 goals — Goal C fair sharing of benefits from genetic resources; Goal D finance and technology
- IUCN — union of governments and NGOs; maintains Red List; not a UN organ
### CITES & Ramsar in India
## Ramsar in India
- India a Party since 1982; 101 Ramsar sites, most in Asia
- First Indian sites (1 October 1981) — Chilika Lake, Keoladeo NP

## CITES in India
- India a Party since 1976; implemented via Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972
- CoP3 — New Delhi, 1981; only CITES COP hosted by India
- COP meets every two to three years; species moved between Appendices only by COP vote
- CoP20 — first CITES COP in Central Asia; India defeated EU proposal on Guggul (Commiphora wightii)

### CBD protocols
- CBD objectives — conservation, sustainable use, fair and equitable benefit-sharing (not compulsory technology transfer)
## Cartagena Protocol
- Adopted 2000, in force 2003; named after Cartagena, Colombia; under CBD
- Biosafety Clearing-House — online platform for LMO information (not a testing lab)
- Nagoya-Kuala Lumpur Supplementary Protocol (Liability and Redress, 2010) — supplementary to Cartagena
- India: GEAC approves GE organisms; MoEF&CC nodal ministry
## Nagoya Protocol
- Adopted COP10, Nagoya, 2010; in force 2014
- Covers plant, animal, microbial genetic resources and traditional knowledge; excludes human genetic material
- Pillars — prior informed consent, Mutually Agreed Terms, compliance
- "Utilisation" — research and development on genetic and/or biochemical composition
- India's first National Report — 3,556 IRCCs, over 60% of global total
- KMGBF adopted 2022
