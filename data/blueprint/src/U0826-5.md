### Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2025 — peripheral immune tolerance
## 1. Winners
- Shimon Sakaguchi, Mary E. Brunkow, Frederick J. Ramsdell — for peripheral immune tolerance
| Laureate | Discovery |
| Shimon Sakaguchi (1995) | Regulatory T cells (T-regs) — CD4⁺CD25⁺ subset in mice |
| Brunkow & Ramsdell (2001) | Gene FOXP3 — mutations cause IPEX syndrome (humans), scurfy (mice) |
## 2. Exam angle
- ⚠ Central tolerance = deletion of self-reactive T-cells in thymus; peripheral tolerance works outside thymus
- Applications: autoimmune diseases (type-1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus), transplants, cancer immunotherapy
### Nobel Prize in Physics 2025 — macroscopic quantum tunnelling
## 1. Winners
- John Clarke, Michel H. Devoret, John M. Martinis — macroscopic quantum tunnelling and energy quantisation in superconducting circuits
## 2. Key science
- Applications: superconducting qubits, circuit QED, quantum amplifiers, sensors
- Example of tunnelling in nature: nuclear fusion in the Sun
### Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2025 — Metal-Organic Frameworks (MOFs)
## 1. Winners
| Laureate | Contribution |
| Susumu Kitagawa (Japan, Kyoto University) | Flexible / "soft" MOFs letting gases flow in and out |
| Richard Robson (UK/Australia) | First concept — copper ions with four-armed organic molecules |
| Omar M. Yaghi (USA/Jordanian-American, UC Berkeley) | Highly stable, designable MOFs — water harvesting from desert air, CO₂ capture |
## 2. Exam angle
- MOFs: crystalline porous materials of metal ions + organic ligands
- ⚠ Unlike rigid zeolites, some MOFs can "breathe"
- Uses: removing PFAS from water, carbon capture, gas storage, drug delivery, catalysis
- ⚠ MOFs = Chemistry; quantum tunnelling = Physics; T-cells = Medicine
