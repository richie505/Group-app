### Occupational structure — definitions, terms and FIRSTs — Definitions and theory
## 1. Definitions and theory
- Meaning: distribution of workforce across primary, secondary, tertiary activities; shifts away from farming as an economy develops
- Clark-Fisher thesis: As income rises, labour shifts primary → secondary → tertiary
- India's structural retardation: occupational structure changed far more slowly than output; services grew without a manufacturing-led stage
### Occupational structure — definitions, terms and FIRSTs — Terms → meaning
## 1. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Unorganised sector | small and scattered units largely outside government control (GS 2025); low pay, no job security, few social benefits |
| Unorganised sector (NCEUS) | unincorporated private enterprises owned by individuals/households, proprietary or partnership basis, fewer than 10 workers |
| Organised manufacturing | registered factories employing 10+ workers (with power) / 20+ (without power) + registered companies; the rest = unincorporated/household (4 Sep 2026) |
| Unorganised workers (Code on Social Security 2020) | home-based, self-employed or wage workers in the unorganised sector (2025-26 glossary) |
| Informal worker (NCEUS) | Unorganised-sector workers + organised-sector workers without employment/social security benefits [GK] |
| Informalisation of formal enterprises | informal employment exists inside the formal sector too - contract and outsourced hiring |
| "Formal informality" | new EPFO jobs often short-term/contractual (2022-23) |
| Jobless growth | GDP growth not matched by formal, quality jobs |
| Premature deindustrialisation | services-led growth; manufacturing never absorbed farm labour |
| Feminisation of agriculture | female workers 55% agricultural labourers, 24% cultivators, yet women hold only 12.8% of operational holdings |

### Changes and issues — slow shift out of agriculture and jobless growth
## 1. Why the shift is slow
| Factor | Effect |
| Land pressure | population pressure on land - agriculture's employment share fell only marginally in the plan era |
| Skills mismatch | services-led growth needs skills; manufacturing never absorbed farm labour (premature deindustrialisation) |
| Capital intensity | capital-intensive growth fails to absorb surplus agricultural labour into formal non-farm jobs (3 Sep 2026) |
| Training | only 4.2% had formal vocational training (PLFS; Apr 2026) |
| Job deficit | ~20 million entrants a year vs ~2 million new jobs; few formal employers (Jan 2026) |
| History | colonial deindustrialisation pushed artisans onto land |
## 2. Jobless growth
- Period: late 1990s - employment growth fell to early-plan levels while GDP grew → more output without matching jobs
- Entrants: about 12 million enter the job market every year
- Firm response: flexibility → away from formal employment (13 Mar 2026)
- Direct factory employment 61% → 47% (2011-2023)
- Contract workers = 42% of factory workforce
