### Articles 323A and 323B — Amendments, subjects, who establishes
## 1. Basics
- Part XIV-A: Articles 323-A and 323-B — inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendations
- ⚠ Tribunals are not placed in Part XI (Union–State relations) or Part X (Scheduled and Tribal Areas)
- Related Parts: Part IX Panchayats (243 to 243-O); Part IX-A Municipalities (243-P to 243-ZG); Part IX-B Co-operative Societies (243-ZH to 243-ZT); Part XIV Services (308 to 323)
- 75th Amendment, 1994: added rent/tenancy clause (h)
## 2. Article 323-A vs Article 323-B
| Basis | Article 323-A | Article 323-B |
| Subjects | Exclusively public service matters — recruitment and conditions of service of Union, State, local-authority and statutory-authority employees (also PSUs) | Taxation, foreign exchange, industrial disputes (and labour), land reforms, ceiling on property (urban land ceiling), foodstuffs, elections to Parliament and State Legislatures; also rent and tenancy |
| Who can create | Only Parliament by law — Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985; State Legislatures have no power | Parliament and State Legislatures — States only within their legislative competence |
| Structure | One Central Administrative Tribunal + State Administrative Tribunals (separate or joint for States); no hierarchy | A hierarchy of tribunals can be created (original + appellate) |
- ⚠ 323A = service matters, Parliament only, no hierarchy; 323B = other subjects, Parliament and state legislatures, hierarchy allowed
- ⚠ 42nd (1976) inserted Part XIV-A; 75th (1994) added rent/tenancy

### Administrative tribunals — CAT jurisdiction, TDSAT and NGT
## 1. Important tribunals
- Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT): 1985 under the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 (Article 323-A) — original jurisdiction over service matters of Union civil servants; appeals to the Division Bench of the jurisdictional High Court (not directly to SC)
- TDSAT: Set up by the TRAI (Amendment) Act, 2000 (parent: TRAI Act, 1997) — disputes in telecommunications, broadcasting and airport tariffs
- Telecom, broadcasting and tariff appeals go directly to the Supreme Court on substantial questions of law; cyber appellate orders go to the High Court
- National Green Tribunal: Statutory — NGT Act, 2010; guided by natural justice, not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908; appeals directly to the Supreme Court within 90 days
- Armed Forces Tribunal Act: 2007
## 2. CAT jurisdiction and procedure
- Jurisdiction: recruitment and service matters of
- All-India Services, Central civil services, civil posts under the Centre, civilian defence employees (not armed forces personnel)
- All India Services = service disputes of Central civil servants
- notified PSUs
- Procedure: not bound by the CPC; follows natural justice; nominal filing fee; parties can appear in person
- ⚠ CAT covers civilian defence employees, not armed forces personnel

### L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997) — High Court review and Basic Structure
## 1. L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)
- Judicial review by the High Courts (Articles 226 and 227) and the Supreme Court (Article 32) is an integral part of the Basic Structure
- Clauses in Articles 323-A and 323-B excluding the jurisdiction of the High Courts and Supreme Court — declared unconstitutional (exclusion clauses Art. 323A(2)(d) and 323B(3)(d))
- Tribunal decisions (e.g. CAT) face scrutiny first before a Division Bench of the concerned High Court; the Supreme Court may be approached after that — not directly to SC
- Tribunals supplement HCs, do not replace them
- Earlier: S.P. Sampath Kumar case (1987) also upheld review (TH, 12 Aug 2026)
## 2. Basic Structure status
- Kesavananda Bharati (1973), Minerva Mills (1980) and L. Chandra Kumar (1997): Judicial review under Articles 32 and 226 is an integral, inviolable part of the Basic Structure
- Parliament cannot abrogate it even through Article 368; constitutional amendments are themselves subject to judicial review on the Basic Structure touchstone

## Nature and working
- Tribunals are quasi-judicial bodies, set up to cut pending court cases and bring domain expertise
- Chairpersons get the status of a High Court judge
- CAT procedure: not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure; follows natural justice; nominal fees; parties may appear in person
- NGT also not bound by the CPC, 1908, because it is guided by natural justice
- Joint administrative tribunals can be set up for two or more States
- R. Gandhi (2010) — members performing judicial functions must be independent of the executive
- L. Chandra Kumar struck down the court-barring clauses: tribunal decisions go first to a High Court Division Bench, then the Supreme Court
### 323-A vs 323-B
- Article 323A — recruitment and service conditions of public servants; Parliament alone can legislate
- Article 323B — both Parliament and State Legislatures can act; subjects include land reforms
- CAT has original jurisdiction over service matters of Union civil servants; appeals go first to a High Court Division Bench, not directly to the Supreme Court
- TDSAT — telecom, broadcasting and airport tariff disputes; its cyber appellate orders lie before the High Court
