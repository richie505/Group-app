### Article 20 — three protections and who gets them
## 1. Article 20 - three protections
| Clause | Protection | Scope |
| 20(1) | No ex post facto criminal law - no conviction except for a law in force at the time; no heavier penalty | Only criminal laws, not retrospective civil or tax laws; bars conviction/greater penalty, not trial |
| 20(2) | No double jeopardy - not prosecuted and punished twice for the same offence | Only prosecution and punishment by a court/judicial tribunal; not Departmental proceedings |
| 20(3) | No self-incrimination - an accused cannot be compelled to be a witness against himself | Only criminal proceedings; covers oral and documentary testimony, not Physical evidence like fingerprints/specimens (Kathi Kalu Oghad 1961) |
- Selvi v State of Karnataka (2010): narco-analysis, polygraph and brain-mapping without consent violate 20(3)
## 2. Who gets them
| Article | Available to |
| Art. 20 | citizens, foreigners and companies (legal persons); non-suspendable in emergency |
| Art. 21 | citizens and non-citizens |
- Art. 20 does not cover: Preventive detention
### Article 22(1)-(2) — rights of arrested persons
## 1. Article 22(1)-(2) - rights of arrested persons
- Rights (ordinary/punitive arrest, Art. 22(1)-(2)):
- Be informed of grounds of arrest (told the grounds)
- Consult and be defended by a lawyer of choice
- Produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours (excluding journey time)
- Art. 22(3) exclusions: not available to an enemy alien (enemy aliens) or a person held under a preventive detention law (preventive detainees)
- Not covered: arrest under court orders, civil arrest, deportation of aliens
### Article 21 — procedure established by law vs due process
## 1. Procedure and discretion
- "Procedure established by law" (text of Article 21): A law is valid if made by the correct legislative procedure — A.K. Gopalan (1950)
- No due process in India; courts could not examine reasonableness (UPSC)
- Due process after Maneka Gandhi (1978): Law must be just, fair and reasonable and free from procedural arbitrariness
- Overruled Gopalan; procedure must be "fair, just and reasonable" → Art. 21 also protects against legislative action; in effect introduced American due process (UPSC)
- Unguided, uncontrolled or uncanalised discretion given to an executive authority violates non-arbitrariness under Article 14 and subverts the Rule of Law — discretion without objective standards or judicial safeguards is ultra vires Article 14
## 2. Gopalan vs Maneka
| A.K. Gopalan (1950) | Maneka Gandhi (1978) |
| "Procedure established by law" = any procedure enacted by the legislature (from the Japanese Constitution) | Procedure must be just, fair and reasonable - not arbitrary |
| Arts. 19, 21, 22 separate | Arts. 14, 19, 21 linked - the golden triangle |
| Art. 21 protects only against the executive | Also protects against arbitrary legislation - in effect American due process |
| "Procedure established by law" | from the Japanese Constitution; more definite than American "due process of law" |
| Due process | USA |
- Comparative note: "Procedure established by law" - Japan vs "due process" - USA
- ⚠ Gopalan = separate Articles, executive only; Maneka = golden triangle 14-19-21, legislation too

### Article 21A and the 86th Amendment
## 1. Article 21A and the 86th Amendment
- Art. 21A: State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of 6 to 14 years, "in such manner as the State may determine"
- Only elementary education is a Fundamental Right, not higher/professional education
- Inserting amendment: 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 (new Art. 21A after Art. 21)
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes loosely say the RTE Act "added" Art. 21A - it was the 86th Amendment; the Act gives effect to it
- Nature:
- Extension of the Right to Life (Art. 21) (PYQ 2019)
- A Fundamental Right (PYQ 2018)
| Same 86th Amendment changed | New content |
| Art. 21A (FR) | Free, compulsory education 6-14 years |
| Art. 45 (DPSP) | Early childhood care and education for children below 6 years |
| Art. 51A (FD) - 51A(k) | Parent/guardian to provide opportunities for education to child/ward 6-14 years (the 11th duty; duty of parents) |
### RTE Act 2009 and earlier education cases
## 1. RTE Act 2009 - provisions
- Enabling law: Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009
- Commencement: Act + Art. 21A in force 1 April 2010
- Sec. 3: Every child 6-14 has a right to free, compulsory education in a neighbourhood school till elementary education is complete
- Quota: 25% entry-level seats in unaided private schools for weaker groups (not minority schools); schools reimbursed per-child expenditure
- Amendments: 2012, 2017, 2019 (assessment and detention allowed at certain stages), 2022

### Preventive detention — Article 22(4)-(7) safeguards, law-making power, laws → year
## 1. Term and safeguards
- Preventive vs punitive: on suspicion of future acts, without trial vs after conviction
- Art. 22(4): no detention beyond 3 months unless an Advisory Board (HC judges or persons qualified to be) reports sufficient cause
- Art. 22(5): detainee told the grounds (grounds communicated) + earliest opportunity to represent (earliest chance of representation)
- Art. 22(7): Parliament may fix the maximum period
- 44th Amendment 1978: cut the period to 2 months, but the clause was never notified (not yet notified) → 3 months still applies (16 Jun 2026; IYB records the 2-month text)
- Public order: SC - preventive detention only for 'public order', not routine 'law and order' (Jun 2026)
## 2. Law-making power

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:11
### Article 20: three protections
- Article 20: protection in respect of conviction for offences; 21 life and liberty; 21A education; 22 arrest and detention
- 20(1): applies to criminal laws; bars conviction and bigger penalty, not always the trial
- 20(2) (double jeopardy): only prosecution and punishment by a court or judicial tribunal, not departmental proceedings
- 20(3): criminal cases; oral and documentary testimony; fingerprints/physical specimens not testimony (Kathi Kalu Oghad, 1961)
- 'Procedure established by law': Japan; 'due process': United States
- Article 21: citizens and non-citizens; includes dignity, health, protection against solitary confinement
### Article 20 notes
- Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010): narco-analysis, polygraph, brain-mapping without consent break Art. 20(3)
- Article 20 protections: citizens, foreigners and legal persons such as companies
- BNSS Sections 47 and 58 ≈ CrPC Sections 50 and 57 (grounds of arrest, production before magistrate)
### Gopalan vs Maneka
- Article 21 uses 'procedure established by law'
- A.K. Gopalan (1950): narrow; law valid if made by correct legislative procedure
- Maneka Gandhi (1978): law must also be just, fair and reasonable; protects against arbitrary legislative action
- Unguided executive discretion breaks Article 14: it is arbitrary and weakens the Rule of Law

### Article 22
## Arrest safeguards
- 22(1): be told grounds of arrest; consult and be defended by a lawyer of choice (procedure now BNSS Secs. 47 and 58)
- Not guaranteed: bail as of right, trial by jury
- Outside 22(1)-(2): arrest under court orders, civil arrest, deportation of aliens; 22(3) omits enemy aliens and preventive detainees
- Vihaan Kumar v. State of Haryana (2025): grounds of arrest must be meaningfully communicated to every arrestee
## Preventive detention
- Detention without trial, on suspicion of future harmful acts; punitive detention follows conviction
- Justified for public order, not routine law and order
- 22(4): three months without Advisory Board report still applies; 44th Amendment wanted two months but never notified
- 22(5): grounds and chance of representation; 22(7): Parliament sets maximum period
- Parliament alone: defence, foreign affairs, security of India; shared with States: public order, security of a State, essential supplies
