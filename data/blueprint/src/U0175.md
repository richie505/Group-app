- 1833 Act:
- End of commercial functions: Company became purely administrative
- Sovereignty (1813): asserted the sovereignty of the British Crown over the Company's Indian possessions (UPSC 2019 key: statements 1 and 2 correct)

- 1813: charter extended 20 years

### Legislature, centralisation and Agra Presidency → Act
## 1. Charter Act 1833 — centralisation
- Title: Governor-General of Bengal → Governor-General of India
- Legislation:
- GG-in-Council got exclusive legislative power for all British India
- Presidencies lost law-making powers
- Laws now called "Acts" (earlier "Regulations")
- Law Member: added as the 4th member of the GG's council
- Agra Presidency: Act provided for splitting the Bengal Presidency
- Present UP separated and placed under Agra Presidency in 1834 (UPPSC 2021 key)
- Renamed North-Western Provinces (1836)
## 2. Charter Act 1853 — separate legislature
- Separated the legislative and executive functions of the GG's Council:
- 6 new legislative members → separate Central Legislative Council (the "mini-Parliament")
- The 6: 4 members appointed by the provincial governments + the Chief Justice and one judge of the Calcutta Supreme Court
## 3. Centralisation trend (1773 to 1861)
| Act | Step |
| 1773 | Governor of Bengal made GG of Bengal; Bombay and Madras subordinated |
| 1784 | Board of Control; stricter supervision of the presidencies |
| 1833 | GG of India; exclusive legislative power with the GG-in-Council - peak of centralisation |
| 1853 | Legislative work separated into a Central Legislative Council with provincial members |
- Reversal: Indian Councils Act 1861 restored legislative power to Bombay and Madras (decentralisation)
### Education, missionaries and civil services → Act
## 1. 1813 — education and missionaries
- Education grant: Rs 1,00,000 (1 lakh) a year for revival of literature, encouragement of learned natives, promotion of science
- ⚠ Trap: some notes print Rs 10,00,000 - wrong; the figure is 1 lakh
- Missionaries: allowed to enter India and propagate religion
- The Act "officially sanctioned the propagation of Christianity"
- Civil servants: training made compulsory
- Andhra: mandated public education as part of Company governance
## 2. 1833 — Section 87
- Section 87: no Indian barred from office on grounds of religion, place of birth, descent or colour
## 3. 1853 — open competition
- Abolished the Directors' patronage → open competitive examination
- APPSC-GS 2018 key: appointments exclusively made by the Directors till 1853
- Macaulay Committee: on the Indian Civil Service, 1854, after the 1853 Act (some notes give the report as 1855)
- Nomination replaced by competitive exams; preference for university graduates
### Universal angles
- Current relevance - civil-service reform: (Apr 2025)
- Fort William College (1800, Wellesley) → replaced by Haileybury (1806)
- Raja Ram Mohan Roy first demanded Indians in the services (1832)
- 1853 Act brought open exams
- Significance:
- 1813 opened India to free trade and missionaries
- 1833 made the Company an administrator
- 1853 opened the path to a legislature + a merit-based ICS → ending with the GoI Act 1858
- AP angle: Madras High Court "court of record" linked to the 1853 Act; the High Court itself came under the Indian High Courts Act 1861 (est. 1862)
- FIRST/LAST: first GG of India Bentinck; first Law Member Macaulay; last Charter Act 1853

### Charter Acts compared
## Charter Act, 1813
- ★ First financial allocation for education — annual grant of Rs 1 lakh
- Ended Company's trade monopoly in India; kept exclusive trade in tea and with China
- Allowed Christian missionaries to enter and preach (Evangelical pressure: Wilberforce, Charles Grant)
## Charter Act, 1833
- Abolished the remaining monopoly over tea and China trade
- Laws of the GG-in-Council called Acts (not Regulations); legislative power for all British India to the GG-in-Council
## Charter Act, 1853
- Abolished Directors' patronage; open competition for civil-service recruitment
- Last Charter Act; the GoI Act 1858 then transferred authority to the Crown
### FIRSTs and LASTs
- T. B. Macaulay — first Law Member (fourth member of the GG's Council) under the 1833 Act
- First Law Commission (1834) — chaired by Macaulay; drafted the IPC
- Charter Act 1833 — final step towards centralisation; made the Company purely administrative
- "The Company is an anomaly but it is part of a system where everything is an anomaly" — Macaulay, Commons speech on the Bill of the Charter Act of 1833
- Charter Act 1853 — the two remaining new legislative members were not the Commander-in-Chief and Law Member; they included the Chief Justice
### Centralisation 1773-1861
