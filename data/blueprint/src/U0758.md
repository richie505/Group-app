### Green hydrogen — colour types and production routes
## 1. Colours of hydrogen
| Type | Produced by | CO₂ emissions | Notes |
| Grey | Steam methane reforming (SMR) of natural gas, or coal gasification | High — released | About 95% of current global production; the cheapest |
| Blue | SMR with carbon capture and storage (CCS) — fossil route (grey hydrogen) + CCS | Low — captured | Transitional — not fully zero-carbon |
| Green | Electrolysis of water using renewable electricity — solar, wind or hydroelectricity (PYQ 2023) | Zero | The cleanest — a focus of India's Mission |
| Pink / purple | Electrolysis using nuclear electricity | Near-zero | — |

| Brown / black | Coal gasification without CCS - lignite / bituminous coal | Very high | The dirtiest |

- Zero-carbon: Only green hydrogen

## 2. Production routes

### Green hydrogen — standard, certification and electrolyser
## 1. Green Hydrogen Standard and certification
- Green Hydrogen Standard (MNRE, Aug 2023): Hydrogen is "green" if well-to-gate emissions not more than 2 kg CO2-eq per kg H2 (12-month average)
- Green Hydrogen Certification Scheme of India (GHCI): Launched April 2025
- Certification: Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE, Ministry of Power) = nodal authority
- Standards for green ammonia and green methanol: Announced / notified Mar 2026
## 2. Electrolyser - meaning
- Electrolyser: Device that splits water into hydrogen + oxygen using electricity

### Technologies — electrolyser types, storage and fuel cells
## 1. Electrolyser types
| Type | Feature |
| PEM | Solid polymer membrane; compact; quick response to (follows) variable RE [GK] |
| Solid oxide | High-temperature steam electrolysis; most efficient [GK] |
## 2. Storage and transport
| Method | Key fact |
| Compression | Compressed gas in high-pressure composite (Type-IV) cylinders/tanks (LENSD) |
| Liquefaction | Liquid hydrogen at -253 °C (cryogenic); very low density 0.071 g/ml → large, bulky tanks (APP) |
| Metal hydrides | Solid metal hydrides - hydrogen absorbed in metal alloys, released on heating [GK] |
| Chemical carriers | Ammonia, methanol, LOHC carry hydrogen for export [GK] |
- Hydrogen train: Stores hydrogen in roof-mounted Type-IV composite cylinders
- Safety body: National Centre for Hydrogen Safety handles storage, transport and flammability risks
## 3. PEM fuel cell - principle
- Fuel cell: An electrochemical device — converts H₂ and O₂ into electricity and H₂O; high efficiency; zero emissions at the point of use
- Anode: H2 splits into protons + electrons - H2 → 2H+ + 2e-
- External circuit: Electrons flow → electricity; protons cross the membrane
- Cathode: 1/2 O2 + 2H+ + 2e- → H2O + heat
- No burning: Hydrogen not burnt; chemical energy → electricity
- Exhaust: Only water vapour and warm air - zero tailpipe emissions
### Applications — industry, green ammonia, steel, mobility, H-CNG
## 1. Uses of hydrogen
- Industry: Petroleum refining — desulphurisation; ammonia for fertilisers; methanol; steel through the direct reduced iron (DRI) process; a chemical feedstock
- Transport: Fuel-cell vehicles — buses, trucks, trains and ships; rocket fuel; blended in compressed natural gas (CNG)
- Power: Fuel cells generate electricity; backup power; grid balancing — storing excess renewable energy; may be used for heating in the future
## 2. Green hydrogen applications
- Hard-to-abate industries: Green steel — replacing coking coal; fertiliser through green ammonia; refineries; also chemicals
- Steel: H2 replaces coking coal in DRI; 4 steel pilots (~₹106 cr)
- Fertiliser - green ammonia: Green ammonia = nitrogen (N2) + green hydrogen; leads green-hydrogen adoption (fertiliser, fuel, marine fuel); SECI discovered prices for fertiliser units (figures on the data page)
- Transport: Heavy-duty vehicles, shipping, aviation (synthetic fuels), railways
- Energy: Long-duration energy storage; peak power
- Chemicals: Green methanol and other chemical derivatives; exported as green ammonia or green methanol energy carriers
- City gas: Hydrogen blending in city gas networks (H-CNG) being evaluated

### Universal angles
- Why hydrogen matters:
- Enables the storage and transport of renewable energy
- Decarbonises steel, cement, fertilisers, heavy transport, shipping and aviation — useful for hard-to-abate sectors where direct electrification is difficult
- Green hydrogen produces only water and replaces fossil-based hydrogen and fuels; reduces dependence on imported fossil fuels
- Critical for India's 2070 net-zero target; important for global climate goals
- Significance: Decarbonises hard-to-abate sectors; energy independence by 2047 and net zero by 2070
- Current relevance:
- Jind-Sonipat hydrogen train (13 Jul 2026)
- Long-term green ammonia and green methanol export agreements with Japanese companies (Jul 2026)
- Criticism & debates:
- High cost: green H2 ~$4/kg vs grey $1.5-2/kg
- Nascent industry; import dependence on electrolysers
- US credits up to $3/kg compete for exports
- Costly cryogenic transport; water stress
- AP angle: Green Hydrogen Valley, Kakinada and Pudimadaka projects
