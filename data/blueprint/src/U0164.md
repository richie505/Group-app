## 1. Murshid Quli Khan — rise and acts
- Rise: Diwan → Subahdar of Bengal → Nawab of Bengal in 1717
- Founder of the autonomous Bengal Nawabi
- Capital shift: Dacca to Murshidabad
- English relations: Wanted customs duties paid by the English → English increased military establishment instead
- Mughal status: Last Subahdar/Governor of Bengal appointed by the Mughal Emperor (UPPSC 2013, 2021)
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes call Sarfaraz the "son of Murshid Quli Khan" – he was Shuja-ud-din's son (Murshid Quli's grandson)
### Terms → meaning (revenue and trade)

## 1. Black Hole episode - narrator, historiographical debate
- Numbers: 146 English prisoners in a small cell → about 120 died
- Holwell's own figure = 123
- Narrator: J.Z. Holwell, a survivor – story rests mainly on his account
- Debate: Historians doubt the numbers; British used the story to justify war

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:163
### Nawabs of Bengal in order
- Order: Murshid Quli Khan → Shuja-ud-din → Sarfaraz Khan → Alivardi Khan → Siraj-ud-Daula
- Murshid Quli Khan — Nawab from 1717; founder of the autonomous Bengal Nawabi; last Subahdar appointed by the Mughal Emperor
- Shuja-ud-din — son-in-law of Murshid Quli Khan; Sarfaraz Khan — his son (Murshid Quli's grandson)
- Alivardi Khan — earlier Deputy Governor of Bihar; took the throne by killing Sarfaraz; ruled just before Siraj
- Siraj-ud-Daula — grandson of Alivardi Khan
- Mir Jafar — puppet Nawab installed after Plassey (not founder of the Nawabi)
### Murshid Quli Khan
- Moved the capital from Dacca to Murshidabad
- Ijaradari — revenue farming: contractors (ijaradars) collected land revenue; also called mal jasmani
- Jagir → khalisa — land brought under direct state revenue administration
- Dastak misuse (1717 farman allowed duty-free Company trade) — loss of revenue to the Nawab
### Siraj vs English: causes
- English protected the runaway Krishna Das
- English fortified Fort William without permission; the French obeyed the order to stop
- English seen to support court groups Ghaseti Begum and Shaukat Jang of Purnea
### Events 1756-57
| Event | Date |
| Seizure of Kasimbazar factory | May 1756 (first) |
| Fall of Calcutta | June 1756 |
| Recapture of Calcutta | January 1757 |
| Alinagar | February 1757 |
### Black Hole & Plassey rewards
- Black Hole (June 1756) — based mainly on survivor J.Z. Holwell's account; historians doubt the numbers
- Mir Jafar's rewards: zamindari of the 24 Parganas, duty-free trade in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa, a large payment
- Plassey — a victory of treachery and a political-financial deal, not a contest of equal armies
### Plassey vs Buxar
- Plassey — commonly called the foundation of British rule; some treat Buxar as more decisive
- Clive's conspiracy — Jagat Seth, Omichund, Mir Jafar
### Exam corner
- Treaty of Alinagar (February 1757) — restored the 1717 privileges; allowed fortification of Calcutta
- Order: Ambur (1749) → Plassey (1757) → Wandiwash (1760) → Buxar (1764)
### Battles → year; Plassey vs Buxar debate
- ★ Battle of Plassey — first decisive military success of the Company
- ★ British success factors: naval superiority, wealth and manpower from Bengal, better internal coordination
