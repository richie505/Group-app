### Ajanta → location, phases, patrons, panels, technique — Ajanta - basics
## 1. Ajanta - basics
- Location: Aurangabad district, Maharashtra; horseshoe-shaped gorge of the Waghora river
- Discovery: 1819 by John Smith (British officer) while hunting tigers
- Caves: About 30 caves (some notes count 25 viharas + 4 chaityas); 5 Hinayana, rest Mahayana
- UNESCO: Among India's first UNESCO World Heritage Sites (1983, with Ellora)
## 2. Phases and patrons
- Patrons: Satavahanas (early), then the Vakatakas (Harishena)
| Phase | Caves | Fact |
| Early (Satavahana) | Caves 9 and 10 | Earliest paintings are Satavahana-era; Shwetagaja / Chhaddanta Jataka in Cave 10 (Svetagaja/Chhaddanta Jataka) = first "completely evolved painting" (APPSC G2 2012 answer: Cave 10) |
| Later (Vakataka king Harisena / Harishena, 5th c.) | Cave 16 | Excavated by his minister Varahadeva |
| Later | Cave 17 | Brahmi inscription + Bhava-chakra (wheel of life) |
| Later | Cave 19 | Chaitya with standing Buddha and two Yakshas |
- Date: Some notes say construction "started by Mauryas"; standard view = c. 2nd century BCE, Satavahana era

### Gupta coins → type and ruler
- Gupta gold coins: Dinara or Dinar (issued in the largest numbers); silver coins: Rupaka — Gupta coins contained gold and silver
- Chandragupta I: The first Gupta ruler to issue coins — the first Gupta coin issuer
- Chandragupta II: Silver Rupaka coins — associated with his victory over the Sakas; known as Sakari
- Silver began after he conquered the Sakas (Western Kshatrapas) of Gujarat (MPPSC 2023); modelled on Saka silver issues
- ⚠ Trap: some notes say "gold coins modelled on Saka silver" - read silver
- The maximum number of Gupta coins was found at Bayana in Bharatpur, Rajasthan — a major Gupta coin hoard site
- Kalachuri coins: Gold, silver and copper
