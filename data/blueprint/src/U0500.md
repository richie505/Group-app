## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Contract farming | Selling produce to an agri-business firm at a price, quantity and/or time fixed in advance |
| "Small Farmer Large Field" | Many small/marginal neighbouring farmers in an area organise into groups, synchronise + harmonise operations → work like one large field, with scale in inputs and marketing (UPSC 2023, option 2) |

### APMC system — State law, notified markets and regulated markets
## 1. Legal framework
- Regulation: APMC Acts enacted by States (agriculture market = State subject) (UKPSC 2021; UPSC)
- AP law: AP (Agricultural Produce and Livestock) Markets Act, 1966 (Act 16 of 1966)
| Step | Instrument |
| 1 | Government declares a notified area |
| 2 | A Market Committee is constituted for it |
| 3 | The committee sets up the notified market yard |
## 2. Regulated markets
- Scale: ~2,477 primary regulated markets + 4,843 sub-market yards
- Features: Open auction, standard weights, recorded price, licensed buyers
- Market fee 1%: AP AMCs collected ₹429.77 crore in 2025-26 (up to Dec 2025); ₹433.39 crore in 2024-25 (to Dec 2024)
### Grading and standardisation — AGMARK, laboratories and AGMARKNET
- AGMARK: Quality certification mark for agricultural produce under the Agricultural Produce (Grading and Marking) Act, 1937
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes print it as "Grading and Marketing Act, 1937" - correct title is Grading and Marking
- Certifying body: Directorate of Marketing and Inspection (DMI), Ministry of Agriculture - not the FCI (UPPSC 2023)
| Laboratory | Place |
| Central Agmark Laboratory | Nagpur (APPSC 2018 key) |
| Central Quality Control Laboratory | Nagpur (APPSC 2025 key) |
| State grading laboratories | Grade produce for packers under Agmark (Delhi entry) |
- AGMARKNET: Portal (2000, NIC) for mandi prices → Agmarknet 2.0 in 2025 (price-information portal)
- Debate: "One Nation, One Regulator" proposals seek to end dual certification (BIS + AGMARK)
### Model Acts and contract farming → provisions
| Model Act | Key features |
| Model APMC Act, 2003 | Direct sale to contract sponsors; private markets; special markets for perishables; farmer-consumer markets; common registration of intermediaries |
| Model APLM Act, 2017 | Whole State as one market area; private yards + farmer-consumer markets; warehouses as market yards; single-point (capped) market fee, unified licence, e-trading; livestock included [GK] |
## Model Contract Farming Act 2018
- Model Agricultural Produce & Livestock Contract Farming and Services (Promotion & Facilitation) Act, 2018:
- Contract farming outside APMC purview (outside the APMC Act); farmer treated as the weaker party; covers livestock and services
- Sponsor must buy the entire pre-agreed quantity
- No right, title or interest in the land vests in sponsor; no permanent structure on farmer's land
- FPOs may contract for farmers; produce insurable
- Online registration: Registering and Agreement Recording Committee (RARC)
- Contract Farming Facilitation Groups (CFFGs) at village/panchayat level; disputes settled at lowest level
- Not in your sources: AP's own contract-farming experience (for example the AP APMC Act amendment on contract farming).
- ⚠ 2003 = Model APMC; 2017 = Model APLM; 2018 = Model Contract Farming
### Farm laws 2020 → provisions and repeal
| Act (Sept 2020) | Core provision |
| Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation) Act | Trade outside APMC mandis (APMC yards) in a "trade area", no State market fee |
| Farmers (Empowerment and Protection) Agreement on Price Assurance and Farm Services Act | Contract farming with sponsors |
| Essential Commodities (Amendment) Act | Removed stock limits on cereals, pulses, oilseeds, edible oils, onion, potato - limits only in extraordinary situations |
- Criticism:
- No legal MSP guarantee; FPTC Act did not mention MSP (UPPSC 2021)
- Enacted via Concurrent List Entry 33 though agriculture = State List Entry 14
- Outcome: Repealed in November 2021 after farmers' movement led mainly from Punjab and Haryana
- AP 1966 Act remains the operative framework
- ⚠ Concurrent Entry 33 used; agriculture = State Entry 14
### e-NAM — launch, State reforms, advantages and coverage — Basics
## 1. Basics
- Launch: April 2016; completed 10 years in April 2026
- Nature: Pan-India electronic trading portal networking existing APMC mandis into a unified national market - virtual market backed by physical APMC mandis; motto "One Nation, One Market"
- Lead implementing agency: Small Farmers Agribusiness Consortium (SFAC)
- Funding: Central Sector Scheme via Agri-Tech Infrastructure Fund (ATIF); now under Integrated Scheme for Agricultural Marketing (ISAM-eNAM)
- Grant: One-time up to ₹75 lakh per mandi for equipment + infrastructure
### e-NAM — launch, State reforms, advantages and coverage — State reforms and advantages
## 1. State reforms and advantages
- Reforms required of states: Allow electronic trading - e-trading/e-auction (APPSC 2016 answer)
- ⚠ Trap: regulating prices was a distractor
- Single licence valid across the state; single-point levy of market fee
- Advantages: Pan-India portal, nationwide market access, transparent price discovery (UPSC 2017: both correct)
- Direct payment to farmer's account
- Modules: FPO trading, farm-gate trading, warehouse trading with e-NWRs, AI assaying; inter-State trade
- Exam angle: "which reform is required of states" and statement pairs on e-NAM's advantages.

### FPOs and cooperative marketing bodies → agency and support — 10,000 FPO scheme
## 1. 10,000 FPO scheme
- Formation and Promotion of 10,000 FPOs (2020): Central Sector; ₹6,865 crore; support till 2027-28
- Aims: bargaining power, economies of scale, lower costs
- 5 years' handholding via Cluster-Based Business Organisations
- Implementing agencies: SFAC and NABARD; full list = SFAC, NABARD, NCDC, NAFED
- NCDC promotes cooperative-sector FPOs (target 1,100 via PACS)
- Norms: 15% of FPOs in Aspirational Districts; one FPO per block; ODOP + ONDC linkage
- Support:
- Equity grant up to ₹2,000 per farmer member (max ₹15 lakh per FPO)
- Credit guarantee up to ₹2 crore per FPO
- Management support ₹18 lakh per FPO over 3 years
- National status: 10,000 FPOs organised (Aug 2026); 56.32 lakh farmers (21.96 lakh women) by 1 Jan 2026
- AP (2024-25): 455 FPOs registered; 99 seed, 97 fertiliser, 98 pesticide licences; 207 FPOs on ONDC; district-level monitoring committees (D-MC) in 13 districts

### Operation Greens and Agriculture Infrastructure Fund → features
## 1. Operation Greens
- Announced: Union Budget 2018-19 (APPSC 2018 key)
- ⚠ Trap: distractors were Operation Flood, Mission Indradhanush and SAMPADA
- Crops: Tomato, Onion, Potato (TOP) – grown seasonally and regionally but eaten all year
- Model: "on the lines of Operation Flood"
- Aim: Curb erratic prices via FPOs, agri-logistics, processing, professional management
- Implementing ministry: Ministry of Food Processing Industries
- TOP to TOTAL:
- Budget 2021-22: extended to 22 perishable crops
- Subsidy pattern:
- Short-term interventions = 50% subsidy on transport + storage
- Long-term value-chain projects = 50% grant-in-aid; 70% for FPOs
## 2. Agriculture Infrastructure Fund
- Size: ₹1 lakh crore medium- and long-term debt-financing facility for post-harvest infrastructure + community farming assets; launched 2020
- Interest subvention: 3% on loans up to ₹2 crore
- Credit guarantee: Through CGTMSE
- Eligible projects: Warehouses, cold stores, processing units, sorting and grading units, custom hiring centres, supply chains
- Largest sanctioned category = custom hiring centres (32,560)
- Mar 2026: ₹84,681 crore sanctioned for 1,71,267 projects, mobilising ₹1.34 lakh crore
- Aug 2025 (earlier): ₹70,665 crore for 1,20,769 projects
- AP angle:
- Farm-gate infrastructure: Funded at Rythu Bharosa Kendra level (now Rythu Seva Kendralu)
- PACS: Conversion into Multi-Purpose Facility Centres
