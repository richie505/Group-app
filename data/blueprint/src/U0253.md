### Books → theme
## 1. Annihilation of Caste (1936) - undelivered speech
- Origin (1936): Presidential address for the Jat-Pat Todak Mandal of Lahore (Hindu reformist body)
- Organisers cancelled the session over its contents → Ambedkar published it himself
- Gandhi replied in Harijan; Ambedkar rejoined
- Critique:
- Caste has religious sanction in the Shastras/Vedas → reform within Hinduism futile
- Whole caste system, not just untouchability, must go

### Ambedkar vs Gandhi and Congress — caste, electorates, village
## 1. Debate on untouchability and caste
| Parameter | Gandhi | Ambedkar |
| Caste | Varna an ideal division of labour; untouchability a corruption | Whole caste system oppressive; annihilate it |
| Term | "Harijan" (children of God) | Rejected "Harijan"; Dalit/Scheduled Castes |
| Method | Moral persuasion, change of heart, temple entry | Legal, constitutional, political rights; state action |
| Electorate - Poona Pact (1932) | Opposed separate electorates; fasted against them (1932) | Demanded them; accepted joint electorates with more reserved seats to save Gandhi's life - settled for 147 reserved seats [GK] |
| Religion | Reform Hinduism from within | Left Hinduism for Buddhism (1956) |
| Governance | Decentralised Gram Swaraj | Strong Centre |
- Common ground: Both sought uplift of the marginalised and stressed education
## 2. Separate electorates
- Ambedkar's view: Political power necessary for the depressed classes to protect themselves (APPSC GS 2016)
- Later view: Felt the Pact made Dalits dependent on caste Hindus
## 3. Gandhism and Congress
- Gandhism: Rejected Gram Swaraj and trusteeship; advocated a strong, centralised constitutional democracy to protect minorities
- Congress:
- Criticised its refusal to treat caste as a political issue

### Conversion to Buddhism → date, place
| Event | Date | Place / fact |
| Yeola declaration | 13 October 1935 | Depressed Classes Conference, Yeola - announced he would leave Hinduism |
| Conversion | 14 October 1956 | Deekshabhoomi, Nagpur |
| Death | 6 December 1956 | Delhi, weeks after the conversion |
- Sikhism considered:
- Supported by B.S. Moonje; opposed by Gandhi, Rajagopalachari, Malaviya
- Dropped fearing "second-rate status" among Sikhs
- Conversion details:
- With his wife + about 5 lakh followers (mostly Mahars)
- Prescribed 22 vows
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes date the conversion to 1935 - that was only the Yeola declaration
- Navayana: His reinterpretation ("Neo-Buddhism"; CGPSC 2019: Neo-Buddhism = Ambedkar) → Dalit Buddhist Movement
### Universal angles
- Current relevance - SC ruling (24 March 2026): Justices P.K. Mishra and Manmohan
- Converting out of Hinduism, Sikhism or Buddhism = immediate loss of SC status under Clause 3 of the Constitution (SC) Order 1950
- Buddhists added in 1990; Sikhs in 1956
- Ambedkar's 1956 conversion called the most symbolically powerful in modern India (6 Apr 2026)
- Significance: Moved the anti-caste struggle from reform within Hinduism → rights, law and religious exit
