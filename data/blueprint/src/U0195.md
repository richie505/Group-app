### Jyotiba Phule and the Satyashodhak Samaj → life, aims, symbol
## 1. Jyotiba Phule
- Jyotiba Phule: An anti-caste reformer — belonged to the Mali community; worked for lower-caste upliftment; founded the Satyashodhak Samaj
- Jyotirao (Jyotiba) Phule (1827-1890), Maharashtra
- 1848: Girls' school in Pune
- Savitribai Phule: Headed the women's section — worked for lower-caste girls
## 2. Satyashodhak Samaj
- Satyashodhak Samaj: Worked for the upliftment of backward classes — the main beneficiaries were Shudras and Ati-Shudras
- Sought liberation from exploitation — opposed exploitation by Arya-Brahmins (a Satyashodhak objective)
- Founded: At Pune in 1873
- Deenbandhu: Spread the movement to remote parts of Maharashtra
- Works: Gulamgiri — criticized Brahmanical domination; Sarvjanik Satyadharm Pustak — advanced anti-caste thought
- Other Satyashodhak objectives:
- End caste and untouchability; break Brahmanical dominance and priestly monopoly over ritual
- Free lower castes from Brahmin priests and religious exploitation
- Education for women, Shudras, Ati-Shudras; social service
- Satyashodhak marriages without priests
## 3. Symbol
- Symbol: King Bali against the Brahmanical symbol of Rama (UPSC)
- Gulamgiri (Slavery, 1873): compared lower castes' condition to slavery
- ⚠ Phule symbol = King Bali; Justice Party symbol = Scales
### Justice Party → background, aim and social base
- Background:
- Brahmins = only about 3% of Madras Presidency population but held over 90% (90%+) of government jobs
- Non-Brahmins met at Madras in 1916 to organise
- Aim: Protect interests of non-Brahmins (PYQ) (APPSC 2008)
- End Brahmin dominance in jobs, education, politics and religion
- Reservation and representation in jobs, education and legislatures
- Social base: Non-Brahmin landed gentry, the intermediate landed castes (Andhra: Reddys, Kammas, Kapus) → secured the Communal G.O. (1921)
- Dalits: Built a parallel "Adi" movement
- Andhra Mahasabha (Vijayawada, 1917): Sought reserved seats for non-Brahmins
- Echoed by Ponaka Pattabhirama Reddy + Tripuraneni Ramaswamy Chowdary
- British support: Backed it partly to weaken Home Rule; party used against Home Rule and Gandhi's movements

### Periyar → Self-Respect Movement, journals, DK and DMK
## 1. Periyar's Congress years
- Posts: President and secretary of the provincial Congress; even then supported the Hindu Religious Endowments Act
- Broke with the Congress in 1925:
- Over non-Brahmin communal representation
- Caste segregation at the Cheranmadevi Gurukulam
## 2. Self-Respect Movement
- Self-Respect Movement (1925)
- Founder: E.V. Ramasamy Naicker (Periyar)
- Launched: 1925 (mid-1920s)
- Aims:
- Attack caste hierarchy, Brahmin priests and religion; reject Brahmanical religion and caste
- Self-respect marriages without priests
- Women's equality and property rights
- Journals: Kudi Arasu + Viduthalai; Revolt (English)
- In Andhra:
- Inspired Bhagya Reddy Varma and Jala Rangaswamy to adopt the "Adi" identity and demand temple entry
- Fed the Justice Party's non-Brahmin politics

### Phule & Satyashodhak Samaj
## Jyotiba Phule
- ★ Satyashodhak Samaj (Society of Truth Seekers) — Jyotiba Phule, 1873; for Dalits and lower castes
- ★ Born 1827, a gardener's son — Mali community
- Title 'Mahatma' — Vithalrao Krishnaji Vandekar, 1888
- Symbol — King Bali (the oppressed) against Rama
- Girls' school, Pune, 1848 — taught by Savitribai Phule, India's first woman teacher
## Works of Phule
| Work | Note |
| Gulamgiri (1873) | 'Slavery'; caste oppression compared with slavery |
| Shetkaryacha Asud | 'The Cultivator's Whipcord' |
| Sarvajanik Satyadharma Pustak | published posthumously 1891 |
| Tritiya Ratna, Brahmananche Kasab | also Phule |
## The Samaj
- Satyashodhak marriages — without priests; allowed inter-caste marriage
- Worked for Shudras and Ati-Shudras against Arya-Brahmin exploitation (not for Arya-Brahmins)
- Spread by Deenbandhu; women's section headed by Savitribai Phule
## Others
- SNDP Yogam — 1903; Depressed Classes Mission — 1906
- Vital-Vidhvansak (1888) — Gopal Baba Walangkar
### Justice Party
## Origins
- Founded by T.M. Nair, P. Theagaraya Chetty, C. Natesa Mudaliar; linked with the South India Peoples Association
- Non-Brahmin Manifesto, December 1916 — signed by P. Thyagaraya Chetty
- English paper 'Justice' — February 1917
- Symbol — Scales
- Brahmins about 3% of Madras Presidency population, over 90% of government jobs
## In power
- First Justice ministry, 17 December 1920 — CM A. Subbarayalu Reddiar
- ★ Panagal Raja (P. Ramarayaningar) — Premier 1921–1926, two ministries; died 16 December 1928
- K.V. Reddy Naidu (Eluru) — minister 1920; Premier 1937
- Formed government four times from 1920
- Communal G.O. of 1921 (not 1937) — proportional share in jobs and education for non-Brahmins; won by intermediate landed castes; forerunner of reservation
- Lost to the Congress in 1937 (no mass base, Andhra–Tamil splits, deaths of leaders)
- C. Natesa Mudaliar — criticised neglect of the poor and Harijans
## Andhra legacy
- Andhra University, 1926 — lasting Andhra legacy; first Chancellor Lord Goschen; Act pushed by Education Minister A.P. Patro; first HQ Bezawada
- Madras Hindu Religious Endowments Act — 1926; Annamalai University — 1929
- Separate Andhra resolution, 1922 — M. Suryanarayana; withdrawn when A.P. Patro urged
## Boyi Bhimanna's works
- Boyi Bhimanna — wrote Paleru, Gudiselu Kalipotunnai, Pilli Shatakam and Paleru Nundi Padmasri Daka
### Periyar's path
- Non-Brahmin movement — end Brahmin dominance in jobs, education, politics and religion
- 1925 — Periyar left the Congress over communal representation and the Cheranmadevi Gurukulam segregation
- Self-Respect Movement — 1925; self-respect marriages without priests
- Journals — Kudi Arasu, Viduthalai, Revolt (English)
- Anti-Hindi agitation 1937; Dravidistan demand 1940
- Dravidar Kazhagam — from the Justice Party, 1944
- DMK — C.N. Annadurai, 17 September 1949; in power 1967
- Order — Satyashodhak Samaj (1873) → SNDP Yogam (1903) → Justice Party (1916) → Self-Respect Movement (1925)
- Tripuraneni Ramaswamy Chowdary (Kavi Raju) song 'Veeragandhamu techinaramu...' — Civil Disobedience Movement
