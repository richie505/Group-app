### Parliament — Article 79 components and the President's place
## 1. Article 79 - components
- Art. 79: Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha
- Covered in Arts. 79-122, Part V
## 2. President's legislative powers
- Summons and prorogues Parliament; dissolves the Lok Sabha
- Addresses Parliament — Article 87
- Assent to Bills (Article 111): Absolute, suspensive and pocket vetoes
- Can return a Bill only once — except a Money Bill
- Nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha
- Ordinances (Article 123): When Parliament is not in session — co-extensive with Parliament's legislative power
- Why President is part of it:
- No bill becomes law without his assent
- Like the Crown-in-Parliament (UK) vs US President, separate from Congress
- Addresses: He does not sit in Parliament
- Art. 86: May address either House or both
- Art. 87: Special address to both Houses at the first session after each general election + first session of each year (2026)
- Exam angle: Assertion-Reason - "Parliament consists of the President and two Houses" with the opening-address reason (both true, R explains A).

### Rajya Sabha — composition (Article 80)
## 1. Rajya Sabha — electors and seats
- Nominated MLAs and Members of Legislative Councils cannot vote
- Seat allocation: Based on population — not allocated equally among the States
- Fourth Schedule; most seats: Uttar Pradesh — 31
## 2. Composition — Article 80
- Maximum strength: 250 — up to 238 elected representatives of States and Union Territories + 12 nominated by the President
- Present strength: 245 — 233 elected + 12 nominated (225 states + 8 UTs - Delhi, Puducherry, J&K + 12 nominated)
- Nominated for special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service
- Union Territories represented: Only three — Delhi, Puducherry, Jammu and Kashmir
- Election: Indirect — by the elected members of State Legislative Assemblies, by proportional representation through the Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV - proportional representation - single transferable vote, Art 80(4))
- UT members as Parliament provides by law - by an electoral college under Parliament's law (CDI)

### Lok Sabha term — Article 83(2), dissolution, extension
## 1. Term (duration) — Article 83
- Five years from the date of its first sitting; can be dissolved earlier by the President (Art 83(2))
- National Emergency: Parliament can extend the term one year at a time → by law, 1 year at a time; not beyond 6 months after the emergency ends
- 5th Lok Sabha (1971-77) extended during the 1975-77 Emergency
- Early dissolution: By the President on PM's advice → cannot be challenged in court
- Dissolved before full term: 4th (1970), 6th (1979), 9th (1991), 11th (1997), 12th (1999), 13th (2004)
- Count: 18 Lok Sabhas constituted so far (2026)
### Rajya Sabha permanence and member's term
## 1. Membership and term
- Term of a member: Six years; one-third retire every two years
- Permanent House: Not subject to dissolution — Article 83(1)
- Casual vacancy: Filled for the remainder of the term
## 2. Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha — term
| Point | Rajya Sabha | Lok Sabha |
| Dissolution | Not subject to dissolution - permanent/continuing House (Art. 83(1)); never dissolved | Dissolved by the President; term 5 years from the date of its first meeting (Art. 83(2)) |
| Rotation | About one-third retire every second year | Whole House elected together |
| Member's term | 6 years, fixed by the RPA 1951, not by the Constitution (CDI) | 5 years |
| Extension | - | During a National Emergency, by law, 1 year at a time, not beyond 6 months after the Emergency ends (IYB) |
- First Rajya Sabha (1952): President curtailed terms of initial members to start the one-third cycle → who retired decided by lottery
- ⚠ RS member's 6 years = RPA 1951, not the Constitution

### Qualifications — Article 84
## 1. Qualifications — Article 84
- Lok Sabha: Citizen of India; at least 25 years old; registered as a voter in a parliamentary constituency
- Rajya Sabha (Article 84): Citizen; minimum age 30 years; registered voter in any parliamentary constituency; no office of profit
- Not holding an office of profit; not of unsound mind; not an undischarged insolvent
- Other qualifications: prescribed by the Representation of the People Act
| Condition | Rajya Sabha | Lok Sabha |
| Minimum age (Art 84; IYB) | 30 years | 25 years |
| Oath | Before an EC-authorised person (Third Schedule form) | Before an EC-authorised person (Third Schedule form) |
| RPA 1951 | Registered elector in a parliamentary constituency | Elector in a parliamentary constituency; must be SC/ST to contest a reserved seat [GK] |

### Disqualifications — Articles 102, 103, Tenth Schedule
## 1. Disqualification
- Article 102 and the Representation of the People Act, 1951
- Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law) — decided by the Speaker; in the Rajya Sabha anti-defection questions decided by the Chairman
- Rajya Sabha disqualifications: Same as the Lok Sabha — Article 102, the RPA 1951 and the Tenth Schedule
- Art. 102 grounds:
- Office of profit under the Union/a state (exempted posts apart)
- Unsound mind declared by a court
- Disqualified by a law of Parliament (RPA 1951)
- Defection - Tenth Schedule
- Art. 103: President decides, after obtaining the Election Commission's opinion and acting on it
- Tenth Schedule (defection): Decided by the Speaker (LS) / Chairman (RS), not the President
- Subject to judicial review → Kihoto Hollohan (1992)
- Grounds: voluntarily giving up party membership; voting/abstaining against the whip without permission and not condoned within 15 days; independent joining a party; nominated member joining a party after 6 months
- ⚠ Art. 102 grounds → President (on EC opinion); defection → Speaker / Chairman
### Vacation of seats — Article 101
## 1. Seat becomes vacant on
- Resignation — addressed to the Speaker (Rajya Sabha: to the Chairman — the Vice-President)
- Disqualification
- Absence for 60 days without permission
- Becoming a member of the other House
- Member of both Houses: Must choose one within 10 days
## 2. Situations
| Situation | Result |
| Elected to both Houses | Intimate choice within 10 days, else the Rajya Sabha seat (RS seat) falls vacant |
| Sitting member of one House elected to the other | Seat in the first House falls vacant |
| Elected to two seats in one House | Must choose one, else both fall vacant |
| Elected to Parliament and a state legislature | MP must resign the state seat within 14 days, else the Parliament seat falls vacant (Prohibition of Simultaneous Membership Rules, 1950) |
| Absence for 60 days | Without permission → House may declare the seat vacant |
- Resignation: In writing to the Speaker/Chairman; subject to acceptance (Art 101(3)(b)), unlike judges or the President (22 Jul 2026)
- May be rejected if not voluntary or genuine → 33rd Amendment streamlined this
- ⚠ 10 days = both Houses; 14 days = Parliament + state legislature; 60 days = absence
### Constitutional time limits — fourteen, thirty and sixty days
## 1. Fourteen days
| Matter | Rule | Basis |
| Impeachment of the President | Prior notice of fourteen days; notice signed by at least one-fourth of the members | Article 61 |
| Money Bill in the Rajya Sabha | Must be returned within fourteen days — else deemed passed by the Rajya Sabha | Article 109 |
| Simultaneous membership of Parliament and a State Legislature | Resign the State Legislature seat within fourteen days — failure vacates the Parliament seat | Representation of the People Act, 1951 |
| Removal of presiding officers | Fourteen days' notice — the Speaker and the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha | Article 90(c) (Deputy Chairman) |
## 2. Thirty and sixty days
- Thirty days — Emergency during Lok Sabha dissolution (Article 352): The proclamation survives for thirty days from the first sitting of the reconstituted Lok Sabha, provided the Rajya Sabha has approved it
- Sixty days — unauthorised absence (Article 101(4)): An MP's seat may be declared vacant if absent from all House meetings for sixty days without permission
- Excluded from the calculation of continuous absence: periods of prorogation and adjournments exceeding four consecutive days

### Sessions and session terminology
## 1. Sessions and sittings
- Summoning: By the President — Article 85; maximum gap between two sessions six months
- Art. 85(1): President summons each House; Houses need not be summoned together
- Six-month gap (Art. 85): Keeps the government accountable + legislative business moving (PYQ APPSC-GS 2025); sets no minimum number of sitting days
- Session: From the first sitting of a House to its prorogation or dissolution — standard sessions: Budget, Monsoon, Winter
- Calendar: No fixed calendar; normally three sessions
- Sitting: A daily meeting of a House during a session — morning and post-lunch sittings
- Adjournment: Ends a daily sitting for a specified time — hours, days or weeks — by the Presiding Officer (Speaker or Chairman) [GK]
- Adjournment sine die: Ends a sitting without assigning a day for reassembly (without fixing a date); then the President prorogues (LENSD, 14 Aug 2026)
- Prorogation (Article 85(2)): By the President (or Governor) — terminates a session, not the House itself; usually after adjournment sine die (LENSD, 14 Aug 2026)
- Dissolution: By the President (or Governor) — ends the life of the House; applies only to the Lok Sabha (the Rajya Sabha is permanent) (CDI)
- Recess: Period between prorogation and the start of a new session (prorogation and reassembly in a new session, PYQ APPSC-G2 2017) — the President may issue ordinances (Article 123) during recess
- Termination of a sitting: By adjournment (or adjournment sine die), or by prorogation/dissolution

### Functions of Parliament — legislative to electoral
## 1. Rajya Sabha presiding officers
- Chairman: The Vice-President — ex officio, Articles 64 and 89(1); not a member of the Rajya Sabha
- Deputy Chairman: Chosen from among the sitting members of the Rajya Sabha — Article 89(2)
## 2. Functions
| Function | Content |
| Legislative | Laws on Union and Concurrent Lists (and State List in special cases); only Parliament can change an existing law (PYQ APPSC-GS 2024); delegated legislation is reviewed (APP; IYB) |
| Executive control | Questions, motions, no-confidence motion (LS); ministers collectively responsible (APP) |
| Financial | Budget, taxes, grants; no tax without LS approval (APP) |
| Constituent | Amends the Constitution under Art. 368, but cannot alter the basic structure (UPSC) |
| Judicial | Impeaches the President, removes the VP, recommends removal of judges, CEC, CAG; punishes breach of privilege (CDI) |
| Electoral | Elects the VP, takes part in the President's election, elects presiding officers (CDI) |
## 3. Electoral functions - detail
- President & VP: Elects the Vice-President (all members of both Houses); participates in electing the President (elected members only) (UPSC)
- Presiding officers:
- Lok Sabha elects the Speaker and Deputy Speaker
- Rajya Sabha elects only the Deputy Chairman (VP is ex officio Chairman) (UPSC)
- Election laws: RPA 1950 and 1951; Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952
### Parliamentary privileges — Basis - Art. 105
## 1. Basis - Art. 105
- Art. 105: Freedom of speech in Parliament; no court proceedings for anything said or any vote given in the House or its committees (immunity)
- Codification debate: Privileges are not codified → still rest on Art. 105(3) (originally the British House of Commons' privileges)
- Codification urged to protect press freedom and citizens' rights
