### Energy sources — definitions and types
## 1. Fossil fuels vs nuclear energy
| Basis | Fossil fuels | Nuclear energy |
| Examples | Coal, petroleum, natural gas | Uranium — a nuclear fuel, ⚠ not a fossil fuel |
| Renewable? | Non-renewable | Non-renewable |
| Origin — comes from | Buried organisms — solar energy originally stored in organisms; the ultimate source is the Sun | The nucleus of an atom — the nuclear source |
| Main process | Combustion — causes carbon dioxide emissions | Fission and fusion |
- Non-renewable: Coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium (nuclear); renewable: solar, wind, biomass, biogas, hydroelectric, tidal
- Non-conventional energy: Renewable energy; Biomass: Renewable organic material
- Fossil-fuel exhaustion causes an energy crisis
- Thermal power: A major power source in Chhattisgarh
- Electricity: Associated with sustainable development
- Photovoltaic technology: Converts solar energy into electricity
- National Solar Mission: Associated with NAPCC
## 2. Classes of energy sources
| Class | Sources | Key point |
| Renewable (replenished naturally; continuously replenished) | Solar, wind, hydro (water), geothermal, tidal, wave, biomass/biofuels | Cleaner; basis of the energy transition |
| Non-renewable (finite) | Fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas); nuclear fuel (uranium, thorium) | Fossil fuels formed over millions of years from organic matter; burning causes air pollution and CO2 |
- Petroleum products: Petrol (gasoline), diesel, kerosene, LPG, naphtha, bitumen, petrochemical feedstock for plastics
- Fossil fuels globally:
- ~75% of global GHGs; ~90% of CO2
- Global fossil subsidies $1.8 trillion (2024)
- APPSC 2025 keys:
- Solar energy = not a fossil fuel
- Wind = renewable
- Solar + wind = energy-transition sources
- Thermal plants run on fossil fuels
- Plastic toothbrushes + gasoline made from petroleum (non-renewable)
- ⚠ Nuclear fuel (uranium, thorium) = non-renewable, but not a fossil fuel
- Exam angle: "which is not a fossil fuel / which is renewable" one-liners.

- Petroleum, petrol, diesel and kerosene — all obtained from crude oil by fractional distillation
