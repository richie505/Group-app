### Theory of kingship
- Balban: Zil-i-Ilahi, the Shadow of God; Alauddin: kingship knows no kinship
| Sultan | Theory / claim |
| Iltutmish | Legitimacy via caliphal investiture (mansur, Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad, c.1229) |
| Balban | Zil-i-Ilahi + Niyabat-i-Khudai; sijda, paibos, Nauroz; "blood and iron" |
| Alauddin | State above ulema; "kingship knows no kinship"; Sikandar-i-Sani |
| Mubarak Shah Khalji | Proclaimed himself Caliph |
| MBT | Rationalist absolutism; investiture from Caliph in Egypt; "Al Sultan Zilli Allah" |
| Firoz | Respected the Caliph |
### The Sultan & the state
- Sultan = Caliph's representative; supreme commander + highest court of appeal
- No fixed succession: nobles, ulema, army decided
- Sharia for Muslims' civil matters; Hindus kept own law; zawabit = royal decrees
- Composite: Perso-Arabic + Turko-Mongol + Indian (JPSC 2021)

### Sultans → inscriptions, coins and relations with the Caliph
## 1. Inscriptions and coins
- Garhmukteshwar Inscription: Balban — at the Garhmukteshwar Mosque; Balban called himself "Helper of a Caliph"
- Mahmud Ghazni: Silver coins with Arabic on one side and Sanskrit — in Devanagari — on the other
- Muhammad Ghori: Coins with Goddess Lakshmi on one side and the Kalma in Arabic on the other
- Iltutmish: Placed the Caliph's name on coins after recognition by the Caliph
- Alauddin Khalji: Used the title Sikandar-i-Sani — inscribed it on coins
- Muhammad bin Tughluq: The coin legend "Al Sultan Zilli Allah" — Sultan as Shadow of God
## 2. Relations with the Caliphate
| Sultan | Relation with the Caliph |
| Iltutmish | Letter of investiture (mansur) from the Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad in c.1229 |
| Alauddin Khalji | Titles Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin (UPPSC 2023 key); did not seek investiture |
| Mubarak Shah Khalji | Proclaimed himself Caliph |
| MBT | Sought investiture from the Abbasid Caliph in Egypt |
| Firoz | Firoz respected the Caliph |
- ⚠ Baghdad Caliph = Iltutmish's investiture; Egypt Caliph = MBT's; Mubarak Shah Khalji = himself Caliph
- Exam angle: BPSC 2022 asked who "opposed the Khalifa's power". The key was "more than one of the above".
### Sultans → theory of kingship
## 1. The Sultan
- Basis: Sultans saw themselves as the Caliph's representatives
- Powers: Supreme in military, legal + political matters; highest court of appeal
- Succession: No fixed rule → rested on backing of nobles, ulema + army; whoever nobles + ulema accepted, or military strength
## 2. Theory of kingship
| Sultan | Theory |
| Iltutmish | Legitimacy through caliphal investiture |
| Balban | Zil-i-Ilahi (Shadow of God / God's shadow) with Niyabat-i-Khudai (divine deputyship); sijda and paibos; Nauroz; "blood and iron"; no low-born men in office [GK] |
| Alauddin | State interest above ulema and law; "kingship knows no kinship"; Sikandar-i-Sani[GK] (some notes: Sikander-i-Azam) |
| MBT | Rationalist absolutism; appointed non-Turks and low-born men [GK] |
### Features of the state — legal basis and nature
## 1. Legal basis
- Sharia: Covered Muslims' civil matters
- Hindus: Hindus kept their own personal law
- Criminal law: Criminal law followed the Sultans' rules
- Zawabit: Royal decrees supplemented the sharia
## 2. Nature of the state
- Composite: Composite of Perso-Arabic, Turko-Mongol and Indian elements (JPSC 2021)
- Perso-Arabic: title Sultan, Caliph's nominal authority, Persian court ritual (sijda, paibos, Nauroz)
- Turko-Mongol: army in decimal units (khan, malik, amir, sipahsalar); horse-based warfare with mounted archers
- Centralisation: Comparatively centralised / essentially a military, centralised state
- Sultan = supreme commander + highest court of appeal
### Central departments (diwans) → head, function, founding Sultan

| Diwan-i-Wizarat | Wazir | Finance and revenue; assisted by the Auditor-General and Accountant-General | From the start |

### Changes in the nobility — the Chalisa to the Lodis
## 1. Slave dynasty and the Chalisa
- Composition: Turkish monopoly
- Chalisa / Turkan-i-Chahalgani: Iltutmish created the Chalisa / Turkan-i-Chahalgani ("The Forty") [GK] = Turkish slave-nobles
- Made and unmade Sultans after him, including over Razia
- Balban, himself one of the Forty, destroyed it
- After Balban: Turkish nobles set aside his nominee Kai Khusrau → raised Kaiqubad → Jalaluddin Khalji took power in 1290
## 2. Later dynasties
| Dynasty | Change in the nobility |
| Khalji revolution (1290) | High office opened to non-Turks + Indian Muslims; Alauddin crushed Balbani + Jalali nobles; banned noble gatherings + spied on nobility |
| Tughlaq | MBT promoted foreigners + low-born men; Firoz made iqtas hereditary → strengthened nobles |
| Lodi | Afghan tribal nobility treated the Sultan as first among equals |
- ⚠ Iltutmish created the Forty; Balban (one of them) destroyed it
### Local and revenue officials → unit and role

| Shiq (division / district) | Shiqdar | In charge of a shiq |
| Pargana (group of villages) | Amil | Revenue officer of a pargana, in charge of revenue collection (UPSC 2019) |
| Village | Muqaddam / Chaudhari | Village headman (BPSC 2022). UPSC 2004 key: the chaudhari was the chief rural land-revenue official |
| - | Khut | Hereditary rural landholder; Alauddin abolished the khut's cess |

- ⚠ Trap: Office of Mir Bakshi did not exist under the Sultans; it is Mughal (UPSC 2019)
### Universal angles
- Significance: Sultanate diwans prefigured the Mughal Diwan, Mir Bakshi + Sadr-us-Sudur
- AP angle:
- Qutb Shahi Mir Jumla = finance; Ain-ul-Mulk = army
- Comparative note: Bahmani state (Majlis-i-Malakat, Wakil-us-Saltanat, Amir-i-Jumla) + Qutb Shahis (Peshwa, Mir Jumla, Dabir's Diwan-i-Insha) inherited the Delhi model

### Theory of kingship
## Balban
- ★ Balban took the title Zil-i-Ilahi (Shadow of God); also Niyabat-i-Khudai (representative / deputy of God)
- ★ Balban — policy of blood and iron; divine right of kings, autocratic Sultan; Sultan ranked next to the Paigambar
- ★ Balban's real name Baha-ud-din; title Ulugh Khan from Nasiruddin Mahmud; did not introduce the Iqtadari system
- Balban introduced sijda, paibos, Nauroz; Turkan-i-Chahalgani was created by Iltutmish
- Balban excluded low-born men from high office (appointing them — MBT)
- Din-i-Ilahi — Akbar; Tuti-i-Hind — Amir Khusrau
## Sultans and the Caliph
- ★ Iltutmish — received khillat from the Caliph of Baghdad (1229); Sultanate became part of Dar-ul-Islam
