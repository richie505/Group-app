# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:158 - World Trade Organization - structure, agreements (AoA, TRIPS, TRIMS, GATS), dispute settlement, India's issues
### Basics
- Marrakesh = WTO agreement; Uruguay = round that created it; Geneva = HQ
- Established 1 January 1995, Marrakesh Agreement, after Uruguay Round
- HQ Geneva; one-country-one-vote, consensus
- Members 166 (Mar 2026) (⚠ older notes: 164); latest Comoros, Timor-Leste (2024)
- FTAs allowed under GATT Article XXIV
### Rounds & Ministerials
- 1986-94 — Uruguay Round: WTO, TRIPS, GATS, AoA; Dunkel Draft (1991)
- 1996 — MC1 Singapore
- 1999, 2003 — Broke down: Seattle, Cancún
- 2001 — Doha Development Agenda; stalled
- 2022 — MC12 Geneva
- 2024 — MC13 Abu Dhabi
- Mar 2026 — MC14 Yaoundé, Cameroon
### Structure
- ▲ Ministerial Conference — highest body; meets at least once every two years
- ▸ General Council — runs WTO between MCs; also sits as DSB, TPRB
- ▸ DSB / TPRB — panels + Appellate Body; trade policy reviews
- ▸ Council for Trade in Goods — oversees GATT; agriculture, TBT, SPS
### Agreements
| Agreement | Subject |
| AoA | Market access, domestic support, export subsidies |
| TRIPS | Minimum IPR standards |
| TRIMS | Trade-distorting investment measures (local content) |

### TRIPS & India
- 1999 — GI Act enacted to meet TRIPS
- 2001 — Doha Declaration: public health first
- 2005 — Patents Act amended
- 2012 — First compulsory licence: Natco-Bayer
- 2020 / 2022 — India-South Africa COVID waiver; limited waiver at MC12

### Exam corner
- ⚠ Dunkel Draft = Uruguay Round (Arthur Dunkel), not Doha
- India's flexibilities: Section 3(d) + compulsory licensing
- Only new agreements: Trade Facilitation + Fisheries Subsidies
- MC14: TRIPS non-violation moratorium also lapsed; 66 members signed e-commerce pact
- DGTR: anti-dumping, countervailing, safeguards
### WTO — establishment, predecessor and FIRSTs
- Established: 1 January 1995 under Marrakesh Agreement, after Uruguay Round
- Predecessor: Replaced GATT (1947/48) system; GATT survives as integral WTO agreement
- Decision-making: One-country-one-vote, consensus-based
- Membership: 166 (Mar 2026) (older notes say 164)
- Latest members: Comoros and Timor-Leste (2024)
- Wider scope: Beyond goods to services (GATS) + intellectual property (TRIPS) + rules-based dispute mechanism
- FTAs: Allowed as MFN exceptions under GATT Article XXIV if they cover "substantially all trade"
- ⚠ Marrakesh = WTO agreement; Uruguay = round that created it; Geneva = HQ

### India's issues and debates
| Issue | Status / India's stand |

| Fisheries Phase 2 | Phase 2 (overcapacity, overfishing) unresolved at MC14 → deferred to General Council |
| SDT | US wants to deny special and differential treatment to China, India, Brazil, Indonesia; India defends it |

### Universal angles
- Current relevance: MC14 Yaoundé (Mar 2026) - e-commerce + TRIPS non-violation moratoria lapsed; no PSH or fisheries Phase 2 deal (Apr 2026)
- Criticism & debates: Consensus paralysis + Appellate Body crisis push countries to FTAs
- Significance: One-country-one-vote gives developing countries bargaining space

### TRIPS & India
- GI Act, 1999 — passed for TRIPS duties; Patents Act amended 2005 for the same
- Doha Declaration (2001) on TRIPS and public health — TRIPS must not prevent members protecting public health
- COVID-19 TRIPS waiver — proposed (2020) by India and South Africa (not Brazil)
- MC12 (Geneva, 2022) — limited waiver for vaccine patents
### India's issues
- MEIS/SEZ export subsidy dispute — brought by the USA (not the EU); panel 2019
- Special and Differential Treatment — US wants to deny it to China, India, Brazil, Indonesia; India defends SDT
- India's main food-security demand — permanent solution for public stockholding
- India opposes Investment Facilitation for Development (plurilateral, 120+ members) in the WTO rulebook
- MC14 (Yaoundé, March 2026) — no permanent PSH solution; both moratoria ended; no fisheries Phase 2 deal
- Moratorium on customs duties on electronic transmissions — ended 31 March 2026; 66 members then signed a plurilateral agreement banning such duties
- JNNSM solar domestic-content rules — challenged by the USA at the WTO; India lost in 2016
### Exam corner
- Only new WTO agreements in its first 30 years — Trade Facilitation Agreement and Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies
- Section 3(d), Patents Act — against evergreening of patents; with compulsory licensing, a TRIPS flexibility
