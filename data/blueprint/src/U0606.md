### Humidity terms → meaning; dew point; AP humidity data
## 1. Humidity
- Absolute humidity: The actual mass of water vapour per unit volume
- Relative humidity: Actual moisture divided by moisture-holding capacity, multiplied by 100
- Warm air can hold more water vapour; cold air can hold less
- Dew point: The temperature at which air becomes saturated
- Condensation: Changes water vapour into liquid or solid form; clouds form through condensation around nuclei at altitude
## 2. Measures of humidity
| Measure | Definition [GK] |
| Absolute humidity | Actual water vapour per unit volume of air (g/m3) |
| Specific humidity | Grams of water vapour per kg (unit weight) of air (g/kg); unaffected by volume changes |
| Relative humidity | Actual vapour as a % of what the air can hold at that temperature |
| Dew point | Temperature at which air becomes saturated and condensation begins |
| Latent heat | Taken in during evaporation, released on condensation → powers thunderstorms and cyclones |
- ⚠ Absolute = per volume (g/m3); specific = per kg (g/kg)
- Relative humidity: RH falls when temperature rises, rises on cooling (same vapour) → warm air holds more vapour
- Higher over oceans, lower over continents
- Saturation: RH = 100% = saturation
## 3. Distribution
- Water vapour: Up to 4% by volume in warm, wet (humid) tropics; under 1% in deserts and polar areas
- Falls with latitude and with altitude
## 4. AP
- Coastal Andhra: RH 70-90% year-round (high heat index)
- Rayalaseema: Often below 30% in peak summer
### Latent heat and the uplift chain → condensation
## 1. Latent heat
| Change | Latent heat | Note |
| Fusion of ice | 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg — 80 cal/g | Melting point 0°C at 1 atmosphere; temperature constant during melting |
| Vaporisation of water | 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg — 540 cal/g | Boiling point 100°C at 1 atmosphere; temperature constant during boiling; boiling point increases with pressure |
- Ice cools: Its high latent heat of fusion absorbs a large amount of heat
- Steam burns more severely than boiling water: High latent heat of vaporisation
- Refrigeration: Uses latent heat absorbed during evaporation
- Clouds and rain: Latent heat released during condensation
## 2. The uplift chain
- Uplift at a front forces air to rise — the uplift of warm air produces cooling
- Adiabatic cooling causes temperature to fall to the dew point — condensation begins when air cools to the dew point
- Condensation changes water vapour into liquid or ice particles — cloud formation results — clouds and precipitation
- Precipitation occurs when droplets grow and fall — rain, snow or sleet
- Final impact: Changes in wind, temperature, humidity and pressure; in weather systems and climatic patterns
### Hydrological cycle — processes; hydrometeorology; India water budget data
## 1. Processes
- In the cycle:
- Evaporation + transpiration (together evapotranspiration)
- Sublimation, condensation, precipitation
- Infiltration/percolation, surface runoff, groundwater flow
- Not in the cycle (distractors): Photosynthesis, combustion, respiration → biological/other processes
## 2. Hydrometeorology
- Definition: Study of transfer of water and energy between the land surface and the lower atmosphere → rainfall, evaporation, floods, droughts
## 3. India's water budget (2026) - data

| Precipitation | ≈ 4,000 BCM a year |
| Utilisable | 1,139 BCM (690 surface + 449 groundwater) |

### Forms of condensation and precipitation → condition; hailstorms
## 1. Hailstorms
- Precipitation as ice pellets — hailstones — formed in strong thunderstorms
- Formation: Strong updrafts in cumulonimbus clouds freeze raindrops; repeated layering increases hailstone size
## 2. Forms → condition
| Form | Condition / note |
| Dew | Moisture condenses on cool surfaces, dew point above 0°C |
| Frost | Dew point at or below 0 °C; forms as ice crystals (Lambasingi, "Kashmir of AP", sees frost at ~2 °C) |
| Fog | Cloud at ground level; air cooled below dew point; often under inversion; visibility under 1 km |
| Mist | Like fog but thinner, wetter (more moisture) and better visibility - over 1 km |
| Smog | Fog + smoke/pollutants; London smog: sulphurous, cool and humid; Los Angeles smog: photochemical, needs sunlight, forms PAN, carries ground-level ozone (UPSC) |
| Rain | Drops over 0.5 mm |
| Drizzle | Fine drops under 0.5 mm, from stratus |
| Snow | Condensation below 0 °C as ice crystals |
| Sleet | Rain freezing into ice pellets as it falls through a cold layer / cold air |
| Hail | Layered ice balls from cumulonimbus with strong updrafts |

- ⚠ London smog = sulphurous; Los Angeles smog = photochemical

### Optical phenomena → cause; rainbow
## 1. Rainbow
- Phenomena: Dispersion, total internal reflection and refraction in water drops
- Sunlight enters a water drop and disperses → TIR at the back of the drop → the dispersed light refracts out
- Primary rainbow: One TIR — red outermost (outer side), violet innermost (inner side)
- Secondary rainbow: Two TIRs — reversed colours; fainter
- The Sun is behind the observer; water drops in front
## 2. Optical phenomena → cause
| Phenomenon | Cause |
| Halo (sun/moon) | Ring of light from sunlight refracting through millions of hexagonal ice crystals in thin high (cirrostratus) cloud; typically a ring of about 22° radius (APPSC-G1 2024 key: statements I and II) |
| Rainbow | Refraction, dispersion and internal reflection of sunlight in raindrops; seen with the sun behind the observer [GK] |
| Corona | Diffraction by small water droplets of thin clouds - coloured rings close to the sun/moon [GK] |
- ⚠ Halo = refraction through ice crystals; corona = diffraction by water droplets
### Precipitation types → mechanism and place; barriers; rain shadow; AP rainfall
## 1. Types of precipitation
| Type | Mechanism | Typical location |
| Convectional | Strong heating, rising air, cooling and condensation; strong / intense heating → air rises, cools, condenses → cumulonimbus; afternoon downpours / thunder showers | The Western Ghats — the example location given |
| Cyclonic or frontal | Convergence or frontal uplift, rising air, cooling and condensation; warm air lifted over cold air along fronts, or rises in a cyclone's low / tropical cyclones | Temperate regions (temperate belts - frontal); Bay of Bengal cyclones / tropical cyclones on India's east coast; western disturbances give 10-15 cm to NW India for rabi |
| Relief-induced / orographic (relief) | Topography modifies rainfall distribution; moist wind forced up a mountain / barrier; heavy on windward side, dry on leeward side in rain shadow | Differs between windward and leeward locations; Western Ghats (Agumbe, Hulikal wettest); Mawsynram; Eastern Ghats in north coastal AP |

- Interior Deccan: Low rainfall because of the Western Ghats rain shadow
- Rain shadow: Leeward side of a range → descending air warms and dries
- South-west monsoon strikes the Western Ghats → Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP and Tamil Nadu interiors lie in its rain shadow
- Eastern side of the Western Ghats gets scanty rain; peninsular rainfall falls west → east

### Cloud seeding, acid rain and cloudbursts — chemicals, coiner, thresholds
## 1. Cloudburst
- Orographic lifting: Warm, moist air forced to rise rapidly over steep mountain slopes — the main trigger mechanism
- Atmospheric instability: Strong convection and towering cumulonimbus clouds
- High moisture in monsoon air masses
- Sudden release: Updrafts weaken and the accumulated water falls intensively
- Cloudburst: About 100 mm/hour or more over 20-30 sq km
- From cumulonimbus: orographic lifting in the Himalaya builds towering cumulonimbus → cloudbursts
- Usually June-August at 1,000-2,500 m in the Himalayas
- Example: Dharali, Uttarkashi (Aug 2025)
## 2. Cloud seeding
- Chemical: Silver iodide (AgI) → crystal structure mimics ice
- Others: Potassium iodide, sodium chloride (common salt), dry ice, liquid propane → act as condensation nuclei
- Limit: Works only when suitable supercooled / moist clouds already exist (Jul and Oct 2025)
- Kazakhstan: Began cloud seeding with UAE help for drought-hit Turkestan (13 Aug 2026)
## 3. Acid rain
- Acid rain: Precipitation with pH below 5.6, from SO2 and NOx
- Term coined by Robert Angus Smith (1872)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:17
### Humidity terms
## Measures of moisture
- Specific humidity — water vapour per unit weight of air (g/kg); not changed when air volume changes
- Absolute humidity — water vapour per unit volume (g/m³)
- Air is saturated at relative humidity of 100 per cent
- Dew point — temperature at which air becomes saturated; condensation starts (vapour → liquid or ice)
- Same vapour content + temperature rises → relative humidity falls (warm air holds more vapour); rises on cooling
- Water vapour decreases with height; up to about 4 per cent in humid tropics, under 1 per cent in deserts and polar areas
- Isobar — equal atmospheric pressure; isotherm — equal temperature
## Latent heat
- Evaporation takes latent heat; condensation releases it — drives thunderstorms and cyclones
- Latent heat of vaporisation of water — 540 cal/g (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg); latent heat of fusion of ice — 80 cal/g
- Steam burns worse than boiling water — latent heat released on condensing
- Refrigerator refrigerant — absorbs heat on evaporation (inside), releases it on condensation (at the back)
- Higher pressure → boiling point increases (pressure cooker cooks faster); lower pressure at altitude lowers it
### Uplift chain
- ★ Clouds come from condensation — mass of tiny water droplets or ice crystals
- Condensation — water vapour changing into liquid or ice particles
- Precipitation — fall of grown droplets as rain, snow or sleet
- Chain: uplift of warm air → cooling → condensation → clouds and precipitation
- Rising air cools adiabatically to the dew point, where condensation begins
- Corona — coloured rings close to Sun or Moon; diffraction by small water droplets in thin clouds
### Clouds
## Cloud types
| Cloud | Feature |
| Cirrus | feathery; high, fair-weather cloud |
| Cirrocumulus | ripples |
| Cirrostratus | thin milky sheet; causes halo; high-altitude |
| Cumulus | heaped cauliflower tops, flat bases; fair weather; convective; can grow into cumulonimbus |

## Related phenomena
- Waterspout — funnel from a cumulus or cumulonimbus cloud over a water body

- Western disturbances — about 10–15 cm winter precipitation in north-western India; useful for rabi crops
- Mango showers — pre-monsoon convectional rain; Mawsynram — windward side (orographic rain)
### Forms of condensation
## Dew, frost, fog, smog
- Dew — liquid moisture on cool surfaces, dew point above 0°C; frost — ice crystals, dew point at or below 0°C
- Dew and frost helped by clear skies, calm air, high relative humidity and long cold nights; clouds and wind stop them
- Fog — ground-level cloud, visibility under 1 km; mist thinner and wetter, visibility over 1 km
- Los Angeles smog — photochemical (needs sunlight, can form PAN); London smog — sulphurous, cool and humid
## Precipitation forms
- Hail — layered ice balls in cumulonimbus with strong updrafts
- Sleet — rain frozen into pellets; snow — ice crystals formed below 0°C
- Rain — drops over 0.5 mm; drizzle — fine drops under 0.5 mm, often from stratus clouds
## Water budget and rainfall
- Combustion is not a stage of the hydrological cycle; evapotranspiration, sublimation, groundwater flow are
- India — about 4,000 BCM yearly precipitation; about 1,139 BCM usable (690 BCM surface, 449 BCM groundwater)
- Wet-bulb temperature above about 31.5°C — body cannot cool by sweating; wet-bulb = dry-bulb only at 100% RH
- AP average annual rainfall about 966 mm — about two-thirds from South-West monsoon, one-third from North-East monsoon
- Eastern side of Western Ghats — leeward, rain shadow, sinking warm dry air; peninsular rainfall falls west to east
### Precipitation types
- Convectional — mango showers, daily equatorial afternoon showers
- Frontal — warm air lifted over cold air along a front; typical of temperate regions
- Relief (orographic) — contrast between windward and leeward sides
- Rain-shadow area — the dry leeward side of a mountain range
- Aravallis — little orographic rain in Rajasthan; winds run roughly parallel to them
- Heavy rain: stored water falls when updrafts weaken
### Optical phenomena & extremes
## Optics
- Sun halo — ring of about 22°; refraction through hexagonal ice crystals in high cirrostratus
- Rainbow — Sun behind the observer, drops in front; refraction, dispersion and internal reflection
- Secondary rainbow — two reflections per drop; colours reversed, fainter (primary: one)
- Primary rainbow — red outside, violet inside

- Acid rain — pH below 5.6; term coined by Robert Angus Smith (1872); mainly from SO2 and NOx
- Cloudburst — about 100 mm per hour or more over 20–30 sq km; usually from cumulonimbus
