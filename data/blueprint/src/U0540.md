# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:96 - Goods and Services Tax - structure, slabs, GST Council, IGST settlement, compensation cess
### Road to GST
- Destination principle: revenue accrues where goods or services are consumed, not where produced
- 2004 — Vijay Kelkar task force: first recommends national GST
- 2008 — Empowered Committee roadmap
- Mar 2011 — 115th Amendment Bill (lapsed)
- Dec 2014 — 122nd Amendment Bill tabled
- 8 Sep 2016 — 101st Amendment Act, 2016; Assam first to ratify
- 1 July 2017 — Rollout at midnight session
- 22 Sep 2025 — GST 2.0 in effect
### Key Articles
| Article | Content |
| 246A | Parliament + States make GST laws; Parliament alone for inter-State |
| 269A | IGST apportionment |
| 279A | GST Council |
| 268A | Service tax: omitted |
### GST Council
- Constitutional body (never statutory; APPSC G1 2024)
- Chair: Union Finance Minister; formed September 2016
- Centre 1/3 votes; States 2/3
- Decision: 3/4 majority; quorum one-half
- Mohit Minerals (2022): recommendations persuasive, not binding
### Components
| Tax | Administered by | Supply |
| IGST | Centre (Art. 269A) | Inter-State and imports |
### Subsumed vs outside
- Subsumed
- 8 Central + 9 State taxes
- Excise, service tax, CVD, SAD
- State VAT, entry tax, octroi
- Not subsumed
- Basic Customs Duty
- Stamp duty, electricity duty
- Outside GST
- Alcoholic liquor (excluded by Constitution)
- Crude, petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas: await Council date
### Rates & mechanisms
- GST 2.0 (56th Council): 5% and 18% + 40% sin goods
- Ceilings in Acts: CGST 20%, IGST 40%
- ITC ends cascading; RCM: recipient pays
- Zero-rated (exports) = full ITC refund; nil/exempt = no ITC
- E-way bill > ₹50,000; GSTIN 15-digit
- Composition: up to ₹1.5 crore
### Compensation cess
- 2017 — 5-year compensation at 14% growth over 2015-16
- June 2022 — Compensation ended; cess to repay loans
- 22 Sep 2025 — Scrapped except tobacco
- 31 Mar 2026 — Cess ended; Health Security se National Security Cess
### Exam corner
- Bill 122nd vs Act 101st
- Council = 279A, not 269A (APPSC G2 2025)
- Canadian dual model; RNR: Arvind Subramanian (2015)
- Assam first to ratify, not implement
- Petrol, liquor, electricity outside → "All of the above" (APPSC-G2 2025)

### Committees and persons → GST role; Revenue Neutral Rate
## 1. Revenue Neutral Rate
- RNR: Revenue Neutral Rate — a rate or structure intended to generate approximately the same revenue under GST as under the earlier tax system (rate keeping revenue = pre-GST level)
- Its calculation is subject to the assumptions used
- Arvind Subramanian committee (2015); some notes date it 2018
## 2. Committees and persons
| Body | Chair | Role |
| Kelkar Task Force (FRBM implementation, 2004) | Vijay Kelkar | First recommended a comprehensive national GST (APP; CDI dates the roadmap 2002) |
| Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers (2000) | Asim Dasgupta (first chair) [GK] | "A Model and Roadmap for GST" (Apr 2008); First Discussion Paper (Nov 2009) |
| Revenue Neutral Rate Committee (2015) | Arvind Subramanian | RNR ~15-15.5%, standard rate 17-18% [GK] (CDI lists it as 2018) |
### Constitution (101st Amendment) — Articles → content
## 1. Bill to Act
- Bill vs Act: 122nd Amendment Bill (tabled Dec 2014) → 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016
- Presidential assent: 8 September 2016
- Exam angle: Bill number (122nd) vs Act number (101st) is the classic trap.
- Ratification: Federal-provision amendment → needed at least half the State legislatures
## 2. Articles inserted, omitted and amended
| Article | Content |
| 246A | Parliament and State legislatures both make GST laws (concurrent GST power); Parliament alone for inter-State supply |
| 269A | IGST on inter-State trade levied by the Union, apportioned between Centre and States on Council advice |
| 279A | GST Council, chaired by Union FM |
- Omitted: Art. 268A (service tax)
- ⚠ Council = Article 279A (not 269A - APPSC G2 2025); 269A = IGST
### GST Council — composition, voting and functions
## 1. GST Council
- A constitutional body — Article 279A
- Created by the Constitution; never a statutory body (APPSC G1 2024)
- Formed by the President in September 2016
- Chairperson: The Union Finance Minister
- Members: The Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance; the Minister in charge of Finance or Taxation from each State (and each UT with legislature) (UPSC)
- Vice-Chairperson: Chosen by State members from among themselves
- Makes recommendations on tax rates, exemptions, threshold limits and other GST-related matters — also model laws, revenue sharing, dispute resolution
- Legislative powers: Parliament and State Legislatures have GST legislative powers; the GST Council makes recommendations
- Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 and G1 2024 tested "269A vs 279A" and "statutory then constitutional" - both statements are false.
## 2. Voting and working
| Voting rule | Value |
| Quorum | quorum = one-half of total members [GK] |
| Centre's weight | 1/3 of votes cast |
| All States together | 2/3 of votes cast |
| Decision | 3/4 majority of weighted votes of members present and voting |
## 3. Binding nature
- Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022): Recommendations persuasive, not binding on Union and States
- Parliament and State legislatures have simultaneous power to legislate on GST
### GST — basics, objectives and nature
## 1. Basics
- GST: Goods and Services Tax — an indirect tax; a multi-stage, destination-based tax
- Introduced through the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016; implemented on 1 July 2017
- Basic principle: Taxation on the supply of goods and services — taxable event: Supply
- Major objectives: A common or unified national market; reduce cascading of taxes
- Destination principle: Revenue ultimately accrues to the jurisdiction where goods or services are consumed — ⚠ not where they are produced
## 2. Objectives and nature (Prep)
- Objectives:
- Replace complex, fragmented structure with a single destination-based tax on value addition = "one nation, one tax" (APPSC 2024)
- End cascading (tax on tax)
- One nation-one market; transparency and compliance (APPSC-GS 2024 answer)
- Nature: Multi-stage, destination-based consumption tax on every value addition
### Taxes subsumed vs retained
- Taxes subsumed: Central Excise Duty, Service Tax, State VAT and various other Central and State indirect taxes
| Subsumed - Central | Subsumed - State (State taxes subsumed) |
| Central Excise Duty, Additional Excise duties | State VAT / Sales Tax, Central Sales Tax |
| Service Tax | Purchase Tax, Luxury Tax, Entry Tax (all forms), Octroi |
| Additional Customs Duty (CVD), Special Additional Duty (SAD) on imports | Entertainment Tax (except by local bodies) |
| Central cesses and surcharges on supply (e.g., KKC, SBC) | Taxes on advertisements, lotteries, betting, gambling |
- Subsumed: 8 Central + 9 State taxes (UPSC)
- "Declared goods of special importance" concept dropped
- Not subsumed: Basic Customs Duty, stamp duty, electricity duty, motor vehicle tax, property tax, alcoholic liquor (State excise), petroleum products
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes list stamp duty as "merged" - it was not
### Items outside GST
- Outside GST:
- Alcoholic liquor for human consumption (alcohol for human consumption)
- Five petroleum products: crude, petrol (motor spirit), high-speed diesel, natural gas, ATF (UPSC) = crude, petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas
- Petroleum: Constitutionally within GST; levied only from a date the GST Council recommends
- ⚠ Petroleum = in the Constitution, waiting for Council date; alcohol = excluded by the Constitution
- PYQ: APPSC-G2 2025 - no GST on petrol/diesel, liquor, electricity to consumers, services of charitable entities → "All of the above"
### Structure and slabs — CGST, SGST, IGST, GST 2.0
## 1. Components
| Component | Full form | Administered by | Levied on / supply |
| CGST | Central Goods and Services Tax | The Centre — administers and collects | Intra-State |
| SGST | State Goods and Services Tax | States | Intra-State (equal halves with CGST) |
| IGST | Integrated Goods and Services Tax | The Centre — administers and collects; apportioned under Art. 269A | Inter-State and imports; revenue goes to destination State |
| UTGST | Union Territory Goods and Services Tax | — (Centre, for UTs without legislature) | Intra-UT |
| Compensation cess | - | - | Luxury/sin goods (for 5-year State compensation at 14% growth over 2015-16) |
- IGST: Revenue settles to the destination (consuming) State; only Parliament legislates on IGST
## 2. Slabs — GST 2.0
- GST 2.0: Old slabs 5, 12, 18, 28% merged into 5% (merit) and 18% (standard) + 40% for sin/ultra-luxury goods
- Approved at 56th Council (3-4 Sep 2025); effective 22 Sep 2025
## 3. Rate-change procedure
- Procedure: Rates changed by government notification on GST Council recommendation, not by amending the Acts
- Ceilings in the Acts: CGST 20%; IGST 40%
### GST terms and mechanisms → meaning — Mechanisms
| Mechanism | Meaning |
| Input Tax Credit (ITC) | Credit for tax paid on inputs set off against output tax; ends cascading (tax on tax); blocked-ITC rules apply |
| Reverse Charge (RCM) | Recipient, not supplier, pays GST on specified supplies |
| Anti-profiteering (Sec 171) | Rate-cut/ITC benefit must reach consumers; National Anti-Profiteering Authority replaced - cases go to Principal Bench of GSTAT from 1 Oct 2024 |
| Destination principle | Revenue accrues to the State of consumption |
| Composition scheme | Turnover up to ₹1.5 cr / ₹1.5 crore (₹50 lakh services); flat 1% goods, 6% services; no ITC |
- Anti-profiteering (Sec. 171):
- National Anti-Profiteering Authority → CCI (Dec 2022)
- From 1 Oct 2024: Principal Bench of GSTAT handles all cases
- Inverted duty structure: Refunds allowed
### GST terms and mechanisms → meaning — Exempt vs nil vs zero-rated
## 1. Exempt vs nil vs zero-rated
| Term | Meaning |
| Nil-rated | 0% rate; no ITC |
| Exempt | Notified out of tax; no ITC |
| Zero-rated | Exports, SEZ supplies → full ITC refund |
- Key contrast: Nil-rated/exempt = no ITC vs zero-rated = full refund
- ITC rule: Not available on exempt or non-taxable supplies
- GST 2.0: Exemption on individual life and health insurance premiums; rare-disease medicines fully exempt (Jun 2026)
## 2. GSTN, e-way bill and e-invoicing
| Term | Meaning |
| E-way bill | Electronic document for moving goods worth > ₹50,000; valid 1 day per 200 km |
| E-invoicing | Digital reporting of invoices → curb fake invoices + verify ITC; HSN/SAC codes |
- Budget 2026-27: Automated registration within 3 working days for low-risk applicants (tax liability up to ₹2.5 lakh/month)
### GST compensation — Act, cess, timeline
- Compensation: Ended June 2022; cess continued to repay loans, now only on tobacco
- Compensation cess timeline:
- Levy extended to March 2026 to repay loans taken to pay compensation
- Scrapped 22 Sep 2025 for all goods except tobacco
- Compensation cess ended 31 March 2026
- Replacement for sin goods (from 1 Feb 2026):
- Higher excise on tobacco under Central Excise (Amendment) Act, 2025
- Health Security se National Security Cess on pan masala units
- Levied on machine production capacity; credited to CFI; not shared with States
- Not in your sources: what happens to any balance left in the compensation cess fund after loan repayment.

- India's GST: first proposed under Vajpayee government; follows Kelkar Task Force; Canadian dual model

- Presidential assent: 8 September 2016

### Key Articles
| Article | Content |
| 246A | GST law-making power: Parliament and States concurrently; Parliament alone for inter-State supply |
| 269A | inter-State GST |
| 279A | GST Council — constituted by the President |
- Input Tax Credit — tax on inputs set off against output tax; stops cascading
- E-invoicing — digital reporting of invoices; stops fake invoices; checks ITC
### GST Council
## Constitution
- Article 279A added by 101st Amendment (which omitted Article 268A)
- Approval by at least half of the State Legislatures (touched federal distribution of powers)
- Constitutional body, never statutory — Mohit Minerals (2022) did not hold it statutory
- Chaired by Union Finance Minister; MoS Finance only a member
## Working
- Quorum: one-half of total members
- Voting: Centre one-third, all States two-thirds; decisions by three-fourths weighted majority of present and voting
- Anti-profiteering: NAA → CCI (December 2022) → GSTAT Principal Bench (from 1 October 2024)
- Outside GST: alcoholic liquor for human consumption, electricity, stamp-duty real estate transactions, ATF
### Components
- GST — multi-stage, destination-based tax on value addition; taxable event supply (not manufacture)
- IGST revenue settled to the destination (consuming) State
- Centre collects CGST and IGST; States collect SGST; GST Council recommends
- AP's largest own tax source (2024-25 RE): SGST — about ₹36,296 crore (38%); State excise (22%) second
### Subsumed vs outside
- Stamp duty — not merged; purchase, luxury and entry taxes merged
- Merged: 8 Central and 9 State taxes (usual count)
- GST replaced indirect taxes (excise, service tax, State VAT), not direct taxes
- Alcoholic liquor for human consumption — constitutionally excluded
- Five petroleum products — within GST constitutionally but not yet levied: crude oil, petrol, high-speed diesel, natural gas, ATF (not lubricating oil)
- Biggest indirect-tax reform since Independence — unified framework for many indirect taxes
- Ends "tax on tax" — cascading of taxes; makes one national market
### Rates & mechanisms
## Rates
- RNR Committee (Arvind Subramanian): RNR 15–15.5%, standard rate 17–18%
- Ceilings: CGST 20%, IGST 40%
- Rates changed by government notification on GST Council advice, within statutory ceilings
- 56th GST Council meeting: 5% merit, 18% standard, 40% de-merit/sin
## Mechanisms
- Zero-rated (exports, SEZ supplies) — full ITC refund; nil-rated — no ITC
- Reverse Charge — liability moves from supplier to recipient
- Composition scheme: goods ₹1.5 crore (1%), services ₹50 lakh (6%); no ITC
- E-way bill — goods above ₹50,000; valid one day per 200 km
- Destination-based — revenue goes where goods/services are consumed
### Compensation cess
- GST 2.0 — exempts individual life and health insurance premiums (not every insurance service)
- Compensation cess withdrawn from 22 September 2025 except on tobacco; levy extended to March 2026 to repay compensation loans
- From 1 February 2026: higher excise on tobacco (Central Excise (Amendment) Act, 2025); Health Security se National Security Cess on pan masala units
- charged on machine production capacity; money to Consolidated Fund of India, not shared with States
- Compensation: five years at 14% annual growth over 2015-16 base; ended June 2022
