### East- vs west-flowing
- East-flowing = deltas + irrigation; west-flowing = estuaries + hydropower
| Feature | East-flowing | West-flowing |
| Sea | Bay of Bengal | Arabian Sea |
| Rivers | Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Pennar, Vamsadhara | Narmada, Tapi, Sabarmati, Mahi, Periyar |
| Mouth | Large deltas | Estuaries → natural harbours |
| Use | Irrigation | Hydropower |

### River types — Himalayan vs peninsular; east- vs west-flowing
| Feature | Himalayan rivers | Peninsular rivers |
| Regime / flow | Perennial (rain + snowmelt) | Seasonal, rain-fed (monsoon-fed) |
| Valley | Deep antecedent gorges | Broad, shallow, graded valleys; older |
| Course | Meandering, shifting | Fixed, straight courses (hard rock) |
| Mouth | Large deltas | Deltas (east) / estuaries (west) |
| Use | Irrigation and navigation | Hydropower; delta irrigation (east-flowing) |
- Antecedent rivers: Rise in the trans-Himalaya; kept their course by cutting gorges as the ranges rose
- Examples: Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra
| Feature | East-flowing (Bay of Bengal) | West-flowing (Arabian Sea) |
| Examples | Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Pennar, Palar, Vamsadhara, Nagavali | Narmada, Tapi, Sabarmati, Mahi, Periyar, Mandovi, Sharavati |
| Mouth | Large deltas | Estuaries, not deltas → natural harbours on the west coast |
| Use | Irrigation | Hydropower |
- ⚠ East-flowing = deltas + irrigation; west-flowing = estuaries + hydropower
### Narmada, Tapi and west-flowing rivers
## 1. Narmada and Tapi
- Narmada: Originates at Amarkantak in the Maikal Range — flows west through a rift valley to the Arabian Sea; the Narmada Valley lies between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges
- Jabalpur and Bhedaghat are situated on it; Dhuandhar Falls and Kapildhara Falls (Anuppur district, Madhya Pradesh) are associated with it
- Tributary: Tawa
- Projects: Sardar Sarovar, Indira Sagar, Omkareshwar, Bargi
- Tapi: Originates at Multai in Betul district, Madhya Pradesh — flows through Surat, Gujarat; flows westward to the Arabian Sea
- Flows between the Satpura and Ajanta ranges; mouth at Suvali near Surat
- Rarity: Tapi = one of only three peninsular rivers running east to west
## 2. West coast drainage
- Count: About 600 small rivers drain the west coast
- Aravalli-born rivers: (VIS 2026)
- West: Luni, Sabarmati
- Periyar: Kerala; Mullaperiyar dam [GK]
### Mahi, Luni and other rivers; rivers crossing lines twice
## 1. Other rivers
- Mahi: Originates in the Vindhyas near Minda (Dhar district, Madhya Pradesh) — flows through Rajasthan (Banswara) and Gujarat; flows southwest into the Gulf of Khambhat; crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say it originates in Madhya Pradesh and flows through Rajasthan and Gujarat (see below)
- Luni: Originates southwest of Ajmer in Rajasthan in the Aravalli region — flows through the south-eastern Thar to the Rann of Kutch in Gujarat (Aravalli-born desert river)
- Sabarmati: Aravallis (Rajasthan) → Gujarat; Ahmedabad
- Kali: Rises near Diggi village in Uttara Kannada, Karnataka — flows westward to the Arabian Sea
- Mithi: Originates at Vihar Lake in Mumbai — Powai Lake water joins it; joins the Arabian Sea near the Bay of Mahim
- Subarnarekha: Tributary — Kharkai
## 2. Rivers crossing lines twice
- Congo: Crosses the Equator twice
- Mahi: Crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice — originates in Madhya Pradesh; flows through Rajasthan and Gujarat; enters the Gulf of Khambhat
- Limpopo: Crosses the Tropic of Capricorn twice — Africa; associated with the name "Crocodile River"; drains into the Indian Ocean

### Godavari — origin, tributaries and seven branches
## 1. Godavari
- Originates at Nasik, or Nashik, in Maharashtra (Origin: Triyambakeswar) — flows from Nashik through Maharashtra, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh; forms a delta on the eastern coast
- Principal tributaries: Pravara, Purna, Manjra (also called Manjira), Penganga, Wardha, Wainganga, Pranhita, Indravati, Maner, Sabari
- Tributaries (AP sheet): Manjeera, Pranahita, Indravathi, Kinnerasani, Sabari (the main tributary) and Sileru
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the largest tributary is the Pranhita
- Width: 2.7 km wide at Rajahmundry
- Sir Arthur Cotton's dam: At Dhawaleswaram
- Seven branches: Divides in the erstwhile East Godavari and West Godavari districts
| Branch | Location / mouth |
| Gowthami | Yanam — joins the Bay of Bengal |
| Vashista | Antarvedi — joins the Bay of Bengal |
## 2. Tributaries by bank (Prep)
- Left bank: Purna, Pranhita (Pranahita) (largest; formed by Wardha + Wainganga, joins near Kaleshwaram), Indravati (rises in Kalahandi, Odisha; flows through Bastar), Sabari (rises in the Eastern Ghats, Odisha; joins near Kunavaram/K.R. Puram)
- Right bank: Pravara, Manjira, Manair
- Wainganga: Left-bank, joins the Godavari via the Pranhita
- Kinnerasani: Listed among Godavari tributaries (APP)
- PYQ 2025 (APPSC GS 2025 key): Indravati and Sabari = the Godavari pair (Ghataprabha is a Krishna tributary)
### Krishna and Tungabhadra
## 1. Krishna
- Originates at Mahabaleshwar in Maharashtra — Vijayawada and Srisailam are situated on it
- Major tributaries: Koyna, Yerla, Varna, Panchganga, Ghataprabha, Bhima, Tungabhadra
- Prep by bank: left — Bhima [GK] (joins in Telangana), Dindi, Musi; right — Tungabhadra (largest), Ghataprabha, Malaprabha, Koyna [bank GK]
- Tungabhadra flows into the Krishna; Hagari — also called Vedavathi — joins the Tungabhadra, flowing from the Western Ghats through Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh
- Musi: A tributary of Krishna — associated with Hyderabad; rises in the Anantagiri hills, Telangana
- Flows through Kurnool, Guntur and Krishna districts — enters the Bay of Bengal at Hamsaladeevi
- Nagarjuna Sagar — near Nandikonda; the Srisailam Hydroelectric Power Station is on the Krishna
- Splits into two branches at Puligada — 64 km below Vijayawada; Diviseema lies between the two branches
## 2. Tungabhadra
- The biggest and most important tributary of the Krishna — 531 km long (Tunga + Bhadra; ~149 km in AP)
- Origin: Varaha Mountains of the Western Ghats
- Joins the Krishna at Sangameswaram (Sangam) in Kurnool district
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes place Sangameswaram in Nandyal district
- Hagari (= Vedavathi) — the biggest tributary of the Tungabhadra — joins it near Siruguppa
- Vedavathi: (Veda + Avathi, Bababudangiri hills) right-bank tributary of the Tungabhadra, joining at Siruguppa (Apr 2026); own tributaries of the Tungabhadra: Vedavathi (Hagari) and Handri
- ⚠ Largest tributary: Godavari = Pranhita; Krishna = Tungabhadra

### Sileru, Sabari, Machkhand and AP border rivers
## 1. Border rivers
- Sileru River: At the tri-junction of Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Odisha
- A tributary of the Sabari River — originates in Andhra Pradesh, flows through Odisha, joins the Sabari at the tri-junction boundary
- Known as Machkand in its upper reaches
- ⚠ (some notes say it joins the Pranhita)
- Tammileru River: Forms the boundary between Krishna and West Godavari districts — subsequently drains into Kolleru Lake; the Tammileru Dam is built on it
- Vedavathi River: Rises from the Western Ghats — flows through Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh; also called Hagari in some parts of Andhra Pradesh
## 2. Machkhand, Sileru and Sabari
- Machkhand — also called Matsya Kundala: Originates in the Madugula Hills of Anakapalli district — initially flows northward, then eastward, and enters Odisha; Duduma Waterfalls are on it
- Sileru: A major tributary of the Sabari (and so of the Godavari) — originates in Andhra Pradesh, flows through Odisha, joins the Sabari at the tri-junction of Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Odisha; its upper reaches are called Machkand
- Sabari — also called the Kolab River: The main tributary of the Godavari — originates in the Sinkaram Hill ranges of Odisha
