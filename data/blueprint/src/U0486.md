### Causes and challenges — push-pull, slums, housing, transport, waste
## 1. Causes
- Major component: Migration (natural increase similar in rural and urban); monsoon failure pushes rural jobless to cities
- Other causes:
- Industrialisation, commercialisation, transport/communication, social change
- Population explosion + poverty-induced migration
- Exam angle: statement sets mixing push (rural unemployment) and pull (urban jobs, amenities) - usually all correct.
## 2. Challenges
- Slums:
- World Bank estimate 177 million slum dwellers → largest in the world
- Census 2011 slum share: Greater Visakhapatnam ~44% (highest among million-plus), Greater Mumbai ~41%, Vijayawada ~41%
- Housing:
- Urban housing shortage ~18.78 million units (2012 Technical Group)
- Waste & water: Municipalities overwhelmed by solid waste
- Climate:
- Two-thirds of urban dwellers face pluvial flooding by 2030
- Urban heat island 3-5°C
- Governance:
- Fragmented authority; cities lack fiscal autonomy (ES 2025-26)
- Rapidly urbanising areas left under panchayats
- ⚠ Highest slum share among million-plus = Greater Visakhapatnam, not Mumbai

## Definitions and concepts
- ★ Urban area (Census 2001): population at least 5,000; at least 75% of working males non-agricultural; density at least 400/sq km
- ★ Census Towns 2011: 3894 (provisional; final 3892); 1362 in 2001
- ★ Urban growth indicates: rise in urban population, number of urban centres, total population, urban income
- ★ A village becomes a town when traders and manufacturers are present (they do not fully depend on village resources)

- ★ UN-Habitat city prosperity criteria: productivity, quality of life, equality — not optimum population
