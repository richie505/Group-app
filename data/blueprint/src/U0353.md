### Democratic decentralisation — meaning, attributes and FIRSTs
## 1. Meaning
- Definition: Transferring power to elected local bodies so people take part in decisions affecting them
- PYQ: Local self-government is best explained as an exercise in democratic decentralisation (UPSC 2017)
- Coined the term: Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
## 2. Attributes of local self-government

| Elected by and accountable to local people | Sovereignty or legislative supremacy |
| Defined local area and functions | Judicial powers (Nyaya Panchayats are separate) |
| Autonomy within State law |  |

### Committees → recommendations and preconditions
| Report | Precondition |
| Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Genuine transfer of power and adequate resources |
| Ashok Mehta (1977-78) | Compulsory taxation powers, no supersession (or polls within 6 months), social audit, and the district as the first point of decentralisation |
| G.V.K. Rao (1985) | Development had become bureaucratised: "grass without roots". Restore the Zila Parishad's role |
| Committee on Policy and Programmes (1988) | Constitutional status, 5-year term, direct elections, State Finance and Election Commissions |
## 1. Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
- Recommended: Three-tier PRI = Gram Panchayat + Panchayat Samiti + Zila Parishad
- Genuine transfer of power + adequate resources
- Acceptance: NDC accepted it in Jan 1958
- Whose cooperation: Mehta committee → local public participation indispensable for development
## 2. Decentralised planning committees
- Dantwala Committee (1978): block-level planning
- Hanumantha Rao Committee (1984): district planning, Collector coordinating
- Both: basic planning function at the district level (UPSC)
- ⚠ Dantwala (1978) = block; Hanumantha Rao (1984) = district; Ashok Mehta = district as first point; G.V.K. Rao (1985) = "grass without roots"
### Objectives and outcomes of Panchayati Raj
- Fundamental objects: People's participation in development + democratic decentralisation (UPSC 2015 answer: 1 and 3)
- ⚠ Trap: Political accountability and financial mobilisation are not the keyed objects
- What PRIs brought: Formal representation of the weaker sections in village governance (APPSC 2019)
- Why adopted: To decentralise the power of democracy (UPPSC 2016)
- Post-73rd Amendment needs: Decentralisation of decision-making, the system as a whole, and administrative powers
- ⚠ Trap: not judicial powers (BPSC 2022)
