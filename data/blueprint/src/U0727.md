### Satellite orbits and the geostationary orbit — terms and features
## 1. Satellite orbits
| Orbit | Fact |
| Low Earth orbit | 160 km to 2000 km — the International Space Station about 400 km above Earth |
| Geostationary | About 36,000 km above the equator — a period of 24 hours |
| Polar | Passes over the poles |
| Elliptical | Different apogee and perigee distances |
| Circular | Centripetal force equals gravitational force |
## 2. Geostationary orbit
- Altitude: About 36,000 km above the equator — more precisely about 35,786 km
- At a fixed height, not dependent on the satellite's mass (CGPSC 2018)
- Period: 24 hours (UPPSC 2016)
- Placement: Launchers put satellites into elliptical GTO → satellite's own motor raises it to GEO
- ⚠ GEO height does not depend on satellite mass
### INSAT system — FIRSTs, design, management and operator
## 1. FIRSTs
| FIRST | Fact |
| APPLE (Ariane Passenger PayLoad Experiment, 1981) | India's first experimental communication satellite; flew on Ariane |
| INSAT-1A (1982) | First INSAT |
| INSAT system | Commissioned 1983 → operational since INSAT-1B |
## 2. Design and management
- Dual role: INSAT = meteorological + communication system
- ⚠ Key: "Meteorological Satellite" (JPSC 2021)
- Multipurpose design: Telecom + TV broadcasting + meteorology + disaster warning + search-and-rescue on one geostationary platform
- Management:
- Joint venture: DoS, DoT, IMD, All India Radio and Doordarshan
- Coordinated by INSAT Coordination Committee (ICC)
- Controlled from MCF, Hassan (Karnataka)
## 3. Failure and operator
- Failure: INSAT-4C lost when GSLV-F02 failed (2006) → replaced by INSAT-4CR (Sep 2007, GSLV-F04)
- Operator today: 10 in-orbit communication satellites transferred to NSIL in 2022
- India has 19 operational communication satellites (2026)
