### Ramsar Convention — definition, Montreux Record and FIRSTs
## 1. Convention and definition
- Ramsar Convention (1971, Ramsar, Iran): Conservation and wise use of wetlands; India joined in 1982
- Wetland definition: Marsh, fen, peatland or water; natural or artificial; fresh, brackish or salt; incl. marine water up to 6 m deep at low tide
- World Wetlands Day: 2 Feb
- Montreux Record: Lists threatened sites; India's = Keoladeo and Loktak

### Important Bird Areas and migratory birds
## 2. Important Bird Areas
- IBA: BirdLife International designation; BNHS = Indian partner
- Telineelapuram and Telukunchi (Srikakulam, AP): IBAs for breeding painted storks + spot-billed pelicans
- Rushikulya mouth (Odisha): IBA hosting olive ridley arribada
- Sambhar Lake (Rajasthan): Mass death of migratory birds from avian botulism, Nov 2019
## 3. Migratory birds → route
- Central Asian Flyway: Arctic/Siberia → Indian Ocean; India = key wintering ground
- India hosted CMS COP13 (Gandhinagar, 2020)
- Bar-headed goose: Breeds on Central Asian high lakes; crosses the Himalaya to winter in India; one of the highest-flying birds
- Amur falcon: SE Siberia/N China → stopover in Nagaland (Doyang, Pangti) in Oct-Nov → winters in southern Africa
- Longest raptor migration

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:42
### Ramsar Convention
## The Convention
- 1971 — adopted at Ramsar, Iran; India joined in 1982
- World Wetlands Day — 2 February (marks adoption of the Convention)
- 22 March World Water Day; 5 June World Environment Day; 22 April Earth Day
- Ramsar wetland definition covers both natural and artificial water bodies
- ★ Sunderbans — a Ramsar site
## Montreux Record
- ★ Montreux Record — register of Ramsar sites where changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring or are likely to occur (technology, pollution, other human interference)
- Lists threatened Ramsar sites; India's entries — Keoladeo and Loktak
## Protection
- Mining within 10 km of a Ramsar site needs approval of NBWL (National Board for Wildlife) and MoEFCC
- Human–tiger conflict growing: a growing share of tigers live outside notified reserves
