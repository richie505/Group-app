### Rajput policy → features, marriages, mansabdars, Mewar
## Akbar-era battles
- Second Battle of Panipat (1556): Akbar's forces — led by him — and Hemu's forces; Akbar's side won — consolidated Mughal power
- Battle of Talikota (1565): The combined Deccan Sultanates — Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda (major opposing forces) — defeated Vijayanagara; a watershed battle that weakened Vijayanagara
- Battle of Haldighati (1576): Mughal forces (Akbar's, led by Man Singh) and Rana Pratap's forces (Maharana Pratap) — a Mughal victory; associated with Akbar and Rana Pratap; ⚠ the result is treated with nuance
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say Haldighati was not decisive
## 1. Conquest + conciliation
- Core policy: Twin policy of conquest and conciliation; submission accepted, autonomy and honour returned
- Rajputs got high posts, matrimonial alliances, religious toleration, autonomy
- 1562 marriage: Akbar married daughter of Raja Bharmal of Amber (Kachhwaha)
- Top mansabdars: Man Singh and Bhagwan Das
- Prominent Rajputs: Raja Man Singh, Raja Todarmal (revenue), Raja Birbal
## 2. Mewar - the holdout
- Mewar: Never submitted under Akbar
- Rana Pratap fought Haldighati (UPPSC 2023)
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes place Haldighati inside the Gujarat campaign; it was part of the Mewar campaign
- Submitted only under Jahangir (Amar Singh; son Karan Singh became a mansabdar)
### Revenue systems → terms and land classes
- Dahsala or Zabti system: Average produce and prices calculated over ten years
- State share: One-third of the produce; cultivator's share: two-thirds
- Raja Todar Mal: Akbar's revenue minister; earlier served Sher Shah
- Built the Zabti / Bandobast (dahsala) system, modelled on Sher Shah's survey-based system
| System | Features |
| Zabti / Bandobast / Dahsala | Designed by Raja Todar Mal (1580); land measured with the bamboo Gaz-i-Ilahi (about 82 cm); revenue = one-third of average produce and prices of the last 10 years, paid in cash |
| Batai / Ghallabakshi | Crop-sharing: harvest divided between state and peasant |
| Kankut | Revenue fixed by estimation of the standing crop by eye/sample |
| Nasaq | Rough assessment from the peasant's past payments |
| Polaj | Cultivated every year |
| Parauti | Fallow 1-2 years |
| Chachar | Fallow 3-4 years |
| Banjar | Uncultivated 5 years or more |
- ⚠ Gaz-i-Ilahi = bamboo measure of Todar Mal's Zabti (1580), about 82 cm

### Religious policy → year and measure
## 1. Akbar
- Religious policy: Tolerance; accommodation; inter-religious discussions
- Guiding principle: Sulh-i-Kul ("peace with all")
- Abolished the pilgrimage tax and jizya
- Ibadat Khana: Established 1575 — for religious discussions; opened to different schools of religion in 1578
- Mahzarnama: Dates to 1579 — a religious authority controversy
- Din-i-Ilahi — also called Tauhid-i-Ilahi: Established 1582 — an ideology incorporating different traditions; Birbal was a Hindu associated with its acceptance
- Aurangzeb: Reimposed jizya in 1679

- Proclaimed: 1582, syncretic moral code / moral order: piety, prudence, kindness; never forced on anyone

- Reverence for sun and fire
- Members: Small circle (about 18) - Abul Fazl, Faizi and Shaikh Mubarak; Birbal the only Hindu disciple

## 2. Banda Singh Bahadur — first sovereign Sikh state
- Profile: Lachhman Dev; disciple of Guru Gobind Singh
- Campaigns:
- Defeated Wazir Khan at Chappar Chiri (1710)
- Conquered Sirhind (1710) to avenge the Guru's younger sons
- State: First sovereign Sikh state; capital Lohgarh
- Measures: Abolished zamindari; coins in the Gurus' names
- Death: Executed in Delhi 1716 by Farrukhsiyar (May 2026)
### Emperor-wise religious policy and Hindu nobles
| Emperor | Policy |
| Babur | Orthodox Sunni; proclaimed jihad at Khanwa and took the title Ghazi[GK] |
| Akbar | Sulh-i-kul (universal peace); abolished jizya and pilgrim tax; Ibadat Khana; Mahzar; Din-i-Ilahi |
| Jahangir | Broadly continued Akbar's tolerance; executed Guru Arjan Dev for political reasons |
| Shah Jahan | Slightly more orthodox; destroyed some newly built temples; retained a high number of Hindu nobles |
| Aurangzeb | Reversed tolerance: jizya 1679, banned festivals like Navroz, executed Guru Tegh Bahadur; patronised the Fatawa-i-Alamgiri; called "Zinda Pir"; alienated Rajputs, Marathas and Sikhs |
- Aurangzeb: Highest number of Hindu nobles/generals in absolute terms (UPPSC 2000 key)
- Largely Marathas and Rajputs taken in during the Deccan wars
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Sulh-i-Kul + Rajput partnership turned a conquest state into a durable empire; underpinned Rajput and Hindu support
- Aurangzeb's reversal (jizya 1679) alienated Rajputs, Marathas, Sikhs; cited among causes of decline
- Mansabdari-jagirdari and Zabti = core of Mughal governance for 150 years
- Criticism & debates:
- Din-i-Ilahi: new religion or a discipleship order? Only a handful joined; lapsed with Akbar
- Under Aurangzeb, too many mansabdars chased too few jagirs (jagirdari crisis) → pressed the peasantry
- Comparative note: Akbar abolished jizya 1564; Aurangzeb reimposed it 1679
- AP angle:
- Golconda's Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah kept friendly relations with Akbar (who sent Masud Beg with gifts)
- Abul Hasan Tana Shah (Golconda): religious tolerance; appointed Madanna and Akkanna to high office; Bhakta Ramadasu was their nephew
- Current relevance:
- Sirhind Fateh Diwas (12-14 May) commemorates Banda Singh Bahadur's 1710 conquest (May 2026)
- Guru Tegh Bahadur's martyrdom = Shaheedi Divas, 24 Nov (Apr 2026)
- Baisakhi recalled as Khalsa foundation day, 13 April 1699 (14 Apr 2026)

### Religious policy
## Chronology
| Year | Measure |
| 1563 | pilgrimage tax abolished (some accounts: 1564) |
| 1564 | jizya abolished |
| 1575 | Ibadat Khana built (opened to all religions 1578) |
| 1579 | Mahzar — drafted by Shaikh Mubarak; Akbar supreme arbiter in religious disputes |
| 1582 | Din-i-Ilahi |
- ★ Ibadat Khana — built by Akbar, for theological discussions with Ulemas, Sayyids, Shaikhs
- ★ Din-i-Ilahi (Tauhid-i-Ilahi) — started by Akbar; prophet Abul Fazl; only Hindu to accept — Birbal
- one God, respect for sun and fire, avoiding meat; never forced (no compulsory conversion)
- ★ Akbar ended the pilgrimage tax
- Akbar — unlettered but a great patron of learning and culture
- Akbar acted against child marriage and Sati; Sikandar Lodi (not Akbar) ended the grain tax
- Zain-ul-Abidin banned cow slaughter; Alauddin Khalji ended privileges of Khuts, Muqaddams, Chaudharis
## Later emperors
- Jahangir — mostly kept tolerance; Guru Arjan Dev executed for political reasons (support to rebel Khusrau)
- Shah Jahan — ended Sijda (prostration)
- Aurangzeb — brought back jizya in 1679; largest empire (conquest of Bijapur and Golconda)
- first coronation 21 July 1658, Delhi — before beating Dara at Deorai (March 1659); Khajwa and Deorai preceded the second coronation, 5 June 1659
- Jagirdari crisis — too many mansabdars / jagirdars for too few jagirs; pressure on peasantry
- 1675 — execution of Guru Tegh Bahadur
