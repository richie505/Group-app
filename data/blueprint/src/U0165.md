### Terms → meaning (farman, dastak, Diwani, Nizamat)
| Term | Meaning |

| Dastak | Free pass for duty-free trade |

### Battle of Plassey (1757) — year, location, commanders, causes
- Battle of Plassey (1757): The British (led by Robert Clive) and Siraj-ud-daula (Siraj-ud-Daulah) — Established British control over Bengal — associated with treachery; the beginning of British supremacy
- Date/place: 23 June 1757, Plassey (Palashi), on the Bhagirathi, present-day West Bengal (Nadia district)
- Commanders: Clive (about 3,000) defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah (about 50,000)
- Siraj = last independent Nawab; had French support
- Causes:
- Company servants misused dastaks
- English fortified Calcutta without consent (French obeyed his order to stop; English did not)
- Company sheltered his enemies
- Black Hole (June 1756): Siraj took Qasim Bazar → Calcutta (Fort William)
- Governor Drake fled to Fulta
- British used it as a pretext for war
- Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757): Restored privileges of the 1717 farman + duty-free trade; Company still plotted to remove Siraj
### Battle of Plassey (1757) — conspirators and results
| Side | Persons |
| Conspirators | Mir Jafar (Mir Bakshi), Rai Durlabh, Jagat Seth, Amir Chand (Omichund), Manik Chand |
| Loyal to Siraj | Mir Madan + Mohan Lal |
- ⚠ Trap: A statement listing Mir Madan among the traitors is WRONG (Jul 2026 practice question)
- Results:
- Mir Jafar → Nawab; paid Rs 1.77 crore
- Company got zamindari of the 24 Parganas + duty-free trade in Bengal, Bihar, Orissa
- Resources to beat the French

### Nawabs of Bengal → tenure and acts (Mir Jafar, Mir Qasim, Najm-ud-Daula) — Mir Jafar — two tenures, Dutch intrigue, removal
## 1. Mir Jafar — two tenures, Dutch intrigue, removal
- Tenures:
- 1: 1757-1760 (after Plassey); concessions = the Rs 1.77 crore payment and the 24 Parganas zamindari
- 2: 1763-1765 (restored after Mir Qasim's fall; died soon after Buxar)
- Dutch intrigue: Plotted with the Dutch against the English
- Clive defeated the Dutch at Chinsura/Bidara (1759) → Dutch left India
- Removal (1760): Could not meet Company demands
- Pension Rs 15,000 a month; some notes print "Rs 1,500 annually"
### Nawabs of Bengal → tenure and acts (Mir Jafar, Mir Qasim, Najm-ud-Daula) — Mir Qasim — accession, reforms, trade duties and conflict
## 1. Mir Qasim — accession, reforms, trade duties and conflict
- Accession: Son-in-law of Mir Jafar; put on the throne in 1760 by Governor Vansittart
- Treaty of 1760:
- Rs 5 lakh for the Company's southern wars
- Reforms:
- Capital: Murshidabad to Monghyr (Munger), Bihar
- Trade duties: Abolished all internal trade duties (inland) (APPSC G2 2017)
- Indian merchants paid the same as the English → dastak benefit gone
- Conflict: Company servants, angry at lost private-trade profits, went to war
- Defeated in 1763 → fled to Awadh
## 2. Najm-ud-Daula - Company control
- Identity: Son of Mir Jafar; Nawab from 1765
- 1765 treaty: Handed defence + administration to a Company-nominated Naib Subahdar (Muhammad Reza Khan)
- Left with the Nizamat in name only
### Battle of Buxar (1764) — allies, commander, outcome
- Battle of Buxar (1764): The British (led by Hector Munro) and the allied forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II — A major British victory — further consolidation of British supremacy; the Treaty of Allahabad — concluded in 1765
## 1. Buxar - allies, British commander, Nawab at the time
- Date/place: October 1764 (22-23 Oct), Buxar (Bihar)
| Ally | Position |
| Mir Qasim | Deposed Nawab of Bengal (ex-Nawab) |
| Shuja-ud-Daulah | Nawab of Awadh |
| Shah Alam II | Mughal emperor |
- British commander: Major Hector Munro
- Nawab of Bengal at the time: Mir Jafar (restored 1763)
- Outcome: Decisive English win
- Shuja fled, sought Rohilla + Maratha help, then surrendered
- Awadh + Allahabad occupied; Shah Alam joined the English

### Treaty of Allahabad (1765), Diwani and treaty chronology — Treaty of Allahabad - parties and terms
## 1. Treaty of Allahabad - parties and terms
- Signed: 1765, after Buxar, by Clive (returned as Governor of Bengal for second time, May 1765)
- Other parties: Shah Alam II + Shuja-ud-Daulah
| Party | Term |
| Shah Alam II | Granted the Company the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in return for Rs 26 lakh a year; kept (got) Kora and Allahabad → Company pensioner |
| Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh) | Got his dominions back except Kora and Allahabad (gave them to the Emperor); paid a war indemnity of Rs 50 lakh[GK]; Awadh became a buffer state under Company protection |
| Nawab of Bengal | Kept the Nizamat (defence, police, justice), funded by Rs 53 lakh a year from the Company [GK] (some notes say the Company received the Rs 53 lakh) |
### Treaty of Allahabad (1765), Diwani and treaty chronology — Grant of Diwani
## 1. Grant of Diwani
- Date: 1765 (APPSC G2 2017; APPSC-G2 2017 key); formally 12 August 1765
- Grantor: Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II under the Treaty of Allahabad
- Recipient: Company under Robert Clive (Governor of Bengal for the 2nd time, from May 1765)
- Terms: Diwani (revenue collection and civil justice) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa
- Annual tribute of Rs 26 lakh to the Emperor
- Exam angle: "Which treaty followed Buxar?" The answer is Allahabad, not Salbai, Bassein or Sugauli.
## 2. Treaty chronology
| Treaty | Year |
| Allahabad | 1765 |
| Madras (Haidar Ali) | 1769 |
| Salbai | 1782 |
- Chronology (UPPSC 2016): Buxar (Oct 1764) → Clive returns (May 1765) → Treaty of Allahabad (1765) → Warren Hastings Governor (1772)
### Dual Government (1765-72) → division, criticism and end
- Period: 1765-72
- Division:
- Company = Diwani (revenue + civil justice)
- Nawab = Nizamat (police, criminal justice / law and order)
- Mughal emperor = nominal sovereign
- Criticism: Power without responsibility
- Naib diwans: Muhammad Reza Khan (Bengal) + Raja Shitab Rai (Bihar) – collected revenue for the Company
- End: Warren Hastings (1772) ended it
- "Tore the mask of Mughal sovereignty" (ended Mughal pretence) → ruled by right of conquest
- Stopped the tribute to Shah Alam
### Robert Clive — titles and record
- Titles: "founder of the British Empire in India" (APPSC 2019; BPSC 2020); "heaven-born general" (William Pitt)
- Governor of Bengal: 1758-60 and 1765-67
- Record: Arcot (1751), Chandannagar (1757), Plassey (1757), Condore (1758)
- ⚠ Clive's two governorships = 1758-60 and 1765-67; Treaty of Allahabad signed in his second term
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Buxar + Allahabad made the Company the real ruler of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; Mughal Emperor its pensioner
- Control of Bengal's revenue turned the Company from trader → territorial power; paid for later conquests
- Criticism & debates: Dual Government separated power from responsibility → 1770 Bengal famine (John Cartier's time)
- FIRST/LAST: Shah Alam II = first Mughal ruler to become a Company pensioner
- Current relevance:
- 23 June 2026 = 269th anniversary of Plassey; recalled for dastak misuse, the Black Hole + Mir Jafar's bribe
- Practice question (Jul 2026): tested the Treaty of Alinagar + 1717 farman privileges; treated Mir Madan as loyal

## Before Buxar
- Not a cause of conflict with Siraj: French refusal to stop fortifying — the French obeyed; the English kept fortifying Calcutta
- Mir Jafar — the Mir Bakshi, chief plotter; Mir Madan loyal to Siraj; Holwell and Drake on the British side
- 1763 — Mir Qasim defeated; fled to Awadh, later an ally against the British at Buxar
## The battle
- Major Hector Munro — British commander (Clive won Plassey; Eyre Coote won Wandiwash; Lally led the French)
- British defeated Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula, Shah Alam II; followed by the Treaty of Allahabad (1765)
- Mir Jafar — Nawab of Bengal during Buxar (restored 1763); Mir Qasim fought as deposed ex-Nawab
- Shuja-ud-Daulah fled and sought help from the Rohillas and the Marathas before surrendering
### Plassey vs Buxar
- Buxar — won through open fighting; Plassey — associated with treachery
- Buxar strengthened British control in eastern and northern India
### Treaty of Allahabad (1765)
- Buxar (not Plassey) brought the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa and made Awadh dependent
- Annual tribute to Shah Alam II — Rs 26 lakh
- Shuja-ud-Daulah — war indemnity Rs 50 lakh; got back his dominions except Kora and Allahabad (to Shah Alam II)
- Buxar + Allahabad — Company the real ruler of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; Mughal Emperor reduced to a pensioner
### Chronology (UPPSC 2016)
- ★ PYQ key order: Clive's re-arrival → Treaty of Allahabad → Battle of Buxar → Warren Hastings as Governor
- ★ Clive's reforms: civil — banned gifts and private business, made internal tax compulsory; military — banned double batta (from 1766)
- Battle of Plassey — 23 June 1757 at Palashi, on the Bhagirathi
- Warren Hastings — Governor of Bengal in 1772, after Buxar (1764) and Allahabad (1765)
### Dual Government (1765-72)
- Company held the Diwani (revenue, civil justice); Nawab kept the Nizamat
- "Power without responsibility" — Company controlled revenue; Nawab formally kept law and order
- Dual Government and the 1770 famine — power separated from responsibility; revenue squeezed as famine spread
- 1772 — Warren Hastings ended the Mughal pretence; ruled by right of conquest
