### Harikatha and Burrakatha → form, region, instruments and use
## 1. Harikatha
- Form: Also Harikathalu; a single artist sings, acts, dances (about 3 hours), with cymbals (Chidatalu)
- Roots: Harikatha deeply rooted in Vizianagaram (Jun 2025)
## 2. Burrakatha
- Home: Birthplace Guntur; folk form of Andhra Pradesh (APPSC-GS 2019 key) and Telangana
- Roles: Group of three - main narrator (stands in middle, plays the burra) + two Vantalu (respond and comment)
- Instruments / format: Gummata, Burra, Tambura, Ukki; chorus "Thandana Taana"
- Political use: Narrators carried news, reform and nationalist messages to unlettered villages
- Praja Natya Mandali: Folk plays for socialist/communist ideals
### Tolu Bommalata, Kathas and Bhagavata Mela → performers, region and format — Tolu Bommalata
## 1. Tolu Bommalata
- Material: Thin, translucent leather figures painted on both sides
- Moved behind a white screen lit by an oil lamp; puppeteer sings verses + dialogue from behind
- Unique feature: Moving the heads of the puppets
- Themes: Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas, with classical music
- Surabhi: Venue: wedding in Sorugu village near Jammalamadugu (Kadapa) → Sorugu came to be called Surabhi
- Family theatre ~140 years old

### Tolu Bommalata, Kathas and Bhagavata Mela → performers, region and format — Yakshaganam and Kuchipudi
## 1. Yakshaganam and Kuchipudi
- Yakshaganam of Andhra: Performed in Telugu; elaborate costumes + epic/puranic stories (Jun 2025)
- Kuchipudi — name: From Kuchipudi village, Krishna district
- Origin: Grew out of Bhagavata Mela dance-drama of hereditary Brahmin families → village-theatre origin for a classical form
- Exam angle: Kuchipudi <- Bhagavata Mela <- Siddhendra Yogi; Tana Shah grant.
- ⚠ Oggu = shepherds (Telangana); Asadi = Madigas; Bhagavatulu = Yadavas
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: AP announced an annual Telugu Mahotsavam to revive Tholu Bommalata, Harikatha, Burrakatha and Padya Natakam
- Nandi Natakotsavams to be revived (Minister Kandula Durgesh, Undavalli Caves, 27 Jul 2026)
- Criticism & debates:
- Decline: lack of patronage, only 3-4 shows a year, youth disinterest, weak welfare for veteran artists
- Significance: Folk performance = living proof of a distinct Telugu culture behind the Andhra Movement; carried it to villages
