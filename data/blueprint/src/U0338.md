### RPA 1951 sections → content — Contesting from more than one constituency - Section 33(7)
## 1. Contesting from more than one constituency - Section 33(7)
- Seat limit — Section 33 / 33(7), RPA 1951: A maximum of two constituencies at a time — Lok Sabha / Parliamentary or State Assembly
- The RPA 1951 was amended in 1996 to cap the number of constituencies at two — Devi Lal had contested from three Lok Sabha seats in the 1991 elections (UPSC 2021 key: statement correct; before 1996 there was no cap)
- Winner of both: must vacate one within 14 days (Sec. 70)
- Reforms:
- One candidate, one seat (Law Commission 255th report)
- Vacating candidate pays for the bypoll (ECI proposal)
### RPA 1951 sections → content — Candidates and the RP (Amendment) Act, 1996
## 1. Candidates
- Security deposit: Forfeited if the candidate fails to secure more than one-sixth of the total valid votes polled
- Rajya Sabha domicile: Before 2003, a candidate had to be an elector in the particular State of contest — the 2003 amendment removed this; any registered elector in India can contest from any State
- Upheld by the Supreme Court in Kuldip Nayar (2006) — removal of the domicile or voter requirement
- Death of a contesting candidate: Before the 1996 amendment, elections were automatically countermanded — after it, no longer; a recognized political party may nominate a replacement within a set timeframe
## 2. Other changes made by the RP (Amendment) Act, 1996
- Nominations:
- Cap of two constituencies (above)
- Higher security deposits
- More proposers for independents/unrecognised parties
- Countermanding: only on death of a recognised-party candidate
- Poll day:
- Paid holiday (Sec. 135B)
- Liquor ban 48 hours before poll (Sec. 135C)
- Bypolls within 6 months (Sec. 151A)
- Disqualification: offences under the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971
- ⚠ Sec. 70 = vacate within 14 days; Sec. 81 = petition within 45 days; Sec. 78 = accounts within 30 days

## 2. Electoral bonds - features
- Scheme: notified 2018 (via Finance Act 2017 amendments)
- Seller: only SBI; bearer bonds
- Denominations: ₹1,000 to ₹1 crore; valid 15 days
- Encashment: only registered parties with at least 1% (1% of the vote) in the last election
- Anonymity: donor identity hidden from public - older notes call it a "transparency" gain

### EVM, VVPAT, NOTA and RVM — FIRSTs and features — EPIC and VVPAT - working and verification
## 1. EPIC and VVPAT
- EPIC: Established voter photo-identification — introduced to prevent bogus voting and impersonation
- VVPAT — Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail: Generates an independent paper slip for verification — visible to the voter for 7 seconds
- Working: prints a slip with candidate's serial number, name and symbol → instant feedback
- Generations: M1, M2 and M3 (current); VVPATs made by BEL and ECIL
- ADR v ECI (April 2024) - detail: candidates placed 2nd and 3rd (runners-up) can seek a check of 5% of EVMs' burnt memory per segment within 7 days of result
- New ECI rules:
- VVPAT slips must be counted wherever EVM data do not match Form 17C
- Penultimate EVM/VVPAT round counted only after postal ballots are finished
### EVM, VVPAT, NOTA and RVM — FIRSTs and features — Remote voting machine and totalisers
## 1. Remote voting machine and totalisers
| Term | Meaning |
| Remote EVM (RVM) | ECI prototype for domestic migrants; up to 72 constituencies from one remote polling station; demonstrated to parties in Jan 2023 |
| Totaliser | Pools count of several control units → booth-wise voting patterns stay hidden; recommended by Law Commission 255th Report (2015) |

- No legal framework in the RPA or Conduct of Election Rules
- Would make booth- and machine-wise checks (EVM, VVPAT, Form 17C) harder
