### Dynasties → period, founder and end
## 1. Founding and end
- Brihadratha: The last Mauryan ruler — assassinated by his commander Pushyamitra Shunga in 185 BCE
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say Pushyamitra killed Brihadratha in 187 BC (APPSC GS 2023 key)

- Shunga dynasty: Approximately 185 BCE to 73 BCE

- Magadha: A region associated with the Shungas; the ancestors of the Shungas originated from Ujjain
- Pushyamitra was described as a Brahmin — the succeeding Kanvas were also described as Brahmins
- Devabhuti: The last Shunga ruler — killed by his minister, Amatya Vasudeva Kanva
- The Kanva dynasty succeeded the Shungas — founded by Vasudeva Kanva

### Sunga rulers and scholars → contributions
## 1. Rituals and literature
- Pushyamitra performed two Ashwamedha Yajnas — information from the Ayodhya Inscription
- Patanjali: Belonged to the Shunga period — author of Mahabhashya; supervised the organization of the two Ashwamedha ceremonies; a contemporary of Pushyamitra
- Agnimitra: The son of Pushyamitra — a Shunga ruler of Vidisha
- Malavikagnimitram: Written by Kalidasa — his first play; the love story of Agnimitra of Vidisha and Malavika — Agnimitra is the protagonist
- Mrichchhakatikam: Written by Sudraka
## 2. Rulers and scholars
| Person | Contribution |

- Pushyamitra's dominions: reached the Narmada; included Pataliputra, Ayodhya and Vidisha (UPSC)
- Rituals: two Ashvamedha sacrifices - inscription issued by Dhana (Ayodhya inscription of Dhana)

- Sanskrit revival: Sunga court led revival of classical Sanskrit (Ashoka had used Prakrit)
- ⚠ Agnimitra = son (Vidisha viceroy); Vasumitra = grandson (beat the Yavanas)
### Pushyamitra and Buddhism — the persecution debate
- For: Divyavadana + Tibetan monk Taranatha (16th c.) - destroyed stupas, bounty on monks' heads
- Critics: Romila Thapar and Etienne Lamotte - exaggerated and sectarian
- Against persecution:
- Buddhist art flourished at Sanchi, Bharhut and Bodh Gaya
- Royal patronage fell, but merchants and guilds kept funding Buddhism
- Hostility probably aimed at monasteries allied with Indo-Greeks
- Bodhi Tree: one tradition blames him for destroying the original Bodhi Tree at Bodh Gaya; Shashanka of Gauda did the same in the 7th c. (2026)
### Besnagar (Heliodorus) pillar — Bhagavatism
- Erected by: Heliodorus, native of Taxila, ambassador of Indo-Greek king Antialcidas, at the court of Sunga king Bhagabhadra
- Site: Besnagar, near Vidisha (MP)
- Nature: Garuda-dhvaja dedicated to Vasudeva; Heliodorus calls himself a Bhagavata
- FIRST: earliest inscriptional proof of Bhagavatism (early Vaishnavism) + absorption of foreigners into Indian religion
- Language/script: Prakrit / Brahmi
### Sunga art sites → features
- Transition: wood/ivory → stone
| Site | Feature |

| Bharhut (MP) | Railings + gateways of early narrative art; inscriptions name the guilds of ivory workers and stone carvers who paid for it |
| Bodh Gaya | Early stone railing |
| Mathura | Mathura style of sculpture began under the Sungas |
- Sanchi south torana: linked to Satakarni II via the inscription of Ananda → gateways often dated to the Satavahana period
- Bharhut PYQ: "The Bharhut stupa is a fine example of Shunga architecture" (RPSC 2023)
