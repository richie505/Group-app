### RTI landmarks, FIRSTs and persons — Sweden 1766 to the 2005 Act — Preamble and nature
## 1. Preamble and nature
- Preamble: Informed citizenry + transparency to contain corruption + hold governments accountable, harmonised with other public interests and confidentiality needs
- Also listed: transparency, accountability, reduced corruption, participation, better service delivery
- Nature: Statutory (legal) right under the Act, giving effect to the fundamental right to know that the SC reads into Article 19(1)(a)
- Judicial roots: Right to know is part of Art. 19(1)(a) (and Art. 21) (cases on the cases page)

### RTI Act sections → content; terms → meaning — Terms → meaning
## 1. Terms → meaning
- Information (2(f)): Any material in any form
- Records, documents, e-mails, opinions, advices, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, samples, models, electronic data
- + information on a private body accessible to a public authority under law
- Right to information (2(j)):
- Inspection of work, documents, records
- Taking notes, extracts or certified copies
- Taking certified samples of material
- Obtaining information in electronic form
## 2. Filing a request
- Request: By post, in person or e-mail, in Hindi, English or the local official language
- Only citizens can apply; information free / fee waived for BPL applicants
- ⚠ Trap: 18 = complaints; 19 = appeals; 20 = penalties; 22 = overrides OSA 1923; 25 = annual report
### Time limits — number game
| Situation / stage | Time limit |
| Normal reply by PIO (Section 7) | 30 days |
| Life or liberty of a person involved | 48 hours |
| Application routed via an APIO | 30 + 5 days |
| Third-party information (Section 11) | 40 days |
| First appeal to First Appellate Authority | Within 30 days of the decision/expiry; decided in 30 days, extendable to 45 |
| Second appeal to CIC/SIC | Within 90 days |
- Section 24 organisations (human-rights allegations): 45 days, with Commission approval
- Exam angle: "reply in 45 days" is the standard wrong pair (APPSC 2025 key: first appeal 30 days and life/liberty 48 hours correct).
### Exemptions — Section 8(1) clauses, 8(2), 8(3), Sections 9 and 24
## 1. Section 8(1) exemptions
| Clause | Exempt information |
| (c) | Breach of privilege of Parliament/Legislature (legislative privilege) |
| (d) | Commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property (IP) |
| (e) | Fiduciary relationship (8(1)(e)) |
| (f) | Information from a foreign government in confidence |
| (i) | Cabinet papers |
| (j) | Personal information (8(1)(j)) |
## 2. Overrides and other exemptions
- Section 8(2): Commission/authority may disclose if public interest outweighs the harm to protected interests, even if covered by the OSA
- Section 8(3): Exempt information disclosed after 20 years
- Except: cabinet deliberations, security/strategic/economic interests, foreign relations, incitement
- Section 9: Copyright of a person other than the State
- Section 24 / Second Schedule: Intelligence and security bodies (May 2026)
- Exception: information on corruption and human-rights violations must still be given
- ⚠ Trap: 8(1)(e) = fiduciary; 8(1)(i) = cabinet papers; 8(1)(j) = personal information

## 1. Political parties under RTI - CIC order and compliance
- 2005 position: Act did not name political parties; parties claimed exemption
- CIC, June 2013: Six national parties (INC, BJP, CPI, CPI(M), NCP, BSP) declared public authorities under 2(h)
- Reason: indirect government funding (land, bungalows, free airtime)
- Parties did not comply; question pending before the Supreme Court
- RTI (Amendment) Bill 2013 to exclude them lapsed (some notes say the 2013 Bill removed them)
- ⚠ Trap: Older notes say a 2018 SC verdict made parties disclose funding under RTI - no such final ruling; follow the CIC-2013 fact
- Why activists want it: Prevent corruption + crony capitalism; check illegal foreign contributions; parties enjoy state privileges (free airtime on DD)
- 2026 demand: Bring recognised national parties under RTI with a transparent, auditable electoral-funding system

### Information Commissions — composition, appointment, term, removal (vs CVC) — Nature and composition
## 1. Nature and composition
- Nature: CIC and SICs are statutory bodies created by the RTI Act (not constitutional)
- Hear second appeals and complaints; powers of a civil court
- Composition: Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 Information Commissioners (both levels)
- SIC selection committee: CM, LoP, Minister
- ⚠ Trap: Chief Justice of the High Court is not on the SIC committee (G2 2025 key)
- SIC removal (Section 17): By the Governor; for proved misbehaviour or incapacity only after a Supreme Court inquiry on Governor's reference
### Information Commissions — composition, appointment, term, removal (vs CVC) — Eligibility and term
## 1. Eligibility and term
- Eligibility: Persons of eminence in public life with experience in law, science and technology, social service, management, journalism, mass media or administration
- Cannot be MP/MLA, hold an office of profit, be connected with a political party, or carry on a business or profession
- Term:
- Originally 5 years or age 65, no reappointment
- After 2019 amendment: Centre notifies term = 3 years (RTI Rules, 2019)
- PIOs: Designated by each public authority (Section 5), not by the President/Governor
## 2. Comparison - CVC
| Item | CVC |
| Composition | A Central Vigilance Commissioner + up to two Vigilance Commissioners |
| Term | 4 years or age 65 |
| Removal | By the President for misbehaviour or incapacity after a Supreme Court inquiry; also on grounds like insolvency |
- ⚠ Trap: CIC/SIC = up to 10 members, term now 3 years; CVC = two VCs, 4 years or age 65
### Penalties on PIOs and compensation — Sections 20 and 19(8)(b)
- Penalty (Section 20): Imposed by the CIC/SIC on the PIO
- ₹250 per day until application received or information furnished
- Maximum ₹25,000
- Grounds:
- Refusing to receive an application
- Knowingly giving incorrect/misleading information
- Procedure: PIO must be heard first; burden of proof on PIO
- Commission can also recommend disciplinary action
- Compensation: Commission can require the public authority to compensate the applicant for any loss (Section 19(8)(b))
- ⚠ Trap: ₹250/day, cap ₹25,000 = Section 20; compensation = 19(8)(b)
### Amendments → changes — RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 and DPDP Act 2023 — RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 - tenure and salaries
## 1. RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 - tenure and salaries
| Item | Original 2005 Act | After RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 |
| Term of CIC/ICs | Fixed 5 years or age 65 [GK] | As notified by the Central Government (3 years under 2019 Rules) [GK] |
| Salary, central level | CIC = Chief Election Commissioner; IC = Election Commissioner [GK] | Fixed by the Central Government [GK] |
| Salary, state level | State CIC = Election Commissioner; State IC = Chief Secretary | Fixed by the Central Government |
- Criticism / autonomy concern: The 2019 amendment let the Centre fix tenure and salaries of Information Commissioners - weakens Commissions' autonomy by putting tenure and pay in executive hands
### Amendments → changes — RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 and DPDP Act 2023 — DPDP Act 2023 and Section 8(1)(j)
## 1. DPDP Act 2023 and Section 8(1)(j)
- DPDP Act 2023, Section 44(3): Rewrote Section 8(1)(j) into a blanket exemption for "personal information"
- Original clause: withheld personal information only if unrelated to public activity or an unwarranted invasion of privacy, with a public-interest override
- Amendment: exempts personal information outright; drops "no relationship to public activity" test + override
- Timeline:
- Centre notified amendment in Nov 2025
- Attorney-General opined it does not dilute RTI (Jan 2026)
- Supreme Court referred the challenge to a Constitution Bench (Feb 2026), noting its "constitutional sensitivity"
- 7 Aug 2026: SC agreed to examine whether DPDP's "en bloc embargo" is repugnant to RTI
- ⚠ Trap: 2019 = tenure and salaries (Centre); 2023 DPDP Section 44(3) = rewrote 8(1)(j)

### Issues and debates — misuse, pendency, activists' safety — Achievements vs misuse
## 1. Achievements vs misuse
- Achievements:

- Reduces corruption and misuse of public funds
- Has raised the sense of responsibility in the bureaucracy, but "miles to go" (UPPSC 2010 key: both true, R not the explanation)
- Misuse: Act enacted for transparency and accountability, misused by both the public and public authorities (APPSC 2019 key: both A and R true)

### Landmarks
