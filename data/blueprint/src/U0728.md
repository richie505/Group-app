### Satellite navigation — GPS basics, NavIC full form, constellation and services
## 1. GPS and atomic clocks
- GPS = Global Positioning System: US Department of Defense programme
- 24 satellites in 6 orbits at 20,200 km, each circling twice a day
- Signals: L1 (1,575.42 MHz) + L2
- Now about 30 satellites (Mar 2026)
- Atomic clocks: 1 microsecond clock error = ~300 m position error
## 2. NavIC
- Full form: Navigation with Indian Constellation (CGPSC 2021); formerly IRNSS
- Status: Fully operational 2018
- Genesis: US denied GPS data in the 1999 Kargil War
- Constellation: 7 satellites - 3 GEO + 4 inclined GSO (at 29° to the equator)
- Coverage: India + 1,500 km beyond its borders
- Bands: L5 and S; newer NVS satellites add civilian L1 signal (compatible with other GNSS) → eases phone adoption
| Service | Users | Accuracy |
| Standard Positioning Service (SPS) | All civilians | Better than 20 m; timing better than 40 ns (LENSD) |
| Restricted Service (RS) | Authorised/military users | Encrypted |
- ⚠ GPS = 24 satellites, 6 orbits, 20,200 km; NavIC = 7 satellites (3 GEO + 4 GSO), regional
### IRNSS and NVS satellites → launch and clock failures
## 1. Launches
- IRNSS-1A to 1G: The 7 + replacements
- Total: 11 navigation satellites launched since 2013 (Mar 2026)
- Launch site: All from Sriharikota; IRNSS-1 series on PSLV
## 2. NVS series
| Satellite | Vehicle / date | Note |
| NVS-01 (second generation) | May 2023, GSLV-F12 | First with indigenous rubidium atomic clock |
| NVS-02 | GSLV-F15, Jan 2025 (100th Sriharikota launch) | Valve glitch → wrong orbit; salvaged for alternate use |
## 3. Clock issues
- First generation: Rubidium clocks from Swiss firm SpectraTime kept failing → ISRO switched to indigenous clocks (SAC) in 2018
- IRNSS-1F failure (13 Mar 2026): Last clock failed
- Launched Mar 2016; 10-year life ended 10 Mar 2026
- Only 3 PNT-capable satellites left (IRNSS-1B, 1I, NVS-01) → below the 4 needed
- 1F continues one-way messaging (15 and 20 Mar 2026)
- IRNSS-1D: Decommissioned to 600 km above GEO (ISSAR 2025; 9 Apr 2026)
- ⚠ Clocks: SpectraTime (Swiss, failed) → SAC indigenous (2018); NVS-01 = first indigenous rubidium clock
### NavIC applications and adoption — devices, chipsets, timing
- First NavIC-based device: Elena Geo Systems → precise directions on infrastructure projects (APPSC 2023)
- Mobile chipsets: Qualcomm Snapdragon chipsets support NavIC
- Vehicle trackers: MoRTH's AIS-140 rule → NavIC-enabled location trackers compulsory in public transport vehicles
- Timing: Government urged electronics makers to use NavIC for Indian Standard Time (Mar 2026)
- Applications:
- Terrestrial, aerial and marine navigation
- Precise timing (power, telecom, banking)
- Vehicle tracking and fleet management
- Smart devices, location-based apps
- Precision farming; disaster management
- Fishermen alerts: Reconfigured IRNSS-1A broadcasts cyclone, tsunami and high-wave warnings to fishermen at sea
### GAGAN and other SBAS → developer and region
- Full form: GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation
- Developers: ISRO and the Airports Authority of India (AAI)
- SBAS concept: Broadcasts real-time corrections for orbit, clock and ionospheric delay → improves GPS accuracy, integrity and availability (UPSC 2025 statement I correct)
- Payloads: On geostationary GSAT-8, GSAT-10, GSAT-15
- Aviation use: Precision approach and landing for civil aviation
- DGCA certified GAGAN for aviation approach guidance in 2015
- Nodal authority: Satellite Navigation Programme Office under ISRO → for NavIC and GAGAN
