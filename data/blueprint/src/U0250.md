### Transfer of power and Attlee's Statement (20 February 1947)
## 1. Transfer of power
- Clement R. Attlee: Prime Minister of Britain from 1945 to 1951 — the Labour Party; PM at the time of Indian Independence
- 20 February 1947: Announced the transfer of power to Indian hands before June 1948
- 1947: Mountbatten replaced Lord Wavell as Viceroy
- 3 June 1947: The Mountbatten Plan was issued — a plan for the transfer of power
- 15 August 1947: India became independent
## 2. Attlee's Statement (20 February 1947) (CDX)
- British rule to end by 30 June 1948 (British to leave by June 1948), even without agreement
- Power could go to provincial governments (hint of Balkanisation)
- Wavell replaced by Mountbatten
## 3. Alternative schemes

- Some notes credit Plan Balkan to Wavell; attribution to Mountbatten is the standard one
### Plans and schemes of 1947 → author, date, provisions — Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan), 1947
## 1. Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan), 1947
- Mountbatten: Became Viceroy in March 1947
- Announced: 3 June 1947
- Rationale: Accepted the League's Pakistan demand to avoid civil war + administrative collapse
- Provisions:
- Punjab and Bengal: Legislative assemblies to meet in two parts (Hindu-majority vs Muslim-majority districts); vote by simple majority on partition → both voted for partition
- Sindh: To decide on its own
- Referendums: NWFP → joined Pakistan despite the Khudai Khidmatgar boycott led by Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan; Sylhet district of Assam → joined East Pakistan
- Boundary Commission: For Punjab and Bengal, to fix borders
- Princely states: Paramountcy to lapse; join India or Pakistan (no third option)
- Governor-General: Common GG permitted, but Pakistan chose Jinnah
- Legal basis: Power transferred on the basis of the GoI Act 1935; armed forces divided
- Advanced the transfer date: June 1948 → 15 August 1947
- Congress's acceptance - reasons: Avoid Balkanisation + secure a strong Centre + stop communal bloodshed
- ⚠ Attlee deadline 30 June 1948 → Mountbatten advanced it to 15 August 1947

- Purpose: Legalised / gave legal force to the Mountbatten Plan
- Advanced date: Independence brought forward from June 1948 to August 1947
### Indian Independence Act 1947 → provisions
## 1. Dominions and offices
- Dominions: Two independent dominions, India and Pakistan, with the right to leave the British Commonwealth
- Offices abolished: Viceroy + Secretary of State for India
- Governor-General: Each dominion got one acting on its cabinet's advice = constitutional head (a common GG allowed)
## 2. Constituent Assemblies
- Made sovereign in both dominions
- Could frame any constitution and repeal any British law, including this Act
## 3. Paramountcy and partition
- Paramountcy: British paramountcy lapsed → the 565 princely states free to accede to India or Pakistan
- Other: Armed forces divided; Boundary Commissions for Punjab and Bengal
- partition votes in Punjab and Bengal; referendums in NWFP and Sylhet
## 4. Other provisions
| Provision | Detail |
| Interim law | Each dominion governed by the Government of India Act 1935 / GoI Act 1935 (as adapted) until its new constitution [GK] |
- Most distinctive feature (APPSC-G2 2017): An Act without a constitution - transferred power but left constitution-making to Indians
### Persons → statements and roles

### Partition → boundaries, assets and consequences
## 1. Radcliffe Boundary Commissions
- Two commissions: Punjab + Bengal
- Composition: Each = 2 Congress + 2 League nominee judges
- Radcliffe Line award: Announced only after independence
- Ready on 12 August but published on 17 August 1947 so that Britain would not be blamed for the violence
## 2. Division of assets
- Partition Council: Divided assets and liabilities
- Pakistan took 17.5% of the assets and liabilities
- Division: Armed forces, services and assets divided between the two dominions
## 3. Consequences
| Factor | Effect |
| Migration | An estimated 10-15 million crossed the new borders; hundreds of thousands killed in communal violence |
| Rehabilitation | New Government of India set up a Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation → refugee camps, resettlement, evacuee property |
| Ceasefire | UN ceasefire 1 Jan 1949 |
- ⚠ Award ready 12 August, published 17 August 1947

- Significance: Transfer of power as dominion status let India and Pakistan become free before writing constitutions
