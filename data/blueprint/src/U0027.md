### Tirthankaras → symbol, nirvana place; FIRSTs
## 1. Tirthankaras
- Jina: Conqueror — victor; Jainism recognizes 24 Tirthankaras (24 Tirthankaras - "ford-makers" who show the path to moksha)
- Historicity: First 22 uncertain; proved only for the last two - Parsvanatha/Parshvanatha (23rd) + Mahavira (24th), corroborated by Jain and Buddhist sources
| No. | Tirthankara | Symbol (cognizance) | Key facts / nirvana place |
| 1st | Rishabhdev — also called Adinath (Rishabhadeva (Adinatha) / Rishabhanatha) | Bull | The first Tirthankara; born Ayodhya to Nabhi and Marudevi; moksha at Mount Kailash (Ashtapada) |
| 12th | Vasupujya | Buffalo | Champapuri (Bihar) |
| 20th | Munisuvrata (Munisuvrat Swami) | Tortoise | Nirvana at Sammed Shikhar |

- Parents: King Asvasena and Queen Vama of Varanasi

- Period: About 250 years before Mahavira
- Teaching: Eternity of matter; followers wore white
- Exam angle: "24th Tirthankara" (APPSC 2025) and "20th Tirthankara - Munisuvrat Swami" (APPSC 2018) are direct one-liners; "24th / 20th / 1st Tirthankara was..." and symbol-matching.

### Jain philosophy — terms and doctrines → meaning
## 1. Doctrines
- Triratna: Right Faith (Samyak Darshana; samyag-darshana), Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnana; samyag-jnana), Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra; samyag-charitra) — the Jain Triratna (Ratnatraya): path to moksha
- Samvara: Stoppage of karmic inflow; Nirjara: elimination of karma
- Syadvada: Relativity of knowledge — associated with Saptabhanginaya
## 2. Jain philosophy — terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Jina | Conqueror (of passions); root of "Jain" |
| Jiva / Ajiva | Soul (consciousness) / matter (non-consciousness) |
| Karma | Material bondage of the soul causing rebirth; clings to the soul |
| Kevala jnana / Kaivalya | Omniscience attained by the soul |
| Siddha | Fully liberated soul |
| Anekantavada | Manifold nature / many-sidedness of reality; truth has many (multiple) facets |
| Syadvada | Theory of conditioned predication / relativity of truth - every statement holds only "from a point of view" (syat = "may be"); seven-fold predication (saptabhangi) [GK] |
| Nayavada | Theory of partial standpoints |
- Souls in natural objects: All objects, animate or inanimate, possess a soul capable of feeling pain → extreme ahimsa
- No creator god: World runs by an eternal law of decay and development; Jainism is non-theistic + rejects Vedic authority
- Karma = material bondage: Liberation: stop inflow + shed karma (nirjara) through penance → Kevala Jnana (omniscience) → Siddha state
- Tattva (reality): origination, decay and permanence (utpada-vyaya-dhrauvya)
- Four forms of existence: Deva, Manushya, Naraki (hell-beings), Tiryancha (animals/plants)
- ⚠ Trap: Yaksha is not one
- Contrast with other schools:
- Buddhism = anatta, pratityasamutpada
- Ajivikas = niyati (fate)
- Charvaka = materialism
## 3. Triratna (Ratnatraya) - three jewels
- Jain Triratna: see Doctrines above
- Buddhist Triratna: Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha (refuges, not a path)
- Exam angle: questions test which set belongs to which religion.
### Panchamahavratas, Anuvratas and Jain practices — vows, rituals, Sallekhana
## 1. The five vows
- Five Mahavratas: Ahimsa, Satya, Brahmacharya, Asteya, Aparigraha
- First four (chaturyama): Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha - taught by Parsvanatha/Parshvanatha
- 5th vow: Brahmacharya (celibacy) - the fifth vow added by Mahavira
- ⚠ 4 vows = Parshvanatha; 5th (Brahmacharya) = Mahavira
- Householders: Milder Anuvratas (non-injury, non-stealing, non-adultery, truth, non-possession) + daily charity of cooked food to the needy
- Ahimsa: Extends to all life forms → vegetarianism; strict followers avoid agriculture (hence trading base)
## 2. Practices and rituals → meaning
| Practice | Meaning |
| Paryushana | Last day = Samvatsari (forgiveness day) |
| Pratikramana | Introspection ritual |
| Padmasana or Kayotsarga | Idol posture of Tirthankara/Jina idols |
| Sallekhana / Santhara | Ritual fast unto death at end of life (life's end) to purify karma |
- Sallekhana — also called Samadhi Maran: The last vow — gradual reduction of food and liquid; a Jain practice (not Buddhist/Sufi/Parsi)
- Chandragupta Maurya performed it at Shravanabelagola
- Exam angle: "Santhara is a ritual of fasting to death amongst - Jains" (APPSC G2 2017).
### Jainism and Buddhism compared
| Point | Jainism | Buddhism |
| Soul | Eternal soul (jiva) in everything / all things | Anatta - no permanent soul (self) |
| Karma | Material bondage | Intention-based; shared belief in karma-rebirth |
| Penance | Severe (extreme asceticism), self-mortification | Middle Way (Middle Path) avoiding extremes |
| Ahimsa | Stricter, even in thought; strict diet | Important but less strict |
| Monks | Digambara monks unclothed | Saffron robes |
| Texts | Agamas (Ardhamagadhi) | Tripitaka (Pali) |
| Goal | Kevala jnana | Nirvana/Buddhahood |
- Shared:
- Rejection of Vedic authority, rituals, sacrifice and Brahmana supremacy; non-theism
- Accept karma and rebirth

### Jain philosophy — terms and doctrines → meaning
## Doctrines
- ★ Anekantavada — core philosophy of Jainism: multiple viewpoints
- ★ Two main doctrines: Anekantavada and Syadvada; every being has many properties (permanent one = attribute)
- ★ Anekantavada and Syadvada — Jain; Sunyavada and Sarvastivada — Buddhist
- ★ Triratna (Right faith, Right knowledge, Right action) — Jainism, means to Moksha
- ★ Souls even in rocks and running water — Jainism
- universe = living souls + five non-living categories (e.g. akasa)
- ★ Four forms of existence do not include Yaksha (demi-gods)
- ★ Samadhi Maran (Sallekhana, Sanyasna Maran) — Jain last vow, slowly cutting food and drink
- ★ No final annihilation of the world in Jainism: universe eternal
- ★ Jainism and Buddhism both: karma and transmigration of soul
- ★ Rishabhanath — first Tirthankara; Parshvanath born at Varanasi
- ★ Ajivika: leader Makkhali Gosala, central idea Niyati (fate), regular sabhas
- Five Mahavratas of monks: Ahimsa, Satya, Brahmacharya, Asteya, Aparigraha
## Religion of Andhra dynasties
- ★ Ikshvaku royal women (Chantisiri, Bodhisiri) supported Buddhism (Nagarjunakonda)
- ★ Satavahana royal religion: Vedic, while supporting Buddhism
- ★ Early Kakatiyas: Jainism (as Western Chalukya feudatories); later Shaivism
### Panchamahavratas, Anuvratas and Jain practices — vows, rituals, Sallekhana
- ★ Gomateshwara statue at Shravanabelagola = Bahubali, not a Tirthankara
- Bahubali — son of the first Tirthankara Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)
### Jainism and Buddhism compared
- ★ Common to both: indifference to Vedas, denial of rituals, non-injury to animals (non-Vedic śramaṇa traditions)
- Not common: avoiding extremes of penance and pleasure
- ★ Self-mortification — not common between Buddhism and Jainism
- ★ Anatmavada (no-self) — Buddhism; from Pratityasamutpada
