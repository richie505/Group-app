### Terms → meaning (green economy to greenwashing)
## 1. Green economy and green technology
- Green economy:
- Reduces environmental risks and ecological scarcity
- Decouples growth from resource use and pollution
- Central theme of Rio+20, 2012
- Green jobs: Work in farming, industry, services or administration that preserves or restores environmental quality
- Green technology: Pollutes less, uses resources sustainably, recycles more of its waste
- ⚠ Trap: Incorrect statement = "it is not expensive for developing countries"; high cost is its main barrier (PYQ key)
## 2. Circular economy
- Definition: Economic system built on reuse, recycling and waste minimisation
- PYQ answer: "promotes the reuse, recycling and repair of materials to reduce waste"
- ⚠ Trap: does not focus exclusively on carbon or fossil fuels
| Linear model | Circular model |
| Take, make, dispose | Reduce, reuse, repair, recycle; keep materials in use [GK] |
| Waste is the end point | Waste becomes a resource, which decouples growth from resource use (CDI) |
## 3. Other terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Green Pharmacy | Green chemistry applied to design, use and disposal of medicines |
| ESG | Three pillars: Environmental, Social, Governance |
| Greenwashing | False or exaggerated claims that a product, firm or investment is environmentally friendly |
### Programmes and initiatives → year and aim — India
## 1. India
- Green Skill Development Programme:
- Launch: 2017 by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, building on the ENVIS network
- Purpose: Trains youth for green jobs → biodiversity conservation, pollution monitoring, waste management, forestry and wildlife, allied environment sectors
- Green Hydrogen Mission: Outlay Rs 19,744 crore; target 5 MMT a year; net zero by 2070
- India's action plans: NITI Aayog + MoEFCC committees → plans for priority waste streams (plastics, e-waste, batteries, tyres, metals)
- MSME schemes:
- MSE-GIFT: Green finance (2023-26)
- AMRUT 2.0: Reuse treated water for 20% of city demand + 40% of industrial demand
- Draft National Resource Efficiency Policy (2019): MoEFCC; promotes secondary raw materials + a National Resource Efficiency Authority
- EPR regimes: Plastic packaging, e-waste, batteries, tyres
- EPR results (2025-26): 308 lakh tonnes of plastic, e-waste and batteries recycled; 296.53 lakh tonnes of EPR certificates generated
- Green Credit Programme: Notified by MoEFCC in October 2023; market-based incentive for voluntary environmental actions (tree plantation, water conservation)
### Programmes and initiatives → year and aim — International
## 1. International
- European Green Deal (December 2019):
- Climate-neutral EU by 2050
- At least 55% GHG cut by 2030 vs 1990
- Legislative package = "Fit for 55"
| Component | Content |
| Farm to Fork | Sustainable food system |
| Circular Economy Action Plan | Resource efficiency, recycling |
| Biodiversity Strategy | Protect and restore ecosystems |
| Just Transition Fund | Support for affected regions |
| Renovation Wave | Energy-efficient buildings |
- UNEP Green Economy Initiative: Launched 2008
- PAGE (Partnership for Action on Green Economy): Launched 2013 in response to the Rio+20 call; brings together UNEP, ILO, UNIDO, UNDP and UNITAR
- ⚠ UNEP GEI 2008 → Rio+20 2012 → PAGE 2013 → GSDP 2017 → European Green Deal 2019
### Green Chemistry and Cleaner Production — persons and principles
- 12 Principles of Green Chemistry: Introduced 1998 by Paul Anastas and John Warner
- Waste prevention rather than cleanup
- Link with EIA & LCA: Green chemistry + cleaner production reduce waste, minimise pollution and ensure sustainability through the whole product life cycle → serves goals of EIA and Life-Cycle Assessment (PYQ key)
### Green finance instruments → fact
| Instrument | Use of proceeds / fact |
| Green bond | Environment-friendly projects: renewables, green transport, climate. Pioneers: EIB 2007, World Bank 2008 |
| Sovereign Green Bonds | Announced in Budget 2022-23; framework 2022; first issue 2023, Rs 8,000 crore |
| Blue bond | Ocean and water projects; world's first sovereign blue bond: Seychelles 2018 |
- India's first Blue Bond: Sagarmala Finance Corporation Ltd, Rs 1,000 crore, planned for FY 2026-27
- RBI Green Deposit Framework: Lets banks raise deposits earmarked for green lending; in force since 2023
- Climate finance taxonomy:
- Announced in Budget 2024-25
- Ministry of Finance's Climate Finance Taxonomy + Ministry of Steel's Green Steel Taxonomy
- Gaps: no final green taxonomy yet; Rs 35 crore per-borrower PSL cap
- ⚠ FIRSTs: green bond EIB 2007; sovereign blue bond Seychelles 2018; India's blue bond Sagarmala
### ESG regulation — BRSR, ratings, taxonomy as remedy
- BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting): Set by SEBI; top 1,000 listed companies must publish audited ESG metrics every year
- ESG ratings: SEBI regulates ESG Rating Providers
- Public financial institutions: Publish Green Bond Frameworks + ESG financing policies; carry ESG impact ratings
- Remedy for greenwashing: A clear taxonomy so regulators can curb greenwashing and "transition washing"
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- SEBI BRSR + taxonomy gap discussed at the Bonn Climate Conference 2026
- Criticism & debates:
- Greenwashing risk without a taxonomy
- Few ULBs issue municipal green bonds (Ghaziabad 2021, Indore 2023 did) → most lack credit ratings
