### Stratification — definition, bases and features
## 1. Bases of stratification in India
- Traditional: Caste, varna, tribe, religion
- Modern: Education, occupation, income, political power, urbanization
## 2. Definition and features
- Stratification: Ranking of groups in a hierarchy of unequal wealth, prestige and power
- Bases tested: Caste, class, gender, age, race, ethnicity
- ⚠ Trap: Language is NOT a basis of social stratification (APPSC-GS 2019)
- Characteristics (Melvin Tumin): Social (not biological), ancient, universal, diverse in form, consequential (decides life chances)
### Caste terms — Varna vs Jati, origins and classification
## 1. Caste terms
- Varna: A four-fold social division; Jati: a birth-based caste group
- Varna = theoretical, Vedic, four-fold; Jati = real, innumerable local groups
- Ranking principle = ritual purity and pollution
- Sanskritization: The adoption of upper-caste practices; Westernization: the adoption of Western values
- Dominant caste: An economically and politically powerful caste
- Untouchability: Social exclusion based on caste; casteism: loyalty to one's own caste
## 2. Origins and classification
- Origins: Harappan social differentiation (citadel vs lower town), no rigid caste; Varna system crystallised in the Later Vedic period
- Classification: Vertical (hierarchical ranking by birth) + horizontal (division into many separate groups)
- Non-Hindus: Muslims - Ashraf, Ajlaf, Arzal
- Exam angle: "which is NOT a basis" type questions; Green's definition; Varna vs Jati.

### Social mobility — types, ascribed vs achieved status
## 1. Social mobility
- Social mobility: The movement of individuals from one social position to another — movement between social positions
| Type | Meaning |
| Vertical | Upward or downward movement; up or down the hierarchy |
| Horizontal | A change with the same status — change without status change (e.g., one clerical job to another) |
| Intergenerational | Between generations; change between parents and children |
| Intragenerational | Within a lifetime; change within one person's lifetime |
| Structural | Mobility caused by change in the economy's occupational structure (e.g., industrialisation), not by individual effort |
- Example of upward mobility: A farmer's son becoming an IAS officer
- Group mobility in caste:
- Sanskritisation (M.N. Srinivas): lower castes copy rituals and lifestyle of higher castes
- Kshatriyaisation (Kulke): martial/landholding groups claim warrior (Kshatriya) status
## 2. Ascribed vs achieved status
| Basis | Ascribed status | Achieved status |
| Obtained | By birth | Through personal effort |
| Examples | Caste, gender, family background | An IAS Officer, a Doctor, a Scientist |
- Increased achieved status in modern India: Education, urbanization, industrialization, reservation policies, globalization

### Systems of stratification — caste vs class, estate, slavery
| System | Features | Examples |

### Caste status, hereditary occupations and recent change
## 1. Caste status
- The social position occupied by an individual within the caste hierarchy — determined by birth; a fixed social position
- Linked with occupation; determines social prestige
## 2. Hereditary occupations
- Hereditary characteristics, occupations, rights and positions are passed from one generation to another
- Hereditary position: Kingship; hereditary right: property ownership
- Hereditary occupations: Traditional occupations; family businesses
- In the caste system: Potter, carpenter, blacksmith, weaver
- Weakened by education, industrialization, urbanization and occupational mobility
## 3. Caste and class today
- Caste link: Agrarian hierarchy broadly matches the caste hierarchy; upper castes own land
- Recent change:
- Inter-caste conflicts now about power, not ritual status
- Jajmani system has weakened
- Caste dominance rests on secular factors

### Mobility routes — education, reservation, migration, Dalit and OBC
- ES 2025-26 (Ch. 13) definition: Inter-generational and intra-generational change in status
- Key determinants: health, education, technology, institutions
- Low mobility entrenches historical inequality
- Education: Better education → better jobs + higher social standing; emerging class system allows upward mobility
- Reservation:
- Southern backward classes (Self-Respect Movement of Periyar) demanded reservation in government jobs
- Led to group mobility
- Broadened the urban middle class with backward classes and Dalits
- Occupational mobility: Ezhavas of Kerala moved from toddy tapping to tailoring and cottage industries
- Migration and urbanisation: City anonymity + caste-blind public spaces weaken caste identity; but migrants carry caste networks and prejudices into cities
- Dalit change - three avenues: State policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation/Westernisation
- Contemporary demands: Dominant peasant castes (Marathas, Gujjars, Patels) seek OBC status for "greater life chances"
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: Gen Z "horizontal" category of young Brahmin, Dalit and OBC voters sharing job and exam worries cuts across vertical caste loyalties
- Significance: ES 2025-26: poverty fell from 21.9% (2011-12) to 2.3% (2023-24)
- AP angle: Kakatiya society stratified but relatively open; martial castes (Velamas, Kammas, Reddys) formed military and local administration
- Criticism & debates: Kshatriyaisation claims (Marathas, Jats, Yadavs) bring mobility but reproduce the hierarchy
