### Mortimer Wheeler's invasion theory — evidence and rejection
- Wheeler (1947): Blamed Aryan invaders
- Cited skeletons huddled together at Mohenjodaro + Indra as "fort-breaker" (Purandara)
- Rejected:
- No archaeological evidence of invasion
- Rig Veda post-dates the decline
- "Massacre" skeletons = multiple burials of different periods
- Decline was gradual
### Evidence → theory (floods, rivers, ecology, climate)
| Theory | Evidence |
| Floods | Indus changed course → repeatedly flooded, then abandoned settlements such as Mohenjodaro; Chanhudaro flooded several times |
| Dried rivers | Ghaggar-Hakra system (densest cluster of settlements) dried up - tectonic shifts, less rainfall |
| Ecology | Deforestation, soil salinity, overgrazing; exhaustion of copper and timber; failing drainage upkeep |
| Disease | Malaria outbreak detected at Mohenjodaro |
| Palaeoclimate | Weakening monsoon + rising aridity c. 1900 BCE |
- IIT Kharagpur study: Continuous weak monsoon/low rainfall for about 900 years caused decline (BPSC 2018 key: 900 years)
- Exam angle: IIT Kharagpur study group - continuous low rainfall for 900 years (BPSC 2018).
- Newest evidence - Deoria Tal (Uttarakhand) (30 Jul 2026, PIB):
- Abrupt rise in oak-to-pine ratio → summer monsoon weakened sharply about 4,200 years ago ("4.2 ka event")
- Cut water in Indus and Ghaggar-Hakra → drought + eastward migration
- ⚠ Trap: 900 years = IIT Kharagpur low-rainfall span; 4,200 years ago = Deoria Tal 4.2 ka event; 200 years = pace of decline
### De-urbanisation and the Late Harappan phase — features
## 1. De-urbanisation
- Pace: Gradual (about 200 years); regional differences in timing
- "Sudden catastrophe" view of early scholars = rejected
- Outcome: Cities → small rural settlements, regional cultures; many sites abandoned
- Migration: East and south
- Eastward: To the Ganga-Yamuna Doab
- Southward: To Gujarat and the Deccan
## 2. Late Harappan phase
- Nature: Primarily Chalcolithic - tools of stone and copper
- No metal objects needing complicated casting (APPSC-G1 2024, statements I and II correct)
- Period: c. 1900-1300 BCE - post-urban
- Settlement pattern: De-urbanisation → smaller rural settlements
- Pottery: Standard designs → varied regional styles
- Uniformity lost: Seals, script, weights, bricks decline / lose uniformity
- Economy: Long-distance trade weakens; local subsistence farming grows

### Continuity — OCP, Copper Hoards, Harappan traits and Vedic links
## 1. OCP and Copper Hoards
- OCP: Listed as a Late Harappan culture with Cemetery-H (Jun 2026); Harappan legacy may have shaped OCP
- Copper Hoards: Often found with OCP in Ganga-Yamuna Doab → commonly linked
- Carriers: Cemetery H + OCP carry traits into later cultures
## 2. Continuity of Harappan traits
- Religious: Pipal + Mother Goddess worship, linga-yoni, fire altars, ritual bathing (→ later temple tanks and ghats); burial practices
## 3. Continuity vs discontinuity with Vedic culture
- Continuity view:
- Ghaggar-Hakra = Rigvedic Sarasvati
- PGW sites overlap Late Harappan - e.g. Bhagwanpura: 13-roomed house with Late Harappan pottery + PGW
- Discontinuity view: Horse, chariot, iron absent in Mature Harappan but central later
- Consensus: Limited folk continuity + major shift with Indo-Aryan speakers
- Rakhigarhi DNA: Female DNA shows no "Steppe Pastoralist" gene → challenges invasion theories; suggests indigenous continuity
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Deoria Tal pollen study (30 Jul 2026) links decline to the 4.2 ka monsoon failure → backs drought theory
- Rakhigarhi DNA + new skeletal study by AnSI (Jun 2026)
- Criticism & debates:
- Aryan invasion rejected; migration and continuity still argued
- Invasion vs internal decline; gradual vs sudden; climate as sole cause vs multi-causal
- AP angle: Copper-age (Chalcolithic) remains at Patapadu (Kurnool) and in Krishna district; Neolithic-Chalcolithic settlements overlap
- Comparative note: 4.2 ka aridity event also linked with fall of Akkad in Mesopotamia
