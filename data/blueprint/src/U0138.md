### Tomb tradition — evolution and FIRSTs
## 1. Humayun's tomb and Humayun-nama
- Humayun-nama: Written by Gulbadan Begum, Humayun's sister
- Humayun's tomb, Delhi:
- Builder: widow Haji Begum (Bega Begum) = standard answer; some notes say Hamida Banu Begum (Akbar's mother)
- First garden tomb in the subcontinent
- First full Persian-style double dome in Mughal architecture (an earlier double dome exists at Sikandar Lodi's tomb)
- Red sandstone with white marble inlay
## 2. Evolution
| Stage | Feature |
| Sher Shah's tomb | Sur transition |
| Itimad-ud-Daula ("Baby Taj") | First Mughal tomb in white marble with pietra dura; pietra dura began in Jahangir's reign |
| Bibi ka Maqbara ("Taj of the Deccan") | Late imitation |
- ⚠ "Baby Taj" = Itimad-ud-Daula; "Taj of the Deccan" = Bibi ka Maqbara
### Architectural terms → meaning and where first seen
| Feature | Meaning | Key example / where first or best seen |
| Double dome | Inner and outer shells | Introduced at Humayun's tomb |
| Pietra dura | Inlay of semi-precious stones | Began under Jahangir; first on a large scale in Itimad-ud-Daula; peak at the Taj Mahal |
| Charbagh | Four-quartered garden | Introduced by Babur (Ram Bagh, Agra) |
| Persian-Indian synthesis | Arch and dome + trabeate, chhatris, Hindu motifs | Fatehpur Sikri, Akbar's tomb |
| Sandstone vs marble | Akbar: red sandstone; Shah Jahan: white marble | Red sandstone in Akbar's phase (Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri); white marble: transition under Jahangir (Itimad-ud-Daula), dominant under Shah Jahan (Taj Mahal) |
- Other hallmarks:
- Calligraphy, arabesque, jali (lattice) work (Humayun's tomb)
- Mirror work in the Sheesh Mahal
- Red sandstone for structure + marble for decoration (Red Fort)
### Fatehpur Sikri — buildings → feature
- Buland Darwaza at Fatehpur Sikri: Associated with Akbar — commemorates Akbar's victory over Gujarat
- Planned capital: Akbar, 1571-1585; built in honour of Sheikh Salim Chishti
- Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site
- Material: Mainly red sandstone

### Taj Mahal — architect, marble source, historians' views, court case
## 1. Basic facts
- Builder & place: Shah Jahan at Agra, on the right bank of the Yamuna
- For: Mausoleum of Mumtaz Mahal (Arjumand Banu Begum), Shah Jahan's wife
- Built: 1632-1648 (older notes give 1653, year the whole complex was finished)
- Chief architect: Ustad Ahmad Lahori
- Marble source: Makrana (Rajasthan) (APPSC GS 2016)
- Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site
## 2. Style and design
- Features: Pinnacle of pietra dura and symmetry; white marble
- Layout: Tomb at the river end of its charbagh, not at the garden's centre
- Design ancestry: Humayun's tomb (garden tomb, double dome, red sandstone with marble) inspired it
## 3. Historians → views on style origin
| Historian | View |
| V.A. Smith | Combination of European and Asiatic genius (APPSC-G1 2022 key) |
| James Fergusson, Percy Brown | Product of the Indo-Persian tradition |
- Other options: James Fergusson, Percy Brown, R.C. Majumdar
## 4. Pollution threat and case
- Supreme Court: Taj Trapezium Zone (TTZ) rulings in M.C. Mehta case (1996)

### Universal angles
- Significance: Zenith of Indo-Islamic architecture
- Blends Persian, Timurid and Indian elements
- Red sandstone + white marble, charbagh, double dome, pietra dura, calligraphy
- AP angle: Qutb Shahi parallels
- Char Minar (1591) / Charminar (1591): Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, marked end of a plague; Persian arches + Deccani stucco
- Golconda Fort: acoustic warning system, piped water
- Qutb Shahi tombs at Ibrahim Bagh: onion domes on square bases, in gardens
- ⚠ Trap: Char Minar is a common distractor in Mughal lists
- FIRST/LAST:
- First Mughal garden = Ram Bagh
- Pietra dura first under Jahangir
- Bibi ka Maqbara = last major imitation
- Comparative note: Bahmani Jama Masjid at Gulbarga has a fully covered courtyard, unlike Mughal open-court mosques
