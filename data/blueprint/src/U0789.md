### Air pollution sources — natural, anthropogenic and fuel emissions
## 1. Sources
- Natural: Forest fires; volcanic eruptions
- Anthropogenic: Fuel combustion (a major source); automobiles; industries; fossil-fuel burning
| Basis | Types / examples |
| Natural sources | Forest fires, volcanic eruptions, dust storms (APPSC 2025 answer: forest fires) |
| Anthropogenic sources | Vehicles, power plants, industry, biomass burning, agriculture (ammonia, methane), waste burning |
- Main source in Indian cities: automobile emissions (APPSC 2025)
- Also asked as Primary source in Indian cities: automobile emissions
- Delhi = city most often in the news for bad air
- NCR: transport = largest primary source → 23% of winter load (CAQM synthesis report, Jan 2026)
- Vehicle/fuel combustion products: CO, NOx, VOCs, PM, unburnt hydrocarbons, lead
## 2. Fuel → emissions
| Fuel | Emissions |
| Coal | High CO₂; SO₂, NOₓ, particulate matter, ash — severe air pollution; especially high pollution among petrol, diesel and coal |
| Petrol and diesel | CO₂, CO, NOₓ, unburnt hydrocarbons, particulate matter — diesel produces more particulate matter than petrol; higher pollution levels |
| LPG and CNG | Lower CO, (unburnt) hydrocarbons and particulate emissions than petrol and diesel — cleaner, but still produce CO₂ |
| Biogas | Low emissions — mainly CO₂ and traces of H₂S when untreated; renewable and relatively clean when purified |
| Hydrogen | The cleanest fuel — zero carbon emissions; only water (vapour) when burned or used in fuel cells (pure combustion); zero CO₂, CO, SO₂ and particulate emissions |
| Wood and cow-dung | Smoke, CO and particulate matter — indoor air pollution |
### Primary vs secondary pollutants and smog
## 1. Smog
- Smog: Smoke + fog; photochemical smog — from nitrogen oxides, hydrocarbons and sunlight; PAN (peroxyacyl nitrate) is associated with it
## 2. Primary vs secondary
| Type | Examples |
| Primary pollutants (emitted directly) | CO, SO2, NOx (NO), PM, lead |
| Secondary pollutants (formed in air) | O3, PAN (ozone), photochemical smog, ammonium sulphate, ammonium nitrate |
## 3. Smog types and the Asian Brown Cloud
| Smog | Formed from | Conditions |
| Classical / industrial (London) | SO2, smoke, particulates + fog | Cold, humid |
| Photochemical (Los Angeles) | NOx + VOCs + sunlight → ozone, PAN | Warm, sunny |
- Asian Brown Cloud: Soot + black carbon + aerosols over South Asia and Indian Ocean in dry season; from biomass and fossil-fuel burning
- ⚠ London smog = cold + SO2; Los Angeles smog = warm + sunlight

### Acid rain — definition, FIRST, causes and chemistry — Definition, pH and FIRST
## 1. Definition, pH and FIRST
- Acid rain: Any precipitation (rain, snow, fog, mist, hail) with pH below 5.6
- Typical acid rain pH 4.2-4.4
- pH 7 = neutral; each pH step = tenfold change in acidity
- Normal rain: Slightly acidic (~5.6) → CO2 dissolves to weak carbonic acid
- ⚠ Trap: CO2 alone does not make "acid rain" in the exam sense
- Term coined: 1872 by Robert Angus Smith (coal burning in Manchester)
### Acid rain — definition, FIRST, causes and chemistry — Causes
## 1. Causes
- Main culprits: SO2 and NOx (APPSC 2018 answer: "SO2 and NO2"); main single factor = SO2
- ⚠ Trap: CO, O3, CH4, CFCs are NOT causes
| Gas | Anthropogenic sources | Natural sources |
| SOx (SO2, SO3) | Coal-fired thermal power plants, smelting of metal sulphide ores, sulphuric acid plants, oil refineries | Volcanoes, seas/oceans, decomposition of organic matter |
| NOx (NO, NO2, N2O) | Vehicles, power plants, fossil-fuel burning, biomass burning | Lightning, forest fires, oceans |
- Exam angle: "which gases cause acid rain" and "excess of which causes acid rain" (RPSC: NOx + SO2 only).
### Acid rain — definition, FIRST, causes and chemistry — Chemistry
## 1. Chemistry
- Reaction: SO2 + NOx + oxygen + water + photo-oxidants (sunlight-formed ozone) → sulphuric acid (H2SO4) + nitric acid (HNO3)
- Relative share: Sulphuric acid ~two-thirds; nitric acid ~one-third of acidity
| Wet deposition | Dry deposition |
| Acids fall with rain, snow, fog as sulphate, nitrate, ammonium, H+ ions | Acidic gases and particles stick to dust/smoke and settle on surfaces in dry areas |
| Occurs in humid regions | About half of atmospheric acidity returns this way (APP) |
### Acid rain — effects, Taj Mahal case and regions affected
## 1. Effects by target
| Target | Effect |
| Lakes and fish | Acidification lowers pH; water becomes inhospitable to fish, frogs, insects |
| Forests and soil | Leaches calcium and magnesium; releases toxic aluminium that damages roots; trees become susceptible to cold, pests, drought |
| Buildings and monuments | Corrodes limestone and marble, metals, paint |
| Human health | Indirect: releases heavy metals from soil into drinking water |
## 2. Taj Mahal — term, zone and case
- Marble cancer: Acid rain decays limestone and marble (CaCO3) buildings, e.g. Taj Mahal → yellow/lustreless = "marble cancer"
- Taj threat: Mainly SO2 (APPSC 2018) from industries around Agra, notably Mathura refinery
- Taj Trapezium Zone (TTZ): ~10,400 sq km trapezium around the Taj → protects Taj, Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri (2026)
- Agra, Firozabad, Mathura, Hathras, Etah (UP) + Bharatpur (Rajasthan)
- M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (1996): SC barred coal/coke use by TTZ industries → shift to natural gas or relocation
## 3. Regions most affected
- Nature: Pollutants carried hundreds of km by wind → transboundary problem
| Region | Feature |
| Scandinavia | Sweden/Norway lakes ← UK and central European emissions |
| Industrial China | Classic case |
| India | largest SO2 emitter globally (2022) → over 20% of anthropogenic emissions (CERA) |

### Sources & smog
## Sources
- Main source of air pollution in Indian cities — automobile emissions
- Natural sources — forest fires, volcanic eruptions, dust storms (APPSC 2025: forest fires)
- Natural source of SOx — volcanoes; of NOx — lightning
- Cadmium — metal production and smelting; PAHs — incomplete burning; ozone — photochemical reactions
- Hydrogen sulphide — rotten-egg smell (sewage, refineries, volcanoes); a toxic pollutant
- Methane — potent greenhouse gas
- Lichens disappear where SO2 is high; mosses show heavy-metal deposition
## Smog and haze
- Photochemical smog — Los Angeles, warm sunny weather; classical smog — London, cold humid weather
- Sunlight makes vehicle NOx and hydrocarbons react into ozone and PAN → forms on sunny days in traffic-heavy cities
- Asian Brown Cloud — dry-season layer of soot, black carbon, aerosols over South Asia and Indian Ocean; from biomass and fossil-fuel burning
## Cleaner fuels
- CNG — mainly methane; SATAT — compressed biogas
- Ethanol Blending Programme — E20-compatible vehicles (not compressed biogas)
- Fuel injection — helps complete combustion
- Hydrogen — cleanest fuel; gives only water (no carbon, CO, SO2, particles)
- CNG and LPG — less CO, hydrocarbons, PM than petrol/diesel, but still emit CO2
### Pollutant → health effect
- Carbon monoxide — reduces blood's oxygen-carrying capacity; lead — brain damage; SO2 — irritates eyes, airways
- Ground-level ozone — formed from NOx and VOCs in sunlight (not emitted directly); harms lungs and plants
- Stratospheric ozone shields from UV; CFCs deplete it
- Ammonia (fertilisers, livestock) — drives secondary particle making (ammonium salts)
### Acid rain
## Pollutant facts
- ★ Steel industry pollutants — SOx, NOx, CO and CO2 (all four; coke making, blast furnace, fuel combustion)
- ★ Gases used in AQI calculation (of CO2, CO, NO2, SO2, methane) — CO, NO2, SO2; AQI launched April 2015 under Swachh Bharat; six categories (Good to Severe); 8 pollutants
- ★ Acid present in maximum amount in acid rain — H2SO4 (sulphuric acid; also nitric acid)
- Primary pollutant — lead from vehicles; ozone, PAN, ammonium sulphate form in the atmosphere (vehicles do not emit it directly)
- Vehicles emit CO, NOx, VOCs, PM, unburnt hydrocarbons
- PM2.5 — crosses the placenta; linked to pre-term birth, low birth weight (AIIMS study, 3 June 2026)
- Indoor pollution — not only biological agents: dust, smoke, pesticides, gases, mosquito coils
## Acid rain
- Acid rain — pH below 5.6; typical 4.2–4.4
- Main single factor — SO2 (SO2 and NOx principal gases)
- Sulphuric acid — about two-thirds of acidity; nitric acid — one-third
- About half returns by dry deposition
- Transboundary problem — Scandinavian lakes acidified by UK and central European emissions
- Forest soils: drains calcium, magnesium; releases toxic aluminium; lowers lake pH
- Marble cancer — yellowing and loss of shine of marble (Taj Mahal)
- Taj Trapezium Zone — protects Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri (not Sanchi Stupa)
- Liming — remedial (neutralises acidity), not preventive
## Control
- NCAP — CPCB coordinates with SPCBs (not CPCB alone)
- Catalytic converters — platinum, palladium, rhodium
- Cloud seeding — silver iodide, potassium iodide, dry ice, common salt
- LPG (like biogas) — burns without smoke; wood, cow-dung give smoke, CO, fine particles

- Lichens — bioindicators of SO2
