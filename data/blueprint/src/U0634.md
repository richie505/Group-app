### Seismic zoning map and BIS codes
## 1. Seismic zoning map
- Official map (IS 1893, BIS): 4 zones: II, III, IV, V
- 2002 revision: After the 1993 Killari quake (struck a low-risk area), old Zone I into Zone II merged
- BIS revised zoning map: Probabilistic hazard assessment (PSHA)
- Drew on 69,519 events (2600 BC-2019) + 36 seismogenic source zones (IIT Madras study, 22 Mar 2026)
- The Centre rolled the revision back in March 2026 after backlash over methodology and cost (8 and 12 Mar 2026) → four-zone map still applies
- Exam angle: "zones merged" (I into II), and Zone VI as a proposed-then-withdrawn change.

### Hazards → regions — earthquakes and landslides
## 1. Hazard → region
| Hazard | Region |
| Landslides | Himalaya, NE, Western Ghats |
| Liquefaction | Indo-Gangetic plain |
- Crustal strain: GSI monitors via 34 GNSS stations
## 2. Landslide hazard zonation
- Landslide Hazard Zonation (landslide zoning): Divides hill areas into homogeneous units by actual or potential susceptibility, hazard or risk (PYQ)
- Zonation maps are probabilistic
- Extent (GSI): 12.6-15% of India (~0.42 million sq km) landslide-prone
- Where: Mostly hilly areas (PYQ)
| Region | Why prone |
| Himalaya (J&K, Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal) | Young, fragile geology, active tectonics (CDI) |
| North-East | Steep slopes, heavy rain (CDI) |
| Western Ghats incl. Nilgiris | Steep escarpments, orographic rain (CDI) |
| Eastern Ghats, Vindhyas | Parts of AP, Odisha (CDI) |
### Landslide mitigation and early warning
## 4. Mitigation
- Structural measures: Retaining walls (against steep slopes); rock bolting; wire mesh; drainage improvement; slope grading
- Non-structural measures: Landslide Hazard Zonation mapping; Early Warning Systems; land-use regulation; awareness
- Regulating settlements in hazard-prone areas; construction in high-risk zones should be restricted; mountain-specific building codes should be enforced
- Early warning: GSI building a regional Landslide Early Warning System under the LANDSLIP project
### Cyclone and tsunami coasts → warning centres
## 3. Warning
- Tsunami Warning Centre in India: Hyderabad — the Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC), established in INCOIS (Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services, Hyderabad; MoES), operating since 2007; uses seismic monitoring, ocean buoys, sirens
- International Tsunami Information Centre: Honolulu
- Remote sensing, GIS and satellite data address tsunami and coastal disaster vulnerability — GIS is spatial data, a disaster-management tool
- ⚠ Tsunami warning = INCOIS, Hyderabad (not IMD)
## 1. Cyclone-prone coasts
- Both coasts: Cyclones from the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea
- East coast gets far more: Bay of Bengal generates about four times as many as the Arabian Sea
- Exposure: 13 coastal states/UTs + 84 coastal districts cyclone-affected
- Most cyclone-prone state: Andhra Pradesh (PYQ, APPSC-GS 2025) → Odisha, TN, WB
