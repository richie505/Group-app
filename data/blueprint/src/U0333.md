### Federalism — definitions, types and FIRSTs
| Type | Meaning |
| Cooperative federalism | Centre and States work together on a common platform |
| Cooperative federalism (NITI Aayog's version) | brings states together on a single platform through a bottom-up approach; also called "marble-cake federalism" |
| Competitive federalism | Competition among States (and between States and the Centre in policy) for investment and citizens |
| Competitive sub-federalism | Competitive sub-federalism = local bodies competing with local bodies, i.e. competition among local bodies (APPSC-G1 2017 key) |
| Cooperative federalism — First recommended by | the Sarkaria Commission (APPSC-G1 2017 key) |
- NITI planning under cooperative federalism: credible village-level plans aggregated upwards
- Competitive federalism:
- Tool: better policies and services → attract investment, improve outcomes
- Driver: NITI's performance rankings
- Competitive sub-federalism example: district rankings (ADP "Champions of Change" dashboard)
- Usual pairing: GST Council = cooperative federalism vs NITI Aayog rankings = competitive federalism
- ⚠ Competitive federalism = States compete; competitive sub-federalism = local bodies compete
### NITI Aayog and its predecessors — how established, status and composition
## 1. Three kinds of body
| Type | Basis | Examples |
| Constitutional | Directly established, empowered and anchored within the text of the Constitution of India | Election Commission, Public Service Commissions, Finance Commission, CAG, Attorney General, Advocate General, National Commissions for SCs, STs and Backward Classes, State Finance and Election Commissions |
| Statutory | Created by an Act of Parliament or a State Legislature | National Human Rights Commission, Lokpal, National Green Tribunal |
| Extra-constitutional / non-statutory | Established through an executive order or cabinet resolution | NITI Aayog, Planning Commission, National Development Council |
## 2. NITI Aayog and its predecessors
| Body | How established | Status |
| Planning Commission | Cabinet resolution, 1950[GK] | Extra-constitutional; abolished and replaced by NITI |
| National Development Council | Executive resolution, 1952[GK] | Extra-constitutional; now inactive, its role taken by NITI's Governing Council (IYB 2026) |
| NITI Aayog | Union Cabinet resolution, 1 January 2015 | Non-constitutional, non-statutory think tank; advisory only |
- Created: Union Cabinet resolution of 1 January 2015; replaced the Planning Commission
- Legal status: Non-constitutional, non-statutory - by a Union Cabinet resolution (UPPSC PYQ answer), not by an Act or Ordinance
- Financial powers: no financial powers (no plan grants) → Finance Commission's domain restored (Jan 2026)

### NITI Aayog — functions, hubs and mandate

### Articles, Acts and institutions of Centre-State cooperation
| Institution | Basis | Key provision |
| GST Council | Art. 279A | Centre and states decide jointly; Centre one-third of votes, states two-thirds |
| Inter-State Council | Art. 263 | Set up 1990 on Sarkaria's recommendation; recommendatory; Chairman: the PM |
| Zonal Councils | Statutory, under Part III of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956 (proposed by Nehru) | Chair: Union Home Minister (all five); each State: CM + 2 ministers; advisory only |
| River boards | River Boards Act, 1956 | - |
- ⚠ Inter-State Council chair = PM; Zonal Councils chair = Union Home Minister
- GST Council critique:
- Curtailed states' taxation powers (many local taxes subsumed)
- Compensation disputes
## Centrally sponsored schemes (CSS)
- Nature: Large part of Centre-State transfers; require matching State contributions, parameters decided by the Centre
- Funding pattern (core schemes):
- Usual = 60:40
- North-Eastern & Himalayan States = 90:10
- Debate: CSS squeeze State priorities
- Fixes: revisit CSS + greater State say in GST design + regular ISC meetings

### Types of federalism
- Cooperative federalism — first suggested by the Sarkaria Commission; put into practice by NITI Aayog (2015)
- "Marble-cake federalism" (NITI Aayog) — Centre and States on a common platform, bottom-up

- Competitive sub-federalism — competition among local bodies / districts; e.g. Aspirational Districts Programme rankings
### Kinds of body
- ★ Extra-constitutional — power/rights not found in the Constitution; e.g. Planning Commission (Cabinet resolution)
- Constitutional body — set up by the Constitution itself (ECI, CAG)
- Statutory body — made by an Act of Parliament / State Legislature; e.g. NHRC, Lokpal, NGT
