### Parliamentary form — features and defining principle
- Features of the parliamentary form (UPSC) - India vs USA
| Parliamentary (India) | Presidential (USA) |
| Nominal and real executives - nominal (President) and real (PM) | President is both head of State and government |
| Majority party rule | Fixed-term executive, independent of legislature |
| Collective responsibility to Lok Sabha | No legislative responsibility of executive |
| Ministers are MPs (dual membership / double membership) | Secretaries are not members of Congress |
| Leadership of PM; LS can be dissolved (dissolution of the Lok Sabha) | No dissolution |
| Political homogeneity; secrecy of procedure | Separation of powers |
- Defining feature: India = parliamentary because of collective responsibility (not separation of powers or judicial review) (APPSC 2025)
- Necessary features: Majority party rule + collective responsibility + PM as centre of power (APPSC 2019)
- Why chosen: Experience under the 1919 and 1935 Acts + to avoid a personality cult
- Underlying principle of Cabinet form: a mechanism of parliamentary democracy for ensuring collective responsibility of the Government to the people (UPSC 2017 key)
- ⚠ Defining feature = collective responsibility; separation of powers = the USA column

### Collective responsibility — Article 75(3)
## 1. Collective responsibility
- Collective responsibility — Article 75(3): Only to the Lok Sabha — not to the Rajya Sabha, Parliament as a whole or the President
- "Sink or swim together": The entire Council, including the PM, resigns if a No-Confidence Motion is passed or the government loses a confidence vote / its majority in the Lok Sabha (also on defeat on a money bill/major policy → whole ministry resigns)
- Ministers from the Rajya Sabha are also collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
- No-confidence motion: Only in the Lok Sabha
- Defeat in the Rajya Sabha does not bring down the government / force resignation
- Loss of LS confidence → whole ministry resigns
- PM's death/resignation: Dissolves the Council
- Binding decisions: Cabinet decisions bind all ministers even if they differed inside; a dissenting minister unwilling to defend a decision must resign (UPSC)
- Homogeneity & secrecy: Political homogeneity; secrecy of Cabinet deliberation ensured by the oath of secrecy → differences ironed out behind closed doors, public show of unity (UPSC)
### Individual responsibility — Article 75(2)
## 1. Individual responsibility
- Individual responsibility: Each Minister answerable to Parliament for administrative and bureaucratic lapses in his portfolio
- Basis: Art. 75(2) → ministers hold office at the President's pleasure
- PM can ask a minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him (UPSC)
- Personal statement: A resigned minister may explain his resignation in the House, with the Speaker's consent
- ⚠ 75(3) = collective (to LS); 75(2) = individual (President's pleasure)

### Cabinet Secretariat and Cabinet Secretary — functions and status
## 1. Cabinet Secretariat
- Status: Functions directly under the Prime Minister; a principal staff agency (UPSC)

### Attorney General — rights in Parliament (Art. 88) and limitations
## 1. Rights of AG in Parliament - Art. 88; right of audience
- Right of audience in all courts in India while on official duty (Advocate General: all courts in the State)
- Article 88 (rights in respect of the Houses of Parliament): Can speak and take part in the proceedings of either House (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha), joint sittings, and any Parliamentary Committee of which he is named a member
- ⚠ Cannot vote on any motion or bill → no right to vote (without vote)
- ⚠ Trap: Some practice questions (Oct 2025) give "Art. 78" for this right - it is Art. 88
- Article 105(4): Privileges and immunities of a Member of Parliament
## 2. Limitations on Attorney General
- Not a member of the Central Cabinet; not a full-time government servant — private practice not completely debarred, but subject to government restrictions
- Prep wording: not a full-time counsel; not debarred from private practice
- Limits:
- Must not advise or hold a brief against GoI, nor in cases where he advises GoI
- Not defend accused in criminal cases or become a company director without permission

### Parliamentary vs Presidential
- Nominal and real executives, dissolution of the lower House — parliamentary features
- Separation of powers — presidential system
- Collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers — clearest parliamentary sign

### Council of Ministers vs Cabinet
- Council of Ministers (Articles 74, 75) rarely meets as a body; the Cabinet meets regularly and takes policy decisions
- "Sink or swim together" — all ministers must defend Cabinet decisions or resign
- Kitchen cabinet criticised as an extra-constitutional power centre that may skip the Cabinet
### Responsibility
- Article 75(3) — collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha; no-confidence motion only there (even Rajya Sabha ministers answer to it)
- Article 164(2) — State Council collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly
- Defeat in the Rajya Sabha does not by itself force the ministry to resign
- PM's death or resignation — the whole Council of Ministers goes out of office
- Article 75(2) — Minister holds office during the President's pleasure; in practice PM can ask him to resign or advise sacking
- President gives oaths of office and secrecy to Union Ministers (Third Schedule)
- Oath of secrecy — keeps Cabinet discussions secret
### Cabinet committees (8)
## The committees
- Eight Cabinet Committees (April 2025): Appointments, Security, Political Affairs, Economic Affairs, Parliamentary Affairs, Accommodation, Investment and Growth, Employment and Skill Development
- Appointments Committee — senior appointments; Parliamentary Affairs — government business and sessions
- PM chairs Appointments, Security, Political Affairs; Accommodation — chaired by a senior minister
- Caste enumeration in the Census — decided by the Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs (not CCEA)
## Related terms
- Kitchen cabinet — informal inner circle of the PM's trusted colleagues and friends (some senior ministers and non-ministers)
- Cabinet dictatorship — criticism of Cabinet control over Parliament in single-party majority times
- Chief Secretary — State equivalent of the Cabinet Secretary

### Law officers
