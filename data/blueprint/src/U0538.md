### Terms → meaning: tax, fee, cess, surcharge
| Levy | Key feature / meaning |
| Tax | Compulsory contribution (pecuniary burden) imposed under authority of law to finance the government; no quid pro quo (no direct quid pro quo) (APPSC 2023) |
| Fee | Payment for a specific service (quid pro quo) |
| Cess | "Tax on tax" for a specific purpose; ends when purpose met; not shared with States (kept wholly by the Centre) |
| Surcharge | Additional (extra) charge on tax payable, % of tax; usually permanent; no surcharge if no tax is due; not shared with States (Art. 271) |
- Exam angle: "pecuniary burden imposed by legislative authority to support government" = Tax (APPSC 2023).
- Example cess: Clean Energy Cess on coal (Finance Act 2010) → renamed Clean Environment Cess (Budget 2016-17)
- Federal grievance: Cesses and surcharges are outside the divisible pool shared with States
### Articles and Amendments — constitutional basis of taxation
| Article / entry | Provision |
| Article 265 | No tax levied or collected except by authority of law |
| Taxing entries | Union List 15, State List 20, Concurrent List 3 |
| Residuary taxing power | Parliament (gift, wealth, expenditure taxes) |
| 80th Amendment, 2000 | Union taxes shared with States on FC advice (effective 1 April 1996) |
| Art. 276 | Business/profession tax is a State levy; cap ₹2,500 a year → not a Union source (APPSC 2018) |
| State List Entry 46 | Agricultural income tax: levied only by States |
- Union taxes: Income tax (non-agricultural), corporation tax, customs, Union excise, CGST/IGST
- Agricultural income: Exempt under Income-tax Act (Sec 10(1))
- Nursery saplings = agricultural income; poultry/dairy are not
### Direct vs indirect taxes — classification
| Basis | Direct tax | Indirect tax |
| Burden | Cannot be shifted to another person — incidence and impact fall on the same taxpayer | Can be shifted to another person — generally through prices (collected by an intermediary (seller) → passed on to consumer through price) |
| Examples | Income Tax, Corporation Tax, Wealth Tax, Capital Gains Tax, Property Tax, Gift Tax | Customs Duty, Excise Duty, Service Tax, Sales Tax, VAT, GST |
| Administered by | CBDT (Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963) | CBIC |
| Price effect | Generally non-inflationary | Inflationary |
- Impact vs incidence: Impact = on whom tax is first imposed; incidence = who finally bears it; shifting moves burden forward/backward
- PYQ: Income, corporate and wealth-type taxes → "all of the above" are direct (APPSC 2021)
- "Paper taxes": Wealth, gift, estate duty → never yielded much revenue
- Exam angle: "which is a direct tax" lists; STT is a direct tax even though collected by the exchange.
### Taxes → base and taxable event
## 1. Taxes and their base
| Tax | Type | Associated with / base |
| Income Tax | Direct (impact and incidence on same person) | Income — of individuals, HUFs, firms, trusts |
| Corporation Tax | Direct | Company income or profit — net profit of companies (22% base rate, effective ~25.17%) |
| Capital Gains Tax | Direct | Gains from the sale of a capital asset |
| Wealth Tax — a historical tax | Direct | Wealth — net wealth (1957; abolished 2015) |
| Securities Transaction Tax (STT) | Direct [GK] (CDI lists under direct) | Value of securities traded on stock exchanges (from 1 Oct 2004) |
| Estate duty, gift tax | Direct (abolished) | - |
| Customs Duty | Indirect (burden shifted to consumer) | The import or export of goods (Customs Act, 1962) |
| Excise Duty | Indirect | The production, sale or licensing of specified goods — Union excise: manufacture of goods (now mainly tobacco, petroleum, liquor by States) |
| Service Tax — a historical indirect tax | Indirect | Services (subsumed in GST) |
| VAT | Indirect | Value added at stages |
| Sales tax / VAT | Indirect | Now largely in GST |
| Entertainment, luxury, entry taxes | Indirect | - |
| GST | Indirect | Goods and services — supply of goods and services (from 1 July 2017) |
## 2. Taxable event for each tax
| Tax | Taxable event |
| Central excise | Manufacture/production of goods |
| Customs | Import or export of goods |
| Sales tax / VAT | Sale of goods |
| Service tax | Provision of service |
| GST | Supply of goods or services |
### Types — progressive, proportional, regressive taxes
| Tax | Feature | Indian example |
| Progressive | The average tax burden increases with income — rate rises with income ("ability to pay") | Income tax |
| Regressive | The relative burden tends to be higher on lower-income groups — poor pay higher share of income | Excise, customs, GST on essentials |
| Proportional | The same tax rate regardless of income level (uniform rate at all incomes) | Sales tax (flat rate) |
| Degressive | The rate initially rises with income and then becomes flat | Capped slab structures |
- India's system: Progressive direct taxes + regressive indirect taxes
- Historically regressive-leaning (indirect-heavy) → becoming more progressive as direct share rises

### Data — tax-GDP ratio: Centre, States, combined, peers
## 1. Concept
- Tax-to-GDP: Total tax revenue / nominal GDP → yardstick of fiscal capacity
- Low ratio = large informal sector or high evasion
- World Bank benchmark: 15% = tipping point for sustainable growth
- Falling ratio: Revenue growing slower than GDP (buoyancy < 1) → weaker fiscal space, more borrowing

### Terms and persons → tax buoyancy, elasticity, Laffer curve, base widening
| Term | Meaning |
| Buoyancy | % change in tax revenue / % change in GDP, including discretionary changes (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP |
| Elasticity | Same response excluding discretionary changes |
| Laffer curve | Inverted-U; revenue zero at 0% and 100% rates |
| Base widening | More taxpayers, fewer exemptions preferred to rate hikes → Chelliah approach (lower rates, broader base) |
| Deadweight costs | Removed by a simpler tax system → productivity gains + fewer distortions in resource allocation (APPSC-G1 2024 A-R) |
- Laffer curve: Popularised by Arthur Laffer (1974); term coined by Jude Wanniski
- ⚠ Buoyancy includes discretionary changes; elasticity excludes them
- Recent buoyancy (2026-27 BE):
- Centre's gross tax buoyancy 0.8 in 2026-27 (BE)
- Direct taxes 1.1 vs indirect 0.3 (GST not keeping pace with GDP)
- Fiscal consolidation: ES 2025-26 credits buoyant revenues → deficit 4.4% of GDP in FY26
- Direct tax cut: Cut in direct taxes raises disposable income (APPSC 2018) → higher consumption, savings, investment
- e.g. income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free under the new regime
- Caution: personal income tax revenue growth in 2025-26 only 0.037% after rate rationalisation

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 3:94
### Tax, fee, cess, surcharge
- Fee — paid for a specific service; involves quid pro quo (a tax has none)
- Article 271 — surcharge money kept by Centre, not shared with States; cesses also outside the divisible pool
### Direct vs indirect
- Direct taxes — generally non-inflationary; indirect taxes raise prices (burden built into prices)
- GST (indirect) — burden passed through prices; property tax (direct) — cannot be passed
- Customs duty — an indirect tax on imports and exports
### Progressivity
- Degressive tax — rate rises with income at first, then becomes flat
- Proportional tax — one rate for all
- Progressive tax — average tax burden increases with income (higher marginal rates on higher slabs)
### Constitutional basis
| Provision | Content |
| Article 265 | no tax levied or collected except by authority of law |
| Article 276 | profession tax — State levy, ceiling ₹2,500 per year |
| State List Entry 46 | agricultural income tax — States only |
- 80th Amendment (2000) on sharing Union taxes — from 1 April 1996
### Taxable events
- GST — from 1 July 2017; indirect tax on supply of goods and services
| Tax | Taxable event / base |
| Sales tax / VAT | sale of goods (VAT: value added) |
| Central excise | manufacture |
| Customs | import / export |
| Service tax | provision of service |
| Corporation tax | company income / profit |
| Income tax | income |
### Key numbers
## Classification
- ★ Income tax — a direct tax (not indirect); incidence and impact on the same person
- other direct taxes: corporation tax, wealth tax, capital gains tax, dividend tax
- VAT — an indirect tax passed to buyers, like excise, service tax, sales tax
- ★ Income best spread out through progressive taxation plus progressive expenditure
- Direct taxes — usually progressive; indirect may be regressive
## Terms
- Surcharge — extra charge on tax payable (none if no tax due); cess — for a specific purpose
- Corporation tax base — net profit of companies
- Wealth tax — started 1957, abolished 2015
- Tax shifting — moving burden forward to consumers or backward to suppliers
- impact — on whom first put; incidence — on whom it finally falls
- Tax elasticity — response of revenue to GDP excluding discretionary changes; tax buoyancy includes them
## Revenue data
- Early planning decades: indirect taxes (excise, customs, sales) led; now direct taxes lead
- Budget 2026–27: income tax — single largest tax source (21% of the rupee)
- Centre's rupee 2026–27: largest overall source — borrowings and other liabilities (24%)
- AP own-tax revenue 2024–25: SGST 38%, State Excise 22%
## Tax-GDP ratio
- Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / nominal GDP; low ratio may show large informal sector or evasion
- World Bank tipping point: 15%; India's 2001–2022 average 16.36%
- Centre's gross tax revenue ≈ 9.9% of GDP in 2019–20
- Falling ratio → revenue growing slower than GDP; less fiscal space, more borrowing
### Indirect tax reform
- Chelliah approach — lower rates with a broader base (more taxpayers, fewer exemptions)
- L.K. Jha — Indirect Taxation Enquiry Committee (1976); proposed VAT, at manufacturing stage (led to MODVAT) — not Shome
| Event | Date |

| CENVAT | 1 April 2000 |
| First State VAT — Haryana | 1 April 2003 |
| State VAT nationwide | 1 April 2005 |

- ★ Wealth tax — a direct tax (cannot be shifted)
- Laffer curve — inverted-U; revenue zero at 0% and 100% tax rates
- Cut in direct taxes → higher disposable income and consumption demand (may also help savings, investment)
- Simpler tax system raises productivity by removing deadweight costs and resource-allocation distortions (APPSC-G1 2024)
### Terms → meaning: tax, fee, cess, surcharge
- ★ Tax — money burden put by the legislature on individuals/property owners to support government
- ★ Agricultural income tax — not shared by Centre with States; it is a State subject

### Direct vs indirect taxes — classification
