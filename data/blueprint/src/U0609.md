### Climate — elements vs factors, definitions and terms
## 1. Elements vs factors
| Elements of climate | Factors or controls of climate |
| Temperature | Latitude |
| Atmospheric pressure | Altitude |
| Wind | Distance from the sea or continentality |
| Humidity | Pressure and wind systems |
| Precipitation | Ocean currents |
| Sunshine or insolation | Vegetation or land cover |
| Temperature range | Jet streams and atmospheric circulation |
- IMD normal (LPA): SW monsoon (Jun-Sep) rainfall 868.6 mm, from the 1971-2020 base period
## 2. Terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Weather | State of the atmosphere at a place and time |
| Climate | Average weather over a long period (WMO standard 30 years) |
| Climograph | A station's monthly temperature (line) + rainfall (bars) on one chart – used to read the climate type |
| Biome | Large natural community of flora and fauna over a major habitat, shaped by climate; contains many habitats (UPSC) |
| Anthropogenic biomes (anthromes) | Ecological units shaped by sustained human use (croplands, villages, cities) |
| Tree line / timber line | Altitude/latitude beyond which trees cannot grow – upper limit of tree growth on mountains or in high latitudes (APPSC GS 2019) → above it lies alpine tundra |
- ⚠ Weather = one place and time; Climate = 30-year average
### Controls of climate → how each operates, Indian examples

### Classifications → scholars, basis and Koeppen groups
## 1. Scholar → basis
| Scholar | Basis |
| Wladimir Koeppen | Empirical scheme based on mean temperature and precipitation, linked to vegetation; variables used: temperature, precipitation and natural vegetation |
| Thornthwaite (1931, revised 1948) | Based on precipitation effectiveness + thermal efficiency (potential evapotranspiration) |
## 2. Koeppen groups → criterion
| Letter | Group | Criterion |
| A | Tropical humid | Coolest (coldest) month above 18 degC |
| B | Dry | Evaporation exceeds precipitation (BS = steppe, BW = desert) |
| C | Warm temperate | Coldest month between -3 and 18 degC |
| D | Cold snow-forest | Coldest month below -3 degC |
| E | Polar | Warmest month below 10 degC (ET = tundra, EF = ice cap) |
| H | Highland | Cold due to altitude (altitude-controlled) |
- Second letters:
- f = no dry season
- m = monsoon
- w = dry winter
- s = dry summer

### Hot deserts, taiga and Mediterranean climate — features and towns
## 1. Hot deserts
- Location: 15-30 deg N and S on the western margins of continents
- Causes: subtropical high + offshore trade winds + cold currents
- Examples: Sahara, Kalahari, Namib, Atacama, Arabian, Thar, Great Australian
## 2. Taiga
- Taiga is the largest land biome
- Wide (very wide, not narrow) winter-summer temperature range; moderate precipitation
- ⚠ Trap: "Narrow range" was the incorrect statement (APPSC G1 2023, 2024)
## 3. Mediterranean climate — features and causes
- Seasonal pattern: Hot dry summers, mild wet winters – rain comes in winter
- Winter: rain from westerly depressions (cyclonic rain)
- Summer: hot, dry and sunny under the subtropical high (drought)
- Reason: Seasonal shift of pressure belts
- Location: 30-45 deg N/S on the western margins of continents
- Economy: Citrus fruit, grapes and wine ("orchard lands")

### Key world regions
## Climatic regions
- ★ Equatorial — convectional rain with thunder at noon almost all year

- Taiga — largest land biome; wide seasonal temperature range, moderate precipitation, lowest productivity of forests

## Climatic order and vegetation
- Equator to poles: equatorial → tropical monsoon → temperate deciduous → tundra
- Savanna vegetation — elephant grass, guinea grass, baobab, acacia; naturally fire-prone
- Taiga (boreal) — Siberia, Canada, Scandinavia
### Extremes
## Records
- Lowest temperature on Earth — Vostok, Antarctica (about −89.2°C); Oymyakon, Verkhoyansk — cold pole among inhabited places
- Highest air temperature — Death Valley, California, 56.7 °C (1913); WMO rejected Al Aziziya, Libya
- India lowest — Dras, about −45°C (1908); highest — Ganganagar, about 50°C (1935)
- Atacama — driest non-polar desert; McMurdo Dry Valleys among driest overall
- Mawsynram, Cherrapunji — over 1,100 cm; funnelling of moist air and orographic lifting
- 2025 — oceans held more than 90% of excess heat
## Climate concepts
- Climograph — rainfall as bars, temperature as a line

- Thornthwaite — 1931, revised 1948; precipitation effectiveness and thermal efficiency

### World climatic regions → location, climate and vegetation
## Mediterranean
- ★ Mediterranean — warm dry summers, mild wet winters, evergreen oak (also olive, shrubs); heavy rain in winter
- ★ Not Mediterranean: dry winter, no dry season, dry very severe winter
- ★ Mediterranean (30°–45°, western parts of continents) — Northern Australia is not a Mediterranean agriculture area
- ★ Teak — not found in Mediterranean forest; olive most typical; citrus, chestnuts, walnuts
## Other regions
- ★ Equatorial — same daily pattern: clear morning, heat, afternoon thunder rain; mean about 27°C, no winter
- ★ Tropical rain forest — leaf litter decomposes fastest, soil surface often bare; climbers
- ★ Cleared tropical rainforest cannot support intensive farming — soils badly leached; fertility lost after 2–3 crops
- ★ Tropical Savannah (Sudan type) — definite dry and wet season; 500–2000 mm rain; no month below 20°C
- ★ Western Europe — rain in all months; in the westerlies belt (40°–65°)
- ★ China type — rain throughout year, more in summer
- ★ Sahara — biggest (non-polar) desert, about 3.6 million sq miles
- ★ Ivory Coast — dense hot humid forests; Gulf of Guinea coast
- ★ Koppen Cwg (humid sub-tropical, dry winter) — North-East India incl. North Bengal, Ganga Plains
- ★ Tree-line lower in Western Himalayas (about 3300–3600 m) than Eastern (3600–3800 m)
- ★ Taiga — characteristic of temperate region (coniferous / boreal; northernmost temperate biome)
## Controls
- ★ Deciduous trees shed leaves in winter to conserve water (less transpiration)
- ★ India — tropical monsoon climate; lies 8°4′–37°6′ N, not exactly between tropical latitudes
- ★ Latitude — main factor deciding climate
- ★ Deserts most likely near 23° latitude (tropical, 15–30°)
### Hot deserts, taiga and Mediterranean climate — features and towns
- ★ Taiga — largest land biome; very large winter–summer temperature range (not narrow)
- ★ Mangroves — special forests of tropical and subtropical coasts; stabilise shoreline, wall against sea intrusion
- ★ Equatorial regions — no four seasons; Mediterranean — rain mainly in winter, dry summers
- ★ China type — rainfall throughout the year; tropical highlands show vertical variation
- ★ Mediterranean (30°–40°) — winter rain; in summer under dry terrestrial winds; cause — shifting of pressure and wind belts
- ★ Winter solstice — pressure belts shift south
- ★ Zaire to Netherlands: equatorial → hot desert → Mediterranean → West European
- ★ Tropical rainforest soil poor in nutrients; high temperature and moisture make dead matter decompose quickly
