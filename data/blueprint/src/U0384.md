### Dominant caste — M.N. Srinivas, criteria and examples
- M. N. Srinivas: The first sociologist to coin the term Sanskritization and to explain Dominant Caste
- Originator: M.N. Srinivas, from study of Rampura village, Mysore (1955)
- Criteria:
- Sizeable arable land
- Numerical strength
- High ritual status
- Later additions: Western education, administrative jobs, urban income
- Example: Bihar (pre-independence): Bhumihar, Rajput, Kayastha; Kurmi was not (BPSC 2004 PYQ)
### Changes in caste and caste in politics — forces → effects
| Force | Effect |
| Modern education | values of equality weaken caste; the decline of Brahmin supremacy (APP) |
| Urbanisation/industrialisation | food taboos weaken; mixing across castes (CDX, APP) |
| Reservation/Mandal | OBCs computed at 52%+; caste identity reinforced (CDX, UPSC) |
| Land reforms | Gramdan failed in the heartlands because of dominant landowning castes (CDI) |
| Minto-Morley Reforms 1909 (separate electorates) | vote-bank politics built on caste associations (colonial roots) |
- Untouchability persists: Only 24 cases under PCR Act in 2023; mostly under-reported
- Present conflict: For power, not ritual status; casteism in politics increased
- Exam angle: RPSC 2013 A-R. Castes are being politicised, and casteism in politics is increasing. Both are true, but R is not the explanation.
### Dominant castes of Andhra Pradesh — AP angle
- Historical rise: Reddys, Kammas, Velamas, Kapus = martial and agrarian castes (the dominant castes of AP); Kayasthas = administrative elite
- Medieval Andhra: Reddys, Kammas, Velamas, Kapus Kshatriyaised through Nayankara system
- Justice Party: Rested on intermediate castes (Kammas, Reddys, Naidus); non-Brahmin mobilisation → Communal G.O. of 1921
