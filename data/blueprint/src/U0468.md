### NSO — creation, functions and IIP
## 1. NSO
- NSO: National Statistical Office — functions under the Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (MoSPI)
- Created 2019 — formed by merging the Central Statistical Office (CSO) and the National Sample Survey Office (NSSO) (NSO = CSO + NSSO merged, 2019)
- Main role: Collection, compilation and dissemination of official statistics
- Head: Chief Statistician of India, also Secretary, MoSPI
- Some notes: rank of Chief Secretary
## 2. Functions
- Estimates and releases National Income — National Income estimates include GDP, GNI, NNI, Per Capita Income and other National Accounts estimates (MoSPI/NSO prepares the national accounts.)
- Computes and releases the Index of Industrial Production (IIP)
- Compiles Energy Statistics and labour and employment statistics; conducts statistical surveys
- Other outputs: CPI (Rural/Urban/Combined), gender statistics, National Industrial Classification
- NSS (old NSSO): Runs field surveys (PLFS, ASUSE, HCES)
## 3. IIP
- Released monthly - on the 28th of each month
- Covers the mining, manufacturing and electricity sectors
- IIP data: From 14 source agencies
### CSO and divisions
- CSO: Formed 2 May 1951 (as Central Statistical Institute); merged into CSO in 1954
- National income estimates by CSO since 1955 (some notes: 1954)
- Industrial Statistics Wing at Kolkata conducts the ASI
- CSO divisions (5):
- National Accounts Division: GDP, consumption, capital formation; annual National Accounts Statistics
- Economic Statistics Division (ESD): ESD compiles the IIP (APPSC 2019; GS 2019)
- Unemployment data: CSO & NSSO (GS 2019)

### Base year → meaning and sequence of revisions
- Base year: Reference year whose prices value real GDP (for an index, the year set = 100)
- Revised roughly every 5-10 years to capture changes in what the economy produces and relative prices
- The base should ideally be revised every 5 years; in practice it has been every 5-10 years. Not in your sources: the next proposed base year.
- Sequence of NAS base years: 1948-49, 1960-61, 1970-71, 1980-81, 1993-94, 1999-2000, 2004-05, 2011-12 (released in 2015), 2022-23 (current)
- 2011-12 series: 2011-12 replaced 2004-05 (APPSC 2017, 2019, 2023)
- IIP: Also rebased to 2022-23; released monthly with a 28-day lag
| Index | New base |
| GDP / national accounts | 2022-23 (from 2011-12) |
| IIP | 2022-23 |
| CPI | 2024 (from calendar year 2012) |
| Merchandise trade indices | 2022-23 (from 2012-13) |
- Exam angle: CPI's old base is the calendar year 2012, not 2011-12 (APPCA).
### 2011-12 series (2015) — method, criticism and persons
- 2011-12 series (2015):
- Headline measure = GVA at basic prices
- MCA-21 company database (enterprise approach) partly replacing ASI; private corporate GVA from the MCA (corporate filings) database
- MCA listed 1,35,802 manufacturing companies vs 67,649 corporate factories in ASI 2011-12
- Back series: Data before 2011-12 recalculated on the new method
- GSDP: 9 sectors (G2 2017)
- Criticism:
- Unusually high manufacturing growth; contradicted physical indicators
- Overestimating the private corporate sector (overestimated private corporate sector)

### 2022-23 series — release, method changes and revisions — Release and choice of year
## 1. Release and choice of year
- New series, base 2022-23 (2022-23 series): Released by NSO, MoSPI on 27 Feb 2026; annual and quarterly estimates for 2022-23 to 2025-26
- Standard: Follows UN SNA 2025 edition
- Why 2022-23: A "normal" year
| Year rejected | Reason |
| 2017-18 | GST transition |
| 2019-21 | COVID |
| 2021-22 | Base effects |
## 2. Method changes
- 2022-23 series:
- double deflation for manufacturing and agriculture (single deflation dropped)
- PFCE benchmarked on HCES 2022-23
- Four institutional sectors: government, public corporations, private corporations, households
- MCA-21: Still used for organised manufacturing; unincorporated share of manufacturing GVA only 13.9%
- Manufacturing GVA: Rs 38.6 lakh crore (14.7% of GDP, 2023-24) does not match ASI + ASUSE totals

### Estimates → timing (release calendar and quarterly GDP)
## 1. Advance, provisional and revised estimates
- Method: Advance estimates use the benchmark-indicator method
- Sequence: First Advance Estimate → Second Advance Estimate → Provisional Estimate → First/Second/Third Revised Estimates (FRE, SRE, TRE)
| Estimate | Timing |
| First Advance (FAE) | Early January of the same year; FY26 FAE 7.4% (LENS, Jan 2026) |
| Second Advance (SAE) | End-February; FY26 SAE 7.6% in the new series (TH, 28 Feb 2026) |
| Provisional (PE) | Was the last working day of May; moved to 7 June from 2026 after consulting ACNAS (LENSD, 17 May 2026) |
| First / Second / Third Revised (FRE/SRE/TRE) | In the following years [GK] |
| Quarterly GDP | About 2 months after the quarter ends (Q1 2026-27 released end-August); older notes say about 1 month |
- SES stage: All-India figure is at the Provisional Estimate (PE) stage when the SES is written
- Why stage matters — always quote the estimate stage:
- AP 2023-24 livestock growth: +4.79% at AE vs -0.51% at FRE → sign flipped
- 2023-24 GSDP (current): ₹14,39,674 cr (AE) vs ₹14,22,094 cr (FRE)
## 2. Quarterly GDP estimates
- Data sources: High-frequency indicators; GST data now improves them
- Q3 FY26 growth: 7.8%
- IMF-flagged weaknesses:
- Lag in disaggregating capital formation
### Surveys → what they measure; informal sector estimation
## 1. Estimation method
- Household/unincorporated sector: Benchmarked on ASUSE + extrapolated with indicators
- Other sources: PLFS = employment; MCA-21 = companies
- Institutional sectors: General government, public corporations, private corporations, households
## 2. Surveys → measures
| Survey | Measures |
| PLFS | Employment, unemployment, LFPR, WPR (monthly, quarterly, annual) |
| HCES | Household consumption; weights for CPI (HCES 2023-24) and PFCE benchmark (HCES 2022-23) |
| ASI | Registered factory sector (organised manufacturing) |
| ASUSE | Unincorporated non-agricultural enterprises (informal sector) |
| TUS | Time use (paid and unpaid work) [GK] |
- ⚠ ASI = registered factories; ASUSE = unincorporated (informal) enterprises
## 3. Survey reforms
- PLFS 2025: monthly all-India estimates + quarterly rural estimates
- ASUSE 2025: Allows quarterly estimates
- District-level estimates: PLFS and ASUSE by 2026
- Timeliness: Reports within 45-90 days; monthly results within 15 days
- New surveys:
- Household Income Survey
- SAS of rural agricultural households 2026-27
