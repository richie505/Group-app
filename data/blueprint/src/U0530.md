### NPA — definitions, classification and types
## 1. Definition
- NPA: Loan/advance whose interest or principal instalment stays overdue for more than 90 days (APPSC-G1 2022 key)
- UPPSC 2009: a loan on which interest or principal is not recovered
- Farm loans:
- Short-duration crops = overdue for 2 crop seasons
- Long-duration crops = overdue for 1 crop season
## 2. Classification - SMA, standard, sub-standard, doubtful, loss
| Class / category | Criterion / meaning |
| Standard | Regular; no overdue beyond 90 days |
| SMA-0 / SMA-1 / SMA-2 | Special Mention Accounts, overdue 1-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 days: early warning, not yet NPA |
| Sub-standard | NPA for 12 months or less (up to 12 months) |
| Doubtful | NPA for more than 12 months |
- Special Mention Accounts: 1-90 days overdue; stressed but still standard → SMA-0, SMA-1, SMA-2
- Day bands: 0-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 days
- Sequence: Standard → SMA-0/1/2 → NPA (sub-standard → doubtful → loss)
- ⚠ SMA = up to 90 days, still standard; NPA = beyond 90 days; doubtful = NPA more than 12 months
### Terms → meaning: NPA and stress measures
| Term | Meaning |
| Gross NPA | All NPAs |
| Net NPA = Gross NPA - provisions | (2026) |
| Stressed assets | NPAs + restructured loans + written-off assets |
| Interest coverage ratio | Operating earnings (EBIT) / interest due |
| Evergreening | Fresh loans to repay old ones → default stays hidden; the AQR ended it |
| PCR | Provisions held / gross NPAs |
| Income recognition | Interest on an NPA not booked as income until actually received |
| IRACP | Framework = Income Recognition, Asset Classification, Provisioning (May 2026) |
- Interest coverage ratio: Shows a borrower's present and emerging risk when a bank lends (UPSC 2020 key: statements 1 and 2)
- ICR below 1 → earnings do not cover interest
### Twin balance sheet problem — Economic Survey 2016-17
- Meaning: Over-leveraged corporates + capital-starved, NPA-laden banks at the same time
- Firms cannot invest → banks cannot lend → private capex stalls
- Framed in: Economic Survey 2016-17 (CEA Arvind Subramanian), chapter on the "festering" twin balance sheet problem
- Mechanism: Loan losses eat bank capital → banks freeze new loans rather than recall old ones → private investment falls (worked example)
### Causes of NPAs — factor → effect
| Factor | Effect |
| Aggressive lending (2010-15) | To infrastructure, steel, power without strict appraisal; stalled projects |
| Global shocks | 2008 crisis + weak exports hurt borrowers' cash flows |
| Evergreening | Default stays hidden |
- Vicious cycle: Aggressive lending + shocks → NPAs → provisioning → capital erosion → weak credit
### Asset Quality Review (2015) and the 4R response
- Conducted by: RBI in 2015 (10 Feb 2026)
- Outcome:
- Disclosed/reported NPAs jumped sharply, peaking in 2018
- Problem became transparent → exposed the twin balance sheet problem
- Led on to the IBC and recapitalisation
- Government response: 4R strategy (Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise, Reform) with IBC, recapitalisation and NARCL
### Rules → new NPA Master Directions (from 1 April 2027) and ECL
## 1. New NPA Master Directions
- Borrower-level tagging: One loan of a borrower is an NPA → all that borrower's loans at the bank become NPAs
- Overdue test: 90-day test stays
- Upgrade: Only after all arrears on every facility are paid
- Identification: Banks must identify NPAs through automated systems (28 Apr 2026; May 2026)
## 2. Expected Credit Loss
- ECL shift: RBI moving from incurred-loss model to a 3-stage Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model
| ECL stage | Provision |
| Stage 1 (low risk) | 12-month expected loss |
| Stage 2 (significant rise in risk) | Lifetime loss |
| Stage 3 (credit-impaired, over 90 days) | Full lifetime provision |
- Loss allowances use the effective interest rate (EIR) method
### Data — GNPA, NNPA and PCR trend
| Date | SCB GNPA | Note |
| March 2015 | 4.28% | Before the AQR (IYB) |
| March 2018 | ~11.2% peak [GK] | After the AQR |
| March 2025 | 2.2-2.3%; NNPA 0.5% | Lowest since 2011-12 (IYB) |
| Sep 2025 | 2.15% | Historic low, below the 2010-11 level (TH 10 Feb 2026) |
| March 2026 | 1.93%; NNPA 0.39% | Decadal low (CDI) |
- Trend: GNPA fallen for eight straight years
- Agriculture GNPA: Still high at 6.0% (some notes say 5.7%) (ES 2025-26)
- PSBs: GNPA 4.97% → 2.58% (Mar 2015 → Mar 2025)
- Large private banks (June 2026): HDFC 1.17%, ICICI 1.38%, Axis 1.28% (19 Jul 2026)
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: New NPA and ECL rules from 1 April 2027 (28 Apr 2026); GNPA at record low 2.15% in Sep 2025 (10 Feb 2026)
- Significance: SCB credit nearly tripled ₹67 lakh crore (2015) → ₹181 lakh crore (2025) as balance sheets were cleaned up; SCB CRAR 17.3%
- AP angle: Andhra Pradesh Grameena Bank → GNPA 0.91%, NNPA nil in FY26 (3 May 2026)
- Criticism & debates:
- ECL raises capital needs
- Low NPAs partly due to write-offs (UPSC: only ~13% of written-off loans recovered)

### Asset classification

### Definition
- NPA — interest or principal overdue for more than 90 days
- Agricultural loans: NPA after two crop seasons (short-duration crops); one crop season (long-duration crops)
- Income recognition: interest on an NPA counted as income only when actually received (part of IRACP framework)
### Key terms
- Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR) = provisions held ÷ Gross NPAs
- Net NPA = Gross NPA − provisions
- Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR) = EBIT ÷ interest due; shows a borrower's present and coming risk
- Evergreening — new loans given to repay old loans, concealing the default
### Twin balance sheet problem
- Twin balance sheet problem — over-leveraged corporates + capital-starved, NPA-laden banks at the same time
- Over-leveraged firms find it hard to invest → private investment drops
### Causes & response
- Heavy lending 2010-15 went mostly to infrastructure, steel and power, without strict checks → stuck projects
- NPA vicious cycle: heavy lending and shocks → NPAs → provisioning and capital erosion → weak credit growth
- Twin balance sheet problem named in Economic Survey 2016–17 (CEA Arvind Subramanian)
- Asset Quality Review (AQR) by RBI — 2015
- Government's 4R strategy — Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise, Reform
- Response included the IBC, recapitalisation and NARCL
### ECL model (from 1 April 2027)
- After the AQR, disclosed NPAs rose sharply and peaked in 2018
- RBI moving from incurred-loss model to forward-looking 3-stage Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model; the 90-day overdue test stays
- Stage 1 assets — provision for 12-month expected loss
- Stage 2 assets — provision for lifetime expected loss
- Borrower-level NPA tagging: account upgraded only after all arrears on every facility are paid
