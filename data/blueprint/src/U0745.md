### Integrated Energy Policy 2006 and pricing reforms → provisions
## 1. Committee and approval
- Prepared by: Planning Commission's Expert Committee chaired by Kirit Parikh
- Timeline:
- Approved by Union Cabinet: December 2008
- Core aim: Meet energy needs of all at least cost, reliably and sustainably, till 2031-32
## 2. Key recommendations
- Pricing: Market-determined / trade-parity pricing of tradable fuels
- Subsidies: Targeted to the poor, not general price subsidy
- Efficiency: Energy efficiency + demand-side management
- Supply security:
- Diversify supply (coal, nuclear, hydro, renewables, overseas assets)
## 3. Energy pricing reforms
| Reform | Year |
| Petrol deregulation | June 2010 |
| Diesel deregulation | October 2014 |
| LPG subsidy via DBT (PAHAL) | 2015 |
- Excise duty: Govt has cut excise duty on petrol and diesel at times of price spikes
- Integrated Energy Policy logic: Trade-parity pricing + targeted subsidy
### IESS 2047 and Draft National Energy Policy 2017 → scenarios and objectives
## 1. IESS 2047
- IESS 2047: NITI Aayog's open-access energy modelling / scenario tool (source of the projections above)
- Launched: 2014
- Scenarios: Models supply and demand to 2047 under Business-As-Usual (BAU) and Ambitious
| Domestic production | 2012 | 2040 BAU | 2040 Ambitious |
| Coal (Mtce) | 582 | 1190 | 1385 |
- Newer NITI study (Feb 2026): "Scenarios Towards Viksit Bharat and Net Zero" recommends:
- Nuclear ~100 GW by 2047, 200-300 GW by 2070
## 2. Draft National Energy Policy, 2017 (NITI Aayog)
- Four objectives: Access at affordable prices, improved security and independence, greater sustainability, economic growth
- Fossil fuels: Nearly 90% of commercial primary energy supply, increasingly imported → cutting fossil use serves sustainability + security
- Areas of intervention:
- Energy consumption (business, households, transport, agriculture)
- Coal production + distribution
| Projection (NITI, IESS-based) | Value |
| Primary energy import dependence | 31% (2012) to 36-55% (2040) |
| Oil imports (MoPNG target) | cut 10% from 2014-15 level by 2022 |

### Energy transition targets → year and target
## 1. Panchamrit — COP26, Glasgow
- Panchamrit (COP26, Glasgow): announced at COP26, Glasgow (2021) — Five targets for India:
- 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030 — the 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030 = live target (30 Mar 2026)
- 50% of energy requirements from renewable energy by 2030
- Reduce projected cumulative carbon emissions by 1 billion tonnes by 2030
- Reduce carbon intensity by 45% by 2030
- Net Zero by 2070
## 2. NDCs and other targets
- Updated NDC (2022):
- Emission intensity cut 45% by 2030 over 2005 (earlier NDC: 33-35%)
- 50% of installed power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030
- New NDC (2031-35): 60% non-fossil capacity + 47% cut in emissions intensity by 2035
- Energy independence: By 2047 (INSA)
- Long-Term Low Emission Development Strategy: Submitted to UNFCCC at COP27 (2022)
- ⚠ 45% by 2030 (2022 NDC) vs 47% by 2035 (new NDC); 50% vs 60% non-fossil

### Terms → meaning (RPO, DER, green bonds)
| Term | Meaning |
| RPO | DISCOMs + open-access/captive users must buy a set share of power from RE |
| Distributed energy resources | Small generation/storage units near the consumer → users become "prosumers"; eases grid load |
| Green bonds | Raise money for projects with climate/environmental benefits; proceeds ring-fenced |
- Which DERs qualify: All four = battery storage, biomass generators, fuel cells, rooftop solar PV (UPSC 2024 key)
- Sovereign green bonds: First issued by RBI for the Government in Jan 2023
- Growing sovereign + municipal green bonds, but only a modest greenium + limited access to global climate finance (2025-26)
- IREDA: Specialised public-sector FI → promotes, develops, finances RE projects + gives technical help
- Set up 1987 under MNRE
- Listed on stock exchanges Nov 2023; Navratna status Apr 2024
- Net-zero financing need: Over $20 trillion by 2070 (Jul 2026)
