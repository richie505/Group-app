### Ikshvaku rulers → period and contributions
- Duration: c. 75 years (about AD 225-300; some notes say 3rd-4th century CE)
- Number of kings: Puranas list 7 kings; inscriptions confirm 4
| Ruler | Contribution |
| Vasishthiputra (Sri) Chamtamula / Santamula | Founder, first a feudatory of the Satavahanas. Performed Vedic yajnas. Tried to make the intelligentsia partners in agricultural development (G2 2012), with gifts of land, ploughs, bullocks and cows |
| Virapurushadatta | The golden age of Buddhism. Queens were great donors. Issued the Alluru and Nagarjunakonda inscriptions. Title Sri Parvatadhipati |
| Ehuvala Chamtamula | Son of Virapurushadatta and Bhatti Mahadevi; ruled about 24 years. First Sanskrit inscriptions in the Telugu lands. Gummadidurru inscription |
## Vasishthiputra Chamtamula — Vedic sacrifices
- Sacrifices: Ashvamedha, Vajapeya, Agnishtoma, Agnihotra + Rajasuya
- Evidence: Ashvamedha platform found at Nagarjunakonda
## Virapurushadatta — matrimonial alliances
- Virapurushadatta's marriage: Rudrabhattarika, the princess of Ujjain → tie with Sakas / Western Kshatrapas (G2 2025)
- Cross-cousin marriage: Maternal uncle's daughter - practised

- Last: Last ruler = Rudrapurushadatta

### Ikshvaku rulers
## Founder: Vasishthiputra Chamtamula
- Vasishthiputra Sri Chamtamula (also Santamula) — founder of the Ikshvaku kingdom at Vijayapuri
- Performed Ashvamedha, Vajapeya and Rajasuya; proof: Ashvamedha platform at Nagarjunakonda
- Titles of Chamtamula: Gosatasahasra, Halasatasahasra
## Virapurushadatta
- Virapurushadatta — golden age of Buddhism under the Ikshvakus; his queens were important donors
- Title of Virapurushadatta: Sri Parvatadhipati
## Ehuvala Chamtamula
- Ehuvala Chamtamula (son of Virapurushadatta) — first Sanskrit inscriptions in the Telugu lands
- Gummadidurru inscription is also his
- Andhrabhrityas — Puranic name for the Ikshvakus in general, not a title of Ehuvala Chamtamula
## Last ruler
- ★ Rudrapurushadatta — last Ikshvaku ruler; issued the Gurajala inscription
### Dynasty facts
- Lion — Ikshvaku symbol; Prakrit — royal language
- Andhra origin proof: southern names and the custom of marrying the maternal uncle's daughter (cross-cousin marriage)
### Titles & names
- Puranas call the Ikshvakus Sri Parvatiyas (Sri Parvatiya Andhras) and Andhrabhrityas
- Chamtamula — gave land, ploughs, bullocks and cows to make scholars partners in farming growth
- Ikshvakus — a bridge dynasty; brought the Amaravati school to its peak
- built the first temples; moved Andhra towards a farming, Puranic order
- did not replace Buddhism with Jainism
### Ikshvaku firsts
- Ikshvakus — first dated inscriptions naming sculptors
- Early Hindu temples at Nagarjunakonda: Pushpabhadraswami, Ashtabhuja Narayana
### Capital & sites
- ★ Vijayapuri — Ikshvaku capital, in the Nagarjunakonda valley (Nagarjuna Sagar valley); not at Amaravati
- Vijayapuri dug by R. Subrahmanyam
### Exam corner
- ★ Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda) — successors of the Satavahanas in the 3rd century CE
- ★ APPSC Group-2 key: Puranic literature calls them Andhra Bhrityas (the statement "Sri Parvathiyas" was marked incorrect)
- ★ UPPCS 2020 key: Ikshvaku rulers called Shriparvatiya in the Puranas
- were lords under the Satavahanas; became free after Satavahana decline
- founder: Shrisantmula
- Virapurushadatta married Rudrabhattarika, princess of Ujjain — linked the Ikshvakus with the Sakas / Western Kshatrapas
### Ikshvaku rulers → period and contributions
- ★ Veera Purushadatta — title Sri Parvatadhipati; Buddhism thrived in Andhra in his time
- ★ Period of the Ikshvakus — golden age of Buddhism in Andhra; Nagarjunakonda (Vijayapuri) a great Buddhist centre
- ★ Sri Chantamula (Vasishthiputra, founder) — made scholars partners in farming
- did Vedic sacrifices; gave lands, ploughs and cattle to Brahmins
- ★ Mathariputra Veerapurushadatta — married Rudradhara Bhattarika, daughter of the Saka ruler of Ujjain
