## NSG
- NSG formed 1975, known as London Club, in reply to India's 1974 Pokhran-I test
- NSG is voluntary and consensus-based; 48 members
- NSG has no permanent secretariat
### Nuclear treaties & India
## NPT and CTBT
- NPT: signed 1968, in force 1970, extended permanently in 1995
- India rejects the NPT as discriminatory — "nuclear apartheid"; wants a time-bound disarmament plan from nuclear powers before signing
- CTBT (adopted 1996) bans all nuclear explosions
- India has not signed the CTBT: no time-bound disarmament promise, and it allows subcritical tests
## Other treaties
- TPNW (binding ban on nuclear weapons) came into force 2021
- India did not sign the TPNW: says it adds nothing to customary international law
- India supports FMCT talks at the Conference on Disarmament (treaty not capping existing stocks)
- CCW (cruel conventional weapons): India is party to all five protocols
### NPT & arms control
- North Korea left the NPT in 2003
- JCPOA dates from 2015 (not 2010)
- US left JCPOA in 2018; Iran dropped all limits on 5 January 2020
- New START (2010) — USA and Russia (not China); Russia paused it in 2023
- INF Treaty (1987) — USA and USSR; US left in 2019
### Organisations
## IAEA
- IAEA HQ Vienna; established 1957
- India joined the IAEA in 1957 as a founding member
## Chemical and biological weapons
- CWC in force 1997; India finished destroying its declared stockpile in 2009
- OPCW is not an EU body; runs the CWC, watches the chemical industry
- OPCW won the 2013 Nobel Peace Prize
- BWC adopted 1972, in force 1975; India is a party; no verification organisation
## FATF
- FATF secretariat at Paris (OECD)
- Grey list = more monitoring; does not by itself mean a call for action
- Call for action = the black list of high-risk jurisdictions
### India's nuclear deals
- India's Additional Protocol to its IAEA safeguards agreement in force 2014
- 2008 NSG waiver (India-US Civil Nuclear Deal): allows civil nuclear trade, import of uranium and reactors
- The waiver does not make India an NPT member
