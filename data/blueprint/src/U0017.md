### Early Vedic polity — nature, units, Early vs Later Vedic
- Nature: Tribal polity (APPSC 2018; not a republic or autocracy) - kinship-based; no formal bureaucracy; no fixed territory
- Basic unit: Jana (tribe) = largest socio-political unit; mentioned 275 times in Rigveda; term Janapada absent
- Hierarchy: Jana > Vis (under Vispati) > Grama (Gramani) > Kula (Kulapa)
| Feature | Early Vedic | Later Vedic |
| Unit | Jana (tribe) | Janapada / Rashtra (territory) |
| Chief | Elective/limited chief | Hereditary king |
| Assemblies | Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha powerful | Vidatha disappears; Samiti weakens |
### Rajan — titles, selection debate, duties
- Rajan: Tribal chief - protector of tribe and its cattle, not ruler of a fixed territory
| Title | Meaning |
| Gopajanasya | Title of the Rajan |
| ardhadeva | Demi-god - title of Puru king Trasadasyu (APP notes garble the name) |
- Election vs hereditary debate:
- Tending towards hereditary, not strictly so - traces of election by the Samiti; assemblies had a role in selection
- APP notes + older PYQ keys = hereditary monarchy (some notes call kingship hereditary)
- In practice: monarchy usual, often passing in the family
- Duties: Protect tribe and cattle; lead in war; keep order, settle disputes - with advice of assemblies and purohita
- Divinity: Kingship not seen as divine / not linked to divinity; exception = Trasadasyu (ardhadeva)
### Vedic assemblies → composition and function
| Assembly | Composition | Function |
| Sabha | Elders, elites; women could attend (Sabhavati) | Advisory, social and judicial |
| Samiti | General tribal members (folk assembly); broader participation | Tribal business; legislative; election of the king (elective - choosing the chief) |
| Vidatha | Whole tribe; earliest assembly; women attended | Secular, religious, economic and military |
| Gana | Group/troop of tribesmen; tribal body | Military unit (army divided into Sardha, Vrata, Gana); military and tribal functions |
- Vidatha: Assembly most often mentioned in the Rig Veda (122 times; Samiti 9, Sabha 8)
- Atharvaveda:
- Sabha and Samiti = twin daughters of Prajapati
- Mentions women's participation in Samiti
- Women in Early Vedic assemblies: Women attended Sabha and Vidatha; women members = Sabhavati
- Later Vedic change: Vidatha disappeared; Sabha + Samiti survived but under chiefs and nobles (limited to nobles and Brahmanas); women were excluded
- Exam angle: "judicial assembly" = Sabha; "earliest/most-mentioned assembly" = Vidatha; "daughters of Prajapati" and "Veda treating Sabha and Samiti as separate" = Atharvaveda (not Rigveda).
### Officials, army and revenue terms → meaning
## 1. Officials
| Official | Role |
| Purohita | Chief priest and adviser |
| Senani | Army commander (army chief) |
| Gramani | Village head (also leader of fighting groups - military leader of the grama) |
| Kulapa / Kulapati | Head of the family |
| Vrajapati | Officer in charge of pasture land |
| Jivagribha / Ugra | Police officials |
- Bureaucracy: None formal; governance kinship-based
## 2. Army and revenue terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Gavishti | "Search for cows" - war often = cattle raids |
| Sena | Temporary militia of able-bodied tribesmen (No standing army) |
| Sardha, Vrata, Gana | Army units |
| Bali | Voluntary offering, not regular taxation (No regular tax) |
- ⚠ Trap: Vrajapati = pasture land; Gramani = village head; Madhyamasi = mediator; Spasa = spies
### Tribes, wars and weapons
- Panchajana (five tribes): Anu, Druhyu, Yadu, Turvasa, Puru
- Wars: Intra-Aryan (e.g. Battle of Ten Kings, Parushni) + against Dasas/Dasyus
- Bharata chief Divodasa defeated Sambara
- Weapons: Of bronze; coat of mail and helmet used (UPSC 2017 key)
### Rig Vedic rivers and Sapta Sindhu — ancient → modern names
## 1. Vedic or Rigvedic river names
| Modern river | Vedic name |
| Ravi | Parushni |
| Jhelum | Vitasta |
| Kabul | Kubha [GK] |
| Kurram | Krumu [GK] |
| Gomal | Gomati [GK] |
| Swat | Suvastu |
| Gandak | Sadaneera (Sadanira, later Vedic) |
| Sutlej | Shutudri (Sutudri) |
| Ghaggar | Drishdvati |
| Indus | Hiranyayi — a name associated with the importance of the Indus River; Sindhu |
| Ghaggar-Hakra | Sarasvati |
| Chautang (Ghaggar tributary) [GK] | Drishadvati |
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say Ghaggar-Hakra = Sarasvati and Drishadvati = Chautang (a Ghaggar tributary)
- Saraswati: Called Matetama (Best of all mothers), Devitama (Best of all goddesses) and Naditama (Best of all rivers) - the most sacred
- Most mentioned: Sindhu
- Yamuna vs Ganga: Yamuna mentioned thrice; Ganga once
- ⚠ Trap (UPSC 2026): Vitasta = Jhelum (not Chenab); Asikni = Chenab (not Jhelum); Yavyavati is not the Beas; only Parushni-Ravi was correct
## 2. Sapta Sindhu - rivers and extent
- Standard list: Sindhu, Vitasta, Asikni, Parushni, Vipas, Sutudri, Sarasvati
- Other features:
- "Samudra" = collection of water, not necessarily the sea
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Assemblies with elective traces = early "democratic" elements in Indian polity
- Rig Veda = only source for the period; assemblies show a proto-participatory tribal order
- Comparative note:
- Later Vedic = hereditary territorial kingship, ratnins, regular taxes, royal sacrifices (Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha)
- Rig Vedic Aryans knew the horse, spoked-wheel chariot, coat of mail, helmet vs Harappans left little horse evidence (UPSC 2017)
- FIRST/LAST: Vidatha = earliest assembly + first to disappear in Later Vedic period
- AP angle: "Andhra" first appears later, in the Aitareya Brahmana (Sunahshepa story), not in Rig Veda
