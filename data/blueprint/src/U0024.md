## 3. Assaka/Asmaka — the Andhra-Telangana link
- Location: Only Mahajanapada south of the Vindhyas, on the Godavari (Telangana/Maharashtra); on the Dakshinapatha route
- Capital: Potana/Potali = Bodhan (Nizamabad, Telangana)
- Suttanipata (Buddhist): Sage Bavari had his ashram on the Godavari in Assaka
- Earliest Andhra-region link in Buddhist texts
### Monarchies vs gana-sanghas — features and clans
| Aspect | Gana-sangha | Monarchy |
| Rule | Assembly of clans - clan heads meeting in an assembly hall (santhagara); shared power | Single hereditary king |
- Republics (gana-sanghas): Vajji, Malla, Kamboja (later, per Arthashastra); rest = monarchies (most)
- Vajji: Confederacy of 8-9 clans (Lichchhavis, Videhas, Jnatrikas, Vajjis)
- Capital Vaishali; an early republic
- Led by Chetaka (Mahavira's maternal uncle)
- Malla: 9 clans

### Rise of Magadha — causes, rivalries, capitals
## 1. Four powers and rivalries
- Four powers: Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti dominated
- Rivalries:
- Pradyota rivalled Bimbisara and Udayana
- Kosala annexed Kashi → Magadha later took it as dowry and by war
- Shishunaga ended Avanti
- Outcome: Magadha emerged supreme (6th-4th c. BCE)
## 2. Reasons for the rise
| Factor | Effect |
| Rivers (Ganga, Son) | Transport + defence |
- Status: By the 6th-4th centuries BCE = most powerful Mahajanapada (APPSC 2018)
## 3. Capitals
- Rajagriha: Ringed by five hills - "almost impregnable"
- Pataliputra (later): At the Ganga-Son confluence

### Causes for the rise of heterodox sects
| Factor | Effect |
| Ritualism: Costly yajnas + animal sacrifice (jeeva himsa) + Sanskrit-only religion | Out of reach of common people |
| Varna rigidity: Brahmin dominance | Kshatriyas resented it (Mahavira and Buddha were Kshatriyas); Shudras kept in servitude |
| Agrarian economy: Vedic sacrifices consumed cattle needed for iron-plough agriculture | Ahimsa fitted farmers |
| Vaishyas: Newly rich from trade + money | Wanted higher status and a creed not frowning on trade and money-lending |
### Heterodox teachers → doctrine — Ajivikas, Charvaka
| Sect | Founder/leader | Doctrine |
| Ajivikas | Founded by Nanda Vachcha, then Kisa Sankichcha; popularised by Makkhali Gosala | Niyati (strict fatalism); rejected karma and free will |
| Charvaka / Lokayata | Brihaspati[GK] | Materialism; only perception is valid; no soul, rebirth or afterlife [GK] |
- Other teachers: Ajita Kesakambali (materialist), Purana Kassapa, Pakudha Kaccayana
- Barabar caves: Gifted to the Ajivikas by Ashoka and Dasharatha
- ⚠ Ajivika founder = Nanda Vachcha; popularised by Makkhali Gosala
- Exam angle: "philosophy of the Charvakas" = Lokayata (APPSC 2025).
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Rajghat: BHU opened 22 boxes of finds (1957-69 digs) → Varanasi's urban origins c. 800 BCE
- Rajghat = capital of the Kashi janapada under the Brahmadatta dynasty until Kosala annexed it
- Vaishya Tekri near Ujjain (capital of Avanti on the Sipra): MP to re-excavate
- AP angle:
- Assaka (Bodhan) on the Godavari = the Telugu-region Mahajanapada
- Nanda reach into Kalinga/Deccan
- Chandragupta and Bhadrabahu took Jainism south
- Significance: Magadha's rise + Mahajanapadas = second urbanisation + first large Indian state; heterodox sects rose on the same economic change
- Comparative note: Monarchies (quick decisions, succession fights) vs gana-sanghas (collective rule, slow and divided)
