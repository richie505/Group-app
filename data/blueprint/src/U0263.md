### Telangana background — Mahasabha, Hyderabad State and Mulki rules
## 1. Nizam Andhra Mahasabha
- Origin: grew out of the Andhra Jana Sangham (1921)
- First Andhra Mahasabha: Jogipet (1930), presided by Suravaram Pratapa Reddy
- Role: vehicle for Telugu cultural and political awakening in the Nizam's state
- Later: split along moderate and Communist lines
## 2. Hyderabad State — data
- Status: Part B state from 1950 to 31 Oct 1956
## 3. Mulki rules and the 1952 agitation
- Origin: from 1888; tightened by Nizam's farman of 1919 (Nizam's era (1919))
- Rule: only residents of about 15 years could get local jobs
- Mulki agitation (1952): under Burgula ministry
- Police firing near City College, Hyderabad (September 1952) killed students
- Outcome: domicile protection → central Telangana demand → Mulki/domicile clause of Gentlemen's Agreement
- Not in your sources: the inquiry or government decisions that followed the 1952 firing.
- ⚠ Jogipet 1930 = first Andhra Mahasabha; Mulki = 1888, tightened 1919
### Arguments for and against Visalandhra
## 1. Arguments for
| Argument | Content |
| River waters | Integrated planning of the Krishna and Godavari basins, upper Telangana to coastal deltas |
| Economic | Andhra's agriculture and coastline + Telangana's minerals and industry = balanced growth |
| Capital | Hyderabad as capital (APCC 1950, 1953; Mahasabha 1950) |
| Telangana's development | Telangana backward under the Nizam, Telugu undeveloped there; Ramananda Tirtha: Telangana would develop in Visalandhra, and a separate Telangana would be weak |
| Agrarian (Communists) | Unification to push land reform and weaken feudal structures |
## 2. Arguments against
- Nehru: called it "Andhra imperialism" (Oct 1953) → strengthened (boosted) separate-Telangana camp
- Rajagopalachari (1949): preferred keeping Hyderabad a multilingual state
- Argument used for separate Telangana: Telangana had higher per capita income and revenue than Andhra State → viable on its own
- Telangana fears: loss of jobs + seats in education to better-educated Andhras; earlier protected by Mulki (domicile) rules
| Area | Apprehension of Telangana leaders |
| Employment | Mulki (domicile) Rules protecting local jobs would be weakened |
| Education and administration | Coastal Andhra's more advanced educational and administrative position would dominate public services |
| Revenue | Telangana's revenue surplus would not be spent on Telangana |
| Development | Regional economic disparities would widen without institutional safeguards |
- ⚠ "Andhra imperialism" = Nehru (Oct 1953); "multilingual state" = Rajagopalachari (1949)

### Gentlemen's Agreement → safeguards for Telangana
| Subject | Safeguard / clause (as finally agreed, Feb 1956) |
| Finance / revenue | Administrative expenditure (common costs) borne proportionately; Telangana's surplus spent only in Telangana; reviewed after 5 years |
| Education | Existing facilities protected; Telangana students to get technical-institution admission priority or at least 1/3 (one-third of seats), whichever more favourable |
| Services / retrenchment | Recruitment by population ratio; proportionate retrenchment from both regions |
| Domicile (Mulki) | Mulki/domicile rule for recruitment in Telangana - recruitment reserved by residence of 12 years (some notes say 15 years) |
| Land | Sale of agricultural land in Telangana controlled by the Regional Council |
| Urdu | Kept in Telangana administration and courts for 5 years, then reviewed by the Regional Council |
| Prohibition | Left to Telangana legislators |

| CM / Deputy CM | From different regions: if CM from Andhra, Deputy CM from Telangana, and vice versa |
| Portfolios | Key portfolios to Telangana: at least two of Home, Finance, Revenue, Planning & Development, Commerce & Industry |

### Regional Committee, Deputy CM post and violations
## 1. Regional Council/Committee
- Basis: Provided by the Gentlemen's Agreement (APPSC GS 2019)
- Statutory: appointed by the President under Article 371(1) (Art. 371); constituted in 1958
- Composition:
- MLAs/MPs from Telangana elected by Telangana legislators; all Telangana ministers (except CM) ex officio
- Some notes: all elected Telangana MLAs
- Powers: development planning, irrigation, agriculture, industry, education, public health, recruitment, prohibition
- Disputes with state government → Centre for final decision
- Fate:
- 1968: extended for five years
- Wound up after 32nd Amendment (1973) removed AP from Art. 371(1)
## 2. Deputy Chief Minister post
- What happened: Sanjiva Reddy did not appoint a Deputy CM; dismissed the post as a "sixth finger"
## 3. Violations alleged later
| Clause | Alleged violation |
| Domicile / employment | Large-scale recruitment of non-locals into Telangana posts in the late 1950s-60s |
| Revenue surplus | Promise to spend Telangana surplus only on Telangana ignored |
- Root flaw: political assurances, not constitutional mandates

### Universal angles
## 1. Visalandhra Mahasabha and merger
- Significance:
- Mahasabha turned a Communist slogan into an all-party demand that shaped the 1956 merger

- Safeguards (Deputy CM post, surplus funds, Mulki rules) weakly enforced; Telangana was carved out in 2014
- Political assurances without constitutional teeth; diluted in the 1950s-60s → 1969 agitation; seen in Telangana as systematically diluted
- AP angle:
- Hyderabad capital from 1956 until 2014 bifurcation
