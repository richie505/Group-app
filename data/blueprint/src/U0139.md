### Mughal painting — emperor → emphasis
## 1. Humayun and Akbar
- Humayun laid the foundation of Mughal painting
- Brought Persian masters Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad → founded the Mughal school (APPSC-G2 2025, statement a)
- Mir Sayyid Ali: A Persian painter — associated with early Mughal painting
- Khwaja Abdus Samad: A Persian painter — helped establish the Mughal atelier
- Atelier — Tasvir Khana: Associated with Akbar — an imperial painting workshop; also called a Painting Karkhana
- Akbar established Painting Karkhanas at Agra, Fatehpur Sikri and Lahore
- European painting: Introduced at the Mughal court by Akbar through the Portuguese — European influence included portraiture and the depiction of smaller distant objects for spatial depth
- Daswant and Basawan: Major Mughal painters during Akbar's period
- Decline: Mughal painting declined during Aurangzeb's period
## 2. Emperor → emphasis
- Evolution: Persian miniature → realistic, colourful Indian style
| Emperor | Emphasis |
| Humayun | Foundation: the two Persian masters |
| Akbar | Illustrated manuscripts. A Tasvir Khana atelier (karkhana) of 100+ painters (Daswant, Basawan, Mukand) produced the Hamzanama, Tutinama, Akbarnama and Razmnama. Features: 3-D figures and realism |
| Jahangir | Zenith / golden age ("golden period" of Mughal painting): portraits, albums, flora and fauna, peak of naturalism and precision; European influence; introduced the halo (nimbus) behind the emperor's head |
| Shah Jahan | Formal court scenes, albums (muraqqas), floral borders, heavy use of gold |
| Aurangzeb | Withdrew patronage, so painters moved to Rajasthan, the Punjab Hills and Bihar, fuelling Rajput and Pahari schools |
- Exam angle: statement sets. "Jahangir's reign = golden period" is correct; statement pairs on Humayun's Persian masters and Jahangir's golden age (APPSC G2 2025).

### Court painters → reign and title
## 1. Jahangir — the zenith
- Mughal painting reached its zenith — the Golden Age of Mughal painting — during Jahangir's period
- Themes included: Portraits, birds, flowers, animals and nature
- Shift from manuscripts toward albums; a rise in individual portraits
- Jahangir patronised European and Persian arts
- Other major painters: Farrukh Beg, Bishan Das, Aqa Reza, Muhammad Nadir, Muhammad Murad, Manohar, Govardhan and Madhav

### Regional painting schools → region and features
| School | Associated with / region and features |
| Pahari School | The north-western Himalayas — Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Tehri Garhwal, Paudi Garhwal. Sub-Himalayan belt (Himachal, Jammu, Almora, Garhwal), 17th-19th c. |
| Rajput School | Rajasthan — a regional medieval painting style. Rajput kingdoms of Rajasthan; Hindu epics, love legends, daily life; bold colours; flat, 2-D |
| Kangra School | The Himalayan region — a regional medieval painting style |

| Kishangarh School | Rajasthan — famous for Bani Thani and the Radha–Krishna theme |

- ⚠ Kalighata painting: Indigenous and post-Mughal — not affected by Mughal painting

### Languages — Persian, Urdu/Rekhta and Deccani

### Persons → contributions: Dara Shikoh and Hindi poets
## 1. Dara Shikoh — Sufism-Vedanta synthesis
- Identity: Shah Jahan's eldest son; Qadiri Sufi (with Miyan Mir and Muhibullah Shah)
- Majma-ul-Bahrain: "Mingling of the Two Oceans" - common ground of Sufism and Vedanta
- Sirr-i-Akbar: "the Great Secret", 1657 - Persian translation of 52 Upanishads
- Latin version (Anquetil-Duperron) took the Upanishads to Europe
## 2. Hindi poets → works and patrons
- Akbar's tolerance: Led to a surge in Hindi literature

### Causes of Mughal decline — factor → effect
| Factor | Effect |
| Aurangzeb's Deccan wars | Last ~27 years camped in the Deccan vs the Marathas → drained treasury |
| Religious policy | Jizya 1679 alienated Rajputs, Marathas and Sikhs |
| Sayyid brothers (kingmakers) | Abdullah Khan + Husain Ali Khan controlled Jahandar Shah and Farrukhsiyar (deposed 1719), then puppet successors |
- Sayyid brothers: Overthrown with Nizam-ul-Mulk's help
- Other causes: Weak successors, noble factions (Turani, Irani, Hindustani), regional powers, Marathas, foreign invasions (Nadir Shah, Abdali)
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Translation movement + Persian-Indian painting + Hindavi/Urdu = composite Indo-Persian culture
- Mughal dispersal of painters → Rajput and Pahari schools
- AP angle:

- Kuchipudi grew under Siddhendra Yogi; Abul Hasan Tana Shah granted Kuchipudi village to its dancers
- Aurangzeb annexed Golconda on 3 October 1687 as the 21st Suba; Nizam-ul-Mulk took part in the siege
- Qutb Shahi Deccani painting (Mir Hashim) = school independent of Mughal style

### Mughal painting — emperor → emphasis
- ★ Daswant — famous painter of Akbar; son of a potter; Ain-i-Akbari lists Daswant, Basawan, Keshav Lal, Mukund, Miskin and others
- ★ Jahangir — moved focus from illustrated manuscripts to albums and individual portraits; events of his life, birds, flowers, animals
- ★ Court language — Persian; Akbar's translation department
- ★ Dhrupad — origin goes to Vedic chanting; began with Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior (1486-1516) — before the Mughal period
### Mughal illustrated manuscripts → reign and nature
- ★ Persian Mahabharata — Razmnama; by Badauni, Naqib Khan, Faizi
- ★ Badauni — Ramayana; Faizi — Leelavati; Abul Fazl — Kaliyadaman
- ★ Dastan-i-Amir Hamza — illustrated by Abdus Samad; first Mughal illustrated manuscript; started under Humayun; took 15 years
### Court painters → reign and title
- ★ Mughal painting reached its zenith under Jahangir; Jahangir mainly supported painting
- ★ Jahangir freed painting from manuscripts → albums and individual portraits
- ★ Jahangir's painters — Manohar, Bishandas, Mansur (Basawan was Akbar's); also Abul Hasan, Madhu, Anant, Govardhan, Farrukh Beg, Daulat

### Regional painting schools → region and features
- ★ Bani Thani — Kishangarh school; by Nihal Chand; inspired by Sawant Singh's poem *Bihari Jas Chandrika*
- ★ Kishangarh (Rajasthan) — famous for painting; patron Sawant Singh (throne 1748)
- ★ Raja Sawant Singh = Nagri Das; verses praising Krishna; Kishangarh rulers supported the Nimbarka sect
- ★ Kalighat — native; not shaped by Mughal painting
- ★ Pahari, Rajput, Mughal, Kangra schools — styles of painting; Pahari — north-western Himalayan hill states
- ★ 'Zari Qalam' — Muhammad Husain of Kashmir, from Akbar
### Musicians and music forms → court
- ★ Sitar — Persian long-necked lute (setar) + Indian veena
- ★ Non-Islamic vocal style (as per the answer key): Taraka
- ★ Title 'Tansen' — from Raja Vikramjit of Gwalior (not Akbar); 'Mian' — from Akbar; also called Ramtanu
- ★ Before Akbar — patron Raja Ramchandra (Singh) of Bhata / Rewa (Bandhavgarh); Akbar sent a firman for him
- ★ Tappa — grew at Muhammad Shah's court; Sadaranga and Adaranga — Khayal composers there; Tarana, Dadra, Ghazal also
