### Beliefs, aims, practices and flashpoints
## 1. Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha
- Focus: Women's (girls') education + reform of marriage customs; challenged Parsi orthodoxy
## 2. Nirankari
- Core belief: Worship of God as Nirankar (formless)
- Practice:
- Issued *hukamnamas* on correct practice
- Own network of worship places and priests
- Growth: Cooperated with the British → became a separate group within Sikhism
## 3. Akali
- Aim: Free gurdwaras from corrupt Udasi mahants
- Later: Became a political party (Akali Dal)
- Flashpoints:
- Nankana Sahib massacre (1921)
- Guru-ka-Bagh (1922)
- ⚠ 1920 = SGPC formed and Akali movement begins; 1921 = Nankana Sahib; 1922 = Guru-ka-Bagh
## 4. Other bodies
- Radhasoami: Inner realisation through the guru
- Dev Samaj: Stressed eternity of the soul, supremacy of the guru, good deeds, abstaining from alcohol and meat
### Kuka (Namdhari) — practices, boycott methods, suppression
- Practices:
- White clothes, no meat, carried a lathi
- Rejected idol worship, worship of tombs, trees and snakes, Brahmin-led rituals
- Revived Guru Gobind Singh's Khalsa ceremonies
- Methods: boycott of British goods, English education, law courts, postal system
- Suppression: After the 1872 Malerkotla clash, dozens of Kukas blown from cannons; Ram Singh deported to Rangoon (Burma)
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Kuka boycott anticipated the Swadeshi and non-cooperation methods
- FIRST/LAST: Satguru Ram Singh = first to use boycott and non-cooperation against British rule
- Comparative note: Namdhari (white dress, lathi, anti-British) vs Nirankari (formless God, pro-British cooperation)

- Boycotted British goods, English education, law courts and the postal system
- After 1872 Malerkotla — Kukas blown from cannons; Baba Ram Singh deported to Rangoon
### Akali movement
- Akali / Gurdwara Reform Movement (1920–25) — free gurdwaras from corrupt Udasi mahants; Kartar Singh Jhabbar
