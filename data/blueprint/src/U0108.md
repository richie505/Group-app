# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:107 - Alauddin Khalji's market control regulations, revenue and military reforms
### Why market control
- Military need directly shaped economic policy
- 1. Mongol invasions ↓
- 2. Large standing army paid in cash ↓
- 3. Low, fixed salaries ↓
- 4. Fix prices of daily goods ↓
- 5. Soldiers live comfortably on low pay
### The four markets
| Market | Goods |

| Sarai Adl | Cloth, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil |
| Third market | Horses, slaves, cattle |
| Fourth market | Miscellaneous goods |

| Post | Role |
| Shahna-i-Mandi | Superintendent of each market |

| Barids, munhiyan | Intelligence, secret spies; daily reports |

### Military reforms
- First Sultan with a large permanent standing army
- Dagh: branding of horses; Huliya/chehra: descriptive roll
- Mongols beaten: Jalandhar (1298), Kili (1299), Amroha (1305), Ravi (1306)
### Revenue terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Biswa | Unit of land measurement |
| Kharaj | Land tax, 50% of yield |
| Ghari / Charai | House tax / grazing tax |
| Khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis | Privileges abolished |
### Barani: revolts & ordinances
| Cause | Ordinance |
| Weak spy system | Reorganised spies |
| Drinking | Prohibited liquor, gambling |
| Nobles' gatherings | Royal permission for marriages |
### Malik Kafur & Warangal
- "Hazar Dinari"; acquired by Nusrat Khan (1299); rose to Malik Naib
- 1303: Delhi defeated at Upparapalli
- 1309-10: Prataparudra II pays annual tribute
- Khazain-ul-Futuh: double-walled fort; Koh-i-Noor tradition
### Exam corner
- Aim of market control: army on low pay (APPSC G2 2017)
- Prohibition + market control + severe punishment = Alauddin (APPSC G1 2018)
- ⚠ Dagh is not a land-revenue measure; Ariz-i-Mumalik is not law minister
- Kharaj 50% (⚠ other notes: one-fifth); reversed by Mubarak Shah
### Alauddin Khalji → posts, kingship, titles, Mongol victories
## 1. Earlier posts
- Earlier posts: Under Jalaluddin, Alauddin was Amir-i-Tuzuk + Ariz-i-Mamalik
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes call this post "minister of law"; it was head of the army department
## 2. Kingship and religion
- Ulema: Openly overrode the ulema / refused their guidance → declared he would act in the state's interest; policy decided by state needs, not religious advice
- Model: Revived Balban's harsh, centralised rule, without Balban's dependence on religious sanction
- Ambitions: World conquest + new religion → Kotwal Ala-ul-Mulk talked him out of both
## 3. Mongol defence
| Victory over Mongols | Year |
| Jalandhar | 1298 |
| Kili | 1299 |
| Amroha | 1305 |
| Ravi | 1306 |
### Officials and departments → role
| Official / department | Role |
| Ariz-i-Mumalik | Army minister; recruited the standing army |
| Shahna-i-Mandi | Superintendent of each market, helped by an intelligence officer |
| Diwan-i-Riyasat | Department that ran the whole market system, under the Naib-i-Riyasat |
| Barids | Intelligence officers; daily reports |
| Munhiyan / munhiyans | Secret spies; daily reports |
| Diwan-i-Mustakhraj / Diwan-i-Mustakharaj | Recovered arrears from revenue officials |
| Malik Naib | Deputy → rank Malik Kafur rose to |
- ⚠ Diwan-i-Riyasat = markets; Diwan-i-Mustakhraj = revenue arrears
### Military reforms → features and FIRSTs
## 1. Standing army
- Army type: First Sultan to maintain a large permanent standing army
## 2. Dagh and huliya
| Term | Meaning |
| Dagh | Branding of horses |
| Huliya / chehra | Descriptive roll of each soldier |
- Purpose: Both stopped fraud / false musters
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes wrongly call dagh a land-revenue measure and huliya an assessment of nobles' wealth
### Market control → aim, markets, enforcement, supply
## 1. Aim and plan
- Objective: Keep a large standing army on low, fixed salaries by lowering + fixing prices of daily goods
- So soldiers could live comfortably on low pay (APPSC G2 2017 key)
- Military need directly shaped economic policy
- Four-point plan:
- Control distribution + end monopolies
- Enforce through officials / a separate administration
## 2. Markets → goods
| Market | Goods |
| Mandi | Food grains (grain) |
| Sarai Adl | Cloth, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil |
| Third market | Horses, slaves, cattle |
| Fourth market | Miscellaneous goods |
- ⚠ Mandi = grain; Sarai Adl = cloth and other goods
## 3. Enforcement
- Checks:
- Daily reports from barids (intelligence officers) + munhiyan / munhiyans (secret spies)
- Slave boys made test purchases to check prices
- Cheating merchants punished
## 4. Supply
- Exam angle: UPPSC keys call the market control "highly successful"; Barani is the main source.
### Revenue reforms → terms, share, taxes
## 1. Alauddin Khalji - assessment and share
- Based revenue collection on measuring land — the first Sultan associated with systematic land measurement; standard unit = biswa
- Revenue assessed on measured area + yield
- Diwan-i-Mustakharaj: A revenue administration and recovery department
- Abolished the traditional tax-collection privileges of Khutts, Mukaddams and Chaudharys — collected revenue more directly; weakened intermediary tax collectors
- Reclaimed Milq and Waqf lands — expanded Khalisa land
- Taxes: Kharaj — a land tax fixed at 50% of the yield; Ghari — a house tax; Charai — a grazing tax on milch animals
- State share (kharaj): Half (50%) of produce (BPSC 2018)
## 2. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Biswa | Standard unit of land measurement |
| Kharaj | State share of produce |
| Ghari | House tax (new) |
| Charai | Pasture/grazing tax (new) |
| Khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis | Intermediaries; had to pay the same taxes as others |
| Kismat-i-khuti | Headman's cess; abolished → rich peasants could not pass on the burden |
- ⚠ Ghari = house; Charai = grazing
### Barani's causes of revolt → ordinances
| Cause of revolt (Barani) | Ordinance in answer |
| Nobles' wealth | Confiscated religious grants + free lands (inam, waqf) |
| Weak spy system | Reorganised the spy system (barids, munhiyan) |
| Drinking | Prohibited liquor, intoxicants + gambling |
| Intermarriage and social gatherings of nobles (marriages between noble families) | Feasts, gatherings + marriage alliances needed royal permission |
- Punishment: Severe punishment to offenders; rape punished severely
- APPSC G1 2018: prohibition + market control + severe punishment = Alauddin
### Malik Kafur and the Deccan campaigns → year, kingdom, ruler
## 1. Malik Kafur - origin, epithet, rank, aim
- Origin: Eunuch acquired by Nusrat Khan in the Gujarat campaign (1299) (BPSC key)
- Epithet: "Hazar Dinari" = bought for 1,000 dinars
- Rank: Rose to Malik Naib (deputy)
- Aim: Plunder and tribute to pay for the army, not annexation
## 2. Deccan campaigns
- Earlier raid: Alauddin himself raided Devagiri in 1294-96 (some notes 1294, others 1295) while governor of Kara = first Turkish attack in the south
| Year (APP) | Kingdom | Ruler |
| 1307 | Yadavas of Devagiri | Ramachandra (agreed to pay tribute) |
| 1309-10 | Kakatiyas of Warangal | Prataparudra II (paid tribute, treaty) |
| 1310 | Hoysalas of Dwarasamudra | Vira Ballala III |
| 1311 | Pandyas of Madurai | Pandya kingdom sacked |
- Dates: Some notes give 1308, 1310 and 1311
- Kakatiya king: Some notes say "Prataparudradeva I"; others Prataparudra II
## 3. Warangal
- 1st invasion (1303): First Muslim attack on Orugallu was in 1303 (Malik Fakruddin Juna) → defeated at Upparapalli by Pothuganti Maili + Recherla Venna
- 2nd invasion (1309-10): Kafur + Khwaja Haji besieged Warangal → Prataparudra accepted treaty + annual tribute
- Loot: Carried to Delhi on 1,000 camels
- Koh-i-Noor: By tradition part of the Kakatiya treasure surrendered in 1310
- Amir Khusrau: Went with the 1310 campaign
- Khazain-ul-Futuh describes Warangal's double walls/double-walled fort, moat + diamond mines
- ⚠ 1303 = Delhi defeated at Upparapalli; 1309-10 = Prataparudra made tributary
### Effects, reversal and dynasty comparison
## 1. Effects and reversal
- Effects:
- Prices stable during his reign / kept low + army strong enough to beat the Mongols
- Rule by espionage + repression bred discontent
- Reversal: Mubarak Shah (1316-20) abolished price controls, restored confiscated lands, withdrew harsh regulations
## 2. Delhi Sultanate dynasties → key contribution
| Dynasty | Key contribution |
| Tomaras | Delhi became an important commercial centre |
| Khaljis | Market control and taxation reforms |
| Tughlaqs | Token currency, capital transfer, canals |
| Lodis | First Afghan dynasty; Agra founded |
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- First state-run price control + rationing system in India
- First land measurement for revenue
- Reign = "imperial period" of the Sultanate
- Despotism + heavy spying hollowed out the state → dynasty fell soon after his death in 1316
- Comparative note: Bahmani kingdom: Mahmud Gawan later brought in dagh and chehra, land measurement + a larger khalisa
- Criticism & debates:
- Controls may have been confined to Delhi
- Hurt merchants + peasants to keep the army cheap
- AP angle:
- First attack on Warangal (1303) failed at Upparapalli → Kakatiya commanders Potuganti Maili + Recherla Venna beat Malik Fakruddin Juna
- 1309-10 attack made Prataparudra a tributary

### Exam corner
## Alauddin's state policy
- Rejected Ulema's advice — guided by the interests of the state
- Unlike Balban, did not depend on religious sanction; revived Balban's harsh, centralised rule
- Kotwal Ala-ul-Mulk talked him out of world conquest and a new religion
- Ariz-i-Mamalik — head of army department
- Reign called imperial period — wide conquests, central control, imperial ambitions
## Market measures
- Aims — fixed prices, sure supply, controlled distribution, abolition of monopolies
- Main source — Ziauddin Barani
- Ban on liquor and gambling, market control, severe punishment — Alauddin Khalji
- Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah (1316–1320) ended price controls, returned seized lands
- Importance — first state-run price-control and rationing system in India; first land measurement for revenue
### Military reforms → features and FIRSTs
- ★ Alauddin introduced price control in Delhi — not to pay lower wages to palace artisans; Malik Kabool was Shahna (market inspector)
- ★ Deccan expeditions were financial ventures; he did not aim to annex the Deccan states (K.S. Lal: greed of wealth)
### Market control → aim, markets, enforcement, supply
- ★ Alauddin's market price control was highly successful; meant to keep a vast standing army
### Revenue reforms → terms, share, taxes
- ★ Collected revenue by measuring cultivated land
- ★ Measurement limited to Delhi and nearby areas, not the entire empire
- ★ Abolished the powers of provincial governors
