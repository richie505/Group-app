### Acts → separate electorates, 1909 to 1935
| Act/event | Step / communities given separate representation |
| Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto) | Separate electorates for Muslims - only Muslims voted for Muslim candidates, plus weightage (more seats than their population share); legalised communalism; Minto "Father of Communal Electorate" |
| Lucknow Pact 1916 | Congress accepts them, plus weightage and a communal veto (three-fourths rule) |
| GoI Act 1919 | Extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans (UPSC: "extended") |
| Communal Award 1932 | Extended to depressed classes; Poona Pact replaced this with reserved seats in joint electorates |
| GoI Act 1935 | Further extended to depressed classes (SCs), women and labour; Muslims given 33% of seats in the Federal Legislature; continued till the Constituent Assembly |
- Jinnah's Point 5 (1929): separate electorates to continue; any community free to opt for joint electorates
- 1909 impact: "legally institutionalised communalism" → seed of the Two-Nation Theory
- Muslim League (1906): came up in the run-up to 1909
- Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 - "Which Act introduced the communal electorate?" = Morley-Minto Reforms.

### Pakistan name (1933) and Lahore Resolution (1940) → persons and roles
## 1. The name Pakistan
- Coiner: Chaudhry Rahmat Ali coined "PAKISTAN" in 1933 (MPPSC 2023, BPSC 2017)
- Pamphlet "Now or Never"; written as a Cambridge student
- Acronym: Punjab + Afghan (NWFP) + Kashmir + Sindh + Baluchistan
## 2. Lahore Resolution
- Session: Muslim League Lahore session, March 1940; adopted 23 March 1940
- Demand: independent states for Muslims in Muslim-majority areas
- Soon called the "Pakistan Resolution"
