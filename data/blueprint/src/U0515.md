### Terms → meaning: stabilisation, structural adjustment, SAPs, LPG — Stabilisation vs structural adjustment
## 1. Stabilisation vs structural adjustment
| Basis | Stabilisation | Structural Adjustment |
| Term | Short-term — strictly short-term | Long-term (medium/long-term efficiency and growth) |
| Nature | Demand-side (demand management) | Supply-side |
| Addresses | Inflation and macroeconomic imbalances — correct short-term imbalances: fiscal deficit, inflation, BoP | Industrial, trade, financial and investment structures |
| Tools | Fiscal deficit cut: less wasteful spending, subsidy cuts, better tax collection; tight monetary policy by RBI to control inflation; rupee devaluation to correct BoP | Industrial policy: delicensing, MRTP relaxation, FDI opening; trade policy: tariff cuts, QR removal, export promotion; financial and tax sector reform; PSU disinvestment, privatisation |
| Lead institution | IMF[GK] | World Bank (IBRD)[GK] |
- Overlap: Some UPSC notes list trade liberalisation under stabilisation too
## 2. IMF-World Bank SAPs
- SAPs: IMF/WB loan packages to crisis countries that reduce fiscal imbalances and adjust the economy to long-term growth (APPSC 2018), conditional on:
- Austerity, privatisation, deregulation
- Currency devaluation
- Better tax collection
- Eighth Plan (1992-97): Began right after structural adjustment and macro-stabilisation
### Terms → meaning: stabilisation, structural adjustment, SAPs, LPG — LPG → meaning and measures
## 1. Background
- The 1991 New Economic Policy was introduced to address the severe 1991 Balance of Payments crisis
- LPG model: Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation — the New Industrial Policy was associated with LPG; LPG reforms were associated with multinational corporations
## 2. Globalisation
- Involves the reduction of import duties on capital goods
- Facilitates expanded entry and participation of Multinational Corporations
- Occurs through trade, foreign direct investment capital and cross-border technology
- Free trade: The reduction or removal of trade barriers
## 3. LPG → meaning and measures
| Pillar | Meaning - actions it covers | Key measures |
| Liberalisation | Reduction of state control - abolition of industrial licensing, price freedom | Industrial licensing abolished except a few; MRTP asset limits scrapped; interest rates deregulated and private banks (HDFC, ICICI) allowed (Narasimham Committee); tax rates cut, base widened (Chelliah Committee); SEBI statutory in 1992 |
| Privatisation | Bigger private role - disinvestment, sale of PSUs, opening sectors to private firms | Disinvestment of PSU equity (first to bridge fiscal deficit, later efficiency); autonomy via Navratna/Maharatna status |
| Globalisation | Integration with the world economy - MNC entry, import duty cuts, free flow of capital/technology | Import substitution → export promotion; QRs and EXIM scrips abolished; market-determined exchange rate (1993); FERA → FEMA (1999) |
- Strategy vs outcome: Liberalisation + privatisation = strategies; globalisation = outcome of them; outsourcing is its major feature
### Liberalisation and privatisation measures → content
## 1. Liberalisation
- Delicensing: Licensing abolished for all but 5-6 industries (hazardous chemicals, aerospace etc.); no curbs on expansion; freedom in pricing
- MRTP: Industrial Policy 1991 abolished asset limits, pre-entry restrictions and controls on mergers/expansion
- Financial sector: RBI shifted from regulator to facilitator; SEBI statutory (1992)
- Trade: Import licensing almost abolished; EXIM scrips and quotas removed
## 2. Privatisation
- Dereservation: Industries reserved for the public sector cut sharply (1991), opened to private players
- Disinvestment: Sale of PSU equity
- Aims: financial discipline, modernisation, FDI
- Private buyers take >51% → ownership and management pass to them
- Sick units: BIFR set up to revive them
- PSU autonomy: Through MoUs; Navratna/Maharatna autonomy
- Navratnas (1997): 9 PSUs; Miniratnas: 97 profit-making PSUs
- New PSE Policy (2021): For Atmanirbhar Bharat; strategic disinvestment as a major structural reform
- ⚠ 9 Navratnas vs 97 Miniratnas
### Trade and exchange reforms → year and institution
- LERMS (March 1992): Dual rate
- 60% of export earnings at market rate, 40% at official rate
- Unified market-determined rate from March 1993
- Import controls: Import licensing dismantled; remaining QRs removed April 2001
| Measure | Detail |
| Tariff cuts | Average import tariff ~125% (late 1980s) to ~30% (early 2000s); general customs duty later cut to 10% |
| Export-led growth | Strategy introduced with LPG in 1991 (PYQ 2025; APPSC 2025); import substitution to export promotion |
| FDI / FII | Automatic route; NRIs/OCBs up to 100%; FIIs allowed into stock markets [GK] |
| Rupee | Current account convertible 1994[GK]; capital account only partly |
| WTO | Founding member, 1995 (successor to GATT, 1948, 23 countries); signed TRIPS, TRIMS, AoA |
- Institutions: SEBI statutory in 1992; FERA replaced by FEMA (1999)
- ⚠ LERMS 1992 → unified rate 1993 → convertibility 1994 → WTO 1995 → FEMA 1999 → QRs removed 2001
### What was and was not part of the 1991 NEP — fiscal measures
## 1. NEP features
- Opening up: Economy opened to foreign and private investment in 1991 (APPSC 2018)
- NOT an NEP feature: Nationalisation, strengthening the License Raj or MRTP, import substitution, reserving more items for SSI
| Part of 1991 NEP | Not part of 1991 NEP |
| Delicensing (all but a few industries) | Nationalisation (APPSC 2018 - the odd one out) |
| Disinvestment of PSU equity | Full capital account convertibility (still not adopted) [GK] |
| FDI liberalisation - automatic route, up to 100% in high-priority industries | Demonetisation [GK] |
| Devaluation, tariff cuts, removal of import quotas | FDI in retail and defence - opened only much later [GK] |
- Insurance: 100% FDI in insurance now in place (May 2026)
- Not in your sources: current FDI caps for multi-brand retail and defence - add from the latest DPIIT FDI policy.
## 2. 1991 fiscal policy measures
- Included:
- Control of public expenditure
- Better tax collection
- Lower peak income/corporate tax rates (Chelliah)
- Not included: Contraction of the tax net – the tax net was widened (APPSC 2019)
### Globalisation → meaning, trade, capital flows, sectoral effects
## 1. Liberalisation vs globalisation
| Liberalisation | Globalisation |
| Removes or reduces domestic controls — reduces restrictions; focuses on removing state controls | Integrates the domestic economy with the world economy (through trade, capital, technology and labour flows) |
| De-licensing | Associated: international trade |
| Deregulation | Associated: foreign investment |
| Import liberalisation | Associated: multinational corporation participation |
| Promotes greater market orientation | Promotes greater global integration; facilitates technology flows |
- Globalisation excludes: Greater state control or reduced foreign trade
## 2. Impact on trade
- Trade: Rapid growth of both exports and imports, more FDI, access to foreign technology
- ⚠ Trap: Imports grew faster than exports – "exports rose more than imports" is not true (PYQ 2025; APPSC 2025)
- Earlier decline:
- India's share of world trade 1.78% (1950) → 0.6% (1995)
- Export share 2.2% (1948) → 0.5% (1983)
## 3. Capital flows
- FDI: India became a top FDI destination; foreign savings fill the savings-investment gap (domestic savings ~30% of GDP)
- Net FDI: $44.0 bn (2020-21) → under $1 bn (2024-25) → recovered to $7.6 bn in 2025-26; gross inflow $94.6 bn (11 Jun 2026)
## 4. Sectoral effects
- Services: IT/outsourcing boom cushioned the CAD; 1,800+ Global Capability Centres earned $38.25 bn of services exports in July 2026 (3 Sep 2026)
- Indian MNCs: Tata, Reliance, Infosys, Sun Pharma went global
- Agriculture and employment: Agriculture neglected; growth concentrated in capital-intensive sectors created too few jobs (Beyond GDP essay)
### Changes after 1991 → outcomes, growth data, direction, sectors reshaped
## 1. Outcomes after 1991
- Foreign Direct Investment inflows increased; foreign-exchange reserves expanded; India's share in global exports grew
- ⚠ Agriculture's share in GDP did not increase enormously — it declined after the 1991 reforms
## 2. Growth data
- GDP growth:
- <1% (1991-92) → 5% (1992-94) → 6.3% (1994-95)
- Industry: -0.8% (1991-92) → 8.6% (1994-95) → 10.6% (1996-97)
- Sector shares: Primary sector falling (38.1% → 34.0%, 1980-81 to 1990-91), services rising
## 3. Direction of change
- Direction: Import substitution to export promotion; lower tariffs; FDI and FPI invited; IT/services exports became a cushion; economy moved to enhanced global integration (PYQ 2025)
| Variable | Direction |
| Share of agriculture in GDP | Fell (services became largest) [GK for direction] |
| FDI inflows | Rose sharply |
| Forex reserves | Rose - from $975 mn (1991) to $701.4 bn (~11 months of import cover, CDI) |
| Informal to formal jobs | Some formalisation; informal sector still dominant |
## 4. Sectors reshaped by reforms vs largely untouched
| Reshaped | Largely untouched / slow |
| Telecommunications - private and foreign entry, falling prices | Railways - remained a state monopoly [GK]; modernisation still in progress (LENS, May 2026) |
| Banking - private banks (HDFC, ICICI), interest deregulation | Power distribution - DISCOM privatisation still debated (LENS, May 2026) |
| Civil aviation - private airlines [GK] | Agriculture - little direct reform |
| Automobiles, consumer electronics | Ports - corporatisation of major ports only now (LENS, May 2026) |
### Criticism → jobless growth, agrarian neglect, crony capitalism
| Criticism | Effect |
| Jobless growth | Output rose faster than jobs; many graduates end in gig work |
| Agrarian neglect | Public investment in agriculture lagged; SAP-type reforms dismantled food-system supports in the Global South (May 2026) |
| Crony capitalism | Gains concentrated among large, politically connected firms |
- Washington Consensus (10 rules): Fiscal discipline, tax reform, trade and FDI liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, property rights – now seen as obsolete (14 Mar 2026)

## 1. Criticism & debates
- Criticism & debates:
- SAP outcomes: Raised poverty and inequality in the Global South – growth fell from 3.2% to 0.7% a year in the adjustment era (13 May 2026; 14 May 2026)
