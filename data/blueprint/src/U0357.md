### Scheduled Tribes and Scheduled Areas — definitions, criteria and data

- Declared by: President, in consultation with the Governor

- preponderance of tribal population
- Compactness and reasonable size
- Viable administrative unit (district/block/taluk)
- Economic backwardness
- Purpose of both Schedules: Area-based protection of tribal land and culture, since STs live in contiguous areas

### Fifth Schedule — Scheduled Areas and the Governor's powers
## 1. Fifth Schedule — Scheduled Areas
- Part X, Article 244(1) — administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes
- Declared / notified by: The President (the authority for area notification)
- Does not make the area a Union Territory or a separate state; does not end the State Government's executive power — it continues, subject to the Fifth Schedule; the Union can give directions on their administration
- Governor:
- Submits a report on the administration of Scheduled Areas directly to the President — annually and whenever the President requires (⚠ not the Chief Minister to the Union Home Ministry)
- May make regulations prohibiting or restricting transfer of tribal land to non-tribals, and regulating money-lending to Scheduled Tribes
- Oversees land transfers and the applicability of central and state laws
## 2. Governor's role — Prep additions
- Application of laws (Para 5): Governor may direct that any Act of Parliament or the State legislature does not apply, or applies with modifications, to a Scheduled Area
- Regulations (Para 5): regulations for peace and good government (G2 2017 key)
- May prohibit/restrict land transfer by or among STs
- Regulate allotment of land and money-lending
- Made after consulting the TAC; may repeal Acts; need the President's assent
- Exam angle: "Governor is NOT authorised to direct that an Act does not apply" is the false statement (APPSC-G2 2017). Regulations are issued by the Governor. TAC size is up to 20.
### Fifth Schedule — Tribes Advisory Council (TAC)
## 1. Tribes Advisory Council (TAC)
- Tribal Advisory Council: Mandatory in each State having Scheduled Areas — Paragraph 4 of the Fifth Schedule; advises on the welfare and advancement of Scheduled Tribes (the key governance body)
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say Tribes Advisory Council (the Constitution's name)
- If the President directs: Also in a State with STs but no Scheduled Areas
- Size: not more than 20 members (up to 20), about three-fourths ST MLAs
- Role: Advises only on ST welfare matters referred by the Governor
- Exam angle: TAC size "up to 20" (G2 2017 - answer key was left blank; the Constitution says not more than 20).

### PESA Act 1996 — year, committee and objectives
## 1. PESA, 1996
- The Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act — also known as PESA; enacted (passed) in 1996; came into force on December 24, 1996
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes give 24 Dec 1996 as the enactment date
- Provides tribal self-governance — extends Part IX of the Constitution to Schedule V areas; empowers traditional Gram Sabhas; recognises governance through customary law
- Gram Sabhas get authority over natural resources, minor forest produce, dispute resolution and project approvals in Schedule V areas
## 2. Act → year → committee
| Item | Fact |
| Full name | Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act |
| Committee | Bhuria Committee (Dilip Singh Bhuria Committee), report 1995 (some notes say 1991) |
| Need | Art. 243M(1) bars Part IX in Scheduled and Tribal Areas |
| Enabling clause | Art. 243M(4)(b) |
- Applies to: Fifth Schedule (Scheduled) Areas only; Part IX extended with exceptions
## 3. Objectives
- Extend Part IX with modifications
- self-rule for the tribal population
- Gram Sabha as the nucleus of village governance
- Administrative framework consistent with traditional practice
- Stop higher-tier panchayats from taking over lower-tier powers
- Not an objective: Creating autonomous regions (that is the Sixth Schedule) (UPSC 2013)
### PESA provisions — Gram Sabha powers, reservation and higher tiers — Gram Sabha
## 1. Gram Sabha
- Level: Habitation level - para, majra, tola
- Functions: Protects traditions and community resources; manages common property; settles local disputes
- Presiding officer: An ST member elected by the Gram Sabha, not the Sarpanch (CGPSC 2015)
- Fixed by each state's PESA rules; check AP PESA Rules 2011 for the exact provision
## 2. Powers of Gram Sabha and Panchayats
| Subject | Gram Sabha / panchayat power |
| Minor forest produce | Ownership of minor forest produce |
| Land acquisition and resettlement | Must be consulted before acquisition for projects (consultation, not consent) |
| Minor minerals | Recommendation mandatory before prospecting licences or mining leases for minor minerals [GK] |
| Intoxicants / liquor | Enforce prohibition or regulate sale and consumption |
| Money lending | Control money-lending to STs |
| Land alienation | Prevent alienation; restore unlawfully alienated land [GK] |
| Village markets, social-sector institutions, local plans incl. Tribal Sub-Plan | Management and control |
### PESA provisions — Gram Sabha powers, reservation and higher tiers — Reservation and higher tiers
## 1. Reservation and higher tiers
- Seats: Reserved in proportion to population; STs get at least 50%
- Chairpersons: All Chairperson posts at every level go to STs.
- Nomination: State may nominate unrepresented tribes to block/district bodies
- Higher-level panchayats: Must not usurp Gram Sabha powers
- District level: States should follow the Sixth Schedule pattern for Zila Panchayat arrangements
- Zila Panchayat in Scheduled Areas: Plans minor water bodies; controls social-sector institutions and Tribal Sub-Plans; other functions conferred by the State (CGPSC 2016 - "all of the above")
- Inconsistent state laws: Lapse one year after assent

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:83
### Fifth vs Sixth Schedule
## Scheduled Tribes
- Article 342 — ST list issued by the President after asking the Governor (Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950)
- Later adding/removing only by Parliament by law
- No STs notified in Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, Puducherry, Chandigarh
- STs ≈ 8.6% of India's population (Census 2011)
- NCST (Art. 338A) — added by the 89th Amendment Act, 2003; NCST set up 2004
## Fifth Schedule
- Governs Scheduled Areas and STs: Part X, Article 244(1) + Fifth Schedule (NE tribal areas: Art. 244(2) + Sixth)
- Scheduled Area tests: mostly tribal, compact and fair size, workable administrative unit, economic backwardness (not industrial development)
- President declares Scheduled Areas; Governor reports to the President annually or whenever required (not the Chief Minister); Union may direct the State
- Para 5 regulations for peace and good government — Governor, after asking the TAC, with President's assent
- Tribes Advisory Council: up to 20 members, about three-fourths ST MLAs; must exist in every State with Scheduled Areas (para 4); advises on ST welfare
- TAC is a Fifth Schedule advisory body, not a Sixth Schedule body with legislative/judicial powers
- President may direct a TAC in a State with STs but no Scheduled Areas
- Parliament amends the Fifth and Sixth Schedules by ordinary law (not Art. 368)
## Sixth Schedule
- Basis: Article 244(2); Article 275(1) — grants-in-aid for tribal welfare / administration of Scheduled and Tribal Areas
- States: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram (autonomous district councils); Nagaland has Art. 371A, not Sixth Schedule
- Areas in Parts I, II, IIA, III of the table to para 20 — "Tribal Areas"
- Autonomous region within a district — Regional Council
- Governor sets how far High Court jurisdiction covers tribal areas' courts
- Application of laws: direction by the Governor in Assam; by the President in Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram
## Part IX and PESA
- Article 243M — excludes Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas from Part IX
- 243M(4)(b) — Parliament may extend Part IX with modifications by ordinary law (not an Art. 368 amendment); PESA, 1996 did this
- PESA applies to Fifth Schedule areas, not Sixth Schedule areas
- Ten PESA States: AP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan (not Kerala)
- AP — Fifth Schedule State; Assam — Sixth Schedule
## AP angle
- Alluri Sitharama Raju district — cut from Visakhapatnam; tribal-majority, has PVTGs
### Fifth Schedule: Governor & TAC
- ★ Putting an area under the Fifth Schedule — stops transfer of tribal land; President, asking the Governor, can change Scheduled Area boundaries; 10 States have Scheduled Areas
- Governor may direct that an Act of Parliament/State Legislature not apply or apply with modifications in a Scheduled Area
- Para 5 regulations: ban/limit tribal land transfer to non-tribals; control money-lending to STs
- PESA Gram Sabha may be at habitation level (para, majra, tola)
- Conflicting State law stops working one year after PESA's assent (24 December 1996)
### Sixth Schedule councils
- Autonomous District Council: 30 members — 26 elected (adult franchise), 4 nominated by the Governor
- Council legislates on inheritance, village administration, social customs, land, forests (not citizenship)
- Judicial power: village courts; executive: revenue and taxes
- Financial assistance: Article 275 grants-in-aid from the Consolidated Fund of India
- Union directions to a State on tribal administration — Fifth Schedule (Sixth: stays with the State executive)
- Sixth Schedule does not cover Manipur (hill-area committees, Art. 371-C) or Nagaland
- FRA 2006 — Other Traditional Forest Dwellers: lived there three generations (75 years) before 2005
