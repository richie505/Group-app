### Muslim reformers → contributions — Sayyid Ahmad Khan - life and writings
## 1. Sayyid Ahmad Khan - life and writings
- Life: 1817-1898
- Aim: Modernisation of Muslims via English education + rational reading of Islam after 1857
- Writings: Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind ("The Causes of the Indian Revolt") on 1857 (1859) (APPSC-GS 2019)
- Journal: Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq
- Language: Promoted Urdu as a unifying language of Muslims
## 2. Sayyid Ahmad Khan - political stand
- Early view: India a bride; Hindus and Muslims "her two eyes" - spoke for Hindu-Muslim unity
- Turn: After the Hindi-Urdu controversy (1867)
- Congress: Asked Muslims to stay away from it; formed the United Indian Patriotic Association (1888) to counter Congress
- Held that Hindus and Muslims needed separate political paths
- MAO Defence Association: 1893[GK], with Theodore Beck; loyalist, anti-Congress

### Aims, beliefs and nature of the movements
## 1. Wahabi
- Aim: Restore Muslim power; fought the Sikhs in Punjab, then the British
- Chief centre: Patna
- British trials: Ambala (1864) + Patna (1865) → suppressed by 1870
- Exam angle: character of the Wahabi movement → anti-British (APPSC G2 2011).
## 2. Faraizi
- Aim: Purge un-Islamic practices; enforce religious duties (faraiz)
## 3. Aligarh
- MAO College: Founded by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan in 1875 at Aligarh
- Original name: Madarsatul Uloom Musalmanan-i-Hind — changed to Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College in 1877
- Promoted modern Western and scientific education among Indian Muslims
- 1875 (school); college classes 1877; modelled on Oxford and Cambridge
- Aligarh Muslim University: Located in Aligarh — established in 1920; MAO College became Aligarh Muslim University (AMU)
- Background: After 1857, Muslims lagged behind because they rejected Western education
- Aim: Social and educational uplift through modern education + rational reading of Islam + cooperation with the British
- Scientific Society: translated Western science, history and philosophy into Urdu
## 4. Deoband
- Spread pure teachings of the Koran and Hadis (Quran and Hadith)
- Keep alive jihad against foreign rulers
- Train religious leaders
## 5. Ahmadiya
- Promoted Western liberal education among Muslims
- ⚠ Deoband kept jihad alive; Ahmadiya opposed jihad
### Aligarh vs Deoband — stand on Congress
| Aligarh | Deoband |
| Western education, loyalty to British | Traditional Islamic learning, anti-British |
| Opposed Congress | Welcomed Congress |
- Deoband stance: Welcomed the Congress (welcomed the Indian National Congress)
- fatwa (1888) against Sir Syed's (Sayyid Ahmad's) United Patriotic Association and MAO Association
- ⚠ 1888 twice: Sayyid Ahmad's United Indian Patriotic Association and Deoband's fatwa against it
### Muslim reform in Hyderabad and Andhra
- Hyderabad/Andhra Wahabi leader: Mubariz-ud-Daula:
- Brother of the Nizam, influenced by Barelvi
- Tried to organise resistance to British influence in the Deccan
- Arrested under British pressure (1839); died in prison
- Nasaraiah: Sayyid Muslim (d. 1825), popular with Madigas
- Preached one God, against idol worship
- Osmania University (1918): Hyderabad; taught in Urdu

## 1. Kuka and Akali
- Ram Singh Kuka: First to use non-cooperation and boycott of British goods (APPSC-GS 2025)
- Exiled to Rangoon after the 1872 Malerkotla clash
- Sikh Gurdwaras Act, 1925: Put gurdwaras under the SGPC = Akali movement's success
## 2. Singh Sabha - places founded, Khalsa College
- Aims: Reform Sikh society; remove un-Sikh practices; promote Gurmukhi/Punjabi literature and education
- Legacy: The Akali movement grew out of it
### Universal angles
- Significance:
- Aligarh built the modern educated Muslim middle class; Deoband kept the orthodox + anti-colonial strand alive
- Namdharis among the earliest movements that resisted British rule in Punjab (2026)
- Criticism & debates:
- Sayyid Ahmad's later political conservatism sowed seeds of communal division
- Critics say Deoband backed the Congress more to oppose Sir Syed than from a positive political creed
- AP angle: Wahabi activity in Hyderabad State under Mubariz-ud-Daula (APPSC G2 2002)
- Comparative note:
- Aligarh (Western education, pro-British, anti-Congress) vs Deoband (orthodox, anti-British, pro-Congress)
