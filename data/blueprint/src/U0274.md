# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:0 - Constitutional evolution - Regulating Act 1773 to the Government of India Act 1935 (act-by-act provisions)
### Company era Acts
- Control shifted from Company to London step by step; the 1919 and 1935 Acts redefined legislative power until 1947
- 1773 — Regulating Act: first parliamentary control of EIC; Warren Hastings GG of Bengal; 4-member council; Supreme Court Calcutta (1774, CJ Elijah Impey)
- 1784 — Pitt's India Act: dual control - Board of Control (political) + Court of Directors (commercial); council cut to 3
- 1786 — GG can override council + act as C-in-C: Cornwallis
- 1793 — Charter: monopoly extended 20 years; GG's grip on Bombay, Madras tightened
- 1813 — Monopoly ended except tea + China trade; missionaries allowed; ₹1 lakh for education
- 1833 — GG of India: Bentinck; Macaulay Law Member; trade ended fully; Section 87: no discrimination
- 1853 — Legislative + executive functions separated; open competition (Macaulay Committee 1854); 6 legislative members; last Charter Act
### Crown rule: 1858 and 1861
- 1858: power to Crown; Canning first Viceroy
- Board of Control + Court of Directors abolished; Secretary of State + 15-member Council of India
- 1861: Indians nominated (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Dinkar Rao)
- Portfolio system (⚠ other notes: Canning 1859, statutory in 1861)
- Viceroy's ordinance power (6 months); Bombay, Madras powers restored
- High Courts Calcutta, Bombay, Madras 1862
### 1892 vs 1909
| Feature | 1892 | 1909 (Morley-Minto) |
| Election | Indirect begins | Formal; separate electorate for Muslims |
| Budget | Discussion, no vote | Discuss + resolutions |
| Questions | 6 days' notice, no supplementary | Supplementary allowed |
| Other | - | S.P. Sinha: first Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council (Law Member); centre 60 additional, unicameral |
### GoI Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford)
- Montagu declaration 20 August 1917: gradual responsible government
- Dyarchy in provinces: reserved (Governor + Executive Council) vs transferred (Governor + Ministers)
- Bicameral Centre: Council of State + Legislative Assembly; direct elections
- "Responsible government" term first; budgets separated
- Chamber of Princes (120; Ganga Singh of Bikaner); High Commissioner in London
- PAC 1921; PSC provided, set up 1 October 1926
### GoI Act 1935
- Federation
- All-India Federation never formed
- Joining optional for states
- Central dyarchy: never worked
- Provinces
- Full provincial autonomy (from April 1937)
- Bicameral in 6: Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP
- Institutions
- Federal Court (1 October 1937, Maurice Gwyer)
- RBI, PSCs; Council of India abolished
- Other
- 3 lists; residuary with GG
- Burma separated; Sindh, Orissa new
- Franchise 3% → 14%
- 321 sections, 10 schedules
### Dyarchy
| Level | Introduced | Ended |
| Provinces | 1919 | 1935 (provincial autonomy) |
| Centre | 1935 | Never took effect |

### Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784 and the Act of 1786
## 1. Regulating Act, 1773
- Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal: Warren Hastings became the Governor-General of Bengal
- Supreme Court at Calcutta (Fort William): Provided for by the Act; established in 1774 — the first Supreme Court
- First Chief Justice: Sir Elijah Impey
- Banned: Private trade and bribes
- Also (Prep): First step by Parliament to control the EIC (first parliamentary control); based on the report of a committee under Lord North; a 4-member Executive Council (a 4-member council); Bombay and Madras made subordinate; Court of Directors to report on revenue, civil and military affairs
- Trigger: Famine + fall in revenue + Company's financial crisis → British Parliament stepped in (1772), took control of Bengal away from the Company
- Company sought a loan of about £1 million (1772)
## 2. Pitt's India Act, 1784 and the Act of 1786
- Pitt's India Act (1784): Established Dual Control (dual control / double government)
- Board of Control → political affairs (politics)
- Court of Directors → commercial affairs (commerce)
- Status: Company territories = "British possessions in India"; Company became a subordinate department of the State
- GG's council: Reduced to 3
- Act of 1786: Governor-General could override his Council and assume the powers of Commander-in-Chief
- First to exercise these powers: Lord Cornwallis
- Override power extended to all future GGs + presidency Governors by Charter Act 1793
## 3. Earlier Acts (Prep)
| Act | Key provisions |
| Act of 1767 | Company to pay the British Government £400,000 a year (APPSC-G2 2017 key) |
| Amending Act 1781 (Act of Settlement) | Separated judicial and executive functions |
### Charter Acts 1793, 1813, 1833 and 1853
## 1. Charter Act, 1813
- Trade monopoly in India: Ended (partially abolished) — except the tea trade and trade with China (both monopolies retained) (except tea and trade with China; except tea and the China trade)
- Christian missionaries: Permitted to operate in India (Missionaries allowed in)
- Education: Annual grant of at least ₹1 lakh for the education of Indians — first such provision (Rs 1 lakh a year for literature, science and education)
- Also (Prep): asserted Crown sovereignty over Company territories (UPSC 2019 key); civil-servant training compulsory
## 2. Charter Act, 1833
- Governor-General of Bengal → Governor-General of India (office created): Lord William Bentinck was the first Governor-General of India
- Law Member: A fourth member added to the Governor-General's Council — Macaulay, the first Law Member
- Commercial status: The Company's commercial role ended entirely — trade monopoly completely abolished in 1833 (Ended completely - all commercial functions ended; Company made purely administrative)
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes say 1833 kept "tea and China" trade - that was the 1813 position
- Section 87: Opened Company service to Indians for the first time — no discrimination based on religion, colour or other such distinctions
- No Indian debarred from office by religion, birth, descent or colour; attempted open competition (not implemented)
- Also (Prep): (Saint Helena Act) legislative power centralised in the GG-in-Council - laws for all British India; legislative powers of Madras and Bombay reduced; Law Commission under Macaulay (origin of the Law Ministry); "final step towards centralisation"; permanent presence of missionaries, Anglican hierarchy expanded
## 3. Charter Act, 1853
- Legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council clearly separated for the first time
- Civil Services: Open competition introduced, Indians included
- Set up by Macaulay Committee (1854)
- Also (Prep): added 6 legislative members; Company's rule extended with no fixed period; territories held "in trust for Her Majesty... until Parliament should otherwise provide" (APPSC-GS 2018)
## 4. Charter Act 1793 and renewals
- Charter Act 1793: GG's control over Bombay and Madras (presidencies) increased / tightened; Board of Control salaries charged to (paid from) Indian revenue; C-in-C not ordinarily a council member; monopoly extended 20 years
- Renewal: Every 20 years (1793, 1813, 1833); the 1853 Act fixed no term
### GoI Act 1858 and Indian Councils Act 1861
## 1. Government of India Act, 1858
- Transfer of power: From the Company to the British Crown (Company rule ended; Crown took over)
- Viceroy: Governor-General designated as Viceroy — Lord Canning was the first Viceroy of India
- Abolished: Board of Control and Court of Directors
- Secretary of State for India: Created, assisted by a 15-member Council (Council of India)
## 2. Indian Councils Act, 1861
- Decentralisation: Restored the legislative powers of Bombay and Madras
- Indian participation in law-making began → beginning of representative institutions; Viceroy nominated Indians (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao) as non-officials
- Portfolio System introduced - a council member given final say over his department(s)
- ⚠ Sources differ: other notes say the portfolio system was introduced by Canning in 1859 and only given statutory recognition in 1861
- Viceroy's Ordinance Power: Introduced (origin of the ordinance power) — an ordinance could last at most six months (6 months)
- High Courts Act, 1861: High Courts of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras established in 1862
- Passed: 1 August 1861
- New provincial councils: Bengal (1862), Punjab (1897); Prep table also lists NWFP (1866)
- ⚠ Trap: Some notes say "NWFP 1866"; actually North-Western Provinces' council, 1886
- Reception: Congress called it inadequate (grievance began with the "sham" Councils Act of 1861)
### Indian Councils Act 1892 and 1861 vs 1892
## 1. Indian Councils Act, 1892
- Budget: Discussion permitted — but no voting on the Budget (budget discussion and questions, but no vote [GK])
- Questions to the executive: Permitted, with six days' prior notice — no supplementary questions
- Principle of (limited) indirect election introduced (nomination on the recommendation of universities, municipalities, chambers)
## 2. 1861 vs 1892
| Feature | 1861 | 1892 |
| Indians | Nominated as non-officials - Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao | More non-officials in central and provincial councils [GK] |
| Election | None | Indirect election begins (principle not named "election") [GK] |
| Budget | No discussion | Budget discussion allowed, no vote [GK] |
| Executive | Portfolio system - a council member given final say over his department(s) | - |
| Other | Viceroy's ordinance power (6 months) | Right to ask questions [GK] |
- 1892 indirect election: Practice began in 1892 (APPSC GS 2019 key)
- 1909 = first formal election; 1892 = indirect start (APP notes say 1909 "first introduced elected members")
- Exam angle: "Indirect election launched in?" The answer is 1892 (APPSC GS 2019).
### Indian Councils Act 1909 - Morley-Minto Reforms
## 1. Indian Councils Act, 1909 — Morley-Minto Reforms
- Separate electorates for Muslims introduced — Lord Minto: "Father of Communal Electorate" (Separate electorate for Muslims)
- Indians in Executive Councils: First associated with the Executive Councils of the Viceroy and Governors
- Satyendra Prasad (S.P.) Sinha: First Indian in the Viceroy's Executive Council, as Law Member (Satyendra Sinha)
- Supplementary questions allowed; members could also discuss the budget and move resolutions
- Central Legislature: Enlarged but still unicameral
- ⚠ Trap: The 1909 Act did not introduce dyarchy and gave no Provincial Autonomy; existing High Courts continued
## 2. Councils (Prep)
- Central Council: Additional members to 60 (central council to 60); total 69 - 37 officials, 32 non-officials
- Official majority at Centre; non-official majority in provinces, blunted by the veto
- Councils enlarged; formal elected representation
- Exam angle: "Which Act..." one-liners; the 2018 key: the Act that allowed some elected representation = 1909.
- ⚠ Indirect election = 1892; separate electorate + formal election = 1909
### GoI Act 1919 - Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
## 1. Government of India Act, 1919 — Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
- Dyarchy in the provinces: Introduced for the first time (Provincial Dyarchy, 1919)
- Bicameral Central Legislature: First time (bicameralism at the Centre) - Council of State (upper) + Legislative Assembly (lower)
- Direct elections: First time (at the Centre) - mostly directly elected; limited franchise (property, tax, education)
- "Responsible government": Term used for the first time
- Budgets: Central and Provincial budgets separated; initial separation of subjects
- Communal representation: Extended
- Chamber of Princes: Established — 120 members; first Chancellor: Maharaja Ganga Singh of Bikaner
- Public Accounts Committee: Set up under this Act in 1921
- Public Service Commission: Provided by this Act; established on 1 October 1926
- Also (Prep): 3 of 6 Executive Council members Indian; High Commissioner for India in London; statutory commission after 10 years
## 2. Montagu's declaration → 1919 Act
- Date: 20 August 1917
- Aim: Gradual introduction of responsible government → basis of the 1919 Act
### Dyarchy and the 1909 / 1919 / 1935 comparison
## 1. Comparison of the Acts
| Feature | Indian Councils Act 1909 | GoI Act 1919 | GoI Act 1935 |
| Dyarchy | Not introduced | Introduced in the provinces | Abolished in provinces; proposed at the Centre |
| Provincial Autonomy | None | Initial separation of subjects | Full Provincial Autonomy |
| Communal representation | Separate electorates for Muslims | Extended | Retained or extended |
| Central Legislature | Enlarged, unicameral | Bicameral | Bicameral Federal Legislature proposed |
| Courts | Existing High Courts continued | Existing High Courts continued | Federal Court (1937) |
## 2. Dyarchy - introduced by, ended by
| Level | Introduced by | Ended by |
| Provinces | GoI Act 1919 - subjects split into transferred (Governor + Ministers) and reserved (Governor + Executive Council) | GoI Act 1935 - replaced by provincial autonomy and responsible government |
| Centre | GoI Act 1935 (reserved and transferred subjects under the GG) | Never took effect - the Federation never came up [GK] |
- Provincial autonomy: Dyarchy in the provinces abolished
- Passed 1935 → responsible governments in force from April 1937
- Key for "dismantled dual rule in the provinces"
- PYQ: Responsible governments in the provinces after ending dual rule = GoI Act 1935 (APPSC-GS 2025; paper printed no key)
- Dyarchy at the Centre: Reserved + transferred subjects → never came into operation
- Defence and foreign affairs = reserved subjects under the Governor-General (GG), not the federal legislature
- ⚠ Dyarchy: 1919 = provinces; 1935 = Centre (never worked)
### GoI Act 1935 → features, basis and federation
## 1. Government of India Act, 1935
- All-India Federation: Provided for the first time — never came into existence (not implemented)
- Dyarchy: Abolished in the provinces; Central Dyarchy proposed (1935) — never implemented
- Provincial Autonomy: Introduced in full
- Federal Legislature: Bicameral, proposed
- Communal representation: Retained or extended
- Federal Court: Established; inaugurated on 1 October 1937 — first Chief Justice: Sir Maurice Gwyer
- Territory: Burma separated from India; Sindh and Orissa created as new provinces
- Residuary powers: Vested in the Governor-General
- Ordinance: Section 42 of this Act is the model for Article 123 (President's Ordinance Power)
- Size (Prep): 14 parts, 321 sections, 10 schedules (APP calls them "Articles"); also RBI, PSCs, Council of India abolished
## 2. Basis and federation
- Basis: 3rd Round Table Conference and the White Paper
- All India Federation: Provinces + princely states as units
- Joining optional for the states → none joined → federation never came into being
- ⚠ Trap: "All India Federation of provinces and princely states" = correct; "Defence and Foreign Affairs under the federal legislature" = WRONG → answer "1 only" (UPSC 2024)
- Exam angle: UPSC 2024 paired "All India Federation" (correct) with "Defence and Foreign Affairs under the federal legislature" (wrong).
## 3. Lists and executive
- Three lists: Federal, Provincial, Concurrent
- Central executive: Vested in the Governor-General (APPSC 2007 key: "Governor-General in Council")
### GoI Act 1935 → legislatures, implemented or not
## 1. Legislatures and representation
- Bicameral centre: Federal Assembly + Council of States
- Bicameral provinces (6): Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces
- Bihar's Legislative Council (bicameral Bihar) came from the 1935 Act (BPSC 2024 key)
- Communal representation: Extended to depressed classes, women and labour
- Franchise: Rose from 3% to 14% of the population (some texts say about 10%)
- Separations: Burma separated from India; Sindh from Bombay; Orissa from Bihar
## 2. Provisions - implemented or not
| Provision | Implemented? |
| Federal Court at Delhi (chief justice + judges) | Yes, set up 1937[GK]; inaugurated 1 October 1937 |
| Council of India (from the 1858 Act) abolished; Secretary of State given advisers | Yes (UPSC, APP) |
| All India Federation | No - none of the states joined |
| Dyarchy at the Centre | No - never came into operation |
## 3. Borrowed into the Constitution
- Features taken: Federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions, administrative details
- Example: Sec. 63 (Governor's address) → Art. 175; Sec. 42 (ordinance) → Art. 123

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:0
### Company era Acts
## Before 1773
- Act of 1767 — Company to pay British Government £400,000 a year
- Parliament intervened in Bengal in 1772 — famine, falling revenue, Company's financial crisis
## Regulating Act, 1773
- Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings — first) with a four-member Executive Council
- Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774) — one Chief Justice (Sir Elijah Impey) + three judges
- Banned private trade and bribes by Company officials
- Did NOT abolish the Court of Directors (lasted until the 1858 Act)
## Pitt's India Act, 1784 and after
- Pitt's India Act, 1784 — Dual Control: Board of Control (political), Court of Directors (commercial); Council cut to three
- territories called "British possessions in India"; Company subordinate to the State
- Act of 1786 — Governor-General (Cornwallis) may override his Council; may also act as Commander-in-Chief (two separate powers)
## Charter Acts
| Act | Key provisions |
| 1793 | Board of Control salaries charged to Indian revenues; override power extended to all future GGs and Presidency Governors; more control over Bombay and Madras |
| 1813 | trade monopoly ended except tea and China trade; missionaries admitted; Rs 1 lakh a year for education |
| 1833 (Saint Helena Act) | monopoly completely ended; Company purely administrative; created Governor-General of India; Law Member (Macaulay); Law Commission under Macaulay (origin of Law Ministry); legislation centralised in GG-in-Council; final step to centralisation |
| 1853 (last Charter Act) | six legislative members added; legislative separated from executive; territories held in trust for the Crown; open competition put into operation |
- Order: Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Act 1793 → Charter Act 1813
- ★ Calling the Charter Act of 1813 the "Magna Carta" — marked the not-correct statement
- 1833 Act only attempted open competition — not implemented
- First Governor-General of India — William Bentinck (1833 Act created the post; he was just first holder)
- First Viceroy — Canning; last Viceroy — Mountbatten
- First Law Member — Macaulay (not Impey)
## Later Acts (for comparison)
- 1892 Indian Councils Act — indirect election; recommendations from universities, municipalities, chambers of commerce
- Nomination of Indians began 1861; separate electorates 1909
- Simon Commission — appointed 1927 (two years early); all seven members British; boycotted
### Crown rule: 1858 and 1861
## Government of India Act, 1858
- Transferred rule from the Company to the Crown; Governor-General became Viceroy
- Crown's powers via Secretary of State for India + 15-member Council of India
- Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished
## Indian Councils Act, 1861 and courts

### GoI Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford)
- Basis — Montagu's declaration, 20 August 1917

### GoI Act 1935
## Basis and structure
- Based on the Third Round Table Conference and the White Paper
- 321 sections, 14 parts, 10 schedules
## Federation and Centre
- Proposed All-India Federation and Central Dyarchy — neither implemented (no princely state joined; accession optional)

- Council of India (1858) — abolished; Secretary of State given advisers
