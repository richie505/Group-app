### Thinkers and books → population theories
| Theory / work | Author / propounder | Core idea |

- Preventive checks: Moral restraint, late marriage → lower birth rate

### Terms → meaning — transition, dividend, TFR — Terms
## 1. Terms
| Term | Meaning |
| "Demographic winter" | Persistent structural decline - fertility below 2.1, inverted pyramid |
| Demographic dividend (UNFPA) | Economic growth potential from a shift in age structure when the working-age share (15-64) exceeds the non-working share (0-14 and 65+) |
| Demographic dividend (alternative) | "A rise in economic growth due to a rise in the share of working people in the population" (APPSC-G2 2017 key) |

| TFR | Average number of children a woman is expected to have over ages 15-49 (reproductive years) |

| "shrinconomics" | AP Health Minister's warning (1 Apr 2026) on the "alarming" fall in fertility |

### Demographic transition stages
## Stages
- ★ Sequence with development: high birth–high death → high birth–low death → low birth–low death
- Five stages: high stationary, early expanding, late expanding, low stationary, declining
- Declining stage — birth rate below death rate
- ★ India's population explosion — 1951–81 (death rates fell, birth rates high) — Stage 2
- India overall now — Stage 3; AP — Stage 4; Japan, Germany — Stage 5
## History
- British period — high birth and death rates, famines and epidemics
- Life expectancy around 1951 — about 32 years
- 1921 — "great divide"; decline in 1911–21, continuous growth after; about 0.19% a year in 1891–1921
## Dividend and elderly
- Dividend channels — labour supply, savings and investment, lower dependency ratio, human capital; rising dependants hurts it
- RBI state-finances study — Kerala and Tamil Nadu crossed the demographic turning point; Bihar, UP have a wider window
- About 78% of elderly have no pension cover

- Marxian view — overpopulation is caused by capitalism (relative surplus population, unemployment)
