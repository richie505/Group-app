# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 4:34
### Dimensions & origin
- Himalayas cover about 5 lakh sq km (not 15 lakh)
- Steep slope faces the plains of India; gentler slope faces Tibet (uneven folds of the Himadri)
- Raised from Tethys sediments in the Tertiary (Eocene to Pliocene); India–Eurasia plate collision about 4-5 crore years ago; still rising
- Namcha Barwa — eastern end (Assam Himalaya), in Tibet; Nanga Parbat — western end
- Nathu La (Sikkim) — Gangtok–Tibet route via Chumbi Valley; Zoji La Srinagar–Leh; Bomdi La Tawang–Lhasa; Banihal Jammu–Srinagar
### Ranges, north to south
## The three parallel ranges
- Three parallel ranges — Himadri (Greater), Himachal (Lesser), Shiwalik (Outer)
- North to south — Karakoram, Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik
- Himadri — northernmost of the three; Shiwalik — outermost (southernmost)
- ★ South to north (western section) — Shiwalik – Lesser Himalaya – Great Himalaya
- ★ Lesser Himalaya — between Shiwalik and Great Himalaya
## Shiwalik
- ★ Shiwalik — foothills; formed in the Cenozoic (Pliocene, about 2-5 million years ago)
- Width 10-50 km, height 900-1,100 m; loose river sediments
- Debris through the Shiwalik helped build the Indo-Gangetic Plain
## Other ranges and features
- Pir Panjal — Lesser Himalaya; about 300 km, Jhelum–Beas sector
- Ladakh Range continues into Tibet as the Kailash Range (not the Zaskar)
- Ladakh Plateau (Trans-Himalaya) — highest plateau in India
- Nanda Devi — Greater Himalaya (Himadri)
- Past the Dihang gorge (near Namcha Barwa) the Himalayas turn south as the Purvanchal hills (500-3,000 m)
