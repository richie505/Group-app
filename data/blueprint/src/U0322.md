### Articles → vesting and extent of Union and State executive power
## 1. Position of the President
- Article 52: There shall be a President of India
- Article 53: Executive power of the Union vested in the President — exercised directly or through subordinate officers
- Nominal / constitutional head of the State (Head of the State, not Head of the Government)
- Real executive: The Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
- First Citizen of India; symbol of the unity, integrity and solidarity of the nation; Rank 1 in the Order of Precedence
- Supreme Commander of the Defence (Armed) Forces — Article 53(2) (regulated by law)
- Integral part of Parliament — Article 79
## 2. Vesting and extent of executive power
| Article | Level | Provision |
| Art. 53 | Union | Executive power of the Union vests in the President; exercised directly or through subordinate officers |
| Art. 73 (extent) | Union | Matters on which Parliament can legislate (Union List) + rights/authority under any treaty or agreement, across the whole of India |
| Art. 74(1) | Union | Council of Ministers headed by PM aids and advises; President acts on that advice |
| Art. 154 | State | Executive power of the state vests in the Governor, exercised directly or through subordinate officers |
| Art. 162 (extent) | State | Matters on which the state legislature can legislate (State List), within the state's territory |
| Art. 163(1) | State | Council of Ministers aids and advises the Governor, except in discretionary matters |
| Art. 371A / Art. 371H | State | Special responsibility (law and order): Governor of Nagaland (Art. 371A) and Arunachal Pradesh (Art. 371H) |
- Formal head: President = constitutional (formal) head
- ⚠ Trap: UPSC notes place it (Art. 154 executive power) in the Council of Ministers - that is where real power lies, but the Article vests it in the Governor
- Concurrent List - default rule: Executive power over Concurrent List subjects stays with the states
- Exception: Centre executes where the Constitution or a Parliamentary law expressly gives it power (proviso to Art. 73 and Art. 162)
- Territorial spread: Follows that of legislative power
### Articles → entrustment of functions and compliance (258, 258A, 256, 257, 365)
| Article | Direction | Condition |
| 258(1) | Union to state | President, with the state's consent, entrusts Union executive functions |
| 258(2) | Union to state | Parliament by law confers powers or duties on state officers; no consent needed |
| 258A | State to Union | State entrusting functions to the Union (APPSC-GS 2023) |
- ⚠ Trap: UPSC notes call Art. 258A "agreements between states on river valley projects" - WRONG; 258A = state entrusting functions to the Union (APPSC-GS 2023)
- Art. 256/257: States must comply with Union laws and not impede Union executive power
- Non-compliance → breakdown of constitutional machinery under Art. 365 → Art. 356
### Emergencies → effect on executive relations (Articles 353, 356, 360)
| Emergency | Effect on executive relations |
| National (Art. 352, via Art. 353) | Union executive can give directions to any state on any matter; state governments are not suspended |
| President's Rule (Art. 356) | President takes over the state's executive functions; the state Council of Ministers is dismissed; High Court powers untouched |
| Financial (Art. 360) | Centre can direct states to observe financial propriety; Money and Financial Bills can be required to be reserved for the President |
- ⚠ National Emergency = state governments not suspended; President's Rule = state Council of Ministers dismissed
### Union Territories → executive heads (Art. 239, Art. 239AA)
- Art. 239: UTs administered by the President through an Administrator
- Executive power exercised by the Lieutenant Governor or Administrator as President's representative
- Delhi - Art. 239AA (69th Amendment, 1991): Legislative Assembly + Council of Ministers
- Public order, police and land outside the Assembly's power
- Not in your sources: the current position on who controls Delhi's services (SC 2023 ruling and the GNCTD Amendment Act, 2023).
### Universal angles
- Current relevance - Ladakh:
- Sui generis elected body with legislative, executive and financial powers - neither Statehood nor a UT with a legislature (Chief Secretary, 11 Sep 2026)
- MHA + Ladakh groups finalised a model with Art. 371-like safeguards instead of the Sixth Schedule (Jul 2026)
- Current relevance - Governors:
- SC: Governor bound by state Cabinet advice when granting remission under Art. 161 (Apr 2026)
- Governors skipping the address to the legislature test constitutional limits (24-26 Jan 2026; Shamsher Singh 1974, Nabam Rebia 2016)
- Criticism & debates: Art. 258(2) and Art. 365 let the Union act in state administration without state consent
