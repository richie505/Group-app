### Causes, demands and outcomes → Factor
| Movement | Cause / demand | Outcome |
| Indigo Revolt | European planters forced indigo under the Tinkathia system | Indigo Commission (1860) declared Tinkathia illegal |
| Deccan Riots | Under ryotwari + Limitation Law of 1859, moneylenders (outsiders from Gujarat and Marwar) seized peasants' land | Peasants attacked moneylenders' houses + burned debt bonds; government passed the Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act, 1879 |
| Oudh Kisan Sabha | Rent hikes + begar (forced labour) by taluqdars | - |
| Eka movement (1921-22) | Region: Hardoi-Bahraich-Sitapur | Turned violent, outside Congress control |
| Bardoli (1928) | - | Disciplined, non-violent Congress campaign led by Patel |
| Tebhaga | Bargadars wanted their share raised from one-half to two-thirds + crop stored in their own godowns | Bargadari Act (1950); short-run failure from limited spread + Hindu-Muslim rift |
| Telangana armed struggle | Nizam's feudal order - jagirdars, deshmukhs + Vetti (forced labour); peasants and tribals | Land seized and redistributed; village soviets set up; called off in 1951 after Indian Army integrated Hyderabad + land reform promised |
- ⚠ Trap: Eka (violent, outside Congress) vs Bardoli (disciplined, non-violent Congress campaign)
- Exam angle: UPSC 2013 asked for Tebhaga's demand, which was to cut the landlord's share from one-half to one-third.

- Indigo Revolt — led by Digambar and Bishnu Biswas
- Pabna Agrarian League (1873-76) — Ishan Chandra Roy and Shambhu Pal
- Deccan Riots (1875) → Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act, 1879 (1859 = Limitation Law)

| Champaran (1917) | Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918 |
## UP, Gujarat, Malabar, Hyderabad
- ★ Bardoli Satyagraha — against a 30% increase in land revenue; Eka — against excessive rent by zamindars and taluqdars
- Oudh Kisan Sabha — against taluqdars' rent hikes and begar
- Moplah — Malabar, 1921; leaders Variyankunnathu Kunjahammad Haji and Ali Musliyar; Muslim tenants against landlords and the British
- attack on Gujarati and Marwari moneylenders = Deccan Riots
| Movement | Region |

| Moplah | Malabar |
| Kheda | Gujarat |

### Cause → outcome
- Tinkathia — indigo on 3/20 of the peasants' land, at poor prices
- Indigo Commission (1860) — declared the Tinkathia system illegal
- Tebhaga = two-thirds — bargadars wanted this share, not one-half
- Telangana armed struggle — land seized and redistributed; village soviets set up
### Telangana armed struggle
- CPI leaders — P. Sundarayya and Chandra Rajeswara Rao
- Memoir Veera Telangana - Naa Anubhavalu, Gnapakalu — Ravi Narayana Reddy (official key; not Mallu Swarajyam)
- Andhra Mahasabha President 1941-46 — M. Suryanarayana (APPSC G2 2019 key); it had become the CPI's organisational front
### Kisan movement in Andhra (Ranga)
- ★ First Kisan school — Nidubrolu (Guntur), Bharatiya Kisan school, est. 1934 by N.G. Ranga
- also called Ramanayidu Rythu Vidyalayam / Peasant Institute; est. 1933-34 with Bharathi Devi and Ranga's brothers
- 1923 — India's first Agricultural Labourers' Mahasabha at Nidubrolu under Ranga
- 1928 — Ranga founded the Andhra Provincial Ryots' Association
- By the 1940s, the Andhra Mahasabha — organisational front of the CPI, moved from cultural work to leftist farm politics
- Munagala estate — zamindar Nayani Venkataranga Rao; Batchala Jagannadh Das with its peasant association
### Andhra anti-zamindari & UP
## UP
- UP Kisan Sabha (1918) — Gauri Shankar Mishra and Indra Narayan Dwivedi, backed by M.M. Malaviya
- Oudh Kisan Sabha (October 1920) — Jawaharlal Nehru and Baba Ramchandra; against rent hikes and begar
- Eka Movement (1921-22) — Madari Pasi; turned violent, stayed outside Congress control
- Bardoli — non-violent Congress campaign led by Patel
## Andhra
- Permanent Settlement (1793, Cornwallis) — made zamindars proprietors, cultivators tenants
- Zamin Rytu Sangam (1930) — Vennelakanti Raghavaiah; paper Zamin Rytu
- Zamindari Abolition Committee (1937, Rajaji) — chaired by T. Prakasam
- Madras Zamindari Abolition Bill — 1947
### Peasant movements → year, region and key point
- ★ Moplah Revolt (1921) — Malabar (Kerala); Muslim tenants against Hindu landlords and the British: no security of tenure, renewal fees, high rent
### Causes, demands and outcomes → Factor
- ★ Tebhaga (1946-47, Bengal) — reduce the landlords' share from one-half to one-third; bargadars / adhiars against jotedars; per the Floud Commission
- ★ Eka Movement — aim: transformation of rent into cash
- ★ Eka (1921-22) started by farmers of Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur (UP); issue: 50% more tax than accepted; led by Madari Pasi
### Leaders → movement and role
- ★ Doddi Komaraiah — killed 4 July 1946, Janagam taluka, Nalgonda, defending a poor washer-woman's land; early martyr of the Telangana struggle
- ★ UP Kisan Sabha (February 1918) — Gauri Shankar Mishra and Indra Narayan Dwivedi with Malaviya's support; Nehru was NOT associated
- ★ Tebhaga (1946) — led by the Kisan Sabha (Bangiya Pradeshik Kisan Sabha); rent cut to 1/3 per the Floud Commission
