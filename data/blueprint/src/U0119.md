## Qutb Shahi dynasty — basics
- Dynasty: 1518-1687 (some notes give 1512 as start), 7 rulers, Shia
- Qara Qoyunlu (Black Sheep) Turkmen from Hamadan
- Official language Persian
- Rule length: 31 years accepted for Quli Qutb Shah (APPSC-GS 2023) - counts from 1512, not 1518
### Qutb Shahi rulers → contributions
| Ruler | Key contribution |
| Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk (1518-43) | Founder, a Turkman from Hamadan. Bahmani tarafdar of Telangana from 1496; title Qutb-ul-Mulk gave the dynasty its name; declared independence in 1518. Defeated by Krishnadevaraya at Goni (1517). Strengthened Golconda fort (renamed Muhammadnagar); built the Golconda Jama Masjid and the new city of Muhammad Nagar. Killed by son Jamsheed |
| Jamsheed / Jamshid (1543-50) | Took the throne by patricide; won the Battle of Koulan over Bidar (1544); created a cabinet with Wakil, Peshwa and Mir Jumla; died of tuberculosis |
| Ibrahim Qutb Shah (1550-80) | Spent 7 years in exile at Vijayanagara; "Malkibharama" / "Malkibharamudu" in Telugu literature; chief patron of Telugu poets. Led the Talikota (1565) alliance. Hussain Sagar (1562) and the Ibrahimpatnam tank date from his reign; first Qutb Shahi coins; Golconda called the "Second Egypt" |
| Muhammad Quli (1580-1612) | Founded Hyderabad (1591); built the Charminar, the Dar-ul-Shifa (1595) and the Jama Masjid (1598). Dakhni/Urdu poet (pen-name Maani). Allowed the Dutch (1605) and the English (1611) to trade at Machilipatnam |
| Muhammad Qutb Shah (1612-26) | Nephew and son-in-law of Muhammad Quli; began the Mecca Masjid (1614); pen name Zillullah |
| Abdullah Qutb Shah (1626-72) | The longest reign; ruled with his mother Hayat Bakshi Begum. Signed the Inqiyad Nama (1636) / 1636 deed of submission to Shah Jahan, accepting Mughal suzerainty. Issued the Bangaru Farmana (1636) to the English. Patron of Kshetrayya; Tavernier visited; Urdu literature peaked |
| Abul Hasan Tana Shah (1672-87) | Last ruler. Relied on Madanna and Akkanna; made a treaty with Shivaji (1677) |
- English arrival (Hawkins): In reign of Muhammad Quli; APPSC 2025 accepted "Quli Qutub Shah"
- ⚠ Trap: APPSC G2 2008 key gave Ibrahim Qutb Shah, though Ibrahim died in 1580 - if Muhammad Quli is not an option, choose Ibrahim
- Dakhni:
- Muhammad Quli wrote Dakhni/Urdu poetry (pen name "Maani", Kulliyat-e-Quli Qutb Shah)
- Urdu peaked under Abdullah; some notes call Ibrahim the "Chaucer of Urdu"
- ⚠ Pen names: Maani = Muhammad Quli; Zillullah = Muhammad Qutb Shah

### Qutb Shahi administration — officials → duty
- Hierarchy: Sultan → Taraf → Sarkar → Pargana (Tahsildar / Deshmukh / Deshpande) → village (Muqaddam, Karanam, Ayagars)
| Official | Duty |
| Peshwa / Wakil | Prime minister; renamed Diwan under Abul Hasan (held by Madanna) |
| Mir Jumla (Jumlat-ul-Mulk) | Finance minister (CDX, APP); CDI Block03 calls it war and foreign affairs |
| Ain-ul-Mulk | Military department |
| Tarafdar | Governor of a Taraf (province): law and order, revenue, troops |
| Fauzdar / Faujdar | Head of a Sarkar (district) |
| Tahsildar / Deshmukh | Head of a Pargana (taluk) |
| Mukaddam / Kulkarni | Village head / accountant |

### Qutb Shahi revenue — sources and terms
- Chief source: Land revenue

| Term | Meaning |
| Haveli | Crown lands |

| Mustajirs | Highest bidders who bought revenue rights in tax farming (mostly Hindus) → exploitation |

### Conquests, battles and Mughal annexation
## 1. Golconda's conquests in Andhra
- Sultan Quli: Took Rachakonda, Devarakonda, Warangal (Shitab Khan), Rajahmundry, Eluru, Kovilakonda
- Ibrahim (after 1565): Gained Kondavidu and Machilipatnam; annexed coastal Andhra (1570-78)
- Unification: Qutb Shahis united Telugu lands for the third time (after the Satavahanas and Kakatiyas)
- Talikota, 1565: Four of the five (all except Berar) combined to defeat Vijayanagara (UPSC)
## 2. Mughal annexation
| Emperor | Action |
| Akbar | Took Ahmadnagar city (1600) + Asirgarh/Khandesh (1601) |
| Shah Jahan | Ended the Nizam Shahis (1633); 1636: Bijapur and Golconda signed ahdnamas - suzerainty + indemnity |
| Aurangzeb | Attacked Golconda (1656) as Deccan subedar; annexed Bijapur (1686), then besieged Golconda |
- Akkanna and Madanna murdered in 1686
- Fall of Golconda: Traitor Abdullah Phani/Khan (Abdullah Khan Pani) opened the gates to Aurangzeb on 21 September 1687 (21 Sept 1687)
- Last ruler: Abul Hasan Tana Shah imprisoned at Bidar, then Daulatabad; died 1699 (some notes say 1700); buried at Khuldabad
- Aftermath: Golconda = Mughals' 21st Suba; Abul Razak Lari honoured for his defence
- ⚠ 1633 Nizam Shahis – 1686 Bijapur – 1687 Golconda

### Qutb Shahi rulers
## Rulers
- Sultan Quli (Qutb-ul-Mulk) — gave the dynasty its name; strengthened Golconda fort; killed by his son Jamsheed
- Ibrahim Qutb Shah — title Malkibharama; issued the first Qutb Shahi coins (coinage does not explain the title); built the high walls of Golconda
- Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah — Dakhni poetry as 'Maani' (Kulliyat-e-Quli Qutb Shah); built Charminar and Dar-ul-Shifa (1595); let the English come to Machilipatnam
- Muhammad Qutb Shah — pen name Zillullah; began the Mecca Masjid (1614), completed by Aurangzeb (1692–93)
- Abdullah Qutb Shah — signed the Inqiyad Nama (1636): accepted Mughal suzerainty; Bangaru Farmana (1636): trading privileges to the English
- Abul Hasan — office of Peshwa/Wakil renamed Diwan, held by Madanna
## Hyderabad and Golconda
- Hyderabad planned by Peshwa Mir Momin Astarabadi after its foundation by Muhammad Quli
- Golconda fort — handclap at the gate heard at the Bala Hisar; Charminar linked with the Char Kaman gateways
- Movva padams = Kshetrayya; Akkanna was a military official
## Patrons (other)
- Rudradaman I — Sanskrit; Satavahanas — Prakrit; Krishnadeva Raya — Telugu; Zain-ul-Abidin — translation
- Kishangarh painting — Sawant Singh; Jahangir — European and Persian arts; Rana Kumbha — music, literature, art

- Dasabandhu lands paid one-tenth; haveli = crown lands
## Other
- Malik-e-Maidan (Bijapur) — a cannon

- Bonalu — began at Jagadamba Mahankali temple, Golconda fort; roots in Kakatiya and Qutb Shahi periods

- Machilipatnam: Dutch factory 1605; English factory 1611 (Captain Hippon, ship Globe)
- Machilipatnam famous for muslin and Kalamkari

### Qutb Shahi rulers → contributions
- ★ Bahmani split into five: Adil Shahi (Bijapur), Nizam Shahi (Ahmednagar), Qutb Shahi (Golconda), Barid Shahi (Bidar), Imad Shahi (Berar)
- ★ Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah = 'Malkibharam' — title from his subjects for peaceful, tolerant rule
- ★ Golconda (Qutb Shahi capital, famed for diamonds) — ruins now in Hyderabad
- ★ Battle of Golconda: Krishnadeva Raya vs Quli Qutb Shah (founder of the Qutb Shahi dynasty; took forts at Warangal, Kondaveedu)
### Bijapur and Ahmednagar persons → contributions
- ★ Ibrahim Adil Shah II (Bijapur, 1580–1627) — called 'Jagadguru' for his secular, mixed-faith outlook; supported Hindu scholars
- ★ Kitab-i-Nauras (Book of Nine Rasas) — by Ibrahim Adil Shah II, in Deccani; songs praising Hindu deities (Saraswati, Ganesha) and Muslim saints; 59 songs, 17 couplets
- ★ He founded Nauraspur as his capital
- ★ Amir Khusrau — creator in India of the early form of Qawwali
### Telugu patronage and literature → author
- ★ Yayati Charitra — Ponnaganti Telaganarya; first work in pure (achcha) Telugu
### Qutb Shahi administration — officials → duty
- ★ Mahasibs / Mustasibs — in charge of public morals
### Qutb Shahi revenue — sources and terms
- ★ Main source of state income: land revenue
- State monopolies: salt, betel leaf, tobacco
### Hyderabad, Golconda and Bijapur monuments → builder / feature
- ★ Gol Gumbad (Bijapur) — mausoleum of Muhammad Adil Shah (17th c.); one of the world's largest domes; has a mosque attached to its complex
- ★ Charminar (1591) — Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah; marks end of plague
### Golconda diamonds, travellers and Machilipatnam trade
- ★ Kohinoor — from the Kollur mines on the Krishna (Palnadu region), Qutb Shahi period; noted by Tavernier
- ★ Given to Shah Jahan by Mir Jumla, minister of Abdullah Qutb Shah (1626–72)
- ★ English factory at Machilipatnam (1611) — permission of Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (1580–1612)
### Conquests, battles and Mughal annexation
- ★ Ahmadnagar (city founded by Ahmad Nizam Shah) conquered and annexed to the Mughal empire by Shah Jahan (1632)
- ★ Shah Jahan sent Mahawat Khan for Daulatabad fort; Fateh Khan bribed 10.5 lakhs
### Universal angles
## 1. Current relevance
- Machilipatnam port: Port works revive the old Gilakaladindi port — a transhipment hub under the Golconda dynasty
- Golconda Blue diamond (23.24 carats): 23.24 ct, fancy vivid blue, pear-shaped; in the news May 2025
- Hussain Sagar: Named after Hussain Shah Wali; built under Ibrahim (1562 / 1563 — notes differ)
- Declared the UNWTO "Heart of the World" (2012)
- Bonalu: Telangana festival began at the Jagadamba Mahankali temple inside Golconda fort
- Goes back to the Kakatiya + Qutb Shahi periods
## 2. Firsts/lasts & significance
- FIRST/LAST:
- First Qutb Shahi to mint coins: Ibrahim
- Last ruler: Abul Hasan Tana Shah
- First European factory in Andhra: Dutch, Machilipatnam 1605
- Significance:
- Hindus held high office (Murari Rao, Jagadeva Rao, Madanna)
- Muharram celebrated as Peerla Panduga
