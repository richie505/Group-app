- Amaram = land to military chiefs instead of cash

### Central administration → officers and departments (Kandachara, Athavane)
## 1. King & council
- King: Supreme head, commander + fountain of justice; bound by Dharma + the ministers' counsel
- Burton Stein: a "segmentary state"
## 2. Officer → role
| Officer | Role |
| Mahapradhana/Mahapradhani | PM (e.g. Saluva Timmarusu); headed the pradhanis (ministers) |
| Dalavayi | C-in-C |
| Rayasam | Secretaries (Kondamarasu, Ayyamarasu) |
| Karanikas | Scribes |
## 3. Departments
- Rayavachakam: Records 9 departments, each headed by a Samprati
| Department / official | Function |
| Athavane / Attavana | Revenue / finance (APP, CDX) |
| Kandachara / Kandacharya | Military: the standing army, headed by the Dalavayi (APPSC-GS 2018; APPSC G2 2019) |
| Bhandara (Sri Bhandara) | Treasury |
| Dharmasana; Pradvivakulu | Judicial department; judges |
- ⚠ Athavane = revenue; Kandachara = military; Bhandara = treasury
### Amuktamalyada → Krishnadevaraya's political ideas
## 1. Nature of the work
- Approach: Devotion + practical advice on kingship and administration; "political matters are discussed" (APPSC G2 2003)
- Genealogy: Records the genealogy of the dynasty but not his campaigns (APPSC G2 2003)
## 2. Political maxims
| Area | Maxim |
| Revenue | Bulk of revenue should go to the army |
| Dharma | King should rule with an eye on Dharma + protect his subjects |
| Trade | Improve harbours; encourage import of horses, elephants and gems; treat foreign merchants generously |
| Welfare | Build tanks and irrigation; win over forest tribes rather than only crush them |
### Provincial administration → units, heads and governors' powers
## 1. Unit → head
| Unit | Head |
| Rajya / Mandala / Rashtra (province) | Prince (Kumara; Sangama princes titled Odeyar/Vodeyar) or Durgadhipati / Durga Dannayaka; office called Chavadi |
| Nadu / Seema (district) | Nadagauda; Parupatyagar revenue officials |
| Sthala / Kampana (sub-district) | Reddi, Gauda, Sthalakaranam; Kavalgar for order |
- Levels: Rajyam/Rashtram (province) > Seema (Nadu in Telugu; headed by Parupatyandaru) > Sthalam (Reddi, Gouda or Sthalakaranam) > Gramam
- Number: About 17 rajyams under Achyutaraya (listed in his inscriptions)
- Vanitas: Aravidu inscriptions divide the Udayagiri, Penukonda and Hampi Rajyams into Vanitas
## 2. Governors
- Powers: Mint coins, remit old taxes and levy new ones; grant tax exemptions; own armies
- Control: Often transferred; Brahmins deliberately posted as dannayakas in border provinces
- Not in your sources: the individual governors of Udayagiri, Penukonda, Chandragiri and Gooty.
### Nayankara system → amaram terms, obligations and scale
## 1. Terms → meaning
| Term | Meaning |
| Amaram | Land the king granted to military chiefs instead of cash salaries |
| Amaranayakas / Nayakas / Poligars | The chiefs holding amarams (also Nayaks, Palaiyagars) |
| Mahamandaleshwara | Their title |
- Status: More autonomy than the Durgadannayakas
- Powers within amarams: Collected taxes, kept law and order, administered justice
## 2. Obligations
- Troops: Maintain a quota of troops (infantry, cavalry, horses, elephants) ready for the king in war
- Court attendance: Attend court at Mahanavami festival with gifts
- Local duties: Keep order, maintain forts, fund temples + irrigation
### Palegars → from amara-nayakas to the poligars of Rayalaseema
## 1. Origin
- Origin: Amara-nayakas → Palegars (Poligars)
- Rise: Central authority weakened after Talikota (1565) → hereditary local rulers; forerunners of the zamindars
## 2. Poligars/Palegars of Rayalaseema - suppression
| Person / place | Fact |
| Thomas Munro | Suppression with military officer Campbell (APPSC G2 2011 key) → then brought in Ryotwari |
### Ayagar system → village functionaries, payment and officers
## 1. Ayagar system - village functionaries, number, mode of payment
- Number: Twelve = 12 Ayagars (Ayagandlu) (APPSC 2012), i.e. 12 hereditary Ayagars
- Functions: Revenue, order, irrigation + records
| Group | Members |
| Officials | Karanam (accountant), Reddi (village head), Talari (watchman), Purohita (priest) |
| Artisans and servants | Kamsali (goldsmith), Kummari (potter), Vadrangi / Vadragi (carpenter), Kase (mason / house-builder), Kammari (blacksmith), Chakali (washerman), Mangali (barber), Madiga |
## 2. Payment terms
| Term | Meaning |
| Manyam lands | Tax-free lands (Mirasi / Bhattuvritti / Jatikattu manyams; Bhattu Vrutti, Jatikattu Manyalu) |
| Mera | A harvest share (share of the harvest) |
- Payment: No cash salary
- Significance: Hereditary + self-sufficient → carried villages through political chaos
## 3. Village officers - duties
| Officer | Duties |
| Karanam | Revenue affairs, village accounts, land + crop registers, temple accounts |
| Reddi | Village head + collector of revenue; under the Kakatiyas also village judge |
| Talari | Watchman for the Sthalakavali area; no salary → collected Merulu (gifts in grain) from farmers + merchants at harvest |
| Begari | Supervised forced labour (vetti) |
- ⚠ Mera = Ayagar harvest share; Merulu = Talari's grain gifts; Meruka = tax on sale and purchase
### Land revenue → terms, land classes and share of produce
- Main revenue: Land tax (main source); land-tax documents = Adhuvanatantram
## 1. Land classes
| Term | Land class |
| Nanja | Wet |
| Punja | Dry |
| Bagayati | Garden |
| Banjara | Waste |
- Andhra terms: Tax rates depended on the quality of land
| Land class | Paid in | Rate |
| Neerarambamu (Magani, irrigated) | Grain | Higher |
| Kaadarambamu (Metta, dry) | Cash | Lower |
## 2. Share of produce
- Usually 1/6; rising to 1/4 or 1/3 on fertile land or in war
- Andhra records: Sangoru (1/2) + Mungoru (1/3)
- Brahmin inam lands paid 1/6
## 3. Other land-linked taxes
- Tax rates: Private owners of workshops paid an industries tax (UPSC 2016 key: both statements correct)
### Tax terms → meaning, and Krishnadevaraya's tax policy — Tax terms
## 1. Tax terms
| Tax / term | Object / meaning |
| Navidadere | Barbers' tax (Kannada navida = barber) [GK]. CDX gives the Telugu name Kondaju, which Sadashiva Raya abolished |
| Maneyanna | House tax (Kannada mane = house) [GK]. The Telugu name is Illari Pannu |
| Kanika / Kanuka | Levy or present: Kalyanakanikey / Kalyana kanika (Gudikalyanam) (wedding tax on both parties), Putrodaya Kanuka (tax on the birth of a son) |
| Siddhaya | Fixed levy. In Vijayanagara: Pinje Siddhayam (cotton ginners), Eidipi Siddhayam (toddy tappers), Jati Siddhayam (castes/professions). Under the Kakatiyas: an extraordinary war-time tax |
| Meruka | Tax on sale and purchase of goods (APPSC G2 2011 key; APHQ places it in the Reddi period; also called a Reddy-period commercial tax). Some notes call it a tax on wetlands |
- APPSC G1 2023 match: Navididere, Malavanam, Maneyanna, Tudimova ↔ taxes on marriage, barbers, ploughs, houses; your bank has no official key
- Not in your sources: the meanings of Malavanam and Tudimova . The APPSC-G1 2023 answer options only work if they mean ploughs and marriage respectively, once Navidadere = barbers and Maneyanna = houses. Confirm these.
### Tax terms → meaning, and Krishnadevaraya's tax policy — Tax policy
## 1. Tax policy
- Marriage tax:
- First remitted/lifted in some areas by Saluva Narasimha
- Then abolished empire-wide by Krishnadevaraya
- Market tolls: Right to collect market (santhalu) taxes auctioned to Sunkandarlu
### Army → wings, firearms, forts and cavalry
## 1. Army wings & composition
- Two wings:
- Siddha / standing army: under the king via the Kandachara, headed by the Dalavayi; paid every 4 months; Nuniz: about 50,000
- Krishnadevaraya's strength: 24,000 horses + 1,200 elephants + 5 lakh infantry
## 2. Firearms & forts
- Firearms: Came with Turkish + Portuguese experts
- Bukka I = first Vijayanagara king to use cannon
- Saluva Narasimha introduced Samugaris (gunmen)
- Forts: Giri, Sthala, Jala, Vana (Rayavachakam), under Durga Dannayakas
- Krishnadevaraya advised making Brahmins fort commanders
## 3. Cavalry & horse imports
- Horse imports: Arabian/Persian horses imported via Goa, Honnavar, Bhatkal
- Trade run by Portuguese + Arab merchants → bullion drain
- ⚠ FIRST: cannon = Bukka I; Samugaris = Saluva Narasimha
### Judicial and police terms → meaning — pradvivaka and the Kavali system
## 1. Judges & law
- King: Supreme judge (Dharmasana); ministers such as Timmarusu ("Dharma Pratipalaka") heard cases for him
- Pradvivaka: Judicial adviser/judge assisting the king (APPSC G2 2019 key: Judicial System)
- Law: Yajnavalkya and Parashara Smritis via Madhavacharya's Parashara Madhaviyam
| Term | Meaning |
| Himsodbhava | Criminal case |
| Dhanodbhava | Civil case |
- Some notes: only four types, perhaps not always in use
- Court hierarchy: Village courts under the Gauda → Nadu/provincial courts → royal court
## 2. Punishments
- Death for treason; mutilation for theft
- Brahmins got life imprisonment instead of death
## 3. Police - Kavali system
| Level | Police |
| Village | Talari |
| Sthala level | Kavali Daru kept law and order |
| Towns | Peta Kavalidaru |
### Nayankara compared → Kakatiya, Vijayanagara and Mughal mansabdari
| Point | Kakatiya nayankara | Vijayanagara amara-nayankara | Mughal mansabdari |
| Origin | Prataparudra II (APPSC 2025 key / statement) | Evolved from (grew out of) the Kakatiya system (CDX) | Akbar [GK] |
| Units / scale | 75/77 nayakas held mandalams and forts (APP); other notes: 75 (or 77), each governing a Mandalam | Amaram fiefs; about 200 nayakas holding amarams, including Shudra chiefs | Numerical ranks (zat/sawar) [GK] |
| Pay / terms | Kept a share of revenue for troops, sent the rest; held a fort and a quarter of the land, paid a fixed sum | Land revenue kept; tribute (Kappam) paid, kept troops, attended Mahanavami | Cash or a transferable jagir [GK] |
| Result / outcome | Loyalty via land; discontent among older feudatories | Semi-independent chiefs, then semi-independent Nayaka kingdoms / independent Nayaka states after 1565 (Madurai, Tanjore, Keladi) | Non-hereditary, centrally controlled [GK] |
- ⚠ Kakatiya 75/77 nayakas vs Vijayanagara about 200
### Universal angles
## 1. Current relevance
- Current relevance: Sadasivakona inscriptions (June 2026): ASI found three Telugu-Tamil-Kannada inscriptions of Sadasiva Raya dated 31 July 1554 (1554 CE) at Sadasivakona, Seshachalam forest (Tirupati district)
- Land grants by a temple Karanam; second inscription written by a Gudi Karanam
- Show revenue assignment + temple grants at work
## 2. Debates & significance
- Criticism & debates: Nilakanta Sastri: "military administration" vs Burton Stein: "segmentary state"
- Nayankara significance: Huge army cheaply, but produced the Nayaka kingdoms that broke away after Talikota
- Comparative note: Ayagar system + Nayankara tenure both continued Kakatiya practice
- Kakatiyas also had 12 Ayagars, paid through a harvest share called Mera

## King and Amuktamalyada
- King — supreme head, commander, fountain of justice; acted within Dharma and with his ministers
- Amuktamalyada advice: improve harbours, encourage imports, treat foreign merchants generously; win over forest tribes instead of just crushing them
- Amuktamalyada records the genealogy of the dynasty, not Krishnadevaraya's campaigns

- Provincial governors — could mint coins, raise/cancel taxes, keep armies; were frequently transferred
- Amaram — nayaka's territory: collected taxes, kept law and order, administered justice
- Amaranayakas' title — Mahamandaleshwara; more freedom than Durgadannayakas
- Palegars — forerunners of the zamindars; Munro introduced Ryotwari after crushing them
