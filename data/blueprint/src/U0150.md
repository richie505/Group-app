### Gurus → contributions — Kabir and Guru Nanak
## 1. Kabir and Guru Nanak
- Kabir:
- Disciple of Ramananda; raised by Muslim weavers Niru and Nima
- One God by many names (Ram, Rahim, Allah, Hari); condemned rituals of both faiths; Hindu-Muslim unity
- Followers = Kabirpanthis; work = Bijak
- Founder: Guru Nanak (1469-1539; some notes give 1469-1538)
- Born at Nankana Sahib (Talwandi) near the Ravi
- Teachings: Nirguna Bhakti, Ek Onkar (God is one), Naam Simran, Sangat; rejected rituals of both religions
- Successor: chose Guru Angad before death (1539) (UPPSC 2023)
### Gurus → contributions — The ten Gurus in order

### Guru Granth Sahib → compiler and contributors
- Guru Nanak: Founded Sikhism — taught Ik Onkar and monotheism
- Guru Arjan Dev: The fifth Sikh Guru — the Adi Granth, also called the Guru Granth Sahib
- It included the compositions of Kabir, Baba Farid, Namdev and Raidas
- Guru Tegh Bahadur: The ninth Sikh Guru — executed in Delhi in 1675
- Guru Gobind Singh: The tenth — the last Sikh Guru; established the Khalsa in 1699
- Compilation: Adi Granth compiled by Guru Arjan; finalised as Guru Granth Sahib by Guru Gobind Singh
- APPSC 2019: Sikh scripture = Adigranth
- Non-Sikh contributors: Kabir, Sufi Sheikh Farid, Namdev, and 41 shabdas of Ravidas
- Script: Gurmukhi
- ⚠ Compiled by Arjan (5th); finalised by Gobind Singh (10th); 116 hymns = Tegh Bahadur; 41 shabdas = Ravidas

### Banda Bahadur and the Sikh misls — events → year
## 1. Banda Bahadur
- Original name: Lachhman Dev (May 2026)
- Appointment: Disciple of Guru Gobind Singh; commissioned by him at Nanded (1708)
| Year | Event |
| 1709 | Seized Samana |
| 1710 | Defeated Wazir Khan at Chappar Chiri; conquered Sirhind → first sovereign Sikh state, capital Lohgarh |
| 1715 | Eight-month siege of Gurdas Nangal, captured |
| 1716 | Executed at Delhi by Farrukhsiyar for refusing to convert |
- Coins: Minted in the names of Guru Nanak and Guru Gobind Singh (UPSC)
## 2. Sikh misls
- Autonomous rule: Khalsa administration between the Sutlej and Jamuna after 1708 (UPSC)
- 12 misls: After Banda, Khalsa organised into 12 misls (e.g. Bhangi, Ahluwalia, Ramgarhia, Kanhaiya, Sukerchakia, Phulkian)
- Unification: Ranjit Singh (Sukerchakia) → kingdom of Lahore
### Ranjit Singh → contributions, treaties and successors
## 1. Rise and state
- Origin: Sukerchakia misl; "Lion of Punjab"; united the Sikh misls (combined force = Dal Khalsa)
- Capital: Captured Lahore in 1799; capital Lahore (APPSC 2018)
- Nature of state: Secular Punjabi state built out of the Khalsa tradition
- Kohinoor: Obtained from Afghan ruler Shah Shuja (1813)
## 2. Treaties with the British
| Treaty | Year | Provision |
| Treaty of Amritsar | 1809 | With Lord Minto (JPSC 2023); fixed the Sutlej as boundary between British and Ranjit Singh's territories |
- Exam angle: capital and Treaty of Amritsar are the most-asked facts.
## 3. Death and successors
- Death: 1839 (some notes misprint 1836) → chaos and indiscipline
- Successors: Kharak Singh, Nau Nihal Singh, Sher Singh, Duleep Singh / Dalip Singh (last; minor, with Rani Jindan as regent)
