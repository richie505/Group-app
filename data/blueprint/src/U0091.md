### Hoysala style — features, terms and sculptors
- Architecture: A distinctive style — star-shaped or stellate plans; soft chlorite-schist stone; lathe-turned pillars; mantapas, kalyanis, pushkaranis; blends Nagara and Dravidian styles
| Feature | Hoysala practice |
| Style family | Vesara in exam keys (Hoysaleshwara = Vesara, CGPSC 2023); CDI calls it an evolution of Dravida |
| Ground plan | Stellate (star-shaped), on a raised jagati platform about 1 m high |
| Stone | Chloritic schist (soapstone) - allows jewel-like carving |
| Pillars | Lathe-turned, finely polished |
| Halls | Open mantapas; several shrines round a central pillared hall |
| Sculpture | Horizontal friezes (elephants, lions, horses, epics); bracket figures Madanikas/Salabhanjikas at Belur |
- Stellate plan: Originated under the Chalukyas (6th c.) → peaked under the Hoysalas (12th c.) (UPSC)
- Kalyani Chalukyas passed on soapstone + star plans
- Pillar type: Carved into 3-4 sections on a single base, with leaping lion/horse heads → influenced Vijayanagara
### Universal angles
- Current relevance: Veeragallu (hero stone) with Kannada inscription found at Neeragunda, Arsikere taluk (unearthed 28 June 2026, reported 7 Aug 2026)
- May settle the long-disputed date of Vishnuvardhana's death
- Comparative note: Vijayanagara architecture followed the Hoysala + Pandya styles

### Hoysala style
- Style: generally Vesara in exam keys (also called an evolution of Dravida)
- Material: soft chlorite schist (soapstone)
- Stellate (star-shaped) plan, peaked in the 12th century
- Raised platform of about one metre — jagati
- Lathe-turned pillars, finely polished (not left rough); three or four sections on one base, leaping animal heads; influenced Vijayanagara architecture
- Madanika (Salabhanjika) bracket figures — Belur (not Somanathapura)
