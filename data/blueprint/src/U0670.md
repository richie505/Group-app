### Green Revolution — persons, inputs, phases and regions
## 1. Persons → role
| Person | Title / role |
| Norman Borlaug | Father of the Green Revolution in the world; HYV wheat (Mexican dwarf wheat); Nobel Peace Prize 1970 |
| M.S. Swaminathan | Father of the Green Revolution in India; brought HYV wheat to India |
| C. Subramaniam | Union Agriculture Minister who backed the strategy |
- ⚠ Father in world = Borlaug; father in India = Swaminathan
## 2. Origin and inputs
- Start: Began 1965 as the New Agricultural Strategy (dated 1968 in current notes); mid-1960s surge in foodgrain output
- Inputs: HYV seeds + chemical fertilisers (esp. nitrogenous) + pesticides + assured canal/tube-well irrigation + mechanisation + MSP
- Aim: Self-sufficiency in food grains → India moved from PL-480 wheat imports to self-sufficiency
## 3. Crops benefited and phases
- Crops most benefited: Wheat and rice (APPSC 2025 key: "Wheat and Rice")
- First phase (mid-1960s-1980s) largely wheat; rice gained in later, wider phase
- Pulses, oilseeds, coarse cereals lagged
- Second phase (1980s): Spread east and west; output growth 3.4% vs 2.3% earlier
- Phases (PYQ 2017): Third phase marked by price policy based on MSP and procurement
## 4. Regions
| Region | Effect |
| Punjab, Haryana, western UP (Trans- and Upper Gangetic Plains) | Core states → food-deficit to surplus |
| AP and TN rice deltas | South gained via irrigation |
| MP, Bihar, Odisha, WB, Assam | Eastern belt lagged in phase 1 |
### Green Revolution — limitations and issues
| Factor | Effect |
| Regional imbalance | Rainfed and hilly areas left out |
| Crop imbalance | Pulses, oilseeds, millets neglected |
| Equity | Gains captured by big landowners (farmers with >10 ha) |
| Environment | Groundwater depletion, salinity; locked in rice-wheat monoculture + groundwater over-use in the north-west |
| Fertiliser imbalance | N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1 vs ideal 4:2:1 |
- Remedy: Swaminathan's Evergreen Revolution
- Exam angle: "Green Revolution mainly increased..." (wheat and rice); father in world vs father in India.

### Cropping systems and farming terms → meaning — Cropping systems
## 1. Cropping systems
| System | Meaning |
| Multiple/double | Two or more crops in succession in one year (paddy then wheat) |
| Intercropping | Two or more crops together in definite rows (e.g. tur with jowar or maize) |
| Mixed cropping | Two or more crops sown together, seeds mixed, without row pattern; insures against failure |
| Parallel cropping | Two crops of different growth habit and duration, no competition (e.g. maize + green gram) |
| Relay cropping | Next crop sown into the standing crop before harvest [GK] |
| Ratoon | Crop regrows from stubble; yield falls each cycle |
| Crop rotation | Fixed, planned sequence to keep soil fertile + cut pests; pulse-cereal rotation leaves fixed nitrogen for next crop |
| Dryland farming | Areas with less than 750 mm (under 75 cm) rainfall + no assured irrigation → millets + pulses |
| Subsistence vs commercial | Own use vs for market |
- Rice-wheat: Dominant system of Indo-Gangetic plains → paddy followed by wheat = double cropping
- Punjab-Haryana rotate wheat, paddy, mustard, pulses
- ⚠ Intercropping = in rows; mixed cropping = seeds mixed, no rows
- Rainfed area: About 51% of net sown area is rainfed → yields most millets, pulses, oilseeds

### Cropping seasons
## Seasons
- Zaid — hot summer between rabi and kharif: watermelon, bitter gourd, cucumber (not rabi)
- Kharif: rice, soybean; moong (kharif pulse), cotton (kharif fibre)
- Rabi: gram (rabi pulse), peas, linseed, mustard, lathyrus
- PMFBY from Kharif 2026 — covers wild-animal attacks and paddy inundation
## Cropping systems and regions
- Indo-Gangetic Plains — main system rice–wheat (paddy then wheat; double cropping)
- Parallel cropping — two crops of different habit/duration without competition, e.g. maize + green gram
- Trans-Gangetic Plains (Punjab, Haryana, Delhi) — wheat, rice; highest productivity
- Upper Gangetic Plains — core Green Revolution belt; Lower Gangetic — rice and jute
- ★ Rice — transplanted crop (less seed, more labour; weed control in puddled fields)
## Millets
- Major millets: bajra, jowar, ragi (finger millet)
- Minor millets: foxtail, little, kodo (kodon), proso

### Crops: conditions & states
## Crop types
- ★ Stimulant crops: tea, coffee, tobacco, opium poppy, datura, hemp, betel nut

## Growing conditions
- ★ Sugarcane: 21–27°C, 75–100 cm rain; frost during ripening lowers sucrose
- Wheat — rabi; cool growing season, 50–75 cm rain, warm sunny ripening; not the hot zaid summer
- Rice — ~25°C, rain above 100 cm, high humidity; clayey/loamy deltaic and coastal alluvium
- Bajra — hot, dry conditions, sandy soils; led by Rajasthan
## Leading States
- Wheat production order: UP > MP > Punjab > Haryana; chief wheat States UP, Punjab, Haryana, MP
- Rice Bowl of India: West Bengal — largest rice producer; also UP, Punjab, AP
- Maize: Karnataka and Madhya Pradesh; rising in AP's Godavari–Krishna districts
- Ragi — Karnataka (peninsular India); jowar — Maharashtra and Karnataka
## Policy
- First phase of Green Revolution (mid-1960s–1980s) focused on wheat; rice in later phase
- MSP and free power favour rice–wheat, including paddy in Punjab

## Green Revolution
- ★ Started 1966-67; first stage: Haryana, Punjab, western UP
- ★ Greatest impact — wheat (production and productivity), then rice
- New Agricultural Strategy — 1965; HYV seeds, fertilisers, irrigation, MSP; aim foodgrain self-sufficiency
- Third phase of agricultural development — price policy on MSP and procurement
- Limitations: unequal gains (big landowners benefited most), groundwater depletion, salinity, crop and regional imbalance
- Core Green Revolution belt: Upper Gangetic Plains (esp. western UP)
## Missions
- BGREI — 2010-11 under RKVY; rice-based cropping system of eastern States
- NFSM — 2007-08 (rice, wheat, pulses); nutri-cereals added 2018-19
- Renamed NFSNM in 2024-25
## Millets and dryland
- Bajra (pearl millet) — ~60% of India's millet output; India 42.75% of world millets
- Dryland farming — rainfall below 750 mm, no assured irrigation

### Cropping terms
- Rice — India's first-ranking cereal; staple of over half the population
- Millets — mostly kharif; climate-resilient, low-input, dryland/rainfed
- Intercropping — two or more crops in definite rows (tur with jowar or maize)
- Relay cropping — next crop sown into the standing crop
- Ratoon — regrowth from stubble; mixed cropping — crops together without row pattern
- Cropping intensity 2021-22: 155.4% (AP: 1.21 in 2023-24, 1.88 in 2024-25)
- Seventh Plan: 15 agro-climatic zones (soil, climate, cropping pattern), 72 sub-zones
- Dalhan Aatmanirbharta Mission (Oct 2025) — 100% MSP procurement of tur, urad, masoor for 4 years
