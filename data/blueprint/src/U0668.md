### Sectors: nature to decisions
- Services lead in GVA, agriculture in jobs
- ▲ Primary — Agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining (direct use of nature)
- ▸ Secondary — Manufacturing, e.g. cement
- ▸ Tertiary — Transport and other services
- ▸ Quaternary — R&D, IT, education, consulting (knowledge)
- ▸ Quinary — Top decision-making: government, top executives (gold collar)

### Sectors — definitions, types and activity → sector
## 1. Activities → sector
| Sector | Activities |
| Primary | Agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining and quarrying (direct use of nature) |
| Secondary | Cement manufacturing |
| Tertiary | Transport is tertiary (APPSC-GS 2018 key) |
| Quaternary | Research and development, IT, education, consulting (knowledge) |
| Quinary | Top decision-making: government, top executives ("gold collar") |
- Mining trap:
- ⚠ Primary by activity, but GVA reporting puts it in broad Industry sector (AP Survey)
- Weight in revised IIP = 11.05% (4 Jun 2026)
## 2. Primary activity types
| Type | Meaning / examples |
| Oldest primary activities | Hunting and gathering |
| Extractive industries | Take what nature provides → mining, capture fishing, hunting, lumbering |
| Genetic industries | Breed plants and animals → agriculture, animal husbandry, fish farming (UPPSC 2016 key: Agriculture) |
## 3. Organised vs unorganised; public vs private
| Organised | Unorganised |
| Registered; regular terms; labour laws apply (PF, minimum wages, leave) | Small scattered units outside regulation; irregular pay; no security |
- Public vs private: A different split, based on ownership
### Collar-colour terms → workers
| Term | Workers |
| Red collar | Primary-sector (outdoor) workers |
| Blue collar | Manual, factory and construction workers |
| White collar | Office and professional service workers |
| Pink collar | Service jobs long held mostly by women: nursing, teaching, clerical |
| Green collar | Environment and renewable-energy jobs |
| Gold collar | Quinary: top decision-makers and experts |
- Red and pink collar: Meanings from general knowledge
- Blue-collar migration (2025): UAE overtook Saudi Arabia as top destination for Indian blue-collar workers
### Structural transformation — Fisher-Clark thesis and global patterns
## 1. Persons → theory
| Person | Contribution |
| Allan Fisher (1935) + Colin Clark (1940) | Fisher-Clark thesis: as income rises, labour and output move primary → secondary → tertiary |
## 2. Global pattern
- Developed countries: Services ≈ 70-80% of GDP (World Bank); quaternary + quinary sectors growing
- Developing countries: Primary sector still large in employment; services growing faster than industry
## 3. India and AP - issues
| Factor | Effect |
| India's deviation: "premature deindustrialisation" | Skipped mass factory-job stage; only 2.9% of global manufacturing value added |
| AP services-led; manufacturing only about 11% of GSDP | "missing middle" → Industrial Policy 4.0 (2024-29) meant to fill this gap |
- Counterpoint: Organised manufacturing jobs rose 6% in FY24 (about 10 lakh) (Economic Survey 2025-26)
### Sectoral shares and employment data — India and AP
| Economy | Primary / Agri | Secondary / Industry | Tertiary / Services |
| India GVA 2024-25 (new base 2022-23) | 21.4% | 25.8% | 52.9% |
| AP GVA 2025-26 (current) | 33% | 24% | 43% |
| AP employment (PLFS 2023-24) | 45.6% | 22.8% | 31.6% |
- Agriculture employment: About 46.1% of India's workers (Aug 2026)
- Pattern (India + AP): Services lead in GVA, agriculture in jobs
- New GDP series (Feb 2026):
- GDP levels 3-4% lower
- Manufacturing = 14.7% of economy
- FY26 growth = 7.6%
- e-Shram: 31 crore+ registered unorganised workers, 54% of them women (Jan 2026)
- AP informal employment (AP Survey 2025-26): 62.3% rural vs 45.6% urban
- ⚠ 45.6% = AP agriculture employment share and also AP urban informal employment
### Universal angles
- Current relevance:

- AP: services-led; manufacturing ~11% of GSDP; Industrial Policy 4.0 (2024-29) targets the manufacturing "missing middle"
- New GDP series (Feb 2026): base year 2022–23; GDP levels 3–4% lower; manufacturing 14.7%; FY26 growth 7.6%
- Revised IIP (June 2026) added water supply and waste management sector
