### Lost-wax (cire perdue)
- Naturalistic bronze and stone sculpture → advanced metallurgy + craft specialisation
- 1. Wax model coated with clay ↓
- 2. Heated: wax melts out ↓
- 3. Molten metal poured into hollow mould ↓
- 4. Clay removed → Dancing Girl

### IVC craft techniques — terms, processes and FIRSTs
| Term / technique | Meaning |
| Lost-wax (cire perdue) | Wax model coated with clay → heated, wax melts out → molten metal poured into hollow mould → clay removed |
| Glazed pottery | Faience/mosaic glass - described as world's first |
| Faience | Beads, bangles, glazed ware; faience/mosaic-glass link = early clue to glass-making |
| Perforated jars | Large hole at base + small holes over wall → probably for straining liquor |
| New techniques | Seal carving + carnelian working listed together as new techniques of the period |
- Lost-wax objects: Dancing Girl; buffalo and goat figures; copper dog and bird of Lothal; bronze bull of Kalibangan (see metal table)
- Continuity: Metal casting continued into Late Harappan and Chalcolithic cultures (e.g. Daimabad bronzes)
- Pottery make: Wheel-made
- ⚠ Trap: "World's first glazed ware" = faience/mosaic glass, not painted red ware
### Pottery and seals — colour, motifs, material
## 1. Pottery
- Dominant colour: Red ware with black painted designs (red-and-black) - typical (BPSC 1995: red)
- Varieties: plain red ware (common) + painted red-and-black
- Motifs: Geometric, floral, pipal leaf, fish-scale
- Sites: Kalibangan = six types of pottery; Lothal = painted jar
## 2. Seal carving as art
- Material: Steatite seals
- Motifs: Unicorn (most common), bull, elephant, tiger, rhinoceros; Pashupati figure; short script lines
