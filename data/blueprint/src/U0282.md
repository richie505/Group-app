- FRs are qualified, not absolute - and they limit Parliament itself (Art. 13)

- Justiciable: SC (Art. 32), HCs (Art. 226)

### Art. 12 & Art. 13

### Fundamental Rights — basics, six groups and number game
## 1. Basics
- Location: Part III — Articles 12 to 35 (24 Articles); justiciable (2026)
- Borrowed from: Mainly the Bill of Rights of the USA (US Bill of Rights)
- Number: Originally seven; presently six (PYQ 2018)
- Right to Property removed by the 44th Amendment (1978) — Article 19(1)(f) deleted; now a legal right under Article 300-A (Part XII); Right to property (Art. 31) removed
- Available against the State — Article 12 defines the State
- Justiciable: Enforceable by courts — the judiciary (Supreme Court and High Courts) is the guardian of Fundamental Rights
- Exam angle: "How many FRs do citizens have?" → Six (APPSC 2018).
| Group | Articles |
| Right to equality | 14-18 |
| Right to freedom | 19-22 |
| Right against exploitation | 23-24 |
| Right to freedom of religion | 25-28 |
| Cultural and educational rights | 29-30 |
| Right to constitutional remedies | 32 |
## 2. Descriptions - who said
- "Magna Carta of India": Fundamental Rights (Part III)

### Features of Part III → justiciable, qualified, negative vs positive
## 1. Justiciable and qualified
- Justiciable: Aggrieved person can move the SC directly (Art. 32); HCs under Art. 226 enforce FRs + other legal rights
- Not absolute: FRs are qualified, not absolute
- Restrictions imposed by law, only on grounds the Constitution names
- Courts judge reasonableness (judicial review, Art. 13, 32, 226)
- Art. 19 lists 11 restriction grounds in all (2026)
- ⚠ Trap: "FRs are absolute" is wrong (PYQ 2019); APPSC GS 2019: "FRs are absolute" and "all FRs are suspended in an emergency" are both false (answer: Neither)
## 2. Nature of rights → Articles
| Feature | Articles |
| Negative rights (limits on the State) | Arts. 14, 15(1), 16(2), 18(1), 20, 22(1), 28(1) |
| Positive rights: confer privileges | e.g. Arts. 21A, 29, 30 |
| Absolute limits on legislative power | Arts. 15, 17, 18, 20, 24 |
| Against whom: mostly against the State action; some also bind private persons | Arts. 15(2), 17, 23, 24 |
- Natural and legal: Both natural and legal; protect individuals and minorities (PYQ 2023: both correct)
### Articles → rights for citizens only vs all persons
## 1. Citizens only vs everyone
- Only citizens (five rights): Articles 15, 16, 19, 29 and 30
- Citizens and foreigners: The remaining rights — Articles 14, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27 and 28
- All persons (citizens + foreigners) also includes 21A [GK for 21A, 22, 24]
## 2. Aliens, enemy aliens and companies
- Denied to aliens: Arts. 29-30, 19, 16 (PYQ G1 2022; APPSC G1 2022 key: I, II and IV)
- ⚠ Trap: Not Arts. 23-24
- Enemy aliens: Lose even the Art. 22 safeguards (Art. 22(3))
- Legal persons (companies): Can claim the all-persons rights, but not Art. 19
### Articles 12 and 13 → meaning of State and law
## 1. Article 12 - "State"
- "State":
- Government and Parliament of India
- Government and legislature of each state
- All local authorities
- Other authorities in India or under the control of the Government of India
- Private bodies: Writs do not ordinarily lie against them, unless the State acts in collusion
## 2. Article 13 - clauses
| Clause | Provision |
| 13(1) | Pre-constitution laws (in force before the commencement of the Constitution) inconsistent with FRs are void to that extent |
| 13(2) | State shall not make a law abridging FRs; post-constitution laws inconsistent are void to that extent |
| 13(3) | "Law" includes ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage |
- ⚠ Trap: APP wrongly says "before Independence"
- Significance: Base of judicial review; makes FRs supreme over ordinary law (UPSC p.364)
### Cases and amendments on Articles 12 and 13 → holdings
| Case / amendment | Article | Holding / test |
| Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal (1967) | 12 | Statutory bodies are "other authorities" |
| R.D. Shetty v International Airport Authority (1979) | 12 | Laid down the tests for an instrumentality or agency of the State |
| Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib (1981) | 12 | A society running a Regional Engineering College is "State"; test is how it was created and controlled, not the form; instrumentality/agency tests - govt share capital, financial aid, deep and pervasive control, monopoly, public function |
| Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002) | 12 | Body is 'State' if financially, functionally and administratively dominated by govt |
| Golaknath (1967) | 13 | FRs have a transcendental position - Parliament cannot abridge them |
| 24th Amendment (1971) | 13 | Added Art. 13(4): Art. 13 does not apply to Art. 368 amendments |
- ⚠ 1967 twice: Rajasthan SEB (Art. 12) and Golaknath (Art. 13)
### Doctrines → meaning and case
## 1. Doctrines of FR interpretation (eclipse, severability, waiver)
| Doctrine | Meaning / core idea | Case |
| Severability | Law void only to the extent of inconsistency; only the inconsistent part is void (invalid part struck) - valid part / rest survives if separable; "Void only to the extent of inconsistency, rest valid" = severability (PYQ 2018; APPSC GS 2018) | A.K. Gopalan (1950); R.M.D.C. (1957) - R.M.D.C. v. Union of India (1957); some notes cite Motor General Traders v State of AP (1984) |
| Eclipse | Inconsistent pre-constitutional law (law violating FRs) is dormant, not dead; revives when the conflict is removed; mainly for pre-constitution laws - Ambica Mills (1974) extended it to post-constitution laws against non-citizens | Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v State of MP (1955) - revived after the 1st Amendment; State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills (1974) - post-Constitution law stays valid for non-citizens (some notes give Ambica Mills as the eclipse case) |
| Waiver | A person cannot waive a Fundamental Right; only statutory or contractual rights can be given up | Basheshar Nath v CIT (1959) |
| Prospective overruling | Ruling operates only for the future | Golaknath (1967) |
| Progressive interpretation | Constitution read as a living document for present needs | Maneka Gandhi (1978) widened Art. 21; e.g. trauma care under Art. 21, SaveLIFE Foundation v. UoI (2026) |
| Absolute liability | Hazardous industry liable without exceptions of strict liability | M.C. Mehta (1987) - Oleum gas leak |
| Territorial nexus | State law valid outside State if sufficient connection | State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C. (1957) |
- ⚠ Severability = only the inconsistent part void; Eclipse = dormant, revives; Waiver = an FR cannot be given up
## 2. Federal interpretation doctrines
- Federal doctrines: Pith and substance (variant: aspect doctrine), colourable legislation, territorial nexus, incidental and ancillary powers, repugnancy
- Exam angle: doctrine-to-case matching; pith and substance is the doctrine for Union-State List encroachment.
### Articles 33-35 and 359 → limiting and suspending FRs
## 1. Limiting FRs
- Method: Only by a constitutional amendment, not ordinary law; subject to the basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati 1973)
| Article | Content / provision |
| 33 | Parliament only may restrict FRs of armed forces, paramilitary, police, intelligence bodies/staff, their telecom staff (even non-combatants like barbers, cooks) |
| 34 | Restrictions on FRs while martial law is in force; Parliament may indemnify officials (pass an indemnity Act); "martial law" is not defined |
| 35 | Only Parliament alone (not states) legislates to enforce certain FRs - 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34 - and punishments for Part III offences - for uniformity |
| 359 | President suspends enforcement of named FRs by order; never Art. 20 and 21 (44th Amendment, 1978) |
## 2. Suspension of Article 32 - emergency, Article 359
- Art. 359: During a National Emergency, the President may by order suspend the right to move any court for enforcement of specified FRs
- Exception: except Arts 20 and 21

### Universal angles
- Current relevance:
- Opposition demand to make the right to vote a Fundamental Right (Jul 2026)
- Now statutory (Sec. 62 RPA 1951) + constitutional under Art. 326
- 75 years of the 1st Amendment (1951) - added Art. 31A, 31B and the Ninth Schedule
- Significance:
- FRs are the bulwark of liberty and the rule of law; check government power + protect minorities
- FRs limit Parliament itself (Art. 13)
- Comparative note: Britain has no codified FRs; rights rest on conventions and the rule of law
- Criticism & debates: Many restrictions; preventive detention; no social-economic rights; Ninth Schedule protection (limited by I.R. Coelho, 2007)
# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:8
### Basics of Part III
## Where and what
- ★ Fundamental Rights are granted by the Constitution
- Called the 'Magna Carta of India'; in Part III, Articles 12 to 35
- Art. 12 defines State; Art. 13 cancels conflicting laws; Arts. 33-35 limits and legislation
- Part IV (Arts. 36-51) = Directive Principles; Part IVA (Art. 51A) = Fundamental Duties
- FRs work against the State; Article 12 says which bodies count as 'State'
- Britain has no written FRs; protection rests on conventions and the rule of law
## Number of groups and property
- First seven groups; now six
- 44th Amendment (1978) removed property from Part III
- Property now protected under Article 300A (Part XII), a constitutional/legal right
## Who gets them
- Citizens only: Articles 15, 16, 19, 29 and 30
- Citizens and foreigners: Articles 14, 20-28
## Interpretation
- Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978) widened Article 21 (forward-looking reading)
### Six groups
| Group | Articles |
| Right to Equality | 14-18 |
| Right against Exploitation | 23-24 |
| Freedom of Religion | 25-28 |
| Cultural and Educational Rights | 29-30 |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | Article 32 alone (not 30-32) |
- Positive rights (educational/cultural privileges): Articles 21A, 29 and 30; other sets mainly limit State action (negative)
- Article 21 available to all persons; 19, 16, 29 only to citizens
- FRs can be changed only by a constitutional amendment, subject to the basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973); an ordinary law cannot
### Art. 12 & Art. 13
## Nature and model
- Based mainly on the US Bill of Rights (not the Irish Constitution)
- FRs are qualified, subject to reasonable restrictions; courts check reasonableness
## Article 12: State
- 'State' includes all local authorities
- Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib (1981): society running a Regional Engineering College = 'State'; test is creation and control, not its legal form
## Article 13
- ★ Article 13: pre- and post-constitutional laws that clash with Part III are void to the extent of inconsistency (Constitution ranks above laws)
- 13(1): laws in force before the commencement of the Constitution (not "before Independence")
- 13(2): State cannot make laws cutting down FRs; such post-constitution laws void to that extent
- 13(3) 'law' includes ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having force of law
- 13(4), added by the 24th Amendment: amendments under Article 368 are not 'law' under Art. 13
## Other provisions tested here
- Art. 22(3) leaves enemy aliens out of its safeguards; Arts. 23 and 24 not denied to aliens
- Art. 33: power to limit FRs of armed forces is with Parliament alone; may cover non-combatants (cooks, barbers)
- Art. 39(d): equal pay for equal work
- Keeping Dalits from a public well → Art. 15(2); from a public shop → Art. 17; both apply against private persons
- Art. 19(4): restrictions on association only on the constitutionally permitted grounds
- I.R. Coelho (2007) limited Ninth Schedule protection; Schedule came with the 1st Amendment (1951)
### Art. 12 cases
- R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority (1979): tests for an instrumentality or agency of the State
- Rajasthan SEB (1967): statutory bodies are 'other authorities'
### Doctrines → case
- Golaknath (1967): FRs have a transcendental position; Parliament cannot reduce them
- 24th Amendment (1971) replied by adding Art. 13(4)
- Kesavananda Bharati (1973) came after: basic structure
| Doctrine | Meaning / case |
| Eclipse | inconsistent law stays dormant and may revive; State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills extended it to post-Constitution laws affecting non-citizens |
| Severability | only the inconsistent portion is void; A.K. Gopalan, R.M.D.C., Motor General Traders |
| Absolute liability | hazardous industries, no strict-liability exceptions; M.C. Mehta, Oleum gas leak (1987) |
| Pith and substance | Union-State List encroachment: looks at true nature and character of the law |
### Limiting FRs
- Article 33: limits on FRs of named forces
- Article 34: restrictions during martial law and indemnity by Parliament
- 44th Amendment (1978): enforcement of Arts. 20 and 21 can never be suspended under Art. 359
- Arts. 14, 19, 22 and the right to move courts under Art. 32 for other rights can be affected
### FR or not?
- Right to employment: not an FR; Directive Principle under Article 41
- Article 21A (education, ages six to fourteen): 86th Amendment, 2002; carried out by RTE Act, 2009
- Right to vote: statutory right under Section 62, RP Act 1951; constitutional basis Article 326; not an FR
