### Plan → focus sector, FIRSTs and programmes introduced
## 1. First and Second Plans
- First Five Year Plan: Started — began — on 1 April 1951; period 1951–56 — the first planned-development model was adopted on 1 April 1951
- Based on the Harrod–Domar Model; prioritized agriculture — the primary sector
- Major focus: Food production, rehabilitation and price stability
- Led to the Community Development Programme (1952)
- Second Five Year Plan: The first major industrial-oriented plan — based on the Mahalanobis Model
- ⚠ The First Plan emphasized agriculture, whereas the Second emphasized heavy industry
## 2. Focus sector in later plans
- First Plan: also Agriculture and irrigation - about a third of spending to agriculture (Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud)
- Annual Plans 1966-69: Agriculture + industry equal weight (Green Revolution)
- Sixth Plan: Power became significant; first plan to focus on gender issues and inter/intra-State inequalities
- Seventh Plan + 1990-92 Annual Plans: Employment thrust
- Eighth Plan: Human development core; moved from heavy industry to infrastructure
- Tenth Plan: Agriculture the prime moving force
- Eleventh Plan: Social sector first; education on top

### Second Plan — outlay data and criticism of the Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy
## 1. Second Plan outlay
- Planned public outlay: Rs 4,800 crore (private Rs 3,100 crore)
- Actual outlay: Rs 4,600 crore = Rs 3,650 crore investment + Rs 950 crore current spending
- Why pruned: Shortfalls from closed economy assumption + food and capital shortages + inflation + 1957-58 foreign-exchange crisis
## 2. Criticism of the Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy
| Factor | Effect |
| Agriculture | Neglected agriculture → food shortages |
| Model assumptions | Closed-economy assumption |
| Employment | Capital-intensive heavy industry → little employment |
| Prices | High inflation by late 1950s |

### Achievements vs failures of planning
## 1. Achievements
- Average growth (1st-11th Plans): ~4.5% vs ~1% pre-Independence
- Agriculture 2-3% (vs 0.3%); industry 6-8%
- Industrial base: Steel plants (Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela), Chittaranjan, Perambur, IITs, Atomic Energy Commission
- Food self-sufficiency: Green Revolution (1966-67), FCI (1965), National Seeds Corporation
- Institutions: SBI (from Imperial Bank, First Plan), NABARD (1982)
- Poverty: Fell 48.3% (1977-78) to 37.4% (1983-84)
## 2. Broad failures
| Failure | Detail |
| Poverty | Persistent poverty and inequality; under the Eighth Plan "the poor became poorer" despite the safety net |
| Unemployment | Employment growth lagged targets |
| Regional imbalance | Across and within States (a Twelfth Plan objective to correct) |
| Industry | Growth below target in several plans (e.g., Sixth) |
| Centralisation | 1960s planning diluted people's participation |
## 3. Implementation failures in later plans
- Eighth Plan:
- Public sector share of outlay 34.3% vs target 45.2%
- Employment growth 2% vs 2.6%
- 10th Plan: Model projection 8.1% unmet (actual 6.2-8.6% in plan years)
- Duplication: Overlapping rural electrification schemes
- Poor budgeting: 11th Plan 9% target hit by infrastructure fund shortfall
- 12th Plan: IMR 25 target missed for lack of funds

## 3. Plan finance - central assistance and formula
- Plan finance: State's own resources (balance from current revenue, public sector surpluses, borrowings) + Normal Central Assistance
- Allocation formula:
- Gadgil formula (1969) → Gadgil-Mukherjee formula (1991)
- Population 60%, per capita income 25%, fiscal performance 7.5%, special problems 7.5%
- State grouping:
- Special Category = 90% grant : 10% loan
- General Category = 30% grant : 70% loan → AP was General Category
- ⚠ Gadgil 1969; Gadgil-Mukherjee 1991
