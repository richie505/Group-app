### IT Act, 2000 — purpose, concepts and framework
## 1. Purpose and concepts
- Purpose: IT Act, 2000 gives legal recognition to transactions by electronic data interchange and other electronic communication ("electronic commerce") (APPSC 2018 PYQ)
- Also legal recognition of electronic records and e-commerce transactions + prevention of computer-based crimes
- Concepts introduced: electronic record, digital signature, certifying authority - "all of the above" (MPPSC 2021 PYQ)
- Status: primary law on cyber crime and e-commerce in India
- Amended: 2008
## 2. Bodies and framework
- Statutory bodies: Controller of Certifying Authorities (statutory regulator of electronic signatures) + CERT-In
- Framework:
- Defines cyber contraventions and crimes with an adjudication framework
- Security of sensitive personal data
- due diligence by intermediaries
- A special Act that overrides IPC provisions where the crime is squarely covered

### Digital signature and PKI — terms → meaning
## 1. Definitions (PYQs)
| Term | Meaning |
| Public Key Infrastructure | Used in the context of digital security infrastructure (UPSC 2020 PYQ); key pairs: private key signs, public key verifies |
| Digital signature (UPSC 2019 key) | Electronic method of signing an electronic document that ensures the original content is unchanged (statement 3 only) |
| Digital certificate | A record identifying the issuing certifying authority + an ID proof for server access |
- ⚠ Trap: A record identifying the issuing certifying authority + an ID proof for server access describe a digital certificate, not the signature (PYQ)
- Functions: Authentication, integrity, non-repudiation
## 2. Legal framework
- Technology: Uses an asymmetric crypto system and hash function
- Issuers: Certifying Authorities licensed by the CCA issue Digital Signature Certificates
- 2008 amendment: Made it technology-neutral as "electronic signature"
- Legal validity: Electronic records and signatures legally recognised (Secs. 4-5)
### CERT-In — status, functions and 2022 directions
## 1. Status and functions
- Origin: Operational since January 2004 under MeitY
- Role: National Incident Response Centre
- Statutory backing: Sec. 70B (inserted 2008)
- Functions:
- Collect/analyse/disseminate incident information
- Platforms & roles:
- Operates Cyber Swachhta Kendra = automated threat-intelligence exchange platform
- CVE Numbering Authority for Indian products
- Empanelled 200 IT security auditing organisations
- NCCC (National Cyber Coordination Centre): Situational awareness of threats
## 2. CERT-In Directions 2022 (Sec 70B)
- Date: CERT-In directions, 28 Apr 2022
- Who must report: Service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates (corporate entities), government bodies, virtual asset service providers
| Obligation | Deadline / period |
| Report cyber incidents | within 6 hours |
| ICT logs kept | 180 days |
| VPN providers keep subscriber data | 5 years |
