# NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:4 - Pre-Harappan and Early Harappan cultures - Mehrgarh, Amri, Kot Diji, Kalibangan-I
### Mehrgarh periods
- Mehrgarh + Early Harappan phase → civilisation grew locally, not from Mesopotamia
- c. 7000 BCE — Period I (aceramic): barley, wheat; sheep, goat, cattle; mud-brick houses
- Period II — Pottery begins; cotton
- Period III+ — Copper working, wheel-made painted pottery, beads
### Mehrgarh
- Baluchistan, near Bolan Pass; excavated by J.-F. Jarrige (1974)
- Earliest agriculture, domestication, pottery; earliest settled life (UPPSC 2008)
- Remains Stone Age to Harappan (UPPSC 2009)
- Burials under houses, flexed, with grave goods (sometimes goats)
### Early Harappan cultures

### Phase & pottery
- Early Harappan: 3300-2600 BCE (⚠ older notes: 2900-2500 or 3200-2600 BCE)
- Called Regionalisation Era; = Hakra phase
- Regional wares: Amri, Kot Diji, Sothi-Siswal, Hakra → Mature pottery uniform
- Early Harappan used the potter's wheel
- Banawali: Pre- + Mature; Mitathal: Pre-, Mature + Late

### Early Harappan cultures → type site and pottery
## 1. Phase and cultures
- Early/Pre-Harappan phase: 3300-2600 BCE
- Older notes: 2900-2500 BCE or 3200-2600 BCE
- Names: Regionalisation Era; corresponds to the Hakra phase
| Culture | Type site / area |
| Amri | Amri (Sindh): transitional pre- to Mature Harappan; rhinoceros remains; later Jhangar traces |
| Kot Diji | Kot Diji (Sindh): fortified pre-Harappan settlement, destroyed by fire; many stone implements |
| Sothi-Siswal | Sothi, Siswal (Rajasthan-Haryana) |
| Hakra | Ghaggar-Hakra (Cholistan) |
| Damb Sadaat | Quetta |
- Other Early Harappan sites: Padri (Gujarat), Kalibangan, Dholavira, Harappa, Balakot, Bhirrana, Gumla
- Banawali: Pre- + Mature Harappan
- Mitathal: Pre-, Mature + Late phases
## 2. Pottery traditions
- Regional traditions: Amri ware, Kot Diji ware, Sothi-Siswal ware, Hakra ware
- Mature Harappan pottery became uniform
- Technique: Early pots hand-made → later wheel-made; Early Harappan used the potter's wheel
### Kalibangan-I → features
- Pre-Harappan level: Earliest ploughed field, criss-cross furrows → mixed cropping
- Settlement: Enclosed by a mud-brick fortification
- Pottery: Six fabrics ("6 types of pottery")
- Excavation: 1961-69 by B. B. Lal and B. K. Thapar
- ⚠ Ploughed field = pre-Harappan level; fire altars = Mature phase

### Early to Mature Harappan transition → features and debates
- Continuity: Kalibangan + Kot Diji pottery and buildings anticipate Mature Harappan → urbanisation gradual and internal
- Early-phase features:
- Copper, the wheel, the plough; granaries + defence walls
- Open problem: The gap between Neolithic Mehrgarh and the urban phase
### Universal angles

- Current relevance: Haryana protected Tighrana and Mitathal (Bhiwani)

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 1:4
### Mehrgarh periods
- Main tamed animals (Neolithic Baluchistan/Indus) — sheep and goats
- Period I — aceramic Neolithic, about 7000 BCE, mud-brick houses
- Period II — pottery begins; Period III onward — copper and wheel-made painted pottery
### Mehrgarh
- Mehrgarh (Baluchistan) — near the Bolan Pass; from c. 7000 BCE; first farming in the subcontinent
- Excavated 1974 by J.-F. Jarrige
- Burials — under or near houses, often flexed, with ornaments, tools, sometimes goats
- Kili Gul Muhammad — Quetta valley; Rana Ghundai — Zhob-Loralai valley
### Early Harappan cultures
- Damb Sadaat (Quetta valley) — Early Harappan-phase village
- Hakra — Ghaggar-Hakra region of Cholistan (not Gujarat)
- Amri and Kot Diji — Sindh; Sothi-Siswal — Rajasthan-Haryana
- Kot Diji — fortified settlement destroyed by fire; many stone implements
- Amri — rhinoceros remains; shift pre- to Mature Harappan
