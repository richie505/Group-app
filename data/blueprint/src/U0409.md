### Cases → rulings
## 1. Indra Sawhney
- Caste criterion: Caste can be the main criterion of backwardness; overruled Balaji on this
- Ceiling: Total reservation not above 50% except in extraordinary cases
- Applied year by year; carry-forward allowed within 50%
- Creamy layer: Excluded from OBCs (Origin: introduced by Indra Sawhney; excludes socially advanced OBCs)
- Promotions: Reservation confined to initial appointment, not promotion → answered by the 77th
- Permanent body: Directed a permanent statutory body for OBC lists → became the NCBC

### Creamy layer and EWS — criteria and income limits
## 1. Creamy layer - status-based exclusions
- Status-based exclusions: Children of constitutional post-holders, Class-I officers, parents both Class-II, senior government and armed-forces/military officers, property owners/large landowners, high earners
## 2. Creamy layer - income limit
- Revisions: ₹1 lakh (1993) → 2.5 lakh (2004) → 4.5 lakh (2008) → 6 lakh (2013) → ₹8 lakh (2017) (₹8 lakh a year), still current
## 3. EWS - extent and criteria
- Extent: 10% reservation (up to 10%) for economically weaker sections not covered by SC/ST/OBC quotas
- Over and above / in addition to existing reservation
- In jobs and education; central institutions (incl. private) and posts
- States: Enabling, not mandatory
- Criteria: Family income below ₹8 lakh a year + asset limits (5 acres farm land, 1,000 sq ft flat)
- ⚠ ₹8 lakh = OBC creamy-layer limit (2017) = EWS income ceiling

# MCQ NOTES OF APP TOPIC 2:135
### Backward Classes Commissions
## First Commission (Kalelkar)
- ★ Kaka Saheb Kalelkar — chairman of the First (All India) Backward Classes Commission
- Appointed by the President on 29 January 1953; opened 18 March 1953
- Appointed 1953, report submitted 1955; report not implemented
- First commission to use social and educational criteria to identify backward classes
- Recommended a caste-based census and reservations
## Second Commission (Mandal)
- Article 340 — President appoints a commission on conditions of SEBCs (Mandal appointed under it)
- B.P. Mandal chaired the Second Commission (1979), set up by the Janata Government of Morarji Desai
- Dealt with reservation in government employment; recommended 27% for OBCs (11 indicators)
- Identified 3,743 backward castes
- Implemented by the V.P. Singh Government in 1990; 27% of Central civil posts reserved for OBCs in 1993
## Later bodies
- National Commission for Backward Classes — permanent statutory body directed in Indra Sawhney for backward-class lists
- NCBC examines requests for inclusion in or exclusion from backward-class lists and advises
- M. Veerappa Moily Oversight Committee (2006) — OBC reservation through expansion of seats, so general-category seats did not shrink
- Justice G. Rohini Commission (2017) — sub-categorisation of OBCs in the Central List, for fair sharing of benefits to under-represented groups
- Finding: about 97% of benefits taken by 25% of sub-castes; about 1,000 communities had zero representation
- Proposal: divide Central-list OBCs into three or four sub-groups
- R.N. Prasad — framed creamy-layer criteria; Ranganath Misra — 15% for minorities; Bhuria — basis for PESA

### Indra Sawhney
- Backward classes under Article 16(4) — identified mainly by caste and social backwardness, not economic criteria alone
- Reservation limited to initial appointments, not promotions
- Creamy layer — specified socially advanced categories, e.g. children of Class-I officers, and of parents who are both Class-II officers; not all OBC children
- Sukhdev case (AP High Court) — caste alone could not be the basis; 112 classes listed using poverty, residence and occupation
### Creamy layer income limit
- Creamy-layer criteria — DoPT Office Memorandum, September 1993, based on the Justice R.N. Prasad Expert Committee
- Income ceiling — ₹8 lakh per annum (revised 2017, still current)
- Kapu reservation — Centre never approved the 2017 BC-F proposal
- Mandal Commission set up through the Ministry of Home Affairs (not Social Justice); 3,743 castes; 27% for OBCs
### EWS and key cases
- Union of India v. Rohith Nathan — parental salary alone cannot place a candidate in the creamy layer
- 103rd Amendment — introduced as the 124th Amendment Bill; inserted Articles 15(6) and 16(6)
- 10% EWS reservation, outside SC/ST/OBC quotas — enabling, not mandatory for States
- EWS criteria — family income below ₹8 lakh, agricultural land below 5 acres, flat below 1,000 sq ft
- Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India — 103rd Amendment upheld 3:2; dissent by CJI U.U. Lalit and Justice S. Ravindra Bhat
- AP local bodies — N.T. Rama Rao introduced 20% BC quota in 1984; later reduced from 34% to 24.15% under GOs 176, 559 and 560
### AP BC groups
- CEI (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006 — 27% OBC reservation; came into force in 2007
- BC-E — added by the Andhra Pradesh Act of 2007; earlier lists had only BC-A to BC-D
