### Rule of Law → origin and Dicey's three elements
## 1. Origin
- A.V. Dicey (British jurist) — Introduction to the Study of the Law of the Constitution (1885)
- Borrowed from Britain with: Parliamentary Government, Single Citizenship, Cabinet System, Prerogative Writs
- In India: Anchored mainly in Article 14 — equality before law and equal protection of the laws, for citizens and foreigners
- Basic Structure: Affirmed in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) and Indira Nehru Gandhi (1975) - Rule of law is a basic feature
## 2. Dicey's three elements
- 1. Absence of arbitrary power (limitation of powers): Supremacy of regular law over arbitrary power — no punishment except for a distinct breach of law proved in the ordinary legal manner
- ⚠ "The State has inherent unlimited police powers" — false
- 2. Equality before the law: All classes equally subject to the ordinary law, administered by ordinary courts — no one above the law
- Dicey opposed the French Droit Administratif; ⚠ "executive officials are subject only to special administrative tribunals" — false
- 3. Primacy of individual rights (liberty and civil rights): The Constitution is the result of individual rights defined and enforced by courts — rights come from judicial decisions, not from the Constitution
- UK: Constitution results from judicial decisions; India: the Constitution is the source of rights — India reverses the third premise and adopts the first two intact (India accepts only (i) and (ii) - here rights flow from the Constitution)
- ⚠ "People's responsibility to the Government" is not a pillar — delete it from the framework; the Rule of Law restrains the government and does not expand citizens' obedience
- Exceptions:
- Art. 361: President and Governors not answerable to courts; no criminal case during term; civil case only after 2 months' notice
### Rule of Law vs Rule by Law; equality before law vs equal protection; classification
## 1. Rule of Law vs Rule by Law
| Rule of Law | Rule by Law |
| Government under law — the government itself is subordinate to law | Government above law — law as an instrument (weapon) of state control; the ruler stays above the law |
| Laws must be just, fair and non-arbitrary | Governs citizens only |
## 2. Equality before law vs equal protection of laws
| Equality before law | Equal protection of the laws |
| British origin (Dicey's rule of law) | American origin (US 14th Amendment [GK]) |
| Negative concept — no special privilege for anyone; no one above law; all equally subject to ordinary law | Positive concept — equal treatment in equal circumstances, like treated alike; permits reasonable classification |
- ⚠ British = negative; American = positive
## 3. Classification
- Classification: Art. 14 forbids class legislation, not reasonable classification
- Twin test: Intelligible differentia + rational nexus with the object
- Reiterated Apr 2026: State cannot treat pensioners differently from serving employees on DA (Kerala/KSRTC)

### Rule of Law & classification
## Origin and index
- Rule of Law taken from Britain (A.V. Dicey, 1885), like Cabinet System and Parliamentary Government

- ★ Rule of Law Index: released by the World Justice Project (measuring since 2008; eight areas)

## Dicey's elements
- Three elements: absence of arbitrary power, equality before law, primacy of individual rights
- Not an element: people's responsibility to the Government
- First element: regular law supreme; no punishment except for breach of law; rejects unlimited state power
- India took the first two but reversed the third: rights come from the Constitution
- Rule of Law: government subordinate to law; a ruler above law = 'rule by law'
