### Satavahana kings and queens → titles and contributions
## 1. Nature of kingship
- Ideal: King = upholder of dharma (Dharmashastra ideal); king upheld Dharmashastras
- Nature: Hereditary + patriarchal; decentralised vs the Mauryas
## 2. Ruler → title / contribution
| Ruler / queen | Title / contribution |
| Gautamiputra Satakarni | matronymics (maternal gotra names) from him; titles Ekabrahmana + Varnasankarya nirodhaka (stopper of the mixing of varnas) → stopped varna-sankara (mixing) |
| Kuntala Satakarni (13th king) | Made Sanskrit the royal language; court of Gunadhya and Sarvavarma; Katantra composed for him |

- Hala's court: Honoured poets Kumarila and Sripalita; his reign = golden age of Prakrit
- Hala's marriage: Sinhala princess Lilavati at the Bhimeswara temple, Draksharamam (Saptagodavari)
- Recorded in Lilavati Parinayam (Kutuhala, Prakrit), which also says a Satavahana king patronised Nagarjuna

### Feudatories → titles and grading
- Polity: Semi-feudal → Samanta rajyas (Janapadas) paid annual tribute + sent troops
- Examples: Chutus in Mysore, Kuras in Kolhapur, Ikshvakus in Vijayapuri
| Feudatory title | Feature |

### Gathasaptasati, Brihatkatha and Katantra → features
## 1. Gathasaptasati
- Nature: An anthology of 700 Prakrit gathas (verses), mainly love and romance, rural life and nature (G2 2025)
- Most important literary source for Satavahana society

## 2. Brihatkatha
- Brihatkatha language: Paishachi (not Sanskrit, G2 2025)
- Size: traditionally 7 lakh verses in 7 stories; original lost
- Content: Mentions slavery
- Legend:
- Gunadhya = court poet of Kuntala Satakarni
- Quarrelled with Sarvavarma over the king learning Sanskrit → lost, gave up Sanskrit, wrote in Paishachi
## 3. Katantra
- Katantra — nature: short Sanskrit grammar
- Katantra — patron: Kuntala Satakarni
- Katantra — purpose: So the king could learn Sanskrit quickly
- ⚠ 700 gathas = Gathasaptasati; 7 lakh verses in 7 stories = Brihatkatha
