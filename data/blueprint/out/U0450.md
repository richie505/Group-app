## U0450 | ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT SINCE INDEPENDENCE – PHASES, GROWTH RATES AND THE HINDU RATE OF GROWTH
### 1. HINDU RATE OF GROWTH
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Coined by | **Prof. Raj Krishna** |
| Year | **1978** |
| Variable | Growth of **national income / real GDP** |
| Rate | About **3.5%** a year (3.52%, 1950-1980) – not 5.5% |
| Period | **1950s to early 1980s** (pre-reform planning era) |
| Per capita growth | Only **1.37%** a year – barely above population growth |
- Blamed on inefficient state planning; broken in the **1980s** (5.1-5.5%).
- 2026 echo: a **"Bharat rate of growth"** of 8%+ through AI-led "Reforms 3.0" (Jun 2026).
!TRAP Hindu rate = **stagnant growth**, not faster growth and not inequality (APPSC-G2 2017).
### 2. ECONOMY AT INDEPENDENCE AND THE PLAN ERA
- Main livelihood on the eve of Independence: **agriculture**; growth before Independence about **1%** a year; agricultural growth only **0.3%**.
- Plan era (Plans 1-11): about **4.5%** a year; agriculture 2-3%, industry **6-8%**.
- **Bombay Plan (1944)**: industrialists themselves sought **state protection** – the Licence Raj was partly a capitalist demand.
### 3. POLICY ORIENTATION 1947-1991 – LICENCE REGIME
| Policy / plan | Year | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| First Industrial Policy | 1948 | IFCI set up |
| **IPR 1956** ("Economic Constitution") | 1956 | **Schedule A = 17** industries for the State; **Schedule B = 12**; rest private under licensing → **Licence-Permit-Quota Raj** |
| Third Plan | 1961-66 | **Import substitution** as the strategy |
| 1980s reforms | 1980s | **Pro-business**: broad-banding of licences, MRTP limit raised to **Rs 100 crore**, tax cuts, NRI deposits |
| 1991 reforms | 1991 | Reserved industries **17 → 8**; delicensing |
- Major focus 1947-1991: **import substitution** with a large public sector (APPSC-GS 2023) – not export promotion, deregulation or less public investment.
### 4. LOW GROWTH DESPITE SAVINGS – ICOR
- **ICOR** = change in capital ÷ change in output; **lower ICOR = more efficient** investment.
- **Harrod-Domar** (First Plan): **g = s/v** (savings rate ÷ capital-output ratio); higher capital-output ratio with the same savings → lower growth.
- **Second Plan**: raised investment from **7% to 11%** of national income; **heavy, capital-intensive** industry (not light consumer industry) with long gestation → high ICOR → low growth.
- Now: GFCF about 30% of GDP (2025-26); net household financial savings ~5.3% of GDP.
### 5. PHASES AND AVERAGE GROWTH
| Phase | Average growth |
|---|---|
| 1950-1980 ("Hindu rate") | ~**3.5%** |
| 1980-81 to 1989-90 | **5.13-5.18%** |
| Post-reform (1990s-2010s) | ~**6.5%** (6.8% from 1993-94); peak **8.5% in 2003-08** |
| Post-Covid (2022-23 series) | 7.2% (2023-24), 7.1% (2024-25), **7.7% (2025-26 PE)** |
- RBI outlook: 2026-27 growth to slow to **6.6%** (Jun 2026).
- **Arvind Subramanian** (ex-CEA): 2011-17 growth about **4.5%**, not the reported 7%.
- AP outgrew India in most years since 2014-15: **9.88% vs 7.4%** (2025-26); CM's figures 13.5% (2014-19) → 10.3% (2019-24) → 11.3% (2024-26).
### 6. HIGHEST AND LOWEST GROWTH YEARS
| Year | Growth | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **1979-80** | **-5.2%** | Drought and second oil shock |
| **2020-21** | **-5.8%** | Covid-19 pandemic |
| 2003-08 | 8.5% (peak phase) | Post-reform boom |
!TRAP -5.2% = 1979-80; -5.8% = 2020-21.

### 9. TURNING-POINT DEBATE – 1980s VS 1991
| View | Argument |
|---|---|
| 1980s turn | **Rodrik-Subramanian (2004)**: transition around **1980** from a **pro-business** (not pro-market) shift; **DeLong (2003)**: take-off in the 1980s, before 1991 |
| 1991 turn | 1980s growth was **debt-fuelled** – internal borrowing + short-term ECBs; **fiscal deficit 8.4% (1990)** → 1991 crisis → sustained high growth after reforms |
- Criticism: growth is job-poor – youth unemployment about 10% (Jun 2026).
