### 2. SAYYID (SIR SYED) AHMAD KHAN (1817-1898)
#### 2.1 Life and Aim
- Was in **judicial service** of the Company in 1857; **loyal to the British**; never connected with the Congress.
- Aim: **modernisation of Muslims** through **English education** and a **rational reading of Islam** after 1857.
- Promoted **Urdu** as the unifying language of Muslims.
#### 2.2 Writings and Journal
| Book / journal | Author | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind** (1859) | Sayyid Ahmad Khan | "The Causes of the Indian Revolt" on 1857 |
| **Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq** | Sayyid Ahmad Khan | Journal of his reformist thought |
| **The Indian Musalmans** (1871) | **W.W. Hunter** | On Muslims under British rule |
#### 2.3 Timeline
| Step | Year |

#### 2.4 Political Stand
- Early view: India a bride; Hindus and Muslims **"her two eyes"** – spoke for unity.
- Turned after the **Hindi-Urdu controversy (1867)**; held Hindus and Muslims needed separate political paths.
- Asked Muslims to **stay away from the Congress**; formed the United Indian Patriotic Association (1888).
- Criticism: his later conservatism sowed seeds of **communal division**.
### 3. ALIGARH MOVEMENT
- Background: after 1857 Muslims lagged because they rejected Western education.
- **Initial aim**: spread **Western scientific education** among Indian Muslims; social uplift with cooperation with the British.
- **MAO College**: founded **1875** as **Madarsatul Uloom Musalmanan-i-Hind**; renamed **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College** in **1877**; modelled on **Oxford and Cambridge**.
- **1920**: MAO College became **Aligarh Muslim University (AMU)**.
- Scientific Society translated Western science, history and philosophy into **Urdu**.
- **Principals**: first principal **H.G.I. Siddons**; then **Theodore Beck (1883-99)**, who backed Muslim separatism; then **Theodore Morison**.
- **Later leaders**: **Chiragh Ali, Altaf Husain Hali, Nazir Ahmad, Mohsin-ul-Mulk**.
- Significance: built the **modern educated Muslim middle class**.
!TRAP Theodore Beck was a principal of MAO College, not its founder; the founder was Sir Syed.
### 4. DEOBAND MOVEMENT
- **Darul Uloom, Deoband** (1866), Saharanpur district, then United Provinces, now **Uttar Pradesh**.
- Founders: orthodox ulema **Qasim Nanotavi (1832-80)** and **Rashid Ahmad Gangohi (1828-1905)**.
- Aims: spread pure teachings of the **Koran and Hadis**; keep alive **jihad** against foreign rulers; **train religious leaders**. Not Western education.
- Stance: **welcomed the Congress**; issued a **fatwa (1888)** against Sir Syed's United Indian Patriotic Association and MAO Association.
- **Mahmud-ul-Hasan** gave Deoband political content → **Jamiat-ul-Ulema**.
- **Shibli Numani** backed Deoband but wanted English and European science taught.
- Critics: backed the Congress more to oppose Sir Syed than from a positive creed.
!KEY APPSC-GS 2019: Darul Uloom Deoband is in → **Uttar Pradesh**.
!TRAP Gangohi belongs to **Deoband**, not to the Taiyuni movement.
#### 4.1 Aligarh vs Deoband
| Feature | Aligarh | Deoband |
|---|---|---|
| Learning | Western, scientific; Urdu | Koran and Hadis |
| British | **Loyal** | **Anti-British**; jihad |
| Congress | **Opposed** | **Welcomed**; fatwa 1888 |
| Later leaders | Hali, Mohsin-ul-Mulk | Mahmud-ul-Hasan |
!TRAP 1888 twice: Sir Syed's United Indian Patriotic Association AND Deoband's fatwa against it.
### 5. WAHABI MOVEMENT
- Named after **Abdul Wahab of Arabia**; Indian founder **Syed Ahmad Barelvi**, inspired by **Shah Waliullah**.
- Aim: restore Muslim power; fought the **Sikhs** in Punjab, then the **British**.
- Chief centre: **Patna**.
- British trials: **Ambala (1864)** and **Patna (1865)** → suppressed by 1870.
!KEY APPSC G2 2011: character of the Wahabi movement → **anti-British**.
### 6. FARAIZI MOVEMENT
- Founder **Haji Shariatullah**, East Bengal (Faridpur), 1818.
- Aim: purge **un-Islamic practices**; enforce religious duties (**faraiz**).
- **Dudu (Dadu) Miyan**, his son and successor, made it a **peasant movement** against **zamindars and indigo planters** – leader of the "Farazi rebellion".
- Faraizis later joined the Wahabis.
!KEY APPSC-GS 2019 pair: **Faraizi – Haji Shariatullah**.
### 7. AHMADIYA MOVEMENT
- Founder **Mirza Ghulam Ahmad**, **Qadian**, 1889.
- Claimed to be a **messiah** – rejected by orthodox Muslims.
- Promoted **Western liberal education** among Muslims; **opposed jihad**.
!TRAP Deoband kept jihad alive; Ahmadiya **opposed** jihad.
### 8. MUSLIM REFORM IN HYDERABAD AND ANDHRA
- **Mubariz-ud-Daula**: brother of the **Nizam**, influenced by Barelvi; Wahabi leader who tried to resist British influence in the **Deccan**; **arrested 1839**; died in prison (APPSC G2 2002).
- **Nasaraiah** (Sayyid Muslim, d. 1825): popular among **Madigas**; preached one God, against idol worship; Vinukonda–Narasaraopet (**Guntur**).
- **Osmania University** (1918), Hyderabad: taught in **Urdu**.
### 9. SIKH REFORM MOVEMENTS
| Movement | Leader / feature |
|---|---|
| **Nirankari** | **Baba Dayal Das**; God as Nirankar (formless) |
| **Namdhari (Kuka)** | **Baba (Satguru) Ram Singh**, Bhaini (Ludhiana, Punjab); white clothes, gave up meat |
| **Singh Sabha** | **Amritsar 1873**, Lahore 1879; Gurmukhi/Punjabi literature; **Khalsa College, Amritsar (1892)** |
| **Akali Movement (1920)** | Removed corrupt **mahants** from gurdwaras; later the **Akali Dal** |
- **Ram Singh Kuka**: **first to use non-cooperation and boycott of British goods** (APPSC-GS 2025); exiled to **Rangoon** after the **1872 Malerkotla** clash.
- Namdhari movement – among the **first to resist British rule in Punjab**.
- Akali movement grew out of the Singh Sabha; success = **Sikh Gurdwaras Act, 1925** (gurdwaras under the **SGPC**).
### 10. PARSI REFORM
- **Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha** (Religious Reform Association), **Bombay, 1851**.
- Founders: **Naoroji Furdonji** (first president), **Dadabhai Naoroji**, **S.S. Bengalee**.
- Journal/organ: **Rast Goftar** ("Truth-Teller").
- Aim: modernise Parsi religion and society; women's education.
!PAIR Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha | Parsi | Bombay 1851 || Singh Sabha | Sikh | Amritsar 1873

### 13. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Deoband = UP** (Saharanpur), not Punjab or Delhi.
2. **Qadian** = Ahmadiya; **Patna** = Wahabi centre; **Calcutta** = Literary Society.
3. Educational Conference **1886**, not 1866 (1866 = Deoband).
4. Aligarh **opposed** Congress; Deoband **welcomed** it.
5. Ahmadiya **opposed jihad**; Deoband kept it alive.
6. Wahabi Indian founder is **Syed Ahmad Barelvi**, not Abdul Wahab (Arabia).
7. **Dudu Miyan** turned Faraizi into a peasant movement; founder was his father.
8. **The Indian Musalmans** = W.W. Hunter; **Asbab-e-Baghawat** = Sir Syed.
9. Beck was a principal, not the founder; Siddons was the first principal.
10. Kuka (Ram Singh) – boycott and exile to Rangoon; Akali – Gurdwaras Act 1925.
