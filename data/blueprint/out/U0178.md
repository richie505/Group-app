### 2. FAMINES IN ANDHRA AND MADRAS PRESIDENCY
- Earlier famines: 1805–07 (all districts), 1811 (Nellore), 1823 (Kadapa, Kurnool, Chittoor, Guntur, Vizag, Nellore), 1839.
- **1832–33 Guntur famine** (**Dokkala / Nandana Karuvu**) = **most serious South Indian famine**.
- **Ganjam (1865–66)** and **Kurnool (1866)** – favourite Andhra famine MCQs.
- **1876–78 Great Madras Famine** – Rayalaseema and coastal Andhra.
- **11 famines** in Andhra in the late 19th century.
- **K.S. Gupta** formed a relief committee in the **1945** famine.
### 3. FAMINE COMMISSIONS
| Commission | Year | Viceroy | Trigger / role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Campbell** (**first**) | **1866** | **John Lawrence** | Orissa famine |
| **Richard Strachey** | **1880** | **Lytton** | Set up after the Great Famine 1876–78 → **Famine Code 1883** |
| **Lyall** | 1898 (after 1896–97) | **Elgin II** | Assessed **causes**; recommended measures against future famines |
| **MacDonnell** | **1901** | **Curzon** | **Early relief** and **more irrigation** |
| **Woodhead** (**last**) | **1944** | **Wavell** | Bengal Famine 1943 |
!TRAP The **Hunter Commission** was **not** a famine body (it was on education).
### 4. FAMINE CODE, 1883
- **Indian Famine Code, 1883** – prepared on the recommendations of the **Strachey Commission (1880)**.
- **Provisions:**
  - **Relief works** for the **able-bodied (fit)**.
  - **Gratuitous relief** for the **old and infirm**.
!PAIR Relief works | Able-bodied | Paid work || Gratuitous relief | Old and infirm | Free aid
### 5. FAMINE RELIEF POLICY
- **Company period:** government **never adopted a famine code**; minimal relief.
- **Public works as relief:** employment on roads and canals. The idea goes back to **Kautilya** (seeds, food, tax exemption, public works).
- **Temple wage:** fixed by **Sir Richard Temple** in the **1877 Madras famine** – about **1 lb (pound) of grain a day** on relief works; criticised as a **starvation wage**.
### 6. NATIONALIST CRITIQUE OF FAMINE POLICY
- Nationalists rejected the view that famines came from **monsoon failure alone**: real causes were **rigid revenue collection in drought** and **export of food grains**.
- **Dadabhai Naoroji and R.C. Dutt** linked famines to **high land revenue** and the **drain of wealth**.
- **R.C. Dutt:** ***Open Letters to Lord Curzon*** (**1900**).
### 7. ARTHUR COTTON'S IRRIGATION RESPONSE
- **Arthur Cotton** (born 15 May 1803): the **Guntur famine (1833)** convinced the Company that irrigation was needed.
- **Veenam Veeranna:** Andhra official who **helped Cotton build the Godavari anicut** at Dowleswaram (APPSC G2 2019).
#### 7.1 Godavari Anicut at Dowleswaram
- Report **1844** → sanctioned **1846** → work began **1847** → main structure finished **April 1852**.
- Cost about **Rs 1,50,000**; **3 main canals**; **6.62 lakh acres** (later 10 lakh acres for two crops).
- Revenue: Rs 20.38 lakh (1853–54) → Rs 55.18 lakh (1894–95).
- Old anicut later replaced by the **Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage**.
