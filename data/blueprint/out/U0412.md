### 3. JUVENILE JUSTICE ACT 2015
#### 3.1 Earlier Juvenile Laws
| Law | Note |
|---|---|
| Children Act 1960 | For Union Territories |
| Juvenile Justice Act 1986 | First uniform juvenile law; boys 16, girls 18 |
| JJ (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2000 | Uniform age **18**; amended 2006 |
| JJ Act 2015 | Response to the **Delhi gang rape case (2012)**; "transfer system" for 16–18 |
#### 3.2 Key Features
- Replaced "**juvenile**" with "**child**"; two categories.
- **Heinous offence** = minimum punishment **7 years or more**.
- 16–18 + heinous offence: **JJB preliminary assessment** (not a finding of guilt) → possible transfer to **Children's Court**.
- **CARA** given **statutory** status; **compulsory registration** of all Child Care Institutions.
- **Working children** = children in need of care and protection.
- Guiding principle: **best interests of the child**; child-friendly, rehabilitation-oriented (APPSC 2019, 2024). Juvenile delinquents are dealt with by **juvenile courts/boards** (APPSC 2019).
| Category / body | Handled by / composition |
|---|---|
| Child in Conflict with Law (CCL) | **Juvenile Justice Board** – Principal Magistrate (MM or JMFC) + **2 social workers**, at least one woman |
| Child in Need of Care and Protection (CNCP) | **Child Welfare Committee** – Chairperson + **4** members |
| Transferred 16–18 heinous cases | **Children's Court** (a Sessions Court) |
- Every district has a JJB and a CWC, each with at least one woman member.
#### 3.3 2021 Amendment
- **District Magistrate** (and ADM) = focal point; DMs issue **adoption orders** instead of courts; CWC member eligibility conditions; Adoption Regulations 2022.
- **Private Member's Bill (Dec 2025)**: lower heinous-offence age threshold from **16 to 14**.
!PAIR CCL | JJB | Magistrate + 2 || CNCP | CWC | Chairperson + 4
### 4. POCSO ACT 2012
#### 4.1 Basics
- **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012**; drafted by **MWCD**.
- Child = **below 18**, gender-neutral for child and offender; consent below 18 **legally irrelevant**.
- After **BNS** (from **1 July 2024**), POCSO stays a separate special law.
#### 4.2 Offences and Procedure
- Offences: **penetrative** and **non-penetrative** sexual assault, their **aggravated** forms, **sexual harassment**, **child pornography**. **Child marriage is NOT** a POCSO offence.
- **Aggravated** = by a person in trust/authority (police, armed forces, public servant).
| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| **19** | **Mandatory reporting** to SJPU or local police; failure: up to **6 months** and/or fine |
| **22** | False complaints punishable (except good faith) |
| **29–30** | **Presumption** / burden of proof on the accused |
- Statement at child's residence or chosen place, by a **woman officer (at least Sub-Inspector)**, **not in uniform**; no child kept at police station at night; no contact with accused.
- **Special Courts**: **in-camera** trial; investigation in **2 months**; trial in **1 year**; compensation and rehabilitation. Monitored by NCPCR and SCPCRs.
#### 4.3 Amendments
| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| **Ordinance, 21 April 2018** | Harsher punishment (up to death) for rape of girls below 12 |
| **2019 Amendment** | Penetrative assault min **10 years**; aggravated min **20 years**, up to life or **death** |
| 2019 Amendment | Child pornography defined; storing without deleting/reporting – fine at least ₹5,000 (repeat ₹10,000); punished giving **chemicals to hasten sexual maturity** |
| POCSO Rules 2020 | Replaced 2012 Rules |
!TRAP 10 = penetrative minimum; 20 = aggravated minimum (2019).

- **Independent Thought v. UoI (2017)**: marital rape exception (Sec. 375 IPC) struck down for wives **below 18**.

- **13 States**, about **12,447** children; **two out of three** faced physical abuse; **53.22%** reported sexual abuse; **over 50%** of abusers known to the child or in trust.
#### 6.2 NCRB
- **2023**: 1,77,335 cases (+9.2%); kidnapping ~45%, POCSO ~38%. **Madhya Pradesh** highest cases (with Maharashtra, UP); **Assam** highest rate (84.2).
- **NCRB 2021**: **97.1%** of child sexual-abuse offenders known to the child.
- Child marriage (NFHS-5): over 27% of women 20–24 married before 18 (newer: 23.3%).

### 7. CHILD LABOUR LAW – 1986 AND 2016
| Feature | 1986 Act | 2016 Amendment |
|---|---|---|
| Below 14 | Banned in **18 occupations, 65 processes**; rest regulated | Banned in **all** occupations and processes (linked to RTE age) |
| 14–18 | — | New "**adolescent**" – banned in **hazardous** work only |
| Exceptions | — | Family enterprise **after school**; child artist in audio-visual entertainment (**excluding circus**) |
| Penalty | — | **Cognizable**; 6 months–2 years and/or ₹20,000–50,000 |
- Hazardous list trimmed to mines, inflammable substances/explosives and Factories Act hazardous processes (earlier 83 entries). **Child and Adolescent Labour Rehabilitation Fund**. Enforcement: **District Magistrate**.
- 1986 Act = first comprehensive child labour law; clashed with RTE 2009.
- **Gurupadaswamy Committee (1979)**, Ministry of Labour: poverty = root cause; **dual approach** – ban hazardous, regulate rest → **1986 Act** and **National Policy on Child Labour, 1987**.
- **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976**; debt bondage across generations.
### 8. MAGNITUDE AND CAUSES OF CHILD LABOUR
- **Census 2011 (5–14)**: **10.1 million** (incl. marginal); **4.35 million** main workers.
- India has the **largest number** of child labourers in the world.
- Sectors: brick kilns, pesticide farm work, toxic chemicals, tea stalls, begging.
- Causes: **poverty**, **cheap labour** demand, family disorganisation, illiteracy, debt bondage, migrant families, kidnapping, weak enforcement.
### 9. ENFORCEMENT – PENCIL, NCLP, REPORTING
| Mechanism | Fact |
|---|---|
| **PENCIL** (2017) | **Platform for Effective Enforcement for No Child Labour**; **MoLE**; components: **Child Tracking System, Complaint Corner, State Government, NCLP, Convergence** (not National Adoption Registry); links Child Labour Committees, police, NGOs (APPSC G2 2025) |
| **NCLP** (1988) | **MoLE** (not MoSJE); withdraws children; **Special Training Centres** for **9–14**: bridge education, vocational training, mid-day meal, stipend |
| **POCSO e-Box** (2016) | **NCPCR**; confidential reporting of sexual abuse, online or offline |
| CHILDLINE **1098** | Integrated with **ERSS-112** |
| cybercrime.gov.in | CSAM reports (I4C, MHA) |
| **Operation Smile / Muskaan** | **MHA** drives to trace and rescue missing children (2015) |
- Process: identification → rescue → rehabilitation.
!TRAP PENCIL wrong options: SPARC, SRIJAN, YUKTI.
- Trafficking: widened by **Criminal Law Amendment Act 2013** (sexual exploitation, slavery, servitude, forced labour, organ removal), irrespective of consent; now **Sec. 143, BNS**; anti-human trafficking units act on it.
