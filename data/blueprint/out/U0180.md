### 2. RAILWAYS – PURPOSE, GUARANTEE SYSTEM, EFFECTS
- **Official reasons:** faster movement, communication, administration and **troop movement**.
- **Colonial economic purpose:** carry **raw materials to ports** and **British goods into Indian markets**.
- **Guarantee system:** private British companies got a guaranteed **5% return** on capital, **paid from Indian revenues** whether or not lines made a profit.
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Motive | Mainly colonial exploitation |
| **Drain** | Nationalists counted the guaranteed interest in the **drain of wealth** |
| **Famine** | Railways also moved grain for famine relief |
| **Unintended effects** | **National market**; shared public travel across castes; **faster nationalist mobilisation** |
- Vernacular press, railways and telegraph built the **all-India public sphere** for nationalism.

### 7. VERNACULAR PRESS ACT, 1878
- **"Gagging Act"** of **Lord Lytton**, passed amid anger over the **1876–77 famine** and the costly **Delhi Durbar (1877)**.
- **Provisions:**
  - District magistrates could make printers/publishers sign **bonds** not to publish seditious matter.
  - Demand **security deposits** (forfeitable); **seize presses** for repeat offences.
  - **No appeal to a court**.
  - Exemption only by **submitting proofs to a government censor** before publication.
- **Discriminatory:** applied **only to Indian-language papers**, not English ones.
- **Evasion:** **Amrita Bazar Patrika** became an **English paper overnight**.
- **Repeal:** **1882** by **Lord Ripon**; pre-censorship dropped, a **press commissioner** appointed.
- **Order:** Dramatic Performances Act (1876) → **VPA (1878)** → Bengal Tenancy Act (1885) → North-Western Provinces and Oudh Act.
- **Order:** Ban on Sati (1829) → Wood's Despatch (1854) → VPA (1878) → Rowlatt Act (1919).

### 10. MACAULAY'S MINUTE AND DOWNWARD FILTRATION
- **Macaulay's Minute (2 Feb 1835):** written as **Law Member** and **President of the GCPI**.
- **Recommendation:** education funds **exclusively** to **English-medium** teaching of Western science and literature; higher education over mass primary schooling.
- **Famous remark:** "a **single shelf of a good European library** was worth the whole native literature of India and Arabia" (APPSC G2 2025).
- **Aim:** a class "**Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste, in opinions, in morals, and in intellect**" – interpreters between rulers and ruled.
- **Downward Filtration Theory:** educate a small upper class first → learning **filters down** to the masses (related to **education**, APPSC G2 2024); criticised for neglecting mass literacy.
- **Wood's Despatch rejected downward filtration**; recommended **grants-in-aid** and mass teaching.
- **Madras impact:** the **1835 resolution banned regional-language instruction** in Madras district schools; Munro's collectorate and tahsildari schools (2 in Kadapa, 1 Nellore, 2 Vizag by 1830) **closed in 1836**.

- **Press and Registration of Periodicals Act, 2023** replaced the **1867** Act (repealed w.e.f. **1 March 2024**); **RNI** is now the **Press Registrar General of India** (2026).
