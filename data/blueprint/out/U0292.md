## U0292 | AMENDMENT OF THE CONSTITUTION – ARTICLE 368, MAJORITIES, PROCEDURE, LIMITATIONS
### 1. ARTICLE 368 – BASICS
- **Part XX**, sole **Article 368** – Parliament's power and procedure to amend.
- Wording: Parliament may, "in exercise of its **constituent power**, amend by way of **addition, variation or repeal** any provision" (within the basic-structure limit).
- **Article 368(2)** mentions **two methods**; Art. 368 itself amended by the **24th (1971)** and **101st (2016)** Amendments.
- Source: **South Africa** (also election of Rajya Sabha members) – not the very rigid USA.
- Nature: **partly rigid, partly flexible** – a synthesis of both.
- **Granville Austin**: process "appears complicated" but "is merely diverse"; one of the most ably conceived aspects.
- **K.C. Wheare**: good balance between rigidity and flexibility; called the Constitution **quasi-federal**.
- Latest enacted amendment (2026): **106th (2023)** – women's reservation.
### 2. PROCEDURE – STEP BY STEP
1. Bill only in **either House of Parliament** – **not a State Legislature**, not the President.
2. Moved by a **minister or private member**; **no prior permission of the President**.
3. **Each House separately** by **special majority**; **no joint sitting** (Art. 108 does not apply) – rejection by either House means the Bill **fails**.
4. Federal provisions: ratified by **half the States**.
5. President **must assent** – cannot withhold or return; made obligatory by the **24th Amendment, 1971** ("shall give his assent").
!TRAP PYQ 2024: "prior permission", "joint sitting" and "President may return" are all FALSE for amendment Bills.
#### 2.1 Bill Types Compared
| Basis | Ordinary | Money | Amendment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Articles | 107, 108 | 109, 110 | 368 |

- **Financial Bill (I)**: starts only in Lok Sabha, but Rajya Sabha may reject or amend it.
### 3. THREE ROUTES OF AMENDMENT
| Route | Requirement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple majority** (outside 368) | Present and voting; **not deemed amendments** | Arts 2-4, 169, citizenship, official language, Fifth/Sixth Schedules, quorum |
| **Special majority** | **Majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting**, each House | **FRs (incl. Art. 32)**, **DPSP**, all other provisions |
| **Special + State ratification** | + **one-half of States** | Federal provisions (Section 5) |
- **Article 32**: special majority, **no ratification** (APPSC G2 2017) – but SC's power under it is **basic structure**.
### 4. SIMPLE-MAJORITY PROVISIONS (OUTSIDE ARTICLE 368)
| Provision | Article |
|---|---|
| Admission/establishment of **new States** | 2 |
| Formation, **boundaries, names** of States | 3 |
| Such laws not amendments | **4(2)** |
| Create/abolish **Legislative Councils** | **169** |
| **Citizenship** | 5-11 (Parliament regulates under **11**) |
| **Official language**, use of English in Parliament | 343 |
| Elections, delimitation | 327 |
| **Fifth and Sixth Schedules** | – |
| **Quorum**, MPs' salaries, rules of procedure, **privileges** | – |
| Number of **puisne judges** of SC; more SC jurisdiction | – |
- **Only Parliament** can change a State's area, boundary or name (Art. 3); "a State may include a Union territory" is not a provision.
- States can only pass a **resolution** for a Council (Art. 169).
!TRAP "Which is NOT amended by special majority?" – official language, citizenship, Fifth/Sixth Schedules.
### 5. FEDERAL PROVISIONS NEEDING STATE RATIFICATION
- **Election of the President** (Arts 54, 55) – not his **qualifications, conditions of office or tenure**.
- **Extent of executive power** of Union and States (73, 162) – not conditions of the Governor's office.
- **Supreme Court and High Courts** (incl. Art. 241).
- **Seventh Schedule** – any List (e.g. Union List), distribution of legislative powers.
- **Representation of States in Parliament** – **Fourth Schedule**.
- **Article 368 itself**.
- **GST Council (Art. 279A)** – added by the **101st Amendment, 2016**.
- Ratified by **not less than half** the States (not all), by **simple majority** of each legislature; **no time limit**.
- **First** ratified amendment: **3rd Amendment (1954)** – re-enacted **Entry 33, Concurrent List**.
- **Rajendra N. Shah (2021)**: **Part IXB** (97th, co-operatives) struck down for State co-operatives for **want of ratification**.
### 6. SPECIAL MAJORITY – DIFFERENT MEANINGS
| Type | Requirement | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. 368 type** | Total membership + 2/3 present and voting, each House | Amendment; removal of SC/HC judges (124, 217), **CAG** (148), **CEC** (324), **SEC** (243K); National Emergency (352) |
| **Art. 61 type** | **2/3 of total membership** | **Impeachment of President** – strongest |
| **Art. 249 / 312** | **2/3 present and voting, Rajya Sabha only** | State List law (249); new **All-India Services** (312) |
### 7. LIMITATIONS – CAN PARLIAMENT AMEND FRs?
| Case / Act | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad** | 1951 | Yes; amendment is not "law" under **13(2)** |
| **Sajjan Singh** | 1965 | Yes; reaffirmed |
| **Golaknath** | 1967 | **No** – amendment is law; **first to limit** amending power |
| **24th Amendment** | 1971 | Restored power; amended Arts 13 and 368; added **13(4)** |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | 1973 | Yes, but **not the Basic Structure**; upheld 24th; reversed Golaknath |
| **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain** | 1975 | Doctrine **first applied** |
| **42nd Amendment** | 1976 | Added **368(4), (5)** – unlimited power, no judicial review |
| **Minerva Mills** | 1980 | Struck down 368(4), (5); **limited amending power is a basic feature**; struck absolute DPSP priority |
- **Kesavananda**: **13 judges, 7:6**, judgment **24 April 1973**; **Preamble part of the Constitution** and of basic structure; gave Parliament the right to amend FRs.
- Sequence: **Shankari Prasad → Golaknath → 24th → Kesavananda**.
!PAIR Golaknath | 1967 | first limited power || Kesavananda | 1973 | first propounded Basic Structure

### 9. CRITICISM
- **No special body** (convention) or **referendum**.
- **No joint sitting** – no deadlock mechanism.
- **No time limit** for ratification.
- States **cannot initiate**; ratification only for a few provisions.
- Simple-majority routes too easy; the 2026 vote shows special majority guards against slim majorities.
