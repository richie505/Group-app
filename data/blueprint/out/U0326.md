## U0326 | EMERGENCY PROVISIONS – NATIONAL EMERGENCY (352), PRESIDENT'S RULE (356), FINANCIAL EMERGENCY (360); S.R. BOMMAI CASE
### 1. SOURCE AND SCHEME OF EMERGENCY PROVISIONS
- Emergency provisions are in **Part XVIII** (Articles 352-360).
- Source of emergency provisions: **Government of India Act, 1935**; the ordinance power (Arts **123/213**) is also a 1935 Act relic.
- Suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency: borrowed from the **Weimar Constitution** (Germany).
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** hoped Art. 356 would remain a "**dead letter**".
#### 1.1 Three Emergencies Compared
| Feature | National (Art. 352) | President's Rule (Art. 356) | Financial (Art. 360) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | **War, external aggression, armed rebellion** (also imminent danger); whole or part of India | President satisfied, **on the Governor's report or otherwise**, that the State cannot be governed per the Constitution | **Financial stability or credit** of India or any part threatened |
| Approval within | **1 month** | **2 months** | **2 months** |
| Majority | **Special majority** in each House | Simple majority | **Simple majority** |
| Duration | 6 months at a time, **extendable indefinitely** | 6 months per approval, **max 3 years** | **No maximum**; continues till revoked |
| Revocation | President any time; must revoke if Lok Sabha disapproves by **simple majority** | President any time; no parliamentary approval needed | President any time |
| Instances | **1962, 1971, 1975** | First: **Punjab, 1951** | **Never declared** |
!NUM Approval: 1 month (352) – 2 months (356, 360) – each approval of 352/356 lasts 6 months
### 2. NATIONAL EMERGENCY (ARTICLE 352)
#### 2.1 Grounds and Procedure
- Proclaimed by the **President** when the security of India is threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellion.
- **44th CAA, 1978** replaced "**internal disturbance**" with "**armed rebellion**".
- **Written advice of the Union Cabinet** mandatory – **Art. 352(3)** (44th CAA). The word "**Cabinet**" appears only once in the Constitution – in Art. 352(3).
- Approval period cut from **two months to one month** by the 44th CAA; approval needs **special majority** (two-thirds present and voting + majority of total membership).
- Notice by **1/10 of Lok Sabha members** – special sitting within **14 days** to consider disapproval.
!TRAP Written Cabinet advice was inserted as Art. 352(3), NOT a new "Article 352A".
#### 2.2 Instances of National Emergency
| Instance | Date | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **1962** | Chinese aggression (war) |
| 2nd | **1971** | Indo-Pak war (external aggression) |
| 3rd | **25 June 1975** | "**Internal disturbance**" – proclaimed by **President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed** |
- 1975 background: **Allahabad High Court** cancelled Indira Gandhi's 1971 election (**Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain**).
- **25 June** observed as "**Samvidhan Hatya Diwas**" since 2025.
### 3. CONSEQUENCES OF NATIONAL EMERGENCY
#### 3.1 Centre-State Relations and Terms
| Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| Legislative | Parliament legislates on **State List** (Art. 250); such laws end **6 months** after Emergency ends |
| Executive | Centre can **direct States on any matter** |
| Financial | President can **modify distribution of revenues** – till end of the **financial year** in which the Emergency ends |
| Terms | Lok Sabha (**Art. 83(2)**) and Assemblies extendable by law **1 year at a time**, not beyond **6 months** after Emergency ceases |
#### 3.2 Article 358 vs Article 359
| Basis | Article 358 | Article 359 |
|---|---|---|
| What | Suspends the **6 freedoms of Art. 19** | Suspends the **right to move courts** for specified FRs |
| How | **Automatic** on proclamation | Needs a separate **Presidential Order** |
| Ground | Only **war or external aggression** – not armed rebellion | External emergency and armed rebellion |
| Limit | Only laws related to the Emergency protected (44th CAA) | **Arts. 20 and 21 can never be suspended** (44th CAA) |
- Acts done during the emergency under Art. 358 cannot be challenged even afterwards.
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973):** Parliament can amend FRs but not the **basic structure**.
!TRAP "Arts. 20 and 21 can be suspended under Art. 359" = WRONG.
### 4. PRESIDENT'S RULE (ARTICLE 356)
#### 4.1 Grounds, Approval, Extension
- On the Governor's report **or otherwise**; **no State Legislature consent** needed.
- Approval within **2 months**; each approval lasts **6 months**; maximum **3 years**.
- **Beyond 1 year** only if a **National Emergency is in operation AND the Election Commission certifies** that elections cannot be held (Art. 356(5)).
!TRAP Both conditions are needed – "AND", not "or".
#### 4.2 Consequences
| Organ | Effect |
|---|---|
| Executive | President assumes State functions; Council of Ministers dismissed; **Governor administers** on his behalf |
| Legislature | Assembly **suspended or dissolved**; **Parliament** legislates (or delegates to the President) |
| Judiciary | **High Court untouched** – President cannot assume or suspend HC powers |
#### 4.3 Article 355 and Article 365
- **Art. 355:** Union's duty to protect States from **external aggression and internal disturbance** and ensure constitutional governance – basis for Art. 356.
- **Art. 365:** failure of a State to comply with **Union directions** – President may hold that constitutional machinery has failed (justifies President's Rule).
#### 4.4 Misuse Debate
- Art. 356 is among the most **controversial** provisions (PYQ 2025); partisan Governor reports.
- **ONOE** critique: deferred State polls could prolong PR, clashing with Art. 356(5) (9 Mar 2026).
### 5. PRESIDENT'S RULE IN AP AND OTHER STATES
| AP spell | Period | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **1st and longest** | **18 Jan 1973 – 10 Dec 1973** | **Jai Andhra** agitation; ended when **Jalagam Vengala Rao** became CM |
| 2nd | 1 Mar 2014 – 8 Jun 2014 | CM **Kiran Kumar Reddy** resigned over bifurcation; Telangana formed 2 June 2014 |
- 1973 spell ended after the **Six-Point Formula**, validated by the **32nd CAA, 1973** (inserted **Art. 371D**).
- **AP High Court (Dec 2020)** asked whether it could record a "breakdown of constitutional machinery" – **Art. 356** (PYQ 2021).
- **First PR in India:** Punjab, 1951. First in AP: January 1973 (PYQ 2019/2021).
- **Never under PR:** **Chhattisgarh and Telangana**.
- **Most times:** **Manipur – 11** (11th in Feb 2025, highest); **Uttar Pradesh – 10**.
- **Manipur:** PR from Feb 2025 after N. Biren Singh resigned; Parliament approved extension till Feb 2026 (Aug 2025); **revoked 4 Feb 2026**, **Y. Khemchand Singh** sworn in as CM.
!TRAP 32nd CAA = Art. 371D; the 33rd CAA concerns resignation of legislators.
### 6. S.R. BOMMAI v UNION OF INDIA (1994)
- **9-judge bench**; landmark on Art. 356.
- **Judicial review:** proclamation is reviewable (mala fide or irrelevant grounds).
- **Floor test:** majority tested on the **floor of the Assembly**; Governor's opinion not conclusive.
- **Assembly** may only be suspended until Parliament approves; no arbitrary dissolution; court can **restore** a dismissed government.
- **Secularism and federalism** are **basic features**; a State government acting against secularism can be dismissed.
#### 6.1 Other Cases
| Case | Year | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| ADM Jabalpur | 1976 | Right to life unenforceable in Emergency; rejected in **K.S. Puttaswamy (2017)** |
| Minerva Mills | 1980 | Art. 352 proclamation **reviewable**; undid 38th CAA's bar |
| S.R. Bommai | 1994 | 9 judges; review, floor test, secularism + federalism |
| Rameshwar Prasad | 2006 | Struck down **Bihar Assembly** dissolution on partisan grounds |
### 7. FINANCIAL EMERGENCY (ARTICLE 360)
- Ground: threat to **financial stability or credit**; approval within 2 months by simple majority; **no maximum** period.
- Effects: directions on **canons of financial propriety**; **cut salaries** of all government servants **including SC and HC judges**; State Money/Financial Bills **reserved for the President**.
- Instances: **never declared**.
### 8. AMENDMENTS ON EMERGENCY
| Amendment | Change |
|---|---|
| 38th CAA (1975) | Made President's satisfaction **non-justiciable** (later reversed) |
| 39th CAA (1975) | Election disputes of **PM and Speaker** beyond courts |
| 42nd CAA (1976) | "Mini-constitution"; Lok Sabha/Assembly terms raised to **6 years** |
| **44th CAA (1978)** | "Internal disturbance" → "armed rebellion"; written Cabinet advice; approval 1 month; Art. 358 not for armed rebellion; Art. 359 cannot touch Arts. 20, 21 |
### 9. NUMBER GAME AND QUICK REVISION
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| National Emergency approval | 1 month, special majority |
| PR / Financial approval | 2 months |
| Max PR | 3 years |
| Special sitting notice | 1/10 of Lok Sabha; within 14 days |
| Bommai bench | 9 judges |

| Max gap between sessions | 6 months (Art. 85) |
#### FIRST / LAST / SPECIAL FACTS
- First PR: Punjab, 1951; first in AP: 18 Jan 1973.
- Only internal-disturbance Emergency: 1975.
- Financial Emergency: never declared.

### 11. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Internal disturbance** is no longer a ground – replaced by armed rebellion (1978).
2. **Art. 352(3)**, not 352A.
3. **1 month** for 352, **2 months** for 356 and 360.
4. Revoking needs only a **simple majority** of Lok Sabha.
5. Art. 358 = **automatic**, Art. 19 only, not armed rebellion.
6. Art. 359 can **never** suspend Arts. **20 and 21**.
7. PR beyond 1 year: Emergency **AND** EC certificate.
8. High Court is **unaffected** by President's Rule.
9. Financial Emergency – **never declared**, no maximum period.
10. AP Six-Point Formula = **32nd CAA**, not 33rd.
