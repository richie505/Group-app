## U0133 | AURANGZEB – RELIGIOUS POLICY, DECCAN POLICY AND RESISTANCE
### 1. AURANGZEB – TITLES AND CORONATIONS
- Regnal title: **Alamgir** ("World Conqueror"). Popular epithet: **Zinda Pir** ("Living Saint"), for his simple, pious life.
- **Double coronation**: first, hurried – **1658** (after **Samugarh**); second, grand – **1659** (after **Deorai**).
- Reign: 1658–1707. The Mughal empire reached its **greatest extent** yet was overstretched.
#### 1.1 Titles of Royal Women
| Title | Holder | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Padshah Begum** | **Roshanara** | Backed Aurangzeb in the succession war |
| **Sahibat-uz-Zamani** ("Mistress of the Age") | **Jahanara** | Given to her later |
!TRAP Roshanara = Padshah Begum; Jahanara = Sahibat-uz-Zamani. Do not swap them.
- FIRST: first Mughal emperor to fight the English – **Child's War (1686–90)**.
### 2. RELIGIOUS POLICY – TAXES AND MEASURES
| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Jizya** | **Reimposed in 1679** (Akbar had abolished it); suspended in the Deccan in 1704 |
| **Temples** | Newly built temples ordered destroyed; **1669 order** – **Kashi Vishwanath** and **Keshava Rai (Mathura)** demolished |
| **Muhtasibs** | Appointed to enforce the **Sharia** (public morals) |
| Court customs | **Jharokha darshan** and **tuladan** stopped; **Navroz** banned |
| **Court music** | Banned at court, yet he **played the veena**; most Persian works on Indian classical music were written in his reign |
| **Zawabit** | State-made secular regulations; he stressed the supremacy of Sharia over them |
- **Hindu nobles' share rose** to about **31.6% (1679–1707)**, many of them Marathas (Athar Ali) – so "anti-Hindu" is not the whole story.
!KEY Jizya reimposed 1679 – the same year the Rajput war began.
### 3. RAJPUT RESISTANCE – MARWAR AND MEWAR
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Trigger | **Maharaja Jaswant Singh** of Marwar died **1678**; Aurangzeb took Marwar under direct control |
| Heir | Posthumous son **Ajit Singh**, championed by **Durgadas Rathore** |
| Mewar war (1679–81) | **Rana Raj Singh** joined Marwar |
| Rebel prince | **Prince Akbar** (his son) joined the Rajputs (**1681**) → fled to **Sambhaji** → finally **Persia** |
- Result: the **Rajput alliance** (pillar of Akbar's empire) was broken.
### 4. JAT AND SATNAMI REVOLTS
| Revolt | Leader | Region / fact |
|---|---|---|
| Jat (**1669**) | **Gokula** | **Mathura** (Tilpat); peasant revolt against heavy revenue |
| Jat (1680s) | **Rajaram** | Plundered **Akbar's tomb at Sikandra** |
| Jat (after 1700) | **Churaman** | Built the power that became **Bharatpur** state |
| Satnami (**1672**) | Satnami sect | **Narnaul (Haryana)**; peasants and artisans; crushed |
!TRAP Satnami revolt of 1672 has no link with **Guru Ghasidas** (19th-century Satnampanth, Chhattisgarh).
!TRAP Jat Rajaram (Sikandra) ≠ Maratha Rajaram (Shivaji's son).
### 5. SIKH RESISTANCE
- **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (9th Guru) – arrested 1675 and **executed at Delhi in 1675** after refusing to accept Islam.
- **Guru Gobind Singh** (10th Guru) – fought the Mughals; after **Chamkaur (1704)** wrote the **Zafarnama**, a Persian letter to Aurangzeb accusing him of breaking oaths sworn on the Quran.
### 6. DECCAN POLICY – BIJAPUR AND GOLCONDA
#### 6.1 Bijapur (1686)
- Long siege (1685–86); Aurangzeb took personal command; fell **September 1686**.
- Last ruler **Sikandar Adil Shah** – presented in silver chains; sent to **Daulatabad**; Adil Shahi dynasty ended.
#### 6.2 Fall of Golconda (1687) – Sequence
1. Ministers **Madanna–Akkanna** murdered (**1686**).
2. Siege **1687**; **Abdur Razzaq Lari** defended (later honoured as **Mustafa Khan**).
3. Traitor **Abdullah Pani** opened the gates – fort opened with "**keys of gold**" (as Akbar opened Asirgarh).
4. Fort taken **1687** → **21st Mughal suba**.
5. Last ruler **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah (Tana Shah)** – imprisoned at Bidar, then **Daulatabad**; died **1699**; buried at **Khuldabad**.
!TRAP Tana Shah was exiled to **Daulatabad**, not Gwalior (Gwalior = Murad Bakhsh).
- **AP angle**: fall of Golconda (1687) ended **Qutb Shahi rule over Andhra**; **Bhakta Ramadasu** was the nephew of Akkanna–Madanna.
!PAIR Bijapur | 1686 | Sikandar Adil Shah || Golconda | 1687 | Abul Hasan (Tana Shah)
### 7. MARATHA CONFLICT
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1666** | **Shivaji at Agra** – insulted, house arrest, escaped in **fruit baskets** |
| **1689** | **Sambhaji** captured at **Sangameshwar** and **executed** (March 1689) |
| 1689–1700 | **Rajaram** (Sambhaji's brother) became Chhatrapati; resisted from **Jinji**; died at **Sinhagad (1700)** |
| From 1700 | **Tarabai** (Rajaram's widow) led the war as **regent** for her infant son **Shivaji II** |
- **"Deccan Ulcer"**: the **27-year** Deccan war drained the treasury (compare Napoleon's Spanish Ulcer).
### 8. LAST YEARS, DEATH AND BURIAL
- Spent the last **25–27 years** camped in the Deccan (1687–1707 phase = war against the Marathas till death).
- **Died 1707 at Ahmednagar**; simple grave at **Khuldabad** (near Aurangabad) – Tana Shah also buried there.
!TRAP Died at Ahmednagar; buried at Khuldabad – not Delhi, not Aurangabad city.

- Last Qutb Shahi ruler: Tana Shah; last Adil Shahi ruler: Sikandar Adil Shah.
- Golconda = 21st suba.
