## U0153 | PORTUGUESE IN INDIA – ALMEIDA'S BLUE WATER POLICY, ALBUQUERQUE, ESTADO DA INDIA, CARTAZ SYSTEM, DECLINE
### 1. THE PORTUGUESE IN INDIA – OVERVIEW
- Portuguese = **first Europeans** to set up sea trade centres in India; **first to come, last to leave** (1961).
- Their state in Asia was the **Estado da India**, with HQ first at **Cochin** and later **Goa**.
- **Goa (1510)** = first European territory in India since **Alexander**.
- New World crops brought by them reshaped Indian cuisine.
### 2. VICEROYS AND GOVERNORS – POLICY AND KEY ACTS
#### 2.1 In Order
| Governor | Years | Title | Key act / policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Francisco de Almeida** | 1505-09 | **First Viceroy** | **Blue Water Policy**; won the **Battle of Diu (1509)** |
| **Afonso de Albuquerque** | 1509-15 | **Real founder** of Portuguese power | **Conquest of Goa (1510)** from Bijapur; **marriage policy** (mixed marriages); abolished Sati in his territories |
| **Nino (Nuno) da Cunha** | 1529-38 | Governor | **HQ shift Cochin → Goa (1530)**; Bassein (1534) and Diu fort (1535) from Bahadur Shah |
#### 2.2 Blue Water Policy
- Introduced by **Almeida**: naval mastery of the **Indian Ocean**, not inland territory.
- Made the Portuguese the strongest naval power in the Indian Ocean after Diu (1509).
!TRAP Blue Water Policy = Almeida (APPSC-G2 2025, G2 Screening 2024). Albuquerque REVERSED it – he went for territorial conquest (Goa, Malacca, Hormuz).
!PAIR Almeida | First Viceroy | Blue Water Policy || Albuquerque | Real founder | Goa 1510, marriage policy
### 3. CARTAZ SYSTEM
#### 3.1 How It Worked
1. Every ship sailing the **Indian Ocean** had to buy a **Cartaz** (pass).
2. Backed by **Caes** (convoy system) and **customs duties**.
3. A ship without a pass was **confiscated or sunk**.
4. Also used in the **Bay of Bengal**.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cartaz** | **Naval trade licence (pass)** for Indian Ocean ships |
| **Caes** | Convoy system backing the Cartaz |
| **Customs duties** | Third tool of the maritime monopoly |
!KEY APPSC-GS 2018: Cartaz = naval trade licence (pass).
- Portuguese **monopoly and heavy duties** caused constant conflict with the **Zamorin of Calicut**.

### 6. HUGLI BASE – PIRACY AND MUGHAL RESPONSE
- From **Hugli** and **Chittagong**, the Portuguese (with **Arakanese pirates**) raided the **Bay of Bengal**.
- **Hooghly** = Portuguese base for piracy.
- **1632**: **Shah Jahan** took Hugli; his Bengal governor **Qasim Khan** defeated the Portuguese.
- **Shaista Khan** (Bengal governor under **Aurangzeb**) captured **Sondip** and **Chittagong** and suppressed the Arakanese pirates.
!PAIR Hugli | granted 1579 | Akbar || Hugli | taken 1632 | Shah Jahan (Qasim Khan)
### 7. CONTRIBUTIONS
#### 7.1 New World Crops and Fruits
- **Tobacco, potato, lady's finger, chilli, tomato, pineapple, sapota, groundnut**.
- **Tomato** brought to **Telugu soil** by the Portuguese – native to the Americas (**Columbian Exchange**) (APPSC-G2 2025 key).
!TRAP NOT a Portuguese New World crop: **wheat**.
#### 7.2 Printing, Religion and Culture
- **Printing press**: **Goa, 1556**; Bible printed in **Kannada** and **Malayalam**.
- **St. Francis Xavier** (Jesuit): reached **Goa 1542**; remains at the **Basilica of Bom Jesus**, Goa.
- **Goa Inquisition** (16th c.): against religious dissent; state system used for conversions.
- **Vindaloo** (from *vinha d'alhos*); Indo-Portuguese houses with **balcaos**.

### 9. PORTUGUESE ON THE ANDHRA COAST AND BAY OF BENGAL
- Posts at **Masulipatnam**, **Pulicat** and **San Thome**; Pulicat was a **pepper and cloth** centre before the Dutch took it (1610).
- Telugu name for the Portuguese: "**Budata Keechu**".
- Influence on the Andhra coast **faded by the mid-17th century** – religious intolerance angered the Golconda Sultans.
- Brought **tomato, chilli, groundnut** to the Telugu country.
- **Machilipatnam factory (1669-70)**, allowed by **Abdullah Qutb Shah**, was **French** (last Europeans in Andhra), not Portuguese.
