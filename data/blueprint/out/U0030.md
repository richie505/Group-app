## U0030 | BUDDHIST COUNCILS, HINAYANA–MAHAYANA–VAJRAYANA, TRIPITAKAS AND PALI LITERATURE
### 1. THE FOUR BUDDHIST COUNCILS
| Council | Place / year | Patron | President | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | **Rajagriha (Rajgir)**, **Saptaparni Cave**; **483 BCE**, soon after the Buddha's death | **Ajatashatru** (Haryanka) | **Mahakassapa** | **Vinaya** (recited by **Upali**) and **Sutta** (by **Ananda**) compiled |
| **Second** | **Vaishali**; **383 BCE**, 100 years after the Buddha's death | **Kalashoka** (Shishunaga) | **Sabakami** | Dispute on monastic rules; split into **Sthaviravadins** and **Mahasanghikas** |
| **Third** | **Pataliputra**; c. **250 BCE** | **Ashoka** (Maurya) | **Moggaliputta Tissa** | **Abhidhamma Pitaka**, **Kathavatthu**; heretics expelled; missions abroad |
| **Fourth** | **Kundalavana, Kashmir**; c. **72 CE** (1st c. CE) | **Kanishka** (Kushana) | **Vasumitra** (Ashvaghosha deputy) | In **Sanskrit**; **Mahavibhasa**; division into **Mahayana and Hinayana** |
- Order of venues: **Rajagriha → Vaishali → Pataliputra → Kundalvana**.
- 4th council held on the advice of monk **Parsva**; settled Sarvastivada disputes of Kashmir–Gandhara.
- The 3rd council is recorded in the **Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa**.
!NUM Presidents: Mahakassapa – Sabakami – Moggaliputta Tissa – Vasumitra; Patrons: Ajatashatru – Kalashoka – Ashoka – Kanishka
### 2. TRIPITAKA

|---|---|---|

| **Sutta** | Buddha's sermons on ethics and doctrine | **5 Nikayas**; Ananda |
| **Abhidhamma** | Philosophical analysis of Dhamma (Q&A) | **7 books**; Moggaliputta Tissa, 3rd council |

### 4. SECTS – HINAYANA, MAHAYANA, VAJRAYANA
| Sect | Ideal / features | Buddha | Texts / spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hinayana / Theravada** | **Arhat** – individual liberation | **Symbols**, no idols | **Pali** canon; **Sri Lanka, Myanmar**, Thailand |
| **Mahayana** (after 4th council) | **Bodhisattva** – postpones nirvana for all beings; Sunyata | **Idol worship** | **Sanskrit** – Prajnaparamita, Lotus Sutra; **China, Japan, Korea, Tibet** |
| **Vajrayana** | Salvation through **magical power (vajra)**; **mantras, mudras, mandalas** | Taras, wrathful deities | **Guhyasamaja**, Manjushrimulakalpa; Bengal–Bihar, Tibet, Nepal, Bhutan |
- **Vajrayana**: emerged in the **7th century AD**, flourished from the **8th c.**; most prominent in the Sultanate period; linked with **Padmasambhava**; centres **Vikramashila**, later **Nalanda**.
- **Six paramitas** (perfections) of the Bodhisattva: **dana, sila, kshanti, virya, dhyana, prajna**.
- **Avalokitesvara** = **Padmapani** ("Lotus-Bearer"), Bodhisattva of compassion.
- **Sthaviravadins** = Hinayana; **Lokottaravadins** = offshoot of the Mahasanghikas; Mahasanghika **deification of the Buddha** fostered Mahayana.
- **Tibetan** Buddhism merges Mahayana and Vajrayana; canon = **Kangyur and Tengyur**.
!TRAP Arhat = Hinayana; Bodhisattva = Mahayana; vajra = Vajrayana.
### 5. PHILOSOPHICAL SCHOOLS
| School | Founder / teacher | Doctrine |
|---|---|---|
| **Sthaviravada** | Elders at the 2nd council | Orthodox, strict Vinaya → Theravada |
| **Mahasanghika** | **Mahadeva's** group | **Relaxed rules**; parent of Chaitika and Mahayana |
| **Vaibhashika (Sarvastivada)** | Based on **Mahavibhasa** (Vasumitra) | Dharmas exist in **past, present and future** ("all exists") |
| **Sautrantika** | **Kumaralata** | Relies on the **Suttas**, rejects Abhidharma authority |
| **Madhyamika / Sunyavada** | **Nagarjuna**; Aryadeva, Buddhapalita, Bhavaviveka | **Sunyata** (emptiness); middle between existence and non-existence |
| **Yogachara / Vijnanavada** | **Asanga, Vasubandhu** (pupils of Maitreyanatha) | **Only consciousness is real** |
!TRAP Aryadeva = Madhyamika (not Yogachara); Yogachara and Vijnanavada are one school.
### 6. NAGARJUNA
- Founder of **Madhyamika (Sunyavada)** school of Mahayana; doctrine of **Shunyata** (void).
- Works: **Mulamadhyamakakarika (Madhyamika Karika)**, **Suhrlekha**, **Ratnavali**, Yogasara, Rasaratnakaram.
- **Ratnavali (Rajaparikatha)**: **welfare state** theory, addressed to a Satavahana king.
- **Suhrlekha**: letter to a friend (Satavahana king); **I-tsing** saw students memorising it.
- Title **"Einstein of India"** – idea of relativity.
- Royal friend **Yajna Sri Satakarni** (Satavahana), who built the **1,500-room Maha Vihara (Parvata Vihara)** at **Sriparvata**.
- Placed at the 4th council by tradition; credited with 32 new sects in Andhradesha.
!TRAP **Siddha Nagarjuna** (founded **Vajrayana** at Sriparvata; Ramireddypalli inscription calls him a Telugu native) is a different person.

### 8. STUPA ARCHITECTURE AND ICONOGRAPHY
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Anda** | Hemispherical dome (the cosmos) |
| **Medhi** | Raised terrace round the base of the dome; carries the upper circumambulatory path |
| **Harmika** | Railed square on top of the dome |
| **Chhatra** | Umbrella over the harmika |
| **Torana** | Gateway |
| **Vedika** | Stone railing round the stupa (uprights + cross-bars + coping) |
| **Pradakshinapatha** | Path for walking round the stupa clockwise |
| **Vihara / Chaitya** | Monks' residence / prayer hall |
| Stupa type | Content |
|---|---|
| **Dhatugarbha** | Relics – **Amaravati, Bhattiprolu, Jaggayyapeta**, Nagarjunakonda |
| **Paribhogika** | Objects used by the Buddha |
| **Uddeshika** | Commemorative, no relics |
- **Sanchi** (Raisen, MP): built by **Ashoka** (3rd c. BCE); encased in stone under the **Shungas**; **four toranas** by the **Satavahanas**; **UNESCO 1989**.
- **Kesariya** (East Champaran, Bihar): called the **largest stupa** in the world; first built c. Ashoka's time.
- Largest stupa in AP: **Amaravati**.
- **Bharhut** (Satna, MP): stone railing and four toranas like Sanchi (one torana survives); railing traditionally **Shunga**; found by **Cunningham, 1873**.
- **Devnimori** (near **Shamlaji**, Aravalli district, **Gujarat**): Mahastupa with a green-schist **relic casket**; inscription in **Brahmi script, Sanskrit**; site now under the **Shyam Sarovar** waters.
- **Feb 2026**: the **Devnimori relics** were taken to **Sri Lanka** (Colombo) for public veneration.
- **Dhamek** (**Sarnath**, UP): marks the place of the **first sermon**; present structure of the **Gupta** period.
- **Aniconic art**: early Buddhism showed the Buddha by symbols – **empty throne**, **footprints**, **Bodhi tree**, **wheel**, **stupa** – not in human form.

### 10. BUDDHISM IN ANDHRA
- **Mahadeva Bhikshu**: at the 2nd council; his **five questions** triggered the schism; led the Mahasanghikas; began **Chaitikavada** in Andhra.
- **Dhanyakataka** = **birthplace of the Mahasanghika** sect; **Andhakas** attended the 3rd council (Kathavatthu).
- **Vinayavatthu** mentions the **"Andhrapatha"**.
- **9 of the 18** early Buddhist sects existed in Andhra.
| Site | Branch / yana |
|---|---|
| **Amaravati** | Hinayana; **Purvasaila** |
| **Nagarjunakonda** | **Aparasaila** |
| **Gudivada** | **Siddhadhika** |
| **Sriparvata** | **Mahayana** |
| **Salihundam** (Srikakulam, on the **Vamsadhara**) | **Vajrayana** |
- **Bhattiprolu** (Guntur): inscribed relic caskets; crystal relic casket.
- **Thotlakonda, Bavikonda** (Visakhapatnam): hilltop monasteries; Brahmi inscriptions.
- Andhra gave **Nagarjuna, Aryadeva, Buddhaghosa, Dinnaga, Bhavaviveka**.
- **2024–25**: AP plans **2 Buddhist circuits** around its **40** Buddhist monuments.
### 11. SPREAD ABROAD AND DECLINE IN INDIA
| Region | How Buddhism reached |
|---|---|
| **Sri Lanka** | Ashoka's mission – **Mahendra, Sanghamitra**; Andhra–Lanka ties via Nagarjunakonda |
| **Tibet** | Mahayana in the **7th c.**; merged with Vajrayana and Bon; **Gelug** school dominant by the 15th c. |
| Central Asia, China, SE Asia | **Kushan-era Silk Route** and sea trade |
- **Decline**: corruption of the Sangha; **Sanskrit replaced Pali**; Brahmanical–**Bhakti** revival; loss of royal patronage; **Turkish raids** (Nalanda sacked by Bakhtiyar Khalji).
- **Odisha's Diamond Triangle** – **Ratnagiri, Udayagiri, Lalitagiri** (Jajpur–Cuttack): on UNESCO's Tentative List; shows all three yanas.
- India has 7 of the 8 Ashtamahasthanas but gets only about **6%** of Buddhist foreign tourists.
