## U0284 | RIGHT TO FREEDOM – ARTICLE 19 (SIX FREEDOMS) AND REASONABLE RESTRICTIONS
### 1. ARTICLE 19 – THE SIX FREEDOMS
- **Right to Freedom** group = **Articles 19-22** (four Articles); Art. 14 belongs to the Equality group.
- Art. 20: protection in respect of conviction; Art. 21: life and personal liberty; **21A** (86th, 2002): education; Art. 22: arrest and detention – preventive detention without an **Advisory Board**: max **three months**.
#### 1.1 Clause → Freedom
| Clause | Freedom | Restricted by |
|---|---|---|
| 19(1)(a) | **Speech and expression** (right to information read in) | 19(2) |
| 19(1)(b) | **Assemble peaceably and without arms** | 19(3) |
| 19(1)(c) | **Associations, unions, co-operative societies** (co-ops added by **97th, 2011**) | 19(4) |
| 19(1)(d) | **Move freely** throughout India | 19(5) |
| 19(1)(e) | **Reside and settle** in any part of India | 19(5) |
| 19(1)(f) | Property – **deleted by 44th Amendment (1978)** | — |
| 19(1)(g) | **Profession, occupation, trade or business** | 19(6) |
- **Seven freedoms originally → six now** (APPSC 2018: "Which Article gives six freedoms?" → Art. 19).
- **Equal opportunity** and the freedom to **own, acquire and dispose of property** are **not** in 19(1).
- **97th Amendment (2011)**: constitutional status to co-operative societies; also **Article 43B** and **Part IXB**. 7th, 44th, 97th did not touch 19(6).
!TRAP (c) co-operatives were ADDED by the 97th; (f) property was DELETED by the 44th.
### 2. AVAILABILITY
- **Citizens only** – not foreigners, not a company as a legal person (citizen shareholders may sue).
- A **British citizen** in India cannot claim **19(1)(g)**. Citizens only, not aliens: Art. 19 and minority rights (Art. 29).
- Available against **State action**; private violation → ordinary remedy. But **Kaushal Kishor (2023)**: Arts. 19 and 21 enforceable against **non-State actors** too.
- A **detained/imprisoned person keeps freedom of expression** (may write/publish books) though movement is lost (APPSC G1 2018).
- In force from **26 Jan 1950** (APPSC 2018).
### 3. FREEDOM OF SPEECH – IMPLIED RIGHTS
| Implied right | Case |
|---|---|
| **Press** and circulation | Romesh Thappar (1950); **Sakal Papers (1961)**; Indian Express (1985) |
| **Hoisting the National Flag** (also on private buildings) | **Naveen Jindal (2004)** |
| **Right to silence** (not singing the anthem) | **Bijoe Emmanuel v. Kerala (1986)** – speech and conscience |
| **Right to information** / to know | State of UP v. Raj Narain (1975); voters' right to know candidates, NOTA, ballot secrecy |
| **Commercial speech** | **Tata Press (1995)** |
| Speech and trade over the **internet** | **Anuradha Bhasin (2020)** – 19(1)(a), 19(1)(g) |
| Telecast; no govt monopoly on airwaves | Cricket Association of Bengal (1995) |
| Against phone tapping | **PUCL (1997)** – safeguards |
- **Freedom of the press is not named directly**; it is read into **19(1)(a)** (APPSC 2019). RTI is not mentioned in the Constitution.
- Speech is protected **irrespective of the medium** – press, TV, internet (APPSC 2019).
- **Films may be pre-censored**, unlike the press (larger audiences); neither has absolute protection.
- **Right to travel abroad = Article 21** (Maneka Gandhi, 1978), not 19(1)(d).
### 4. REASONABLE RESTRICTIONS – 19(2) TO 19(6)
| Clause | Freedom | Grounds |
|---|---|---|
| 19(2) | Speech | **Eight**: sovereignty and integrity, security of State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence |
| 19(3) | Assembly | Sovereignty and integrity; public order (not morality, not security of State) |
| 19(4) | Association | Sovereignty and integrity; public order; morality |
| 19(5) | Movement, residence | General public interest; **protection of Scheduled Tribes** |
| 19(6) | Profession, trade | General public interest; **professional/technical qualifications**; **State trade or monopoly** |
- **Not a 19(2) ground**: **sedition**, "general public interest", economic grounds, unbecoming criticism. 19(2) grounds are **exhaustive** (Kaushal Kishor, 2023 – no extra limits on ministers' speech).
- Count: **11** reasonable-restriction heads across Art. 19 (2026).
#### 4.1 Grounds Added by Amendments
| Amendment | Added |
|---|---|
| **1st (1951)** | Public order, friendly relations, incitement; rewrote **19(6)** – qualifications and **State monopoly** (protecting nationalisation) |
| **16th (1963)** | **Sovereignty and integrity of India** in 19(2), (3), (4) |
#### 4.2 Test of Reasonableness
- **State of Madras v. V.G. Row (1952)**: nature of the right, purpose, extent and urgency of the evil, **proportionality**.
- **Courts** decide; restrictions must not **eclipse the right**; weigh public interest and prevailing social values.
### 5. SPEECH AND PRESS CASES
| Case | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Romesh Thappar v. Madras (1950)** | Ban on journal **Cross Roads** struck; "public order" then not a ground → **1st Amendment** |
| **Kedar Nath Singh (1962)** | Sedition (s.124A IPC) valid only for **incitement to violence** |
| **Bennett Coleman (1972)** | **Newsprint policy** fixing pages violated press freedom |
| Aveek Sarkar (2014) | **"Community standards"** test for obscenity |
| **Shreya Singhal (2015)** | **Sec. 66A IT Act** struck – vague, overbroad |
| **Anuradha Bhasin (2020)** | Internet shutdowns must be **proportionate, temporary, published** |
!TRAP Sakal Papers (press) was 1961; Kedar Nath (sedition) was 1962.
### 6. ASSEMBLY, ASSOCIATION AND STRIKES
- Assembly must be **peaceful and unarmed**; excludes violent or armed assembly. **Gherao** is not peaceful assembly; 19(3) allows public-order limits.
- Right to protest is read from 19(1)(a), (b) and 21. **22 May 2026**: SC – peaceful protest is everybody's right but must not occupy public space or disturb citizens.
| Action | Status | Case |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstration | Protected; strike not | **Kameshwar Prasad (1962)** |
| Strike | **Not an FR – only a legal right** | All India Bank Employees' Assn (1962) |
| Strike by govt employees | No right | **T.K. Rangarajan (2003)** |
| **Bandh** | **Illegal** – curbs others' freedoms | **CPI(M) v. Bharat Kumar** |
- **FCRA**: Centre defends curbs under **19(4)** (25 Jun 2026); INSAF (2020) – only associations in active politics barred from foreign funds.
### 7. MOVEMENT, RESIDENCE AND PROFESSION
- 19(1)(d): internal movement; 19(1)(e): reside and settle. Under **19(5)**, outsiders' entry and settlement may be limited to protect **Scheduled Tribes'** culture, land and property (Inner Line areas).
- **19(6)**: State may prescribe **professional or technical qualifications** and run any trade **to the exclusion of citizens** (State monopoly), inserted by the 1st Amendment.
- **No right to a dangerous or immoral trade** (e.g. trafficking).
### 8. ARTICLE 19 DURING EMERGENCY – 358 vs 359
| Article 358 | Article 359 |
|---|---|
| Art. 19 suspended **automatically** | Needs a **Presidential order** |
| Only for emergency on **war or external aggression** (44th Amendment), not armed rebellion | Suspends only **enforcement** of the rights named |
| Covers only Art. 19 | **Arts. 20 and 21 can never be suspended** (44th) |
### 9. NEW CRIMINAL LAWS (FROM 1 JULY 2024)
- **BNS, 2023** replaced the IPC (organised crime, mob lynching); **BNSS, 2023** replaced CrPC (statutory **Zero FIR**, video recording of search and seizure).
| Subject | Old | New |
|---|---|---|
| Sedition | s.124A IPC (in abeyance 2022) | **s.152 BNS** – endangering sovereignty, unity, integrity |
| Criminal defamation | IPC 499-500 | **s.356 BNS** |
| Hate speech | — | **s.196 BNS** |
| Unlawful assembly (five or more) | IPC s.141 | **s.189 BNS** |
| Prohibitory orders | CrPC s.144 | **s.163 BNSS** |
