## U0033 | PERSIAN AND MACEDONIAN INVASIONS – ALEXANDER'S CAMPAIGN, BATTLE OF HYDASPES, EFFECTS ON INDIA
### 1. ACHAEMENID (PERSIAN) INVASION
| Ruler | Indian territory |
|---|---|
| **Cyrus** (founder, c. 558–530 BC) | **First** Achaemenid to **campaign** into the Indian north-west (**Gandhara**) |
| **Darius I** (522–486 BC) | **First Persian ruler to capture/annex** Indian land: Indus lands, **Punjab and Sindh west of the Indus** = **20th satrapy** |
- **Herodotus:** the most populous, fertile satrapy; paid **360 talents of gold dust** = **one-third** of the empire's revenue.
!TRAP First to campaign = Cyrus; first to annex (20th satrapy) = Darius I.
### 2. PERSIAN INFLUENCE

### 3. INDIA ON THE EVE OF ALEXANDER
- **North-west**: small states (**Taxila, Porus's Punjab, Gandhara**) – no united resistance.
- **Magadha:** **Nanda** dynasty under **Dhana Nanda** (Greek **Agrammes**), last ruler; commander **Bhaddasala**; unpopular for **heavy taxation**.
- **Causes of success**: political disunity; superior army; help from Indian allies like **Ambhi**.

### 6. BATTLE OF HYDASPES
- **Hydaspes** = Greek name of the **Jhelum** (Sanskrit **Vitasta**); **326 BC**; Alexander defeated **Porus** (UPPSC 2013).
- Porus was **not executed**: impressed, Alexander **restored and enlarged** his kingdom.
- **Bucephala** (after his horse) and **Nicaea** founded nearby.
| Battle | Year | Between | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ten Kings (Dasharajnya)** | – | **Sudas** (Bharatas) vs ten kings | Sudas won; **Rigveda Mandala VII**, near the **Parushni (Ravi)** |
| **Kalinga War** | **261 BC** | Ashoka vs Kalinga | Turned Ashoka to Buddhism and Dhamma |
### 7. RETREAT AT THE BEAS
- Army refused to go beyond the **Beas (Hyphasis)** – not the Ravi; **12 stone altars** on its northern bank.
- Causes: **war weariness, homesickness, disease, fear of the Nanda army**.
- Withdrew down the **Indus**; **Nearchus** led the fleet.
!PAIR Hydaspes | Jhelum | Vitasta || Hyphasis | Beas | Halt point

### 9. EFFECTS OF THE INVASION
- Conquered area split into **three parts under Greek governors**; most states returned to rulers.
- **Direct contact** and **new land and sea routes** between Greece and India.
- Small NW states weakened – helped **Chandragupta Maurya**, who expelled the Greek satraps with **Porus (Parvataka)** as ally.
- **Sandrokottas** = Chandragupta; fixes Mauryan dates (**c. 322 BCE**); invasion = **first dated event** of Indian history.
