## U0471 | DEMOGRAPHIC TRANSITION THEORY AND INDIA'S POSITION; DEMOGRAPHIC DIVIDEND AND ITS WINDOW
### 1. POPULATION THEORIES → PROPOUNDER
| Theory | Propounder | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Principle of Population (1798) | **T.R. Malthus** | Population grows **geometrically**, food **arithmetically**; **positive checks** raise deaths, **preventive checks** (late marriage, restraint) lower births |
| **Demographic transition** | **Warren Thompson** (1929, early form); **Frank Notestein** (1945, standard form) | Birth and death rates fall as societies develop |
| **Optimum population** | **Edwin Cannan** | Population giving the **highest income per head** |
| **The Population Bomb** (1968) | **Paul Ehrlich** | Neo-Malthusian warning |
- **Demographic transition** = slow change in the pattern of population growth over a long period.
### 2. DEMOGRAPHIC TRANSITION MODEL
| Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | High | High | Low growth |
| II | High | **Falling** | **Population explosion** |
| III | Falling | Low | Growth slows |
| IV | Low | Low | Stable, low growth |
- Order with development: **high birth & high death → high birth & low death → low birth & low death**.
- **India: late Stage 3, moving into Stage 4**; **Andhra Pradesh: Stage 4** (low fertility).
- **Stable population**: birth rate = death rate and no migration.
- **Urbanisation reduced both birth and death rates** (education, work, medical care).
- Low birth rates / ageing / declining population in news: **Italy, Japan, South Korea**.
!TRAP India is NOT in Stage 4 fully; AP is.
### 3. KEY TERMS
| Term | Meaning / base |
|---|---|
| **CBR / CDR** | Births / deaths per **1,000 mid-year population** |
| **IMR** | Infant deaths (before age **1**) per **1,000 live births** |
| **MMR** (maternal mortality ratio) | Maternal deaths per **1,00,000 live births** |
| Maternal mortality rate | Per women aged **15-49** |
| **TFR** | Total live births an imagined woman would have through the reproductive ages at current age-specific fertility rates |
| **Dependency ratio** | **(0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64)** |
| Working age | **15-64**; young dependents **below 15**; old **65+** |
| Youth (**NYP 2014**) | **15-29** years; approved **9 Jan 2014**, replaced NYP 2003 |
!TRAP MMR is per 1,00,000 live births, not per 1,000.

### 6. DEMOGRAPHIC DIVIDEND AND ITS WINDOW
#### 6.1 Meaning
- **Demographic dividend**: growth potential from a high share of the **15-64** working-age group.
- **Demographic bonus** (around 2016): jump in the productive-age population.
- Working age **15-64 = 62.9%**; aged 20+ (Census 2011) **59.29%**; under 14 about **29% (28.67%)**; above 65 **5-6%** (4.8% in Census 2011).
#### 6.2 Window
- India's window: **2005-06 to 2055-56**.
- Peak: around **2041**, when the 20-59 share reaches about **59%** (Economic Survey 2018-19).
- 0-19 share: **41% (2011) → 25% (2041)**; 60+ share **8.6% → 16%**; **20.5% (325.3 mn) by 2051**.
### 7. DEPENDENCY RATIO
- Lower ratio = fewer dependants per worker → **potential dividend** (UPSC).
- Falling mainly due to **fall in fertility**; the working-age (15-59) group is relatively high.
- Fell **79% (1975) → 58% (2008) → about 48% (2021)**.
- **Old-age dependency ratio (2011): 14.2%** (60+ relative to 15-59).
- Dependent population in the pyramid: **0-14** (and 65+).
### 8. CONDITIONS TO HARNESS THE DIVIDEND
- Need **health, education, skills and adequate jobs**; otherwise a **"demographic nightmare"** (Economic Survey 2018-19).
- Full benefit needs **skill development** (closing the skill demand-supply gap).
- **Female LFPR**: 23.3% (2017-18) → **41.7% (2023-24)**; cutting it would weaken the dividend.
- Highest economically active women share of working-age women (1996, options): **China**.
### 9. INTER-STATE DIVERGENCE AND ANDHRA PRADESH
#### 9.1 RBI State Typology
| Phase | States | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Ageing** | **Kerala, Tamil Nadu** | Working-age share already falling; about 30% of social spending on pensions |
| **Wide window (youthful)** | **Bihar, Uttar Pradesh** | Long dividend ahead |
| **Narrowing** | **Telangana, Uttarakhand** | Intermediate |
- **Kerala 60+ = 22.8% by 2036** → end of its dividend phase.
- Kerala's lowest population growth: heavy investment in **literacy** (90.9% then; 94% in 2011, female 92.1%).
#### 9.2 Determinants of Birth Rate
- **Female education and higher age at marriage**; **child survival** (lower IMR); **urbanisation** and cost of children; **contraception** access.
- High literacy linked to low birth rate; urbanisation lowers birth rate (both correct).
- Rising **Couple Protection Rate** cuts birth rate most when couples are **younger**.
- More male children born each year; infant mortality among boys is higher than girls.
- Low sex ratio causes: high maternal deaths, female child deaths, female foeticide, more male births (about **105 boys per 100 girls**).
#### 9.3 Andhra Pradesh
- Decadal growth: peak **21.13% (1981-91)** → **9.21% (2001-11)** vs India 17.72%; literacy 67.35%.
- **Median age 32.5** (India 28); by 2036 under-14 share 25% → 15%; 60+ **10% → 19%**.
- AP is not in the wide-window phase like Bihar and UP.
