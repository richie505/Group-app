## U0001 | SOURCES OF ANCIENT INDIAN HISTORY – ARCHAEOLOGICAL, LITERARY, FOREIGN ACCOUNTS; DATING METHODS
### 1. SOURCES – TYPES AND RELIABILITY
- Three groups of sources: **archaeological** (inscriptions, coins, monuments, excavated sites), **literary** (religious and secular texts) and **foreign accounts**.
- **Inscriptions** are the **most reliable** source.
- Foreign accounts **supplement** Indian sources and fill gaps where Indian texts are silent; cross-check them with inscriptions and archaeology.

### 3. RELIGIOUS LITERATURE AS A SOURCE
#### 3.1 Vedic, Buddhist and Jain Texts
| Text | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| **Aitareya Brahmana** (Vedic) | **Earliest literary reference to Andhras** (Sunahshepa story – Vishwamitra's disowned sons) |
| **Vinayavatthu** | First Buddhist mention of "**Andhrapatha**" |
| Bhimasena, **Serivanija** Jatakas | **Andhranagari** = Dhanyakataka; P. C. Ray saw its river as the Krishna |
| **Chulakalinga Jataka** | Kalinga–Assaka conflict; Assaka capital Potali |
| Kathavatthu | Andhakas as an important Buddhist branch |
| **Milindapanho** | Dialogue of Indo-Greek **Menander** (Milinda) with monk **Nagasena** |
| **Dipavamsa, Mahavamsa** | Sri Lankan Pali chronicles; Mauryan history; Andhra Buddhism; date the Sangam age |
| Nayadhammakahao (Jain) | Ikshvaku ruler Yashodhara and son Dhanada |
| Pitakas (Tripitaka), Dighanikaya | Other Buddhist texts |
| Kalpasutra; Fourteen Purvas | Jain texts; Purvas = earliest Jain holy book |
| Smritis (Narada, Vishnu, Brihaspati, Katyayana) | Varna, law, inheritance in the Gupta age |
- Earliest Jain literature was in **Ardha-Magadhi** (twelve Angas), finally collected in the 6th c. AD at **Vallabhi**.
- Mahabharata: Andhras fought for the **Kauravas**. Buddhist name for the region: **Andhakaratta**.
!TRAP Milinda-panha is linked to Nagasena, not Nagarjuna (a later Mahayana, Madhyamika thinker).
### 4. FOREIGN ACCOUNTS
#### 4.1 Travellers and Writers in Chronological Order
| Order | Writer / work | Country, language | Ruler / period | Key content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Megasthenes – Indica** | Greece, Greek | **Chandragupta Maurya**; envoy of Seleucus Nikator; 4th c. BCE | Pataliputra; Brahmins and Shramanas; **30 Andhra forts**, 1,00,000 infantry |
| 2 | **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** | Anonymous Greek sailor | c. 60 CE | Andhra–Rome trade; **Koddura** only east-coast port; Arikamedu; Saka–Satavahana conflict |
| 3 | **Pliny – Naturalis Historia** | Rome, Latin | 1st c. CE | **Roman gold drain** to India, about 50 million sesterces a year |
| 4 | **Ptolemy – Geographia** | Greek | 2nd c. CE | **Masulia (Machilipatnam)** textile trade centre; Ben Ra (Vengi) |
| 5 | **Fa-Hien** | China | **Chandragupta II** | Buddhist pilgrimage; Gupta prosperity |
| 6 | **Hiuen Tsang – Si-Yu-Ki** | China, 7th c. CE | **Harsha** and **Pulakeshin II** | "King of Travellers"; Nalanda; Sriparvata vihara lacked patrons |
| 7 | **I-Tsing** | China | 671–695 CE, after Harsha | Landed at **Tamralipti**; studied at Nalanda |
| 8 | **Al-Biruni – Kitab-ul-Hind** | Khwarizm, **Arabic** | Came with **Mahmud of Ghazni**, 11th c. | Hindu religion, Puranas, Sanskrit, Yoga; Kalachuri Gangeyadeva of Tripuri |
- Others: Al-Masudi (10th c., Multan Sun Temple); Ibn Battuta (Muhammad bin Tughlaq); Strabo, Arrian, Deimachus. Chinese writers called India "**Yin-tu**".
!TRAP The wrong pair in 2018 and 2019 was I-Tsing – Ashoka. Al-Biruni wrote in Arabic, not Persian.
!KEY Megasthenes = 30 fortified towns of the Andhras (80 is only a text variant).

### 6. AP SOURCES AND EARLIEST REFERENCES TO ANDHRAS
#### 6.1 AP Inscriptions and Sites
- **Bhattiprolu**: Raja Kubhiraka, 3rd c. BC; **Nigama-sabha** (guild assembly); Roman coins found.
- **Amaravati** and **Nagarjunakonda** (Ikshvaku): inscriptions and stupas are key sources.
- **Alluru**: many donations to communities and institutions, including bullock carts to Buddhists.
- **Yerragudi** (Kurnool): Ashokan edicts prove Mauryan rule in Andhra; **13th Major Rock Edict** lists the Andhras.
- All Satavahana inscriptions: **Prakrit**, Brahmi script.
#### 6.2 Andhra Firsts
| First / earliest | Source |
|---|---|
| Literary reference to Andhras | Aitareya Brahmana |
| "Andhrapatha" in a Buddhist text | Vinayavatthu |
| "Andhrapatha" in an inscription | **Maidavolu** plates of Pallava Sivaskandavarma, c. 300 CE |
| Foreign mention of Andhra forts | Megasthenes |
| First Telugu word | "**Nagabu**" (Satavahana) |
| First Telugu inscription | **Kalamalla** (Renati Choda) |
| First mention of Kakatiyas | Magallu inscription |
- Latest: field site from Satavahana to Vijayanagara in the **Nallamala Tiger Reserve**, Yerragondapalem region (14 Jun 2026).
### 7. SCRIPTS AND DECIPHERMENT
| Script | Use / region | Decipherment |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmi** | Ashoka's edicts in Prakrit; parent of Telugu | **James Prinsep, 1837** |
| **Kharosthi** | **Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra**; **right to left** | Prinsep, Lassen, Norris, 1830s, using bilingual Indo-Greek coins |
| Greek and Aramaic | Ashoka at Kandahar | – |
| **Tamil-Brahmi** | Earliest Tamil script; Mangulam, Sittanavasal, Arittapatti | c. 3rd c. BCE |
- Ashoka's edicts are the **earliest decipherable inscriptions**; earliest stone inscriptions are in **Prakrit**.
- Pre-Ashokan Brahmi: Anuradhapura, Piparaha, Sohgaura, Mahasthan.
- Latest: Tamil-Brahmi traders' names found in Egypt's **Valley of the Kings** (Apr 2026).
### 8. COINS AS A SOURCE
| Series | Point to remember |
|---|---|
| **Punch-marked** | **Earliest extant coins**, mostly silver |
| **Indo-Greek** | First with **rulers' names and portraits** |
| Kushana | Large gold issues |
| **Gupta** | **Largest number** of gold coins (dinaras) |
| Satavahana | **Ship-motif** coins (Yajna Sri, Vasisthiputra Pulumavi); lead, copper, potin; **no nickel**; Karpanamulu copper |
| Ikshvaku | Santamula I gave crores of gold coins |
| Roman | Found at **Bhattiprolu**, **Nagarjunakonda** |
- Earliest textual coin terms: **kahapana, nikkha**, kamsa, kakanika in **Pali texts**, corroborated by punch-marked coins.
- Gautamiputra Satakarni re-struck **Nahapana's** coins. Mada is a Kakatiya coin.
- Latest: ASI excavating **Balirajgarh** (Madhubani, Bihar) found punch-marked coins (Apr 2026).

### 10. DATING METHODS
| Method | Principle |
|---|---|
| **Stratigraphy** | Superposition: lower layer is older |
| **Radiocarbon (C-14)** | C-14 decay in organic remains; half-life about **5,730 years** |
| **Dendrochronology** | Counting annual **tree rings** |
| **Thermoluminescence** | Light released on **heating pottery** |

### 13. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Fa-Hien** = Chandragupta II; **Hiuen Tsang** = Harsha; I-Tsing came after Harsha.
2. **Rudradaman** = Junagadh, not Nanaghat.
3. **Kushana** = large gold issues; **Gupta** = largest number.
4. Satavahanas did **not** use nickel.
5. "Andhrapatha": Vinayavatthu (text) vs Maidavolu (inscription).
6. **Kharosthi** runs right to left.
7. Al-Biruni wrote in **Arabic**.
8. Ptolemy's Masulia was a trade centre, not a Roman army headquarters.
9. Indica is by Megasthenes, not Aryabhata.
