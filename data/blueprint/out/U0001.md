## U0001 | SOURCES OF ANCIENT INDIAN HISTORY – ARCHAEOLOGICAL, LITERARY, FOREIGN ACCOUNTS; DATING METHODS
### 1. SOURCES – TYPES AND RELIABILITY
- Three groups: **archaeological** (inscriptions, coins, monuments, sites), **literary** (religious and secular texts) and **foreign accounts**.
- **Inscriptions** are the **most reliable** source; foreign accounts only **supplement** Indian sources and fill gaps where Indian texts are silent.
- Chinese writers called India "**Yin-tu**" (or Thian-tu), a form of Hindu/Sindhu.

### 3. RELIGIOUS LITERATURE AS A SOURCE
#### 3.1 Vedic, Buddhist and Jain Texts
| Text | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| **Aitareya Brahmana** | **Earliest literary reference to Andhras** (Sunahshepa story – Vishwamitra's rejected sons) |
| **Vinayavatthu** | First Buddhist mention of "**Andhrapatha**" |
| Bhimasena, **Serivanija** Jatakas | **Andhranagari** (Andhakapura) = **Dhanyakataka**; P. C. Ray saw its river as the Krishna |
| **Chulakalinga Jataka** | Kalinga–Assaka conflict; Assaka capital Potali |
| **Milindapanho** | Dialogue of Indo-Greek **Menander I (Milinda)** with monk **Nagasena** |
| **Dipavamsa, Mahavamsa** | Sri Lankan Pali chronicles; Mauryan history; Andhra Buddhism |
| **Nayadhammakahao** (Jain) | Ikshvaku ruler **Yashodhara** and son **Dhanada** |
- Other Buddhist texts: Jatakas, Dighanikaya, Pitakas (Tripitaka). Jain: Kalpasutra.
- Earliest Jain holy book: **Fourteen Purvas**; earliest Jain literature in **Ardha-Magadhi** (twelve Angas), finally collected 6th c. AD at **Vallabhi**.
- Mahabharata: Andhras fought for the Kauravas. Buddhist name for the region: "**Andhakaratta**".
!TRAP Milindapanho is a dialogue in which Nagasena takes part (not Nagarjuna, a later Mahayana Madhyamika thinker).
### 4. INSCRIPTIONS
#### 4.1 Early and Post-Mauryan
| Inscription | Ruler / issuer | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Boghaz-Koi** (Asia Minor, Turkey) | – | 14th c. BC; names **Indra, Varuna, Mitra, Nasatya**; Aryan movement from Iran to India |
| **Sohgaura** copper plate (Gorakhpur, UP) | Mauryan | **Earliest known copper plate**; earliest royal order to preserve food grains for famine |
| Mahasthan (Bogura, Bangladesh) | – | Famine relief |
| **Junagadh/Girnar** (c. 150 AD) | **Rudradaman I** (Saka) | **Earliest perfect Sanskrit**, first long Sanskrit inscription; first **Kavya style**; Brahmi; repair of **Sudarshana lake** without forced labour; conflict with Satavahanas |
| **Hathigumpha** (Udayagiri, near Bhubaneswar) | **Kharavela**, Chedi/Mahameghavahana | Prakrit, Brahmi; undated, damaged; salutes the Jinas; 13 years of reign; **Nanda** canal (Mahapadmananda); wars incl. against Satakarni; names Tamil kingdoms; Krishna river = "**Kannabenna**" |
| Ayodhya (of Dhanadeva) | Pushyamitra Shunga | Two Ashvamedha sacrifices |
| Sarnath image | Kanishka | 81 AD, his 3rd year |
| **Rabatak** (Afghanistan, found 1993) | Kanishka | **Bactrian language, Greek script**; names Saket, Kaushambi, Pataliputra, Sri Champa – not Shravasti |
- Junagadh rock also carries **Ashoka's 14 edicts** (boulder near Uparkot fort) and records of **Skandagupta**; it proves **Chandragupta Maurya's** rule in western India and mentions both Chandragupta and Ashoka. The lake was first built by Mauryan governor **Pushyagupta**.
#### 4.2 Satavahana and Andhra Records
| Inscription | Issuer | Records |
|---|---|---|
| **Nanaghat/Naneghat** | **Queen Naganika** | Satakarni I; early Satavahana history; label "**Rajan Simuka Satavahana**" – first ruler titled Rajan |
| **Nasik Prashasti** | **Gautami Balashri** | Gautamiputra Satakarni = "**Ekabrahmana**"; destroyer of Shakas, Yavanas, Pahlavas |
| **Myakadoni** (Bellary) | **Pulumavi III** | Basis of the **Karnataka-homeland** theory |
| **Bhattiprolu** | Raja **Kubhiraka**, 3rd c. BC | **Nigama-sabha** (guild assembly); Roman coins found |
| **Alluru** | – | Many donations to communities and institutions; Buddhist community got bullock carts |
| **Maski** (Karnataka) | Ashoka | First edict naming "**Asoka**"; "**Gulmika**" = village headman; mentions the Kalinga victory |
| 13th Major Rock Edict | Ashoka | Lists Andhras inside the Mauryan empire |
| **Yerragudi** (Kurnool) | Ashoka | Proves Mauryan rule in Andhra |
| Maidavolu plates | Pallava **Sivaskandavarma** | "Andhrapatha" first in an inscription (Prakrit, c. 300 CE) |
- All Satavahana inscriptions: **Prakrit language, Brahmi script**. Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda inscriptions and stupas (Ikshvaku) are key AP sources.
- **Kalamalla**: first Telugu inscription (Renati Chodas). **Magallu**: first to mention the Kakatiyas. **Kazipet**: Beta Raja I the first Kakatiya king. First Telugu word "**Nagabu**" (Satavahana period).
!PAIR "Andhrapatha" | Vinayavatthu | first in Buddhist text || "Andhrapatha" | Maidavolu plates | first in inscription
#### 4.3 Gupta and Chalukya
| Inscription | Issuer / composer | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Allahabad Pillar** (Prayaga Prashasti) | **Harisena** for **Samudragupta** | Conquests; Sanskrit |
| **Bhitari** | **Skandagupta** | Repulse of the **Hunas** |
| **Aihole** | **Ravikirti** for **Pulakeshin II** | Harsha stopped at the **Narmada**; Sanskrit, Southern Brahmi |
| Lohnara | Pulakeshin II, 630 AD | Does NOT mention Harsha |
- Dashpur inscription: oldest advertisement tradition in India.
!TRAP Composer vs king: Harisena → Samudragupta; Ravikirti → Pulakeshin II; Gautami Balashri → Gautamiputra Satakarni.
### 5. SCRIPTS AND DECIPHERMENT
| Script | Use / region | Decipherment |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmi** | Most Ashokan edicts, Prakrit; parent of Telugu script | **James Prinsep, 1837**, from Ashokan inscriptions |
| **Kharosthi** | **Right to left**; only Ashokan **Shahbazgarhi** and **Mansehra** (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, near Peshawar) | Prinsep (1835) and others (Lassen, Norris), using **bilingual Indo-Greek coins** |
| Greek and Aramaic | Ashoka at Kandahar | – |
| **Tamil-Brahmi** (Tamizhi) | Earliest Tamil script, c. 3rd c. BCE | Sites: **Mangulam, Sittanavasal, Arittapatti** (Pandya region) |
- Ashokan edicts = **first decipherable inscriptions** (Prakrit in Brahmi); **Prakrit** = language of the earliest stone inscriptions and of the earliest AP inscriptions.
- **Pre-Ashokan Brahmi**: Anuradhapura (Sri Lanka), Piparaha, Sohgaura, Mahasthan.
### 6. COINS AS A SOURCE
| Series | Point |
|---|---|
| **Punch-marked** (silver) | **Earliest extant** Indian coins |
| **Indo-Greek** | First with **rulers' names and portraits** |
| **Kushana** | Large gold issues |
| **Gupta** | **Largest number** of gold coins (dinaras) |
| **Satavahana** | **Ship-motif** coins of **Yajna Sri** and Vasishthiputra Pulumavi = sea trade; lead, copper, bronze, potin; **Karpanamulu** = copper coins; **no nickel**; Gautamiputra re-struck **Nahapana's** coins |
| Ikshvaku | **Santamula I** gave away crores of gold coins |
| Kakatiya | Gold **Mada** (not Satavahana); Nishkam, Tankam, Varaha |
| Roman | Found at **Bhattiprolu** and **Nagarjunakonda** |
- First clear textual coin terms: **kahapana, nikkha, kamsa, kakanika** in **Pali** texts, corroborated by punch-marked coins.
- Apr 2026: ASI began excavating **Balirajgarh** (Madhubani, Bihar) – punch-marked coins, Mauryan-to-Pala habitation.
### 7. FOREIGN ACCOUNTS
#### 7.1 Greek and Roman Writers
| Order | Writer / work | Country, language, period | Key content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Megasthenes – Indica** | Greek; 4th c. BCE; envoy of **Seleucus Nikator** to **Chandragupta Maurya**, lived at Pataliputra | Brahmins and Shramanas; first foreign work on Andhras: **30 fortified towns**, **1,00,000 infantry**; gold-digging ants |
| 2 | **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** | Anonymous Greek sailor; c. 60 CE | Andhra–Rome trade; **Arikamedu** Roman market; **Koddura** only east-coast port; **Saka–Satavahana conflict** |
| 3 | **Pliny – Natural History** | Rome, **Latin**; 1st c. CE | Roman gold drain to India, about **50 million sesterces** a year, for spices, pearls, muslin |
| 4 | **Ptolemy – Geographia** | Greek; 2nd c. CE | **Masulia/Maisolia = Machilipatnam**, great textile market (not an army HQ); **Ben Ra (Vengi)** |
!TRAP Indica is by Megasthenes, not Aryabhata. Megasthenes' forts = 30, not 80 (80 is only a text variant).
#### 7.2 Chinese, Arab and Later Visitors
| Order | Traveller / work | Ruler / period | Key content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Fa-Hien** (Faxian) | **Chandragupta II** "Vikramaditya" | Buddhist pilgrimage; Gupta prosperity |
| 6 | **Hiuen Tsang – Si-Yu-Ki** | **Harsha** + **Pulakeshin II**; 7th c. CE | Studied at Nalanda; Mahayana; "King of Travellers"; **Sriparvata vihara lacked patrons** |
| 7 | **I-Tsing** | **671–695 CE**, after Harsha | Landed at **Tamralipti**; Nalanda; monk life and relics |
| – | Al-Masudi | 10th c. | Multan Sun Temple |
| 8 | **Al-Biruni – Kitab-ul-Hind** (Tahqiq-i-Hind) | Khwarizm; **Arabic** (not Persian); came with **Mahmud of Ghazni**, 11th c. | Hindu religion, Puranas, Sanskrit, Yoga; Kalachuri **Gangeyadeva** and capital **Tripuri** |
| – | Ibn Battuta – Rihla | Delhi 1333; qazi under Muhammad bin Tughlaq | Sultanate |
| – | Matwalin | Chinese | China–India relations under the Chalukyas |
- Order: **Megasthenes → Fa-Hien → Hiuen Tsang → I-Tsing → Al-Biruni**.
!TRAP Wrong pair in 2018 and 2019: I-Tsing – Ashoka. I-Tsing came in the late 7th c.

### 9. DATING METHODS
| Method | Principle |
|---|---|
| **Stratigraphy** | Superposition: lower layer is older |
| **Radiocarbon (C-14)** | Decay of C-14 in organic remains; half-life about **5,730 years** |
| **Dendrochronology** | Counting annual **tree rings** |
| **Thermoluminescence** | Light given on heating pottery/burnt material |

### 12. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Rudradaman = Junagadh**, not Nanaghat; Naganika = Nanaghat.
2. **Fa-Hien = Chandragupta II; Hiuen Tsang = Harsha + Pulakeshin II**; I-Tsing never saw Ashoka.
3. **Al-Biruni wrote in Arabic**, not Persian.
4. **Kushana = large gold issues; Gupta = largest number** of gold coins.
5. Satavahanas used **no nickel**; Mada is a **Kakatiya** coin.
6. **Kharosthi runs right to left**, only at Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra.
7. Rabatak mentions **Sri Champa, not Shravasti**; Bactrian in Greek script.
8. Lohnara inscription does **not** mention Harsha; Aihole does.
9. Devichandraguptam is by **Vishakhadatta, not Bilhana**; Samarangana Sutradhara by **Bhoja**.
10. Ptolemy's Maisolia was a **trade centre**, not a Roman army headquarters.
