## U0400 | NHRC AND SHRCs – COMPOSITION, POWERS, FUNCTIONS AND LIMITATIONS
### 1. STATUS AND ESTABLISHMENT
- **NHRC** = **statutory** body under the **Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA), 1993**; **not constitutional**.
- Framed in the light of the **Paris Principles** (UNGA **1993**): independence, pluralism, broad mandate.
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Ordinance; Act deemed in force | **28 September 1993** |
| NHRC constituted | **12 October 1993** |
| Act received assent | **January 1994** |
- **SHRC is optional**: a State Government "**may**" constitute one under PHRA 1993.
!TRAP APPSC-GS 2023: "NHRC is a constitutional body" and "established in 1994" are **both false**. APPSC-G2 2025: constituted in **1993**.
!PAIR NHRC | statutory | PHRA 1993 || UPSC, CAG | constitutional | Constitution

### 2. COMPOSITION – NHRC
| Component | Who |
|---|---|
| **Chairperson** | Former **CJI** or (since **2019**) a **Supreme Court Judge**; before 2019 only a former CJI |
| Member | One former or serving **SC Judge** |
| Member | One former or serving **HC Chief Justice or HC Judge** |
| Members | **Three** persons with human-rights knowledge, **at least one woman** (earlier two) |
| **Deemed members** | Chairpersons of other national commissions (7) |
- **Secretary-General**: administrative and financial powers (not judicial) under the Chairperson's control.
#### 2.1 Deemed Members
| Before 2019 (4) | Added in 2019 (3) |
|---|---|
| National Commission for **Minorities** | National Commission for **Backward Classes** |

| National Commission for **STs** (added 2006) | **Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities** |
| National Commission for **Women** | – |
- Deemed members are **not equal in status** to ordinary members.
- **NCBC**: set up 1993 by Act; constitutional status by the **102nd Amendment (2018)**. CCPD works under Sec. 75(1) of the RPwD Act, 2016.
### 3. APPOINTMENT, TERM AND REMOVAL – NHRC
#### 3.1 Selection Committee (Section 4) – Six Members
1. **Prime Minister** – Chairperson
2. **Speaker of Lok Sabha**
3. **Union Home Minister**
4. **Leader of Opposition, Lok Sabha**
5. **Leader of Opposition, Rajya Sabha**
6. **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha**
!TRAP The **Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha (Vice-President) and the **CJI** are NOT members; the **Deputy Chairman** is.
- **Appointed by the President** (APPSC 2025) on the committee's advice. A vacancy in the committee does not invalidate its decisions.
- Sitting SC Judge or sitting HC Chief Justice appointed only **after consulting the CJI**.
#### 3.2 Term, Resignation, Removal
- **Term:** **3 years or age 70**, whichever earlier (since 2019; earlier 5 years); **eligible for reappointment** (5-year limit removed).
- After office: **ineligible** for further jobs under Central or State Govt.
- **Resignation:** in writing to the **President**.
- **Removal by the President:** insolvency, paid outside employment, infirmity of mind or body, unsound mind, conviction for **moral turpitude** (direct removal); **proved misbehaviour or incapacity** after a **Supreme Court inquiry** on the President's reference.
!TRAP "Members are not eligible for re-appointment" is the WRONG statement (MPPSC).
### 4. STATE HUMAN RIGHTS COMMISSION
| Item | SHRC |
|---|---|
| Chairperson | Former **HC Chief Justice or HC Judge** (since 2019; earlier only CJ) |
| Members | **Three** (cut from five, **2006**): Chairperson + HC judge/district judge with 7 years' experience + human-rights expert |
| Selection committee (Section 22) | **Chief Minister** (chair), **Speaker** of Assembly, **State Home Minister**, **LoP** in Assembly; Chairman and LoP of the Council where bicameral |
| Appointed by | **Governor** |
| Removed by | **President** only (same grounds as NHRC) |
| Resignation to | **Governor** (2006) |
| Consultation | Sitting HC Judge or District Judge appointed after consulting the **Chief Justice of the HC** |
| Acting Chairperson | **Governor** authorises a member in a vacancy |
| Annual report | To **State Government** → laid before **State Legislature** with memorandum of action taken and reasons for non-acceptance |
!KEY Both NHRC and SHRC chairs are **removed by the President**; SHRC is appointed by the Governor. HC Chief Justice is only **consulted**, not a committee member.
### 5. NHRC vs SHRC – COMPARISON
| Basis | NHRC | SHRC |
|---|---|---|
| Chair | Former CJI or SC Judge | Former HC CJ or Judge |
| Selection head | Prime Minister | Chief Minister |
| Appointed by | President | Governor |
| Removed by | President | President |
| Resign to | President | Governor |
| Jurisdiction | All subjects | **State List + Concurrent List** |
| Report | Central Govt → **each House of Parliament** | State Govt → State Legislature |
### 6. FUNCTIONS AND POWERS
#### 6.1 Functions (Section 12)
- **Inquiry** suo motu, on petition, or on **court direction** into violations or **negligence by public servants**.
- **Intervene in court proceedings** with the **approval of the court**.
- **Visit jails** and detention centres to study living conditions (since 2006, **without intimating the State Govt**).
- Review constitutional and legal safeguards and factors inhibiting rights (incl. terrorism); study international treaties.
- Promote **research**, human rights **literacy**; **encourage NGOs**.
#### 6.2 Powers
- **Civil court powers (CPC 1908)**: summon and examine on oath, documents, affidavits, requisition **public records**.
- Recommend **compensation, prosecution, interim relief** (interim relief even during inquiry since **2006**).
- Government informs action taken within **one month**.
!TRAP NOT a function: **punishing violators** or enforcing its recommendations as decrees. SHRC too cannot punish.
#### 6.3 Specialised Mandates
| Mandate | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Bonded labour** | **Supreme Court (1997)** entrusted monitoring of the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, **1976** |
| **Custodial deaths** | Report to NHRC within **24 hours**; post-mortem **videography**; independent investigation |
| **Encounter deaths** | Reporting + magisterial inquiry per NHRC guidelines and **PUCL v Maharashtra (2014)** |
| Transgender welfare | **Advisory 2.0 (May 2026)**: Census categories, legal-aid cells, Garima Greh expansion |
### 7. LIMITATIONS
| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Time bar** | No inquiry after **one year** of the alleged act |
| **Armed forces** | Only **seeks a report from the Central Government** and recommends; Centre informs action within **three months** |
| **Purview** | Only violations by **public servants**; not purely private complaints |
| **Sub judice / overlap** | Not a matter already before an SHRC or another statutory commission |
| **Non-binding** | Recommendations **advisory**; Government must respond but cannot be compelled |
- "**Human rights**" = rights relating to **life, liberty, equality and dignity** guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in International Covenants and enforceable by Indian courts. Members and officers are deemed **public servants**.
- "**Armed forces**" (Sec. 2(1)(a)) = naval, military, air forces and other armed forces of the Union.
- Called a "**toothless tiger**": non-binding advice, one-year bar, armed-forces limit.
- **30 Apr 2026:** Allahabad HC said NHRC exceeded its mandate by directing an EOW probe into 558 aided madrasas.
!NUM 1 month = action (general); 3 months = action (armed forces); 1 year = time bar; 24 hours = custodial death report.
### 8. DIVISION OF FUNCTIONS AND TRANSFER
- SHRC: only **State List** and **Concurrent List** subjects; NHRC covers all.
- **UTs (2019):** the Centre may **confer** a UT's human-rights functions on an SHRC; **Delhi** is handled by the **NHRC**.
- **No overlap:** SHRC will not inquire into a case being probed by the NHRC or another statutory commission, and vice versa.
- **Transfer:** the **2006** amendment enabled NHRC to **transfer complaints** to the concerned SHRC.
- **Human Rights Courts (Sec. 30):** State Govt, with the **HC Chief Justice's** concurrence, notifies a **Court of Session** in each district for speedy trial; **Special Public Prosecutor (Sec. 31)**: an advocate of **7 years'** practice.
### 9. AMENDMENTS 2006 AND 2019
| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| **2006** | NCSC and NCST chairs became deemed members; resignation to President / Governor |
| **2006** | Jail visits without intimating State Govt; interim relief during inquiry |
| **2006** | SHRC **5 → 3** members; transfer of complaints to SHRC |
| **2019** | Term **5 → 3 years** (or 70); reappointment limit removed |
| **2019** | Chair: CJI **or SC Judge**; SHRC chair: HC CJ **or Judge**; deemed members **4 → 7**; expert members 2 → 3; UT functions to SHRC |
### 10. REPORTS, PARIS PRINCIPLES AND GANHRI
- NHRC annual report → Central Govt (and State Govt concerned) → laid before **each House of Parliament** with a memorandum of action taken.
- **GANHRI** accredits NHRIs through its **Sub-Committee on Accreditation**: **'A' = fully compliant**, 'B' = partly, based on Paris Principles conformity.
- **May 2024:** GANHRI **deferred** NHRC-India's re-accreditation for the **second year in a row** (also 2023), citing independence from government; NHRC says its **'A' status** continues.
### 11. ANDHRA PRADESH SHRC
- Constituted by the **AP Government** under PHRA 1993.
- After bifurcation, **Kurnool** was notified as the SHRC **headquarters** (planned earlier at Vijayawada); the office was inaugurated in **Sept 2021**.
- Chairperson then: **Justice M. Seetharama Murti** (retired HC judge); reported **without a chairperson since March 2024**.
- **28 Jun 2026:** in the **Gade Sai Krishna** custodial death case, NHRC directed the **AP SHRC, DGP and Chief Secretary** to report within a week.
- **5 Feb 2026:** a YSRCP delegation petitioned NHRC Chairperson Justice V. Ramasubramanian over law and order in AP.
- **21 May 2026:** SHRCs in the highest-GDP States get only 80 paisa per capita; several run with over 40% vacancies.
