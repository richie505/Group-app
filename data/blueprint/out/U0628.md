## U0628 | RAINFALL DISTRIBUTION, VARIABILITY, EL NINO, DROUGHT AND FLOOD-PRONE AREAS
### 1. RAINFALL ZONES AND EXTREMES
| Zone | Areas |
|---|---|
| Very heavy | **Western Coast, Northeast India** |
| Heavy | **Western Ghats, Meghalaya** |
| Moderate | Most of the Northern Plains, parts of Central India |
| Scanty | W Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana, rain-shadow Deccan |
| Very scanty | **Western Rajasthan, Thar** |
- **Mawsynram (Mausimram)**, Meghalaya – **highest** average annual rainfall in India; **Agumbe** (Western Ghats, Karnataka) is among the wettest.
- Lowest: **Leh** (Ladakh) and **Jaisalmer** (Thar).
- **AP**: highest in **Sileru–Sabari** valleys (**140–152 cm**); state average ~**966 mm** – **two-thirds SW monsoon, one-third NE monsoon**.
### 2. RAINFALL GRADIENT AND DURATION
- **Northern plains**: rain **decreases east to west** (winds get drier): **Kolkata > Patna > Prayagraj > Delhi > Bikaner**.
- **Peninsula**: rain decreases **west to east** (rain-shadow lee of the Ghats).
- **Monsoon duration decreases from south to north**: **< 75 days** in W Rajasthan (late arrival, early exit) vs **> 120 days** in the SW peninsula.
!KEY Plains: E → W decrease. Peninsula: W → E decrease.
### 3. RAIN SHADOW AND LOW-RAINFALL REGIONS
| Region | Reason |
|---|---|
| Leeward Deccan (interior Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rayalaseema) | Rain shadow east of the **Western Ghats** |
| Rajasthan | Arabian Sea branch runs **parallel to the Aravallis**; Bay branch arrives dry |
| Ladakh | Behind the Great Himalaya – cold desert |
### 4. WINTER AND NORTH-EAST MONSOON RAINFALL
| Region | Winter rain source |
|---|---|
| **Coromandel coast / Tamil Nadu** | **NE (retreating) monsoon**, Oct–Dec |
| Rayalaseema, south Prakasam | NE monsoon – its failure causes drought |
| **North-west India** | **Western disturbances** (Mediterranean) |
### 5. RAINFALL VARIABILITY
- **CV = (standard deviation / mean) × 100**; higher CV = more variable rain.
| Variability | Regions |
|---|---|
| **Low CV (< 25%)** | West coast, Western Ghats, NE, eastern peninsula |
| **High CV (> 50%)** | **Western Rajasthan, Kutch, Ladakh** |
!TRAP Low-rainfall areas have the HIGHEST variability.
### 6. EL NINO LINKAGE
| Term | Meaning / effect |
|---|---|
| **El Nino** | Central/east equatorial Pacific **≥ 0.5 °C warmer** for **five consecutive overlapping 3-month periods**; **weakens** the monsoon |
| **La Nina** | Generally **strengthens** the monsoon; colder winters; more Bay cyclones |
| **Positive IOD** | Can **offset** El Nino |
- Deficient years: **2015** – 86% of LPA; **2023** – El Nino 2023–24 (AP deficit −16.5%).

### 8. DROUGHT-PRONE AREAS
#### 8.1 Types
| Type | Based on |
|---|---|
| **Meteorological** | Rain deficit |
| **Agricultural** | Soil moisture |
| **Hydrological** | Falling river flow (**reduced streamflow**) and groundwater |
| Flash drought | Sudden heat + no rain |
#### 8.2 Extent
| Category | Rainfall | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Total drought-prone | – | About **68%** of India's **agricultural (cultivable/sown) land** |
| **Chronically drought-prone** | **< 750 mm** | About **33%** |
| Drought-prone | **750–1,125 mm** | About **35%** |
- 68% = 33% + 35%.
- Chronic drought regions: **western India – Rajasthan, Gujarat**; AP – **Rayalaseema and south Prakasam** (NE monsoon failure + falling groundwater).
#### 8.3 Drought Management
| Milestone | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Green Revolution** (late 1960s) | Reduced the need for famine-relief work |
| Drought Prone Areas Programme | Holistic drought-management programme |
| **Disaster Management Act, 2005** (not 2006) | National disaster-management law |
| **Manual for Drought Management, 2016** (revised **2020**) | Declaration indicators: **rainfall deficit, vegetation, soil moisture, water** |
### 9. FLOOD-PRONE AREAS
- About **12%** of India's land is prone to **floods and river erosion** (NIDM Annual Report 2023–24).
- Worst-hit: **Brahmaputra valley** – "**sorrow of Assam**" (not Bihar); **Kosi**, Ganga plains, coastal deltas.
- Himalayan rivers swell in the monsoon → frequent floods; cloudburst flash floods rising in the Himalaya and NE.
- **Mitigation**: embankments, drainage channels, sea dykes, real-time flood forecasting; e.g. **37,760 sq km** flood-prone area protected in West Bengal.
