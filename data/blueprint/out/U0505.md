## U0505 | INDIAN INDUSTRY – STRUCTURE, GROWTH, IIP AND PUBLIC SECTOR
### 1. EVOLUTION OF MODERN INDUSTRY (TO 1947)
#### 1.1 Industrial FIRSTs – Chronology
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1818 | First cotton mill attempt, **Fort Gloster** near Kolkata (failed) |
| 1854 | First successful cotton mill, **Bombay** – **Cowasjee Nanabhoy Davar** (Bombay Spinning and Weaving Co., Tardeo, 7 July 1854) |
| 1855 | First jute mill, **Rishra** (Bengal) |
| June 1906 | **Munagala Raja's** textile unit, **Vijayawada** – first of its kind in Madras Presidency |
| 1907 | **TISCO**, Jamshedpur (private, NOT a PSU) |
| 1932 | **Tariff protection** sets off the growth of the **sugar** industry |
- 1900-1947: steel, cement, sugar and paper came up.
- Cotton textiles = **oldest large-scale industry**; centres Mumbai and Ahmedabad; **largest textile centre = Mumbai**.
#### 1.2 Textile Firms by Founding Year
**Bombay Dyeing (1879) → Lakshmi Mills, Coimbatore (1910) → Raymond (1925) → Grasim / Gwalior Rayon (1947)** (APPSC-GS 2024 key: B, A, D, C).
!TRAP First cotton mill = Bombay 1854 (Indian-run); first jute mill = Rishra 1855 (foreign-run). Fort Gloster 1818 failed.
### 2. GROWTH PHASES AND POLICY SINCE 1951
| Phase | Character | Growth (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 1951-65 | Public-sector-led heavy industrialisation | 7-8% |
| 1965-80 | Deceleration: **wars, droughts, infrastructure bottlenecks, licensing** | about 4% |
| 1980s | Recovery with partial liberalisation | 7-8% |
| 1991 onward | Liberalisation; services outgrow industry | – |
- **Second Plan (1956-61):** basic and heavy industries – **Nehru-Mahalanobis model**.
- **IPR 1956** reserved **17** industries for the public sector; **1991** policy cut this to **8**.
- **Premature deindustrialisation (Dani Rodrik):** manufacturing share peaks at a lower income level than in early industrialisers.
!TRAP 1965-80 slowdown was NOT due to import liberalisation.
### 3. INDUSTRIAL STRUCTURE SINCE 1991
#### 3.1 Sectoral Shares
- **Services, not industry,** gained the largest GDP share after 1991.
- Industry = about **25.3% of GVA**; real industry GVA grows about 7%.
- **Manufacturing** real share steady at **17-18%**; nominal share fell only due to relative prices.
- Public sector about **13-14% of GDP** → **private sector dominates** industrial output.
- Post-LPG growth favoured **capital- and skill-intensive** sectors.
!TRAP "Industry's share rose significantly compared to services" is FALSE (APPSC-GS 2023: only II and III correct).
- Medium- and high-tech manufacturing = **46.3%** (ES 2025-26).
### 4. STRUCTURAL PROBLEMS
| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| Capacity under-utilisation | Main cause = **deficient basic infrastructure** (APPSC 2007); weak demand too |
| Regional concentration | **Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka**; eastern and central States lag |
| Capital intensity | Highest in manufacturing (2017-18) = **Madhya Pradesh** |

- **Capital intensity** = reliance on physical assets over labour.
- **Ranchi** = industrial town on the Chota Nagpur plateau ("Ruhr of India").
### 5. INDEX OF INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTION (IIP)
#### 5.1 Meaning and Compilation
- Measures **short-term changes in volume** of industrial production against a base period.
- Compiled **monthly** by **NSO (MoSPI)**, which replaced the CSO.
- **Base year 2011-12 → 2022-23**, effective **1 June 2026**.
- New basket **1,042 products** (old: 407 items).
#### 5.2 Coverage and Weights
- Sectors: **mining, manufacturing, electricity**. NOT included: **agriculture, services, construction**.
- New additions: gas, water supply, sewerage, waste management, minor and rare-earth minerals; renewable electricity shown separately.
- Weights based on each sector's **GVA share in 2022-23**: manufacturing **76.1%**, mining **11.1%**, electricity and gas **10.9%**, water supply 2.02%.
- **Use-based groups:** primary, capital, intermediate, infrastructure/construction, consumer durables, consumer non-durables. 2011-12 series: **primary goods highest (~34%)**, **capital goods lowest (8.22%)**.
- **Index of Services Production (ISP):** base 2024-25; first release **14 July 2026**; 60-day lag.
#### 5.3 Growth Trend
- **FY 2023:** capital goods and infrastructure/construction goods grew fastest.
- FY 2024-25: IIP **4.0%**, ICI 4.5%; FY 2025-26: IIP 4.1%.
- **Jun 2026:** IIP **7.3%** (23-month high); new ICI 5% (later 6%), led by iron ore (+43.9%). Jul 2026: ICI 5.4%.
### 6. INDEX OF CORE INDUSTRIES (ICI)
- Compiled monthly by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT** (Ministry of Commerce and Industry).
- Revised to **base 2022-23** in July 2026 (w.e.f. April 2023); counts **only raw coal**, uses **gross steel output** (double counting removed).
| Feature | 2011-12 series | 2022-23 series |
|---|---|---|
| Number | 8 | **9 – iron ore added (4.905%)** |
| Combined weight in IIP | 40.27% | **32.88%** |
| Highest | Refinery products (28.04%) | **Electricity (30.93%)** |
| Second | Electricity (19.85%) | Refinery products (22.57%) |
| Lowest | Fertilisers (2.63%) | Fertilisers (2.73%) |
- **New order:** Electricity 30.93 > Refinery 22.57 > Steel 17.58 > Crude oil 7.43 > Coal 5.59 > Iron ore 4.90 > Cement 4.41 > Natural gas 3.84 > Fertilisers 2.73.
- In the earlier 2004-05 base, the eight core industries carried **37.90%**; in 2011-12 refinery products moved ahead of electricity and steel.
- **Full list:** coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement, electricity (+ iron ore).
!TRAP NOT core: **transport** (APPSC-G2 2011), **chemicals**, **textiles**. Construction is NOT in IIP.

### 7. MAJOR INDUSTRY PROFILES
#### 7.1 Textiles
- **Largest employer after agriculture:** about **4.5 crore direct**, 10 crore+ indirect.
- Segments: **mill, powerloom, handloom**; **powerlooms** make most cloth.
- Cooperative cotton units grew fastest (14.8% a year, 1999-2000 to 2005-06).
#### 7.2 Steel, Cement and Others
- India **2nd** in crude steel: **149.4 MT (2024)**; China 1,005.1 MT (over one-third of world output).
- About 93% of planned ironmaking is coal-based → main **imported input = coking coal**.
- **Cement:** about 87% in a few States (Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh), near limestone.

### 8. PUBLIC SECTOR – ROLE AND CPSE PROFILES
#### 8.1 Commanding Heights
- Public sector entered **capital-heavy, long-gestation** sectors private capital shunned; built utilities and infrastructure for **regional balance**.
- Early base: Hindustan Aircraft, **Sindri Fertilizers, Chittaranjan Locomotives, Integral Coach Factory, HMT, ITI, Hindustan Cables**, penicillin unit.

### 9. CPSE CATEGORIES – MAHARATNA, NAVRATNA, MINIRATNA
#### 9.1 Criteria and Powers
| Category | Introduced | Key criteria | Invest without Govt approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maharatna | **2010** (Cabinet Dec 2009; w.e.f. 19 May 2010) | Already Navratna; listed; 3-year average turnover >₹25,000 cr, net worth >₹15,000 cr, net profit >₹5,000 cr; global presence | **₹5,000 cr** per project |
| Navratna | **1997, first 9 CPSEs** | Miniratna-I + Schedule 'A'; Excellent/Very Good MoU in 3 of 5 years; **60+** on six parameters | **₹1,000 cr** or 15% of net worth |
| Miniratna-I | 1997 | Profit 3 years in a row; pre-tax profit **₹30 cr+** in one year; positive net worth | **₹500 cr** or net worth |
| Miniratna-II | 1997 | Profit 3 years in a row; positive net worth | **₹300 cr** or 50% of net worth |
- All tiers: no budgetary support or Government guarantee; no default on Govt loans.
- Basis: **turnover, net worth, net profit**; purpose: autonomy to **compete globally**; granted by **DPE**.

### 10. PSU PERFORMANCE, INSTITUTIONS AND REFORMS
#### 10.1 Numbers and Performance
- **CPSE** = Central Govt or other CPSEs hold **51%+** equity.
- Number: **5** (start of First Plan) → **244 by 1990** (APPSC-G1 2004) → 250+ now.
- **2024-25:** 291 operating CPSEs – **226 profit-making, 63 loss-making**.
- **ONGC** = traditional top profit-maker; **BSNL** loss-making (2014-15).
- FY22-25: rising CPSE **dividends** lifted non-tax revenue; **disinvestment receipts fell**.
#### 10.2 DPE, PESB, PE Survey
- **DPE** (now under **Ministry of Finance**; earlier Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises): four schedules of CPSEs, monitors MoUs, grants Ratna status.
- **Public Enterprises Survey:** DPE, on the **Estimates Committee's** advice; covers CPSEs with **over 50% GoI equity**; excludes departmental undertakings, banks, insurers.
- **PESB** selects **board-level executives** of CPSEs.
#### 10.3 Autonomy and Reform
| Committee / body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Arjun Sengupta (1984)** | Autonomy → **MoU system** |
| **Rangarajan (1993)** | Disinvestment |
| **BRPSE (2004)** | Revival of sick CPSEs |
- MoU: annual targets for all CPSEs (incl. subsidiaries); via a High Power Committee.
- **New PSE Policy 2021:** four **strategic** sector groups (atomic energy, space, defence; transport, telecom; power, petroleum, coal, minerals; banking, insurance, finance) keep **1 to 4** PSEs; **non-strategic** PSEs privatised or else closed (DPE with DIPAM).
- Aug 2026: MSMED (Amendment) Bill → every CPSE to settle MSME invoices on **TReDS**.
