## U0629 | NATURAL VEGETATION OF INDIA AND FOREST COVER (INDIA STATE OF FOREST REPORT)
### 1. CLASSIFICATION AND AREA RANKING OF FOREST TYPES
- Classification by **H.G. Champion and S.K. Seth (1968)**: **5 major groups, 16 type groups** – Moist Tropical, Dry Tropical, Montane Subtropical, Montane Temperate, Alpine.
| Rank / superlative | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Most widespread** | **Tropical deciduous (monsoon) forests = 65.6%** of forest area (dry + moist deciduous) |
| Rank 1 single type | **Tropical dry deciduous** – largest type group (~**3,13,617 sq km**) |
| Rank 2 | **Tropical moist deciduous** (~**1,35,492 sq km**) |
| Rank 3 | Wet evergreen – ~**8%** (51,249 sq km); semi-evergreen 4.1% |
| Tallest trees | Tropical wet evergreen (> 45 m) |
- Descending share: **dry deciduous > moist deciduous > wet evergreen**.
- **Tropical dry deciduous** ("monsoon forest") is the largest forest type group in India.
!TRAP Moist deciduous (37%) is NOT the largest single type – **dry deciduous** is (common wrong option).
- **Tropical deciduous** forests are also the most widespread in Asia. Globally, **temperate coniferous** forests hold the highest share of forest area.
!KEY APPSC G2 2025: Most widespread forest type of India = tropical deciduous (65.6%).
### 2. TROPICAL FOREST TYPES → RAINFALL, REGION, SPECIES
| Type | Rainfall | Where | Species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical wet evergreen** | **> 200 cm**, 15–30 °C | W Ghats (west slopes), NE (upper Assam, Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura, Nagaland), **A&N**, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Tamil Nadu coast | **Rosewood (Dalbergia), ebony, mahogany**, gurjan, ironwood, jamun, canes |
| Semi-evergreen | 200–250 cm | Fringes of evergreen belts | Mixed |
| **Moist deciduous** | **100–200 cm** | E slopes of W Ghats, **Bhabar–Terai**, Himalayan foothills, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, WB, A&N | **Teak, sal**, sandalwood, arjun, mahua |
| **Dry deciduous** | **70–100 cm** | Central Gangetic plains to south India; peninsular interior; AP (**Nallamala**, Eastern Ghats, **Seshachalam**) | Teak, **sandalwood** (south), Terminalia, **red sanders** |
| **Thorn** | **< 70 cm** (humidity < 50%, 25–30 °C) | Rajasthan, SW Punjab, W Haryana, **Kutch**, Saurashtra, **western AP / Rayalaseema** | **Acacia, babul, Ziziphus, Prosopis**, cacti |
- **Evergreen**: dense, multi-layered; **maximum plant diversity and biomass**; leaf litter decomposes fastest; epiphytes and climbers reach the canopy; no dry season so leaves are not shed together. Grow near the equator (**10°N–10°S**); cover about 7% of the earth's land.
- **Tropical rainforest in India**: Assam and Kerala.
- **Moist deciduous**: shed leaves in the dry season; **teak** dominant. **Dry deciduous**: transition to thorn forest; major type of central India.
- **Thorn**: open, stunted, long roots, thorns/spines; **western** (not eastern) AP.
- **Desert vegetation** (< 25 cm): xerophytes of W Rajasthan; date palms in oases.
- A&N: **mangrove + evergreen + deciduous** together; North Andamans wet evergreen, Middle Andamans moist deciduous.
- **Cinchona** – evergreen tropical plant; does not grow in Chhattisgarh. **Mahogany** – native to the Americas, not Himalayan.
!PAIR Rosewood | Evergreen | > 200 cm || Teak | Deciduous | 100–200 cm

### 4. HIMALAYAN ALTITUDINAL ZONATION
| Zone (bottom → top) | Altitude | Species |
|---|---|---|
| Tropical foothills | – | **Sal** |
| Sub-tropical | **1,000–1,800 m** | **Chir pine**; Khair |
| Moist temperate | **1,500–3,000 m** | **Oak, deodar**, blue pine, silver fir |
| Sub-alpine | – | **Birch, rhododendron**, fir |
| Alpine meadows | Up to ~**4,750 m** (2,900–6,000 m) | Grasses – **Bugyals** (Uttarakhand), **Margs** (Kashmir) |
| Dry alpine scrub | Above ~4,000 m | **Juniper** |
- **Deodar** is the highest of sal, chir pine, deodar; most common in the temperate W Himalaya (1,500–2,500 m).
- **Oak–Rhododendron** (Uttarakhand) = temperate: Banj Oak, Burans (Rhododendron arboreum), Kaphal, Aynor.
- Alpine vegetation begins ~**3,000 m** in the western and ~**4,000 m** in the eastern Himalaya (east nearer equator and Bay moisture); tree line higher in the east. Alpine forests are not found on the Western Ghats.
- Eastern Himalaya: oaks, laurels, maples, rhododendrons, alder, birch.
- **Southern (windward) slopes** – denser forest (more rain, sunlight); northern slopes thinner.
- Himalayan soils lack humus; the Himalaya has the largest area under forest cover.
### 5. MONTANE (SHOLA), LITTORAL AND MANGROVE FORESTS

- **Mangroves** – **Rhizophora, Avicennia, Sonneratia**; **stilt and knee roots**; submerged at high tide; line deltas on both coasts.

### 7. FOREST TERMS, COVER CLASSES AND SILVICULTURE
- **Forest cover** = land **> 1 ha** with canopy density **≥ 10%**, irrespective of ownership.
| Class | Canopy density |
|---|---|
| **Very Dense (VDF)** | **≥ 70%** |
| **Moderately Dense (MDF)** | **40–70%** |
| **Open (OF)** | **10–40%** |
| Scrub | < 10% – not forest cover |
| Silvicultural term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Coppice** | Regrowth from **stool shoots** after felling; **short rotation** (fuelwood) |
| **High forest** | Crop raised from **seed**; **long rotation** (timber) |
| **Selection system** | Mature trees removed **singly or in groups**; **uneven-aged** forest |
- **Afforestation** – planting on never-forested land; **reforestation** – re-establishing depleted forest; **social forestry** – trees for community needs; Joint Forest Management = community participation.
- **TOFI** (Trees Outside Forests in India) – AP Forest Dept + USAID, Nov 2022.
### 8. INDIA STATE OF FOREST REPORT
- Published by **Forest Survey of India** (**1981, Dehradun**; MoEFCC); **biennial**.
#### 8.1 ISFR 2023
| Indicator | Figure |
|---|---|
| Forest cover | **7,15,343 sq km = 21.76%** (dense forest 57.34% of it) |
| Tree cover | **1,12,014 sq km = 3.41%** |
| Forest + tree cover | **8,27,357 sq km = 25.17%** (82.7 million ha) |
| Net gain 2021–23 | **1,445.81 sq km** – only 156 sq km forest + 1,289 sq km tree cover outside recorded forests |
- Largest forest area: **Madhya Pradesh** (77,073) > **Arunachal Pradesh** (65,882) > **Chhattisgarh** (55,812 sq km); Odisha 61,204 sq km.
- Highest % of area: **Mizoram** (85.34%); Arunachal 78.67%; Meghalaya ~76%. Largest very dense forest: **Arunachal** (20,985.32 sq km).
- Ascending %: Maharashtra 16.53 < MP 25.00 < Odisha 33.67 < Chhattisgarh 41.28.
- Earlier reports: ISFR 2003 ~19% forest; 2017 forest 21.54%; 2019 forest + tree 24.56%; ISFR 2021 – **AP** had the maximum increase.
- Tree cover maximum – East Deccan (key at the time). **Jodhpur** – minimum very dense forest (zero in 2023). **Paschim Champaran** (831.03 sq km) > Kaimur (526) in dense deciduous forest.
- **SDG India Index 2023–24**: AP 18.28%; best Mizoram 84.53%.
- **FAO GFRA 2025** (Oct 2025): India **3rd** in annual net forest gain (191,000 ha/yr, after China, Russia) and **9th** in total forest area.
#### 8.2 Forest Fires
- About **36%** of forest cover prone to frequent fires (**ISFR 2015**); **dry deciduous** most vulnerable.
- AP: largest fire-affected area in 2023–24 (5,286.76 sq km); 3rd in large-fire events; Chittoor and Annamayya fire risk (Apr 2026).
#### 8.3 AP Forests
- Forest cover ~**30,085 sq km** (ISFR 2019: 29,137.40); mainly **dry deciduous**; **Nallamala** = biggest forest; mangroves + littoral 418 sq km.
- The **38,436.58 sq km** figure adds **8,351.62 sq km of scrub**, which is not forest cover.
- Highest deforestation globally: tropical zone (SOFO 2024); world forests ~4.1 billion ha (31%); 4.3 million ha primary rainforest lost in 2025.
- Debate: **Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam** – ~140 crore saplings vs only 156 sq km true forest gain.
### 9. FOREST POLICY, LAW AND LEGAL CLASSES
| Act / policy | Provision |
|---|---|
| **National Forest Policy 1988** | **33%** of area under forest/tree cover; **66%** in hills; India at 25.17% |
| National Forestry Action Programme | One-third of India under forest |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act 1980** | **Central approval** to divert forest land; AP diverted 149.57 ha (Apr–Dec 2025) |
| **CAMPA** | Manages compensatory afforestation funds |
| **Indian Forest Act 1927** | **Reserved, Protected, Village** forests |
| **WPA (Amendment) 2002** | **Conservation reserves, Community reserves** |
| Class | Degree of protection |
|---|---|
| **Reserved** | Highest – activities **barred unless permitted** (AP ~85.8%) |
| **Protected** | Activities **allowed unless prohibited** |
| **Village** | Reserved forest **assigned to a village community** |
### 10. PROTECTED AREAS, TIGER RESERVES AND SPECIES PROJECTS
#### 10.1 Project Tiger
- **1973**: 9 reserves (18,278 sq km) → **58** (84,488 sq km, ~**2.6%** of land).
- Tigers: **1,411 (2006) → 3,682 (2022)** – over **70%** of the world's wild tigers; world's largest population; ~30% outside reserves.
- **MP** has the most reserves (**9**). **NTCA** – statutory under **WPA 1972**; headed by the Environment Minister.
- Newest: **56th Guru Ghasidas–Tamor Pingla** (Chhattisgarh; linked to Kanha) → **57th Ratapani** (MP, 2024) → **58th Madhav** (Shivpuri, MP, 2025; Sakhya Sagar Ramsar site inside).
- **Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam (NSTR)** – India's **largest** TR; AP only after bifurcation; 63–67 tigers; gaur and tigress translocation from Sep 2026.

### 11. BIOSPHERE RESERVES AND RAMSAR SITES
#### 11.1 Biosphere Reserves
- India has **18** notified Biosphere Reserves; **13** are in UNESCO's **MAB** World Network – newest **Cold Desert** (HP), **Sep 2025**; **Panna** listed in **2020**.
- Largest: **Kachchh** (~12,454 sq km); **Sundarbans** BR 9,630 sq km.
- **Agasthyamalai** BR – SC-ordered eviction raises tribal-rights concerns.
#### 11.2 Ramsar Sites
- **Ramsar Convention** – signed **2 Feb 1971** at **Ramsar, Iran**, for wetlands of international importance.
- India's first sites: **Chilika** and **Keoladeo** (1981).
- India has **103** Ramsar sites (Oct 2026) – latest **Sukhna Wetland** (Chandigarh) and **Sauj Jheel** (UP), 15 Oct 2026.
