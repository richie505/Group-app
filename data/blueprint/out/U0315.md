## U0315 | STATE LEGISLATURE – ASSEMBLY, LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL AND ARTICLE 169
### 1. FRAMEWORK – PART VI, ARTICLES 168-212
- State Legislature: **Part VI, Articles 168-212** (APPSC-GS 2018); Parliament: Part V, Articles 79-122.
- **Article 168**: unicameral = Governor + Legislative Assembly; bicameral = **Governor + Legislative Assembly + Legislative Council**.
- Assembly – **Article 170**; Council – **Article 171**.
#### 1.1 Article → Content
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| 168 | Constitution of State Legislatures |
| 169 | **Creation / abolition** of Legislative Councils |
| 170 / 171 | Composition of Assembly / Council |
| 172 | Duration (5 years) |
| 173 | Qualifications – age **25** (Assembly) / **30** (Council) |
| 174 | Sessions, prorogation, dissolution – gap **≤ 6 months** |
| 175 / 176 | Governor's address and messages / **special address** |
| 178-187 | Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Chairman, Deputy Chairman; 187 – Secretariat |
| 189 | Voting and **quorum** |
| 190-193 | Vacation of seats and disqualification |
| 194 | **Powers and privileges** of MLAs and committees |
| 196-201 | Legislative procedure; 197 ordinary Bills; 198-199 Money Bills; 200 assent; 201 reserved Bills |
| 202 | **Annual Financial Statement** |
| 207 | **Recommendation for Money Bills** |
| 208 | **Rules of procedure** (APPSC-GS 2018 key) |
| 210 | Language – Hindi / English or official language of the State |
| 211 | Bars discussion of a **High Court judge's conduct** |
| 212 | Courts barred from inquiring into proceedings |
| 213 | Ordinances |
!TRAP Age is in Art. 173, not 170/171. Art. 208 = rules of procedure; Art. 194 = privileges.
### 2. LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY – STRENGTH, TERM, QUORUM
#### 2.1 Strength – Article 170
- **Not more than 500, not less than 60**, by **direct election** (FPTP).
| State / UT | Seats |
|---|---|
| **Uttar Pradesh** (largest) | **403** |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | **175** |
| **Telangana** | **119** |
| **Goa, Mizoram** | **40** each |
| **Puducherry** (smallest UT Assembly) | **33** (30 elected + 3 nominated) |
| **Sikkim** (smallest; Art. 371F) | **32** – exception to the 60 minimum |
- **Arunachal Pradesh**: minimum 30 under Art. 371H, but actually has **60**.
#### 2.2 Term and Reservation
- **5 years** (Art. 172); can be **dissolved**, cannot be abolished.
- **National Emergency**: Parliament may extend it by law **one year at a time** (Art. 172(1)), not beyond **six months** after the Emergency ends.
- **Art. 332**: SC/ST seats in proportion to population. No caste reservation in the Rajya Sabha and Council.
- **104th Amendment, 2020**: ended **Anglo-Indian** nomination (**Art. 333**).
- **106th Amendment, 2023**: about **one-third** seats for **women** (after post-census delimitation).
#### 2.3 Quorum and Seats
- **Quorum (Art. 189(3))**: **10 members or one-tenth**, whichever is **greater** (70-member House → 10).
- **Art. 190**: no one can be a member of **both Houses** or of the legislatures of **two States**.
- **Art. 190(3)(b)**: resignation takes effect only on the **Speaker's / Chairman's acceptance**.
- **Art. 190(4)**: seat **vacated** after **60 days' absence** without permission – vacation, not Art. 191 disqualification.
- First disqualified for 60 days' absence (Council of States): **Barjinder Singh Hamdard** (Independent, Punjab), 2000.
### 3. LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL – COMPOSITION (ARTICLE 171)
| Elected / nominated by | Share |
|---|---|
| **Local bodies** (municipalities, district boards etc.) | **1/3** |
| **MLAs** – from among non-MLAs | **1/3** |
| **Graduates** – 3 years' standing, residing in the State | **1/12** |
| **Teachers** – 3 years' standing, secondary school and above | **1/12** |
| **Nominated by Governor** (on Cabinet advice) | **1/6** |
- Nomination fields (five): **literature, science, art, co-operative movement, social service**.
- Election by **PR-STV**, secret ballot.
!TRAP Co-operative movement is a Council field, NOT a Rajya Sabha field (Art. 80: literature, science, art, social service). **Industrialists / commerce** are NOT a category.
!TRAP Wrong statement: "1/13th elected by the Legislative Assembly" – MLAs elect **one-third**.
!NUM 1/3 – 1/12 – 1/12 – 1/3 – 1/6 = local bodies – graduates – teachers – MLAs – Governor.
#### 3.1 Size and Tenure
- **Maximum one-third** of Assembly strength; **minimum 40** (Art. 171); **Parliament fixes** actual strength.
- **Permanent body** – cannot be dissolved; member term **6 years**; **one-third retire every second year**; can be **abolished** (Art. 169).
- Minimum age **30**; **Chairman elected** by the Council from its members (**Art. 182**), not nominated by the Governor.
- Called the Upper House / House of Elders. **Largest Council: Uttar Pradesh (100)**. Sikkim has no Council.
- Rajya Sabha compared: also permanent; 6 years; one-third retire every second year; minimum age 30; maximum 250.
### 4. ASSEMBLY VS COUNCIL
| Basis | Legislative Assembly | Legislative Council |
|---|---|---|
| Article | 170 | 171 |
| Election | Direct, FPTP | Indirect, PR-STV + nomination |
| Size | 60 to 500 | ≤ 1/3 of Assembly; min 40 |
| Life | 5 years; dissolvable | Permanent; 6-year member term |
| Minimum age | 25 | 30 |
| Presiding | Speaker | Chairman (Art. 182) |
| Money Bill | Originates only here; final | Recommends within **14 days** |
| Ordinary Bill | Overrides; re-passes | Delay **3 + 1 = 4 months**; no veto |
| Bill from the Council | Assembly can reject – Bill **ends** | No second chance |
| Demands for grants | **Voted only here** | Discusses only |
| Deadlock | **No joint sitting** in States; Assembly prevails | – |
- Council's power is **only delaying** – much weaker than the Rajya Sabha; no role in ratifying constitutional amendments.
### 5. MONEY BILLS AND ORDINARY BILLS
#### 5.1 Money Bills – Articles 198, 199, 207
- Introduced **only in the Assembly**, by a minister, on the **Governor's recommendation** (**Art. 207(1)**; APPSC-GS 2018 key).
- **Art. 199(3)**: **Speaker certifies** a Money Bill – sole and final.
- **Art. 198(2)**: Council returns within **14 days**, else deemed passed; cannot reject or amend; Assembly may accept or reject its recommendations.
#### 5.2 Ordinary Bills – Article 197
- Council holds a Bill **not more than 3 months** on first passage; after re-passage, **1 month** – maximum **4 months**; then deemed passed.
- Governor may reserve a State Bill for the President (**Art. 200**); President considers it (**Art. 201**).
!TRAP Council delay: 14 days (Money Bill) vs 3 + 1 months (ordinary Bill). There is NO joint sitting in States (unlike Art. 108).
### 6. ARTICLE 169 – CREATION AND ABOLITION OF COUNCILS
1. **Assembly resolution by special majority** – majority of **total membership** + **two-thirds present and voting**.
2. Resolution is **recommendatory** – does not oblige the Union to introduce a Bill.
3. **Parliament** enacts a law by **simple majority**.
4. Not deemed an amendment under **Art. 368** (APPSC-GS 2018).
- **Governor has no role** in starting or approving the resolution; the Speaker has no separate role.
- Constitution fixes maximum and minimum limits; Parliament fixes strength. The Council's existence depends on the **will of the Assembly**.
!KEY Assembly = special majority; Parliament = simple majority; Governor = no role.
### 7. STATES WITH LEGISLATIVE COUNCILS
- **Six** (2026): **Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh**.
- **J&K** Council abolished by the J&K Reorganisation Act, **2019**; **Tamil Nadu** abolished its Council in **1986**.
- **1956**: 18 reorganised States had bicameral legislatures.
- An older PYQ key listed Maharashtra as not having a Council – bicameral status has varied; lists showing J&K or only four States are out of date.
- **Karnataka (2026)**: Saleem Ahmed elected Council Chairperson (38-30); G.S. Patil became the 18th Assembly Speaker.
- **Telangana**: Council Chairman **Gutha Sukender Reddy**; Assembly Speaker **Gaddam Prasad Kumar**; 9 Sep 2026 – two BRS MLCs suspended for remarks against the Speaker.

### 9. PRESIDING OFFICERS
#### 9.1 Election and Vacating Office
- **Art. 178**: Speaker and Deputy Speaker elected by the Assembly from its members; Speaker presides and keeps order.
- On dissolution the Speaker continues until just before the **first meeting of the new Assembly**.
- **Art. 179**: Speaker **resigns to the Deputy Speaker** (APPSC-GS 2018 key); removed by a resolution of a **majority of all the then members** after **14 days' notice**.
- **Art. 181**: during a removal resolution the Speaker **does not preside**, but may speak and **vote in the first instance** (APPSC-G1 2022 key).
- Council: **Chairman** presides (Art. 182); Chairman and Deputy Chairman resign to each other.
#### 9.2 Powers
- **Casting vote** only, in a tie (**Art. 189**).
- **Certifies Money Bill** (Art. 199(3)).
- Decides **Tenth Schedule** defection – subject to judicial review (**Kihoto Hollohan, 1992**).
- Adjourns or suspends the House for lack of quorum; allows a **secret sitting**.
- **Deputy Speaker** acts with full powers when the post is vacant or the Speaker is absent.
- **PRS Annual Review of State Laws 2025 (4 Jun 2026)**: Assemblies sat an average **24 days** (Odisha 43, Nagaland 7); ~33% of Bills passed in a single sitting; Deputy Speaker vacant in **8 States**; Jharkhand had none for 21 years.

### 11. PROCEEDINGS AND INDIRECT ELECTIONS
- **Art. 208**: order of proceedings set by Rules of Procedure.
- Governor's address on the **first day of the first session of each year**, followed by discussion → voting on demands (Assembly only).
- **MP Assembly order**: oath / affirmation → Governor's address → introduction of Ministers → mention of death.
| Election | MLAs vote? |
|---|---|
| **President** | Yes – elected MLAs (Art. 54) |
| **Vice-President** | **No** – only MPs |
| **Rajya Sabha** | Yes – elected MLAs, PR-STV; nominated MLAs cannot vote |
| **Legislative Council** | Yes – elect one-third of MLCs |
- Related: State Council of Ministers responsible to the Assembly (Art. 164(2)); 15% cap, minimum 12 (91st Amendment, 2003).
