## U0470 | DEMOGRAPHY OF INDIA – CENSUS 2011 FINDINGS, POPULATION GROWTH, DENSITY, SEX RATIO, LITERACY
### 1. CENSUS HISTORY
- **First Census attempted: 1872** (not 1871); **first synchronous / regular Census: 1881**; held every **10 years** since.
- **Census 2011**: **15th** national Census; motto **"Our Census, Our Future"**; the last Census published.
- **Census 2027**: includes **caste enumeration**; population enumeration from **February 2027**; mascots **Pragati and Vikas**.
- **Delimitation Bill 2026** is reported to use **2011 Census** data.
- **Census Town** definition criticised as it may **undercount urbanisation** (MoHUA, Sep 2026).
!TRAP First Census 1872; first synchronous Census 1881. Population did NOT stay unchanged across Censuses.
### 2. PHASES OF POPULATION GROWTH
#### 2.1 Phases
| Phase | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1901-21 | **Stagnant**; population **fell in 1911-21** |
| **1921** | **"Year of the Great Divide" / "Demographic Divide"** |
| 1921-51 | Steady growth |
| 1951-81 | Rapid growth; **1961-71 = "explosion"** |
| 1981-2011 | High but slowing |
- **1921** Census: the **only Census with negative decadal growth (−0.31%)**; faster growth after it. **1951 is NOT the demographic divide.**
#### 2.2 Decadal Growth Record
| Decade | India's decadal growth |
|---|---|
| **1961-71** | **24.80%** (highest) |
| 1971-81 | 24.66% |
| 1981-91 | 23.87% |
| 1991-2001 | **21.54%** (about 21%) |
| **2001-11** | **17.70%** – India's **lowest** decadal growth after 1921 |
- **Highest average annual exponential growth: 1971-81 (2.22%)**; 1991-2001 about **1.97%**; 2001-11 **1.64%**.
- **Largest decadal growth of urbanisation: 1971-81 (46.1%)**; UP's highest urban population growth also 1971-81 (**60.89%**).
- Highest literacy increase: **1991-2001 (about 12.62 points)**; 2001-11 about **8.20 points**.
#### 2.3 Population Totals
| Year | Population |
|---|---|
| 1951 | About **36 crore** |
| 1991 | **84.6 crore** |
| 2001 | **102.87 crore** (1028.74 million) – almost tripled since 1951 |
| 2011 | **121.09 crore** (1,21,08,54,977; 1210.85 million) |
| 2025 (State of World Population) | **1463.9 million** |
- India = about **17.5%** of world population (not 18.5%); high growth, likely to overtake China.
- **Replacement level** for zero population growth: **2.1 children per woman**.
- World population by religion (descending): **Christians, Muslims, Hindus, Buddhists**.

### 4. SEX RATIO
#### 4.1 National Trend
| Census | Sex ratio |
|---|---|
| 1901 | **972** |
| 1941 | 945 |
| **1951** | **946** |
| 1961 → 1971 | 941 → 930 (**sharpest decline**) |
| 1991 | **927** |
| 2001 | **933** |
| 2011 | **943** |
- Trend is **fluctuating**, not steadily falling. Male : female = 1000 : 933 (2001); 1000 : 943 (2011 final).
- **Child sex ratio (0-6)** has **continuously decreased**: **976 (1961), 962 (1981), 927 (2001), 919 (2011)**.
#### 4.2 States and UTs

| Highest UT | **Puducherry 1,037** (females outnumber males) |
| Lowest State | **Haryana 879** (861 in 2001) – though rich by per capita income |
| Lowest UT | **Daman and Diu 618** (710 in 2001; highest decline 1991-2001, 960 → 710) |
| Descending order 2011 | **Kerala, Puducherry, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh** |
| Southern order | Kerala 1,084 → TN 996 → AP |
| UP | 912 (2011); 898 (2001) |
| Lowest among States asked (2001) | Gujarat (920) |

| Rose 1991-2001 | Uttarakhand (936 → 962) |
| Fell 1991-2001 | Delhi (827 → 821), Haryana (865 → 861), Punjab (882 → 876) |
| Metro with highest sex ratio | **Chennai** (957 in 2001; 985 in 2011 P) |
- Persons aged 7+ (2011 P): males outnumber females in **Bihar, Maharashtra, UP and West Bengal** (all four).
- Religious sex ratio (descending): **Christian 1023, Buddhist 965, Jain 954, Muslim 951**.
- Cities: northern cities mostly unbalanced; eastern cities scattered, western cities concentrated; western cities have less rural migration.
#### 4.3 Child Sex Ratio (0-6)
- **Lowest: Haryana (834)** in both rural and urban areas; most boys per 100 girls (0-6): **Haryana (120)**; fewest: **Arunachal Pradesh (102)**.
- 2011 provisional: **Mizoram 971, Meghalaya 970**, Punjab 846, Haryana 830; highest among States asked: **Chhattisgarh (969)**.
#### 4.4 International and NFHS
- Sex ratio (2011) decreasing order: **Bangladesh, Pakistan, India, China**; most favourable among countries asked: **Finland**.
- Fear of falling world sex ratio: rising **sex-determination tests**.
- **NFHS-5 (2019-21)**: overall sex ratio **1,020** (NFHS-4: 991) – first time more women than men; **sex ratio at birth 929** (up from 919).
!PAIR Census 2011 | sex ratio | 943 || NFHS-5 | sex ratio | 1,020

### 6. LITERACY
#### 6.1 Trend
- **18.33% (1951)** (male 27.2%, female 8.9%) → **72.98% (2011)**.
- Female literacy 2011 provisional about **65.5%**; final **64.6%**. M.P.P.C.S. 1991 key gives India's female literacy as 63.0 percent.
- 1991-2001: male-female gap narrowed **24.8 → 21.6**; 2011 national gap **16 points**.
#### 6.2 State Rankings
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Highest | **Kerala ~94.0%** (93.9% P; 90.9% in 2001; 89.81% in 1991) |
| Second | **Mizoram** (91.3%) – after Kerala in 2001 and 2011 |
| Descending | Kerala (94.0), Mizoram (91.3), Goa (88.7), Maharashtra (82.3) |
| Descending (2011 P) | Tripura (87.75), Himachal (83.78), Sikkim (82.20), Nagaland (80.11) |
| Lowest | **Bihar 61.8%** (63.82% P; also lowest in 1991); male 71.2%, female 51.5% |
| Ascending lowest four | **Bihar (61.8), Arunachal (65.4), Rajasthan (66.1), Jharkhand (66.4)** |
| Maximum increase 2001-11 | **Bihar** (47.0% → 61.8%) |
| Highest UT | **Lakshadweep 91.8%** (also highest UT female and male literacy) |
| Highest rural (2001, among options) | Goa (79.67%); overall rural – Kerala |
| Highest religion group | **Jains** |
- **Female literacy**: Kerala 92.1, Mizoram 89.3, Goa 84.7 (highest); lowest final **Bihar 51.5%**; provisional lowest Rajasthan (52.66%); Rajasthan 52.1% lowest among States asked; 2001 lowest Bihar (33.1%); highest 2001 among States asked Mizoram; 1991 descending Kerala, Mizoram, Goa, Nagaland.
- **Gender gap**: largest **Rajasthan (28 points)**; largest among States asked UP (77.3% vs 57.2%); smallest **Meghalaya (3.1 points)**.
- **Districts**: most literate **Serchhip (Mizoram) 98.76%**; least **Alirajpur (MP) 37.22%** (2011 P). UP: Gautam Buddh Nagar (80.1%), then Kanpur Nagar (79.7%); 2001 PYQ – Auraiya; UP 2001 literacy 56.3% (male 68.8%, female 42.2%).
- Kerala's highest literacy is not explained by Christian share (highest Christian share is Nagaland).

- Mohenjodaro and Harappa are "dead cities" – their discovery in digging does not explain it.
