### 2. ELECTORAL COLLEGE (ARTS. 54, 55)
#### 2.1 Who votes and who does not
| Votes | Does NOT vote |
|---|---|
| **Elected** MPs of both Houses (LS + RS) | **Nominated** MPs and nominated MLAs |
| **Elected** MLAs of States | **All** members of Legislative Councils (elected and nominated) |
| Elected MLAs of **Delhi and Puducherry** (added by **70th Amendment, 1992**) | UTs without Assemblies |
- A **CM who sits in the Legislative Council cannot vote** (APPSC GS 2025).
- APPSC G2 2023 key: elected MPs, elected MLAs of States, Delhi/Puducherry MLAs.
- Members of Legislative Councils vote in **neither** the Presidential nor the Vice-Presidential election.
- Elected MLAs vote for Lok Sabha (as citizens), Rajya Sabha and the President.
#### 2.2 Method and value of vote
- **Indirect** election (college of elected MPs and MLAs); **proportional representation by single transferable vote**, **secret ballot**; taken from **Ireland** (Art. 55(3)).
- PR gives representation of minorities. PR-STV is used for President, VP, Rajya Sabha and Legislative Council.
- **MLA vote = (State population / elected MLAs) × 1/1000** – varies from State to State → **uniformity among States**.
- **MP vote = total value of MLAs' votes / total elected MPs** – uniform → **parity between Union and States** (Art. 55(1)).
- Population base: **1971 Census**, frozen by the **84th Amendment** till the first Census after 2026.
- Nomination: **50 proposers + 50 seconders** (since 1997; earlier 10 + 10). Vice-President: 20 + 20.
#### 2.3 Election disputes (Art. 71)
- Decided **only by the Supreme Court**; its decision is final.
- **Acts done** before the election is declared void **remain valid**.
- **Art. 71(4)**: election cannot be challenged or delayed because of **vacancies in the electoral college**; a dissolved Assembly cannot take part.
### 3. QUALIFICATIONS, TERM, OATH, EMOLUMENTS
- **Art. 58**: citizen of India (not necessarily by birth); **35 years**; qualified for **Lok Sabha**; no **office of profit** under Union, State or any local/other authority.
- **Education is not a qualification.**
- **Art. 56**: **5 years** from entering office; outgoing President continues until the successor enters office.
- **Art. 57**: re-election **any number of times**; no term limit.
- **Art. 60**: oath by the **CJI** (in absence, senior-most SC judge): "**preserve, protect and defend** the Constitution and the law". Not by the VP.
- **Resignation** in writing to the **Vice-President**, who informs the **Speaker**.
- **Art. 59**: not a member of any legislature; **rent-free** residence.
- Emoluments fixed by **Parliament by law**, listed in the **Second Schedule**, charged on the Consolidated Fund; **cannot be diminished** during the term.
- Salary **₹5 lakh per month** (Finance Act, 2018, from 1 Jan 2016).
### 4. IMPEACHMENT (ART. 61)
1. Ground: **"violation of the Constitution"** only (APPSC GS 2022: Art. 61).
2. Charge in **either House**: resolution signed by **one-fourth** of members after **14 days' notice**.
3. Passed by **two-thirds of total membership** of that House (simple majority not enough).
4. Other House **investigates** (President may appear or be represented); passes by **two-thirds** → impeached.
- **Quasi-judicial** procedure; borrowed from the **USA**.
- **Nominated MPs take part**; **elected MLAs** (States, Delhi, Puducherry) **do not**.
- **No President has ever been impeached.**
- "Impeachment" is used only for the President; judges and the VP are "**removed**".
!PAIR Election | elected MLAs vote | nominated MPs do not || Impeachment | nominated MPs take part | MLAs do not
### 5. PRESIDENT vs VICE-PRESIDENT
| Basis | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Electors | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (States, Delhi, Puducherry) | **All MPs**, elected and nominated; no MLAs |
| Qualification linked to | Lok Sabha | **Rajya Sabha** |
| Removal | Impeachment | RS resolution agreed to by LS |
| Resigns to | VP | President |
| Part of Parliament | Yes (Art. 79) | No |
| Vacancy | Election within 6 months | No 6-month deadline (Art. 68) |
- **11th Amendment (1961)** ended the joint-meeting method for electing the VP.
- Electoral college for the **16th VP election (2022)**: **788** members.
- RS Chairman (VP) is not a member; Deputy Chairman is a member.
### 6. VACANCY AND ACTING PRESIDENT (ARTS. 62, 65)
- Causes: expiry, death, resignation, impeachment, disqualification, election declared void.
- Expiry: election completed **before** the term ends (Art. 62(1)).
- Other vacancy: election **within six months** (Art. 62(2)); new President gets a **full 5-year term**.
- **VP acts** (Art. 65(1)); while acting, he stops functioning as RS Chairman – **Deputy Chairman presides**.
- Both offices vacant: **CJI**, then senior-most SC judge – **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**; not the PM.
- **M. Hidayatullah** – only CJI to act as President (**20 July–24 August 1969**).
- **July 2025**: VP **Jagdeep Dhankhar resigned**; **C.P. Radhakrishnan** elected – Arts. 65, 68 tested; third mid-term VP vacancy.
### 7. POWERS OF THE PRESIDENT
#### 7.1 Executive and appointments
- Appoints **PM** (majority leader – a **convention**) and Ministers, **Governors (155)**, SC/HC judges, **CAG (148)**, **AG (76)**, UPSC, **Election Commissioners (324)**, Finance Commission, Ambassadors and High Commissioners (on ministerial advice, not own discretion).
- **Art. 78**: seeks information from the PM.
- **Art. 240**: **regulations** for A&N Islands, Lakshadweep, DNH and Daman & Diu, and **Puducherry** when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved.
| Appointment | Basis |
|---|---|
| CJI, SC/HC judges | Arts. 124(2), 217(1): consultation with CJI; collegium (CJI + 4, Third Judges Case 1998) |
| CAG, Governors, AG | Advice of PM / Council (Art. 74) |
- APPSC G2 2025 key: CJI, judges, AG, Governors – all formally on the PM's recommendation (Art. 74).
#### 7.2 Legislative
- **Part of Parliament (Art. 79)**; no Bill becomes law without assent.
- Summons/prorogues Houses; **dissolves Lok Sabha**; summons **joint sitting** (chaired by **Speaker**).
- Addresses under **Arts. 86, 87** (first session after general election and first session of each year); sends messages.
- **Nominates 12** to Rajya Sabha.
- Prior recommendation for **Money Bills** and **Art. 3** Bills.
- 2026: addressed the joint sitting at the start of the Budget Session (Art. 87).
#### 7.3 Financial
- Budget laid in his name; Money Bills and demands for grants on his recommendation; advances from the **Contingency Fund**.
#### 7.4 Judicial, emergency, military, diplomatic
- **Art. 72** pardon (a **judicial power**); **Art. 143** advisory opinion – **not binding**.
- Emergencies: **352** National, **356** State, **360** Financial.
- **Supreme Commander**; declares war and makes peace on Council's advice; treaties in his name.
### 8. ORDINANCE (ART. 123)
- Only when Parliament (either House) is **not in session**; has the force of an Act.
- Must be **laid** before Parliament; lapses **six weeks after reassembly**; may be **withdrawn** any time.
- Subject to **judicial review**.
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **D.C. Wadhwa (1987)** | Repeated **re-promulgation** in Bihar = **fraud on the Constitution** |
| **Krishna Kumar Singh (2017)** | Seven judges (5:2); laying is **mandatory**; satisfaction reviewable |
- Latest: **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** (President Droupadi Murmu; gazette **16 May 2026**): SC judges **33 → 37**, excluding the CJI.
- Governor's parallel power: **Art. 213**.
### 9. VETO POWER (ART. 111)
| Veto | Meaning | Indian President? |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute** | Withholds assent – private members' Bills; Government Bills after Cabinet resigns | Yes |
| **Suspensive** | Returns once; must assent if re-passed – **Ordinary Bills only** | Yes – **not Money Bills** |
| **Pocket** | Keeps Bill pending – **no time limit** | Yes |
| **Qualified** | Overridable by higher majority (US) | **No** |
- **Money Bill**: assent or withhold only. **Constitutional Amendment Bill**: **must assent (24th Amendment)**.
!TRAP "Suspensive veto on Money Bills" = FALSE (APPSC G1 2024).
### 10. PARDONING POWER (ART. 72 vs 161)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pardon** | Removes **both sentence and conviction** |
| **Commutation** | **Lighter form** (death → rigorous life) |
| **Remission** | **Shorter period**, same character (2 years → 1 year) |
| **Respite** | Lesser sentence for special reasons (**pregnancy, disability**) |
| **Reprieve** | **Temporary stay** of execution |
- Art. 72 covers: (a) **court-martial**, (b) offence against **Union law**, (c) **death sentence**. Amnesty is outside Art. 72.
| Point | President (72) | Governor (161) |
|---|---|---|
| Offences | Union laws | State laws |
| Death sentence | Can pardon | Cannot pardon; can suspend, remit, commute |
| Court-martial | Yes | **No** |
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Maru Ram (1980)** | Exercised on Council's advice |
| **Kehar Singh (1989)** | May examine evidence afresh; **no right of oral hearing** |
| **Epuru Sudhakar v Govt of AP (2006)** | **Limited judicial review** – mala fide, arbitrary, irrelevant grounds |
| Madras HC Full Bench (Apr 2026) | Governor bound by Cabinet advice on remission |
### 11. POSITION OF THE PRESIDENT (ART. 74)
- **Nominal / de jure** executive; PM = **real / de facto**.
- **42nd Amendment (1976)**: advice **binding**.
- **44th Amendment (1978)**: may **return once** for reconsideration, then must act.
- **Art. 74(2)**: advice **cannot be inquired into** by any court.
- Limited discretion: PM in a **hung Parliament**; dismissing a government that lost majority; dissolving LS; **pocket veto**.
- Ambedkar's model; no term limit criticised. Compare: **USA** two terms (**22nd Amendment**); **South Korea** single 5-year term.
