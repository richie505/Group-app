## U0290 | CONFLICT AND HARMONY BETWEEN FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS AND DPSP
### 1. FR VS DPSP – BASIC DIFFERENCES
| Point | Fundamental Rights | DPSP |
|---|---|---|
| Courts | **Justiciable** | **Non-justiciable** (Art. 37) |
| Nature | Mostly **negative** – limit the State | **Positive** directions to the State |
| Aim | **Political democracy** | **Social and economic democracy** |
| Violation | Law **void under Article 13** (to the extent of inconsistency) | Law **cannot be struck down** merely for breaking a DPSP |
- Main difference: DPSPs are **not enforceable in courts**, but are **fundamental in the governance** of the country; the State must apply them in making laws.
- **"FRs are the means and DPSPs are the ends"** – complementary, not competing.
- The **Preamble** may be used to decide the scope of FRs and DPSPs.
- **Parliament may amend FRs to implement DPSPs** (within the basic structure).
!TRAP The **44th** Amendment did NOT give DPSPs primacy over Arts 14 and 19 – the attempt was the **42nd** (struck down in Minerva Mills).
### 2. LANDMARK CASES – POSITION AND ORDER
| Case | Year | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **A.K. Gopalan** v. State of Madras | 1950 | **Narrow reading of Article 21** (personal liberty, not property) |
| **Champakam Dorairajan** | 1951 | **FRs prevail** over DPSPs; communal order violating Art. 29(2) struck down – **first FR-DPSP conflict case** |
| **In re Kerala Education Bill** | 1958 | **Harmonious construction**; FRs still prevail in direct conflict |
| **Golaknath** v. State of Punjab | 1967 | **Parliament cannot amend FRs**; FRs "transcendental"; **prospective overruling**; first time SC limited the amending power |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | 1973 | FRs amendable but **not the Basic Structure** (doctrine first given) |
| **Maneka Gandhi** | 1978 | Widened Art. 21 – procedure must be **fair, just and reasonable** |
| **Minerva Mills** | 1980 | **Balance between Parts III and IV** is part of Basic Structure |
| **Sanjeev Coke** | 1983 | 39(b) includes **privately owned** resources; doubted Minerva's 31C ruling (obiter) |
- Chronology: **Gopalan → Champakam → Golaknath → Kesavananda → Minerva Mills → Sanjeev Coke**.
- Bench size: **Kesavananda – 13 judges (largest)**; **Golaknath – 11 judges** (second largest).
- Golaknath, Kesavananda, Minerva Mills concerned **property rights and amending power**.
!TRAP Champakam did NOT adopt harmonious construction – that was Kerala Education Bill (1958).
### 3. CONSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE TO CHAMPAKAM
- **1st Amendment, 1951** inserted **Article 15(4)** – special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes, SCs, STs.
- Right changed: **Article 15**, overriding the **Article 29(2)** bar applied in the case.
!KEY 15(4) answered Champakam; 31A, 31B and the Ninth Schedule answered the land-reform judgments.
### 4. AMENDMENTS RESPONDING TO COURTS
| Amendment | Year | Response to | Key change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | 1951 | Champakam, land-reform cases | Art. 15(4), **31A, 31B, Ninth Schedule** |
| **4th** | 1955 | – | Adequacy of compensation made non-justiciable |
| **17th** | 1964 | – | Widened **"estate"** in 31A; land under personal cultivation acquired only at market value |
| **24th** | 1971 | **Golaknath** | Amend any part incl. FRs; added **13(4), 368(3)**; **President's assent mandatory** |
| **25th** | 1971 | **Bank Nationalisation (R.C. Cooper, 1970)** | "Compensation" → **"amount"**; inserted **Art. 31C** |
| **26th** | 1971 | **Privy Purses case** (Madhav Rao Scindia, 1970) | Abolished privy purses |
| **42nd** | 1976 | Kesavananda | 31C to **all DPSPs**; unlimited amending power – struck down in Minerva Mills |
### 5. ARTICLE 31C – STEP BY STEP
| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| **25th Amendment, 1971** | (1) Laws for **39(b) and (c)** not void for violating **Arts 14, 19 (and 31)**; (2) a declaration that a law gives effect to that policy cannot be questioned in court |
| **Kesavananda, 1973** | **First part upheld**; **second part** (bar on judicial review) **struck down** |
| **42nd Amendment, 1976** | Extended protection to laws implementing **any DPSP** |
| **Minerva Mills, 1980** | Struck down the extension; 31C back to **39(b), (c) only** |
| **Property Owners Association, 2024** | **Original 31C** (as upheld in Kesavananda) **continues in force** |
- Minerva Mills also held that an **Emergency proclamation is subject to judicial review**.
- Scope today: narrow DPSP primacy, only for **39(b)-(c)** laws.
!TRAP APPSC G1 2024: "the first provision of 31C was invalidated in Kesavananda" is WRONG – it was the **second**.
### 6. HARMONIOUS CONSTRUCTION AND BASIC STRUCTURE
- Meaning: read **Part III and Part IV together** so that both operate as far as possible.
- Cases: **Kerala Education Bill (1958)**, **Minerva Mills (1980)**.
- Minerva Mills: FR-DPSP **balance is part of the Basic Structure**; absolute primacy to either destroys it.
- Justice Chandrachud: the two together are the **conscience of the Constitution**.
- DPSPs strengthen FRs: **48A + 21** = right to healthy environment; **45 + 21** → **21A** (86th Amendment, 2002).
- Non-justiciability does not stop DPSPs from being used in interpretation.
### 7. NINTH SCHEDULE AND ARTICLE 31B
- Added by the **1st Amendment, 1951**, under PM **Jawaharlal Nehru**, to protect **agrarian reforms** in certain States.
- Grew to **over 280 entries**; not limited to land reforms.
| Case | Year | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Waman Rao** | 1981 | Entries after **24 April 1973** (Kesavananda date) open to challenge |
| **I.R. Coelho** | **2007** | No blanket immunity; test = Basic Structure + **Arts 14, 19, 21 ("golden triangle")** |
- Reservation Acts placed in the Ninth Schedule are also open to judicial review.
### 8. ARTICLE 39(b) – MATERIAL RESOURCES OF THE COMMUNITY
| Case | Year | View |
|---|---|---|
| **Ranganatha Reddy** | 1977 | **Krishna Iyer (minority)**: includes **all privately owned** resources |
| **Sanjeev Coke** | 1983 | Followed Krishna Iyer view |
| **Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra** | **5 Nov 2024** | **9-judge Bench, 7:2**, CJI **D.Y. Chandrachud**: **not every private resource** is a community material resource |
- Test: the resource's **nature, scarcity, effect on community well-being** and consequences of **concentration** in private hands.
- Rejected the broad Krishna Iyer view.
!PAIR Ranganatha Reddy | 1977 | all private resources || Property Owners Association | 2024 | not every private resource
