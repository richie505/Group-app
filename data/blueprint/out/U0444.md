## U0444 | BASIC CHARACTERISTICS OF THE INDIAN ECONOMY – FEATURES OF A DEVELOPING ECONOMY
### 1. TYPES OF ECONOMIES
#### 1.1 Comparison
| Type | Core feature | Example / proponent |
|---|---|---|
| **Capitalist** | Private ownership, profit motive, market prices; inequality is a feature | USA |
| **Socialist** | Collective ownership, equitable distribution, State welfare | Socialist elements in Sweden, Norway |
| **Command / controlled (planned)** | **Central plan replaces the market**; government decides what, how and for whom | Former USSR, North Korea |
| **Gandhian** | Decentralised, self-contained villages, cottage industries | **Gandhian Plan 1944 – S.N. Agarwal** |
| **Traditional** | **Custom and tradition** decide what, how and for whom | – |
| **Mixed** | **Coexistence of public and private sectors** | **India**, UK, Germany |
| **Dual** | Modern sector + traditional agrarian sector | Lewis model |
#### 1.2 Mixed Economy vs Joint Sector
- **Mixed economy**: public and private sectors go side by side; the State **regulates the private sector through taxes, subsidies and credit**; planning treats both as complementary; combines capitalism and socialism.
- **Joint sector**: **one enterprise jointly owned** by government and private investors.
- **Public sector**: government ownership or control (e.g. **Bhilai Steel Plant**); **private sector**: private ownership or control.
- **"Commanding heights"** held by the State (heavy industry, infrastructure, finance); private sector in consumer goods under regulation.
#### 1.3 Why India Is Mixed
- A **pragmatic middle path** between liberal-capitalist, Soviet and Gandhian models; aim – **growth by democratic means** with a social order based on **justice**.
- The Indian model protects the interest of **both State and Person**.
!KEY UPPSC 2024: India is an underdeveloped economy AND a mixed economy.
### 2. NATURE OF THE ECONOMY – LABELS
| Label | Defining feature |
|---|---|
| **Developing** | Low per capita income; low capital formation (needs a **high savings rate**); high public investment in infrastructure is a precondition for private growth |
| **Underdeveloped** | Low PCI, low capital formation, **predominance of agriculture**, **disguised unemployment**, high dependency ratio |
| **Dual** | Modern industrial/urban sector coexists with a traditional, subsistence agrarian sector |
| **Labour-surplus** | Surplus farm labour with **marginal productivity near zero** – the **Lewis** setting |
| **Mixed** | Public-private coexistence (this is NOT a feature of underdevelopment) |
- India is a **developing** economy that still shows **underdeveloped** features.
### 3. FEATURES OF AN UNDERDEVELOPED ECONOMY
- **Low per capita income**; **inequitable income distribution**; slower increase in national income.
- **Low capital formation** – depends on savings and good financial institutions.
- **Heavy dependence on agriculture**; **high population growth**; **high dependency ratio**.
- **Disguised unemployment**: more workers than needed; **marginal productivity zero**; removing extra workers would not cut output; mainly **agriculture/primary** and the unorganised sector.
- India: predominance of agriculture, low PCI, massive unemployment. **Industrial dominance** marks a developed economy, not India.
!TRAP "Lack of natural resources" is NOT a characteristic of the Indian economy.
### 4. INDIA AT INDEPENDENCE AND CAUSES OF UNDERDEVELOPMENT
#### 4.1 Features at Independence
- **First National Income Committee (1951)**: 1948-49 national income **₹8,710 crore**; per capita **₹225**.
- Agriculture: **72%** of the workforce (**1951**).
- Weak industrial base: capital goods only **one-fourth** of industrial output.
- Literacy below **16%**; life expectancy **32 years** (1947); thin social overhead capital.
- Colonial legacy (from **1757, Battle of Plassey**): India turned from textile exporter into a **raw-material supplier and captive market** – NOT a supplier of manufactured goods; economy stagnated.
- **Angus Maddison**: India's share of world GDP **22.6% (1700) → 3.8% (1952)**.
#### 4.2 Causes
| Cause | Note |
|---|---|
| Colonial legacy | Deindustrialisation |
| Capital deficiency | Low savings |
| Population pressure | High dependency ratio; surplus workers in farming |
| Low productivity | Disguised unemployment |
| Weak human capital | Low literacy, low life expectancy |
- **Most important cause**: **co-existence of under-utilised manpower and under-utilised natural resources** (APPSC key) – NOT high density, lack of capital or low PCI alone.
- Recorded real causes: rapid population growth with high dependency, low human development, high poverty.
- **Not** a main cause: **working efficiency of the population** (APPSC G2 2017).
- Ending poverty needs **all of**: population control, higher savings and more productive investment.
#### 4.3 Economists
| Economist | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Adam Smith** (Scottish) | **Father of (Modern) Economics** / Capitalism – not Keynes |
| **J.M. Keynes** | Modern macroeconomic thought |
| **Ragnar Nurkse** | **Vicious circle of poverty**: low income → low saving → low investment → low productivity → low income |
| **W. Arthur Lewis** | **Dual-sector model**: surplus labour moves from subsistence to modern sector |
| **Angus Maddison** | World GDP share history |

### 5. SECTORS BY ACTIVITY
#### 5.1 Primary, Secondary, Tertiary
| Sector | Nature | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Uses/extracts natural resources | Agriculture, forestry, **fishing**, **dairy farm**, **mining and quarrying**, **mineral exploration** |
| **Secondary** | Converts raw materials into goods | **Manufacturing**, **textile mills**, **weaving cloth**, **construction**, **electricity, gas, water supply** |
| **Tertiary** (service sector) | Services, not physical goods | **Trade**, **transport**, **storage** (incl. storage of farm produce), communication, **banking**, real estate, **marketing**, health, education, social work, personal services |
- Eight industrial divisions: agriculture; mining and quarrying; manufacturing; electricity, gas and water supply; construction; trade; transport and storage; services.
- Mining is primary but grouped under "industry" in the three-way agriculture/industry/services split.
#### 5.2 Real vs Financial Sector
| Basis | Real sector | Financial sector |
|---|---|---|
| Involves | Production and flow of goods and services | Money and financial assets |
| Examples | **Farmer harvesting crops**, **textile mills making fabric** | **Bank lending**, corporate bonds |
!KEY Match (G1 2022, 2023): Trade = Service; Water supply = Industrial; Fisheries = Primary; GDP estimation = CSO (now NSO).
### 6. OWNERSHIP STRUCTURE
| Sector | Ownership |
|---|---|
| Public | Government (general government + public corporations) |
| Private | Private corporations + households |
| Joint | Government + private in one firm |
| Cooperative | Member-owned |
- **Largest share of national income**: **private sector** (APPSC G2 2008).
- GVA is compiled for **four institutional sectors**: **general government, public corporations, private corporations, households** (cooperatives are NOT a separate one).
- **Gross domestic savings** (descending): **household > private corporate > public corporations > government**.
- Private corporate investment: **12.3% of GDP (FY23) → 11.2% (FY24)**; its share of GFCF at a decadal low of **33%** (Aug 2026).
### 7. SECTORAL COMPOSITION – GDP SHARE VS EMPLOYMENT
| Period | GDP / GVA share | Employment share |
|---|---|---|
| 1947 | Agriculture ~50% | Agriculture 75%+ |
| 1951 | Agriculture dominant | Agriculture **72%** |
| 1980-81 → 1990-91 | Primary 38.1% → 34.0%; secondary 25.9% → 24.9% | Agriculture 66.7% (1981) |
| 1993-94 | – | Agriculture 64.75%; industry 12.43%; services 22.82% |
| 2001 → 2011 | – | Agricultural workers 58.2% → 54.6% |
| 2024-25 (new series) | **Tertiary 52.9%, secondary 25.8%, primary 21.4%** | Agriculture 42.4% (Q2 FY26) |
- Agriculture now about **18% of GVA** (17.88%, 2025-26 SAE) but employs **46.1%** of workers (PLFS 2023-24).
- **Tertiary** is the **largest contributor** to GDP; its share has **risen since 1980**.
- **Structural change**: agriculture's output share fell, industry and services grew, but jobs stayed mostly in agriculture (G1 2022 assertion-reason: both true, R explains A).
- Debate – **premature leapfrogging**: services rise while manufacturing stagnates.
### 8. DUAL ECONOMY – ORGANISED VS UNORGANISED, RURAL VS URBAN
- **Organised** = formal, regulated, social security; **unorganised** = informal.
- Unorganised sector: **over 80% of workers**, about **45% of output**.
- Organised-sector employment growth **declined** in 1994-2007 (Economic Survey 2009-10).
- Census 2011: **68.8% rural, 31.2% urban**; rural share of workforce **72.4%**.
- **Work participation rate 39.8%** (Census 2011) – higher in less developed States (AP, Chhattisgarh, HP, Karnataka, NE) as subsistence work needs manual labour.
- Regional imbalance: per capita income gaps, poverty ratio, sectoral mix; inter-state Gini of PCI **0.230 (1994-95) → 0.261 (2025-26)**; Telangana's PCI = **1.35 times** AP's (2024-25).
### 9. DEMOGRAPHIC FEATURES
- Census 2011: **121.09 crore**; sex ratio **940** (933 in 2001); females **48.46%** (provisional; final 48.53%).
- **TFR**: 2.7 (NFHS-3) → 2.2 (NFHS-4) → 2.0 (NFHS-5) → **1.9** now, below the **replacement level 2.1**.
- Median age: 24 (2011) → about 29; about **36 by 2036**.
- Dependency ratio expected to fall from 65% to 54% over a decade; **demographic dividend peaks around 2030**.

| Measure | Position |
