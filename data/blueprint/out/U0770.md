## U0770 | SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT – MEANING, NATURE, SCOPE AND COMPONENTS
### 1. MEANING AND DEFINITION
#### 1.1 Core Meaning
- Sustainable development = using resources **today** without compromising **future generations'** ability to meet their needs.
- Balance of **economic, social and environmental** concerns – not growth alone.
#### 1.2 Brundtland Commission (1987)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Body | **World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED)** |
| Chairperson | **Gro Harlem Brundtland** (Prime Minister of Norway) |
| Report | **"Our Common Future"** (Brundtland Report) |
| Year | **1987** |
| Contribution | **First definition**; coined/popularised "Sustainable Development" |
- Definition: "Development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**."
- Our Common Future **linked economic development with environmental stability**; it laid the foundation for Rio 1992.
!KEY First definition of sustainable development = **Brundtland Report, 1987**.

#### 3.1 Rio 1992 Details
- **Agenda 21**: comprehensive action plan for SD at **local, national and global** levels; emerged from UNCED 1992.
- **UNFCCC**: drawn and opened for signature at UNCED, Rio, 1992.
- **Forest Principles**: a **non-binding** statement (not a treaty).
- **UN Commission on Sustainable Development (CSD)**: reviews implementation of Agenda 21 and the Rio Declaration.
- Rio 1992 = **first Earth Summit**.
#### 3.2 Rio+20 Details
- Rio+20 = **20-year follow-up to 1992** and **10-year follow-up to 2002**; the **third** international conference on SD, hosted by **Brazil**.
- "The Future We Want" is a **2012** outcome, **not 1992**.
- **SDGs were born at Rio+20**; part of Agenda 2030 since 2015.
- **PAGE (Partnership for Action on Green Economy)**: emerged at UNCSD 2012; five agencies – **UNEP, UNDP, ILO, UNIDO, UNITAR**.
#### 3.3 Stockholm vs Rio vs Rio+20 – Easy Comparison
| Point | Stockholm 1972 | Rio 1992 | Rio+20 2012 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal name | UNCHE | UNCED | UNCSD |
| Theme word | **Human Environment** | **Environment and Development** | **Sustainable Development** |
| Main body / output | UNEP; Stockholm Declaration | Agenda 21; Rio Declaration; UNFCCC; CBD | "The Future We Want"; SDG decision; PAGE |
| Popular name | First world environment conference | First Earth Summit | Rio+20 |
- Memory aid: the formal names move from **environment** (1972) to **environment + development** (1992) to **sustainable development** (2002 and 2012).
!TRAP UNEP came out of **Stockholm 1972**, not Rio 1992. Rio+5 was in **New York**, not Rio.

- Agenda 2030 dimensions: **economic, social, environmental**.

### 5. THREE PILLARS AND TRIPLE BOTTOM LINE
#### 5.1 Pillars
| Pillar | Alternate phrasing (2024–25) |
|---|---|
| **Economic development** | Economic growth |
| **Social equity** | Social inclusion |
| **Environmental protection** | Environmental protection |
- UN's three pillars: **economic growth, environmental protection, social equity** – interlinked.
- **Triple bottom line**: **People, Planet, Profit** (social, environmental, economic) – business version of the three pillars.
!TRAP Odd-one-out: **political, military (security) or cultural** dimensions are NOT among the three classic pillars.
#### 5.2 Related Terms
- **Economic sustainability** does NOT mean maximising profits without considering social or environmental factors.
- **Poverty alleviation** is NOT an environmental-protection issue; that list = climate change, pollution, environmental degradation, resource depletion.
### 6. GOALS AND PRINCIPLES
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intergenerational equity** | Present must not deplete resources at the cost of future generations |
| **Precautionary principle** | Lack of full scientific certainty is **no reason to postpone** cost-effective measures against environmental harm |
| **Polluter pays** | Polluter bears the cost of preventing and controlling pollution |
| **CBDR** – Common but Differentiated Responsibilities | Shared duty; **developed nations carry more** (capacity, historical contribution) |
| **Carrying capacity** | Human use within what ecosystems can support (finite Earth) |
- Principles endorsed at **Rio 1992**; underpin international environmental law.
#### 6.1 How the Principles Work Together
- **Intergenerational equity** is the heart of the Brundtland definition: today's users hold resources in trust for future users.
- **Precautionary principle** acts **before harm** happens, even when science is not fully certain.
- **Polluter pays** acts **after or during** pollution: the cost falls on the polluter, not on the public.
- **CBDR** applies equity **between nations**: all share the duty, but rich nations do more because of their capacity and past emissions.
- **Carrying capacity** sets the outer limit: no use beyond what ecosystems can support.
!PAIR Precautionary principle | Prevent before certainty | Vellore case || Polluter pays | Polluter bears cost | Vellore, M.C. Mehta
!TRAP Odd-one-out as a goal: **"maximising economic growth at any cost"** – SD needs balance.
### 7. NATURE AND SCOPE
#### 7.1 Includes and Excludes
| Includes | Excludes |
|---|---|
| Meeting **present needs** (development not rejected) | Growth that **runs down natural capital** |
| Equity **within and across generations** | Treating environment and development as **opposites** |
| Inclusion – **"leave no one behind"** | Profit without social/environmental concern |
- Assertion-reason logic: the **present generation may use resources**, but not in a way that compromises future generations' ability to meet their needs.
#### 7.2 Natural Capital
- **Natural capital** = renewable and non-renewable resources created by nature: **air, water, land, minerals, forests**.
- **Roads** are NOT natural capital (man-made).
#### 7.3 National Environment Policy, 2006
- Policy framework for environmental conservation and SD.
- **Conservation and livelihood security are linked**, not opposed.
- **Wetlands (Conservation & Management) Rules, 2010**: framed on the recommendation of an **Expert Panel** under NEP 2006.
### 8. MEASURES OF SUSTAINABILITY
| Measure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Green GDP** | GDP adjusted for **natural-resource depletion and environmental damage** |
| **Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI)** | **Adds unpaid work**; **subtracts pollution, crime, resource loss** |
| **Ecological footprint** | Productive land and sea needed to meet consumption and absorb wastes; in **global hectares** |
| **Earth Overshoot Day** | Date when humanity's demand **exceeds what Earth can regenerate** that year |
- **Earth Overshoot Day**: related to **environmental degradation**; conceived by **Andrew Simms** (New Economics Foundation, UK); first campaign **2006** with **Global Footprint Network**.
- Why new measures: ordinary **GDP counts output only**; it treats loss of forests, water or minerals as income and ignores pollution. Green GDP and GPI correct this, while ecological footprint and Earth Overshoot Day show demand against nature's supply.
!PAIR GDP | Counts output only | Ignores depletion || Green GDP | Output minus depletion and damage | Sustainability measure
### 9. JUDICIAL RECOGNITION IN INDIA
| Case / body | Principle |
|---|---|
| **Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996)** | Made **sustainable development, precautionary principle and polluter pays** part of Indian environmental law |
| **M.C. Mehta v. UoI (1996)** | **Polluter pays** |
- **National Green Tribunal**: HQ **New Delhi**; benches at **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai** – **no bench at Hyderabad**.
- NGT must apply: **sustainable development, precautionary principle, polluter-pays principle**.
