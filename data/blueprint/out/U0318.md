### 3. HIGH COURT JUDGES
#### 3.1 Appointment and Qualifications
- **Chief Justice**: by the **President** after consulting the **CJI and Governor**.
- **Other judges**: after consulting CJI, Governor and **HC Chief Justice**; for a common HC, **Governors of all States** concerned.
- Appointed by **warrant**; the **Governor cannot appoint** or remove.
- **Qualifications**: citizen; **10 years judicial office** or **10 years HC advocate**; **no distinguished jurist** route (only SC, Art. 124(3)(c)).
- Collegium: **HC CJ + 2** senior-most HC judges → **CJI + 2** senior-most SC judges.
- Sanctioned strength (18 Sep 2025): **1,122** for all HCs, **804** filled. **8 Aug 2026**: 30 HC judges appointed (Madras 15, Karnataka 6, Calcutta, MP 1).
#### 3.2 Tenure, Transfer, Removal
- Retire at **62** – raised from **60** by the **15th Amendment, 1963** (R explains A); **114th Amendment Bill (2010)** sought 65.
- **Resignation** to the **President** (Art. 217(1)(a)).
- **Art. 222**: **President** transfers HC judges after consulting the **CJI** – only HC judges; **no provision to transfer SC judges**.
- **Justice H.R. Khanna (80th Report, 8th Law Commission)**: at least one-third of HC judges from another State.
- **Removal**: Art. 217(1)(b) / **218** with Art. 124(4) and **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968** – President's order after Parliament's address.
| Judge | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Soumitra Sen** (Calcutta HC) | Resigned **2011** after Rajya Sabha voted for removal |
| **G.R. Swaminathan** (Madras HC) | Motion noticed by **107 MPs**, Dec 2025 |
| **Yashwant Varma** (formerly **Allahabad HC**, not Madras) | Inquiry report to be tabled despite resignation (Jul 2026) |
- **19 April 2004**: 25 of 28 Punjab and Haryana HC judges went on mass leave.

### 4. HIGH COURT POWERS
- **Art. 226**: writs for **FRs and other legal rights** ("any other purpose") – **wider than Art. 32**; discretionary; can reach outside its territory if the **cause of action** arises within. Art. 32 itself is an FR (R does not explain A).
- **Art. 227**: administrative and judicial **superintendence** over all subordinate courts and tribunals; may act **suo motu**; **227(4)** – not over Armed Forces courts/tribunals. SC has no Art. 227-type power.
- **Appellate**: second civil appeals only on a **substantial question of law**; Sessions Court **death sentences** need HC confirmation (**BNSS 2023**, replaced CrPC from **1 July 2024**).
- **HC Chief Justice**: dual capacity; administrative acts open to **writs under Art. 226** before another judge of the same HC – no absolute immunity.
#### 4.1 Court of Record and Contempt
- Records have **evidentiary value**, cannot be questioned in a lower court; power to punish **contempt** (Arts **129 / 215**).
- **Contempt of Court Act, 1926** – first major law; **H.N. Sanyal Committee (1961)** → **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971**: **civil** = wilful disobedience; **criminal** = scandalising or obstructing justice. Aim – public confidence (R explains A).
- Jul 2026: SC (CJI Surya Kant bench) shifted "bulldozer justice" contempt pleas to HCs. Sep 2026: SC refused to stay Bombay HC contempt proceedings over a judicial officer's outburst.
- Notable: **Uttarakhand HC** declared the entire animal kingdom legal entities; Madras HC – RTI applicant must state object of request.

### 7. JUDICIAL REVIEW – MEANING, ORIGIN, THEORY
- Power of the SC and HCs to test **laws and executive orders** against the Constitution and declare them **void**; not a judgment on policy wisdom, not an appeal.
- The term is **not used in the Constitution**; part of the **basic structure** (**Kesavananda 1973**; **L. Chandra Kumar 1997**).
- **Origin USA**: **Marbury v. Madison (1803)**, CJ **John Marshall**; roots in **Sir Edward Coke** vs King James I. Exists in Canada, Australia; **UK** – parliamentary sovereignty, no voiding of Acts.
- Parliamentary form first in Great Britain; presidential form first in the USA; FRs amendable first held in **Shankari Prasad**.
- Basis: written Constitution + **federalism** + FRs. **Symbol of federal government** (APPSC key) – courts as umpire of the Centre-State division. **Theory**: **supremacy of the Constitution** / limited government.
- India = **American judicial supremacy + British parliamentary sovereignty**; judicial review **limits Parliament's sovereignty** – laws cannot break the Basic Structure.
- Grounds: violation of FRs; lack of **legislative competence**; contravening the Constitution / Basic Structure – not disagreement with majority policy.

### 9. LIMITS, AMENDMENTS AND OVERCOMING A JUDGMENT
- **Narrower than USA**: Art. 21 "**procedure established by law**" vs US "**due process of law**" (US SC = "third chamber").
- **Political questions** avoided; writs refused where an alternative remedy exists; delimitation review limited but possible if capricious (**Kishorchandra Chhanganlal Rathod, 2024**).
| Amendment | Effect |
|---|---|
| **1st (1951)** | After land-reform / reservation rulings; created the **Ninth Schedule** (assent 18 June 1951) |
| **38th (1975)** | President's / Governor's satisfaction under Arts 123, 213, 352 non-justiciable |
| **39th (1975)** | Art. 329A shielded PM's election – clause (4) struck down in **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)** as violating basic structure |
| **42nd (1976)** | Barred HCs from reviewing **central laws** |
| **43rd (1977)** | **Restored** that power |
| **44th (1978)** | Reversed the 38th bar |
!TRAP 42nd barred → 43rd restored; 38th barred → 44th reversed.
#### 9.1 Overcoming a Judgment
- Legislature **cannot simply overrule** a judgment. Options:
1. **Ordinary / validating law** removing the basis of the judgment (retrospectively if needed).
2. **Constitutional amendment** – subject to the Basic Structure.
3. **Review petition** (Art. 137); **curative petition** as last resort.
- Example: **Tribunals Reforms Act 2021** re-enacted struck-down conditions → struck down again in **Madras Bar Association (2025)**.
### 10. JUDICIAL ACTIVISM AND PIL
#### 10.1 Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Judicial activism** (= judicial dynamism) | Wider intervention shaping policy; opposite of **restraint** |
| **PIL** (APPSC 2019) | Legal action for public / general interest in which the public or a class has a pecuniary or other interest |
| **Social Action Litigation** | Other name of PIL – **outcome of judicial activism**, not restraint (APPSC-G2 2023) |
| **Locus standi** relaxed | Any public-spirited citizen can move court |
| **Epistolary jurisdiction** | Letters, postcards, newspaper reports as petitions |
- PIL under **Art. 32 (SC)** or **Art. 226 (HC)**; in line with **Art. 39A**; court-made.
#### 10.2 Origin and Stages
- **Origin USA** (APPSC-G2 2025); term coined **1947** by **Arthur Schlesinger Jr.**; restraint also developed in the USA.
- India: **mid-1970s, post-Emergency**, on the SC's initiative – **V.R. Krishna Iyer, P.N. Bhagwati** (pioneers), with O. Chinnappa Reddy, D.A. Desai.
- **First PIL**: **Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)** – speedy trial for undertrials; filed by **Kapila Hingorani ("Mother of PILs")**.
- PIL era under CJI **Y.V. Chandrachud (1978-85)**; CJI keyed at PIL's introduction – **P.N. Bhagwati** (CJI from 1985).
| Stage | Feature |
|---|---|
| Mid-1970s | Open access; activism the "new feature" |
| 1979-1980s | PIL; Art. 21 expanded – livelihood, education, health, environment |
| 1990s on | Governance – CVC, 2G, coal blocks, candidates' affidavits |
| 2017 | **Puttaswamy** – privacy a fundamental right |
#### 10.3 Instruments and Doctrines
- **Suo motu cognisance**; **continuing mandamus** (case kept open); **guidelines** filling a vacuum (**Vishaka 1997** → POSH Act 2013); **Art. 142**; court-appointed **expert committees**.
- Genuine SC evolutions: **Basic Structure**, **PIL**, removal of handcuffs, continuing mandamus, expansion of Art. 21.
- **Not** an SC evolution: "exemptions to FRs" (Arts **31A-31C, 33**) – constitutional text (APPSC 2017).

### 11. JUDICIAL OVERREACH DEBATE
| Basis | Review / restraint | Activism |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Interpretative, legalistic | Creative, proactive |
| Question | "Is the law valid?" | "Is the law just?" |
- **Overreach**: courts entering the executive / legislative domain → strains **separation of powers**.
- Concerns: courts doing executive work, weak PIL enforcement, flood of letter petitions, **delays in other cases** (not reduction), PIL misuse; Art. 142 "complete justice" undefined, bypasses democratic scrutiny.
- **Justice A.S. Anand**: activism should not become **judicial adventurism**. **Upendra Baxi**: courts should fill a vacuum, not replace democratic institutions – ideal **judicial prudence**.
- Merits: wider access to justice, executive accountability. PIL rethink debate (May 2026) – misuse, not ending access.
- **Mar 2026**: SC ban on an NCERT chapter on judicial delay and corruption criticised as censorship under Art. 19.
