## U0606 | HUMIDITY, EVAPORATION, CONDENSATION, CLOUDS AND FORMS OF PRECIPITATION
### 1. HUMIDITY – MEASURES AND DISTRIBUTION
- **Humidity** = presence of water vapour in the air. Humid summer heat = **muggy weather**.
| Measure | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute humidity** | Weight of water vapour per unit **volume** of air | g/m³ (grains per cubic foot) |
| **Specific humidity** | Water vapour per unit **weight** of air; **not changed** when volume changes | g/kg |
| **Relative humidity (RH)** | Actual vapour ÷ capacity × 100 | % |
| **Dew point** | Temperature at which air becomes **saturated**; condensation starts | °C |
| **Saturation** | **RH = 100%** | — |
!TRAP Absolute = per **volume** (g/m³); specific = per **kg** (g/kg).
- Warm air holds more vapour. Same vapour + **temperature rises → RH falls**; cooling → RH rises.
- RH is higher over oceans, lower over continents.
- Water vapour: up to **4%** in humid tropics; **under 1%** in deserts and polar areas; **falls with height and latitude**.
- **AP**: Coastal Andhra RH **70–90%** (high heat index); **Rayalaseema below 30%** in peak summer.
### 2. EVAPORATION, LATENT HEAT AND THE UPLIFT CHAIN
#### 2.1 Latent Heat
- **Evaporation takes** latent heat; **condensation releases** it → drives **thunderstorms and cyclones**.
| Change | Latent heat | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fusion of ice | **80 cal/g** (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg) | Melts at 0°C; temperature constant |
| Vaporisation of water | **540 cal/g** (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg) | Boils at 100°C; temperature constant |
- Latent heat of vaporisation is **much greater** than that of fusion.
- **Steam at 100°C burns worse** than boiling water – it releases latent heat on condensing.
- Ice cools because its latent heat of fusion absorbs much heat.
- **Refrigerator**: refrigerant absorbs heat on evaporation (inside), releases it on condensation (at the back). An **air-conditioner takes heat out** of room air.
- **Higher pressure raises boiling point** (pressure cooker); lower pressure at altitude lowers it.
#### 2.2 Uplift Chain
1. Warm air uplifted.
2. **Adiabatic cooling** to the dew point.
3. **Condensation** (vapour → liquid or ice) around nuclei → **clouds** (tiny droplets or ice crystals).
4. Droplets grow and fall → **precipitation** (rain, snow, sleet).
- Clouds **float** because of low density – tiny droplets fall very slowly, held up by gentle updrafts.
### 3. HYDROLOGICAL CYCLE
| In the cycle | NOT in the cycle |
|---|---|
| Evaporation, **transpiration** (= evapotranspiration) | **Photosynthesis** |
| **Sublimation**, condensation, precipitation | **Combustion** |
| Infiltration/percolation, runoff, **groundwater flow** | **Respiration** |
- **Transpiration**: plants lose vapour mainly through **stomata**; moves water up, controls temperature.
- **Forests = "water engineers"**: interception, transpiration, deep roots pump soil water, groundwater recharge.
- **Hydrometeorology**: study of transfer of **water and energy between land surface and lower atmosphere** (rainfall, evaporation, floods, droughts).
- **India water budget (2026)**: precipitation **~4,000 BCM**/year; utilisable **1,139 BCM** (**690 surface + 449 groundwater**).
- Heavy bursts exceed soil infiltration → water pools and evaporates → recharge falls (Jun 2026).
### 4. FORMS OF CONDENSATION
| Form | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Dew** | Moisture on cool surfaces when they fall below dew point; **dew point above 0°C** |
| **Frost** | **Dew point at or below 0°C**; ice crystals (Lambasingi, AP, ~2°C) |
| **Fog** | Cloud at ground level; often under inversion; **visibility under 1 km** |
| **Mist** | Thinner, wetter; **visibility over 1 km** |
| **Smog** | Fog + smoke/pollutants |
- Dew/frost best with **clear sky, calm air, high RH, long cold nights**.
- Prevented by **cloudy or windy** nights and low humidity. **No dew on a cloudy night** – clouds reflect back Earth's radiation.
!PAIR London smog | sulphurous | cool, humid || Los Angeles smog | photochemical | sunlight, PAN, ground-level ozone
### 5. CLOUDS
#### 5.1 Cloud Families
| Family | Cloud | Appearance | Weather |
|---|---|---|---|
| High (8,000–12,000 m; ice crystals) | **Cirrus** | Thin, **feathery, silky**, wispy | Fair; no rain |
| High | **Cirrostratus** | Thin **milky sheet**; causes **halo** | Warm front coming |
| High | **Cirrocumulus** | Small **globular** masses, ripples ("mackerel sky") | Fair |
| Middle (2–6 km) | **Altostratus** / altocumulus | Grey-blue sheet; sun as through ground glass | Light continuous rain |
| Low (below 2 km) | **Nimbostratus** | Dark, thick, shapeless | **Steady, prolonged rain** |
| Vertical (4,000–7,000 m) | **Cumulus** | Heaped **cauliflower**, flat base | Fair; convective; grows into Cb |
| Vertical (to ~**15 km**) | **Cumulonimbus** | Towering **anvil** top | **Thunderstorms, rainstorms, hail, cloudbursts** |
- Cirrus: ice crystals, above 20,000 ft. Cirrostratus: **highest-altitude cloud** (over 18,000 ft).
- **Rain-bearing**: nimbostratus and cumulonimbus; "**nimbus**" = rain-bearing.
- **Drizzle** comes from **stratus**.
!KEY APPSC-G1 2024 match: cirrus – silky; cirrostratus – milky; cirrocumulus – globular; cumulus – cauliflower.
!KEY APPSC 2019: **Cumulonimbus** produces rainstorms.
#### 5.2 Thundercloud Growth and Waterspouts
- Cumulonimbus grows from **condensation, high temperature and humidity, orography, vertical wind** (all four) – strong updrafts in hot, humid tropics.
- **Waterspout**: funnel from cumulus/cumulonimbus over water; recorded over the **Brahmaputra in Barpeta, Assam** (26 Aug 2026).
### 6. FORMS OF PRECIPITATION
| Form | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Rain** | Drops **over 0.5 mm** |
| **Drizzle** | Fine drops **under 0.5 mm** |
| **Snow** | Ice crystals formed **below 0°C** |
| **Sleet** | Rain **frozen into pellets** falling through cold air |
| **Hail** | **Layered** ice balls in **cumulonimbus** with strong updrafts |
- **Coloured (blood) rain**: dust or spores in rain – **Sirocco dust over Italy**; **Kerala's red rain (2001)**.
- **Cloudburst**: about **100 mm/hour** or more over **20–30 sq km**; from cumulonimbus; orographic lifting in the Himalaya; usually June–August at 1,000–2,500 m; stored water falls when updrafts weaken. **Dharali, Uttarkashi** (Aug 2025).
- **16th Finance Commission** recommended adding **heatwaves and lightning** to nationally notified disasters (Apr 2026).
### 7. TYPES OF PRECIPITATION
| Type | Mechanism | Place |
|---|---|---|
| **Convectional** | Strong heating → air rises, cools → cumulonimbus; afternoon showers | **Equatorial belt** (rain almost daily, all year); **mango showers** |
| **Cyclonic / frontal** | **Warm air lifted over cold** along fronts; cyclone lows | **Temperate regions**; Bay of Bengal cyclones on east coast; **western disturbances** (10–15 cm, NW India, rabi) |
| **Orographic (relief)** | Moist wind forced up a barrier; windward wet, leeward dry | **Western Ghats** (Agumbe), **Mawsynram** (windward) |
- Equatorial areas get rain all year – high temperature and humidity cause convectional rain most afternoons.
!TRAP Mango showers = convectional; Mawsynram = orographic.
### 8. RAIN SHADOW AND RELIEF
- **Rain-shadow area**: dry **leeward** side; descending air **warms and dries**.
- **Eastern side of Western Ghats** is leeward – interior Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP, Tamil Nadu get scanty rain; **peninsular rainfall falls west → east**.
- **Himalayas**: orographic barrier, confine the monsoon.
- **Aravallis**: little rain in Rajasthan – winds run **parallel** to them (no effective barrier).
| AP place | Rainfall |
|---|---|
| **Rayalaseema** | In rain shadow |
| **Hagari valley** | **Lowest** (below 51 cm) |
| **Sileru, Sabari valleys** (north coastal AP) | **Highest** (140–152 cm) – SW monsoon + Eastern Ghats |
- AP average rainfall **~966 mm**: about **two-thirds SW monsoon**, one-third NE monsoon.
- 2026 El Niño year: **Sri Sathya Sai** district 57% rain deficit (Aug 2026).
### 9. OPTICAL PHENOMENA
| Phenomenon | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Halo** (sun/moon) | **Refraction** through hexagonal **ice crystals** in thin high **cirrostratus/cirrus**; ring of about **22°** |
| **Rainbow** | **Refraction, dispersion and total internal reflection** in raindrops; **sun behind** observer, drops in front |
| **Corona** | **Diffraction** by small **water droplets** of thin clouds; coloured rings close to sun/moon |
- **Primary rainbow**: one TIR; **red outside, violet inside**.
- **Secondary rainbow**: **two** TIRs; **colours reversed**; fainter.
!KEY APPSC-G1 2024 key: halo statements I and II correct.
!TRAP Halo = refraction in ice crystals; corona = diffraction by water droplets.
### 10. CLOUD SEEDING AND ACID RAIN
- **Cloud seeding**: **silver iodide (AgI)** – crystal shape mimics ice; others: potassium iodide, **sodium chloride**, **dry ice**, liquid propane.
- Works **only when suitable clouds already exist**.
- **AP cloud seeding (July 2004)**: silver iodide–sodium chloride.
- Kazakhstan began seeding with **UAE** help for drought-hit Turkestan (Aug 2026).
- **Acid rain**: **pH below 5.6**, from **SO₂ and NOx**; term coined by **Robert Angus Smith (1872)**.

| Instrument / line | Measures / joins |
|---|---|
| **Hygrometer** | Humidity (APPSC-G1 2018) |

- **Wet-bulb temperature**: lowest by evaporation; equals dry-bulb only at **100% RH**; above **31.5°C** body cannot cool by sweating.
- **Rain gauge sequence**: **Mauryan Arthashastra** (drona, ~13.2 kg water) → **Korea's Cheugugi (1441)** → **Castelli (1639)**.
