## U0136 | MUGHAL ECONOMY – AGRICULTURE, KARKHANAS, TRADE, SILVER INFLUX, INDIAN OCEAN COMMERCE
### 1. AGRICULTURE – CROPS AND MARKETED SHARE
- About **15%** of rural produce reached the market (APPSC G2 2017); **cash revenue** pushed peasants to sell.
| Crop group | Crops |
|---|---|
| **Jins-i-kamil** ("perfect crops") | **cotton, sugarcane, indigo, oilseeds, opium** |
| **New World crops** (via the **Portuguese**) | **tobacco, maize, chillies** |
| Regional | **Gujarat** – indigo + food grains; **Bengal** – sugar + rice |
- Tobacco, cocoa and rubber were domesticated in the New World (UPSC 2019).
- **Shahab (Shihab) Nahr** – restored **Firuz Shah's Rajabwah** canal; repaired under Akbar by Delhi governor **Shihabuddin Khan**, later Nuruddin Muhammad.
- **Vijayanagara** contrast – agriculture through ponds/tanks, dams and wells.
- 18th century: agriculture stagnant; peasants paid the state, zamindars, jagirdars and revenue-farmers.
### 2. LAND REVENUE – ZABT OF RAJA TODARMAL
- **Zabt** designed by **Raja Todarmal** – link between **Sher Shah** and Akbar in land revenue. **Ain-i-Dahsala** introduced by Akbar, helped by **Khwaja Shah Mansoor** and **Muzaffar Khan Turbati**.
- State share = **one-third** of average produce of the **past ten years**, mainly in **cash** → rural economy monetised, peasants linked to markets.
- Measured with the **Ilahi Gaz** (bamboo rod with iron rings).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Polaj** | Cultivated annually |
| **Parauti** | Fallow one year |
| **Chachar** | Fallow 3–4 years |
| **Banjar** | Uncultivated |
| **Patta** | Title deed |
| **Qabuliyat** | Deed of agreement |
!TRAP Patta = title deed; Qabuliyat = deed of agreement.
### 3. SOCIAL CLASSES
| Class | Features |
|---|---|
| **Umara** (nobility) | High mansabdars – **Turanis, Iranis, Hindustanis, Rajputs**; rich but dependent on the emperor |
| **Zamindars** | Hereditary rural superior-right holders |
| **Khud-kasht** | Peasant cultivating land in **own village** |
| **Pahi-kasht** | Peasant cultivating land in **another village** |
| Artisans | Worked in state karkhanas or private enterprise; often in guilds |
- 18th-century society: extreme poverty beside extreme wealth.
### 4. TRADER CLASSES AND COMMUNITIES
| Group | Role / region |
|---|---|
| **Banjaras** | Carried **bulk goods** (grain, salt) over long distances in **bullock caravans** (APPSC G2 2017) |
| **Seths, Bohras** | Wholesale, **long-distance** trade |
| **Baniks / Beoparis** | **Local retail** traders |
| **Mahajans** | Moneylenders-cum-merchants |
| **Sarrafs (shroffs)** | Money-changers and bankers; issued **hundis** |
| **Nagarseth** | Head of a merchant community; dealt with officials |
| **Marwaris** | **Oswals, Maheshwaris, Agarwals** of Rajasthan |
| **Chettis** | **Coromandel** coast merchants |
| Muslim merchants | Dominated **Malabar** trade |
- Merchant strikes recorded (e.g. **Ahmedabad**); Gujarati merchants were Hindu, Jain and Muslim.
- Sultanate pattern: overseas trade – **Multanis and Khurasanis**; inland – Gujarati, Marwari, Bohra merchants.
!TRAP Seths and Bohras = long-distance; Baniks = local.
### 5. CREDIT – HUNDI, BIMA, SHROFFS
- **Hundi** – **letter of credit** issued by **shroffs** against deposited cash; encashed later or at another place (APPSC GS 2018).
- **Bima** – **insurance** of goods in transit, by sarrafs, for a premium.
- Shroffs tested coins, kept deposits, lent money and issued hundis.
!TRAP Hundi distractors: **Farman** (royal order), **Parcha**, **Rokad** (cash).
### 6. COINS
| Coin / term | Metal / meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mohur** | **Gold** |
| **Rupiya** | **Silver** – fixed by **Sher Shah** at **178 grains**; continued by Akbar |
| **Dam** | **Copper**; **40 dams = 1 rupee** |
| **Taza sikka** | Newly minted (current/previous year) |
| **Chalni (chalani)** | In circulation in the **current reign** |
| **Khazana** | Minted in **earlier reigns**; accepted at a discount (JPSC 2023) |
| **Sikka** | Right of coinage in the ruler's name; with the **khutba** = sign of sovereignty |
- Golconda minted only copper; Mughal silver circulated there (Thevenot).
### 7. KARKHANAS – ROYAL WORKSHOPS
- Run by the **Mir Saman (Khan-i-Saman)**; a **Sultanate tradition**.
- Produced court luxuries: **gold/silver articles, fine textiles, carpets, arms, jewellery**.
### 8. PORTS AND TRADING CENTRES
| Port | Epithet / trade |
|---|---|
| **Surat** | Chief west-coast port; **Bab-ul-Makka (Gate of Mecca)** – Haj port; also "Meccai dwar"; English ships 1608, Hawkins firman 1609, Roe 1615 |
| **Cambay (Khambhat)** | Old Gujarat port; declined with silting |
| **Masulipatnam** | **Qutb Shahi port**; **Kalamkari (chintz)**, saltpetre; **Dutch 1605 → English 1611 → French 1669** |
| **Hugli** | **Portuguese** base in Bengal; expelled by **Shah Jahan, 1632** |
| **Lahari Bandar** | Port of **Thatta** in the **Indus delta (Sind)**; major 17th-century port |
| Product | AP centre |
|---|---|
| Saltpetre | Machilipatnam, Narsapur, Pulicat |
| Damascus swords | **Nirmal** |
| Carpets / durries | **Warangal** |
- **AP angle**: **Machilipatnam** = first European trade centre in Andhra (Dutch, 1605); copper traded at **Motupalli**; AP is a top tobacco producer – **Tobacco Board at Guntur**.
### 9. EXPORTS, IMPORTS, FIRMANS AND SILVER INFLUX
| Exports | Imports |
|---|---|
| Textiles (calico, **muslin**, chintz), **saltpetre**, **indigo**, sugar, opium, spices | War horses, **silver**, ivory, porcelain, wine, glass, watches, tin, copper |
| Firman | Year | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Golden Firman** – Abdullah Qutb Shah | **1632** | Free trade for the English at Golconda ports |
| Shah Jahan's exemptions | 1652 / 1665 | English / Dutch |
| Farrukhsiyar's firman | 1716/1717 | Cut English duties |
- India = "**sink of precious metals**": Europeans paid in **bullion**; silver came from **Spanish America (New World)**; **Careri (c.1690)** traced its route. Effect: **more rupee coinage, rise in prices**.
- Merchant groups: Asian – Gujaratis, Bohras, Chettis, Malabar Muslims, Armenians; European – Portuguese, Dutch, English EIC (1600), French. Trade balance favoured India till mid-18th century.
- 18th-century Bengal (EIC staples): **cotton, silk, saltpetre, opium** – not copper.
- British period: Indian exports heavily taxed in Britain; British goods favoured in India → handicrafts ruined.
- Latest (May 2026): India raised import duty on gold and silver from **6% to 15%**; bullion imports stayed strong.
