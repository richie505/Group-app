## U0536 | FINANCIAL INSTABILITIES – CRISES, SYSTEMIC RISK, SHADOW BANKING, MACROPRUDENTIAL REGULATION
### 1. FINANCIAL CRISES AND SCAMS – CAUSE AND INDIA IMPACT
| Episode | Cause | India impact / response |
|---|---|---|
| **Harshad Mehta scam (1992)** | Bank money moved into stocks via **ready-forward G-Sec deals** | **SEBI made statutory**; **NSE** screen trading |
| **East Asian crisis (1997)** | Fall of the **Thai baht**, short-term foreign debt | Limited harm – **capital controls**, low short-term debt |
| **Global Financial Crisis (2007-09)** | **US sub-prime mortgages** | FPI outflows, market crash; Basel III; **FSDC (Dec 2010)** |
| **IL&FS (2018)** | Defaults by an **infrastructure NBFC** | **NBFC liquidity crisis**; tighter NBFC rules |
| **PMC Bank (2019)** | Hidden exposure to **one realty group**; weak checks under **dual control (RBI + State Registrars)** | **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020** |
| **Yes Bank (2020)** | Bad loans, governance failure | **RBI moratorium**, **SBI-led reconstruction**; CEO Prashant Kumar (from Mar 2020) replaced by **Vinay Tonse** on 6 Apr 2026 |
### 2. SUB-PRIME CRISIS MECHANICS
1. Loans to **weak borrowers** → **securitised** into mortgage-backed securities sold worldwide.
2. **Rates rise** → **home-loan defaults**.
3. **Failure of US banks** → crash in the Indian stock market.
- **Teaser loans**: low fixed rate first, higher later → a form of sub-prime lending; **future default risk** for banks (UPSC 2011).
- **Credit Default Swap**: insurance-like derivative on bonds; seller pays on default; **counterparty risk**.
!TRAP Sub-prime crisis was caused by a **real estate recession**, **not oil prices** (UPPSC 2010).
### 3. SHADOW BANKING AND NBFCS
- **Shadow banking** = financial activity of **non-bank entities** (e.g. NBFCs) **outside regular bank regulation** (UPPSC 2014).
- **Asset-liability mismatch**: **borrow short** (CPs, mutual fund money), **lend long** (infrastructure, housing) – exposed by the **IL&FS** default.
- NBFCs: registered under the **Companies Act**; **cannot take demand deposits, issue cheques or join the payment system**; deposits **not DICGC-covered**.
- **Scale-Based Regulation (Oct 2022)**: **Base, Middle, Upper, Top** layers.
### 4. SYSTEMIC RISK – SYSTEMICALLY IMPORTANT INSTITUTIONS
| Category | Named by | Entities |
|---|---|---|
| **D-SIBs** | **RBI** | **SBI, ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank** – extra **CET1 surcharge** |
| **D-SIIs** | **IRDAI** | **LIC, GIC Re, New India Assurance** |
| **G-SIBs** | **Financial Stability Board** | **No Indian bank** |
- **"Too big to fail"**: failure would disrupt the whole system → closer supervision and higher governance norms.
### 5. FSDC AND STABILITY BODIES
| Body | Chair / publisher | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **FSDC** (Dec 2010) | **Union Finance Minister** | Macroprudential supervision, inter-regulatory coordination, financial literacy and inclusion; **not a NITI Aayog organ** |
| FSDC sub-committee | **RBI Governor** | – |
| **CFSA** | Co-chaired by **RBI Deputy Governor + Finance Secretary** | MPPSC 2020 |
| **Financial Stability Report** | **RBI**, **half-yearly (June, December)** | Dec 2025 FSR: banks and NBFCs resilient in stress tests; June 2026 FSR flags **fintech unsecured lending** risk |
- RBI regulates banks and NBFCs, IRDAI insurers; FSDC coordinates all regulators.
### 6. MACROPRUDENTIAL TOOLS
| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Countercyclical capital buffer (CCyB)** | Build capital in good times, release in downturns; RBI kept it **inactive** (May 2026) |
| **Leverage ratio** | Non-risk-based cap on borrowing against capital |
| **LTV caps** | Limit loan against property value (housing) |
| **Risk weights** | Higher weights to cool hot sectors |
### 7. COOPERATIVE BANKS AND STRESS TESTS
- **BR (Amendment) Act, 2020**: **urban and multi-state cooperative banks** under **RBI supervision**.
- Deposit insurance: **₹5 lakh** per depositor per bank (DICGC).
- **Prompt Corrective Action (PCA)**: weak banks watched on **capital, asset quality (NPAs), leverage**; can limit **lending, branch growth, dividends**.
- Bank health: **GNPA 2.2%, NNPA 0.5%** (Sep 2025); **CRAR 17.3%** (Mar 2025).
- AP angle: **AP credit-deposit ratio 157.40%** – lending very high compared to deposits.
