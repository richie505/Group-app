### 2. SIMLA DEPUTATION, 1906
- Date: **1 October 1906**; organised and led by **Aga Khan III**; met **Lord Minto** at Simla (Shimla).
- Demanded **separate electorates** and **weightage** for Muslims, citing their "contribution to the **defence of the Empire**".
- Also wanted special safeguards for Muslims in **government services**.
### 3. FOUNDING OF THE ALL INDIA MUSLIM LEAGUE
#### 3.1 Founding facts
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Date | **30 December 1906** |
| Place | **Dacca (Dhaka)**, at the session of the **All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference** |
| Proposed by | **Nawab Salimullah of Dacca** |
| First president | **Aga Khan** |
| London branch | **Syed Ameer Ali, 1908** |
| Headquarters | **Lucknow** |
#### 3.2 Aims
- Promote **loyalty to the British Empire**.
- **Protect the political rights and interests of Muslims** in British India.
- Keep Muslims **away from the Congress**.
- The League **supported the partition of Bengal**.
!TRAP Armed revolt was **NOT** an aim of the early League.
!TRAP **1 Oct 1906** = Simla Deputation; **30 Dec 1906** = League founded at Dacca.
- Lord Minto used separate electorates for Muslims to **turn them against the Congress**.
### 4. INDIAN COUNCILS ACT, 1909 (MORLEY-MINTO REFORMS)
#### 4.1 Name and aim
- Popular name: **Morley-Minto (Minto-Morley) Reforms**; also called the Government of India Act 1909.
- Introduced by **John Morley** (Secretary of State) and **Lord Minto** (Viceroy).
- Aim: **rally (win) the Moderates** and **reward Muslim loyalty** through limited concessions.
#### 4.2 Provisions
| Area | Provision |
|---|---|
| Councils | Central Legislative Council enlarged: **additional members up to 60**; provincial councils enlarged |
| Majority | **Official majority kept at the Centre**; non-official majority in provinces (but Governor's veto made them powerless) |
| Electorates | **Separate electorates for Muslims** with **weightage**: Muslim members elected **only by Muslim voters** |
| Election | **Indirect, multi-tier** elections (not direct universal elections); elected non-official members for the first time |
| Budget | Could **discuss** the budget, ask **supplementary questions**, move resolutions; could **not vote** on the budget or question military affairs |
| Executive Council | Indian member: **S.P. Sinha (Law Member)** |
- **1909** = legal beginning of separate (communal) electorates.
- Critique: the **most short-lived** constitutional experiment; **Ramsay MacDonald**: an incomplete deal between democracy and bureaucracy.
!KEY "Which Act introduced the communal electorate?" → **Indian Councils Act, 1909**.
### 5. ACTS → ELECTORAL FEATURE
#### 5.1 Comparison table
| Act | Feature introduced |
|---|---|
| **Indian Councils Act, 1861** | Associated Indians with legislative councils |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1892** | **First indirect elections** (principle of representation); members could **discuss / ask questions on the budget** |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1909** | **Separate Muslim electorates**; weightage |
| **GoI Act, 1919** (Montagu-Chelmsford) | **Provincial dyarchy** (reserved and transferred subjects); **bicameralism and direct elections at the Centre**; post of **Indian High Commissioner** created |
| **GoI Act, 1935** | **Provincial autonomy**; **dyarchy at the Centre**; **All-India Federation** provided; federal and bicameral legislature |
#### 5.2 Separate electorates extended
| Year | Groups |
|---|---|
| 1909 | **Muslims** |
| 1919 | + **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans** |
| 1935 | + **Depressed classes, women, labour** |
#### 5.3 The 1935 Act – extra facts
- **Abolished provincial dyarchy**; dyarchy started at the Centre.
- **Abolished the India Council**.
- Governor-General's Executive Council got more Indian members; the GG kept final authority.
- Passed **August 1935**; the **longest** British Act of Parliament to that time.
!TRAP "Dyarchy at the Centre as well as in the Provinces" is **NOT** a feature of any Act.
!TRAP Montagu-Chelmsford = **1919**, not 1929 (APPSC G1 2019).

### 6. LUCKNOW SESSION AND LUCKNOW PACT, 1916
#### 6.1 Why Lucknow (December 1916) is historic
- **Reunion of Moderates and Extremists**: Extremists re-entered Congress **after 9 years** (since Surat 1907); Tilak's return.
- **Lucknow Pact**: Congress–League alliance; League led by its "**Young Party**" (Jinnah, Ali Brothers).
- Tilak repeated "Swaraj is my birthright, I shall have it" at the Lucknow session.
- **1916** = high point of Congress-League unity; **1916–1922** = period of agreement.
#### 6.2 Terms of the Pact
| Clause | Detail |
|---|---|
| Goal | **Self-government** accepted by both |
| Separate electorates | **Congress accepted** Muslim separate electorates (major concession) – the **first time (1916)** |
| Weightage | Muslims in **minority provinces** got more seats than their population share |
| Communal veto | No bill on a community passed if **three-fourths** of that community's members opposed it |
| Others | **Separation of executive and judiciary**; **elected majorities** in provincial legislatures |
- Limit: Congress and League still held separate annual sessions.
- **AP:** **Besant toured Andhra** (Kakinada, Rajahmundry, Eluru) after the Lucknow session.
### 7. CRITIQUE – FATHER OF THE COMMUNAL ELECTORATE
- **Lord Minto** = **Father of the Communal Electorate**.
- Separate electorates divided the electorate on religious lines and fed later separatism.
- **11 May 2026:** separate electorates re-entered public debate in commentary on religion-based voting patterns.
- **AP angle (repression under Minto-Morley):** **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** and **Bodi Narayana Rao** arrested **18 July 1908** for a **Swarajya** article = **first Telugu political prisoners**.
### 8. MUSLIM LEAGUE – LATER SESSIONS AND LEADERSHIP SHIFT
#### 8.1 Sessions
| Session | Place / fact |
|---|---|
| **First annual session, 1907** | **Karachi** (not its founding) |
| Second session, 1908 | **Amritsar** |
| 1916 | Lucknow, presided by **Jinnah** |
| **1930** | **Allahabad**: **Iqbal** proposed a Muslim state in the **North-West** |
| **1940** | **Lahore**, 22–24 March; presided by **Jinnah** |
#### 8.2 Leadership shift
- **1912–24:** League dominated by younger Muslim nationalists; their nationalism took a communal view of politics.
- **Jinnah** joined in **1913**; later led the movement for **Pakistan**; criticised Gandhi's support of the **Khilafat Movement** and warned him not to encourage fanaticism of Muslim religious leaders.
- **1937–39:** League alleged "**Hindu Raj**" under Congress ministries.
#### 8.3 Two-nation milestones
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **1930** | **Iqbal** – first idea of a separate Muslim nation; Hindus and Muslims two nations |
| **1933** | **Chaudhry Rahmat Ali** coined "**PAKISTAN**" (pamphlet *Now or Never*) |
| **23 March 1940** | **Lahore (Pakistan) Resolution** – separate independent states for Muslims; put Jinnah's **Two Nation Theory** into effect |
| 23 March 1943 | **Pakistan Day** celebrated |
| **16 August 1946** | **Direct Action Day**; large riots in **Calcutta** |
- Lahore Resolution: drafted by **Sikandar Hayat Khan**; presented (moved) by **A.K. Fazlul Haq** (Premier of Bengal); approved by **Khaliquzzaman**; Jinnah presided.
- **Liaquat Ali Khan** – Pakistan's **first Prime Minister**.
- Quote: "Nehru is a patriot, while Jinnah is a politician" – attributed to Iqbal (Nehru, *Discovery of India*).
!TRAP Muslim state **idea** = **Iqbal (1930)**; the **name** "Pakistan" = **Rahmat Ali (1933)**.
