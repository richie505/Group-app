## U0171 | ANGLO-MARATHA AND ANGLO-SIKH WARS; CONQUEST OF SINDH; DOCTRINE OF LAPSE AND ANNEXATIONS
### 1. WARS OF EXPANSION – ORDER AND SPANS
| Series | Span | Number of wars |
|---|---|---|
| Carnatic Wars (Wandiwash 1760 in the Third) | 1746–63 | 3 |
| **Anglo-Mysore** | **1767–99** | **4** (in 32 years) |
| **Anglo-Maratha** | **1775–1818** | **3** |
| Anglo-Nepal | 1814–16 | 1 |
| **Anglo-Sikh** | **1845–49** | **2** |
- Mysore wars began **first (1767)**; the Maratha wars overlapped them.
- The British effectively took India **from the Marathas, not the Mughals**; after **1818** British paramountcy was unchallenged.
- **Order (BPSC 2025):** Wandiwash (1760) → Nand Kumar hanged (1775) → Salbai (1782) → Tipu's death (1799).
- First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46) came before the Santhal revolt (1855–56), Lytton's Delhi Durbar (1877) and the Ilbert Bill.
### 2. ANGLO-MYSORE WARS
#### 2.1 Causes
- British interest in **Malabar pepper and cardamom**; Mysore's threat to **Madras**; Mysore's **French** ties.
- Capital: **Srirangapatnam (Seringapatam)**.
#### 2.2 Wars → treaty and Governor-General
| War | Years | GG / commander | Treaty / result |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 1767–69 | — | **Treaty of Madras (1769)**: mutual restitution + defensive alliance; Nizam, Marathas, British first allied against Haidar |
| Second | **1780–84** | **Warren Hastings** (APPSC-GS 2025) | **Porto Novo (1 July 1781)** – **Eyre Coote** beat Haidar; **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)** – status quo |
| Third | 1790–92 | **Cornwallis** (led the army himself) | Trigger: Tipu attacked **Travancore (1789)** – British ally, Company's only pepper supplier; **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)** – Tipu gave up **half his territory**, paid **Rs 3 crore**, sent **two sons as hostages** |
| Fourth | **1799** | **Wellesley** | Feared a Tipu–**Napoleon** alliance; Tipu killed; Mysore partitioned, no treaty (APPSC-G1 2004) |
#### 2.3 Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan
| Ruler | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Haidar Ali** | Modern **arsenal at Dindigul (1755)** with French help |
| **Tipu Sultan** | Sent **ambassadors to France** for European technology; built a **navy** (APPSC-G1 2018); said to have joined the **Jacobin Club**; planted a **"Tree of Liberty"** at Srirangapatnam |
### 3. ANGLO-MARATHA WARS (1775–1818)
#### 3.1 Wars, GG and battles
| War | GG | Key events |
|---|---|---|
| **First (1775–82)** | **Warren Hastings** | Bombay government backed **Raghunath Rao** against the **Barabhai** council (for Madhav Rao II); defeat at **Talegaon** → **Convention of Wadgaon (1779)**; British lost badly |
| **Second (1803–05)** | **Lord Wellesley** | **Holkar** defeated Peshwa **Baji Rao II** at Poona (1802) → Baji Rao fled and signed **Bassein**; **Arthur Wellesley** won **Assaye, Argaon**; **Lord Lake** won **Delhi, Laswari** |
| **Third (1817–18)** | **Lord Hastings** | **Pindari** campaign + Peshwa's attack on the **Poona Residency**; Peshwa, Bhonsle, Holkar rose; **Peshwaship abolished (1818)**; Baji Rao II pensioned |
#### 3.2 Treaties → year and parties
| Treaty | Year | Parties / outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Surat** | 1775 | Bombay government + Raghunath Rao |
| **Purandar** | 1776 | Calcutta Council + Barabhai; **cancelled Surat** |
| **Wadgaon** (Convention) | 1779 | After Talegaon; British gave up gains |
| **Salbai** | **1782** | **Warren Hastings + Mahadji Scindia**; status quo; British kept **Salsette**; about **20 years of peace** (APPSC 2018) |
| **Bassein** | **1802** | **Peshwa Baji Rao II** – Subsidiary Alliance; subsidiary force in Maratha land; Peshwa **gave up chauth claims on the Nizam** |
| **Deogaon** | 1803 | **Bhonsle of Nagpur** |
| **Surji-Arjangaon** | 1803 | **Scindia** – British gained **Delhi** and control of the Mughal emperor |
| **Rajpurghat** | 1805 | **Holkar** |
| Poona, Gwalior (1817), Mandasor (1818) | 1817–18 | Third war; Peshwaship ended |
- **Order:** Surat → Purandar → Wadgaon → Salbai.
- **Baji Rao II** – first Maratha sardar to accept the Subsidiary Alliance; died **1851** → adopted son **Nana Sahib** denied the pension.
!TRAP **Arthur Wellesley** (Assaye; later Duke of Wellington) ≠ **Lord Wellesley** the GG.
### 4. SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE AND THE NIZAM
- **Wellesley (1798–1805)** – introduced the **Subsidiary Alliance**; forward policy; kept the British flag flying while Europe fell to Napoleon.
- **Order of acceptance:** **Hyderabad (1798)** → Mysore → Oudh → Scindia; **Hyderabad = first princely state**.
- **First Mysore War:** Nizam + Marathas + British against Haidar.
- **Third War:** Nizam Ali joined the British; got back half the Rayalaseema lands (Bellary, Gurramkonda, Gandikota, Kambham, Kadapa) under Seringapatam (1792).
- **Kharda (1795):** Marathas defeated the Nizam; **Sir John Shore** kept neutral; Resident **Kirkpatrick** reported → Nizam accepted the alliance (1798).
- **1800:** Nizam ceded **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary** = **Ceded Districts (Dattamandalalu)** to pay for the force.
- **1801:** Carnatic Nawab's **Nellore and Chittoor** annexed on a charge of **collusion with Tipu**; Wellesley merged Andhra into the Madras Presidency.
### 5. ANGLO-NEPAL WAR AND OTHER WARS
- **Anglo-Nepal War (1814–16)** – **Lord Hastings**; first campaign (1814) without result; **1815** Almora (Kumaon) won, **Ochterlony** forced terms.
- **Treaty of Sugauli** – signed **2 Dec 1815**, ratified **4 Mar 1816**; Nepal ceded **Kumaon, eastern Garhwal, parts of the Terai**; Company got **Ranikhet and Nainital** (not Kathmandu, not Shimla) (APPSC-GS 2018).
- **First Anglo-Burmese (1824–26)** – **Amherst**; **Yandabo (1826)** – Assam, Manipur, Arakan, Tenasserim.
- **First Anglo-Afghan (1838/39–42)** – **Auckland** (Tripartite Treaty, 1838).
!TRAP "1813–23" is **Lord Hastings' tenure**, not the Nepal war (1814–16).
### 6. RANJIT SINGH AND THE SIKH STATE
- **Treaty of Amritsar (1809)** – Ranjit Singh + British (**Minto I**); **Sutlej** fixed as the boundary.
- **Khalsa army** modernised with European officers – **Ventura and Allard** (joined 1822) trained infantry and cavalry; **Fauj-i-Khas** model brigade.
- **Ranjit Singh died 1839** (not 1836) → chaos.
- **Successors:** **Kharak Singh** (1839–40) → **Nau Nihal Singh** (died 1840) → regent **Chand Kaur** → **Sher Singh** (1841–43) → **Duleep Singh** (1843; mother **Jind Kaur** regent) – **last Sikh ruler**.
### 7. ANGLO-SIKH WARS (1845–49)
| | First War (1845–46) | Second War (1848–49) |
|---|---|---|
| GG | **Lord Hardinge I** | **Lord Dalhousie** |
| Cause | Sikh army **crossed the Sutlej** | Murder of **Vans Agnew and Anderson** at **Multan** |
| Battles | **Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal, Sobraon** | **Ramnagar, Chillianwala, Gujrat (21 Feb 1849)** |
| Result | **Treaty of Lahore**; Amritsar; Bhairowal | **Punjab annexed (1849)**; **Kohinoor** taken; Duleep Singh pensioned |
- **Sobraon (1846)** lost through the betrayal of **Lal Singh and Tej Singh**.
- **Chillianwala (13 Jan 1849):** **Lord Gough** vs **Sher Singh Attariwala**; **undecided**, costly.
#### 7.1 Treaties of 1846
| Treaty | Date | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Lahore** | **9 Mar 1846** | Land south of the Sutlej ceded; indemnity; extended British control in Punjab |
| **Amritsar** | **16 Mar 1846** | **Kashmir sold to Gulab Singh for Rs 75 lakh** |
| **Bhairowal (Bhyrowal)** | **Dec 1846** | **British Resident** became virtual ruler of Punjab |

### 8. CONQUEST OF SINDH (1843)
- **GG:** **Lord Ellenborough (1842–44)**; general **Sir Charles Napier** vs the **Amirs of Sindh**.
- **Battles:** **Miani** and **Dabo**; Napier followed a **"bullying policy"**.
- **Aims:** recover prestige after the **First Afghan War**, counter **Russia**, secure **Indus trade** – **not** the Doctrine of Lapse.
- Called the **"Tail of the Afghan Storm"**.
### 9. PARAMOUNTCY
- **Lord Hastings** turned subsidiary alliances into **paramountcy**; imposed humiliating treaties on the Peshwa and Scindia; ended non-intervention.
- **Rajputana (1818):** Udaipur, Jaipur, Jodhpur and others accepted **subordinate alliances** – British protection in exchange for giving up **external relations**.
- States kept internal administration but had **no international life**.
- **Grounds for intervention:** to give effect to their **international commitments** (APPSC-G2 2017 key); **misgovernance** (Coorg, Awadh).
### 10. DALHOUSIE – DOCTRINE OF LAPSE AND ANNEXATIONS
#### 10.1 Doctrine of Lapse
- **Lord Dalhousie (1848–56):** a dependent state whose ruler died **without a natural heir** lapsed to the Company; an **adopted son** could not inherit the state.
| State | Year |
|---|---|
| **Satara** | **1848** (first) |
| **Jaitpur, Sambalpur** | **1849** |
| **Baghat** | **1850** |
| **Udaipur** (Chhattisgarh, not Mewar) | **1852** |
| **Jhansi** | **1853** |
| **Nagpur** | **1854** (Raghuji III died without heir) |
#### 10.2 Four grounds of annexation
| Ground | Cases |
|---|---|
| **Conquest** | **Sindh (1843), Punjab (1849), Lower Burma/Pegu (1852)** |
| **Lapse** | **Satara to Nagpur**; Jhansi |
| **Misgovernance** | **Coorg + Central Cachar (1834, Bentinck)**; **Awadh (1856)** |
| **Abolition of titles/pensions** | **Carnatic and Tanjore (1855)**; Nana Sahib's pension (after 1851) |
- **Sikkim (parts, 1850, e.g. Darjeeling):** Dalhousie, on the excuse that the king detained **two English doctors**.
- **Awadh (1856):** wanted for its **cotton market** and agrarian wealth.
#### 10.3 Dalhousie's imperialism → 1857
1. Lapse annexations + **Awadh (1856)**.
2. Loss of titles and pensions (Nana Sahib, the Mughal house).
3. Princes, **taluqdars** and **Awadh sepoys** alienated.
4. **Revolt of 1857**.
- By **1856** British India stretched from the **Hindukush to Burma**.
- Dalhousie also: **railways, telegraph, Public Works Department (1854)**.
- **Order:** Awadh (1856) → Indigo Revolt (1859–60) → Second Afghan War (1878–80) → Ilbert Bill (1883).
