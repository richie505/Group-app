## U0599 | EARTHQUAKES – CAUSES, SEISMIC WAVES, MEASUREMENT, DISTRIBUTION, EFFECTS
### 1. EARTHQUAKE BASICS AND TERMS
- An earthquake is a **geological** disaster, not an atmospheric one; a natural event **not yet predictable accurately** (time, place, magnitude) (APPSC 2022).
- **Seismology** = branch of geology that studies earthquakes. **John Milne** = **Father of Modern Seismology** (early horizontal seismograph).
#### 1.1 Focus vs Epicentre
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Focus (hypocentre)** | Point of origin **inside** Earth where rupture starts and **energy is released**; shallow or deep |
| **Epicentre** | Surface point **directly above** the focus; **first** to record waves; intensity and damage **worst** here |
- Typical crustal quakes: **10–29 km** deep. Rare **continental mantle** quakes start **80+ km below the Moho**; **Stanford** made the **first global map** of them.
#### 1.2 Seismic Doublet
- **Seismic doublet**: two **independent** quakes of **similar magnitude**, close in time and place (unlike aftershocks, which taper off).
- **26 Jun 2026**: USGS-described doublet hit **Caracas, Venezuela** (M 7.1 and M 7.5) on the Caribbean–South American **strike-slip** boundary; 589 dead.
!TRAP Energy is released at the FOCUS; waves are first recorded at the EPICENTRE.
### 2. CAUSES AND TYPES OF EARTHQUAKES
| Type | Cause | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Tectonic** | Fault stress exceeds rock strength → rupture; at divergent, convergent, transform boundaries | Himalayas; **San Andreas** (transform) |
| **Volcanic** | Rising magma fractures rock; volcanic structures collapse | Ring of Fire volcanoes |
| **Reservoir-induced** | Weight of water when large reservoirs fill | **Koyna 1967** (Maharashtra) – India's classic case |
| **Intraplate** | Old **hidden faults** in stable shields | **Latur (Killari) 1993**, Koyna, Jabalpur, Bhuj (Kutch) |
| Induced (other) | Mining, hydraulic fracturing (fracking); low magnitude | – |
- **Latur 1993**: far from any plate boundary → quakes strike stable areas along hidden faults (APPSC 2025).
- **Koyna** more earthquake-prone: dam on an **old fault plane**, triggered by water-level changes (R explains A).
- All four are causes: fault development, movement along a fault, volcanic eruption, folding of rocks.
- San Andreas movement is **tectonic**, not induced. Ring of Fire = volcanic and convergent-margin activity (not transform).
- **AP**: reservoir-triggered tremors possible near **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Polavaram** and old Eastern Ghats faults.
### 3. SEISMIC WAVES
| Wave | Arrival | Particle motion | Medium |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P (primary)** | **1st** (fastest) | **Longitudinal**, push-pull, along direction of travel | **Solid, liquid, gas** |
| **S (secondary)** | **2nd** | **Transverse**, perpendicular to travel | **Solids only** |
| **Surface (L)** | **Last** (slowest) | **Love**: side to side; **Rayleigh**: rolling like ocean waves | Along surface; **most damage** |
- **Shadow zone**: no S-waves beyond about **105°** from the epicentre → **outer core is liquid**.
- Love and Rayleigh waves are both **surface waves**.
!PAIR Love wave | Side to side | Surface || Rayleigh wave | Rolling | Surface
### 4. MEASUREMENT – MAGNITUDE VS INTENSITY
| Feature | Magnitude | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | **Energy released at the focus** | **Observed effects** on people, buildings, ground at a place |
| Nature | Quantitative; **one value per quake** (APPSC 2025) | Qualitative; **falls with distance** from epicentre |
| Scale | **Richter** (Charles F. Richter, **1935**); now **Moment Magnitude (Mw)** | **Modified Mercalli** (MMI) and **MSK**, both **I–XII** |
- **Richter**: logarithmic, based on **wave amplitude**; each +1 = **10× amplitude**, about **31× energy**.
- **Mw**: based on **rupture area** of the fault and size of seismic waves; has largely replaced Richter for strong quakes.
- MMI: I (not felt) to XII (total destruction).
!TRAP "Richter measures intensity" is strictly wrong – it measures magnitude; intensity = Mercalli. But one APPSC key accepted Richter for "intensity" as the closest option.
#### 4.1 Instruments
| Instrument | Use |
|---|---|
| **Seismograph** | **Records** seismic waves (APPSC 2025) |
| **Seismometer** | The sensor (pendulum/spring) inside a seismograph; broadband ones in India's network |
| Seismogram | The recording itself |
| **Seismoscope** | Only shows **that** a tremor occurred and its time |
| **Tiltmeter** | Ground **tilt** at faults and volcanoes |
| **Creepmeter** | Slow **slip (creep)** along a fault |
### 5. WORLD DISTRIBUTION
| Belt | Plate setting | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Circum-Pacific (Ring of Fire)** | **Subduction**; **most earthquakes** (~**90%**) | **Japan**, Philippines, Indonesia, **Chile**, Andes, **Alaska**, **California** |
| **Mid-Continental (Alpine-Himalayan / Mediterranean-Himalayan)** | **Continental collision**; second | Italy, Türkiye, Himalayas, India |
| Mid-oceanic ridges (Mid-Atlantic) | **Divergent**; **shallow, milder** quakes | Iceland |
| Hotspots | **Mantle plumes** under moving plate | **Hawaii, Galapagos**, Reunion |
- Ring of Fire = earthquakes, volcanoes, Pacific Ocean (**not forest fires**); holds about **75% of active volcanoes** → most eruptions.
- **Japan** = most earthquake-prone nation. **World's largest earthquake: M 9.5, Chile, 22 May 1960**.

### 7. EFFECTS OF EARTHQUAKES
| Effect | Condition / region |
|---|---|
| **Tsunami** | Needs **M > 6.5**, focus shallower than **~70 km**, **vertical uplift** of sea floor; over **80%** from megathrust subduction quakes (Aug 2025) |
| Landslides | Fragile Himalaya and North-East |
| **Liquefaction** | Soft alluvial soils of the **Indo-Gangetic Plain** (Bihar, UP, WB) |
| **Seismic seiches** | Standing waves in rivers, lakes, reservoirs, ponds; reported in the Myanmar quake |
| Fires | Broken gas and power lines |
- About **95%** of quake deaths occur in **poorly built 1–3 storey houses**.
- Tsunami Early Warning System built after the **2004 Sumatra** earthquake.
### 8. INDIA'S TECTONIC SETTING AND SEISMIC ZONES
- India lies in the **Alpine-Himalayan belt**, which continues east through the North-East (Arakan Yoma) into **Myanmar**; **Sagaing Fault** = Indian–Eurasian boundary.
- **Himalayan region** has the highest concentration of high zones (mostly **IV and V**) due to many **longitudinal thrust zones** (R explains A).
- Peninsula (Latur, Koyna, Jabalpur): severe intraplate quakes possible. **Karnataka Plateau** is NOT a high-intensity zone.
#### 8.1 Seismic Zones (IS 1893: 2016, in force)
| Zone | Risk | MSK intensity | Zone factor | Areas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V** | Very high | IX+ | **0.36** | Entire **North-East**, J&K, Ladakh, Himachal, Uttarakhand, parts of north Bihar, **Kutch**, **A&N** |
| **IV** | High | VIII | **0.24** | **New Delhi**, rest of Bihar, parts of UP, Punjab, Himalayan foothills, Indo-Gangetic belt |
| **III** | Moderate | VII | **0.16** | Kerala, Goa, coastal areas, **Vijayawada**, Chennai, Amaravati region, much of coastal AP |
| **II** | Low (least active) | VI or less | **0.10** | Stable Peninsula: **Visakhapatnam**, Hyderabad |
- **56%** of India's area is in Zones III–V. **Delhi = Zone IV** (Earthquake Disaster Risk Index Report, 2019). **Bhuj is not in Zone IV** (it is V).
- After **Latur 1993**, **Zone I merged into Zone II** → four zones (IS 1893: 2002).
#### 8.2 Andhra Pradesh
- AP lies in **Zones II and III**; **no area in Zone V** (Assam, Bihar, J&K have) (APPSC 2024).
- **Visakhapatnam = Zone II** (BIS; May 2026); **Vijayawada = Zone III**.
!TRAP Visakhapatnam is Zone II, not V; New Delhi is Zone IV.
#### 8.3 The 2025 Code and Rollback
| Event | Date | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| IS 1893 (Part 1): 2025 | Notified by BIS, **6 Nov 2025** | Probabilistic (**PSHA**) model; new top **Zone VI** (parts of Kashmir, North-East, Kutch); **>60%** of land in moderate-to-high zones |
| Withdrawal | **3 Mar 2026** | Cost objections from **MoHUA, Metro Rail corporations, NDSA** |
| IS 1893 (Part 1): 2016 | Restored | Four-zone map in force |
- Withdrawn map: built by **IIT-Madras** for NDMA; PGA 0.15–0.75 g (Zone II to VI); **69,519** events; **36** seismogenic zones.
- Debate (22 Mar 2026): experts say restored 2016 values are **too low** for the Himalaya; NDMA called the new values **"too high"**; safer PSHA codes vs construction cost.

### 10. VOLCANOES – PRODUCTS AND TYPES
- Products: **pyroclastic debris**, ash, volcanic bombs, lava. Gases: mostly **water vapour**, then **CO₂, SO₂, H₂S**.
| Type | Features | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Shield** | Fluid basalt, gentle slopes | **Mauna Loa** |
| **Composite / strato** | Viscous lava, steep, explosive; **~60%** of Earth's volcanoes | Etna, Mayon, Fuego |
| **Caldera** | Collapse depression after explosive eruption | **Mount Aso** (Japan) – among world's largest; Taal |
| **Complex** | Several vents, overlapping cones, domes | **Taal** (Philippines), **Dukono** (Indonesia) |
| Strombolian | Frequent, mild bursts | Fuego (every 15–20 min) |
| Vulcanian | More explosive | – |
- Activity: **active**; **dormant** (quiet long, may erupt); **extinct** (no eruption expected).
### 11. VOLCANIC LANDFORMS
| Intrusive form | Shape / position |
|---|---|
| **Batholith** | Huge, often dome-shaped **granite** mass at depth; mountain cores; exposed by denudation |
| **Laccolith** | **Dome**-shaped, **flat base**, fed by a pipe (Karnataka granite domes) |
| **Lopolith** | **Saucer**-shaped (concave upward) |
| **Phacolith** | **Wavy** mass at base of a syncline / top of an anticline |
| **Sill / sheet** | **Horizontal**, parallel to beds (thin = sheet) |
| **Dyke** | **Vertical** wall cutting across beds; fed the **Deccan Traps** (western Maharashtra) |
- **Extrusive**: lava plateaus (Deccan), cinder and composite cones, craters, calderas, lava flows.
- **Ladakh Magmatic Arc**: Trans-Himalaya, between **Indus and Shyok** suture zones.
!TRAP APPSC G1 2024: "phacoliths are large domed mounds" is WRONG – domes are laccoliths.
