## U0788 | ENVIRONMENTAL POLLUTION – TYPES, SOURCES AND IMPACTS
### 1. TYPES OF POLLUTION – SOURCE AND IMPACT
| Pollution type | Major primary source (APPSC 2025 key) | Key impacts |
|---|---|---|
| Air | **Industrial emissions** (SO2, NOx, PM); vehicle exhaust | Respiratory and heart disease; acid rain; poor visibility |
| Water | **Sewage discharge**; industrial effluents | Water-borne diseases, **eutrophication**, fish kills |
| Soil | **Chemical fertilisers and pesticides**; industrial waste | Loss of fertility, toxic crops, soil fauna imbalance |
- Air pollution deaths: **~7 million a year (WHO)**; household air pollution ~3.8 million.
- Highest chemical pollution from industrial effluents: **leather industry** (water and soil).
- Survey (Yale, 2024-25): **53%** Indians faced water pollution, **52%** air pollution.
!KEY APPSC 2025: soil pollution's main source = fertilisers and pesticides; water pollution's = sewage.
### 2. CLASSIFICATION OF POLLUTANTS
| Basis | Types | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Form | **Primary** (as emitted) vs **secondary** (formed by reaction in air) | Plastics vs **ozone, PAN** (peroxyacyl nitrate) |
| Existence in nature | **Quantitative** vs **qualitative** | CO2 vs insecticides, herbicides |
| Natural disposal | **Biodegradable** vs **non-biodegradable** | Sewage, heat vs plastics, **DDT** |
| Source geometry | Point / line / area / diffuse (non-point) | Chimney; highway; mining area; **farm runoff** |
- **Xenobiotics**: man-made chemicals foreign to living systems (pesticides, **PCBs**, plastics); resist degradation.
!TRAP Ozone and PAN are secondary pollutants; plastics are primary. Farm runoff is non-point (diffuse).
### 3. POLLUTANT → EFFECT AND MATCHED PAIRS
| Pollutant | Effect |
|---|---|
| SO2 + NOx | **Acid rain** (H2SO4, HNO3) |
| Acid rain | **Marble cancer**: acids react with **calcium carbonate** of the Taj Mahal's marble |
| CFCs / Freon | Ozone depletion – ozone hole |
| DDT | Persistent, non-biodegradable; **biomagnification** |
| N2O | Greenhouse gas |
| **SF6** | Extremely potent greenhouse gas (absorbs infrared, lasts thousands of years); also an electrical insulator |
| Incineration of plastics / PET | Greenhouse gases |
!PAIR Marble cancer | acid rain (SO2) | Biomagnification – DDT || Greenhouse effect | CO2 | Lung cancer – asbestos/particulates
- The four pairs above were asked in **APPSC 2023**.
!TRAP SF6's use as an electrical insulator is unrelated to its greenhouse potency.
### 4. BIOMAGNIFICATION AND BIOACCUMULATION
- **Bioaccumulation**: build-up of a persistent pollutant inside one organism over time.
- **Biomagnification**: rise in its concentration at each higher **trophic level** of the food chain.
- Typical pollutants: **DDT** and **mercury** (methyl mercury in fish – Minamata).
- Top consumers (fish-eating birds, humans) carry the highest load.
### 5. TOXIC POLLUTANTS – SOURCES
| Pollutant | Source / effect |
|---|---|
| **Mercury** | Coal combustion, smelters, chemical effluents; mercury, cadmium, lead also from **synthetic idol paints** |
| **Lead** | Motor vehicles, smelters → **brain damage**; household/decorative paints capped at **90 ppm** by MoEF&CC |
| **Coal ash** | Disposal pollutes soil; heavy metals **arsenic, mercury, lead, cadmium, chromium** |
| **Brominated flame retardants** | In electronics, plastics, foam, textiles; persistent and endocrine-disrupting; released from **e-waste** |
| **Ammonia (NCR)** | **77-80%** from fertilisers + livestock |
| **Open burning** (e.g., **Bhogi bonfires**) | Tyres, plastics, flex, e-waste → **dioxins, furans**, CO, SO2, NOx, lead, cadmium, mercury (APPCB advisory, 11 Jan 2026) |
### 6. MAJOR POLLUTION DISASTERS
#### 6.1 Disaster Types
- **Man-made disasters**: from human action, negligence or technology.
- **Industrial disasters** (Bhopal, LG Polymers): mostly **toxic gas leaks**; radioactive leaks (Chernobyl) form a separate group.
#### 6.2 Year, Place, Pollutant
| Event | Year | Place | Pollutant / cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Los Angeles smog | 1943 | Los Angeles, USA | **Photochemical smog**: sunlight on NOx and hydrocarbons from vehicles (about 24-32°C) |
| London smog | 5-11 Dec 1952 | London | **Classical smog**: water vapour + SO2/H2S on dust at low temperature; ~4,000 killed |
| Minamata disease | Found 1956 | Minamata, Kumamoto, Japan | **Methyl mercury** in **Chisso Corporation** wastewater, biomagnified in fish |
| **Bhopal gas tragedy** | **2-3 Dec 1984** | Bhopal, MP | **Methyl isocyanate (MIC)** from **Union Carbide** pesticide plant; **42 tonnes** released; over 2,500 deaths |
| Chernobyl | 26 April 1986 | Ukraine (then **USSR**) | Radioactive leak from reactor; >4,000 deaths |
| Fukushima | 2011 | Japan | Hydrogen explosion and radioactive leak after earthquake-tsunami |
| **LG Polymers** | **7 May 2020** | R.R. Venkatapuram, **Visakhapatnam (AP)** | **Styrene** vapour leak |
!TRAP Bhopal = MIC; LG Polymers = Styrene; Minamata = Methyl mercury. Chernobyl was in the USSR in 1986, not Russia.

### 9. CEPI AND INDUSTRY COLOUR CATEGORIES
#### 9.1 Comprehensive Environmental Pollution Index
- Developed by **CPCB with IIT Delhi (2009)**; scores clusters **0-100** for air, water and land.
- Three factors: **pollutant, pathway, receptor** (exposed people).
| CEPI score | Category |
|---|---|
| **70 or above** | Critically polluted |
| 60 to below 70 | Severely polluted |
- 2009: **43 of 88** clusters critically polluted.
- AP: **Visakhapatnam** assessed; **Patancheru-Bollaram** (then AP, now Telangana) critically polluted.
#### 9.2 Red / Orange / Green / White (CPCB, 2016)
- Basis: **Pollution Index** = function of **water pollution, air pollution and hazardous waste** generation.
| Category | Pollution Index (2016 scheme) |
|---|---|
| Red | 60 and above |
| Orange | 41-59 |
| Green | 21-40 |
| White | 20 and below (practically non-polluting) |
- 2025 revision added a **Blue** category.
- AP Common Zoning Regulations 2026: **nine land-use categories**; white/green industries noted.
