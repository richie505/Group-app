## U0632 | INDIA – SOIL TYPES, CHARACTERISTICS AND DISTRIBUTION; SOIL EROSION AND CONSERVATION
### 1. SOIL FORMATION AND COMPOSITION
#### 1.1 Soil Formation Factors
- Five factors acting together: **parent material (rock)**, **climate**, **relief (topography)**, **organisms (biological activity)** and **time**.
- Parent rock passes on traits (e.g. limestone → calcium-rich soil); warm, moist climates form soil faster than cold or arid ones.
- Two origins: **residual (in situ)** soils dominate **Peninsular India**; **drifted (ex situ)** soils dominate **North India**.
#### 1.2 Soil Composition
- Good soil by volume: **mineral 45%, organic matter 5%, water 25%, air 25%**.
- **Soil organic carbon (SOC)**: binds particles into aggregates, raises infiltration, feeds microbes; **clay soils hold more SOC** than sandy soils; topsoil holds the most.
- India's average SOC reportedly fell from **1% to 0.3%** over 70 years.
#### 1.3 Soil Profile (Horizons)
| Horizon | Meaning |
|---|---|
| O | Organic litter |
| A | Topsoil, humus-rich |
| E | Leached layer |
| B | **Subsoil** – minerals washed down from above **accumulate** |
| C | Weathered parent material |
| R | Bedrock |
- Order from surface: **O → A → B → C → R**.
### 2. MAJOR SOIL TYPES OF INDIA
- **Eight** major types: alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid, saline and alkaline, peaty and marshy, forest and mountain.
#### 2.1 Alluvial, Black, Red, Laterite
| Soil | Area / share | Formation and properties | Regions | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alluvial** | 15 lakh sq km, **45.6%** – most widespread | River deposits (drifted); Bhangar and Khadar | Indo-Gangetic Plain, river valleys, deltas, coasts, Terai | Rice, wheat, sugarcane, cotton, jute |
| **Black (regur / black cotton)** | 5.47 lakh sq km, **16.6%** | Weathered **Deccan Trap basalt**; clay **>62%**; **highest moisture retention**; deep summer cracks – **self-ploughing**; neutral to alkaline; rich in **Ca, K, Mg**; poor in **N, P, humus** | Deccan Plateau: Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP, W. AP, N. Karnataka | **Cotton**, jowar, chillies, tobacco |
| **Red and yellow** | 5.18 lakh sq km | **Granite, gneiss** under low rainfall; red from **iron oxide**, yellow when **hydrated**; porous, light; poor in lime, humus, phosphoric acid; low moisture retention | Peninsular India; Kanyakumari–Jhansi, Kutch–Rajmahal | Ragi, groundnut, tobacco |
| **Laterite** | 2.48 lakh sq km | **Heavy leaching**, rainfall **>200 cm**, high temperature; iron and aluminium oxides; **acidic, infertile**; poor in N, phosphate, calcium; excess iron oxide and potash | Summits of Western and Eastern Ghats, Vindhya–Satpura, Rajmahal hills, NE states; **also AP** | **Tea, coffee, rubber, cashew** (with manure) |

| Soil | Area / share | Formation and properties | Regions | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 3. ALLUVIAL TERMS AND KAREWA SOILS
#### 3.1 Khadar, Bhangar, Bhabar, Tarai
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Khadar** | **New alluvium**; renewed by flood silt every year; more fertile; ideal for intensive agriculture |
| **Bhangar** | **Old alluvium** on higher ground beyond flood reach; **kankar** (calcareous concretions); old **Kachhari clay**; pale reddish brown |
| **Bhabar** | Coarse, pebbly **piedmont** belt (8–16 km) south of the Shiwalik; streams **disappear** |
| **Tarai** | **Marshy lowland south of Bhabar**; now largely reclaimed |
!TRAP The claim that Khadar holds calcareous deposits is FALSE – kankar belongs to Bhangar.
#### 3.2 Karewa Soils
- **Lacustrine (old lake-bed) deposits** of gravel and mud; placed in the Tertiary rock system.
- Plateaus above the Jhelum plains in the **Kashmir Valley** and Bhadarwah valley.
- Famous for **saffron (Pampore)**, almonds and walnuts.
- 19 Feb 2026: high-density apple planting spreading onto Karewas with new rootstocks.
### 4. SOIL PROPERTIES COMPARED
| Property | Soil |
|---|---|
| Highest moisture retention | **Black** |
| Deficient in N, P, humus | Black (and most Indian soils) |
| Deficient in lime, humus, phosphoric acid | Red |
| **Acidic** | **Laterite, peaty, forest** |
| Neutral to alkaline | Black, saline-alkaline |
| **Building material** | **Laterite** – cut into bricks that harden in air |
| Rich in phosphorus | Desert |
- **Tea**: slightly acidic soil without calcium; cannot bear standing water → hill slopes.
- **Coffee**: red and laterite soils of the Western Ghats (not alluvial).
- **AP laterite**: poor in nitrogen. !TRAP "N is plentiful in AP laterite" is WRONG.
### 5. BLACK SOIL AND PEATY SOIL – SPECIAL ANGLES
- **Very deep black soils**: **north-western Deccan Plateau** (APPSC G2 2025).
- **Peaty soils**: areas of **heavy rainfall, high humidity and good vegetation**; organic matter builds faster than it rots.
!TRAP "Semi-arid climate with salinity" describes **saline** soil, not peaty soil (G1 2024 distractor).
### 6. ICAR / USDA CLASSIFICATION
| Soil order | Share of India's area |
|---|---|
| **Inceptisols** (largest) | ~39.7% |
| Entisols | ~28.1% |
| Alfisols | ~13.6% |
| **Vertisols** (black soils) | ~8.5% |
- Decreasing area among four (PYQ): **Entisols > Alfisols > Aridisols > Ultisols**.
- **Soil order** = most general level of USDA Soil Taxonomy; smallest order in India = **Mollisols**.
- Entisols: about 16% of the world's ice-free land; on newly deposited or eroded material.
### 7. SOIL EROSION
#### 7.1 Types
| Type | Process | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Sheet** | Thin uniform topsoil layer removed by overland flow; widespread, **least noticed** | Gentle cultivated slopes |
| **Rill** | Small channels cut by intense rain | Bare fields |
| **Gully** | Deep trenches; land unfit; **final stage** | **Chambal ravines** |
| **Wind** | Deflation, saltation, surface creep | Thar Desert |
| Mass (landslide) | Gravity on slopes | Himalayan monsoon landslides |
| Tunnel | Subsurface removal; sinkholes | Parts of Australia, Africa |
!NUM Increasing soil loss: splash → sheet → rill → gully. Water = most powerful erosion agent.
- Wind erosion worst on **bare, dry, fine-textured** soil with strong, frequent winds.
#### 7.2 Factors Controlling Erosion Rate
- Rainfall **intensity** and duration, **slope**, **vegetation cover**/land use, soil texture.
!KEY Rainfall **intensity** matters more than **total** rainfall.
- Intense bursts exceed **infiltration capacity** → water runs off or evaporates instead of recharging; two 2026 studies (Nature, Nature Geoscience) link concentrated rain to drier soils.
- **RUSLE** predicts long-term water erosion on slopes; ICAR made national **C- and P-factor maps**; a 2026 study pairs it with GIS for NE India's hills.
- Steep slopes lose carbon-rich topsoil.
### 8. SOIL CONSERVATION
| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Contour ploughing / bunding** | Along contour lines on hilly, sloping, rainfed land; slows runoff |
| **Terracing** | Steps on steep slopes |
| **Strip cropping** | Alternate erosion-resistant crops (grass) with row crops |
| **Shelter belts** | Tree rows cutting wind speed (arid W India) |
| **Crop rotation** | Different crops by season; raises fertility, cuts pests, prevents erosion (APPSC 2024) |
| **Check dams** | Trap sediment, stop gully growth |
| **Conservation agriculture** | FAO concept; **minimum/zero tillage**; **retains** crop residue as cover |
| Afforestation; banning shifting cultivation | Checks erosion |
| Straw checkerboards (China) | Stabilise desert dunes |
| Biochar | Raises water holding; cuts soil N2O by ~30–50% |
- Others: agroforestry, seed balls; **contour bunding, relay cropping, zero tillage** aid **carbon sequestration**.
- **Soil conservation** = protecting soil from harm – controlling erosion **and** keeping fertility high (APPSC 2025).
- **Seed modification does NOT** improve soil fertility; crop rotation, mixed and multiple cropping do.
- Problem → remedy: deforestation → afforestation; soil erosion → **soil stabilisation**; habitat destruction → restoration; biodiversity loss → conservation.
### 9. LAND DEGRADATION AND PROBLEM SOILS
#### 9.1 Salinity, Alkalinity, Waterlogging
- Caused by **unscientific irrigation and poor drainage**; salts hinder water and nutrient uptake.
- Affected: **Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, UP, Bihar**; canal-irrigated **Punjab** after the Green Revolution. **Maximum alkali area: Uttar Pradesh**.
- 50% of world's arable land could be affected by 2050 without action.
| Problem | Remedy |
|---|---|
| **Alkaline (sodic)/saline** | **Gypsum** + about one foot of water for leaching (long-term solution) |
| **Acidic** | **Lime** |
| Salinity (other) | Drainage, leaching, salt-tolerant crops |
| Waterlogging | Drains, land levelling |
!TRAP Gypsum → alkaline soils; lime → acidic soils.
#### 9.2 Causes → States
| Cause | States |
|---|---|
| Overgrazing | Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra |
| Over-irrigation | Punjab, Haryana, western UP |
| Mining; deforestation | Jharkhand, Odisha, MP, Chhattisgarh (mining); hills (deforestation) |
#### 9.3 Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas
- By **ISRO's Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad**, June 2021: **97.85 mha (29.7%)** of India degraded in 2018-19 (28.76% in 2003-05).
- Desertification rose in **28 of 31** states/UTs; water erosion is the largest driver.
#### 9.4 Global Status
- **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026** (75 countries): soil-biodiversity loss **4th of 7** threats (after erosion, SOC loss, nutrient mismanagement); sustainable practices worsening in **58%** of assessments, improving in ~10%.
### 10. SCHEMES AND ANDHRA PRADESH
#### 10.1 Soil Health Card
- Launched **19 Feb 2015** at **Suratgarh, Rajasthan**; slogan "**Swasth Dharaa, Khet Haraa**"; cards **every two years**.
- **12 parameters**: N, P, K, S, Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B, pH, EC, organic carbon (micronutrients: Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B).
- **25.79 crore** cards generated (Feb 2026); **Uttar Pradesh** issued the most.
#### 10.2 Watershed
- **IWMP** (2009-10) merged in **2015-16** into the Watershed Development Component of **PMKSY**; other programmes: NMSA, RKVY, Desert Development Programme.
#### 10.3 AP Angle
- 22 soil categories; 10 predominant cover >88% of AP; six agro-climatic zones.
- Cultivated area: **red ~60%, black ~25%**, deltaic alluvium 5–6%, laterite ~1%.
- **Kurnool** (Jul 2026): Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing, target **92,000 acres**, with **Jeevamrutham**.
