## U0458 | ECONOMIC GROWTH VS ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT; MEASURES OF DEVELOPMENT
### 1. GROWTH VS DEVELOPMENT
| Basis | Economic growth | Economic development |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Sustained rise in **actual (real) output** | Growth + better living standards, capabilities, distribution (**normative**) |
| Nature | **Quantitative** | Qualitative + quantitative |
| Scope | Narrow | **Broad; includes growth** |
| Measure | Real GDP/GNP, per capita income | HDI, poverty ratio, literacy, life expectancy, inequality |
| Distribution | Not a concern | Central concern |
| Relation | **Means**; necessary, not sufficient | **End** |
- One-liner: growth = increase in output; development = increase in **wellbeing**. GDP alone cannot measure development.
- Rise in absolute or **per capita real GNP** is NOT higher development if **poverty and unemployment rise**.
- Growth without development: **jobless growth** or unequal gains. Development needs a **multi-dimensional** approach.
- Economic growth usually comes with **inflation** (rising income raises demand). Government focuses on both micro and macro issues.
- Kerala: **2nd in HDI** among States but **7th in per capita NSDP**.
### 2. MODELS AND THE "NEW VIEW"
| Model / view | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Western / modernisation model** (e.g. Rostow) | Key factor = **economic growth** (GDP, industrialisation) (APPSC 2018) |
| Gandhian model | Decentralisation, village industry |
| Kerala model | High human development at modest income |
| Trickle-down | Growth benefits reach the poor automatically (early Indian plans) |
| Capability approach | Broader than GDP |
| Directive Principles | Adequate livelihood, reduced inequality – not just national income |
- **New view (1970s)**: framed by **Dudley Seers**, *The Meaning of Development* (**1969**) – reduce **poverty, unemployment, inequality**.
!TRAP New view does NOT stress the **simple growth rate** (APPSC-G2 2017).
### 3. INDICATORS OF DEVELOPMENT
- Valid: **institutional change**, **structural transformation** (agrarian → industry and services), **inclusive growth**, human resource development, rising capital formation.
- Best indicator of structural transformation: rising share of **manufactured products in exports**.
!TRAP NOT an indicator: a **larger GDP share from the primary sector** (APPSC-G2 2008).

### 5. MEASURES OF DEVELOPMENT
| Measure | Developer / year | Components / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Per capita real income** | **Simon Kuznets**, US, **1934** (Nobel 1971) | **Oldest** measure; hides distribution; "best index" = growth of per capita real income yearly |
| **PQLI** | **Morris D. Morris**, mid-1970s (1979), Overseas Development Council | **Literacy, IMR, life expectancy at age one**; 0–100 |
| **HDI** | **UNDP, 1990**, Mahbub ul Haq (with Sen) | Life expectancy **at birth**, mean and expected schooling, **GNI per capita (PPP)**; no urbanisation |
| **MPI** | **OPHI + UNDP, 2010** | Health, education, living standards; 10 indicators |
| GII | UNDP | Health, empowerment, labour |
| **BNI** | **Economic Survey** | Water, sanitation, housing |
| **GNH** | **Bhutan**, 4th King **Jigme Singye Wangchuck**, 1970s | Alternative to GDP |
| **MEW** | **Nordhaus and Tobin**, 1972 | Welfare |
| **NEW** | **Paul Samuelson** | Welfare |
| State Health Index | NITI Aayog | – |
- Order of introduction: **Per capita income → PQLI → HDI**.
- First HDR 1990: **Mahbub ul Haq and Inge Kaul**. First State HDR: **Madhya Pradesh**.
- Highest life expectancy (options): **Japan**, 85.00 years (2025).
| Index | Measures | Compiled by |
|---|---|---|
| WPI | Wholesale prices | **Office of the Economic Adviser** |
| CPI | Retail prices | NSO |
| IIP | Industrial output (mining, manufacturing, electricity) | **NSO** |
!TRAP PQLI = life expectancy at **age one**; HDI = **at birth**. GNI per capita (PPP) is in HDI, not PQLI. "IIP measures wholesale prices" is wrong.

### 8. DETERMINANTS OF GROWTH; CAPITAL-OUTPUT RATIO
| Economic | Non-economic |
|---|---|
| **Capital formation**, natural resources, technical progress, human capital, labour | Institutions, rule of law, **social behaviour**, political stability, population quality |
- **Capital formation** = most important growth factor (Planning Commission). Higher saving/investment → higher growth; rising investment reduces poverty.
- **Higher ICOR** = more capital per extra output → **lower growth**.
- Capital formation may fail to raise output: idle/misused capital, missing inputs, weak demand (**all of these**); capital-intensive investment adds few jobs.
- **Corruption** = a barrier (governance factor), not a contributor. Technocrats and bureaucrats are NOT a major factor.
- Development and population growth affect **each other**. Slow public-sector jobs: reassessment of the State's role (NEP 1991).
### 9. SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT AND AP DATA
- **Brundtland** (*Our Common Future*, **1987**): meets present needs **without compromising future generations**; does not ban resource use.
- Sustainable growth = policy for **economic and social well-being** (APPSC 2018 key). **Neemrana** (Rajasthan) = model.
- **Green GDP** = GDP minus natural resource depletion and environmental degradation costs.
- **Green economy (UNEP)**: low-carbon, resource-efficient, socially inclusive; not only agriculture.
- **AP**: GSDP 2025-26 **₹17.62 lakh crore** (+10.75%); per capita **₹2,94,507** vs India ₹2,19,575. **Swarna Andhra @2047**: 15%+ growth, ₹308 lakh crore GSDP, ₹55 lakh per capita, **651 KPIs**. AP = first State with **monthly economic reports**. Claim that **AP Economic Development Board** has 6 divisions is incorrect. Vision 2020 for India: **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**.
