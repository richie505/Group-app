## U0449 | OCCUPATIONAL STRUCTURE OF INDIA – TRENDS AND INTER-STATE VARIATION
### 1. MEANING AND THEORY
- **Occupational structure** = spread of the working population among different occupations – across **primary, secondary and tertiary** activities.
- **Clark-Fisher thesis**: as income rises, labour shifts **primary → secondary → tertiary**.
- **Structural retardation** (India): the occupational structure changed far more slowly than output; India moved from agriculture **directly to services** with no manufacturing-led stage.
- Reason the structure stayed about the same: **investment directed towards capital-intensive sectors**.
- **Premature deindustrialisation**: manufacturing fails to absorb enough farm labour.
### 2. WORKFORCE SHARE BY SECTOR OVER TIME
| Year / survey | Agriculture share of workers |
|---|---|
| 1951 | **72%** |
| Census 2001 | 58.2% |
| Census 2011 | **54.6%** |
| PLFS 2023-24 | **46.1%** |
| PLFS Q2 FY26 | **42.4%** |
- **Agriculture** = always the **highest employment share** (falling). At the **eve of Independence**, the main livelihood was agriculture.
- 2022-23: agriculture about **18% of GVA** but **42.4%** of the workforce – the GDP-share vs employment-share gap.
- 2021-22 workforce: primary **43.96%**, secondary **22.24%**, tertiary **33.80%**.
- Urban employment: services **62.0%** (Q2 FY26).
- PLFS Jan-Mar 2026: rural agricultural employment 58.5% → **55.8%**; rural secondary 22.6%, tertiary 21.7%.
- Workforce size: **61.6 crore** (PLFS 2025); 56.2 crore employed in Q2 FY26.
- Agricultural households = **57.8%** of rural households (NSSO 70th round, 2013).
!TRAP Highest employment share = **agriculture**; an old APPSC-GS 2007 key "cottage industries" is wrong.
### 3. CENSUS WORKER CATEGORIES AND CENSUS FACTS
| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Main worker** | Worked 6 months or more in the reference year |
| **Marginal worker** | Worked less than 6 months |
| **Cultivator** | Works on own/leased land |
| **Agricultural labourer** | Works on another person's land for wages |
| **Household industry** | Production in the household, by family members |
| **Other workers** | All other work – services, trade, factories |
- First Census of the modern system: **1872** (Lord **Mayo**); first **regular**, scientific Census: **1881** (Lord **Ripon**).
- **Census 2011** = **15th** since 1872, **7th** after Independence. **Census 2027** = first **app-based digital** Census.
- Census 2011: workers **48.19 crore**; work participation rate **39.8%**; male workers about **332 million**.
### 4. CULTIVATORS VS AGRICULTURAL LABOURERS
| Category | Census 2001 | Census 2011 | Share 2011 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cultivators | 127.3 million | **118.8 million ↓** | **24.7%** |
| Agricultural labourers | 106.8 million | **144.3 million ↑** | **30.0%** |
- **2011** = first Census in which **labourers outnumbered cultivators** (APPSC-G1 2017: cultivators down, labourers up).
- Cause – **fragmentation**: average holding **2.28 ha (1971) → 1.08 ha (2015-16)**; small and marginal farmers ~**86%**.
### 5. RURAL VS URBAN AND WOMEN WORKERS
- Census 2011: **72.4%** of workers in **rural** areas, against **68.8%** of population (APPSC-GS 2025).
!TRAP 72.4% = rural share of **workers**; 68.8% = rural share of **population**.
#### 5.1 Feminisation of Agriculture
- Female workers: **55%** agricultural labourers, **24%** cultivators; women hold only **12.8%** of operational holdings.
- Drivers: **subsistence farming, unpaid family work, distress employment** (not formal manufacturing jobs).
- Rural women self-employed: 60% (2011-12) → 73% (2023-24).
- UN: **2026 = International Year of the Woman Farmer**.
#### 5.2 Female Labour Force Participation (15+)
- **23.3% (2017-18) → 37% (2022-23) → 40% (2025)**; from 30% in 2019-20.
- Rural 33.0% → **45.9%**; urban 23.3% → 27.7% (2019-20 to 2025).
- Rural women = **80%** of the rise in female workers (2017-18 to 2023-24).
- Women = **32.5%** of employment (2025); share in salaried jobs 16.6% → 18.2%; 40-44% of women outside the labour force cite caregiving.
### 6. LABOUR INDICATORS AND EMPLOYMENT STATUS
| Indicator | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **LFPR** | % of population **working or seeking/available** for work |
| **WPR** | % of population **employed** |
| **Unemployment rate** | % of the **labour force** that is unemployed |
| **Usual status (ps+ss)** | Reference period **365 days** |
| **Current weekly status** | Reference period **7 days** – used in monthly/quarterly bulletins |
- Monthly PLFS began with **April 2025** data. July 2026: rural unemployment 5% → **4.5%**, LFPR rose (may show distress entry into low-productivity work).
- Employment status (PLFS 2025): **regular wage/salaried 22% → 24%**; **self-employment 58% → 56%**.
- Educated unemployment 6.5% (urban 7.2%); 29% of tertiary-educated youth unemployed (2023-24).
### 7. INFORMAL AND UNORGANISED EMPLOYMENT
#### 7.1 Definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Unorganised sector (NCEUS)** | Unincorporated private enterprises of individuals/households, proprietary or partnership, **fewer than 10 workers** |
| Unorganised sector (general) | **Small, scattered** units largely **outside government control** (GS 2025); low pay, no job security |
| **Organised manufacturing** | Registered factories – **10+ workers with power / 20+ without** |
| Informal worker (NCEUS) | Unorganised-sector workers + organised-sector workers without social security |
| Unorganised workers (Code on Social Security 2020) | Home-based, self-employed or wage workers in the unorganised sector |
| **Informalisation of formal enterprises** | Contract and outsourced workers inside formal firms |
| **"Formal informality"** | New EPFO 'formal' jobs that are short-term, low-wage or contractual |
- **NCEUS**: chaired by **Dr. Arjun Sengupta**; formed **20.09.2004**, term ended **30.04.2009**.
#### 7.2 Data, Codes and Portals
- Informal employment: **over 80%** of the workforce (Mar 2026); non-farm informal share about **78%** (PLFS 2022-23).
- **NSS 73rd round (2015-16)**: unincorporated non-agricultural enterprises employed about **11 crore**; working owners now 60% of their workforce.
- **e-Shram**: **31 crore+** unorganised workers registered.
- **Four Labour Codes** merged **29** laws: Code on Wages **2019**; Industrial Relations, Social Security, OSH **2020**; expected to raise formalisation from 60.4% to 75.5% + 77 lakh jobs by 2029-30.
- Social-protection coverage: **19% (2015) → 64.3% (2025)** – India **2nd after China**.
- Direct factory employment **61% → 47%** (2011-2023); **contract workers = 42%** of factory workforce.
!TRAP NCEUS cut-off = fewer than **10** workers, not 20.
### 8. GIG AND PLATFORM ECONOMY
- **Gig worker**: earns outside the **traditional employer-employee relationship**; defined **for the first time** in the **Code on Social Security, 2020** (Section 2(35)).
- **Platform work**: work outside an employer-employee relationship where organisations/individuals use an **online platform** to reach others or provide services.
- Gig workers = about **2%** of the 61.6 crore workforce; not yet a separate PLFS category.
### 9. JOBLESS GROWTH AND SLOW SHIFT OUT OF AGRICULTURE
- **Jobless growth**: output grows without a matching rise in quality employment – **late 1990s**, when employment growth fell to early-plan levels while GDP grew.
- **Employment elasticity** (job growth per unit of output growth): **negative in agriculture**; **construction** and services most employment-intensive (RBI study, 2014).
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| History | **Colonial deindustrialisation** pushed artisans onto land |
| Land pressure | Agriculture's share fell only slightly in the Plan era |
| Capital intensity | Growth failed to absorb surplus farm labour |
| Skills | Only **4.2%** had formal vocational training |
| Job deficit | ~20 million entrants a year vs ~2 million new jobs; about 12 million enter each year |
- Firms prefer flexibility → away from formal employment.
### 10. TYPES OF UNEMPLOYMENT
| Type | Feature | Linked with |
|---|---|---|
| **Disguised** | More people than needed; **marginal productivity near zero** | **Agriculture** (rural) |
| **Seasonal** | Work only in **sowing/harvest** | Agriculture; 30-35 million seasonal migrants a year |
| **Structural** | **Skill-job mismatch** (new technology) | Industry, urban, educated |
| **Frictional** | **Short gap** between jobs | Urban/organised |
| **Cyclical** | Demand falls in a **business-cycle** slump | Industry |
