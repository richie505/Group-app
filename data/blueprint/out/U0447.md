## U0447 | ROLE OF THE IT AND ITES INDUSTRY IN INDIA'S DEVELOPMENT
### 1. ORIGINS – POLICIES AND INSTITUTIONS
| Policy / institution | Year | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Software Export Scheme** | **1972** | Computers imported against an **export obligation** |
| Computer Policy / Software Export Policy | 1984 / 1986 | Liberalised imports and data links |
| **NASSCOM** | **1988** | Industry body; annual Strategic Review = data source |
| **STPI** | **1991** | Under the **Department of Electronics**; satellite data links + **single-window** clearances; now in Tier-2/3 cities |
| NPIT 2012 | 2012 | Targets for 2020 |
| **MeitY** | separate ministry **2016** | Earlier **DeitY** |
- The **Department of Electronics** first spotted the software export opportunity; **STPI** = India's **first export-park model** for software.
- **NPIT 2012** targets (2020): revenue USD 100 bn → **300 bn**; exports USD 69 bn → **200 bn**; **10 million** more skilled workers; **one e-literate person in every household** (not two).
!TRAP 1972 = Software Export Scheme; 1991 = STPI; 2016 = MeitY.
### 2. REVENUE AND EXPORTS
- 1990s boom: software exports grew **over 50% a year after 1995-96** – low cost + international quality; IX Plan (1997-2002) CAGR **52%**.
- Question-period data: exports CAGR about **60%** over five years; software and services growth about **28%** in rupee terms.
- Software services exports **2022-23: US $185.5 bn** (RBI); IT-BPM revenue FY2022-23 USD 245 bn.
- **FY2025 (NASSCOM):** revenue **USD 282.6 bn** (+5.1%); exports **USD 224.4 bn** (~**79%**); domestic USD 58.2 bn.
- Software/computer services = India's **largest services export** and **largest services forex earner** → offsets the **merchandise trade deficit** in the BoP.
### 3. EMPLOYMENT
- **~58 lakh (5.8 million)** direct jobs (FY2025); about 1.26 lakh added.
- Mostly **organised** jobs with contracts and social security; high share of **women**.
- **Quarterly Employment Survey (QES)** – **Labour Bureau**; **9 non-farm sectors** (manufacturing, construction, trade, transport, education, health, accommodation & restaurants, IT/BPO, financial services).
- In QES 2021 rounds **manufacturing** had the largest share (~39-41%); IT/BPO ~7% but **fastest growth (152%)**.
- Biggest tech employers (2018): large **Indian IT services firms** (TCS, Infosys), not listed multinationals.
### 4. FIRMS, HUBS AND DESTINATIONS
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|

| AP | IT Park, **Madhurawada (Rushikonda), Visakhapatnam** – AP IT & GCC Policy 4.0 |
| Destinations | **USA and Europe** |
### 5. CHALLENGES
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| External dependence | Visa rules, protectionism, client spending cycles |
| Automation | Routine **ITES/BPM** most exposed to **generative AI** |
| Jobs paradox | Fast GVA growth, few jobs – skill-intensive and automated |
| Other risks | Urban concentration, Bengaluru water stress, Chennai floods 2015, digital divide |
- Skill gap: employers want AI-ready skills – cloud, GenAI, data analytics (May 2026).
- IT earns forex and formal jobs but serves a **skilled few in a few cities** → cannot deliver broad development alone.
### 6. DIGITAL INDIA AND DATA PROTECTION
- **Digital India**: launched **1 July 2015** (MeitY); **nine pillars**, first = Broadband Highways.
- **BharatNet**: renamed from **NOFN** (2015); optical fibre to about **2.5 lakh Gram Panchayats**.
- **IndiaAI Mission**: Cabinet approval **March 2024**, outlay **Rs 10,372 crore** (compute capacity).
- **DPDP Act 2023**: assent **11 August 2023**; **Data Protection Board of India** imposes penalties up to **Rs 250 crore**; **DPDP Rules 2025** notified **November 2025**, 18-month phased rollout.
