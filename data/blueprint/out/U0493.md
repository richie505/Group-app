## U0493 | GREEN REVOLUTION – COMPONENTS, ACHIEVEMENTS AND CRITICISM; WHITE, YELLOW, BLUE AND RAINBOW REVOLUTIONS
### 1. GREEN REVOLUTION – BACKGROUND AND TIMELINE
#### 1.1 When and Where
- HYV seeds were first introduced in the **Rabi season of 1966–67**, during the **Plan Holiday (three Annual Plans, 1966–69)** that followed the Third Five Year Plan.
- First crop transformed: **wheat**; **pioneer state: Punjab**; rice gained later in a wider phase (AP and TN deltas).
- Indian Green Revolution is linked with **Pantnagar** – G.B. Pant University of Agriculture and Technology, India's **first agricultural university (1960)**.
- First **Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK)**: **Puducherry, 1974**; part of the National Agricultural Research System (NARS).
#### 1.2 Key Persons
| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| **M.S. Swaminathan** | Father of the Green Revolution in **India** |
| **Norman Borlaug** | Green Revolution in the **world**; Mexican dwarf wheat |
| **C. Subramaniam** | Union Minister for Food and Agriculture (1964–66) who led the policy push; **Bharat Ratna 1998** |
#### 1.3 ICAR Projects – Chronology
- **KVK 1974 → Lab to Land 1979 → NATP 1998 → NAIP 2006**.
!TRAP First agricultural university = Pantnagar (1960); first KVK = Puducherry (1974). Do not swap them.
### 2. COMPONENTS – THE PACKAGE APPROACH
#### 2.1 The Package
1. **HYV seeds**
2. **Assured irrigation** (canals, tubewells)
3. **Chemical fertilisers and pesticides**
4. **Mechanisation**
5. **Institutional credit**
6. **MSP / assured procurement**
#### 2.2 HYV Traits
- **Short stature (dwarf)**: stays upright, resists lodging.
- **Mature quicker** → allows **double cropping**.
#### 2.3 Programmes Before and With the Green Revolution
| Programme | Year | Coverage / feature |
|---|---|---|
| **IADP** (Intensive Agricultural District Programme) | **1960** | Pilot in **7 districts of 7 States**; AP's district = **West Godavari** |
| **IAAP** (Intensive Agricultural Area Programme) | **1964–65** | Extended to **114 districts**; **117 blocks in AP**, starting in West Godavari |
!PAIR IADP | 1960 | 7 districts || IAAP | 1964–65 | 114 districts
### 3. HYV VARIETIES
| Variety | Crop / note |
|---|---|
| **Taichung Native-1 (TN-1)** | **First semi-dwarf rice introduced in India (1965)** |
| **IR-8** | Dwarf rice from **IRRI, Philippines**; "**miracle rice**" (1966) |
| **Lerma Rojo 64-A, Sonora 64** | **Mexican** (Borlaug) dwarf wheat |
| **Kalyan Sona, Sonalika** | **Indian** dwarf wheat selections; resist lodging |
- Green Revolution = HYVs of **rice and wheat** suited to local conditions.
!TRAP First semi-dwarf rice in India = TN-1 (1965), not IR-8. IR-8 is the IRRI "miracle rice".

- Rice gains came in the **AP (Godavari–Krishna) and TN deltas**.
- AP's IADP district and the start of IAAP in AP: **West Godavari**.

### 5. OUTCOMES – GAINS
- **Crops**: wheat gained the most (initial phase); rice later.
- **Regions**: **Punjab, Haryana, western UP**; rice in AP and TN deltas.
- **Self-sufficiency**: India stopped relying on **PL-480** imports.
- **Foodgrains**: about **70–75 MT** (mid-1960s) → record **357.73 MT in 2024–25**.
### 6. OUTCOMES – COSTS AND CRITICISM
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Disparity | **Regional + interpersonal**; farmers with **more than 10 ha** benefited most |
| Crop bias | Neglect of **pulses, oilseeds, millets / coarse cereals** |
| Groundwater | Punjab and Haryana extract **35–57% more than recharge** |
| Soil and ecology | **Salinity**, loss of biodiversity, skewed **N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1** (not 1:1:1) |
- **No true food self-sufficiency** despite a three-fold foodgrain rise (1950–1990): GR helped only certain areas and large farmers; focused on wheat, rice and some cash crops, not coarse cereals; foodgrain prices stayed high.
- Debate: water-intensive **rice-wheat monoculture** vs crop diversification.
- **Johl Committee**: shift **20% of paddy area** (crop diversification).
!TRAP Assertion-reason: HYV + fertiliser + irrigation explain BOTH higher output AND ecological damage.
### 7. SECOND GREEN REVOLUTION, EVERGREEN REVOLUTION AND BGREI
#### 7.1 Second Green Revolution and Evergreen Revolution
- **3 Jan 2006**: PM **Manmohan Singh**, at the **93rd Indian Science Congress, Hyderabad**, called for a **Second Green Revolution** (non-food crops, horticulture, dryland farming, small farmers).
- **Evergreen Revolution**: concept of **M.S. Swaminathan** – raise productivity **in perpetuity without ecological harm**; sustainable, climate-smart farming.
- **Aug 2025**: PM inaugurated the M.S. Swaminathan Centenary Conference, theme "**Evergreen Revolution, The Pathway to Biohappiness**".
#### 7.2 BGREI
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Full name | **Bringing Green Revolution to Eastern India** |
| Launched | **2010–11** |
| Parent scheme | Sub-scheme of **RKVY** |
| Aim | Rice-based productivity in eastern India via **HYVs, irrigation, balanced nutrients, IPM** |
| States (7) | **Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Eastern UP, West Bengal** |
- "**Purvodaya**": later idea of shifting the rice bowl eastward – same logic.
### 8. WHITE REVOLUTION – OPERATION FLOOD
#### 8.1 Background
- **GCMMF (1946)**: milk marketing (**AMUL**); **NDDB set up 1965** to copy the AMUL model.
- **Operation Flood (1970)** by **NDDB**: national milk grid; **world's largest dairy development programme**.
- Model: **Amul three-tier cooperative**, **Anand, Gujarat**; village society = base. White Revolution **started in Gujarat**.
- **Dr Verghese Kurien** – **Father of the White Revolution**, with **Tribhuvandas Patel** at Anand; also linked with the **Institute of Rural Management, Anand**.
#### 8.2 Phases
| Phase | Years | Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| I | **1970–80** | Financed by the **World Food Programme**; milk sheds in **18 states** linked to Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai |
| II | **1981–85** | Milk sheds **18 → 136**; urban markets up to 200 |
| III | **1985–96** | **30,000** new dairy cooperatives |
#### 8.3 White Revolution 2.0
- **Sept 2024**: launched by **Ministry of Cooperation**; **75,000** new dairy cooperative societies.
- Milk output **239.3 MMT (2023–24)** → about **248 MT** a year; India = **world's largest producer**.
