## U0313 | GOVERNOR – APPOINTMENT, POWERS, DISCRETION, ARTICLE 200/201 AND ARTICLE 356
### 1. OFFICE OF THE GOVERNOR – ARTICLES 153-159
- **Nominal head**; the **CM and Council of Ministers** are the real executive.
- **Article 168**: the Governor is an integral part of the State Legislature without being a member.
#### 1.1 Articles on the Office
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| 153 | A Governor for each State; **7th Amendment, 1956** – same person for **two or more States** |
| 154(1) | **Executive power** of the State vested in the Governor |
| 155 | **Appointed by the President** by warrant under his hand and seal (neither directly nor indirectly elected) |
| 156 | Term – holds office during the **pleasure of the President** |
| 157 | Qualifications – citizen of India, completed **35 years** |
| 158 | Conditions – not an MP or MLA (must vacate seat), no office of profit; emoluments fixed by Parliament by law, cannot be reduced during term |
| 159 | **Oath** by the **Chief Justice of the High Court** (in his absence, the senior-most judge) |
| 202(3) | Emoluments **charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State** |
- A Governor of two or more States: salary shared among the States in the **ratio fixed by the President**.
- **Conventions:** an **outsider** to the State; the State CM is consulted (not compulsory).
#### 1.2 Term, Resignation, Removal
- Normal term **5 years**, but continues **until his successor enters office** (Art. 156(3)); may be removed or transferred earlier and reappointed.
- **Resigns to the President**; answers to the President (Art. 156(1)); the **State Legislature cannot remove** him.
- **No procedure or grounds for removal** in the Constitution.
- Not answerable to either House of Parliament, the State Legislature or the State Council of Ministers.
!TRAP APPSC-G1 2019: "The Governor quits office immediately after his term expires" is WRONG – he stays until the successor takes office.
#### 1.3 B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)
- Pleasure doctrine is **not unfettered**; reasons need not be given, but removal **must not be arbitrary**.
- Cannot be removed merely because the **Centre's government changes** or he is out of tune with the Centre; **limited judicial review**.
#### 1.4 Immunity – Article 361
- **Art. 361(1)**: not answerable to any court for acts done in office.
- **Art. 361(2)**: **no criminal proceedings** started or continued, and no arrest, during the term – even with a court's permission.
- **Art. 361(4)**: civil proceedings for personal acts can be started only after **2 months' notice** (so he can be sued).

#### 2.3 Ordinance and Pardon – Governor vs President
| Basis | President | Governor |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinance | **Art. 123**; Union + Concurrent List; co-extensive with Parliament | **Art. 213**; State + Concurrent List |
| Approval of ordinance | Parliament | **State Legislature** (not the President) |
| Prior Presidential instruction | Not applicable | Needed when a Bill with same provisions would need **prior sanction or reservation** |
| Lapse | 6 weeks after Parliament reassembles | 6 weeks after the Legislature reassembles |
| Withdrawal | Any time – 123(2)(b) | Any time – 213(2)(b) |
| Pardon | **Art. 72**, all cases | **Art. 161**, offences against **State laws** only |
| Discretion | Very limited | **Comparatively wider** |
- Governor's ordinance has the **same force as a State Act**; must be placed before the Assembly.
- **Art. 161** – pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions; suspend or commute sentences.
- **Perarivalan (2022)**: Governor **bound by Cabinet advice** on remission under Art. 161 (reaffirmed 2026).
### 3. CHIEF MINISTER AND COUNCIL OF MINISTERS
#### 3.1 Union vs State
| Basis | Union | State |
|---|---|---|
| Aids and advises | President – Art. 74 | Governor – **Art. 163** |
| Collectively responsible to | Lok Sabha – 75(3) | **Legislative Assembly** – **164(2)** (not the Council) |

| Non-member Minister | 6 consecutive months – 75(5) | 6 consecutive months – **164(4)** |
- A passed **no-confidence motion** makes the whole ministry including the CM resign.

### 4. DISCRETIONARY POWERS – ARTICLE 163
- **163(1)**: Council headed by CM aids and advises, **except where he acts in his discretion** (discretion explicitly mentioned); courts cannot review acts done in his own discretion.
- **163(2)**: Governor's decision on whether a matter is discretionary is **final** – but not complete immunity from judicial review.
- **163(3)**: courts **cannot inquire** into advice tendered by ministers.
- Governor is **not bound in all matters**: he can reserve a Bill **against ministerial advice**. His discretion is **wider than the President's**.
#### 4.1 Constitutional vs Situational Discretion
| Constitutional (express) | Situational |
|---|---|
| **Reserving a Bill** for the President (Art. 200) | Appointing a CM in a **hung Assembly** |
| **Report** recommending President's Rule (Art. 356) | Dismissing a ministry that lost majority |
| Seeking information from CM (Art. 167) | Dissolving the Assembly when majority lost |
| **Administrator of adjoining UT** (Art. 239(2)) | Asking Government to prove majority |
| Sixth Schedule royalty share; special responsibilities under 371-371J | – |
- **Art. 239(2)**: as administrator of an adjoining UT, the Governor acts **independently of his State Council of Ministers**.
- Special responsibility for law and order: **371A Nagaland**, **371H Arunachal Pradesh**. Fifth Schedule – reports to the President on Scheduled Areas; Sixth Schedule – Autonomous District Councils.
!TRAP Appointing Ministers is NOT constitutional discretion. Hung Assembly = situational; Art. 200 reservation, Art. 356 report, Art. 239 UT = constitutional.
#### 4.2 Hung Assembly – Order of Invitation (Sarkaria / Punchhi)
1. Pre-poll alliance
2. Largest single party with support
3. Post-poll coalition
4. Post-poll alliance with outside support – then an **early floor test**.
- **Tamil Nadu, May 2026**: Governor invited **C. Joseph Vijay** on **120 letters of support** (Art. 164) with an early floor test; sworn in as CM.
- **Kurian Joseph Committee (TN, 2026)**: proposes a **new Schedule** codifying the Governor's discretion; revisits Rameshwar Prasad's **cooling-off** period.
### 5. BILLS – ARTICLE 200 AND ARTICLE 201
#### 5.1 Governor's Options – Art. 200
1. **Assent**, 2. **withhold** assent, 3. **return once** for reconsideration (**not a Money Bill**), 4. **reserve** for the President.
- **Re-passed** returned Bill – Governor **must assent**; cannot refuse again.
- A Bill may be reserved **only once**; again only if materially altered.
- **Mandatory reservation**: only a Bill that **derogates from the powers of the High Court**.
- Constitution sets **no time limit** for returning an ordinary Bill.
#### 5.2 President's Options – Art. 201
- Assent, withhold, or (non-Money Bill) direct the Governor to return it; Legislature reconsiders within **6 months**; President **not bound** to assent afterwards – **absolute veto**.
- **No fixed time limit** for the President. Apr 2025: President withheld assent to TN's anti-NEET Bill.
#### 5.3 Reserved to Get Protection
| Article | Bill |
|---|---|
| 31A / 31C | Compulsory acquisition of property |
| 254(2) | Concurrent List repugnancy with central law |
| 288 | Taxes on water / electricity in inter-State river projects |
| 304(b) | Restrictions on inter-State trade – **previous Presidential sanction** before introduction |
| 360 | Money Bills during a Financial Emergency |
!KEY APPSC-GS 2025: reservation of Bills = an example of **central control over States**; APPSC-G1 2024 key "Only III" – mandatory reservation is only the High Court derogation case.

### 8. PRESIDENT'S RULE – ARTICLE 356
#### 8.1 Provisions
- When the government of a State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** – on the **Governor's report or otherwise** (suo motu).
- Grounds: **Art. 356** and **Art. 365** (State fails to follow Union directions).
- Parliamentary approval; **6 months** at a time; **maximum 3 years** (APPSC 2019 key).
- **Beyond 1 year** only if a **National Emergency** is in force and the **Election Commission certifies** elections cannot be held (**44th Amendment**).
- State Legislature's powers exercised by or under Parliament (**Art. 357**); Assembly **suspended or dissolved**. Central laws reach the State through an **Art. 357(1)(a)** resolution – e.g. Water Amendment Act extended to Manipur (Dec 2025).
#### 8.2 FIRSTs and Frequency
| Fact | Answer |
|---|---|
| FIRST imposition | **Punjab, 1951**; PEPSU, 1953 |
| First dismissal of an elected government | **Kerala, 1959** |
| Times used | Over **125** |
| Never imposed | **Telangana, Chhattisgarh** (APPSC 2019 key) |
| Most often | **Manipur**; 11th spell from **13 Feb 2025**, revoked **4 Feb 2026** (Y. Khemchand Singh CM) |
- **Former J&K**: 6 months of **Governor's Rule under Section 92** of the J&K Constitution, then Art. 356 (e.g. 2018); after 2019, President's Rule under the Reorganisation Act until Oct 2024.
- **Ayodhya (Dec 1992)**: BJP governments of **UP, MP, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh** dismissed; **Bihar not** (Bihar had President's Rule in 1995).
- PYQ key: no provision for Governor's Rule – **Mizoram**.
#### 8.3 Judicial Review – S.R. Bommai Guidelines
- Proclamations reviewable; Centre must show **relevant material**.
- Majority tested by **floor test** in the Assembly, **not in Raj Bhavan**.
- Assembly **not dissolved before Parliament approves**; removed ministry **can be restored**.
- Upheld dismissal of MP, Rajasthan, HP governments on **secularism**.
| Proper use | Improper use |
|---|---|
| Hung House with no alternative | Intra-party disputes |
| Flagrant defiance of constitutional directions | Maladministration / misrule alone |
| Physical breakdown | Ordinary law and order |
- **AP High Court episode (2020)**: "constitutional breakdown" observation – stayed by the Supreme Court.
