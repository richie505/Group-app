## U0173 | VICEROYS OF INDIA – CANNING TO MOUNTBATTEN: KEY MEASURES, ACTS AND EVENTS
### 1. FIRSTS, LASTS AND ORDER
| Fact | Person |
|---|---|
| **First Viceroy** (GoI Act / Proclamation 1858); **last Company GG** | **Lord Canning** |
| **Only Viceroy murdered in office** | **Lord Mayo** – by convict **Sher Ali Afridi**, **Andamans, 1872** |
| **First census** (non-synchronous) | **1872, Mayo** |
| **First synchronous (regular) census** | **1881, Ripon** |
| **Only Jewish Viceroy** | **Lord Reading (Rufus Isaacs)** |
| **Last Viceroy**; first GG of free India | **Mountbatten** |
| **First and last Indian GG** | **C. Rajagopalachari** (1948–50) |
- **21 Viceroys** ruled India from 1858 to 1947.
- **Curzon** – longest tenure among these (about 7 years, 1899–1905).
- **Order:** Canning → Lawrence → Mayo → Northbrook → Lytton → Ripon → Dufferin → Lansdowne → Elgin II → Curzon → Minto II → Hardinge II → Chelmsford → Reading → Irwin → Willingdon → Linlithgow → Wavell → Mountbatten.
### 2. VICEROYS 1856–1899 – KEY MEASURES
| Viceroy | Tenure | Key measures and events |
|---|---|---|
| **Canning** | 1856–62 | **Revolt of 1857**; withdrew **Doctrine of Lapse**; **universities at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1857)**; **IPC**; **Indian Councils Act 1861**; **portfolio system**; income tax; **High Courts Act 1861** |
| Elgin I | 1862–63 | **Wahabi** movement |
| **John Lawrence** | 1864–69 | **Masterly Inactivity** (non-interference in Afghanistan); **Shimla summer capital (1864)**; telegraph link with Europe; **Indian Forest Department**; canals and state railways |
| **Mayo** | 1869–72 | **Financial decentralisation (1870)**; first census; **Mayo College, Ajmer** and **Rajkot College**; statistical survey; **Dept of Agriculture and Commerce** |
| **Northbrook** | 1872–76 | Abolished **income tax**; **Kuka** movement |
| **Lytton** | 1876–80 | **Royal Titles Act (1876)**; **Delhi Durbar 1877**; **Vernacular Press Act + Arms Act (1878)**; **Second Afghan War (1878–80)** – **Forward Policy**; ICS age cut **21 → 19**; **Strachey** Famine Commission; appointed by **Disraeli** |
| **Ripon** | 1880–84 | **Repealed VPA (1882)**; **First Factory Act (1881)**; **Local Self-Government Resolution 1882** ("**Magna Carta**"; Ripon = "**Father of Local Self-Government**"); **Hunter Commission (1882)**; **Ilbert Bill (1883)** – "White Mutiny"; sent by **Gladstone's** Liberal government |
| **Dufferin** | 1884–88 | **INC founded (1885)** – mocked it as a "**microscopic minority**"; **Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885)** – Burma added **1 Jan 1886**, last major expansion |
| **Lansdowne** | 1888–94 | **Second Factory Act (1891)**; **Indian Councils Act 1892**; civil services split into imperial, provincial, subordinate; **Durand Commission (1893)** |
| **Elgin II** | 1894–99 | **Famine and plague 1896–97**; **Lyall Commission (1897)**; **Rand and Ayerst** killed by the **Chapekar brothers (1897)**; **Munda** uprising (1899) |
!TRAP Lytton is **not** linked with the Ilbert Bill; **Ripon** is. The VPA was passed by **Lytton (1878)**, not Curzon.
### 3. VICEROYS 1899–1948 – KEY MEASURES
| Viceroy | Tenure | Key measures and events |
|---|---|---|
| **Curzon** | 1899–1905 | **Partition of Bengal (1905)**; **Police Commission (1902)**; **Universities Commission + Indian Universities Act 1904**; **Ancient Monuments Act 1904**; ASI reorganised; NWFP; Dept of Commerce and Industry; **Coinage and Paper Currency Act 1899**; **Calcutta Municipal Act 1899**; **Imperial Cadet Corps (1901)**; empire "carved in granite" |
| **Minto II** | 1905–10 | **Morley–Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909)**; **separate electorates**; **18 July 1908** – **Gadicherla** and **Bodi Narayana Rao**, first Telugu political prisoners |
| **Hardinge II** | 1910–16 | **Delhi Durbar 1911**; **capital Calcutta → Delhi**; **Delhi Conspiracy bomb 1912**; sent **Gokhale** to South Africa (1912); memoir "**My Indian Years**" |
| **Chelmsford** | 1916–21 | **Rowlatt Act (1919)** – detention without trial; **Jallianwala Bagh**; **GoI Act 1919** (Montagu–Chelmsford; PM **Lloyd George**) |
| **Reading** | 1921–26 | **Chauri Chaura**; **Swaraj Party** |
| **Irwin** | 1926–31 | **Simon Commission**; **Royal Commission on Labour (1929)**; **Civil Disobedience (1930)**; **Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931)** |
| **Willingdon** | 1931–36 | Repression of Civil Disobedience; **Communal Award**; **Poona Pact** |
| **Linlithgow** | 1936–43 | **Second World War**; **August Offer (1940)**; **Cripps Mission**; **Quit India (1942)** |
| **Wavell** | 1943–47 | **Wavell Plan + Simla Conference (1945)** – failed on Jinnah's stand; **Cabinet Mission 1946** (Cripps, A.V. Alexander, Pethick-Lawrence); Interim Government under Nehru (Asaf Ali – Railways); memoir "**The Viceroy's Journal**" |
| **Mountbatten** | 1947–48 | Replaced Wavell after **Attlee's statement (20 Feb 1947)**; **Mountbatten Plan**; **Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947)** abolished the Viceroy and the Secretary of State; held **Jinnah** most responsible for Partition |
- **Order:** Home Rule (1916) → Rowlatt Act (1919) → Simon Commission (1927) → Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931).
- First Afghan War = **Auckland**, not Wavell.
### 4. DELHI DURBARS, SUMMER CAPITAL AND THE SHIFT TO DELHI
| Durbar | Viceroy | Occasion |
|---|---|---|
| **1877** | **Lytton** | **Victoria proclaimed Empress of India (Kaiser-i-Hind)** on **1 Jan 1877**, held while millions starved |
| 1903 | Curzon | Coronation of **Edward VII** |
| **1911** | **Hardinge II** | Coronation of **George V** and Queen Mary – the **only Durbar attended by the monarch** |
- **Summer capital:** **Shimla**, declared by **Sir John Lawrence in 1864** (APPSC 2018 key).
- **Partition of Bengal (Curzon):** announced **20 July 1905**, effective **16 Oct 1905**; the **Vande Mataram** movement forced its revocation.
- **12 Dec 1911 announcements:** Partition **annulled**; **Bihar and Orissa** a separate province (**1912**); **capital → Delhi**.
- **Delhi Conspiracy (1912):** **Rash Behari Bose** and **Sachin Sanyal** threw a bomb at **Hardinge II** in his state entry into Delhi.
!TRAP 1911 Durbar = **George V**, not Edward VII (1903).
### 5. ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA AND THE CENSUS
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1784 | **William Jones** founded the **Asiatic Society** (Calcutta) |
| **1861** | **Canning** made **Alexander Cunningham** Archaeological Surveyor – **ASI set up** |
| 1871 | ASI a **separate department** |
| 1901–02 | **Curzon** appointed **John Marshall**; took charge as Director-General in 1902 |
| **1904** | **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act** – Curzon |
- **Curzon** revived and set up the **Department of Archaeology in organised form** – APPSC G1 key gives **Curzon** for "Department of Archaeology"; if Canning is not an option, Curzon is the answer.
- ASI motto: **"Pratnakirti Apavarini"**; ASI is under the **Ministry of Culture**; **AMASR Act 1958** protects monuments over 100 years old (in news, Jun 2026).
- **Census:** **1872 (Mayo)** non-synchronous → **1881 (Ripon)** first synchronous.
!PAIR 1861 | Canning | ASI set up || 1904 | Curzon | Monuments Act
### 6. ACTS → YEAR → VICEROY
| Act | Year | Viceroy |
|---|---|---|
| **Indian Councils Act** (3 Indians nominated – **Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao**) | **1861** | **Canning** |
| **Royal Titles Act** | **1876** | **Lytton** |
| **Vernacular Press Act**, **Arms Act** | **1878** | **Lytton** |
| **First Factory Act** | **1881** | **Ripon** |
| **Ilbert Bill** | **1883** | **Ripon** |
| Second Factory Act | 1891 | Lansdowne |
| **Indian Councils Act** | **1892** | **Lansdowne** |
| **Indian Universities Act** | **1904** | **Curzon** (not Lansdowne) |
| **Indian Councils Act** (Morley–Minto) | **1909** | **Minto II** |
| **Rowlatt Act** | **1919** | **Chelmsford** |
- **Councils Act 1861:** **portfolio system** strengthened the Viceroy over his council; let the GG issue **Ordinances (max 6 months)**.
- **VPA:** magistrates could make publishers sign a **bond** without prior government permission; **Ripon repealed it (1882)**.
- **First Factory Act (1881):** limited hours of women and children; banned employment of children **below 7**.
- **Ilbert Bill:** showed racial discrimination – Indian judges could not try European British subjects on equal terms.
!KEY Lytton owns three: **Royal Titles, VPA, Arms Act**; Ripon repealed the VPA.
### 7. FINANCIAL DECENTRALISATION
- **Mayo's Resolution, 14 Dec 1870** – start of financial decentralisation.
- Before 1870 provinces sent all revenue to the Imperial Treasury at Calcutta and got grants for each item.
- Provinces got **fixed annual grants** to run **police, jails, education, medical services and roads**, and could raise **local taxes** to balance their budgets.
- **Later steps:** **Lytton (1877)** widened the provincial heads; **Ripon (1882)** divided revenue heads into **imperial, provincial and divided**.
