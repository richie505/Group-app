## U0623 | INDIA – PHYSIOGRAPHIC DIVISION: THE HIMALAYAS (RANGES, REGIONAL DIVISIONS, PASSES)
### 1. DIMENSIONS, ORIGIN AND STRUCTURE
#### 1.1 Dimensions
| Dimension | Figure |
|---|---|
| Length | **2,414 km** – **Nanga Parbat (west)** to **Namcha Barwa (east)** (about 2,400 km) |
| Width | **500 km in Kashmir** to **200 km in Arunachal** |
| Depth | 240–320 km |
| Area | About **5 lakh sq km** (not 15 lakh) |
#### 1.2 Origin
- Raised from **Tethys** sediments during the **Tertiary** (Eocene to Pliocene, ~60–7 million years ago).
- **Indian plate** collided with **Eurasia** about **4–5 crore years** ago; the Himalayas are **still rising**.
- **Trans-Himalayas** (Tibetan Himalaya) were formed **before** the Himalayas.
#### 1.3 Structure (APPSC Group 1 2024: statements I, II, III true)
- **Wider in Kashmir**, narrower in the North-East.
- Folds of the **Himadri are asymmetrical**; core of **granite**.
- **Steep slope faces the plains** of India; **gentle slope faces Tibet**.
- **Significance**: India's northern boundary and climatic barrier; Shiwalik debris built the **Indo-Gangetic Plain**.
- Relief share: **plains** form the largest part of India's area – about **43%** (APPSC 2024).
!TRAP The Himalayas cover about 5 lakh sq km, not 15 lakh.
### 2. CLASSIFICATIONS AND COUNTS
| Classification | Count | Names |
|---|---|---|
| Northern mountains | **3** | Himalayas, Trans-Himalayas, Purvanchal |
| Longitudinal parallel ranges | **3** | **Himadri** (Greater), **Himachal** (Lesser), **Shiwalik** (Outer) |
| Regional (west to east, Burrard) | **4** | Punjab, Kumaon, Nepal, Assam Himalaya |
### 3. PARALLEL RANGES – NORTH TO SOUTH
#### 3.1 Sequence and Features
| N → S | Range | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Karakoram** | Trans-Himalaya; **K2**; Siachen, Rimo, Baltoro glaciers |
| 2 | **Ladakh** | South of Karakoram; continues into Tibet as the **Kailash Range** (not the Zaskar) |
| 3 | **Zaskar** | Trans-Himalaya; Kashmir Valley separates Pir Panjal from it |
| 4 | **Himadri (Greater)** | **Northernmost** of the three, **oldest, loftiest, most continuous**; average **6,000 m** |
| 5 | **Himachal (Lesser / Middle)** | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mussoorie, Mahabharat (Nepal); average width 50 km, 3,700–4,500 m; hill stations Shimla, Nainital, Mussoorie |
| 6 | **Shiwalik (Outer)** | **Youngest, narrowest, outermost**; foothills |
- Himadri: highest point **Everest** (~8,850 m).
- **Pir Panjal**: Lesser Himalaya; about **300 km**, **Jhelum–Beas** sector; runs south-east to north-west through HP and J&K.
- **Mahabharat Range**: Lesser Himalaya in central Nepal, south of the main range.
- **Ladakh Plateau** (Trans-Himalaya): **highest plateau in India**; **Aksai Chin** (~38,000 sq km, Chinese-occupied Indian territory) is part of it.
- Trans-Himalaya is also called the **Tibet Himalaya** (most parts in Tibet).
#### 3.2 Shiwalik
- Formed in the **Cenozoic** (Pliocene, about 2–5 million years ago) from **loose river sediments**; simple **Jura-type** folds.
- Width **10–50 km**; height **900–1,100 m**.
- Shiwalik Hills are part of the Himalaya (outermost range).
#### 3.3 Comparison of the Three Parallel Ranges
| Feature | Himadri (Greater) | Himachal (Lesser / Middle) | Shiwalik (Outer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position | Northernmost | Middle | Southernmost, outermost |
| Age | Oldest | Intermediate | Youngest (Pliocene) |
| Height | Average 6,000 m; loftiest peaks | 3,700–4,500 m | 900–1,100 m |
| Width | Most continuous range | Average 50 km | 10–50 km, narrowest |
| Composition | Granite core; asymmetrical folds | Folded ranges, valleys | Loose river sediments |
| Examples | Everest, Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mussoorie, Mahabharat | Foothills; duns behind them |
| Features | Glaciers (Gangotri) | Hill stations Shimla, Nainital, Mussoorie | Debris built the plains |
!TRAP APPSC 2025: the northernmost of the three parallel ranges is the Himadri, not the Shiwalik.
#### 3.4 Sequence Questions
- N → S: **Karakoram, Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik**; also Karakoram, Ladakh, Zanskar, Shiwalik.
- S → N (west): **Shiwalik – Lesser Himalaya – Great Himalaya**; Lesser Himalaya lies between them.
- Northward: **Pir Panjal, Zanskar, Ladakh, Karakoram** (Pir Panjal Middle Himalaya; rest Trans-Himalaya).
- S → N: **Dhauladhar, Zanskar, Ladakh, Karakoram**.
- Hills S → N: **Satmala** (Maharashtra), **Kaimur** (MP–UP–Bihar), **Naga** (Indo-Myanmar), **Pir Panjal** (J&K–HP).
### 4. VALLEYS, DUNS AND KAREWAS
| Feature | Location |
|---|---|
| **Kashmir Valley** | In the Himachal; between Great Himalaya (NE) and Pir Panjal (SW); mainly **tectonic** |
| **Kullu Valley** (HP) | Between **Dhauladhar and Pir Panjal** |
| **Duns** | Longitudinal valleys between the **Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik** (called **duars** in the east) |
| Examples of duns | **Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun, Nalagarh Dun** |
| **Karewa** | Kashmir Himalaya; plateau-like lake terraces above the Jhelum plains; locally **Vuhr**; soils used for **Zafran (saffron)** |
### 5. REGIONAL DIVISIONS (BURRARD)
| Division | Between rivers | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Punjab (Kashmir/Himachal) Himalaya** | **Indus – Sutlej** | Nanga Parbat, Rakaposhi, Haramosh; mainly sedimentary rocks |
| **Kumaon Himalaya** | **Sutlej – Kali** | Uttarakhand; Nanda Devi, Trisul, Panchchuli; Nainital, Bhimtal lakes |
| **Nepal Himalaya** | **Kali – Teesta** (APPSC 2025) | Everest, Kanchenjunga, Dhaulagiri |
| **Assam Himalaya** | **Teesta – Dihang** (Brahmaputra) | Ends at Namcha Barwa knee-bend |
- The Himalaya bends sharply at the **Dihang gorge** near Namcha Barwa and turns south as **Purvanchal**.

- **K2** is often given as the **highest peak of India** (claimed territory); Kanchenjunga is the highest under Indian control.
- **Nanda Devi** = **fourth highest** in India (after K2, Kanchenjunga, Nanga Parbat); keys gave Kumaon Himalaya (UPSC 2003) and Garhwal Himalaya (IAS 2003).
- Descending: **Everest, Godwin Austen, Kanchenjunga**.
!TRAP Nanda Devi and Trisul belong to the Greater Himalaya, not the Middle Himalaya.
#### 6.2 East-to-West Orders
- **Namcha Barwa** (Tibet), **Kanchenjunga** (Sikkim/Nepal), **Everest** (Nepal–China), **Nanda Devi** (Uttarakhand).
- Kanchenjunga, Everest, Annapurna, Dhaulagiri.
- Garo Hills, Nanda Devi, Kamet, K2.

- **Avalanche-prone**: Zoji La, Khardung La, Rohtang, Mana.
- West → east: **Banihal, Mana, Nathu La, Bum La**.
- Among options asked, the key gave **Zoji La** as the highest pass.
- **Nelang Valley**: Uttarakhand (Gangotri NP, Uttarkashi), near China border; reopened to tourists in **2015**.
### 9. PURVANCHAL AND NORTH-EASTERN HILLS
- Beyond the Dihang gorge the Himalaya turns south as **Purvanchal** (500–3,000 m).
- **Garo-Khasi-Jaintia** and **Naga** hills run **east–west**; **Mizo** and Rakhine hills run **north–south**.
| Hills | State |
|---|---|
| **Patkai Bum** | Along India–Myanmar border (NE–SW); key: **Arunachal Pradesh** |
| **Naga Hills** | Nagaland |
| Manipur Hills | Manipur (Loktak) |
| **Barail** | Assam – links Naga and Mizo hills; highest range of Assam |
| **Mizo (Lushai) Hills** | **Mizoram** (and Tripura) |
| **Garo, Khasi, Jaintia** | **Meghalaya** (Shillong Plateau); Nokrek Peak in Garo |
| Miri Hills | Arunachal Pradesh |
| Chin Range | Reaches Manipur |
| **Arakan Yoma** | Western Myanmar; Himalayan extension; hills Naga, Chin, Lushai, Patkoi; highest **Natma Taung (Mt. Victoria)** |
- **Patkai hills** touch Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland – **not Tripura**.
- **Molasse Basin**: **Mizoram** – soft, unconsolidated deposits (APPSC 2025).
- **Shillong**: Khasi Hills; "**Scotland of the East**".
- Three ranges on the India–Myanmar boundary: key answer "None of the above" (of the options given).
