## U0300 | VETO POWERS, ORDINANCE-MAKING POWER (ART. 123) AND PARDONING POWER (ART. 72)
### 1. VETO POWERS – TYPES (ARTICLE 111)
| Veto | Meaning | Applies to | Indian President? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Absolute** | Withholds assent; Bill ends | Private members' Bills; Government Bills when Cabinet resigns before assent | Yes |
| **Suspensive** | Returns **once** for reconsideration; must assent if re-passed (with or without amendment) | **Ordinary Bills only** | Yes – **not Money Bills** |
| **Pocket** | Neither assents, rejects nor returns – keeps the Bill pending | Ordinary Bills | Yes |
| **Qualified** | Overridable only by a higher (2/3) majority | US practice | **No** |
- **Art. 111** prescribes **no time limit** for the President.
!TRAP The **10-day rule** (Bill becomes law if not decided) is a **US** feature, not Indian.
### 2. VETO BY TYPE OF BILL
| Bill | Union legislation | State legislation (reserved) |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary** | Ratify, reject or **return once** | Ratify, reject or return |
| **Money** | Ratify or reject; **cannot return** | Ratify or reject; cannot return |
| **Constitutional Amendment** | **Must assent** (**24th Amendment, 1971**); cannot reject or return | Not applicable |
- Money Bills are usually brought with the President's **prior recommendation**, so he usually assents.
- The one-time return power can apply to **Financial Bills of Categories I and II**.
!KEY Money Bill = cannot return | Constitutional Amendment Bill = must assent | Ordinary Bill = return once.
### 3. VETOES ACTUALLY USED
| Veto | President | Bill |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute** | **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** (on Council of Ministers' advice) | **PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954** |
| **Pocket** | **Giani Zail Singh** | **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986** |
- Pocket veto = taking no action on a Bill; used **only once** at the Union level.
- **2002**: ordinance on **electoral reforms** returned by the President; the Union Cabinet sent it back unchanged → **assented** under Art. 123.
### 4. STATE BILLS RESERVED FOR THE PRESIDENT (ARTS. 200, 201)
- **Art. 200**: Governor may assent, withhold, return (non-money Bill) or **reserve** a Bill for the President.
- **Art. 201**: for a reserved Bill the President **declares assent or withholds assent**; for a non-money Bill he may direct its return for reconsideration.
- No time limit is written in Art. 200 or 201.
- **Governor has no pocket veto**.
- **8 April 2025 (State of TN v Governor)**: indefinite inaction under Art. 200 held justiciable; ten TN Bills deemed assented.
- **Nov 2025 (Presidential reference, Art. 143, five judges)**: courts **cannot fix timelines** for the Governor or President; **no deemed assent**; powers cannot be used arbitrarily.
### 5. ORDINANCE – BASIS AND CONDITIONS
- President: **Art. 123** (Part V); Governor: **Art. 213** (Part VI).
- Nature: a **legislative power of the Executive** (President is part of Parliament) – not an executive or administrative function.
- When: only when **either or both Houses are not in session**; **none** while both Houses are sitting.
- Satisfaction: circumstances need **immediate action**; issued on **ministerial advice**.
- Force: **same force, effect and limits as an Act** of Parliament / State Legislature.
- Origin: relic of the **Government of India Act, 1935** (Governor-General's ordinance power).
### 6. ORDINANCE – CAN vs CANNOT
| Can | Cannot |
|---|---|
| Amend or repeal ordinary Central Acts | **Amend the Constitution (Art. 368)** |
| Operate **retrospectively** | Violate FRs – it is "law" under **Art. 13(2)** |
| Introduce or amend **tax** provisions | Go beyond Parliament's legislative competence |
| Be **withdrawn** any time | – |
- Scope of an ordinance = scope of Parliament's ordinary legislative power.
!TRAP "The President cannot withdraw or repeal an ordinance once issued" = FALSE.
### 7. LIFE OF AN ORDINANCE
1. Promulgated during recess.
2. **Laid** before the House(s) on reassembly.
3. **Lapses six weeks after reassembly** unless approved; Houses meeting on different dates → counted from the **later date**.
4. Ends earlier if both Houses **disapprove** by resolution, or if **withdrawn**.
5. **Maximum life = six months + six weeks** – maximum gap between sessions is six months (**Art. 85** Parliament; **Art. 174** States) + six-week window.
!NUM 6 weeks after reassembly | 6 months maximum gap | 6 months + 6 weeks maximum life
- Linked time limit: **Art. 101(4)** – MP's unexcused absence for **60 days** can vacate the seat; periods of **prorogation** and **adjournments of more than four consecutive days** are excluded.
### 8. SATISFACTION AND JUDICIAL REVIEW
| Year | Event | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1975 | **38th Amendment** | Satisfaction under **Arts. 123, 213, 352** made final / non-justiciable |
| 1978 | **44th Amendment** | Reversed the 38th |
| 1987 | **D.C. Wadhwa v State of Bihar** | Routine **re-promulgation** without placing before the legislature = **fraud on the Constitution** |
| 2017 | **Krishna Kumar Singh v State of Bihar** | **Seven judges**; re-promulgation unconstitutional; **laying is mandatory**; satisfaction **justiciable** |
- Ordinances are **subject to judicial review**.
- Latest: **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** (gazette 16 May 2026; judges 33 → 37 excluding CJI) revived debate on ordinance misuse and executive overreach.
- Criticism: frequent ordinances **bypass parliamentary deliberation**.
- AP angle: AP used a State ordinance (**Art. 213**) on **organ-transplant committees**.
### 9. PARDONING POWER (ARTICLE 72)
#### 9.1 Forms
| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pardon** | Removes **both sentence and conviction**; as if the offence was never committed |
| **Commutation** | Lighter form of punishment (death → rigorous life imprisonment) |
| **Remission** | Shorter period, **same character** (2 years RI → 1 year RI) |
| **Respite** | Lesser sentence for special circumstances – **pregnancy, mental or physical disability** |
| **Reprieve** | **Temporary stay** of execution, especially death, to seek pardon |
!TRAP Commutation = changes the **form**; Remission = reduces the **period**.
#### 9.2 Scope and nature
- Art. 72 covers: (a) sentence by **court-martial**; (b) offence against a **Union law**; (c) **death sentence**.
- Not State-law sentences in every case; amnesty is outside Art. 72.
- A **judicial power** of the President, exercised on the **Council of Ministers' advice**.
### 10. ARTICLE 72 vs ARTICLE 161 AND CASES
| Point | President (Art. 72) | Governor (Art. 161) |
|---|---|---|
| Offences | Against **Union laws** | Against **State laws** |
| Death sentence | **Can pardon** | **Cannot pardon**; can suspend, remit or commute |
| Court-martial | **Yes** | **No** |
| Forms | All five | Same, except as above |
- **Only the President** can pardon a death sentence and a court-martial sentence.
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Maru Ram (1980)** | Power exercised on Council of Ministers' advice |
| **Kehar Singh (1989)** | President may examine evidence afresh; **no right of oral hearing** |
| **Epuru Sudhakar v Govt of AP (2006)** | **Limited judicial review** – mala fide, arbitrary or irrelevant grounds |
### 11. COMPARISON WITH THE USA
| Feature | India | USA |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute veto | Yes | **No** |
| Qualified veto | No | **Yes** – overridden by 2/3 of Congress |
| Pocket veto | Yes – no time limit | Yes – **10-day rule** |
