## U0775 | BIODIVERSITY HOTSPOTS IN INDIA, ENDEMISM AND BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY ACT 2002
### 1. BIODIVERSITY HOTSPOT – CONCEPT AND CRITERIA
- **Definition:** a biogeographic region with a large reservoir of biodiversity that is **threatened with destruction**.
- Basis: **richness, endemism and threat**. **Habitat destruction** is the main cause of biodiversity loss.
- Proposed by **Norman Myers, 1988**; adopted by **Conservation International (CI), 1989**.
#### 1.1 Both Criteria Needed
| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Endemism | At least **1,500 endemic vascular plant** species (over **0.5%** of world total) |
| Threat | Lost at least **70%** of original habitat |
!KEY APPSC 2017: Hotspots are marked by **endemic flowering plants and threat perception**.
#### 1.2 Timeline and Count
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1988 | Myers proposes the concept |
| 1989 | CI adopts it |
| 1999 | Numerical thresholds (published Myers et al., 2000); **25** hotspots |
| 2016 | **36th**: North American Coastal Plain |
- Count grew 25 → 34 → 35 → **36** (present).
- Once **15.7%** of Earth's land; about **86%** of that habitat lost; now only about **2.5%** of land.
- Hold about **50% of vascular plants** (1,50,000 species) and **42% of vertebrates** as endemics.
#### 1.3 Distribution and Types
- Most lie in the **tropics**; South-East Asian islands among the richest; but hotspots are **not only tropical**.
- **Temperate hotspots:** Mediterranean Basin, California Floristic Province, North American Coastal Plain.
- **Hottest hotspots:** **8** with highest endemism under greatest threat; include **Western Ghats-Sri Lanka**.
- **Coldspots:** relatively low diversity but losing habitat fast; still matter for rare species, ecosystem functions and genetic diversity.
### 2. HOTSPOTS vs KBAs vs GLOBAL 200
| Concept | Body | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Biodiversity hotspot | **Conservation International** (Myers) | 1,500+ endemic plants and 70%+ habitat lost; large regions |
| Key Biodiversity Area | **IUCN-led** partnership (global standard, 2016) | Single **sites** important for global persistence of biodiversity |
| Global 200 | **WWF** | Priority ecoregions (terrestrial, freshwater, marine) chosen for representativeness, not only threat |
### 3. INDIA'S FOUR HOTSPOTS
| Hotspot | Indian part |
|---|---|
| **Himalaya** | Eastern Himalaya, North-East (whole chain is the hotspot) |
| **Indo-Burma** | North-East south of the Brahmaputra + **Andaman Islands** |
| **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka** | South-western India |
| **Sundaland** | Only the **Nicobar Islands** |
- Indo-Burma does **not** cover western India.
#### 3.1 Hotspot or NOT
| Region | Global hotspot? |
|---|---|
| Western Ghats, Eastern Himalaya, North-East, Nicobar | Yes |
| **Eastern Ghats** | No |
| Aravallis, **Vindhyas**, Satpuras | No |
| **Thar Desert** | No (APPSC 2024) |
| Sundarbans, Gangetic Plains, Deccan Plateau, Naro Hills | No |
!TRAP APPSC 2018 answer "Eastern Himalayas and Western Ghats" reflects the old two-hotspot count; India now has four. The odd one out is usually a desert or the Eastern Ghats.
#### 3.2 Threats by Hotspot
| Hotspot | Threats |
|---|---|
| Western Ghats | Monocultures, invasive species, tourism, infrastructure (IUCN 2025), hydropower, fires |
| Himalaya | Young fragile geology, landslides, infrastructure |
| Indo-Burma | **Jhum** (shifting cultivation), logging, hunting |
| Sundaland/Nicobar | Invasives, **2004 tsunami** |
### 4. WESTERN GHATS
- Also **Sahyadri**; about **1,600 km** through **6 States**: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu.
- "**Water tower of peninsular India**": feeds Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery. One of the **8 hottest hotspots**.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site 2012**: serial site of **39 component areas in 7 clusters**. IUCN 2025 Outlook: "**significant concern**".
- Western Ghats alone: about **5,000** vascular plants, **1,700 (34%)** endemic, **58 endemic genera**.
#### 4.1 Gadgil vs Kasturirangan
| Panel | Year | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **WGEEP – Madhav Gadgil** (set up by MoEF 2010, report 2011) | 2010 | **Whole Ghats** as ESA: 142 talukas in 44 districts, three ESZs |
| **HLWG – K. Kasturirangan** | 2012 | Set up after State opposition; ESA limited to about **37%** (~60,000 sq km) |
- MoEF&CC issued **draft** ESA notifications across six States; legal force on notification under the **EP Act, 1986**.
- Supreme Court ordered eviction of encroachers in **Agasthyamalai Biosphere Reserve** (Kerala-TN; UNESCO MAB 2016).
### 5. ENDEMIC SPECIES
- **Endemism:** a species occurs naturally in one region only.
| Species | Region |
|---|---|
| **Boswellia ovalifoliolata** | Andhra Pradesh (APPSC 2022) |
| Cycas beddomei, **Golden Gecko** (micro-endemic) | Seshachalam hills, AP |
| **Hangul** (Kashmir stag) | Kashmir, Dachigam NP (APPSC 2018) |
| Asiatic lion | Gir, Gujarat |
| **Lion-tailed macaque** (wanderoo) | Western Ghats upper canopy; TN, Kerala, Karnataka (not AP); diurnal |
| **Nilgiri tahr** | Western Ghats (not Himalaya) |
| Podocarpus (Nageia) wallichianus | Only endemic gymnosperm tree of Western Ghats |
| Nilgiri marten, Malabar spiny dormouse | Western Ghats (Agasthyamalai) |
| Malabar civet | Western Ghats; nocturnal; first reported from Travancore |
| **Nicobar pigeon** | Sundaland |
| **Hoolock gibbon** | Indo-Burma |
| Great Indian Hornbill | Western Ghats; largest hornbill in India; state bird of Arunachal Pradesh and Kerala |
- Golden Mahseer (Tor putitora) is a **fish** of Himalayan streams; Parthenium is an invasive weed from tropical America.
- **AP:** Eastern Ghats (Seshachalam, Nallamala) are rich in endemics (Red Sanders, Golden Gecko) but **not a global hotspot**; three new plant species found there in **May 2026**.
### 6. BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY ACT 2002
- Gives effect to **CBD (1992)**; Presidential assent **5 February 2003**.
| Tier | Body | Role |
|---|---|---|
| National | **NBA, Chennai** (2003) | Foreign/commercial access and **IPR**; advises Centre; civil court powers |
| State | **SBBs**; UT Biodiversity Councils | Commercial use by **Indian** entities |
| Local | **BMCs** | Document biodiversity, prepare PBRs, levy collection fees |
- **Section 37:** Biodiversity Heritage Sites. **Section 38:** threatened species (1,500+ plants declared by States).
#### 6.1 Amendment Act 2023 (in force 1 April 2024)
- Offences **decriminalised** (replaced by penalties).
- **AYUSH** practitioners and codified traditional knowledge exempt from prior intimation to SBBs; cultivated medicinal plants exempt.
- **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024** with statutory National Biodiversity Authority Fund.
### 7. BMCs, PBRs AND HERITAGE SITES
- **PBRs are prepared by BMCs** (APPSC 2019). About **2.62 lakh BMCs** (2024); ~**2.72 lakh PBRs** (Jun 2026).
- BMCs are key to **Nagoya Protocol** objectives and can levy collection fees.
- **BHS:** notified by the **State Government** with local bodies on SBB advice; no change to ownership or livelihood rights; traditional uses continue; may protect crop wild relatives.
- Count: **54** (NBA, March 2026); 45 (Feb 2024) → 48 (Apr 2025).
| BHS | State |
|---|---|
| **Nallur Tamarind Grove** (first, 2007) | Karnataka |
| Ameenpur Lake (first water-body BHS) | Telangana |
| Borjuli wetland (wild rice Oryza rufipogon, Jul 2026) | Assam |
| Kasampatty | Tamil Nadu |
### 8. ACCESS AND BENEFIT SHARING
- Commercial benefits from bioresources shared with providing communities; covers plant, animal, microbial resources and traditional knowledge; **excludes human genetic material**. CBD 1992 → **Nagoya Protocol 2010**.
- **Kani model:** Kani tribe's knowledge of **Arogyapacha** (Trichopus zeylanicus) → **TBGRI** drug **Jeevani** (anti-fatigue).
- ABS raised over **₹266 crore** since 2008: **Red Sanders 45%** (₹120 crore), seed sector 32.3%.
- Repository rule: **25-40%** to repository, **60-75%** to local communities via SBBs/UTBCs.
- **AP:** APSBB vision of **zero biodiversity loss by 2050**; Red Sanders makes AP vulnerable to biopiracy and the largest ABS earner.
