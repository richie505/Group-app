## U0793 | SOLID WASTE, PLASTIC WASTE, E-WASTE, BIOMEDICAL AND CONSTRUCTION WASTE – RULES AND MANAGEMENT
### 1. SOLID WASTE – DEFINITIONS, CATEGORIES AND BIODEGRADABILITY
#### 1.1 Definitions and Categories
- **Solid waste**: thrown-away material in **solid, liquid, semi-solid or containerised gaseous** form.
- **E-waste (ITU)**: discarded electrical and electronic equipment and parts **not meant for reuse**.
- **Biomedical waste**: waste from **diagnosis, treatment or immunisation** of humans or animals (blood, sharps, cultures, expired medicines).
| Category | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Municipal | Household + commercial: organics, plastics, paper, metals |
| Industrial / Hazardous / E-waste / Biomedical | See sections 3, 7, 8, 9 |
| Agricultural | Crop residues, paddy husk, cattle dung (biodegradable; dung → biogas under GOBARdhan) |
| Construction & demolition | Mostly **inert** – concrete, bricks, dirt, rocks, debris |
#### 1.2 Hazardous Waste – Four Criteria
- **Ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity** (NOT biodegradability).
- **Household hazardous waste**: **paints, batteries**, fluorescent lamps, aerosol cans, tyres, pesticides.
- Healthcare waste: **~85% general non-hazardous, ~15% hazardous** (infectious, chemical, radioactive) (WHO).
#### 1.3 Biodegradable vs Non-biodegradable
- **Biodegradable**: broken into simpler, harmless substances by micro-organisms (mainly **bacteria**). **Aerobic** decomposition → **compost**; **anaerobic** → **methane/biogas**.
| Biodegradable | Non-biodegradable |
|---|---|
| **Sewage**, domestic garbage, urine, agricultural residues, cattle dung | **Plastic, DDT, asbestos**, insecticides |
| Food/kitchen waste, **vegetable peels**, fruit waste, **paddy husk**, cow-dung | **Tin/tin scrap**, aluminium foil, metal scrap, **lead** |
| Paper, wood, plant waste, **cellulose, starch, proteins** | **Glass** bottles, plastic cups/bags |
| **Wool** (natural keratin fibre), cotton | **Synthetic rubber/tyres** (natural **rubber** is biodegradable) |
| Polymers: **PHBV, Nylon-2-Nylon-6**, polylactic acid, polyglycolic acid | **Polythene** (polymer of **ethylene, C2H4** – carbon + hydrogen), **PVC** (vinyl chloride), polystyrene, nylon, Teflon |
- Biodegradable polymers are eco-friendly because microbes degrade them.
- Non-biodegradable plastics persist and break into **microplastics**; polythene bags cannot be destroyed.
!TRAP "Which is NOT biodegradable": tin, metal, glass, plastic, PVC, insecticides. "Which IS": wool, peels, sewage, rubber.
### 2. INDIAN MSW – DATA AND RECORDS
- Composition: **~55–60% biodegradable**, 15–20% recyclable, 15–25% hazardous or non-recyclable.
- Generation: **over 62 million tonnes** MSW a year; **~60%** urban.
- SBM-U processing **16% (2014) → 82% (2026)**.
| Record | Fact |
|---|---|
| Highest solid-waste State | **Maharashtra** (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| Mega city with most solid waste per person yearly | **Delhi** |
| Model zero-waste, landfill-free city | **Indore** (MP) |
| Largest Charkha (used plastic) | Noida, eve of Gandhi 150 |
| India's first major nurdle landing | **Kerala**, **MSC Elsa 3** shipwreck (LDPE, HDPE) |
### 3. WASTE HIERARCHY, 3R/5R AND CIRCULAR ECONOMY
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **3R** | Reduce, Reuse, Recycle ("**Regain**" is NOT a 3R – APPSC-GS 2025) |
| Top priority | **Reduction at source** (generating less waste) |
| Reuse | Using the item again in the **same form** |
| Recycling | Converting waste into **other usable materials** (APPSC-GS 2024) |
| 5R | Adds **Refuse** and **Repurpose (or Recover)** |
| Least preferred | **Landfilling** (after reduction, reuse, recycling, recovery) |
| RRR Centre | Permanent in every city under SBM-U 2.0 (Reduce, Reuse, Recover) |
- **Legacy waste**: old MSW in dumpsites; releases methane, pollutes groundwater. **Biomining** sorts it into recyclables, RDF, bio-soil and inert fill → supports **circular economy**.
#### 3.1 Industrial By-products → Reuse
| Waste | Source industry | Reuse |
|---|---|---|
| **Fly ash** | Coal thermal plants | Bricks, cement, roads – mandatory **within 300 km**; partly replaces **Portland cement** (UPSC 2015) |
| **Steel slag** | Steel plants | Road base, agricultural soil, cement (all three – UPSC 2020); **Surat** steel-slag road |
| **Red mud** | **Alumina refining** | Trials in cement/bricks |
| **Phosphogypsum** | **Phosphoric acid / phosphatic fertiliser** plants | Gypsum substitute, soil conditioner |
| Chromium sludge | Tanneries | – |
- **Fly Ash Utilisation Notification** (MoEFCC) sets the 300 km rule.
### 4. DISPOSAL AND TREATMENT METHODS
| Method | Process | Suited to |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanitary landfill** | Compacted, covered, lined cells | Only residual inert waste; **last resort**; not for unsegregated biodegradable waste |
| **Composting (aerobic)** – **Indore method** | Turned at intervals; microbes oxidise organics | Wet/organic waste |
| **Anaerobic pit** – **Bangalore method** | Alternate layers of MSW and night soil; **4–6 months** | Organic waste |
| **Vermicomposting** | **Earthworms** (biological method) | Household organic waste |
| **Incineration** | Controlled burning **in oxygen** at high heat; may give steam for power; cuts volume **~90%** | **Hazardous, biomedical** |
| **Pyrolysis** | Heating **without oxygen** → gas, oil, char | Mixed waste; costly |
| **Plasma gasification** | Electricity + high heat, no combustion → syngas; disposes all organic wastes |– |
| **RDF** | Shredded, dehydrated high-calorific MSW | Fuel in **cement kilns** and thermal power plants |
- **Compost**: earthworms and microbes (bacteria, fungi) **speed up** decomposition in pits. **Vermiculture** = science of raising earthworms.
- **Pyrolysis and plasma gasification** = waste-to-energy; compost and vermicompost give **manure, not energy**.
- Urban India uses composting, incineration and landfilling – **"all the above"** (APPSC-G2 2025); **uncontrolled dumping** is not workable.
- **APPCB, March 2025**: mandated **RDF in cement kilns** to cut coal use.
#### 4.1 Waste → Method (APPSC-G2 2023 match)
| Waste | Method |
|---|---|
| Organic | Composting |
| E-waste | Recycling |
| Hazardous / biomedical | Incineration (biomedical at CBMWTF) |
| Broken glass | Landfilling |
| Non-recyclable high-calorific dry waste | RDF / co-processing in cement kilns |
#### 4.2 Other Pollution Solutions
| Solution | Addresses |
|---|---|
| **Oilzapper** | Bacteria mix for oil spills and oily sludge |
| **Microbial fuel cell** | Wastewater + electricity |
| UV purification | Kills microorganisms in drinking water |
| Hydrogen fuel cells | Clean fuel; emit only water vapour |
| **EPR** | Producer's end-of-life duty |
### 5. SOLID WASTE RULES AND COLLECTION CHAIN
| Rules | Key points |
|---|---|
| **MSW (M&H) Rules 2000** | Primary goal: **promoting segregation** (APPSC-G2 2025); ULBs collect, segregate, store, transport, process, dispose |
| **SWM Rules 2016** (from 8 Apr 2016) | Beyond municipal areas; **3 streams** – biodegradable, dry, domestic hazardous; EPR for producers/brand owners; user fees, spot fines; no untreated waste to landfill; **criteria for landfill and waste-processing sites** |
| **SWM Rules 2026** (under EPA 1986; in force **1 Apr 2026**) | **4 streams: wet, dry, sanitary, special care**; landfills only for inert residue; higher landfill fee for unsegregated waste; legacy dumps mapped with time-bound biomining and quarterly reports |
- 2016: used diapers/napkins wrapped into the **dry** bin; 2026: separate **sanitary** stream. **Special care = domestic hazardous** (batteries, chemicals, paint cans, bulbs). Wet = food scraps, peels, garden waste; paper, plastics, metals, glass → dry.
- **Bulk Waste Generators**: treat wet waste on site or get an **EBWGR** certificate.
- **Environmental Compensation** follows the **polluter-pays** principle (not precautionary).
- Draft "SWM Rules 2024" proposed 1 Oct 2025 – not the notified rules.
- **Collection chain**: door-to-door → segregated transport → transfer station → **Material Recovery Facility** → processing/recycling → residual landfill.
- **Supreme Court**: clean environment part of **Art. 21** (Feb 2026); **18 Aug 2026** (Justice S.V.N. Bhatti) – **generators**, not only sanitary workers, answerable for waste at the doorstep.
!PAIR Segregation rules: 2000 | segregation goal | – || 2016 | 3 streams | 2026 = 4 streams
### 6. PLASTIC WASTE MANAGEMENT
#### 6.1 Rules Timeline and Thickness
| Year | Instrument | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | Plastic Waste (M&H) Rules | Carry bags **40 microns** |
| 2016 | PWM Rules 2016 | **50 microns**; PWM fee; promoted **plastic roads** |
| **12 Aug 2021** | PWM Amendment Rules 2021 | **75 microns from 30 Sep 2021**; **120 microns from 31 Dec 2022**; phase out identified SUP **by 2022** (APPSC-GS 2021) |
| **1 Jul 2022** | SUP ban + **EPR Guidelines** on plastic packaging | Both effective July 2022 |
| 2024 | PWM Amendment Rules 2024 | Defined **biodegradable plastics**; makers need **CPCB** certification |
| 31 Mar 2026 | PWM Amendment Rules 2026 | Rigid, flexible, multi-layered classes; rigid recycled content **30% → 60% by 2028-29**; unmet targets carried forward up to **3 years** (not five); WtE, co-processing, waste-to-oil allowed |
- Compostable plastics: **IS/ISO 17088**.
#### 6.2 Single-use Plastic Ban – 1 July 2022
- **19 identified items** banned for manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale and use: **ear buds with plastic sticks, balloon sticks, plastic flags, candy and ice-cream sticks, thermocol for decoration**, plates, cups, cutlery, straws, cigarette packets.
- NOT banned: **HDPE milk bottles**. Ban covers only **~11%** of plastic waste (CSE).
- Enforcement: **SPCBs/PCCs + local bodies**, National Level Taskforce, State Special Task Forces.
#### 6.3 Extended Producer Responsibility
- EPR: producer's duty for environmentally sound management of a product till **end of life**.
- **First EPR in Indian law: E-waste (Management and Handling) Rules 2011** (not Hazardous Wastes Rules 2016).
- **PIBOs** = Producers, Importers and Brand Owners (packaging outside the SUP ban).
| Category | Example | Recycled content 2025-26 → later |
|---|---|---|
| I – Rigid | PET, HDPE bottles | 30% → 60% |
| II – Flexible single-layer | Carry bags, chips packets | 10% → 20% |
| III – Multi-layered | Multi-layer packs | 5% → 10% |
- **CPCB EPR portal**: recyclers generate tradable EPR certificates.
#### 6.4 Resin Codes, Additives, Microplastics
| Code | Plastic |
|---|---|
| 1 | **PET** (bottles) |
| 2 | **HDPE** |
| 3 | **PVC** |
| 4 | LDPE |
| 5 | **PP** |
| 6 | PS |
| 7 | Others incl. **polycarbonate** (releases **BPA**, endocrine disruptor) |
- **Bisphenol A**: building block of polycarbonate (sunglasses, reusable and baby bottles, food packaging) and **epoxy resins**.
- Contain plastic: **cigarette butts (cellulose acetate filter), eyeglass lenses, car tyres** – all three. Synthetic rubber = tyres, not water bottles.
- **Microplastics: up to 5 mm** (APPSC-GS 2023); **nurdles** = primary pellets **1–5 mm**.
- **Microbeads** in cosmetics banned under PWM Rules; **FSSAI** study found microplastics in all tested sugar and salt samples.
#### 6.5 Global Action and Plastics Treaty
- **Bangladesh** – first to ban thin plastic bags (**2002**); **New Zealand** – thin produce bags in supermarkets (**July 2023**).
- China's **National Sword Policy (2018)** and Thailand's 2025 measure limit plastic waste imports.
- **UNEA-5.2 (Nairobi, March 2022)**: "End Plastic Pollution" → **INC**. Last full session **Geneva (INC-5.2, Aug 2025) – no agreement**. Disputes: **production caps, chemicals of concern, finance**.
- **INC-5.3 (Geneva, Feb 2026)**: organisational only; new chair **Julio Cordano** elected.
### 7. E-WASTE
#### 7.1 Rules
- **E-waste (Management) Rules 2022**: notified **Nov 2022**, in force **1 April 2023**; lineage 2011 → 2016 → 2022; **106 EEE items incl. solar PV** waste.
- Only **registered** producers, manufacturers, refurbishers, recyclers may deal; e-waste not to be mixed with other waste.
- EPR: annual targets on past sales; register on **CPCB portal**; buy **digital EPR certificates** from registered recyclers.
- **Battery Waste Management Rules 2022**: replaced 2001 rules; EV, portable, automotive, industrial batteries under EPR.
#### 7.2 Magnitude
- India = **3rd-largest** e-waste generator (after **China, USA**); **~2.2 million tonnes (2025)**.
- **3.54 lakh tonnes** = collected and processed in **FY 2021** (MeitY; APPSC-GS 2021).
- **Global E-waste Monitor**: **ITU and UNITAR**. GEM 2024: **62 million tonnes** in 2022, only **22.3%** formally recycled; **82 Mt by 2030**.
#### 7.3 Recycling Chain and Hazards
- Chain: consumer → collection centre / producer take-back → **authorised dismantler** → **authorised recycler** → recovered materials. Citizens: give devices to authorised recycler after **wiping data** (APPSC-GS 2025).
- **78%** processed informally. Informal hubs: **Seelampur (Delhi), Moradabad (UP)**.
- **R2 (Responsible Recycling) Code**: environmentally responsible practices in electronics recycling; promotes reuse (UPSC 2021) – not wastewater.
- Old computers release **beryllium, cadmium, chromium, mercury, lead**, PCBs, PBBs – NOT **heptachlor** (insecticide) or **plutonium** (UPSC 2013).
| Constituent | Harm |
|---|---|
| Mercury | Brain, kidney; bioaccumulates in fish |
| **Cadmium** | Kidney and bone |
| **PBBs** (brominated flame retardants) | Persistent; endocrine effects |
- Improper disposal releases toxics into soil and water → harms ecosystems and biodiversity (APPSC-GS 2025). "Invisible e-waste": vapes, USB cables, LED accessories.
### 8. BIOMEDICAL WASTE
- **BMW Management Rules 2016** replaced **1998 Rules (10 categories)**; amended 2018, 2019; **4 colour categories**; **barcoding and GPS tracking**.
| Colour | Waste | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Yellow** | Anatomical, soiled, **expired medicines**, chemical | Incineration / plasma pyrolysis / deep burial |
| **Red** | Contaminated **recyclable plastics** (tubes, IV sets, catheters, syringes without needles) | Autoclave/microwave + shredding |
| **White (translucent)** | **Sharps** – needles, blades | Autoclave, shredding, encapsulation |
| **Blue** | **Glassware**, metallic implants | Disinfection, recycling |
- Why treat before disposal: **prevent infection spread and keep people healthy** (APPSC-GS 2025); stop syringe reuse.
- **COVID-19**: CPCB guidelines (2020) + **COVID19BWM** tracking app.
!TRAP IV sets and catheters go to Red, not Yellow; "10 categories" is the 1998 Rules.
### 9. C&D AND HAZARDOUS WASTE; CONVENTIONS
#### 9.1 Construction and Demolition Waste
- ~**12 million tonnes** a year; first recycling plant **Burari (Delhi)**.
- **Environment (C&D Waste Management) Rules 2025**: effective **1 April 2026**; **EPR** framework (MoEFCC) on producers with projects of **20,000 sq m+**; recycling targets **25% (2025-26) → 100% (2028-29)**; recycled-material use **5% (2026-27) → 25% (2030-31)**.
#### 9.2 Hazardous Waste Law
| Law | Provision |
|---|---|
| **EPA 1986** | Passed after **Bhopal** (1984); umbrella for hazardous waste, CRZ, EIA, Contaminated Sites Rules |
| **MSIHC Rules 1989** | Manufacture, storage, import of hazardous chemicals |
| **CAEPPR** | Chemical accident crisis groups |
| **Public Liability Insurance Act 1991** | **No-fault** insurance, quick relief |
| **HOWM Rules 2016** | Hierarchy: **prevention** > minimisation > reuse > recycling > recovery > **co-processing** > safe disposal; imports per Basel, PIC |
- Disposal: **secure landfills**; landfill = last resort.
| Disaster | Chemical |
|---|---|
| **Bhopal**, 2-3 Dec 1984, Union Carbide | **Methyl isocyanate** |
| **LG Polymers, Visakhapatnam, May 2020** | **Styrene** vapour |
#### 9.3 Conventions
| Convention | Subject / India |
|---|---|
| **Basel (1989; in force 1992)** | Transboundary movement of hazardous waste; India ratified June 1992 |
| **Rotterdam (2004)** | **Prior Informed Consent** for hazardous chemicals, pesticides in trade; India acceded 2005 |
| **Stockholm (2001)** | **POPs**; now 34 chemicals incl. **PFAS**, chlorpyrifos |
| **Minamata (2013)** | **Mercury**; dental amalgam phase-out by **2034**; ends mercury lamps |
| Ramsar / ITPGRFA / Kigali | Wetlands / plant genetic resources / HFCs |
| Rio: UNCCD, CBD, UNFCCC | Desertification, biodiversity, climate |
- **Transboundary movement**: moving hazardous waste for reprocessing or storage, often to developing countries (APPSC-G2 2025).
- Mercury sources: **pesticides, dental amalgam, fluorescent lamps, coal plants** – all four.
- **PFAS** ("forever chemicals"): in water, food packaging; persist; accumulate in animals (UPSC 2024 – all correct).
