## U0657 | LINGUISTIC GROUPS OF INDIA – LANGUAGE FAMILIES, EIGHTH SCHEDULE LANGUAGES, BILINGUALISM
### 1. LANGUAGE FAMILIES OF INDIA
#### 1.1 Four Main Families
| Family | Share (Census 2011) | Languages | Area |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indo-Aryan** (Indo-European) | **78.05%** (older ~73%) | Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Odia, Assamese, Gujarati, Punjabi, Sindhi, **Kachchhi**, **Konkani**, **Bhili**, **Halbi** (Bastar), **Gor Boli/Lambadi**; **Dardic: Kashmiri** | North, west, east; largest by area and speakers |
| **Dravidian** | **19.64%** (older ~20%) | Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam; **Kui/Kuvi, Gondi, Kurukh** | Mainly South; central pockets |
| **Austro-Asiatic** (Austric) | **1.11%** (older ~1.38%) | **Munda**: Santhali, Mundari, Ho, Savara; **Mon-Khmer**: Khasi, Nicobarese | Jharkhand-Odisha-WB tribal belt, Meghalaya, Nicobar |
| **Sino-Tibetan** (Tibeto-Burman) | **1.01%** (older ~0.85%) | **Bodo**, Manipuri, Garo, Naga languages, Ladakhi, Bhotia | NE, Sikkim, Himalaya, Ladakh |
- Dravidian branches: Southern – Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam; **South-Central – Telugu**; Central/North – Kui, Gondi, Kurukh.
- Most tribal languages of **central and eastern India are Austric** (APPSC 2007).
- **Khasi (Mon-Khmer)** – evidence that the Meghalaya plateau was once part of Peninsular India.
- **Romani** (Roma/Gypsies) is related to Indian Indo-Aryan languages.
!TRAP APPSC G1 2024: "Dardi is Sino-Tibetan" was the FALSE statement – Dardic is **Indo-Aryan**.
!PAIR Khasi | Austro-Asiatic | Mon-Khmer || Garo | Sino-Tibetan | Bodo group

### 5. EIGHTH SCHEDULE
| Year / Amendment | Added | Total |
|---|---|---|
| 1950 | Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu | **14** |
| **21st, 1967** | **Sindhi** | 15 |
| **71st, 1992** | **Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali** | 18 |
| **92nd, 2003** | **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** | **22** |
| 96th, 2011 | "Oriya" → **Odia** (no new language) | 22 |
- Linked to **Articles 344(1) and 351**; 22 cover **96.71%** of mother tongues (18 covered 91% before 2003).
- **No fixed criteria**: **Ashok Pahwa Committee (1996)**, **Sitakant Mohapatra Committee (2003)**.
- Benefits: interpretation in Parliament, authoritative Constitution translation (**Art. 394A**), central funds.
- **Not included**: **English (never)**, **Bhojpuri**, Kokborok, Khasi, Garo, **Gor Boli** (demand continues, 23 Jul 2026; Banjara/Sugali of AP).
!KEY UPPSC 2025: Bhojpuri is NOT in the Eighth Schedule; Nepali, Dogri, Bodo are.

### 7. OFFICIAL LANGUAGE – ARTICLES 343–351
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| 343 | Union language: **Hindi in Devanagari**, international numerals |
| 344 | President's **Official Language Commission** and Parliamentary Committee |
| 345 | State Legislature adopts State official language(s) |
| 346 | Language between States / State and Union |
| 348 | English in SC, HCs and Acts |
| 350 / **350A** | Representations in any language / **mother-tongue instruction at primary stage** |
| **350B** | **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities** (1957, Allahabad) |

- **Official Languages Act, 1963**: English continues with Hindi after 1965.

### 9. LANGUAGES OF ANDHRA PRADESH
- **AP Official Language Act, 1966**: **Telugu** official; **2022 Amendment** made **Urdu second official language statewide** (reaffirmed 17 Mar 2026). AP official = **Telugu and Urdu** (2019 PYQ).
- Telugu: Dravidian, scheduled, **classical since 2008**; "**Italian of the East**".
- **CESCT** (CIIL wing) moved to **Nellore**.
- **Telugu Language Day – 29 August**, birth anniversary of **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy** (Vyavaharika Telugu movement; script and dictionaries for Savara).
- Earliest Telugu word "**Nagabu**" (Amaravati); first full Telugu inscription **Kalamalla (575 CE, Renati Cholas, Kadapa)**.
- Dialects: **Coastal (Krishna-Godavari)** = base of standard Telugu; **Kalinga/Uttarandhra** = Srikakulam-Vizianagaram-Visakhapatnam.
- Agency languages: **Savara, Koya, Kui, Gadaba**; Urdu – **Deccani**, from Qutb Shahis (Persian official, Telugu spoken).
### 10. BILINGUALISM AND ENDANGERED LANGUAGES
- Census 2011: **~26% bilingual, ~7% trilingual**.
- **UNESCO Atlas: ~197** endangered Indian languages – most of any country.
- Threats: globalisation, English, migration, nuclear families, no mother-tongue schooling, **lack of scripts** (having a script is not a threat).
| Scheme | Year | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **SPPEL** | **2013** | Education Ministry; **CIIL Mysuru**; languages under **10,000** speakers |
| **Bhasha Sangam** | 2018 | Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat; sentences in all 22 |
| **Adi Vaani** | 2026 | First AI translation platform for tribal languages |
- **Odisha**: bilingual tribal dictionary series (APPSC 2019); 21 tribal languages in 1,483 schools.
- Centre's **Devanagari** push for script-less NE tribal languages opposed by Tripura's CPI(M).
