## U0359 | PUBLIC POLICY – MEANING, TYPES, POLICY CYCLE (FORMULATION, IMPLEMENTATION, EVALUATION), POLICY INSTRUMENTS
### 1. MEANING OF PUBLIC POLICY
- **Public policy** – planned state action to tackle public issues, reach development goals and regulate behaviour; the **bridge between political intent and administrative action**.
- Indian bases: Constitution, laws, **judicial interpretation** (e.g. **Right to Food** read into **Article 21**), NITI Aayog agendas, Budget, Economic Survey.
#### 1.1 Definitions → thinker
| Thinker | Definition / idea |
|---|---|
| **Thomas Dye** | "**Whatever governments choose to do or not to do**" – inaction is policy too |
| **David Easton** | "**Authoritative allocation of values** for the whole society"; **systems model** |
| **Harold Lasswell** | "Projected programme of goals, values and practices"; founder of **policy sciences** |
| **James Anderson** | "Purposive course of action" by actors dealing with a problem |
!TRAP Easton gives both a definition and the systems model; Lasswell = policy sciences; Dye = "do or not to do".
### 2. TYPES OF POLICY – LOWI CLASSIFICATION
| Lowi type | Does | Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| **Distributive** | Benefits to specific groups; **costs borne by all** | **PM-KISAN**, farm input subsidies |
| **Redistributive** | Transfers from **one group to another** | **Land reforms**, progressive income tax |
| **Regulatory** | **Limits** individual/firm behaviour | **Licensing**, food-safety standards |
| **Constitutive** | **Creates or reorganises institutions** | **NITI Aayog**, delimitation |
### 3. POLICY CYCLE
| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. **Agenda setting** | Problem identified; media, advocacy, experts push it |
| 2. **Formulation** | Research, stakeholder consultation, **cost-benefit analysis**, impact assessment |
| 3. **Adoption** | Option formally chosen |
| 4. **Implementation** | Planning, resources, coordination |
| 5. **Monitoring and evaluation** | Data test whether goals are met; feedback |
| 6. **Termination / revision** | Failing policy ended or changed; **policy learning** goes back to agenda setting |
- **Communication and transparency** run through **every stage** (not only evaluation).
- Example: **Draft National Policy for Women, 2016** – **Pam Rajput Committee**.
- AP example: GoM under Excise Minister **Kollu Ravindra** framing a new retail excise policy; 2024-26 policy expires **30 Sep 2026**.

| Model / concept | Proponent |

| **Mixed scanning** | **Amitai Etzioni** |
| **Optimal model** | **Yehezkel Dror** |

### 5. POLICY INSTRUMENTS
| Instrument | Indian illustration |
|---|---|
| **Regulation** | Licensing, standards; Delhi EV policy – only e-three-wheelers and N1 trucks registered from 1 Jan 2027, e-two-wheelers from 1 Apr 2028 (Jun 2026) |
| **Taxes and subsidies** | DBT subsidy reform, **PLI** incentives |
| **Direct provision** | Schools, PHCs |
| **Information** | **RTI s. 4** proactive disclosure, campaigns |
| **Nudges** | Behavioural design – **Thaler and Sunstein** |
- **Hood's NATO tools:** **Nodality, Authority, Treasure, Organisation**.
### 6. IMPLEMENTATION AND POLICY FAILURE
| Approach | Idea | Author |
|---|---|---|
| **Top-down** | Central direction, faithful execution; **chains of clearances** derail programmes | **Pressman and Wildavsky**, "Implementation" (**1973**) |
| **Bottom-up** | Front-line officials shape outcomes – **street-level bureaucracy** (**1980**) | **Michael Lipsky** |
| Bottom-up | **Backward mapping** | **Richard Elmore** |
- **Why policies fail:** weak coordination, low resources, poor targeting, capacity gaps.
- **Remedies:** phased roll-out with milestones, **real-time outcome tracking**; AP's **Action Taken Reports** on **seven white papers** (from 22 Jun 2026).
- **Split system** – separates policy from implementation.
### 7. EVALUATION AND DELIVERY REFORMS
- **DMEO** – apex M&E office under **NITI Aayog**; evaluates Central schemes suo motu or on request; advises States.
- **Gender budgeting** and **Outcome Budget** – both **2005-06**.
- Outcome-based governance through ATRs and DMEO evaluations.
- **17 Sep 2026:** NITI Aayog (with WRI India) – second **India Electric Mobility Index** (weights 50:30:20).
- **DBT** – from **1 January 2013**; cuts ghost beneficiaries and middlemen; may exclude those without digital access. **JAM** links Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, Mobile.
- **Social audit** – **MGNREGA s. 17**, by the **Gram Sabha**. **AP model:** statewide NREGS social audits **2006**; independent **SSAAT 2009**.
### 8. WELFARE STATE, SOCIAL POLICY AND LEGISLATION
#### 8.1 Welfare state and DPSPs
| Article | Welfare mandate |
|---|---|
| 38 | Social order of justice; reduce inequalities |
| 39 | Livelihood, equal pay, no concentration of wealth |
| 41 | Right to work, education, public assistance |
| 42 | Just and humane work conditions; maternity relief |
| 43 | Living wage for workers |
| 46 | Interests of SCs, STs, weaker sections |
| 47 | Nutrition, living standards, public health |
#### 8.2 Social policy terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Social policy | State's overall framework for social well-being |
| **Social justice** | Ending social and economic inequalities (Art. 38, 46) |
| **Social welfare** | Targeted services for vulnerable – disabled, destitute children, widows |
| **Social defence** | Rehabilitation in deviant/vulnerable situations (drugs, beggary, juveniles) |
- **Welfare** = short-term, consumption; **development** = long-term, production.
- Social development programmes: **Mid-Day Meal, SSA, Rural Health Mission**; NOT Look East Policy (foreign policy).
- **RSBY** – health insurance, a social protection programme.
!TRAP APPSC-GS 2019: **general health services for all** is NOT a targeted welfare service.
#### 8.3 Social legislation
- Aim: protect the vulnerable; minimum floors for dignified life; "**freedom of contract → fairness in contract**". APPSC-GS 2019 aims question – "**all of the above**".
| Type | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Protective** | Guard against exploitation | **Factories Act 1948**, Bonded Labour Act 1976 |
| **Regulatory** | Control conduct | **Minimum Wages Act 1948**, **Dowry Prohibition Act 1961** (not enabling) |
| **Enabling** | Create entitlements | RTI, NREGA, RTE, NFSA |
#### 8.4 Rights-based entitlements
| Law | Guarantee |
|---|---|
| **RTI Act, 2005** | Information within **30 days** |
| **NREGA, 2005** | **100 days** of work |
| **RTE Act, 2009** | Free schooling, **6–14** (Art. 21A), not 6–18 |
| **NFSA, 2013** | Enabling food entitlement |
- Protection of Civil Rights Rules, 1977 – Central Ministries: Home Affairs, MoSJE, Tribal Affairs (primary responsibility with States/UTs).
### 9. WELFARE MINISTRIES AND EVOLUTION OF WELFARE APPROACH
#### 9.1 Ministries
- **Ministry of Welfare (1985)** → renamed **Social Justice and Empowerment (1998)**; **Tribal Affairs** split off **1999**; **Minority Affairs** carved out **29 January 2006**; **WCD** a separate ministry 2006.
| Ministry | Deals with |
|---|---|
| **MoSJE** | SC, OBC welfare; **senior citizens**; **transgender** welfare; Departments: Social Justice and Empowerment + **Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities** |
| **Tribal Affairs** | STs, **PESA** implementation, **FRA**, tribal education |
| **Minority Affairs** | Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis, Jains (not Hindus) |
| **WCD** | Women, children, ICDS, **Poshan** |
#### 9.2 Plan phases
| Phase | Approach |
|---|---|
| 1950s–70s | State-led planning and welfare; **Central Social Welfare Board 1953** (crèches for children 0–5 of working mothers) |
| **6th Plan** | Women: welfare → **development** |
| **8th–9th Plans** | **Empowerment** |
| 2000s | **Rights-based** laws |
| Now | "**New welfarism**" – targeted, tech-driven DBT instead of legal rights |
- **AP Social Welfare Fund** (aid to voluntary organisations) – **1975**.
- **ES 2025-26:** social protection covers **64.3%** (22% in 2016); spending only **4.0% of GDP**. AP spent ₹1.49 lakh crore of ₹2.83 lakh crore on welfare in two years (Aug 2026).
