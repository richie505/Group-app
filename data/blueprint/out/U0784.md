## U0784 | IMPACTS OF CLIMATE CHANGE ON INDIA – MONSOON, AGRICULTURE, HIMALAYAN GLACIERS, SEA-LEVEL RISE, EXTREME EVENTS

### 2. HEAT WAVES, WET-BULB TEMPERATURE AND HEALTH
#### 2.1 IMD Definitions and Colour Alerts
- **Heat wave**: max at least **40°C in plains**, at least **30°C in hilly regions**; or actual max **≥45°C** whatever the normal.
| Colour | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Normal | No action |
| Yellow | Heat alert | Stay updated |
| Orange | Severe heat | Be prepared |
| **Red** | Extreme heat | **Take action** |
- Causes: winding **Rossby waves → heat domes**; **loo** over Gangetic plains; **not** cold Arabian Sea currents.
#### 2.2 Wet-Bulb Temperature
- Lowest temperature air reaches by evaporative cooling; combines **heat + humidity**; equals **dry-bulb at 100% humidity**; measures heat stress better than air temperature.
- **Survivability limit ~35°C wet-bulb**: sweat can no longer cool the body. **World Bank**: India among the first to cross it often (UPSC 2025).
- Kerala and Tamil Nadu: wet-bulb about 31°C (7 Jun 2026). CMIP6: ~40 coastal districts may warm >1°C by 2040.
- Indians faced **19.8 heatwave days** in 2024; 6.6 due to climate change (Lancet Countdown).
#### 2.3 Heat Action and Health
- **Ahmedabad (2013)**: **South Asia's first Heat Action Plan**.
- AP: heat rising in **Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur**; hottest place **Rentachintala (Palnadu)**; **APSDMA Heatwave Action Plan 2026**: heatwaves kill more in AP than any other disaster. **AP Heat Wave Action Plan** = model of early warning.
- **Dasra "Under the Weather"**: nearly **40% of districts** at high risk.
- Warmer, wetter → **dengue and malaria** spread; floods → cholera, hepatitis; urban waterlogging → leptospirosis, typhoid.
### 3. MONSOON, WATER AND DROUGHT
- **Over 80% of rain in under 100 days** of SW monsoon; SW monsoon = about **75%** of annual rain; **NE monsoon (Oct-Dec) ~11%**.
- **Each 1°C warming → ~7% more moisture** → more extreme rain, flash floods (Brahmaputra, Ganga, Krishna basins).
- Extreme **pre-monsoon** rain events **doubled 1981-2020**.
- **El Niño weakens** SW monsoon (cuts rice, sugarcane); **La Niña boosts** it.
- **68%** of India drought-prone (NDMA); drought types: **meteorological, agricultural, hydrological**; main cause: inadequate and uneven rain; peninsular surface water scarce in poor years.
- **National Water Mission** (NAPCC, **30 Jun 2008**): **20%** rise in water-use efficiency. Agriculture uses ~80% of fresh water.
- **Urban floods**: floodplain encroachment, wetland loss, poor drainage, heavier rain.
### 4. AGRICULTURE AND LIVESTOCK
- Climate change already **cutting wheat** output; **wheat/maize lose 4-10% yield per 1°C**.
- Extreme heat cuts **milk yield 15-25%** (FAO-WMO, 23 Apr 2026); heat stress above 25°C.
| Programme | Detail |

| **NICRA** | **ICAR** project, **2011**, led by **CRIDA, Hyderabad**; 448 climate-resilient villages in 151 districts |

| ACASA-India | GIS crop vulnerability atlas |
- Climate-resilient varieties: **2,996** released 2014-2025; heat-tolerant wheat **HD 3385**; digital soil-health tools.

### 5. HIMALAYAN GLACIERS AND GLOFs
| Event | Place | Date |
|---|---|---|
| **Chamoli disaster** | **Uttarakhand** | **Feb 2021** |
| **South Lhonak lake GLOF** | **Sikkim** | **Oct 2023** |
| Thame floods | Nepal | 2024 |
| Nepal flash floods | Nepal | 26 Aug 2026, >1,000 dead |
- **Gangabal and Nundkol lakes** (Harmukh, Ganderbal): highly GLOF-prone; ice-contact lakes grew 26% (1992-2024); Seismic Zone V.
- **Zanskar** glaciers move slower than 30 years ago (IIT Bombay).
- **Gangotri** melting = **global warming** (APPSC-G2 2025); retreated about **1,727 m** since 1935, faster recently.
- **Hanging glaciers**: stranded on steep slopes (not valley floors); permafrost melt → ice-rock avalanches → LLOFs/GLOFs.
- **Permafrost thaw** releases CH4 + CO2, damages infrastructure.
- **ICIMOD** (Kathmandu) **Snow Update**: snow persistence **23.6% below normal** in 2025 (23-year low); ~27% below in 2025-26.
#### 5.1 Bodies and Missions
- **GSI**: glacier mass balance, GLOF mapping, Himalayan glacier inventory.
- **Himansh**: **NCPOR** station, above **4,000 m**, **Lahaul-Spiti**, **2016**.
- **NMSHE** (NAPCC): **12 thematic areas**; Himalaya supports **51 million hill farmers**; melt threatens lean-season flows of **Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra** and hydropower.
### 6. SEA-LEVEL RISE, COASTS, CYCLONES AND FISHERIES
- Causes: **thermal expansion + melting glaciers and ice sheets**; sea level up **~11 cm since 1993**. **Melting polar ice and sea-level rise** = most important effect of warming.
- "Sea-level rise of one metre by 2070" = **not** a correct statement.
- **INCOIS (Hyderabad)**: **Coastal Vulnerability Index** maps from **7 parameters**; **NCCR, Chennai**: **33.6%** of India's coastline eroding (1990-2018).
#### 6.1 AP Coast
- Coastline **1,053 km**, Srikakulam to Nellore. Godavari delta = **Konaseema**.
- **Erosion hotspots: Uppada (Kakinada)** + Krishna coast.
- Cyclones (**Hudhud, Titli, Michaung**) peak **October-December**; worst hit **North Coastal AP, Nellore/Bapatla**; harm paddy and aquaculture but feed rabi crops and tanks. **Rayalaseema**: chronic drought.
#### 6.2 Cyclones and Marine Heatwaves
- Cyclones **more frequent in Bay of Bengal**; but Arabian Sea cyclones rose **52%** and very severe ones **150%** (2001-2019).
- **Marine heatwaves**: **doubled since 1982**; raise cyclone **winds up to 20%**, **rain up to 12%** → rapid intensification.
- Fisheries: **>90%** of oceans had MHWs in 2025 → lower oxygen, harm fish.
### 7. CLIMATE VULNERABILITY AND CLIMATE RISK INDEX
- **NICRA**: 651 districts assessed → **310** highly/very highly vulnerable. Over 80% of urban people in hazard-prone districts.
- **DST (2021)** most vulnerable states: **Jharkhand, Mizoram, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Assam, Bihar, Arunachal Pradesh, West Bengal**.
- **AP SAPCC**: **north and central** very high; **southern coast moderate**; **south-west** relatively low. SAPCC 2025-30: **8 adaptation measures** for energy (heat → thermal power; erratic rain → hydropower).
#### 7.1 Germanwatch Climate Risk Index
- **Germanwatch**: German NGO (**Bonn**); released at COPs.
- **CRI 2026** (COP30, Nov 2025; 1995-2024): India **9th** most affected; **430 events**, **$170 billion** losses, 1.3 billion people hit; annual rank **10th → 15th**.
- **CRI 2021** = **16th edition**, India **7th** (score 16.67); 2022 and 2023 editions not published.
- World Bank: **45 million** Indians into extreme poverty within 15 years. ODI: India could lose 3-10% of GDP a year by 2100.
### 8. OBSERVED GLOBAL IMPACTS AND BIODIVERSITY
- **Already observed** (APPSC-GS 2023): **significant reduction in ice-sheet mass**; Greenland and Antarctic losing mass for two decades.
- **WMO 2025**: oceans hold >90% of excess heat; Arctic sea ice near record low; glacier loss threatens water for **~2 billion people**.
- **Ice core**: cryogenic indicator; from Antarctic/Greenland or mountain glaciers; **lower layers older**.

#### 8.1 Biodiversity
- **Rutgers study**: warming wipes out about **twice as many ocean species as land** (APPSC-G1 2019).
- **IPBES 2019**: ~8 million species; **up to 1 million threatened**; **40% of amphibians**.
- Species shift **poleward and upslope**; krill loss → Emperor penguin breeding failure.
### 9. TIPPING POINTS AND 1.5°C VS 2°C
- **Tipping point**: limit where small changes cause large, **self-feeding, irreversible** shifts.
- **Loss of Arctic sea ice**: bright ice melts → darker ocean absorbs heat → self-feeding warming (APPSC-G2 2025).
| Tipping elements (listed) | NOT tipping elements |
|---|---|
| Greenland and West Antarctic ice sheets, AMOC, permafrost, **warm-water coral reefs**, **Amazon**, **Arctic sea ice** | Sahara dunes, Deccan basalts, European temperate deciduous forests |
- **UNSG Brief #12 (Jun 2026)**: warming ~1.3°C; **coral 1.2°C threshold already crossed** (most fragile; recovery >15 years); **Amazon** beyond ~2°C may flip to savanna (carbon source).
- Beyond **3°C**, land biosphere may turn from sink to source.
| Impact (IPCC SR1.5, 2018) | 1.5°C | 2°C |
|---|---|---|
| Coral reefs lost | 70-90% | >99% |
| **Ice-free Arctic summer** | **Once a century** | **Once a decade** |
| Sea-level rise by 2100 | ~10 cm lower | Higher |
- Paris Agreement: well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C.
