## U0502 | PUBLIC DISTRIBUTION, PROCUREMENT AND BUFFER-STOCKING; FOOD SUBSIDY AND FCI REFORM
### 1. FOOD MANAGEMENT – OBJECTIVES
- Department of Food and Public Distribution works through **procurement, storage and movement, public distribution, buffer stocks**.
| Arm | Objective |
|---|---|
| **PDS** | Minimum grain at affordable prices for low-income groups; equitable distribution; control of price swings |
| **Procurement** | Assure **MSP**; free grain for the weak under PMGKAY; market intervention to check prices |
- Buying at support prices gives price stability, remunerative prices and stocks for PDS.
!TRAP **Export of foodgrains** is NOT an objective of food management (BPSC 2020).
### 2. FOOD CORPORATION OF INDIA
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Set up | **14 January 1965** (same year as APC) |
| Act | **Food Corporations Act, 1964** |
| Recommended by | **Food Grains Prices Committee (L.K. Jha, 1964)** |
| HQ | **New Delhi** |
| Role | Nodal agency buying **wheat and paddy** under price support, with State agencies |
- **Missions**: (1) procurement at MSP; (2) storage, movement and distribution; (3) operational and **buffer stocks**; (4) grain at reasonable prices to weaker sections through **TPDS**.
- NOT a mission: **regulating markets in agricultural products** (State APMC laws) (APPSC 2018).
- **Digital systems**: **OSM (Online Storage Management)** from **1 April 2022** – storage, movement, distribution under TPDS (APPSC 2021); **Anna Darpan** (2024) – FCI supply-chain system of the Dept of Food and PD, **not Agriculture Ministry**.
### 3. PROCUREMENT SYSTEMS
| System | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Centralised** | FCI (directly or via State agencies) buys for central pool |
| **DCP (1997, same year as TPDS)** | Designated States procure, store, distribute; Centre pays State's economic cost minus CIP |
| **Open-ended** | **Wheat and paddy** – all produce meeting standards at MSP |
| **Target-based** | **PSS** pulses and oilseeds – cap **25%** of production |
- **OMSS (Domestic)**: FCI sells wheat and rice from the Central Pool in the open market at **predetermined prices** to moderate prices.
- RMS 2026-27 wheat: over **35 MT**, up 17%, above the 34.5 MT target.
### 4. PRICE TERMS AND FOOD SUBSIDY
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MSP** | Floor price **before sowing** (CACP → CCEA) |
| **Procurement price** | Price at which government actually buys (once announced separately at harvest; later merged) |
| **Central Issue Price (CIP)** | Price at which the **Centre issues grain to States** for PDS |
| FPS retail price | Fixed by **States** (operational role) |
| **Ceiling price** | **Maximum** price, protects consumers |
| **Economic cost** | **MSP + bonus + procurement incidentals + distribution cost** (UPSC 2019) |
| **Food subsidy** | **Economic cost minus CIP** |
| **Explicit subsidy** | Shown in budget (incl. reimbursement to DCP States) |
| **Hidden subsidy** | Not shown, e.g. FCI carrying cost financed by **NSSF loans** |
- Acquisition cost = MSP + bonus + incidentals; pooled grain cost ≈ **two-thirds** of economic cost.
- Subsidy components: FCI subsidy (NFSA, other schemes, strategic reserve) + subsidy to DCP States.

### 6. BUFFER STOCKS AND STORAGE
- **Buffer norms** fixed by **CCEA** each quarter – as on **1 April, 1 July, 1 October, 1 January**.
- 1 April norm: **210.40 LMT** (rice 135.80, wheat 74.60); 1 July wheat norm about **275 LMT** (rice ~135).
- **Strategic reserve**: **30 LMT wheat** (from 1.7.2008) and **20 LMT rice** (from 1.1.2009).
- **Excess stocks**: **604.02 LMT** on 1 April 2026 – nearly **three times** the norm → high carrying cost.
- **CAP (Cover and Plinth)**: open storage on raised plinths under polythene covers.
- **Steel silos** under **PPP**.
- **IGMRI**: **Hapur (UP)**; field stations Hyderabad and Ludhiana; trains SAARC officials (first batch 17-26 November).
- **Grain Storage Plan (cooperative sector)**: PACS silos of 50/100/200 MT; PACS chosen by District Cooperative Development Committees; **NCDC** implements.
### 7. PDS EVOLUTION
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1940s | Rationing – **Bengal famine**; revived in 1960s shortages |
| **June 1992** | **RPDS** – backward, hilly, drought areas |
| **June 1997** | **TPDS** – BPL and APL entitlements |
| **25 Dec 2000** | **AAY** – **35 kg/family/month**, poorest |
| **2013** | **NFSA** – legal entitlement to about **800 million** (not 400 million) |
| **1 Jan 2023** | Free grain under **PMGKAY** to 80 crore+; extended to **31 Dec 2028** |
- Sequence: **PDS → RPDS → TPDS → AAY**.
- PMGKAY: ~₹11.80 lakh crore by Centre; motto "**One Nation-One Price-One Ration**"; Covid-era PMGKY gave 5 kg extra.
- **Fortified rice** (iron, **vitamin B12, folic acid**) to Dec 2028, Centre-funded **₹17,082 crore**.
### 8. SHANTA KUMAR HIGH LEVEL COMMITTEE (2014-15)
- PDS leakage about **46%** (NSS 2011-12); only **~6%** of farmers sell at MSP.
- Recommendations: expand **DCP**; private players in storage and logistics; hand procurement in surplus States (**Punjab, Haryana, AP**) to States so FCI focuses on **eastern States**; cut NFSA coverage **67% → ~40%** (not 50%); **cash transfers** in well-connected areas; restructure FCI debt.
### 9. PDS REFORMS AND ONORC
| Reform | Period / content |
|---|---|
| **End-to-End Computerisation of TPDS** | **Oct 2012 – Mar 2020**: digitised cards, online allocation, ePoS |
| **IM-PDS** | From **April 2018**; national portability |
| **SMART-PDS** | **Apr 2023 – Mar 2026**; 4 modules |
| **SARTHAK-PDS** | **2026-2031** (CCEA, 28 May 2026); umbrella incl. SMART-PDS, intra-State movement, FPS margins; AI/ML, blockchain tracking from procurement to FPS |
- **ONORC**: portability via **ePoS**, **Aadhaar-seeded** cards, biometric/iris authentication; migrant draws full or part ration anywhere, family draws the rest at home.
- App: **Mera Ration** (APPSC 2021); rollout target **1 June 2020** (APPSC 2018); now all **36 States/UTs**, ~80 crore beneficiaries.
- **DBT in food** (cash for grain): **Chandigarh, Puducherry**, urban **Dadra and Nagar Haveli** (piloted 2015).
- **Delhi**: first to launch **e-ration card** service (March 2015).
- Anti-leakage measures: e-PoS, ONORC, biometrics (ending procurement is not one).
