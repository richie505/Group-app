## U0604 | INSOLATION AND HEAT BUDGET; DISTRIBUTION OF TEMPERATURE, INVERSION
### 1. HEATING AND COOLING OF THE ATMOSPHERE
| Process | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Insolation** | Incoming solar radiation; direct heating by **short waves** |
| **Conduction** | Heats **lower layers** of air in contact with warm ground |
| **Convection** | **Vertical** transfer by rising warm air (troposphere) |
| **Advection** | **Horizontal** transfer by wind (e.g. the **Loo**) |
| **Terrestrial radiation** | **Long-wave** heat from the earth = **main heater of air** |
!KEY APPSC-G2 2023 match: Advection – horizontal; Convection – vertical; Insolation – direct; Conduction – lower layers.
- Air is **heated from below**, not directly by the sun. Earth re-emits long-wave infra-red (8,000–25,000 nm), absorbed by CO₂ and water vapour.
!TRAP UPSC 2024: "Atmosphere is heated more by incoming solar radiation" = WRONG; "greenhouse gases absorb long waves" = right.
### 2. INSOLATION – FACTORS AND DISTRIBUTION
- Factors: **angle of sun's rays**, **length of day**, **transparency** (clouds, dust, vapour), **earth-sun distance**, land-sea distribution, slope and aspect.
- **Perihelion ~3 Jan, 147 million km**; **aphelion ~4 Jul, 152 million km**.
- Equator gets direct rays; poles oblique rays. Insolation falls poleward; **higher over continents than oceans** at the same latitude.
- **Maximum insolation and highest temperatures: subtropical deserts (20–30° N)**, not the equator – clear skies, dry air.
- Heat **surplus in tropics**, deficit at poles; winds and currents carry heat poleward.
### 3. ALBEDO AND CLOUDS
- **Albedo** = share of incoming radiation a surface reflects.
- Order: **fresh snow (highest, APPSC-G1 2019) > sand > grass > forest > water**.
| Surface | Albedo (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Fresh snow | **75–95%** |
| Dry sand | 20–40% |
| Grass | 20–25% |
| Crop land | 15–25% |
| Water (sun high) | below 10% |
- **High, thin clouds trap heat** (warming); **low, thick clouds reflect sunlight** (cooling).
- **Ice-albedo feedback** (positive): melting ice exposes darker ocean → more absorption. **Arctic** warms **3–4 times** the global average, at least 2°C (Apr 2025).
### 4. EARTH'S HEAT BUDGET (100 UNITS)
| Of 100 units | Units |
|---|---|
| Reflected to space (clouds 27, scattering 6, snow/ice 2) = **planetary albedo** | **35** |
| Absorbed by atmosphere | **14** |
| Absorbed by surface | **51** |
- Surface radiates all 51: **17 to space**, **34 to atmosphere** (6 direct, 9 convection/turbulence, 19 latent heat).
- Atmosphere sends **48** (14 + 34) to space → balance; earth neither warms nor cools.
!NUM 35 + 14 + 51 = 100; 17 + 48 = 65 out.
### 5. GREENHOUSE EFFECT
- Atmosphere **transparent to short-wave** sunlight, **opaque to long-wave** terrestrial radiation; re-radiates part back.
- GHGs **CO₂, CH₄, water vapour, N₂O** trap heat and raise temperature (APPSC-GS 2025).
- **Without atmosphere: about −18°C** instead of **+15°C** (UPSC 2025).
| Gas | Share of effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | **~60%** | Fossil fuels, deforestation, cement |
| Methane ("marsh gas") | ~20% | Paddy, wetlands, livestock |
| CFCs | ~15% | Refrigeration, aerosols |
| N₂O | ~5% | Fertilisers |
- **Water vapour** = commonest GHG, a climate feedback.
- **GWP (100 yr)**: CO₂ 1; methane **28–36**; N₂O **265–298**.
- Global land temperature up **1.59°C** since pre-industrial (UNEP Emissions Gap 2025). Oceans took a record **+23 ZJ** in 2025 (~90% of excess heat).
### 6. VERTICAL DISTRIBUTION
- **Normal lapse rate 6.5°C per km**.
- Why: ground heats air from below; higher air is **thinner, with less water vapour and dust**.
- **Himalayas** are cool despite low latitude – **altitude**.
### 7. HORIZONTAL DISTRIBUTION – ISOTHERMS AND THERMAL EQUATOR
- **Isotherms** join places of equal temperature; run east-west; **more regular in Southern Hemisphere** (mostly ocean).
| Month | Over land | Over oceans |
|---|---|---|
| **January** (NH) | Bend **equatorward** | Bend **poleward** (oceans warmer; land cools faster) |
| **July** | Bend poleward | Reverse |
- **Thermal equator**: line of highest mean temperature; **mostly north of the Equator** (more land); moves with the sun.
- India, winter: **20°C isotherm** runs **Tapi mouth → Mahanadi delta**; temperature rises north to south.
- **January 18°C isotherm** splits India into tropical (south) and sub-tropical (north).
- Controls: latitude, altitude, sea (equable coasts), **ocean currents** (cold currents lower coast temperatures, help west-coast deserts), relief.
### 8. RANGE OF TEMPERATURE
- **Seasonal range maximum in high latitudes** (APPSC-G1 2023); largest annual range in **high-latitude continental interiors (Siberia)**; **minimum at the equator**.
- **Interior** = larger annual and daily range (continentality); **coasts** = equable, small range.
- **Cloudy nights warmer** – clouds send back Earth's heat (blanket); **no dew** on cloudy nights. **Clear nights cooler** – frost.
- India: highest winter daily range in **NW India (14–18°C)**; highest daily range in **Rajasthan deserts**.
- **Chennai**: annual mean 26°C, rainfall ~63 cm, annual range **9°C**.
- AP: **Rayalaseema** high daily range; coast low (**Visakhapatnam, Kakinada** sea breezes).
### 9. TEMPERATURE INVERSION
- Temperature **rises with height** (normal lapse reversed).
- Conditions: **long winter nights, clear skies, calm dry air, snow cover**.
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Ground (radiation) | Surface night cooling |
| **Valley (air drainage)** | Cold air drains into valleys → **frost pockets** on floor; slopes warmer → **orchards on upper slopes** |
| **Permanent** | Polar areas; **stratosphere** = permanent inversion layer |
- Effects: **fog, frost, smog**; pollutants trapped in the **planetary boundary layer**. North Indian winter mornings: inversion + moisture → fog.
### 10. EXTREMES, URBAN HEAT ISLANDS AND HEAT WAVES
#### 10.1 Records
| Record | Place | Value |
|---|---|---|
| World hottest | **Death Valley** | 56.7°C |
| World coldest | **Vostok** | −89.2°C |
| India coldest | **Dras** (Ladakh) | −45°C (1908) |
| AP hottest | **Rentachintala** (Palnadu) | above 47°C |
| AP coldest | **Lambasingi** (ASR) – "Kashmir of AP" | ~2°C |
- **Urban heat island**: concrete and asphalt store heat. Chennai vegetation 25% → 10% (2014–24).
- **TRISHNA**: **ISRO–CNES** (Indo-French) thermal-infrared satellite; land surface temperature, urban heat islands; **PSLV launch due 2027**.
- **Heat dome**: stuck high pressure under a slowed, winding jet stream.
#### 10.2 IMD Heat Wave and Cold Wave Criteria
| Region | Heat wave max | Cold wave min |
|---|---|---|
| **Plains** | **40°C** | **10°C** or below |
| **Coast** | **37°C** | — |
| **Hills** | **30°C** | **0°C** or below |
- Heat wave: departure **4.5–6.4°C**; severe: **above 6.4°C**. Coast: departure 4.5°C or more with max at least 37°C.
- Plains also by actual max: **45°C = heat wave**, **47°C = severe**.
- Cold wave (plains, actual): **4°C** or below; severe **2°C** or below.
- **NDMA** heat guidelines: **2019**.
#### 10.3 AP Angle
- **AP Heat Wave Action Plan since 2016**; **APSDMA** district-wise SMS alerts (Apr 2026).
- Severe-heatwave districts (HAP 2026): Tirupati, Prakasam, SPSR Nellore, Markapuram, Guntur, Bapatla, Palnadu.
- **2026 peak: 48.1°C at Piduguralla** (Palnadu), 21 May 2026; 45°C crossed in 18 districts.
