## U0463 | ENVIRONMENTAL DEGRADATION AS A DEVELOPMENT CHALLENGE; GREEN ACCOUNTING AND GREEN GDP
### 1. FUNCTIONS OF THE ENVIRONMENT
| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Supplies resources** (first function) | Both **renewable** (trees, fish) and **non-renewable** (fossil fuels) |
| **Assimilates waste** | Absorbs and neutralises wastes |
| **Sustains life** | Genetic and biodiversity |
| **Aesthetic services** | Scenery and amenity |
- **First function** of the environment = **supply / production of raw materials** (APPSC-GS 2023 key).
- **Absorptive capacity** = power to take in degradation = maximum pollutants an ecosystem can process and neutralise without harm (APPSC-GS 2024 key).
- **Crisis** comes when extraction > regeneration and waste > absorptive capacity; the environment then **cannot sustain life**. The world is at the threshold of an environmental crisis.
!TRAP "Supplies renewable resources only" is wrong. **Recycling** is NOT a function of the environment; nor is "it disorganizes waste that benefits human life".
### 2. RESOURCE DEPLETION AND ITS CAUSES
#### 2.1 Key terms
- **Resource depletion** = use faster than regeneration (APPSC-GS 2024 key: B).
- A forest managed **sustainably for timber while keeping biodiversity** is NOT depletion.
#### 2.2 Resource -> cause (APPSC-GS 2025 match)
| Resource | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Groundwater** | Deep bore tubewells for irrigation |
| **Topsoil** | Brick production |
| **Biodiversity habitats** | Expansion of agriculture |
- Four-pair question key: A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4.
### 3. CAUSES OF ENVIRONMENTAL DEGRADATION
#### 3.1 Human-driven causes vs conservation practices
| Human-driven causes | NOT causes (conservation) |
|---|---|
| **Deforestation** (APPSC-GS 2024 key) | Sustainable agriculture |
| Chemical-intensive farming | Renewable energy production |
| Groundwater over-extraction, dams | **Wildlife conservation** |
| Road and urban expansion, industrial emissions, overfishing | |
- India's **two threats**: **poverty-induced degradation** + **pollution from affluence** and rapid industrial growth.
#### 3.2 Causes by sector
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Green Revolution: HYV monoculture + chemical fertilisers | Cut **soil fertility** |
| HYV irrigation needs | **Water-table depletion** |
| Fertiliser, pesticide, herbicide use | Polluted land, water, air + reduced biodiversity |
| Soil: **Urea = ~56%** of fertiliser use | Overuse degrades soil and water |
| Air: urban particulate pollution + coal-based power | Main reasons for India's **low EPI score** |
- Urea alternative: **green urea** (green hydrogen + captured CO2) = low-carbon.
### 4. ENVIRONMENTAL KUZNETS CURVE
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| X-axis | **Per capita income** |
| Y-axis | **Environmental degradation** (pollution) |
| Shape | **Inverted U** |
| Logic | Pollution rises at low income, falls after a **turning point** |
| Named after | **Simon Kuznets** (his original curve: income **inequality**) |
| First shown for environment | **Grossman and Krueger** (1991) |
!TRAP Kuznets Curve = income vs inequality; Environmental Kuznets Curve = income vs degradation. Both inverted U.
### 5. GROWTH VS ENVIRONMENT DEBATE
| View | Argument |
|---|---|
| **Trade-off view** | Environmental rules slow growth; **"grow first, clean up later"** (EKC logic) |
| **Necessity view** | Degradation raises **health costs** and lowers **labour efficiency** (World Bank estimates) |
- Conventional GDP ignores depletion, so growth can hide loss of **natural capital**.
### 6. GREEN ECONOMY AND GREEN GDP
- **Green economy** = reduces environmental risks and ecological scarcities; development **decoupled** from resource use and pollution; builds **green industries** and **green jobs** (APPSC-GS 2025 key).
- **Green jobs** = preserve or restore environmental quality.
- **Green GDP** = conventional GDP adjusted for environmental consequences = GDP **net of natural-resource depletion and degradation costs**.
- **Uttarakhand** = **first State** to adopt green GDP, as **Gross Environment Product (GEP)**.
### 7. GREEN ACCOUNTING FRAMEWORKS
| Person / body | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Sir Partha Dasgupta** | Chaired the **Expert Group on Green National Accounts** (set up **2011**, report **2013**) |
| **Paul Samuelson** | **NEW** (Net Economic Welfare) |
| **Nordhaus and Tobin** | **MEW** (Measure of Economic Welfare) |
| **World Bank** | Developed the **Green Index**; 2013 cost-of-degradation estimate |
| **MoSPI** | Environment accounts on **UN SEEA**; **EnviStats India** |
- **SEEA** (System of Environmental-Economic Accounting) Central Framework adopted by the **UN Statistical Commission in 2012**.
- MoSPI ecosystem and natural-asset accounts released **since 2018**.
- **EnviStats India** (annual) - six components: conditions and quality; resources and use; residuals; extreme events; human settlements and health; protection and management.

### 9. COST OF DEGRADATION AND LAND DEGRADATION DATA
#### 9.1 Cost to India
- **World Bank (2013)**: India's annual cost of environmental degradation **about 5.7% of GDP** (**Rs 3.75 lakh crore**, for 2009).
- How: higher **healthcare spending** + lower **labour productivity**.
#### 9.2 Global
- About **one-third** of global land transformed by humans; **60%+ of rivers** dammed or diverted; about **40%** of land degraded.
#### 9.3 ISRO-SAC Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas (2021)
| Period | Degraded area |
|---|---|
| 2003-05 | 94.53 Mha (28.76%) |
| 2011-13 | 96.40 Mha (29.32%) |
| **2018-19** | **97.85 Mha (29.77%)** |
- Leading processes: **water erosion (10.98%)**, **vegetation degradation (8.91%)**.
- Desertification rose in **28 of 31** States/UTs between 2011-13 and 2018-19.
- **Restoration**: **21.76 Mha** restored in 2011-2020 (461.14 Mt carbon), beating the original **Bonn pledge**; **Telangana first (4.18 Mha)**; **AP among the top five**.
- **AP districts**: **Anantapur, Kurnool and Kadapa** are the most desertification-vulnerable.
