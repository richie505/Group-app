## U0164 | BENGAL – MURSHID QULI KHAN TO SIRAJ-UD-DAULA; BLACK HOLE EPISODE; BATTLE OF PLASSEY (1757)
### 1. NAWABS OF BENGAL IN ORDER
- **Order:** Murshid Quli Khan → Shuja-ud-din → Sarfaraz Khan → Alivardi Khan → Siraj-ud-Daula.
| Nawab | Period | Key act / status |
|---|---|---|
| **Murshid Quli Khan** | 1717–27 | Founder of the **autonomous Bengal Nawabi**; ijaradari; **last Subahdar appointed by the Mughal Emperor** |
| **Shuja-ud-din** | 1727–39 | **Son-in-law** of Murshid Quli; by 1735 "too absolute to regard any orders from court" |
| **Sarfaraz Khan** | 1739–40 | Shuja-ud-din's son (Murshid Quli's grandson); weak; killed by Alivardi at **Giria (1740)** |
| **Alivardi Khan** | 1740–56 | Earlier **Deputy Governor of Bihar**; took the throne by killing Sarfaraz; banned English and French fortification of Calcutta and Chandernagore |
| **Siraj-ud-Daula** | 1756–57 | **Grandson of Alivardi**; got imperial farman as Subahdar of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa; lost Plassey |
!TRAP Sarfaraz was the son of **Shuja-ud-din**, not of Murshid Quli Khan.
!TRAP **Mir Jafar** was the puppet Nawab installed after Plassey, not the founder of the Nawabi.
### 2. MURSHID QULI KHAN
#### 2.1 Rise and capital
- Rise: **Diwan → Subahdar → Nawab of Bengal (1717)**.
- **Capital shift: Dacca → Murshidabad.**
- Wanted customs duties from the English; the English increased their military establishment instead.
#### 2.2 Revenue and trade terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ijaradari** | **Revenue farming**: contractors (ijaradars) collected land revenue (APPSC 2018) |
| **Mal jasmani** | Another name for this contract-based revenue collection |
| **Jagir → khalisa** | Jagir land brought under direct state (crown) revenue administration |
| **Farman of 1717** (Farrukhsiyar) | Duty-free trade for the Company |
| **Dastaks** | Misused by Company servants for private trade → Nawab lost revenue |
### 3. CAUSES OF CONFLICT – SIRAJ vs ENGLISH
| Cause | Point |
|---|---|
| Misuse of dastaks | Private trade of Company servants under the 1717 farman |
| **Fortification of Fort William** | Without the Nawab's permission; the **French obeyed** the order to stop, the English did not |
| **Shelter to fugitives** | English protected the runaway **Krishna Das** |
| Court factions | English seen to support **Ghaseti Begum** and **Shaukat Jang of Purnea** |
### 4. EVENTS 1756–57
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| May 1756 | **Kasimbazar** factory seized (first act) |
| June 1756 | **Calcutta fell**; Governor **Drake fled to Fulta**; Holwell surrendered |
| 20 June 1756 | **Black Hole** episode |

### 5. BLACK HOLE EPISODE
- **146** English prisoners in a small cell → about **120** died (**Holwell's** own figure: **123**).
- Narrator: **J.Z. Holwell**, a survivor; the story rests mainly on his account.
- **Debate:** historians doubt the numbers; the British used the story to justify war.
### 6. BATTLE OF PLASSEY (23 JUNE 1757)
#### 6.1 Facts
- Fought at **Palashi** (West Bengal) on the **Bhagirathi**, about 150 km north of Calcutta, near Murshidabad.
- **Robert Clive** (East India Company) vs **Siraj-ud-Daula**.
- **Mir Madan** fought for Siraj; **Mir Jafar** (commander-in-chief) and **Rai Durlabh** (Diwan) led divisions but did not join the fight.
#### 6.2 Conspirators
| Conspirator | Who |
|---|---|
| **Mir Jafar** | Commander of the Nawab's army; promised the throne |
| **Jagat Seth** | **"World banker"** – Bengal's great banking house |
| **Omichund** | Merchant; deceived with a decoy treaty |
| **Rai Durlabh** | Nawab's Diwan; secret deal with the English |
| Yar Lutuf Khan | Officer in the plot |
!KEY Plassey = a **victory of treachery**; a political-financial deal, not a contest of equal armies.
#### 6.3 Rewards and gains
- Mir Jafar made Nawab; Company got the **zamindari of the 24 Parganas**, **duty-free trade in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** and a large payment.
- Company turned from traders to a political power; Bengal money paid for the defeat of the French.
- 23 June 2026 marked the 269th anniversary of Plassey.
### 7. SIGNIFICANCE – PLASSEY vs BUXAR
| Plassey (1757) | Buxar (1764) |
|---|---|
| Conspiracy: Jagat Seth, Omichund, Mir Jafar | Open military victory; the real military test |
| Usually = **foundation of British rule**; **first decisive military success** of the Company | Confirmed Plassey; UPPSC 2012 treats it as most decisive |

### 11. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. Capital moved **Dacca → Murshidabad**, not the reverse.
2. **Ijaradari** = revenue farming, also called mal jasmani.
3. Sarfaraz = **Shuja-ud-din's son**.
4. Alivardi ruled **just before** Siraj; Siraj was his **grandson**.
5. Black Hole narrator = **Holwell**; date 20 June 1756.
6. **Alinagar** (Feb 1757) came before Plassey.
7. Order: **Ambur → Plassey → Wandiwash → Buxar**.
8. The **French obeyed** Siraj's ban on fortification; the English did not.
9. Plassey = treachery; Buxar = open military victory.
