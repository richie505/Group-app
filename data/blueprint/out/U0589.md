## U0589 | THE UNIVERSE AND THE SOLAR SYSTEM – ORIGIN THEORIES, THE SUN, PLANETS, ASTEROIDS, COMETS, METEORS
### 1. UNIVERSE – BASICS, AGES AND DISTANCE UNITS
- **Cosmology** = study of the origin, evolution and structure of the universe. Standard model = **Big Bang + dark matter + dark energy + CMB**.
- Age of the universe: **13.8 billion years** (APPSC 2019). Age of the Earth and solar system: **4.6 billion years**.
- Most abundant element in the universe and the Sun: **hydrogen**, then **helium** (Sun ~74% H, ~24% He).
- **Milky Way** = our **barred spiral galaxy** (rotating disc + central bulge + dark-matter halo).
- Solar system: Sun, 8 planets, dwarf planets, satellites, asteroids, comets, meteoroids, cosmic dust. The Sun's gravity gives the **centripetal force** that keeps planets in orbit.
- Planets are **non-luminous**; they shine by reflecting sunlight. Stars twinkle, planets do not.
#### 1.1 Distance Units
| Unit | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Astronomical unit (AU)** | Mean Earth–Sun distance | ~**149.6 million km** (149.598 × 10⁶ km) |
| **Light year** | Distance light travels in vacuum in one year (distance, not time) | **9.461 × 10¹² km** (~9.46 trillion km) |
| **Parsec** | Largest unit | **3.26 light years** |
- Sunlight takes **8 min 19 s** to cross 1 AU (~150 million km).
- Light years are used for star distances because the **speed of light is constant**.
!TRAP Order of size: AU < light year < parsec. A light year measures distance, not time.
### 2. ORIGIN THEORIES
| Theory | Proponent | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **Nebular** hypothesis | **Immanuel Kant**; revised by **Laplace** | 1755; 1796 |
| **Planetesimal** hypothesis | **Chamberlin and Moulton** | 1904 |
| **Tidal** hypothesis | **James Jeans and Harold Jeffreys** | – |
| Nova hypothesis | Hoyle and Lyttleton | – |
| Interstellar dust (Earth from gas and dust) | **Otto Schmidt** | – |
| **Big Bang** (expanding universe) | **Georges Lemaitre** (1894–1966) | 1927 |
| **Steady State** | **Hoyle, Bondi and Gold** | 1948 |
- Big Bang evidence: **red-shift** of receding galaxies (**Edwin Hubble, 1929**) and the **Cosmic Microwave Background** (detection of microwaves – leftover glow).
- Present view: all planets formed from one spinning **protoplanetary disc**; hot inner disc kept only metals and rocky minerals (Mercury–Mars); ices (water, ammonia, methane) stayed in the cool outer disc (giant planets).
!PAIR Kant | proposed nebular | 1755 || Laplace | revised it | 1796
!TRAP Lemaitre proposed the Big Bang (1927); Hubble only showed expansion (1929). Steady State = Gold and Bondi (with Hoyle).

### 4. STARS – LIFE CYCLE AND TERMS
#### 4.1 Life Cycle
- All stars: **nebula** (gas-dust cloud) → **protostar** → **main sequence**.
- Low/medium mass (like the Sun): **red giant** → planetary nebula → **white dwarf**. The Sun becomes a red giant after its hydrogen is used up.
- Massive: **supergiant** → **supernova** (explosion of a dying star) → **neutron star** or **black hole**.
- **Chandrasekhar limit** (~1.44, ≈1.4 solar masses) = highest mass of a white dwarf; above it the star collapses into a neutron star or black hole.
#### 4.2 Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nebulae** | Giant clouds of dust and gas |
| **Cepheids** | Pulsating stars that brighten and dim; **period–luminosity** relation measures distance (cosmic yardsticks) |
| **Pulsar** | Fast-spinning **neutron star** emitting beams, formed when a massive star runs out of fuel and collapses |
| **Black hole** | Remains of a large star dying in a supernova; high density lets **no light/radiation** escape |
| **Magnitude** | Lower number = brighter (logarithmic); **absolute magnitude** = brightness at **10 parsecs** |
- **Colour = surface temperature**: **blue** (hottest, 50,000–28,000 K) → white → **yellow** (Sun, 6,000–4,900 K) → orange → **red** (coolest).
- **Polaris** in **Ursa Minor**, ~323 light years, within 1° of the north celestial pole; **Vega** will be the pole star in ~**12,000 years** (axis wobble).
- Nearest star: the **Sun**; next: **Alpha Centauri** system.
- Hubble took the first direct image of another star's surface: **Betelgeuse** (Orion, ~700 ly).
- In a spacecraft midway to the Sun: sky is **black** and stars **do not twinkle** (no atmosphere).
!TRAP Brittle Star is an echinoderm, not a space object; pulsar, quasar, black hole are.
### 5. THE SUN
- Closest star; **99.8%** of the solar system's mass; diameter **109 × Earth's**. Plasma state (an old key said "liquid state only").
- Energy: **nuclear fusion** of hydrogen into helium in the core (**~15 million °C**). Mean distance 1 AU.
#### 5.1 Layers (centre outward)
| Layer | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Fusion H → He; ~15 million °C |
| Radiative and convective zones | Between core and photosphere |
| **Photosphere** | Visible bright surface above the convection zone; **~5,500–6,000°C** (5,778 K); sunspots; supergranules ~30,000 km, ~24 h life |
| **Chromosphere** | Reddish lower atmosphere, seen at eclipses |
| **Corona** | Outer halo, seen in a **total solar eclipse**; **over 1 million °C** |
#### 5.2 Solar Activity
| Phenomenon | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Sunspots** | Cooler, darker magnetic patches; mark the **~11-year solar cycle**; magnetic poles reverse at each peak; matched by currents in the 35,000-km Near-Surface Shear Layer |
| **Solar flares** | Bursts from magnetic reconnection; classes **A, B, C, M, X** (X strongest) |
| **CMEs** | Plasma clouds (not rock) from the corona, **250–3,000 km/s**, reach Earth in **1–3 days** |
| **Aurora** | Solar particles hit the atmosphere: **Borealis** (north), **Australis** (south) |
- Effects on Earth: geomagnetic storms, auroras, damage to satellites, GPS, aviation, radio and power grids.
!TRAP The corona (outer) is hotter than the photosphere (surface).
### 6. PLANETS – ORDER, DATA AND SUPERLATIVES
- **8 planets** (9 by the old view). Inner/**terrestrial**: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (outermost inner). Outer/**Jovian**: Jupiter (first outer), Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. **Gas giants** Jupiter, Saturn; **ice giants** Uranus, Neptune.
- Between Mars and Uranus: **Jupiter and Saturn**; between Earth and Sun: Mercury and Venus. All revolve **counter-clockwise**.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 57.9 | 4,879 | **88 days** | 5.4 |
| Venus | 108.2 | 12,104 | 224.7 days | 5.2 |
| Earth | 149.6 | 12,756 | 365.25 days | **5.5** |
| Mars | 228 | 6,792 | 687 days | 3.9 |
| Jupiter | 778.5 | **142,984** | 11.86 years | 1.3 |
| Saturn | 1,432 | 120,536 | 29.4 years | **0.7** |
| Uranus | 2,867 | 51,118 | 84.07 years | 1.3 |
| Neptune | 4,515 | 49,528 | **164.9 years** | 1.6 |
- Size: **Jupiter > Saturn > Uranus > Neptune > Earth > Venus > Mars > Mercury**.
- Revolution period rises with distance; if the Sun–Earth distance fell by a quarter, the year would be **shorter**.
- Average temperature: Venus 464°C > Mercury 167°C > Earth 15°C > Mars –65°C > Jupiter –110°C > Saturn –140°C > Uranus –195°C > Neptune –200°C > Pluto –225°C.
- Earth's tropical year: **365 d 6 h 9 min 9.51 s**.

| Feature | Planet |

| Stays closest to Earth longest on average | **Mercury** (APPSC 2019) |

- Jupiter, Saturn: mostly **hydrogen and helium**; methane tints **Uranus/Neptune** blue-green (one key named Jupiter, Saturn and Mars for H, He, methane). Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune also have rings.

### 7. MOONS AND PLANETARY FEATURES
| Moon | Planet | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Titan** | Saturn | Largest moon of Saturn (5,150 km; Rhea 2nd); thick atmosphere; liquid **methane lakes** ~100 m deep (APPSC 2019); Cassini re-analysis: probably no global ocean, only warm pockets (~20°C) |
| **Ganymede** | Jupiter | **Largest moon** in the solar system, bigger than Mercury |
| **Europa** | Jupiter | Ocean under icy crust |
| Io, Callisto | Jupiter | Io most volcanically active |
| Enceladus | Saturn | Icy moon, habitability studies |
| **Phobos, Deimos** | Mars | Mars's two moons |
| **Charon** | Pluto | Largest of Pluto's 5 (Charon, Styx, Nix, Kerberos, Hydra) |
- Counts: Earth 1, Mars 2. **Saturn has the most** (274 after 128 confirmed in March 2025); March 2026 MPC/IAU added 15 (**11 Saturn, 4 Jupiter**): Saturn **285**, Jupiter **101** (Jupiter 79 in 2018 keys; 97 in Jan 2026).
- The Moon is a natural satellite, not a planet.
### 8. EARTH, MOON AND ORBIT TERMS
#### 8.1 Earth's Unique Features
- **Goldilocks (habitable) zone**: temperature "just right" for liquid water; Earth lies in it (APPSC 2019). Life needs right distance + liquid water + protective atmosphere.
- Earth-like planets: **K2-18b** (~124 ly, Leo; possible biomarker molecule); 27 circumbinary planets via **TESS**; PRL's **TOI-6038A b** via PARAS-2, Mount Abu.
| Sphere | Evolution |
|---|---|
| Lithosphere | **Differentiation**: heavy iron sank to core, light material formed crust |
| Atmosphere | H-He lost to solar wind → **degassing** (water vapour, N₂, CO₂) → **photosynthesis** added oxygen |
| Hydrosphere | Degassed vapour condensed → rain → oceans |
| Magnetic field | **Geodynamo** of liquid-iron outer core; shields from solar wind |
- 2026 study: some pole reversals lasted ~**70,000 years**, not 10,000.
#### 8.2 Perihelion vs Aphelion
| Basis | Perihelion | Aphelion |
|---|---|---|
| Date | ~**3 January** | ~**4 July** |
| Distance | ~147 million km | ~152 million km |
| Speed | Fastest | Slowest |
| N. Hemisphere | Winter | Summer |
- **Kepler's Second Law** (equal areas in equal times) explains the speed change. Seasons come from **axial tilt**, not distance.
#### 8.3 The Moon
- Diameter 3,475 km; mean distance 384,400 km (363,300–405,500); gravity **1/6** of Earth (weight falls to 1/6).
- Rotation = revolution = **27 d 7 h 43 min** (tidal locking) → same face. Synodic month **29.5 days**.
- **Perigee** (closest): supermoon, higher tides (bright full moon of 22 Dec 1999). **Apogee**: **micromoon**, 6–7% smaller, 10–14% dimmer.
- **Blue moon**: 2nd full moon in a calendar month (every 2–3 years). **Blue micromoon** in 2026 after 5+ years. Half moon: Sun–Earth–Moon angle **90°**.
| Basis | Solar eclipse | Lunar eclipse |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Moon between Sun and Earth | Earth between Sun and Moon |
| Phase | **New moon** (Amavasya) | **Full moon** |
| Feature | Corona, **diamond ring** (start/end of totality) | Reddish moon (blood moon, July 2018) |
- Totality is limited because the Moon's shadow is smaller than Earth. Longest annular eclipse of the millennium: **15 January 2010** (an old key wrote 17 Jan).
- **Apollo 11**: 20 July 1969, Sea of Tranquility; Armstrong and Aldrin walked, Collins orbited. **Blue Marble** image: Apollo 17.
!TRAP -helion = Sun (Earth's orbit); -gee = Earth (Moon's orbit).
### 9. ASTEROIDS, COMETS, METEORS AND DWARF PLANETS
| Basis | Asteroids | Comets |
|---|---|---|
| Made of | Rock and metal | Ice, frozen gases, dust, rock |
| Where | **Between Mars and Jupiter**; largest **Ceres** (~1,000 km) | Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud |
| Orbit | Less eccentric | **Highly elliptical** |
| Tail | Usually none | Coma and tail, **points away from the Sun** (radiation pressure + solar wind) |
- **Kuiper Belt** (30–50 AU, beyond Neptune) → short-period comets; **Oort Cloud** → long-period comets.
| Comet | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Halley's** | Short-period, ~76 yr; last 1986, next **2061** |
| **Hale-Bopp** | Long-period, ~2,534 yr; 1995; named after Alan Hale and Thomas Bopp |
| **Shoemaker-Levy 9** | Found 24 Mar 1993; hit Jupiter **16–22 July 1994** |
| **3I/ATLAS** | C/2025 N1; third interstellar object after 'Oumuamua (2017), Borisov (2019); hyperbolic orbit; NASA found water; **JWST found methane** (a first) |
- **Meteoroid** = body in space; **meteor** = bright streak (shooting star) burning up; **meteorite** = piece reaching ground; **bolide** = meteor exploding in air.
#### 9.1 Dwarf Planets
- **IAU 26th General Assembly (14–25 Aug 2006)**: planet must orbit the Sun, be nearly round, and **clear its orbit**. Pluto fails → dwarf planet; trigger **Eris**.
- Dwarf planets: **Ceres** (asteroid belt); **Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, Eris** (Kuiper belt). Pluto: Indian name **Yama**; 39 AU; 2,376 km.
!TRAP Eris is a dwarf planet, not a moon of Jupiter. Smallest planet: Pluto (old) vs Mercury (now).
