## U0589 | THE UNIVERSE AND THE SOLAR SYSTEM – ORIGIN THEORIES, THE SUN, PLANETS, ASTEROIDS, COMETS, METEORS
### 1. UNIVERSE – BASICS, AGES AND DISTANCE UNITS
- **Cosmology** = study of the origin, evolution and structure of the universe.
- Age of the universe: **13.8 billion years** (APPSC 2019). Age of the Earth and solar system: **4.6 billion years**.
- Most abundant element in the universe and the Sun: **hydrogen**, then **helium** (Sun ~74% H, ~24% He).
- **Milky Way** = **barred spiral galaxy** (disc, bulge, dark-matter halo).
- The Sun's gravity gives the **centripetal force** keeping planets in orbit. Planets are **non-luminous** (reflect sunlight); stars twinkle, planets do not.
#### 1.1 Distance Units
| Unit | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Astronomical unit (AU)** | Mean Earth–Sun distance | ~**149.6 million km** (149.598 × 10⁶ km) |
| **Light year** | Distance light travels in one year | **9.461 × 10¹² km** (~9.46 trillion km) |
| **Parsec** | Largest unit | **3.26 light years** |
- Sunlight takes **8 min 19 s** to reach Earth. Light years are used because the **speed of light is constant**.
!TRAP Order of size: AU < light year < parsec. A light year measures distance, not time.
### 2. ORIGIN THEORIES
| Theory | Proponent | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **Nebular** hypothesis | **Immanuel Kant**; revised by **Laplace** | 1755; 1796 |
| **Planetesimal** hypothesis | **Chamberlin and Moulton** | 1904 |
| **Tidal** hypothesis | **James Jeans and Harold Jeffreys** | – |
| Nova hypothesis | Hoyle and Lyttleton | – |
| Interstellar dust | **Otto Schmidt** | – |
| **Big Bang** (expanding universe) | **Georges Lemaitre** (1894–1966) | 1927 |
| **Steady State** | **Hoyle, Bondi and Gold** | 1948 |
- Big Bang proof: **red-shift** (**Hubble, 1929**) and **microwave background** radiation.
- Planets formed from one spinning disc: hot inner disc kept only metals and rocky minerals; ices stayed in the cool outer disc (giant planets).
!TRAP Lemaitre proposed the Big Bang (1927); Hubble only showed expansion (1929). Steady State = Gold and Bondi (with Hoyle).

### 4. STARS – LIFE CYCLE AND TERMS
#### 4.1 Life Cycle
- **Nebula** → **protostar** → **main sequence**; then low/medium mass (Sun): **red giant** → planetary nebula → **white dwarf**; massive: **supergiant** → **supernova** → **neutron star** or **black hole**.
- **Chandrasekhar limit** (~1.44 solar masses) = highest white-dwarf mass; above it the star collapses.
#### 4.2 Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nebulae** | Giant clouds of dust and gas |
| **Cepheids** | Brighten and dim; **period–luminosity** relation measures distance |
| **Pulsar** | Spinning **neutron star** from a collapsed massive star |
| **Black hole** | Remains of a large star's supernova; **no light** escapes (high density) |
| **Magnitude** | Lower number = brighter (logarithmic); **absolute magnitude** = brightness at **10 parsecs** |
- **Colour = surface temperature**: **blue** (hottest, 50,000–28,000 K) → white → **yellow** (Sun, 6,000–4,900 K) → orange → **red** (coolest).
- **Polaris** (**Ursa Minor**) is within 1° of the north celestial pole; **Vega** becomes pole star in ~**12,000 years**.
- Nearest star: the **Sun**; next: **Alpha Centauri** system.
- First direct image of another star's surface (Hubble): **Betelgeuse**. In space the sky is **black** and stars **do not twinkle**.
!TRAP Brittle Star is an echinoderm, not a space object.
### 5. THE SUN
- **99.8%** of the solar system's mass; diameter **109 × Earth's**; plasma core (an old key said "liquid").
- Energy: **nuclear fusion** of hydrogen into helium in the core (**~15 million °C**). Mean distance 1 AU.
#### 5.1 Layers (centre outward)
| Layer | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Fusion H → He; ~15 million °C |
| **Photosphere** | Visible surface above convection zone; **~5,500–6,000°C** (5,778 K) |
| **Chromosphere** | Reddish lower atmosphere, seen at eclipses |
| **Corona** | Seen in **total eclipse**; **over 1 million °C** |
#### 5.2 Solar Activity
| Phenomenon | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Sunspots** | Cooler dark magnetic patches; **~11-year cycle**; poles reverse at peak |
| **Solar flares** | Bursts from magnetic reconnection; classes **A, B, C, M, X** (X strongest) |
| **CMEs** | Coronal plasma (not rock), **250–3,000 km/s**, reach Earth in **1–3 days** |
| **Aurora** | **Borealis** (north), **Australis** (south) |
- Effects: geomagnetic storms, satellite and grid damage.
### 6. PLANETS – ORDER, DATA AND SUPERLATIVES
- **8 planets** (9 earlier). Inner: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Outer: Jupiter, Saturn (**gas giants**), Uranus, Neptune (**ice giants**).
- Between Mars and Uranus: **Jupiter and Saturn**.
#### 6.1 Planet Data
| Planet | Distance (million km) | Diameter (km) | Revolution | Density (g/cm³) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 57.9 | 4,879 | **88 days** | 5.4 |
| Venus | 108.2 | 12,104 | 224.7 days | 5.2 |
| Earth | 149.6 | 12,756 | 365.25 days | **5.5** |
| Mars | 228 | 6,792 | 687 days | 3.9 |
| Jupiter | 778.5 | **142,984** | 11.86 years | 1.3 |
| Saturn | 1,432 | 120,536 | 29.4 years | **0.7** |
| Uranus | 2,867 | 51,118 | 84.07 years | 1.3 |
| Neptune | 4,515 | 49,528 | **164.9 years** | 1.6 |
- Size: **Jupiter > Saturn > Uranus > Neptune > Earth > Venus > Mars > Mercury**.
- Period rises with distance; a smaller Earth orbit gives a **shorter** year. Tropical year: **365 d 6 h 9 min 9.51 s**.
#### 6.2 Superlatives
| Feature | Planet |
|---|---|
| Nearest Sun, smallest (now), fastest orbit | **Mercury** |
| Stays closest to Earth longest on average | **Mercury** (APPSC 2019) |
| Hottest (464°C), brightest, **Earth's twin**, Morning/Evening Star, nearest to Earth (41.4 million km) | **Venus** |
| Densest | **Earth** |
| **Red Planet**; day 24 h 37 min, tilt 25°12' near Earth's (23 h 56 min; 23°30'); ice caps, subsurface ice | **Mars** |
| Largest, heaviest, fastest spin | **Jupiter** |
| Least dense (less than water); famous **rings**; looks yellow | **Saturn** |
| Rotates on its side; lowest single temperature (~–224°C) | **Uranus** |
| Farthest, coldest average (–200°C), longest year | **Neptune** |
| Retrograde rotation | **Venus and Uranus** |
| No natural satellites | **Mercury and Venus** (APPSC 2019: Venus) |
- Jupiter, Saturn: mostly **hydrogen and helium** (one key: Jupiter, Saturn, Mars); **methane** tints Uranus/Neptune blue-green. Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune also have rings.
- Old keys: longest year = **Pluto** (247.9 yr); Neptune farthest **1979–1999**; nearest to Earth = Mars (78.4 million km). **Magellan** reached Venus orbit 10 Aug 1990 (launched 4 May 1989).
!TRAP Hottest = Venus, not Mercury. Coldest average = Neptune; lowest single reading = Uranus. Saturn is warmer than Neptune.
### 7. MOONS AND PLANETARY FEATURES
| Moon | Planet | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Titan** | Saturn | Saturn's largest (5,150 km; Rhea 2nd); thick atmosphere; **methane lakes** ~100 m deep (APPSC 2019) |
| **Ganymede** | Jupiter | **Largest moon** in the solar system, bigger than Mercury |
| **Europa** | Jupiter | Ocean under icy crust |
| **Phobos, Deimos** | Mars | Mars's two moons |

### 8. EARTH, MOON AND ORBIT TERMS
#### 8.1 Earth's Unique Features
- **Goldilocks (habitable) zone**: "just right" for liquid water; Earth lies in it (APPSC 2019).
- Earth-like **K2-18b**: possible biomarker molecule.
| Sphere | Evolution |
|---|---|
| Lithosphere | **Differentiation**: heavy iron sank to core, light material formed crust |
| Atmosphere | H-He lost to solar wind → **degassing** (water vapour, N₂, CO₂) → **photosynthesis** added oxygen |
| Hydrosphere | Degassed vapour condensed → rain → oceans |
| Magnetic field | **Geodynamo** of liquid-iron outer core; shields from solar wind |
- 2026 study: some pole reversals lasted ~**70,000 years**, not 10,000.
#### 8.2 Perihelion vs Aphelion
| Basis | Perihelion | Aphelion |
|---|---|---|
| Date | ~**3 January** | ~**4 July** |
| Distance | ~147 million km | ~152 million km |
| Speed | Fastest | Slowest |
| N. Hemisphere | Winter | Summer |
- **Kepler's Second Law** (equal areas in equal times). Seasons come from **tilt**, not distance.
#### 8.3 The Moon
- Mean distance 384,400 km; gravity **1/6** of Earth (weight falls to 1/6).
- Rotation = revolution = **27 d 7 h 43 min** (tidal locking) → same face. Synodic month **29.5 days**.
- **Perigee** (closest): supermoon, higher tides (bright full moon of 22 Dec 1999). **Apogee**: **micromoon**, 6–7% smaller, 10–14% dimmer.
- **Blue moon**: 2nd full moon in a month (every 2–3 years); **blue micromoon** in 2026 after 5+ years. Half moon: angle **90°**.
| Basis | Solar eclipse | Lunar eclipse |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Moon between Sun and Earth | Earth between Sun and Moon |
| Phase | **New moon** (Amavasya) | **Full moon** |
| Feature | Corona, **diamond ring** (start/end of totality) | Reddish moon (blood moon, July 2018) |
- Totality is limited: the Moon's shadow is smaller than Earth. Longest annular eclipse of the millennium: **15 January 2010** (keyed 17 Jan).
- **Apollo 11**: 20 July 1969; Armstrong and Aldrin walked, Collins orbited. **Blue Marble**: Apollo 17.
!TRAP -helion = Sun (Earth's orbit); -gee = Earth (Moon's orbit).
### 9. ASTEROIDS, COMETS, METEORS AND DWARF PLANETS
| Basis | Asteroids | Comets |
|---|---|---|
| Made of | Rock and metal | Ice, frozen gases, dust, rock |
| Where | **Mars–Jupiter**; largest **Ceres** (~1,000 km) | Kuiper Belt, Oort Cloud |
| Orbit | Less eccentric | **Highly elliptical** |
| Tail | Usually none | Coma and tail, **points away from the Sun** (radiation pressure + solar wind) |
- **Kuiper Belt** (30–50 AU, beyond Neptune) → short-period comets; **Oort Cloud** → long-period comets.
| Comet | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Halley's** | Short-period, ~76 yr; last 1986, next **2061** |
| **Hale-Bopp** | Long-period, ~2,534 yr; Alan Hale and Thomas Bopp |
| **Shoemaker-Levy 9** | Found 24 Mar 1993; hit Jupiter **16–22 July 1994** |
| **3I/ATLAS** | Third interstellar object; NASA found water; **JWST found methane** (a first) |
- **Meteoroid** = body in space; **meteor** = shooting star; **meteorite** = reaches ground; **bolide** = explodes in air.
#### 9.1 Dwarf Planets
- **IAU 26th General Assembly (14–25 Aug 2006)**: planet must orbit the Sun, be nearly round, and **clear its orbit**. Pluto fails → dwarf planet; trigger **Eris**.
- Dwarf planets: **Ceres** (asteroid belt); **Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, Eris** (Kuiper belt). Pluto: Indian name **Yama**; 2,376 km.
!TRAP Eris is a dwarf planet, not a moon of Jupiter. Smallest planet: Pluto (old) vs Mercury (now).
