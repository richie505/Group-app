## U0166 | DUAL GOVERNMENT IN BENGAL AND ITS CONSEQUENCES; THE BENGAL FAMINE OF 1770
### 1. DUAL GOVERNMENT (1765–1772)
- **Basis:** **Second Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** – **Shah Alam II** gave the **Diwani** of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; tribute **₹26 lakh**.
- **Architect:** **Robert Clive**, in his second term (**1765–67**).
| Diwani (with EIC) | Nizamat (with Nawab) |
|---|---|
| **Revenue** collection and finance (plus civil justice) | **Police, criminal justice**, general administration |
| **Real power without responsibility** | **Responsibility without power or funds** |
- Conflict between the two: local nobility sidelined; growing resentment.
- **Why "dual":** hide imperial ambitions; avoid **British Parliament's scrutiny**; keep other European companies paying duties to the "Nawab"; lack of British manpower. Verdict: **"a sham"**.
### 2. NAIB DIWANS
- The Company did not collect revenue itself at first; it worked through **Indian deputies**.
| Deputy | Post | Region |
|---|---|---|
| **Muhammad Reza Khan** | **Deputy (Naib) Diwan of Murshidabad**, appointed by Clive after Allahabad; also Naib Nazim | **Bengal** |
| **Raja Shitab Rai** | Naib Diwan | **Bihar (Patna)** |
- Both were removed and tried when the system ended (1772).
### 3. CONSEQUENCES
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Revenue extortion** | Heavy, exploitative collection ruined Bengal |
| **Weavers** | Forced to sell to Company agents at **low prices**; handicrafts declined |
| **Private trade** | Company servants misused the duty-free **dastak**; **Mir Qasim** had earlier **abolished internal duties for all** in protest |
| **Wealth drain** | Bengal's surplus revenue bought Indian goods for export = **R.P. Dutt's Mercantilist phase (1757–1813)** |
- In under two decades **Bengal, the richest province**, fell into acute poverty, famines and epidemics.
### 4. BENGAL FAMINE OF 1770
- Struck **Bengal and Bihar** (lower Gangetic plain), 1769–70; about **7–10 million** died, roughly **one-third** of the population.
- **Causes:** monsoon failure; **high land revenue demanded in cash** → peasants sold grain; grain exported.
- **Governor:** **John Cartier (1769–72)**.
- **Company response:** **little or no relief**; **revenue collection continued**.
!KEY Famine of 1770 is **directly linked** to the Dual Government: revenue squeezed as famine spread.
### 5. ABOLITION (1772) AND PARLIAMENTARY REACTION
- **Warren Hastings** abolished it in **1772** (Court of Directors' decision); the **Calcutta council** acted as Diwan.
- Direct collection by Company servants; **treasury Murshidabad → Calcutta**; tribute to Shah Alam II stopped.
- Company sank into debt and sought a **government loan (1772)** → Parliament intervened → **Regulating Act, 1773**.
- Later systems: Permanent Settlement (1793), Subsidiary Alliance (1798), Ryotwari.
### 6. SANYASI-FAKIR REVOLT AND ANANDAMATH
- **Sanyasi (Fakir) Revolt, 1763–1800:** causes – **1770 famine**, Company insensitivity, **curbs on pilgrims**.
- Leaders: **Majnum Shah, Bhawani Pathak, Devi Chaudhurani** (not Shah Alam II).
| Book | Author | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Anandamath (1882)** | **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** | Set in the 1770 famine / Sanyasi revolt; has **Vande Mataram** |
| **Durgeshnandini (1865)** | Bankim | Pathan–Mughal conflict in Akbar's reign |
| **Sitaram** | Bankim | His **last** novel |
- Bankim's birthplace **Naihati** (Rail Coach Factory and Museum, Rail Budget 2012-13); Bankim and **Jodu Nath Bose** were the **first graduates of Calcutta University**.

#### FIRST / LAST / SPECIAL FACTS
- First Governor of Bengal: **Clive (1758)**; last: **Hastings (1772–73)**; first Governor-General of Bengal: **Hastings (1773)**.
- First formal Company control of revenue in India.
- Andhra's Guntur (Dokkala) famine 1832–33 is separate.
