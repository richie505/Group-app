## U0517 | INTERNATIONAL TRADE - COMPOSITION AND DIRECTION OF INDIA'S EXPORTS AND IMPORTS; TRADE DEFICIT
### 1. BASIC TERMS OF FOREIGN TRADE
- **Exports**: goods and services sold to the rest of the world; **imports**: goods and services bought from it.
- **Net exports = X − M**: positive = **trade surplus** (exports > imports); negative = **trade deficit** (imports > exports).
- **Closed economy**: neither exports nor imports; an **open economy** has both.
- Payment for foreign trade is recorded in the **Balance of Payments**.
| Aspect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Volume** | Money value of exports and imports |
| **Composition** | Items traded |
| **Direction** | Country-wise / region-wise pattern |
- **Import substitution** = inward-looking strategy; **export promotion** = outward-looking strategy.
### 2. TRADE THEORY AND TRADE TERMS
#### 2.1 Theory
| Theory | Thinker | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute advantage** | **Adam Smith** | Produce a good at lower absolute cost than others |
| **Comparative advantage** | **David Ricardo** | Specialise in the good with lower **opportunity cost** |
- India's **services surplus vs structural merchandise deficit** = Ricardian comparative advantage in services.
!TRAP Adam Smith = absolute advantage; Ricardo = comparative advantage. Do not swap.
#### 2.2 Types of trade
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intra-industry trade** | A country imports and exports the **same product** (e.g. cars) - asked 2025 |
| Inter-industry trade | Exchange of different products (comparative advantage) |
| **Entrepot trade** | Import, store/trade and **re-export**, with or without processing/packaging ("goods imported for export") |
| **Dumping** | Exporting at a price below the home-market price or cost |
| **Free trade** | Little or no tariff and no quantitative restriction ("absence of tariff") |
#### 2.3 Trade barriers and remedies
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tariff** | Tax on imports |
| **Import quota** | **Quantitative restriction** on the amount of imports |
| **Import licensing** | Administrative restriction on imports |
| **Anti-dumping duty** | Protects against goods exported below normal / home-market value |
| **Countervailing duty** | Offsets **foreign subsidies** (e.g. US preliminary CVD of 125.87% on an Indian product, Mar 2026) |
| **Rules of origin** | Decide the country of origin; decide which goods get **FTA concessions** |
- India used the **most anti-dumping measures in 1995-2005**.
!PAIR Anti-dumping duty | price below normal value || Countervailing duty | foreign subsidy
### 3. TERMS OF TRADE AND EXCHANGE RATE
#### 3.1 Terms of trade
| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Net (NTT)** | Export price index / import price index × 100 (revised base, Mar 2026) |
| **Gross (GTT)** | Import quantity / export quantity |
| **Income (ITT)** | NTT × export quantity index |
#### 3.2 Devaluation and trade
- **Devaluation/depreciation**: exports become **cheaper abroad**, imports **dearer at home** → exports rise, imports fall.
- **J-curve**: after depreciation the trade balance **first worsens** (import bills rise at once), then improves as volumes adjust.
- **Marshall-Lerner condition**: devaluation improves the trade balance only if export + import demand price elasticities **sum to more than 1**.
- 16 Sep 2026: the weak rupee was credited in part for the Aug 2026 export surge.
- **1991 New Trade Policy**: import substitution and QRs → **price-based regime**; **two-step rupee devaluation (July 1991)**.
!KEY Higher duties on exports would NOT improve the BoP; import substitution, rupee devaluation and higher import tariffs would.

### 6. DIRECTION OF TRADE
#### 6.1 Partners 2024-25
| Export destination | Share | Import source | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USA** | **19.77%** | **China** | **15.73%** |
| UAE | 8.37% | Russia | 8.85% |
| Netherlands | 5.2% | UAE | 8.79% |
| China | 3.26% | Saudi Arabia | 4.18% |
- **Surplus** with **USA** and **Netherlands**; **deficit** with **China, Russia, UAE, Saudi Arabia**.
- **USA = largest trade surplus** 2019-20, 2020-21 to 2022-23; **$35.32 bn** in 2024-25. USA = largest importer of Indian leather.
- Labour-intensive exports moved from the US towards the Middle East, Africa, Europe and Asia; crude sources diversified.
#### 6.2 Largest partner by period
| Period | Largest partner / fact |
|---|---|
| 2006-07; 2019-20; 2023-24; 2024-25 | **USA** largest trading partner (then China, UAE) |
| 2008-09 / 2009-10 | Asia and ASEAN largest partner region and import share |
| 2009-10 | UAE largest export share |
| 2011 | China and UAE largest share of India's trade |
| 2003-04 | China largest buyer of India's steel (23%) |
| **FY 2025-26** | **China largest overall trading partner** ($151.1 bn), overtaking USA; **USA still largest export market** |
- Older answers: USA = maximum volume of trade; **OPEC** = largest import trade (question period).
#### 6.3 China
- Goods deficit over **$100 bn** (Jun 2026); calendar 2025 trade $155.62 bn, deficit $116.12 bn; H1 2026 imports a record **$79.41 bn** (15 Jul 2026).
- From China: **APIs, solar PV cells, semiconductor chips**, telecom machinery. To China: **iron ore, cotton**, chemicals, marine products.
!TRAP India does NOT import iron ore from China; it exports iron ore, including to China.
- Responses to China dependence: **QCOs, NTBs, PLI**.
- **Nathu La** (Sikkim-Tibet): closed after 1959, reopened **2006** for trade with China.
#### 6.4 BRICS and GCC
- India-BRICS trade **$203.1 bn (FY21) → $417.5 bn (FY26)**; deficit $226.1 bn; imports from China $131.6 bn; largest BRICS export market **UAE ($37.4 bn)** (10 Sep 2026).
- **GCC** formed **1981**: Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE.
### 7. TRADE DEFICIT - SIZE, HISTORY AND CAUSES
#### 7.1 Record since independence
- **Trade surplus only twice: 1972-73 and 1976-77.**
- Deficit highest in **2012-13 (US$ 195.66 bn)**, fell to **2016-17 (US$ 112.44 bn)** as both POL and non-POL deficits fell.
#### 7.2 Size
| Item (2024-25) | Exports | Imports |
|---|---|---|
| Merchandise | **$437.70 bn** | **$721.20 bn** |
| Goods + services | $825.25 bn | $919.92 bn |
| Period | Deficit |
|---|---|
| FY25 merchandise | **$283.5 bn (+17.6%)**, Economic Survey 2025-26; RBI BoP basis $286.9 bn |
| FY26 merchandise | **$337.3 bn** (RBI BoP); Q4 alone $83.4 bn (oil, gold) |
| Oct 2025 | $21.8 bn, after US 50% tariffs cut exports to the US (−20.4% in Sep 2025) |
| Aug 2026 | Total deficit **$9.4 bn** (vs $11.6 bn); total exports $82.7 bn (+25.4%), imports $92.1 bn |
- Aug 2026: goods exports **+26.1% to $43.8 bn**, goods imports $70.7 bn; exports grew more than imports in absolute value (16 Sep 2026). May 2026 goods exports a record **$45.2 bn**.
#### 7.3 Causes since 1991
1. **Oil and gold** imports (gold imports **+82%**, April 2026).
2. South-East Asian crisis 1997-98.
3. Competition from **China**; **non-tariff barriers** of partners.
4. **Import liberalisation**: capital goods, APIs, electronic components.
5. Rising imports from China and FTA partners.
!TRAP "Lacklustre services" is NOT a cause - services were in surplus (PYQ 2017).
- **Twin deficit hypothesis**: rising fiscal deficit goes with rising trade deficit.

### 9. AP ANGLE - EXPORTS AND PORTS
- AP's top exports: **marine products**, pharma, insecticides, automobile components, chemical products, cement, barytes, under-garments.
- **Visakhapatnam Port** handled **50.87%** of India's marine (seafood) exports in **2024-25** (up from 29.67% in 2023-24).
- Kandla (first EPZ) was later followed by Santacruz, Falta and **Visakhapatnam** EPZs.
### 10. EXPORT PROMOTION - SEZs, BODIES AND FOREIGN TRADE POLICY
#### 10.1 EPZ to SEZ
| Milestone | Detail |
|---|---|
| **First EPZ** | **Kandla (Gujarat), 1965**; later Santacruz, Falta, Visakhapatnam; converted to SEZs after 2000 |
| SEZ concept | **EXIM Policy 1997-2002** (PYQ key: EXIM Policy 2000-01) |
| SEZ Policy | Announced **1 April 2000** |
| **SEZ Act** | **2005**; SEZ Rules **2006** |
| **Agri Export Zones** | Nodal agency **APEDA** |
| First Agriculture Export Facilitation Centre | **Pune, May 2021** |
- SEZ = duty-free enclave treated as **foreign territory** for trade and tariffs; simple procedures, business-friendly environment.
- SEZ aims: **export competitiveness**, **FDI**, internationally competitive hassle-free environment, level playing field, jobs, infrastructure.
#### 10.2 Bodies
| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Department of Commerce** | Frames and monitors FTP |
| **APEDA** | Agri exports; AEZs (under Department of Commerce) |
| **MPEDA** | Marine products |
| EXIM Bank | Lines of Credit, buyer's/seller's credit, project-export finance |
- **Agriculture Export Policy 2018**: aimed to double agri exports.
#### 10.3 Foreign Trade Policies
| Policy | Target / feature |
|---|---|
| FTP 2004-09 (Aug 2004) | Double India's share of world merchandise trade; **1.5%** by 2009 |
| FTP 2015-20 | Raise share of world exports **2% → 3.5%**; services share **4.2% by 2022** (PYQ 2019) |
| **FTP 2023** | From **1 April 2023**, **no end date**; **$2 trillion exports by 2030** |
- FTP 2023 pillars: duty-free input schemes, **Districts as Export Hubs**, logistics, better FTA use.
- **RoDTEP** replaced the WTO-challenged **MEIS** (FTP 2015-20).
- **RBI rupee trade settlement**: via **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts** to push the rupee in cross-border trade (APPSC G2 2025).

- **NAMA-11** (WTO coalition on industrial tariffs): India, Argentina, Brazil, Egypt, Indonesia, Namibia, Philippines, South Africa, Tunisia, Venezuela.
- **WTO MC13**: Abu Dhabi (2024). **MC14**: **Yaounde, Cameroon (26-30 Mar 2026)** - no declaration; the e-commerce customs-duty moratorium lapsed (30 Mar 2026).
