## U0609 | ELEMENTS AND FACTORS OF CLIMATE; WORLD CLIMATIC CLASSIFICATION (KOEPPEN)
### 1. WEATHER, CLIMATE AND ELEMENTS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Weather** | State of the atmosphere at a particular place and time |
| **Climate** | Average weather over a long period; **WMO normal = 30 years** |
| **Climograph** | Monthly temperature as a **line** + rainfall as **bars**; used to read the climate type |
| **Biome** | Large natural community of flora and fauna over a major habitat, shaped by climate |
| **Anthromes** | Human-made biomes shaped by sustained human use: **cropland**, villages, cities |
| Elements (measured conditions) | Factors / controls |
|---|---|
| Temperature, temperature range | **Latitude** (main factor) |
| Atmospheric pressure | Altitude |
| Wind | Distance from the sea (continentality) |
| Humidity, **cloudiness** | Pressure and wind belts, jet streams |
| Precipitation, sunshine | Ocean currents, relief, vegetation / land cover |
!TRAP **Cloudiness** is an element, not a control. **Distance from a river** is NOT a control of climate (**APPSC GS 2025**).
### 2. CONTROLS OF CLIMATE – HOW EACH OPERATES
| Control | How it works | Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| **Latitude** | Insolation falls towards the poles | Mainland **8°4′–37°6′ N**; south of the Tropic of Cancer = tropical |
| **Altitude** | Normal lapse rate about **6.5°C per 1,000 m** | **Shimla** (7,866 ft) cooler than **Amritsar** (756 ft), same latitude |
| Distance from sea | Coasts equable; interiors extreme (continental) | A&N Islands equable, humid; monsoon arrives first |
| Relief / mountain barriers | Windward wet, leeward rain-shadow | **Himalayas** keep north India **3–8°C warmer** in winter; without them north India would tend to be a desert |
| Ocean currents | **Cold currents** cool and dry tropical west coasts; warm currents make coasts warmer, wetter | – |
| Winds, pressure belts | Monsoons, westerlies, western disturbances, cyclones | – |
- **Tropic of Cancer crosses 8 states**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram (Mahi river crossed twice).
- India has a **tropical monsoon** climate though it does not lie wholly within the tropics. IMD SW monsoon normal (Jun–Sep, 1971–2020) = **868.6 mm**.
- At the **winter solstice**, pressure belts shift **south**.
### 3. KOEPPEN AND THORNTHWAITE CLASSIFICATION
- **Wladimir Koeppen**: empirical scheme on **mean temperature and precipitation**, linked to **natural vegetation**.
- **Thornthwaite (1931, revised 1948)**: **precipitation effectiveness** and **thermal efficiency** (potential evapotranspiration).
| Letter | Group | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Tropical humid | Coldest month above **18°C** |
| **B** | Dry | Evaporation > precipitation (BS = steppe, BW = desert) |
| **C** | Warm temperate | Coldest month between **–3 and 18°C** |
| **D** | Cold snow-forest | Coldest month below **–3°C** |
| **E** | Polar | Warmest month below **10°C** (ET tundra, EF ice cap) |
| **H** | Highland | Controlled by **altitude** |
- Second letters: **f** = no dry season; **m** = monsoon; **w** = dry winter; **s** = dry summer.
#### 3.1 Koeppen Codes in India
| Code | Type | Area |
|---|---|---|
| **Amw** | Monsoon with short dry season | **West coast south of Goa** |
| **As** | Dry summer, winter rain | **Coromandel coast (Tamil Nadu)** |
| **Aw** | Tropical savanna | Most of the Peninsular plateau; **Andhra Pradesh** |
| **BShw** | Semi-arid steppe | North-west India |
| **BWhw** | Hot desert | Extreme western Rajasthan |
| **Cwg** | Humid subtropical, dry winter | **Ganga plain**, E Rajasthan, N MP, NE India incl. North Bengal |
| **Dfc** | Cold humid winter | **Arunachal Pradesh** |
| E | Polar | High Himalaya |
!KEY **APPSC-G1 2024** match: Amw–west coast; As–Coromandel; Cwg–Ganga plain; BShw–NW India.
- **AP**: between **12°41′ N and 19°07′ N**; predominantly **Aw**; four climatic regions: humid coastal, deltaic, semi-arid **Rayalaseema**, hill micro-climates (Araku, Lambasingi, Horsley Hills). AP has **six agro-climatic zones** (older books: seven); India has **15**.
### 4. WORLD CLIMATIC REGIONS
| Region | Location | Climate | Vegetation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equatorial (selvas)** | 0–10° N/S: Amazon, Congo, Indonesia, SE Asia | Hot, wet all year; mean ~27°C; annual range only **2–3°C**; afternoon convectional thunder rain; no seasons | Evergreen hardwoods (**mahogany, ebony**), multi-layered canopy, epiphytes, lianas |
| **Tropical monsoon** | India, SE Asia, N Australia | Wind reversal; summer rain, 100–200 cm | **Teak, sal**; shed leaves in dry season |
| **Savanna (Sudan type)** | Sudan belt, E Africa, Llanos, Campos | Wet summer, dry winter; 500–2,000 mm; no month below 20°C | Tall grass, scattered trees; "**Land of Big Game**" |
| **Hot desert** | **15–30°**, western margins | Rain **below 25 cm**; large daily range | Xerophytes |
| **Mediterranean** | **30–45°**, western margins | **Winter rain** | Evergreen oak, olive, cork, shrubs |
| **China type** | Central/S China, **SE USA**, S Brazil, E Australia (east margins) | Rain all year, more in **summer** | – |
| **British / Marine West Coast** | NW Europe, British Columbia, S Chile, New Zealand (40–65°, westerlies) | Mild; rain all months, 50–250 cm; low ranges | Oak, beech, maple, birch |
| **Taiga** | N North America, Eurasia (NH only) | Long severe winters, **very large annual range** | Conifers |
| **Tundra** | Arctic fringe; alpine above tree line | Permafrost | Mosses, lichens; no trees |
- Order equator to poles: equatorial → tropical monsoon → temperate deciduous → tundra. Zaire to Netherlands: equatorial → hot desert → Mediterranean → West European.
- **Asia** has all climatic zones (Equator to North Pole). **Western Europe**, **Norway**: rain in all months (westerlies).
- **Ivory Coast**: dense hot humid forest (Gulf of Guinea). **Ethiopia** is NOT equatorial; Congo, Gabon, Zaire lie on the Equator.
#### 4.1 Identify the Climate from a Description
| Description | Climate |
|---|---|
| Range 2–3°C, rain every month | Equatorial |
| Hot wet summer, long dry winter | Savanna / monsoon |
| Very hot, large daily range, rain < 25 cm | Hot desert |
| Warm dry summer, mild wet winter | Mediterranean |
| Long severe winter, very large annual range | Taiga |
### 5. MEDITERRANEAN CLIMATE AND HOT DESERTS
- **Mediterranean**: hot dry summers (subtropical high, dry offshore winds), mild wet winters (**westerly depressions**). Cause: **seasonal shift of pressure and wind belts**. Economy: citrus, grapes, wine ("**orchard lands**"); olive most typical; also chestnuts, walnuts.
| Region | Towns |
|---|---|
| Mediterranean basin | Rome, Athens |
| California | Los Angeles, San Francisco |
| Central Chile | **Santiago** |
| SW Cape | **Cape Town** |
| SW and S Australia | **Perth**, Adelaide |
!TRAP NOT Mediterranean: **Darwin**, **New York**, **Northern Australia**. NOT Mediterranean features: dry winter, no dry season, severe winter. **Teak** is not a Mediterranean tree.
- **Hot deserts** (15–30°, western margins; deserts most likely near 23°): subtropical high + offshore trade winds + cold currents. Sahara, Kalahari, Namib, Atacama, Arabian, Thar, Great Australian.
- **Temperate deserts**: Patagonia, Gobi, Ladakh.
### 6. FORESTS AND BIOMES
| Forest | Climate | Vegetation | Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical rainforest (selva)** | Over **200 cm** rain; hot, humid | Broadleaf evergreen, not shed together; climbers, epiphytes | Amazon, Congo, Indonesia; 10°N–10°S |
| **Tropical deciduous (monsoon)** | **100–200 cm** seasonal rain | Shed leaves in dry season | India, Myanmar, Africa |
| Temperate deciduous | Distinct seasons; between tropics and polar (**APPSC G2 2025**) | Broadleaf, shed in **autumn** | E USA, W Europe, E Asia |
| **Taiga (boreal)** | Cold, long winters | Needle-leaved evergreen softwood, conical | Canada, Scandinavia, Siberia, Alaska |
| Mediterranean | Dry summer, wet winter | Evergreen shrubs, oaks, cork | Med basin, California, Chile, SW Australia, Cape |
| **Mangrove (tidal)** | Saline coasts | Salt-tolerant, **pneumatophores** (roots breathe in waterlogged mud) | Sundarbans; tropical/subtropical coasts; stabilise shoreline |
- **Rainforest**: highest biodiversity (over half of known species); "**lungs of the planet**" (Amazon); **leaching** is the main soil problem; soils nutrient-poor; litter decomposes fastest, ground often bare; cleared land loses fertility after **2–3 crops**. **Congo Basin** = 2nd-largest rainforest (Cameroon, CAR, DR Congo, Rep. Congo, Eq. Guinea, Gabon).
- **Taiga**: **largest land biome**; **very wide temperature range** (not narrow; **APPSC G1 2023, 2024**); moderate precipitation; acidic **podzol**; lowest productivity of forests; spruce, fir, pine; northernmost temperate biome.
- Deciduous trees shed leaves to cut transpiration.
- **Tree line**: altitude/latitude beyond which trees cannot grow (**APPSC GS 2019**); alpine tundra above it. Lower in Western Himalaya (3,300–3,600 m) than Eastern (3,600–3,800 m).

### 8. CLIMATIC EXTREMES
| Record | Place |
|---|---|
| **Highest air temperature** | **Death Valley** (Furnace Creek), California, **56.7°C, 1913**; WMO rejected **Al Aziziya**; lowest point in N America (–86 m) |
| Hottest land surface | **Dasht-e-Lut** (Iran), 70.7°C (NASA Aqua, 2005) |
| **Lowest recorded** | **Vostok**, Antarctica, **–89.2°C** (21 July 1983) |
| Cold pole (inhabited) | **Verkhoyansk –67.8°C (1892)**; **Oymyakon –67.7°C (1933)** |
| **Driest** | **Atacama** (N Chile, S Peru, west of Andes; nitrate, copper); McMurdo Dry Valleys driest polar |
| **Wettest** | **Mawsynram** (mean 1,187 cm); with Cherrapunji over 1,100 cm (funnelling + orographic lift) |
| Largest hot desert | **Sahara** (~3.6 million sq miles) |
| Largest desert | **Antarctica** |
| India highest / lowest | **Ganganagar** ~50°C (1935) / **Dras** –45°C (1908) |
- 2025 (WMO): oceans held **over 90%** of excess heat; ~90% of ocean surface had marine heatwaves.
- Bakhira Lake (UP) sediments: about **25,000-year** record of monsoon. About 40 coastal districts may warm over 1°C by 2040.
