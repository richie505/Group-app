## U0157 | DANISH AND OTHER MINOR EUROPEAN SETTLEMENTS (TRANQUEBAR, SERAMPORE)
### 1. EUROPEAN POWERS AND THEIR SETTLEMENTS
#### 1.1 Order of Arrival
- Order in India: **Portuguese → Dutch → British → Danish → French**.
- Portuguese came **first (1498)** and left **last (1961)**; the **French** came **last (1664)**.
- In **Andhra** the order differs: the French were the **third** power to arrive there.
#### 1.2 Settlement → Power
| Settlement | European power |
|---|---|
| Pondicherry | French (HQ; founded 1673-74 by **Francois Martin**) |
| Chandernagore | French |
| Goa, Daman, Diu | Portuguese (taken by India in **1961**) |
| Tranquebar (Tharangambadi) | **Danish** |
| Serampore | **Danish** |
| Chinsura (Chinsurah) | Dutch |
| Sadras | Dutch |
!TRAP Tranquebar and Serampore were **Danish**, not Dutch; Chinsura and Sadras are the Dutch ones.
### 2. DANISH EAST INDIA COMPANY
#### 2.1 Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1616** | Danish East India Company founded: Denmark enters Indian trade |
| **1620** | **Tranquebar** (Tharangambadi), near **Tanjore** on the **Coromandel** coast: first Danish settlement |
| 1636 | Danish factory at **Balasore** (textiles, saltpetre) |
| **1699** | **Serampore** near Calcutta: second major settlement, centre of Danish activity in Bengal |
| **1706** | **Danish-Halle Mission** at Tranquebar |
| **1845** | Serampore and Tranquebar **sold to the British** |
#### 2.2 Danish Settlements → Present State
| Danish settlement | Present location |
|---|---|
| Tranquebar / Tharangambadi | **Tamil Nadu** |
| Serampore (Srirampur) | **West Bengal** (near Kolkata) |
| Balasore | **Odisha** |
### 3. SALE OF DANISH SETTLEMENTS (1845)
- Denmark, in **financial difficulty**, sold Serampore and Tranquebar to the **British** in **1845**.
- Governor-General at the time: **Hardinge I**. This ended the Danish presence in India.
### 4. TRANQUEBAR MISSION – ZIEGENBALG AND SCHULZ
#### 4.1 Missionaries and Contributions
| Missionary | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Bartholomaeus Ziegenbalg** (1682-1719) | German **pietist**; founded the **Danish-Halle Mission** (1706); worked in **Tamil**; translated the Bible (New Testament) into **Tamil** |
| **Benjamin Schulz** (Schultze) | German Lutheran of the Danish-Halle Mission; **first to translate the Bible into Telugu**; Telugu grammar + dictionary |
| **Subbarayudu** (Anandarao) | Helped with the Telugu Bible; mission school in **Visakhapatnam** |
- Danish-Halle Mission = **earliest lasting Protestant mission in South India** (Danish-German).
- Schulz learnt Telugu from **1719** (Madras, Cuddalore, Pulicat); translation begun **1727**; published at **Halle** (not Serampore) in **1746-47**.
- Order: Danish company (1616) → Schulz begins (1727) → published (1746-47).
- Ziegenbalg's method (learn the language, vernacular Bible, schools) became the template for later Andhra missions: **Lutheran – Guntur**; **Baptist – Ongole**.
!KEY APPSC G2 2019: Telugu Bible, 1746-47 = **Benjamin Schulz**.
### 5. SERAMPORE MISSION
- **Serampore Trio** (Baptists): **William Carey, Joshua Marshman, William Ward**.
- Worked from Danish Serampore to **bypass early East India Company bans** on missionaries.
| Work | Detail |
|---|---|
| Serampore Mission Press | Baptist press in Danish Serampore |
| Telugu Bible (1807-1810) | First Telugu Bible **printed in India** |
| Serampore College | Founded **1818** |
!TRAP The press was the Baptist mission press in Danish Serampore, not "Dutch".
### 6. OSTEND AND SWEDISH COMPANIES
| Company | Year | Presence |
|---|---|---|
| **Ostend Company** (Austrian Netherlands) | Chartered **1722** | Banquibazar (Bengal), Covelong near Madras; suspended within a decade under British-Dutch pressure |
| **Swedish** East India Company | **1731** | Traded mainly with **China**; negligible in India |
- **Jesuits**: Catholic, linked with the Portuguese.
### 7. LEGACY
- Danish legacy = **educational and linguistic**, not commercial; trade impact negligible.
- AP angle: start of printed **Telugu prose** (Schulz's Bible and Serampore Telugu printing, 1807-10).
