## U0549 | FISCAL POLICY - OBJECTIVES, INSTRUMENTS; FISCAL, REVENUE, PRIMARY AND EFFECTIVE REVENUE DEFICITS
### 1. FISCAL POLICY - MEANING AND EVOLUTION
- **Fiscal policy** = government policy on its **revenue (taxation) and expenditure**, plus **public debt**, to shape aggregate demand, allocation and distribution.
- Word from Latin **fiscus** = basket / public purse.
- Made by the **Ministry of Finance**; Union Budget prepared by the **Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**.
- **Article 112** calls it the **"Annual Financial Statement"**; the word **"Budget" is not in the Constitution**. The budget is itself an instrument of fiscal policy.
#### 1.1 Persons and FIRSTs
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **J.M. Keynes** | **General Theory (1936)**; fiscal policy as a tool against the Great Depression; active demand management |
| **V.P. Singh** | **Long-Term Fiscal Policy**, laid in Parliament on 19 Dec 1985 (Budget year **1985-86**) as Finance Minister |
| **Richard Musgrave** | Three budget functions; popularised **fiscal federalism** (1959) |
| **N.K. Singh** | Committee 2016, report 2017: general govt debt **60%** = Centre 40% + States 20% |
### 2. OBJECTIVES AND FUNCTIONS (MUSGRAVE)
| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Allocation** | Provide **public goods**; the **primary purpose of a budget** |
| **Distribution** | Reduce inequality through **progressive taxation** and welfare spending |
| **Stabilisation** | Control **inflation and unemployment**; smooth the cycle |
- Objectives: **full employment, price stability, economic growth, equitable distribution**, resource mobilisation, **balanced regional development**.
- Progressive taxation evidence: direct taxes = **58.8%** of total taxes (FY25); ITRs 6.9 crore (FY22) to 9.2 crore (FY25).
#### 2.1 What is NOT a Fiscal Objective
- **Directing interest rates**, managing **liquidity and money supply** = monetary policy objectives.
- **Regulation of inter-state trade** - not a fiscal objective.
- **Equitable distribution of income and assets** - NOT a monetary policy objective (it is fiscal).
!TRAP Components (taxation, expenditure, debt) and objectives (growth, employment, price stability, equity) are different things; the objectives do not explain the components.
### 3. INSTRUMENTS - FISCAL VS MONETARY POLICY
- Fiscal tools: **taxation**, **public expenditure** (capex as **pump priming**), **public borrowing/debt**, the **budget**, subsidies/DBT, PLI, transfer payments, deficit financing.
- Public borrowing: **internal** (bonds, T-Bills, small savings) and **external** (multilateral, sovereign bonds); external borrowing carries **currency risk**.
- Public expenditure (Centre + States) ≈ **28% of GDP** (ES 2016-17); Union alone **13.6%** (BE 2026-27).
| Policy | Authority | Tools | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiscal | Govt (MoF) | Taxation, expenditure, public debt, subsidies, DBT, PLI | Slow (budget cycle) |
| Monetary | **RBI / MPC** | **Repo**, CRR, SLR, OMOs, LAF | Fast (MPC **bi-monthly**) |
- Monetary policy is **complementary** to fiscal policy.
!TRAP **Interest rate / repo rate** = monetary instrument, NOT a fiscal tool.
### 4. BUDGET TYPES, RECEIPTS AND EXPENDITURE
#### 4.1 Budget Types
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Balanced | Receipts = expenditure |
| Surplus | Receipts > expenditure |
| Deficit | Expenditure > receipts |
| Zero-based | Every item justified afresh, no base from last year |
| Performance | Spending linked to work done |
| **Outcome** | Outlays against measurable results; first **2005-06** |
| **Gender** | First Gender Budget Statement with Union Budget **2005-06** |
#### 4.2 Receipts
| Receipt | Types |
|---|---|
| Revenue | **Tax** and **non-tax** (e.g. PSU dividends) |
| Capital - non-debt | **Recovery of loans**, **disinvestment**, asset monetisation |
| Capital - debt | Market borrowings, small savings, other liabilities |
- Budget 2026-27: **borrowings and other liabilities = 24 paise** of every rupee - largest single receipt item.
#### 4.3 Expenditure
- **Revenue vs capital**: capital creates assets or reduces liabilities.
- **Plan vs non-plan**: distinction **ended in Budget 2017-18** (Rail Budget merged the same year).
- Developmental (growth, social services) vs non-developmental (interest, defence, administration).
- **Charged (Art. 112(3))** - not voted by Parliament: President's emoluments, **debt charges**, salaries of **Supreme Court judges**. Rest is **voted**.
### 5. DEFICIT DEFINITIONS AND FORMULAS
| Deficit | Formula | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue (RD)** | Revenue expenditure - revenue receipts | Borrowing for day-to-day consumption (dissaving) |
| **Effective RD (ERD)** | RD - grants for creation of capital assets | True consumption deficit (Rangarajan concept) |
| **Fiscal (FD)** | Total expenditure - (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts incl. recovery of loans) | **Total borrowing requirement** |
| **Primary (PD)** | FD - interest payments | Current imbalance, excluding past debt |
| Budget (old) | Total expenditure - total receipts **including borrowings** | Ad hoc T-bill era |
| **Monetised** | Part financed by RBI (**high-powered money**) | Inflationary |
| Gross primary | GFD - interest payments | - |
- **Fiscal deficit = budget deficit + borrowings and other liabilities** (market borrowings). FD always equals borrowings and other liabilities.
- **GFD** = total borrowing requirement; formula starts with **net borrowing at home**. Net FD = gross FD - net lending of the Centre.
- **ERD** introduced in **Union Budget 2011-12**; added to FRBM by the **2012 amendment**. ERD shows capital receipts used for consumption.
- Budget deficit stopped being the headline measure after ad hoc T-bills ended (1997).
!TRAP Budget deficit is NOT FD minus interest; that is the primary deficit. PD + interest = FD.
### 6. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN DEFICITS AND CALCULATION
- **FD > PD** always (positive interest). **FD > RD** always in practice. **ERD ≤ RD** (never greater).
- **FD > budgetary deficit**: it includes borrowings from RBI plus other liabilities.
- Largest deficit in the GoI budget = **fiscal deficit**. Descending order: **fiscal > revenue > primary**.
- 2026-27 BE: FD 4.3 > RD 1.5 > PD 0.7 (% GDP).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Zero primary deficit** | FD = interest; borrowing only to pay interest - **debt-trap signal** |
| Debt trap | Borrowing to make interest payments |
| Zero RD | Balanced revenue budget |
| Primary surplus | Non-interest spending covered by non-debt receipts |
#### 6.1 Worked Examples
- FD ₹1,50,000 crore - interest ₹1,20,000 crore = **PD ₹30,000 crore**.
- Gross primary deficit: ₹50,000 - ₹1,500 = **₹48,500 crore** (non-debt capital receipts not deducted again).
- FD ₹4 crore on GDP ₹400 crore = **1% of GDP**.
### 7. DEFICIT FINANCING AND FINANCING THE FD
- **Deficit financing (India)**: drawing down **cash balances**, **borrowing from RBI**, **issuing new currency**. RBI money-printing = **most inflationary** route; public borrowing absorbs liquidity.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ad hoc T-bills** | Automatic monetisation from the **mid-1950s**; **1994** RBI-Government agreement; abolished from **1 April 1997** (ended 31 March 1997) |
| **WMA** | Temporary RBI advances for short receipt-payment gaps, from 1 April 1997 |
| **Overdraft** | WMA used beyond the limit |
- From **2006-07** RBI barred from subscribing to **primary issues** of G-Secs, except under escape clause.
- Sources of FD finance: **market borrowings**, **small savings/NSSF**, **provident funds**, **external loans**; indirect bank borrowing via **SLR**.
- **Domestic borrowings** fill most of the FD (about **98-99%**).
- **8th Plan** public outlay ₹4,34,100 crore - largest source **government borrowings (46.6%)**.
- External liabilities in the Budget at **current exchange rates**, not historical; high borrowing kept real interest rates high.
!TRAP **PSU dividends** are **non-tax revenue**, NOT a source of financing the fiscal deficit.
### 8. FISCAL STANCE, CROWDING OUT, MULTIPLIER, TWIN DEFICIT
| Stance | Action | Use / risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Expansionary** | More spending / lower taxes | **Recession**; inflation risk |
| Contractionary | Less spending / higher taxes | Overheating |
| Neutral | Balanced effect | Stable times |
- **Counter-cyclical**: deficits in slumps (**Atma Nirbhar Bharat, 2020**), consolidation in booms. Pro-cyclical deepens volatility.
- **Recession**: increase spending on **public projects**; fiscal stimulus = **cutting tax rates**, **more spending** - abolishing subsidies is NOT.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Crowding out** | Govt borrowing raises **interest rates**, **private investment falls** |
| Crowding in | Public capex draws private investment |
| **Fiscal multiplier** | Change in GDP ÷ change in govt spending or taxes |
| Capex multiplier | **2.5-3** in India - **largest for capital expenditure** |
- **Quality of deficit**: high **RD/FD** ratio = borrowing funds consumption; 2026-27 RD 1.5 / FD 4.3 ≈ one-third.
- **Twin deficit** = FD + **CAD**. Higher FD widens CAD: dissaving cuts national savings, widens the savings-investment gap. Best CAD financing = **FDI**; FPI unstable.
### 9. FISCAL RULES AND DEFICIT TRENDS
| Rule | Target |
|---|---|
| **FRBM Act 2003** | FD **3%**, RD eliminated; amended **2018** |
| FRBM 2003 did NOT | Stipulate elimination of primary deficit by 2008-09 |
| FY 2021-22 path | FD below **4.5% by 2025-26** - met at 4.4% |
| From 2026-27 | **Debt-GDP** replaces FD as anchor; **50 ± 1% by March 2031**; central debt 55.6% (BE 2026-27) |
| EU Stability and Growth Pact (1997) | Deficits below 3% of GDP |

- Controlled double-digit inflation (early 1990s) by containing the budgetary deficit and unproductive spending.
#### 9.2 AP Angle
- AP BE 2026-27: **RD 1.11%** (₹22,002 crore), **FD 3.84%** (₹75,868 crore) of GSDP; 2025-26 RE: RD 2.33%, FD 4.57%. AP still runs a **primary deficit**.
- 18 States above 3% FD in FY24; 10 States slipped into revenue deficit between FY19 and FY25.
### 10. SUBSIDIES AND MEASURES TO REDUCE THE DEFICIT
- Major heads: **food, fertiliser, petroleum (LPG)** - forex reserves are not a subsidy head. Total subsidies ≈ **8.5%** of Union expenditure (2026-27).
- **Urea** = largest fertiliser subsidy item. **Fertiliser DBT (2018)**: paid to companies after retail sale on **PoS machines** with **Aadhaar** authentication.
- **Hidden subsidies**: **tax concessions, cheap credit**, under-priced health and education.
- Financial subsidy is a big source of budget deficit (true); "higher in Indian agriculture than developed countries" - false.
- Q1 FY27 major subsidies up **37.4%** on crude prices; may overshoot by ~₹50,000 crore.
- Deficit cut: spending side = **rationalising subsidies through DBT**, cutting unproductive spending, **downsizing bureaucracy**; receipts side = widening tax base, **disinvestment** (selling PSU shares). FDI or privatising higher education is not a measure.
