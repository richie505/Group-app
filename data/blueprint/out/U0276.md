## U0276 | SOURCES OF THE INDIAN CONSTITUTION – BORROWED FEATURES AND THEIR ORIGIN COUNTRIES
### 1. A "BAG OF BORROWINGS" – OVERVIEW
- "**Drawn from various sources**" is a listed salient feature: most of the world's constitutions + the **GoI Act 1935** → the **lengthiest written constitution** in the world.
- Original: **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules** → now about **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules**.
- **Philosophical part** (FRs, DPSPs) and **political part** (parliamentary system) borrowed; **structure** largely from the 1935 Act.
- Criticisms: **"bag of borrowings"**, **"elephantine"** (length), **"lawyers' paradise"** (legal detail).
- Reply: features adapted to Indian conditions; Ambedkar called DPSP a **"novel feature"**; federal-unitary switch is India's own.
- The **USA** has the world's **first written Constitution**, also the **shortest** (only **7 Articles**).
- The **Constitution** is the highest authority in the Indian political system.
!KEY Answer key view: **British Constitution** had the most profound influence (parliamentary government, cabinet, rule of law); the GoI Act 1935 is the single largest **textual** source.
### 2. GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT 1935 – THE SINGLE LARGEST SOURCE
- Structural and administrative backbone:
  1. **Federal scheme** (federal system largely adopted from the 1935 Act; division of powers)
  2. **Office of Governor**
  3. **Judiciary** (Federal Court as model; integrated judiciary)
  4. **Public Service Commissions**
  5. **Emergency provisions**
  6. **Administrative details**
- 1935 Act's **Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists** → Constitution's **Union, State and Concurrent Lists**.
- Its administrative detail is a reason for the Constitution's **bulk**.
- The 1935 Act did **NOT** provide for a written Constitution made by Indians; that demand was accepted in principle in the **August Offer, 1940**.
### 3. PARLIAMENTARY FEATURES – BRITAIN
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| **Parliamentary government**, conventions of **cabinet system** | Britain |
| **Single citizenship** (USA has dual) | Britain (APPSC GS 2019) |
| **Bicameralism** | Britain (APPSC GS 2024) |
| **Rule of Law**, legislative procedure | Britain |
| Office of **Speaker** and his role | Britain |
| **Prerogative writs** | Britain |
| **Parliamentary privileges** | Britain |
| Nominal head (President like the Crown) | Britain |
- Britain has **no single written constitution**; India borrowed the system and its conventions.
!TRAP "Which feature is from Britain?" – cabinet system, single citizenship, bicameralism. Distractors: USA, Canada.
### 4. RIGHTS AND JUDICIAL FEATURES – USA
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| **Fundamental Rights** (Part III, Arts. 12-35) | USA |
| **Judicial review** | USA |
| **Independence of judiciary** | USA |
| **Idea of the Preamble** | USA |
| Post of **Vice-President** | USA |
| **Impeachment of the President** | USA |
| **Removal of SC/HC judges** | USA |
- Judicial review in India is **narrower**: Art. 21 uses "procedure established by law", not "due process".
- **Integrated judiciary**: one chain of courts enforces both Central and State laws; the **USA** has separate federal and state courts.
- FR chapter from the USA is **not** the reason for the Constitution's length.
### 5. FEDERAL FEATURES – CANADA AND AUSTRALIA
#### 5.1 Canada
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| **Federation with a strong Centre**; "**Union of States**" with a strong Centre (Art. 1) | Canada |
| **Residuary powers with the Centre** (Art. 248 – Parliament) | Canada |
| **Appointment of Governors** by the Centre | Canada |
| **Advisory jurisdiction** of the SC (**Art. 143**) | Canada |
- Indian federalism is based on the **Canadian** pattern; "Union of States": not the result of an agreement, no right to secede.
- In the USA and Australia, residuary powers are with the **States**.
#### 5.2 Australia
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| **Concurrent List** | Australia |
| **Joint sitting** of both Houses (**Art. 108**, presided by the **Lok Sabha Speaker**) | Australia |
| **Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse** | Australia |
| **Language of the Preamble** | Australia |
- Makers followed the Canadian scheme of division but **added a third list** – the Concurrent List.
- Concurrent List: Parliament and State Legislatures both legislate; **Union law prevails** in a clash.
!PAIR Residuary powers | Canada | not USA || Concurrent List | Australia | not Canada
### 6. DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES AND ELECTIONS – IRELAND AND SOUTH AFRICA
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| **Directive Principles** (Part IV) | **Ireland** (Ireland took them from **Spain**) |
| **Method of electing the President** | **Ireland** |
| **Nomination** of members to Rajya Sabha | **Ireland** |
| **Procedure for amendment** (**Art. 368**) | **South Africa** |
| **Election of Rajya Sabha members** | **South Africa** |
!TRAP Election of RS members = South Africa; nomination to RS + President's election = Ireland. Amendment procedure is NOT from Ireland or France.
### 7. EMERGENCY, JAPAN, USSR AND FRANCE
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| **Emergency provisions** (general scheme) | **GoI Act 1935** (APPSC G2 2023 key) |
| **Suspension of FRs during Emergency** | **Weimar Germany** |
| **Procedure established by law** (Art. 21) | **Japan** |
| **Fundamental Duties** (Art. 51A) | **USSR** |
| Ideals of **social, economic and political justice** (Preamble) | **USSR** |
| Five-Year Plans / Planning Commission | USSR |
| **Republic**; **liberty, equality, fraternity** (Preamble) | **France** |
- Fundamental Duties: **not** in the original Constitution; added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**; 11th duty by the **86th Amendment**.
- In a National Emergency, FRs can be suspended **except Articles 20 and 21** (Art. 20 – protection in respect of conviction; Art. 21 – life and personal liberty); **Art. 19** is the most easily suspended.
!TRAP When a list gives "Emergency provisions except Financial Emergency – Weimar Germany" and no 1935 option, it is the accepted match; when both appear, Emergency = 1935 Act, suspension of FRs = Weimar.
!TRAP Fundamental Duties = USSR (not France); justice ideals = USSR (not Australia) – two separate USSR ideas.
