## U0209 | HOME RULE MOVEMENT – TILAK AND ANNIE BESANT; IMPACT
### 1. BACKGROUND
- Home Rule = a **popular movement during the First World War**, launched in **1916** by **Tilak** and **Annie Besant**; demanded **self-government**.
- **16 June 1914:** Tilak released from **Mandalay** jail after **6 years**.
- Besant's self-government campaign began in **1915**, but her **League** came only in **September 1916**.
- It **filled the gap** between **Gokhale's death (1915)** and **Gandhi's rise (1920)**.
!TRAP APPSC 2024: "Tilak founded the Home Rule League in 1914" is **FALSE** – he was just released in 1914; his League came in **April 1916**.
### 2. THE TWO HOME RULE LEAGUES
#### 2.1 Comparison
| Feature | Tilak's **Indian Home Rule League** | Besant's **All India Home Rule League** |
|---|---|---|
| Founded | **28 April 1916** | **September 1916** |
| Place | **Belgaum** (Bombay Provincial Conference); based at **Poona** | **Madras**; HQ **Adyar (Gokhale Hall)** |
| Area | **Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, Berar** | **Rest of India, including Bombay city**; about **200 branches** |
| Office-bearers | President **Joseph Baptista**; Secretary **N.C. Kelkar** | Organising secretary **George Arundale**; Madras League: Gen. Secy **C.P. Ramaswamy Aiyar**, Treasurer **B.P. Wadia** |
| Strength | Better organised | Reached a wider audience |
- **S. Subramania Iyer** was also associated with Home Rule.
- **Jinnah** popularised the movement in **UP**.
- Allied bodies of Besant's League: **Young Men's India Association**, **Boy Scouts Association**, **Order of Sons of India**.
!TRAP **H.S. Olcott** was **not** in the Home Rule League; he belongs to the **Theosophical Society**.
!TRAP Tilak's area excluded **Bombay city**; Besant's area included it.
#### 2.2 Fate of the League
- Tilak's League **merged into** Besant's All India Home Rule League.
- **1920:** under **Gandhi as President**, renamed **Swarajya Sabha**; soon **merged into the Congress**.
### 3. BAL GANGADHAR TILAK
- Born **23 July 1856, Ratnagiri**.
| Title | Origin |
|---|---|
| **Lokmanya** | Popular title ("beloved of the people") |
| **Father of Indian Unrest** | British journalist **Valentine Chirol** |
- Papers: **Kesari (Marathi)** and **Mahratta (English)**, both **1881**.
- Accused the Congress of the politics of "**pray, petition and protest**".
### 4. ANNIE BESANT
#### 4.1 Life and titles
- Born **1 October 1847**, **Clapham, London** (Irish origin); **Anglo-Irish nationalist**; called the "**Iron Lady of Ireland**".
- In England, an early member of the **Fabian Society** – backer of **Fabianism** (British gradual, reformist socialism).
#### 4.2 Theosophy and institutions
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1889** | Joined the **Theosophical Society** |
| 1893 | Came to India |
| **1898** | **Central Hindu College, Varanasi** – later the core of **BHU** under **Madan Mohan Malaviya** |
| **1907** | **President of the Theosophical Society** |
| Jan 1914 | **The Commonweal** (English weekly, Madras; editor Besant) |
| July 1914 | Bought the **Madras Standard** → renamed **New India** (a daily) |
| **1917** | **First woman President of the INC** (Calcutta) |
| 1925 | **Commonwealth of India Bill** |
- Also founded the **National College, Madanapalle**.
- Protégé: **Jiddu Krishnamurti** – wrote **At the Feet of the Master**.
- **Commonwealth of India Bill** (also **Dominion of India Bill**): her draft dominion constitution; proposed **dominion status** and a **parliamentary system**.
- **1918:** visited **Bihar (Patna)** (18 April, 25 July) to strengthen the League.
!KEY APPSC GS 2019: TS President **1907**, first woman INC president **1917**, Central Hindu College **1898** – all correct.

### 8. LUCKNOW SESSION AND PACT (DECEMBER 1916)
#### 8.1 Reunion of Moderates and Extremists
- The **Surat split (1907)** was healed at **Lucknow, 1916**.
- Made possible by **Tilak and Besant's** efforts and the **deaths of Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta (1915)**.
- The Lucknow Congress was where **Home Rule supporters showed their political strength**.
#### 8.2 Lucknow Pact – terms
| Term | Detail |
|---|---|
| Electorate question | **Congress accepted separate electorates** for Muslims – what it had opposed in 1909 |
| Goal | Self-government |
| Negotiators | **Tilak and Jinnah** |
- Tilak's "two united fists" remark was made on this Pact.
### 9. IMPACT – BESANT'S INTERNMENT AND THE AUGUST DECLARATION
#### 9.1 Besant's internment (1917)
- **15 June 1917:** Besant interned at **Ootacamund** with **B.P. Wadia** and **G.S. Arundale**.
- Effect: public anger; strengthened the movement; **drew in the Moderates**.
- **17 September 1917:** released under Congress-League pressure; then **INC President (Calcutta, 1917)**.
#### 9.2 Montagu (August) Declaration
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Date | **20 August 1917** |
| By | **Edwin Montagu**, **Secretary of State** (Lord **Chelmsford** = Viceroy) |
| Objective | Increasing Indian association in administration; "**progressive realisation of responsible government**" through self-governing institutions |
| Effect | British answer to Home Rule; blunted the agitation → **1919 Act** |
- Split over the Mont-Ford proposals: **Congress special session, Bombay (Aug 1918)** under **Hasan Imam**.
!TRAP Montagu = **Secretary of State**, not Chelmsford (Viceroy).
#### 9.3 Importance
- Built **village organisation**; trained workers for later **Gandhian** movements.
- Shifted the movement from elite to masses; linked town and country → prepared ground for Gandhi.
- **Moderates-Extremists unity:** the Surat rift closed at Lucknow (1916), and Besant's internment (1917) pushed even Moderates into the agitation.
- **Congress-League unity:** 1916 was the high point; Congress accepted at Lucknow the separate electorates it had opposed under the **1909 Act**.
- **Spread:** Tilak's League covered Maharashtra, Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar; Besant's about 200 branches covered the rest of India; Jinnah carried it to UP; in Andhra it used Telugu pamphlets and libraries as lecture venues.
- **Limit:** the August Declaration blunted and effectively ended the agitation; the Mont-Ford proposals split the Congress at the Bombay special session (Aug 1918).
### 10. HOME RULE IN ANDHRA
#### 10.1 Branches and leaders
- **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** – **Secretary of the Andhra branch**; led Home Rule branches in Andhra.
- Branches: **Guntur, Vijayawada, Kakinada**; **52 branches** by **1917** – one of the strongest Home Rule regions.
- Leaders: **Konda Venkatappayya, Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao, K.V. Reddy Naidu, Pattabhi Sitaramayya**.
- Propaganda: Telugu pamphlets (**Swarajya Paramoddesham**); libraries used as lecture venues.
- Gadicherla translated Sarojini Naidu's *Awake Mother* as **Nuthana Haindava Matrigeetham**.
#### 10.2 Supporting newspapers
| Paper | Editor |
|---|---|
| **Andhra Mata** | Strongly backed Home Rule (APPSC G2 2017) |
| **Desamata** | **Chilakamarti** |
| **Krishna Patrika** | **Mutnuri Krishna Rao** |
#### 10.3 Besant's tour and after
- After Lucknow (1916), Besant toured **Kakinada, Rajahmundry, Eluru**; organised by **Mocherla Hanumantha Rao**.
- On her arrest: "**Confinement Day**" protests at **Tenali** and **Vijayawada**.
- **20 December 1917:** Andhra delegation under **Nyapati Subba Rao** met **Montagu** for a **separate Andhra province**.
