## U0460 | HUMAN DEVELOPMENT INDEX – COMPONENTS, METHODOLOGY, INDIA'S RANK AND TREND
### 1. HUMAN DEVELOPMENT – CONCEPT AND ORIGIN
#### 1.1 The Idea
- **People and their capabilities**, not growth alone, are the ultimate criteria of development.
- Human development approach: focuses on **people, opportunities and choices**; broader than an economic measure.
- **UNDP** was the first to focus on the human aspect of development – **first HDR, 1990**, which had an HDI.
- HDI developed by a team led by **Mahbub ul Haq** (with **Amartya Sen**; team incl. **Inge Kaul**).
- HDR is published by **UNDP**, released most years since 1990; it compares countries on **education, health and per-capita income** (APPSC-GS 2025 key: "Human Development Report").
- UNDP's two categories of poverty: **income poverty** and **human poverty** (both).
- **Minimum Needs Programme** = same as investment in the human approach.
#### 1.2 Persons → Contribution
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Amartya Sen** | **Capability approach** (*Development as Freedom*): development = expanding real freedoms; co-conceived HDI |
| **Martha Nussbaum** | Extended Sen's approach with a list of **central human capabilities** (*Women and Human Development*) |
| **Mahbub ul Haq** | Conceived the HDI; led the first HDR team |
#### 1.3 GDP vs Human Development
| Basis | GDP / per-capita income | Human development |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Economic measure | Broader, people-centred |
| Focus | Income and output | Capabilities and well-being |
| Dimensions | One-dimensional | Multidimensional |
- Income (GNI) is a **means**, not the end; **higher GNI per capita does not guarantee a higher HDI**.
- **Same GNI, different HDI** shows the role of **national policy choices** (APPSC-G1 2024).
- Example: **Malaysia** has higher GNI per capita than **Chile**, but Chile has ~**7 years** more life expectancy and **3** more years of expected schooling → much higher HDI.
!KEY GNI cannot replace HDI – income is a means; HDI looks at what income buys in health and knowledge.
### 2. DIMENSIONS, INDICATORS AND GOALPOSTS
| Dimension | Indicator | Minimum | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long and healthy life** | **Life expectancy at birth** | **20** | **85** |
| **Knowledge (education)** | **Expected years of schooling** | 0 | **18** |
| **Knowledge (education)** | **Mean years of schooling** | 0 | **15** |
| **Decent standard of living** | **GNI per capita (PPP $)**, log | $100 | $75,000 |
- HDI = composite of **life expectancy, educational attainment, per capita GNI**; max value **1**, min **0**.
- **Before 2010**, per capita **GDP** was used; GNI and the two schooling indicators came with the **2010 revision**.
#### 2.1 What HDI Leaves Out (odd-one-out)
- NOT in HDI: **condition of environment**, **urbanisation**, **health and nutrition** (as a separate item), human security, inequality, poverty, gender gaps, empowerment, participation, MPI.
!TRAP HDI indicators = income level, education, life expectancy – NOT environmental quality. "Health and nutrition" is not a constituent.
### 3. METHODOLOGY
1. **Dimension index** = (actual − minimum) / (maximum − minimum). Example: actual 50, min 20, max 80 → (50−20)/(80−20) = **0.50**.
2. **Education index** = **arithmetic mean** of expected-years and mean-years sub-indices.
3. **Income**: uses the **logarithm** of GNI per capita → **diminishing returns** to income.
4. **HDI** = **geometric mean** of the three dimension indices (**since 2010**) – a strong dimension cannot fully offset a weak one.
- **PPP dollars** allow fair cross-country comparison across price levels (based on ICP surveys; goalposts first used 2011 PPP).
!TRAP Education index = arithmetic mean; HDI = geometric mean. Expected schooling max 18; mean schooling max 15.
### 4. HDI CATEGORIES AND INDIA'S SCORE

### 7. RELATED INDICES
| Index | Introduced (HDR) | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **GDI** | **1995** | **Ratio of female HDI to male HDI** |
| **Capability Poverty Measure** | **1996** | Lack of basic capabilities |
| **Human Poverty Index** (HPI-1 developing, HPI-2 developed) | **1997** | Deprivation; **replaced by MPI in 2010** |
| **IHDI** | **2010** | HDI discounted for inequality |
| **GII** | **2010** | Reproductive health, empowerment, labour market |
| **MPI** | **2010** | Overlapping deprivations; **OPHI + UNDP** |
| **PHDI** | **2020** | HDI adjusted for **CO2 emissions and material footprint** |
- **IHDI vs HDI**: no inequality → HDI = IHDI; **IHDI < HDI whenever inequality exists**; HDI = "potential", IHDI = "actual"; the gap = loss.
- GDI reading: a ratio near 1 = gender parity.
!TRAP GDI = ratio (1995); GII = inequality (2010). HPI 1997, replaced by MPI 2010.

### 8. MULTIDIMENSIONAL POVERTY – GLOBAL AND NATIONAL
#### 8.1 Global MPI vs National MPI
| Feature | Global MPI | National MPI |
|---|---|---|
| By | **OPHI + UNDP** (2010) | **NITI Aayog** |
| Method | **Alkire-Foster** | Alkire-Foster |
| Dimensions | Health, education, living standards | Same, equally weighted |
| Indicators | **10** | **12** = 10 + **maternal health** + **bank account** |
- **MPI = Headcount (H) × Intensity (A)**; intensity = average deprivation share among the poor.
#### 8.2 National MPI Trend (NITI, Progress Review 2023)
- Headcount: **24.85% (2015-16, NFHS-4) → 14.96% (2019-21, NFHS-5)**; MPI value **0.117 → 0.066**.
- **13.5 crore** people escaped poverty; rural **32.59% → 19.28%**.
- Highest: **Bihar** (51.89% → 33.76%, largest fall), then **Jharkhand** (28.81%). Lowest: **Kerala**, Goa, Delhi, Punjab.
- Longer trend: **55.3% (2005-06) → 14.96% (2019-21) → est. 11.28% (2022-23)**.
- Government now leans on MPI over income poverty lines.
#### 8.3 Income Poverty and Relief
- Income poverty 2011-12 (Planning Commission): **AP 9.20%** (rural 10.96%, urban 5.81%) vs **India 21.92%**.
- RBI update of **Rangarajan** line (HCES 2022-23): rural highest **Chhattisgarh (25.1%)**, lowest **Himachal Pradesh (0.4%)**; urban lowest **Tamil Nadu (1.9%)** (Oct 2025).
- **PMGKAY**: free foodgrains (5 kg per person per month; AAY families 35 kg) to about **81.35 crore**; extended **five years from 1 January 2024**.
- **Kerala**: moving to **extreme-poverty-free** status.

### 11. INCLUSIVE GROWTH AND HUMAN CAPITAL
- Three pillars: **Market Economics, Empowerment, Pragmatism** (APPSC 2025).
- **11th Plan (2007-12)**: "Towards Faster and More Inclusive Growth" – first to make inclusive growth its objective.
- Instruments: **SHGs** (women's credit and livelihood); financial inclusion – **RBI FI-Index 67.0 (2025)**, up from 53.9 (2021). WEF IDI: India 62nd of 74 emerging economies.
- **Human capital formation**: investment in **education, health, skills (on-the-job training), migration and information**; gives future returns because people become more productive.
- Poverty falls with education: **35.1%** with no formal education poor vs **14.9%** with post-secondary (Jun 2025).
