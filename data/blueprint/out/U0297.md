## U0297 | DOCTRINES OF CONSTITUTIONAL INTERPRETATION
### 1. FEDERAL DOCTRINES – MEANING AND FIELD
| Doctrine | Meaning | Field |
|---|---|---|
| **Pith and substance** | Judge a law by its **true nature**; incidental trespass is fine | Union–State lists |
| **Colourable legislation** | What cannot be done **directly** cannot be done **indirectly**; "fraud on the Constitution" | **Competence, not motive** |
| **Territorial nexus** | State law valid outside the State if there is a **sufficient nexus** | Extra-territorial operation |
| **Harmonious construction** | Read conflicting provisions so **both operate** | FR vs DPSP; provision vs provision |
| **Incidental and ancillary** | An entry covers matters incidental to it; entries get the **widest meaning** | Entries |
| **Parens patriae** | State as legal protector of citizens unable to protect themselves | Welfare |
- Reconciling Central and Provincial laws = **pith and substance** (APPSC G2 2017).
- True character is read from the **whole Act, its object and its effect**, not just its title.
### 2. LEADING CASES
| Case | Doctrine | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **State of Bombay v F.N. Balsara (1951)** | Pith and substance | Prohibition Act upheld as public health though it touched imports |
| **A.S. Krishna (1957)** | True character | Whole Act, object, effect |
| **State of Bombay v R.M.D.C. (1957)** | Territorial nexus | State tax on prize competitions run from outside |
| **Sri Venkataramana Devaru (1958)** | Harmonious construction | Temple entry **Art. 25(2)(b)** reconciled with **Art. 26(b)** |
| R.D. Joshi v Ajit Mills; Calcutta Gas | Incidental and ancillary | Widest meaning of entries |
| **K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo (1953)** | Colourable legislation | Leading case |
!TRAP 1957 = R.M.D.C. (nexus) and A.S. Krishna (true character); 1951 = Balsara (pith and substance).
### 3. FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS DOCTRINES vs FEDERAL DOCTRINES
| FR doctrine (Art. 13) | Meaning | Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Eclipse** | Pre-Constitution law conflicting with FRs is overshadowed, not void | Bhikaji Narain (1955) |
| **Severability** | Only the invalid part goes if the rest is complete | R.M.D.C. (1957) |
| **Waiver** | FRs **cannot be waived** | Basheshar Nath (1959) |
- Federal doctrines: pith and substance, colourable legislation, territorial nexus, incidental and ancillary, repugnancy.
### 4. REPUGNANCY – ARTICLE 254
- **Concurrent List**: Parliament's law prevails over a repugnant State law.
- **State List**: State law prevails in its field; **Art. 254(2)** applies only to Concurrent List matters.
- Art. 254(2): State law with **President's assent** prevails in that State, but **Parliament may later override** it.
### 5. JUDICIAL AND EXECUTIVE DOCTRINES
| Doctrine | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **Prospective overruling** | First applied in **Golaknath (1967)** |
| **Stare decisis** | **Art. 141** – SC's law binds all courts |
| **Pleasure** | **Art. 310**, safeguards in **Art. 311** against dismissal, removal, reduction in rank |
| **Separation of powers** | **Functional, not rigid** (Ram Jawaya Kapur, 1955); basic structure (Kesavananda) |

### 7. CONSTITUTIONAL MORALITY AND TRANSFORMATIVE CONSTITUTIONALISM
- **Constitutional morality** = fidelity to constitutional values over popular morality; phrase used by **Ambedkar, after Grote**.
| Case | Doctrine used |
|---|---|
| **Navtej Johar (2018)** | Both |
| **Sabarimala (2018)** | Both |
| **NCT Delhi (2018)** | Constitutional morality only |
| **Joseph Shine (2018)** | Transformative constitutionalism only |
- Criticism: subjective; a route to **judicial overreach**.
- Significance: keeps federal lawmaking workable and sets how FRs operate against laws.
