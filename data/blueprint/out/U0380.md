## U0380 | FAMILY – TYPES, FUNCTIONS AND CHANGING STRUCTURE IN INDIA
### 1. FAMILY AS AN INSTITUTION
- The **basic, primary** social institution; the child's **first school** (language, customs, values, norms).
- **Murdock** definition: common residence, economic co-operation, reproduction.
- Gives caste, religious, language and **national identity** (nationality = national, not language, identity).
- Approves sex relations (prevents disorder); passes on rituals; protects from risks.
- **Dysfunction** = repeated failure of functions (abuse, alcoholism).
### 2. TYPES OF FAMILY
| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Structure | nuclear (conjugal); joint/extended |
| Residence | patrilocal, matrilocal, **neo-local** (separate new home), bilocal, **duo-local** (each spouse in own natal home) |
| Descent / authority | patrilineal, **matrilineal** (mother's line) / patriarchal, matriarchal |
| Mates | monogamous; polygamous (polygyny, polyandry) |
- **Orientation** = born into; **procreation** = set up after marriage.
### 3. FUNCTIONS BY SCHOLAR
| Scholar | No. | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **Murdock** | 4 universal | sexual regulation, reproduction, economic cooperation, education (socialisation) |
| **Ogburn** | 7 | incl. status-giving, affectional; all except **affection** pass outside: "**loss of functions**" |
| **Talcott Parsons** | 2 | primary socialisation; stabilisation of adult personality |
- Inheritance = passing on property (consumption is separate).

### 5. CHANGING STRUCTURE AND URBAN FAMILIES
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Industrialisation | **production → consumption unit**; youth economically independent |
| Urbanisation, migration | limited housing → joint families break up |
| Women's employment | more female-headed households |
- **T.K. Oommen**: urban families by livelihood, authority structure, social milieu, value pattern (not only size); proprietary class = joint, strong elder-male authority.
- Jun 2026: AP CM **N. Chandrababu Naidu** urged a joint-family return and incentives for 2+ children.

| Community | Region | Feature |

### 8. HIGH-RISK FAMILIES
- Face severe social, economic, health or psychological challenges.
- Causes: economic (poverty, debt, job insecurity); social (breakdown, migration, **exclusion**); psychological (stress, addiction).
- Characteristics: substance abuse, single-parent households, conflict.
- Single-parent/female-headed rise: widowhood, marital instability, male migration.
- **Elderly care** rests on filial piety, strained by nuclearisation and migration.
