## U0298 | SEPARATION OF POWERS AND CHECKS AND BALANCES
### 1. CONSTITUTIONAL GOVERNMENT
- **Constitutional government = limited government**, limited by the terms of the Constitution (UPSC 2020, 2021); it defines, restrains and checks power to stop **executive tyranny**.
- Best safeguard of liberty in a liberal democracy = **separation of powers** (UPSC 2021).
- **Constitutionalism**: limits + rule of law + checks and balances + judicial review + FRs.
- A **majoritarian** position cannot override constitutionalism.
- **PIL** widens access to justice for the poor but is **not indispensable** for the rule of law, which existed before PIL via **Arts. 14, 32, 226**.
### 2. MONTESQUIEU AND THE INDIAN DEGREE
- **Montesquieu**: division of powers best protects liberty; "**The Spirit of Laws**" (**1748**); drew on the British constitution.
- Parliamentary system = **fusion**: executive drawn from and accountable to the legislature; ministers must be legislators. Presidential = separation.
- **Nominal head** President; **real executive** Council of Ministers.
- **Ambedkar** preferred **responsibility over stability**; reasons: familiarity (1919/1935 Acts), diversity, avoid American-style conflict.
- India: **no rigid separation**; implicit, not absolute; overlapping functions → checks and balances.
- **Art. 50 (DPSP)**: separate judiciary from executive in public services. Judiciary insulated: **Arts. 50, 121, 211, 235**.
- AP angle: **Madras Presidency** separated the Collector's judicial powers in **1816**.
### 3. LEGISLATURE OVER EXECUTIVE
- In India the **executive is subordinate to the legislature** (BPSC 2001).
| Device | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Question Hour, Zero Hour | Daily watch |
| **No-confidence motion** | Removes government – **Lok Sabha only** |
| Impeachment (**Art. 61**) | Removes President |
| Collective responsibility | **Art. 75(3)** Lok Sabha; **164(2)** Assembly |

### 5. JUDICIAL CHECKS
- **Judicial review** of laws and executive action: **Arts. 13, 32, 226**; ultra vires law is void.
- **Art. 121**: no discussion in Parliament on an SC/HC judge's conduct, except on a removal motion; **Art. 211** for State Legislatures.
- **Arts. 122, 212**: courts cannot question legislative proceedings for irregular procedure.
- **Art. 142** complete justice: Vishaka guidelines before **POSH Act, 2013**; scope undefined, overreach risk.
!TRAP 121/211 = legislature cannot discuss judges; 122/212 = courts cannot question procedure.
