## U0596 | EARTH MOVEMENTS – CONTINENTAL DRIFT, SEA-FLOOR SPREADING, PLATE TECTONICS AND PLATE BOUNDARIES
### 1. THEORIES IN ORDER
| Theory | Proponent / year | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Continental drift | **Alfred Wegener** (German), **1912** (APPSC 2018) | **Pole-fleeing force** (Earth's rotation) + **tidal force** (Sun and Moon) |
| Convection current | **Arthur Holmes**, 1930s | Radioactive decay heat in mantle → currents; rising limbs split crust (ridges), sinking limbs pull plates (trenches) |
| Sea-floor spreading | **Harry Hess**, early 1960s (1961) | Magma rises at mid-ocean ridges → new crust spreads → destroyed at trenches |
| Palaeomagnetic stripes | Vine and Matthews, 1963 | Symmetrical normal/reversed bands either side of ridge |
| Plate tectonics | **1967**, McKenzie and Parker; separately Morgan | Rigid lithosphere plates on semi-fluid **asthenosphere**; driven by mantle convection |
- Wegener's forces were **too weak** → theory rejected. Holmes gave drift the **driving force** Wegener lacked.
- Plate tectonics is **based on sea-floor spreading**; it was **not** given by H.H. Hess alone in 1967 – developed in the 1960s by Wilson, Morgan, Parker, McKenzie, Hess and others.
!KEY 1912 Wegener → 1930s Holmes → 1961 Hess → 1967 plate tectonics.
### 2. PANGAEA, LAURASIA AND GONDWANALAND
- Original single landmass: **Pangaea** (Carboniferous), surrounded by mega-ocean **Panthalassa** (named by Wegener).
- Split into **Laurasia** (north) and **Gondwanaland** (south), separated by the **Tethys Sea**.
| Name | Present landmasses |
|---|---|
| Laurasia | North America, Greenland, Europe, Asia (minus India) |
| Gondwanaland | South America, Africa, **India**, Australia, Antarctica, Madagascar, Arabia |
!TRAP India = Gondwanaland (APPSC 2018), not Laurasia. North America is NOT part of Gondwanaland.
### 3. EVIDENCES OF CONTINENTAL DRIFT
| Evidence | Example |
|---|---|
| Jig-saw fit | South America and Africa (best at continental-shelf edge); Bullard's 1964 computer fit |
| Rock belts | ~2,000-million-year belt of **Brazil coast** matches **West Africa** |
| Placer deposits | **Ghana** coast gold has no local source; source rock in **Brazil plateau** |
| Tillite | Glacial deposits of India have counterparts in southern continents |
| Fossils | **Mesosaurus** (South Africa and Brazil – not India and Australia); Glossopteris; lemurs ("Lemuria") |
- UPSC 2025: rock belts, placer gold and the third evidence – **all correct**.
### 4. SEA-FLOOR SPREADING
- Rocks record the magnetic field of their time (**palaeomagnetism**).
- Away from the ridge: **age, density, thickness** of oceanic crust and sediment cover **increase** (APPSC 2019).
- Ocean-floor rocks much younger than continental; oldest ~**200 million years**.
- Spreading always **adds** new crust; subduction **destroys** old crust – the two roughly balance.
- First direct observation of a full spreading episode: seafloor sank 4 m, spread over 1 m, 160 million cubic metres of lava in 16 days → spreading comes in **bursts**, not steadily.
### 5. PLATES – MAJOR AND MINOR
- **Seven major plates** (APPSC 2018): Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic.
| Minor plate | Location |
|---|---|
| **Cocos** | Between **Central America** and Pacific plate |
| **Nazca** | Between **South America** and Pacific plate |
| **Caroline** | Between Philippine and Indian plates, north of New Guinea |
| Philippine | Between Asiatic and Pacific plates |
| Arabian | Mostly Saudi Arabian landmass |
| Caribbean | Borders South American plate; Venezuela on this boundary (Jul 2026) |
- Plate movement is monitored with **GPS** data (APPSC 2021); GSI runs 34 GNSS stations building strain models.
### 6. PLATE BOUNDARIES
| Boundary | Motion | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Divergent (constructive) | Apart; new crust | **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**; **Red Sea**, East African Rift (Nubian, Somalian, Arabian plates near **Afar triple junction**) |
| Convergent (destructive) | Collision / subduction | **Himalayas** (continent-continent); **Andes** (Nazca under South American); **Mariana Trench** (ocean-ocean) |
| Transform (conservative) | Slide past horizontally | **San Andreas** (not divergent); **Sagaing Fault** (Myanmar, Indian–Eurasian) |
- Convergent: earthquakes from **subduction friction** (Pacific Ring of Fire), mountain building.
- **Owen Fracture Zone** separates Arabian and Indian plates.
- **Ladakh Magmatic Arc**: between Indus and Shyok suture zones; northward subduction of **Neo-Tethys** plate.
### 7. THE INDIAN PLATE
1. Gondwanaland broke up ~**225 million years** ago.
2. Palaeomagnetic latitudes show India moving **north** from south of the equator; Peninsular Plateau part of African landmass until the Cretaceous.
3. **Deccan Traps** erupted as India passed near the equator over the **Reunion hotspot**.
4. Collision with Eurasia began ~**40–50 million years** ago (4–5 crore); Tethys closed; sediments folded into the Himalayas.
5. India moves north at ~**5 cm/year** → Himalayas still rising (about **5 mm/year**).
- India underthrust Asia 45–20 million years ago, lifting Tibet; pushed deeper under western Tibet → west more rugged.
- **Delamination**: denser lower layer peels off and sinks into the mantle at ~**100 km** beneath southern Tibet (94 seismic stations).
- Collision explains the Himalayas, **Indo-Gangetic foreland basin** and India's earthquake risk.
### 8. DIASTROPHISM
| Movement | Force | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Orogenic** | Horizontal (tangential) | Mountain building by folding/faulting – e.g. Himalayas |
| **Epeirogenic** | Vertical | Uplift or warping of continents and plateaus |
- Sudden movements (earthquakes, volcanoes) are treated separately.
### 9. FOLDING AND FOLD MOUNTAINS
- Compression bends layers: **anticline** = up-arch; **syncline** = down-trough.
- Rising pressure: symmetrical → asymmetrical → **overfold** → recumbent → **nappe** (recumbent fold broken by thrust, pushed far forward).
- Siwaliks: simple **"Jura" type** folds; **youngest** Himalayan range. Himalayas began ~70 million years ago.
| Young fold (Tertiary / Alpine) | Old fold (worn down) |
|---|---|
| Himalayas, Alps, Andes, **Rockies** | **Aravallis** (among India's oldest), **Appalachians**, Urals |
- **Eastern Ghats** (AP): old, eroded, discontinuous hills of the Gondwana shield – not young fold mountains.
### 10. FAULTING
| Fault | Force | Movement / example |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Tension | Hanging wall **down**; East African Rift, Rio Grande |
| Reverse | Compression | Hanging wall **up** |
| Thrust | Compression | Low-angle reverse; Himalayan **MCT, MBT, HFF** |
| Strike-slip | Shear | Horizontal; San Andreas, Sagaing |
- **Horst** = uplifted block (block mountain); **graben** = sunken block (rift valley). Vosges and **Rhine Valley** (APPSC 2022: horsts = block mountains).
- **Satpura** = block mountain between rift valleys of **Narmada and Tapi**.
- Indo-Gangetic Plain: Himalayan Frontal Fault (north) + normal fault (south).
- East African Rift: chain of mostly freshwater lakes; **Lake Malawi** at southern end; **Lake Victoria is not a rift lake** (APPSC G1 2024). Red Sea is also a rift.
