## U0669 | WORLD AGRICULTURE – TYPES OF FARMING, MAJOR CROP BELTS AND AGRICULTURAL REGIONS
### 1. SHIFTING CULTIVATION – LOCAL NAMES
- Also called **slash-and-burn**: forest cleared by burning; temporary cultivation; low technology; in thick forests of **high rainfall**.
- A **primitive subsistence** type – not capital-intensive, not export-oriented.
- Regions: **Amazon Basin, Tropical Africa, South-East Asia, North-East India**.

### 2. TYPES OF FARMING – SUBSISTENCE
| Type | Features | Regions |
|---|---|---|
| Shifting cultivation | Burn forest; temporary plots | Amazon, Tropical Africa, SE Asia, NE India |
| **Nomadic herding** | Seasonal migration with animals for **fodder and water**; **dry (arid)** or cold lands | Sahara, Central Asia, **Eurasian steppes**, Arabian Desert, Rajasthan, J&K |
| **Intensive subsistence** | Small plots, **family labour**, high yield per hectare; **rice** in wet areas, **wheat** in drier parts | **Monsoon Asia** – East, South, SE Asia |
- **Intensive** = high labour and capital per unit of land; **extensive** = large areas with low input.
- Indian agriculture = **small and marginal holdings**, not large farms.
!TRAP Nomadic herding is NOT found in the Amazon and Congo basins – those have shifting cultivation.
### 3. TYPES OF FARMING – COMMERCIAL
| Type | Features | Regions |
|---|---|---|
| **Plantation** | Large estates; single cash crop; capital-intensive; **export-oriented monoculture**; **product of colonialism** | Tea – India, Sri Lanka; coffee – Brazil; rubber – Malaysia; cocoa – West Africa (Ghana) |
| **Commercial grain** | Large mechanised farms; **wheat**; **monoculture**; temperate grasslands | **Prairies** (USA, Canada), **Pampas** (Argentina), **Steppes** (Russia, Ukraine), **Downs** (Australia) |
| **Mixed** | **Crops + livestock on the same farm**; equal weight; crop rotation, intercropping; mechanised | **NW Europe, eastern USA/North America**, Argentina, SE Australia, NZ, South Africa |
| **Dairy** | Milk; needs **refrigeration and transport** | **Denmark, Netherlands**, NZ, US Midwest, SE Australia |
| **Mediterranean** | Fruits, olives, citrus, **viticulture** | Mediterranean Basin, **California, Central Chile, Cape Province**, **SW Australia** |
| **Livestock ranching** | Large-scale **commercial** rearing on ranches | Western USA, Pampas, Australia, NZ, South Africa |
- Commercial grain has a **high commodity (marketed) rate** because of large mechanised farms and **low local demand** (wheat dominance is true but not the reason).
- Ranching = commercial; nomadic herding = seasonal migration.
!TRAP Sugarcane is NOT the classic plantation pairing; cocoa is West Africa, not South Asia.
!TRAP Commercial grain regions do NOT include the Mediterranean Basin; mixed farming is NOT in Monsoon Asia.
#### 3.1 Modern Types
- **Vertical farming**: **year-round** crops in **non-tropical** regions; more efficient than land farming; works in harsh environments (APPSC 2021).
- **Hydroponics** = soil-free; **aquaponics** = hydroponics + **fish rearing**.
!TRAP Neither hydroponics nor aquaponics is agroecological.
### 4. WHITTLESEY'S AGRICULTURAL REGIONS
- **Derwent Whittlesey (1936)**: world mapped into **13** agricultural regions.
| Region | Areas | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Mediterranean** | Mediterranean basin, California, central Chile, SW Cape, SW Australia | **Winter rain**, viticulture, citrus, olives |
| **Oriental intensive** | Monsoon Asia | Wet paddy; **high output per hectare, low output per worker** |
- **Viticulture** = grape production (wine-making use = **viniculture**); a branch of horticulture.
!KEY A-R: Commercial viticulture is specific to Mediterranean Europe **because** grapes go to wine-making – A and R true, R explains A.
### 5. AGRICULTURAL REGIONALISATION – GEOGRAPHERS
| Method / index | Geographer |
|---|---|
| 13 world agricultural regions (1936) | **Derwent Whittlesey** |
| **Crop combination** (1954) | **J.C. Weaver** (modified by Doi, Rafiullah) |
| **Crop diversification** | **Bhatia**, Jasbir Singh; Gibbs-Martin |
| **Crop concentration** | **Bhatia** |
| **Agricultural efficiency** | **Kendall** (ranking coefficient), Enyedi, Shafi |
| **4 macro agricultural regions of India** | **Sengupta and Sdasyuk** |
- Sengupta and Sdasyuk (**1968**, Census monograph): India in **60 micro** regions; first to give **4 macro** regions.
- India has **20 agro-ecological zones** (uniform physiography, climate, growing period, soil).
!TRAP Bhatia = diversification AND concentration; Weaver = combination.

| Wine | France (exam key); OIV 2024: Italy, France, Spain |

| Iron ore, bauxite | Australia |
| Copper | Chile |
| Diamonds | **Botswana by value**, **Russia by volume** |
- Rubber: **Malaysia** traditionally the leading producer and exporter; exam key (2010) said India for largest rubber cultivation.
- **Rice**: India 2nd producer, **largest exporter** (since 2014-15; more than Thailand, Vietnam, Pakistan combined); top producer and consumer per exam key.
- **Tea exporter**: China (exam key 2015); 2024 – **Kenya** by quantity, China by value; then India, Sri Lanka. India was top tea producer and consumer in 2007-08.
!TRAP "India's domestic tea consumption is very poor" – FALSE.
- Largest fishing ground: **Northwest Pacific**.
- Not a cocoa producer: **Latvia**.

#### 6.2 Coffee Varieties
| Variety | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Arabica** | Highland, mild; originated in **Ethiopia** |
| **Robusta** (*Coffea canephora*) | Hardier lowland; more caffeine; origin **Africa**; grown in India |
| **Mocha** | An Arabica named after the port of **Mocha, Yemen** |
- Sri Lanka coffee collapsed from **1869** due to **leaf rust** (*Hemileia vastatrix*, a fungus).
- Mango regular-bearing varieties: **Dasheri-51, Bangalora (Totapuri), Neelam, Amrapali** – not Dasheri-1, Chausa, Langra.
### 7. CROP DOMESTICATION – OLD WORLD VS NEW WORLD
| Old World | New World (Americas) |
|---|---|
| Wheat, barley, rice, sugarcane, tea, **coffee** | **Maize, potato, tomato, tobacco, cocoa, rubber**, cassava, groundnut, chilli |
- Transfer via the **Columbian Exchange** (after Columbus, **1493**).
!TRAP Coffee is an **Old World** crop; cocoa, rubber, chilli are New World.
### 8. GLOBAL GREEN REVOLUTION
| Person / institute | Fact |
|---|---|
| **William Gaud** | Coined "**Green Revolution**", **1968** |
| **Norman E. Borlaug** | **American** (born Cresco, Iowa, 1914); semi-dwarf wheat in **Mexico** with Rockefeller Foundation; first HYV seeds; **Nobel Peace Prize 1970** |
| **IRRI** | **Los Baños, Philippines**, **1960**; released **IR8** "miracle rice" |
| **CIMMYT** | **Mexico**; maize + wheat; with ICAR formed **BISA (2011)** – Punjab, MP, Bihar |
| M.S. Swaminathan, C. Subramaniam | India's key figures; core **Punjab, Haryana, western UP** |
- **Borlaug Award** – agricultural science; started **1972**.
- **Shaktiman-I and II** – GM crops of **maize**.
!TRAP Borlaug is American, not British; term 1968 (Gaud) vs Nobel 1970 (Borlaug).
### 9. SUSTAINABLE AGRICULTURE AND TERMS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Organic farming** | Avoids synthetic inputs and **GMOs**; crop rotation + manures |
| **Natural farming** | No compulsory synthetic fertilisers / chemical pesticides |
| **Permaculture** | Self-sustaining designed ecological systems |
| **IPM** | Biological + ecological control: bio-pesticides, pheromone traps, predators |
| Viticulture / Sericulture | Grapes / silk |
| Apiculture / Pisciculture | Bee-keeping / fish rearing |
- **Green agriculture**: IPM, integrated nutrient supply, scientific water management, appropriate crop varieties.
- **ZBNF**: started by **Subhash Palekar**.
- **APCNF**: world's largest agro-ecological programme – **10.20 lakh farmers, 4.89 lakh ha** (Jul 2026).
- **National Mission on Natural Farming**: **8.80 lakh ha**, 18.19 lakh farmers (Mar 2026).
- **Horticulture** output (~**362-367 MT**) now exceeds foodgrains (2026).
- **2026** = UN **International Year of the Woman Farmer**.
- 7 Sept 2020: AP MoU with **Netherlands** – food processing, horticulture, aquaculture.
- Aug 2026: AP pushing farmers from paddy to drought-hardy crops amid rain deficit.
