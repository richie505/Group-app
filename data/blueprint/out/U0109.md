## U0109 | TUGHLAQ DYNASTY – GHIYASUDDIN, MUHAMMAD BIN TUGHLAQ'S EXPERIMENTS, FIROZ SHAH'S REFORMS
### 1. TUGHLAQ DYNASTY AT A GLANCE
| Ruler | Reign | Remembered for |
|---|---|---|
| **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** | 1320-25 | Revenue capped at one-tenth; **first canals**; Warangal; **Tughlaqabad** |
| **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (Juna Khan / Ulugh Khan) | 1325-51 | **Five experiments**; Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi; Ibn Battuta |
| **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** | 1351-88 | **Most canals**; welfare departments; jizya on Brahmins; hereditary iqtas |
| **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** | to 1412 | **Last Tughlaq**; Timur's invasion **1398** |
- Tughlaq period: **1320-1414** (Ghiyasuddin to **Khizr Khan** taking Delhi).
### 2. GHIYASUDDIN TUGHLAQ (1320-25)
- Earlier name **Ghazi Malik**; son of a Turkish slave and a Jat woman; **Governor of Punjab** under Alauddin against the Mongols.
- Killed **Khusrau Shah** and became Sultan in **1320**.
- Agrarian policy: rise in land revenue limited to about **one-tenth**, raised slowly; remission in drought; waste land farmed; **first Sultan to start canal works**; organised the postal system.
- Sequence: Sultan (1320) → Juna Khan attacked Warangal (**1321-22**, retreated on false news of Sultan's death) → Warangal fell after a **five-month siege (1323)**, renamed **Sultanpur** → died at Afghanpur (1325).
- **Prataparudra II** was taken towards Delhi and died in the **Narmada**.
- Built **Tughlaqabad**. Died **1325** when a **wooden pavilion collapsed at Afghanpur**; Ibn Battuta suspected a plot by Juna Khan.
### 3. MUHAMMAD BIN TUGHLAQ'S FIVE EXPERIMENTS
| Experiment | Cause / aim | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Transfer of capital** Delhi → **Devagiri (Daulatabad)**, 1327 | Central base to control the Deccan | Mass migration, hardship; court **returned to Delhi** |
| **Token currency** of **copper and brass** (c. 1329-30) | **Shortage of silver** | **Mass forgery**; coins withdrawn and redeemed at heavy cost |
| **Taxation in the Doab** | More revenue | **Coincided with famine**; peasant revolts and flight |
| **Khurasan expedition** | Central Asian conquest | Huge army raised, then **disbanded unpaid** |
| **Qarachil (Kumaon hills)** | Secure the hill frontier | Army **destroyed** in the hills |
- **Barani** lists these five schemes. MBT raised land revenue to **one-half** of produce; called an "unlucky idealist".
- **First to introduce token currency** in India = MBT (Barani: lack of silver). It failed because the state had **no proper control over issue** of coins.
- Token coins were copper/bronze, **not gold**, not meant for West Asian trade. New gold coin = **Dinar** (Ibn Battuta); silver coin = **Adli**.
!TRAP Daulatabad is MBT's renamed capital, not a Firoz town.
### 4. MBT'S AGRARIAN MEASURES AND TERMS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi** (Diwan-i-Kohi) | MBT's **agriculture department**: waste land under cultivation, better crops, direct aid; trial land of **30 × 30 karohs** |
| **Taqavi (sondhar)** | State loans for **seeds and wells** (MBT); written off by Firoz |
| **Swarga-dwari** | Famine-relief and cultivation camp on the **Ganga near Kannauj** during the Doab famine |
| **Uluq / Dawa** | **Horse** couriers every four miles / **foot** couriers, three stations to every mile |
- MBT died at **Thatta (1351)** on the way to Sindh, chasing the rebel **Taghi** after a Gujarat revolt.
### 5. IBN BATTUTA AND HISTORIANS ON MBT
- **Moroccan ("Moorish")** traveller (**1304-1377**), born **Tangiers**; real name Abu Abdullah Muhammad.
- In India **1333-1342** under MBT; made **Qazi of Delhi**; sent as **envoy to China (1342)**.
- Work: **Rihla (Kitab-ul-Rihla)** in **Arabic**: Sultanate and Deccan; detailed account of the **postal system**; recorded that **sati** needed the Sultan's permission.
- Called MBT eccentric, hot-tempered, but generous.
- **First foreign traveller to Vijayanagara**, under **Harihara I**.
| Historian | Remark |
|---|---|
| **Barani** | MBT a "**mixture of opposites**"; wrote **Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi** and **Fatawa-i-Jahandari** |
| **Badauni** | On MBT's death: "the king was **freed from his people** and they from their king" |
### 6. REVOLTS AND NEW STATES IN THE SOUTH
| Revolt / state | Year | Leader |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal Andhra freed (Reddis) | c. 1325 | **Prolaya Vema Reddi** |
| Madurai Sultanate | 1335 | **Jalaluddin Ahsan Shah** |
| Warangal recaptured (Musunuri) | **1336** | **Kapaya Nayaka** (after **Prolaya Nayaka**) |

- Sources: **Vilasa copper plate** (Prolaya Nayaka) and **Madhura Vijayam** (**Gangadevi**).
- Bahmani later split into **five Deccan sultanates**.
!KEY 1336 = Vijayanagara founded AND Warangal recaptured by Kapaya Nayaka.
### 7. FIROZ SHAH TUGHLAQ – REVENUE, JIZYA, IQTA
- Placed on the throne at **Thatta (1351)**; ruled till **1388**.
- Wazir **Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul** paid compensation to those who suffered under MBT.
- Wrote off taqavi loans; **fixed land revenue**; abolished about **24 taxes**; kept the **four Islamic taxes** (kharaj, jizya, khams, zakat).
- Made **jizya a separate tax**, extended to **Brahmins for the first time** (ulema's advice).
- Restored iqtas and made them **hereditary**.
- Campaigns: two failed **Bengal** expeditions against **Haji Iliyas** (second 1359-60); **Jam Babaniya** of Sind agreed to pay tribute.
### 8. FIROZ SHAH – DEPARTMENTS, CANALS, TOWNS
| Department | Function |
|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Khairat** | **Charity**: orphans, widows, marriages of poor Muslim girls |
| **Diwan-i-Bandagan** | **Slaves** (about 1,80,000) |
| **Dar-ul-Shifa** (Dar-ul-Shafa) | **Free hospital** |
| **Diwan-i-Istihqaq** | **Pensions and grants** |
| **Public Works Department** | Canals, towns, buildings |
| Employment bureau | Jobs for the unemployed |
#### 8.1 Canals
- **Largest number of canals** among Delhi Sultans; began state-backed canal irrigation. Irrigation cess **haq-i-sharb = one-tenth** of produce.
| Canal | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Yamuna to Hisar** | **150 miles**, the longest |
| **Sutlej to Ghaggar** | **96 miles** |
| Sirmur hills to Hansi | Irrigation canal |
| Ghaggar to Firozabad; Yamuna to Firozabad | Irrigation |
| **Rajabwah** | Restored in Akbar's time as the **Shihab (Shahab) Canal** |
#### 8.2 Towns, Pillars, Books
- Towns: **Firozabad** (Firoz Shah Kotla), **Fatehabad**, **Hisar Firoza**, **Firozpur**, **Jaunpur**; fruit gardens.
- **Ashokan pillars** brought to Delhi from **Topra (Ambala)** and **Meerut**.
- Autobiography: **Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi**.
- **Nagarkot** campaign: about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** from the **Jawalamukhi** temple; some translated into Persian by **Azizuddin Khalid Khani** as **Dalail-i-Firoz Shahi**.

### 10. SUCCESSORS AND END
- Weak successors after 1388: **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq II**, **Abu Bakr**, Nasiruddin Muhammad Shah fought each other.
- **Timur** invaded **1398** under **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah**, the last Tughlaq; Daulat Khan Lodi briefly recognised; **Khizr Khan** took Delhi **1414**.
- Verdict: MBT's schemes bold but badly carried out; Firoz's welfare state pleased nobles and ulema but weakened the centre (hereditary iqtas, slave corps).
