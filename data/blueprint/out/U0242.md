## U0242 | QUIT INDIA MOVEMENT 1942 - CAUSES, COURSE, UNDERGROUND ACTIVITY, REPRESSION, SIGNIFICANCE
### 1. CAUSES OF THE QUIT INDIA MOVEMENT
| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Failure of the Cripps Mission** (March-April 1942) | No immediate self-government - the main trigger |
| **Japanese advance** in the East | Malaya, Singapore overrun; **Rangoon fell March 1942**; Japanese at India's eastern frontier; rumours of bombing of **Vizag and Kakinada** |
| **Economic grievances** | Rising prices, wartime shortages |
- UPSC 2013: Quit India was launched **in response to the Cripps Proposals**.
- Britain freed India in 1947 because it had become **weak in WWII** (A and R true, R explains A).
- British PM during Quit India: **Winston Churchill** (1940-45).
- **C. Rajagopalachari's** pamphlet **"The Way Out"** offered a solution to the constitutional deadlock.
### 2. THE QUIT INDIA RESOLUTION
#### 2.1 Meetings and Dates
| Date | Meeting / Place | Event |
|---|---|---|
| **14 July 1942** | **CWC, Wardha** | Resolution passed - **"Wardha Resolution"**; Gandhi empowered to lead a non-violent mass movement |
| **7-8 August 1942** | **AICC, Bombay**, **Gowalia Tank Maidan** (now **August Kranti Ground**) | Resolution ratified on **8 August** - "August Resolution" |
| **9 August 1942** | All India | Movement begins; **Operation Zero Hour** arrests |
- At Wardha and at Bombay the resolution was **moved by Jawaharlal Nehru** and **seconded/supported by Sardar Patel**.
- The movement was **primarily launched at Bombay**.
#### 2.2 Demands of the Resolution
- **Immediate end of British rule**.
- A **provisional government** after the British withdrew.
- **Mass civil disobedience** under Gandhi.
- NOT in it: continuing British rule until the war ended.
#### 2.3 Gandhi's Instructions
| Group | Instruction |
|---|---|
| Government servants | Stay in service but **pledge loyalty to Congress** |
| Soldiers | **Not to fire** on Indians |
| Students | **May leave studies** |

### 4. REPRESSION
#### 4.1 Viceroy and Operations
- Viceroy: **Lord Linlithgow** (1936-43, **longest-serving Viceroy**); called it **"the most serious rebellion since 1857"** - reason: a huge peasant rising (R explains A).
- Linlithgow called Gandhi's movements **"Political Blackmail"**.
- **Operation Zero Hour:** pre-dawn arrest of Congress leaders on **9 August 1942** (the day after the resolution) → movement became **leaderless, spontaneous and decentralised**.
- **Baldev Sahai** resigned as Advocate General in 1942 in protest against the arrests.
#### 4.2 Detention Places
| Place | Detention / Event |
|---|---|
| **Ahmednagar Fort** | CWC members held |
| **Aga Khan Palace, Poona** | Gandhi held; **21-day fast, February 1943**, against state violence |
| Poona (1944) | **Kasturba Gandhi died** in detention |
- Effect of the fast: **three members of the Viceroy's Executive Council resigned**.
- Brothers **Gangaram and Kheemdev** were killed in police firing at **Almora**.
### 5. UNDERGROUND ACTIVITY
| Leader | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Jayaprakash Narayan** | Escaped **Hazaribagh Jail** (Diwali, **9 November 1942**) with **Yogendra Shukla**; organised sabotage; formed the **All India Azad Dasta** at **Bakri Ka Tapu** (Nepal Terai) - guerrilla war |
| **Aruna Asaf Ali** | **Hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank**; main organiser of the underground network |
| **Usha Mehta** | Ran the secret **Congress Radio** (heard up to Madras) |
| **Ram Manohar Lohia** | Regular **broadcasts on Congress Radio** |
| **Sucheta Kripalani** | Went into hiding; an organiser of the underground movement; arrested 1944 |
| Others | Biju Patnaik, Chhotubhai Puranik, Achyut Patwardhan, R.P. Goenka |
- Yogendra Shukla was later arrested at **Muzaffarpur**, sent to Patna.
- **Sabotage:** bridges, railway tracks, telegraph lines - especially in **UP and Bihar**.
- Widest riots: **Bihar and United Provinces** (Azamgarh, Ballia, Gorakhpur; Gaya, Bhagalpur, Saran).
- **JP**: called **"Lok Nayak"**; wrote **"Towards Struggle"** and **"Prison Diary" (1975)**; declared **Sampoorna Kranti** at Gandhi Maidan, Patna, **5 June 1974**; NOT a member of the Viceroy's Executive Council.

### 7. PARALLEL GOVERNMENTS
| Place | Name / Leader | Period / Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ballia** (UP) | **Chittu Pandey** | August 1942; freed Congress leaders; **about a week (shortest)** |
| **Tamluk** (Midnapore, Bengal) | **Jatiya Sarkar** | **Dec 1942 - Sept 1944**; cyclone relief, school grants, paddy distribution, **Vidyut Vahinis** |
| **Satara** (Maharashtra) | **"Prati Sarkar"** - **Y.B. Chavan, Nana Patil** | Mid-1943 - 1945; **longest**; Nyayadan Mandals (people's courts), village libraries, prohibition, "Gandhi marriages" |
| **Talcher** (Odisha) | Parallel government | 1942 |
| Ahmedabad | Young Congress Socialists | Ward-wise Azad Government; taxes, policing |
- APPSC 2016: "Prati Sarkar" = **Satara**.
- **Raiyati Sarkar** - NOT Kheda (Gujarat).
### 8. QUIT INDIA IN ANDHRA
#### 8.1 Kurnool Circular and Leaders
- **Kurnool (Andhra) Circular, 29 July 1942:** action programme against government offices; drafted by **Kala Venkat Rao**; seized by police at Kurnool.
- APCC meeting at **Machilipatnam** under **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**.
- **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**: jailed at **Ahmednagar Fort**; wrote the diary **"Feathers and Stones"**.
- **Andhra University**: resolution under **Mamidipudi Venkatrangayya**.
- **Tanguturi Prakasam** emerged as the leading Andhra figure.
- Underground paper **"Quit India"**; circulated **"Rahasya Udyamam Saginchandi"**.
#### 8.2 Places and Incidents
| Place | Incident |
|---|---|
| **Tenali** | Police firing **12 Aug 1942** (about 9 killed); **Madras-Puri train burnt**; Tenali bomb case |
| Guntur | Firing **13 Aug** |
| Bapatla (Guntur) | Railway station burnt |
| **Bhimavaram** | Firing **17 Aug**; Collectorate attacked |
| **Kakinada** | Lawyers demanded **Gandhi's release** |
| Guntur-Vijayawada | Rail lines torn up |
- Quit India = the **"final, decisive event"** of the freedom struggle in Andhra.
### 9. ATTITUDE OF PARTIES
- **CPI opposed** it - backed the British war effort after **Nazi Germany attacked the USSR**; CPI ban lifted **23 July 1942**; lost heavily in **1945-46 elections**.
- Did NOT support: **Muslim League, Hindu Mahasabha, CPI, Unionist Party (Punjab), princely states**; **RSS** kept away.
- **Jinnah/League condemned** it - British and Muslims came closer in hatred of Congress (A and R true, R explains A).
### 10. SIGNIFICANCE
- **Gandhi's final mass movement**; culmination of the national movement - next need was a way to transfer power (A and R true).
- Awakened the people, who adopted "Do or Die".
- Showed independence could not be postponed after the war.
- The **Struggle-Truce-Struggle** strategy is linked with the Quit India call.
