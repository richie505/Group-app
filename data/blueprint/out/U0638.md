## U0638 | ANDHRA PRADESH – CLIMATE, RAINFALL REGIONS, CYCLONE VULNERABILITY, DROUGHT-PRONE AREAS
### 1. CLIMATE AND SEASONS OF AP
- Climate: generally **hot and humid**; summer temperatures quite high (IMD report; APPSC 2025 key).
- Rayalaseema = **semi-arid**; coastal Andhra = humid, cyclone-exposed.
| Season | Period | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | **January–February** | mostly dry, **13–30 °C**; light rain from retreating systems and western disturbances |
| Summer | **March to mid-June** | hot; **mango showers** in **April–May** (pre-monsoon, help mangoes ripen) |
| SW monsoon | **mid-June to September** | starts in the second week of June; peaks in **July** |
| Retreating / NE monsoon | **October–December** | October = transition from rains to winter; withdraws Oct–Nov |
- Time order: **Winter → Summer → SW monsoon → Retreating monsoon**.
- India-wide: SW monsoon June–September; retreating monsoon October–November; monsoon arrives **first in Kerala**.
- Summer monsoon blows **SW → NE**; retreating monsoon **NE → SW**.
#### 1.1 Temperature Extremes → Place
| Extreme | Place |
|---|---|
| **Hottest place** | **Rentachintala (Palnadu)** |
| Other hot spots | Kurnool, Kadapa, Nandyal, Vijayawada, **Nandigama (NTR)** |
| Heat-wave belt | **Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur** (peak May–June, rising) |
| **Coldest place** | **Lambasingi (Alluri Sitharama Raju)** – **"Kashmir of AP"**, about **2 °C**; snow-like frost (APPSC-GS 2019) |
- IMD: Heat Action Plans in **23 heatwave-prone** regions (2026).
- CMIP6 study (7 Jun 2026): excess SW-monsoon rain for AP and TN; **1 °C-plus** rise in 40 coastal districts by 2040.
### 2. RAINFALL – TWO MONSOONS
| Basis | SW monsoon | Retreating / NE monsoon |
|---|---|---|
| Share of AP rain | **about two-thirds (65–70%)** | **about one-third** |
| Peak month | **July** (wettest) | **October** |
| Key region | most of AP | **Rayalaseema, Nellore, Tirupati, Prakasam** (south coast) |
| Normal (2024-25) | **574.7 mm** | **285.3 mm** |
- Normal annual rainfall ≈ **860 mm**.
- Bay of Bengal cyclones bring much of the Oct–Dec rain to southern States.
- **River floods** come mainly in the **SW monsoon (Jul–Sep)**; most **cyclones** in **Oct–Dec**.
#### 2.1 AP vs Neighbours (NE-Monsoon Rain)
- India gets maximum rain from the **SW monsoon (about 75%)**; Jharkhand too.
- **Tamil Nadu** gets the **maximum NE-monsoon rain** (40–50% of its annual rain); its main rainy months **November–December**; **Chennai** has the lowest SW-monsoon rain.
- NE-monsoon rain falls on the **Coromandel coast** (Köppen **As**), southern AP, SE Karnataka, SE Kerala – **not the Gujarat coast**.
- AP lies **in between**: about one-third of its rain in Oct–Dec.
- **Gujarat** (semi-arid) has annual rainfall variability **above 60%**.
#### 2.2 IMD Report and 2026 Deficit
- IMD: **"Observed Rainfall Variability and Changes over Andhra Pradesh State"** – variability **quite high**; **least rainfall = South-western region (Rayalaseema)** (APPSC-GS 2023); summer begins in March.
- **2026 El Niño year**: SW-monsoon deficit **51.2%** (1 Jun–25 Aug); **all 28 districts** deficient; cause: El Niño subsidence + all seven lows at the head of the Bay.
- El Niño review (8 Jul 2026): round-the-clock control rooms at State and district levels.
### 3. REGIONAL DISASTER PROFILE
| Region | Main hazards |
|---|---|
| Coastal Andhra (Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Nellore) | cyclones, **storm surge**, floods, coastal erosion |
| Godavari–Krishna deltas | river floods |
| Rayalaseema, southern Prakasam | **chronic drought**, groundwater depletion, heat |
| Inland plains (Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur) | **heat waves** |
- **Anantapur is not** in the coastal region.
- AP needs **multi-hazard planning**: east-coast cyclones + two major deltas + drought-prone interior.
!TRAP Regions do **not** share identical hazards.

### 5. RAIN-SHADOW RAYALASEEMA AND DROUGHT
- Rayalaseema lies in the **rain shadow** of the **Western Ghats + Karnataka–Telangana highlands** → little SW-monsoon rain → depends on the NE monsoon.
- When the NE monsoon fails → **chronic drought** in Rayalaseema and **southern Prakasam**; falling groundwater worsens it.
- **Anantapur = lowest-rainfall district**.
- Rain-shadow areas of India: interior **Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP, Tamil Nadu**; western Rajasthan is scanty (Thar).
- Greatest rural distress: **Prakasam, Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa** (rain failure → indebtedness).
- Areas under **750 mm** a year are classed **chronically drought prone**; 750–1,125 mm = drought prone.
#### 5.1 Drought-Proofing Projects
- **Watershed projects** raise groundwater but **yields stay flat** when **crop diversification and soil management** are neglected (APPSC 2025 key).
- **WDC-PMKSY 2.0**: degraded and rain-fed land via integrated natural-resource management (**Dept of Land Resources**).
| Project | Fact |
|---|---|
| Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing | Kurnool, Jul 2026; 92,000 acres; millets, pulses, oilseeds |
| Veligonda Phase-I | Krishna water to Prakasam, Nellore, Kadapa |
| 100-day water plan | launched at **Yadiki (Anantapur)**, 6 Apr 2026; Water Users' Associations, desilting, check dams |
| **Gandikota reservoir** | on the **Pennar**, part of **Telugu Ganga**, YSR Kadapa |
### 6. DROUGHT MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK
- **NDMA Drought Guidelines 2010**; **Manual for Drought Management revised 2016** (Ministry of Agriculture) – **multi-indicator** approach, at least three triggers.
- **Mandatory** indicators: **rainfall deviation + dry spells**; **impact** indicators: crop sowing, satellite vegetation index (NDVI), soil moisture, hydrology.
- Meteorological drought trigger: seasonal rain below **75% of normal**.
| Institution | Role |
|---|---|
| **Crop Weather Watch Group** | **Ministry of Agriculture**; meets **weekly** in the monsoon |
| NADAMS | satellite monitoring of agricultural drought |
| Nodal ministry | **Agriculture** for drought, pest attack, hailstorm, frost/cold wave; **MHA** for other disasters |
- **Swaraj Abhiyan v. Union of India (2016)**: SC ordered the drought manual updated and a **National Disaster Mitigation Fund** set up.
### 7. CYCLONE VULNERABILITY
- Coastline **1,053.07 km** (MHA remeasurement, Dec 2024); **third-longest** after **Gujarat (2,340.62 km)** and **Tamil Nadu (1,068.69 km)**; old figure **974 km** (then longest on the east coast).
- Bay cyclones form between **8° N and 14° N**; peak **October–December** (late October typical).
- **Most vulnerable stretch: Ongole to Machilipatnam** (APPSC-GS 2018).
- About **44%** of the State is vulnerable to **tropical storms** and related hazards.
- **Flood Hazard Atlas of AP** (**NRSC**, with West Bengal): built from multi-date **satellite data 2000–2020**.
- **Most exposed districts: Nellore and Srikakulam**; cyclone-prone: Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Nellore (APPSC 2025 key).
- **Storm surge** can flood villages up to **10 km inland**.
- **IMD Cyclone Warning Centre, Visakhapatnam** – one of seven (Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Thiruvananthapuram, Visakhapatnam, Bhubaneswar).
- IMD warned of the season's first cyclone over the Andaman Sea (19–21 Sep 2026), likely to cross Odisha.

### 9. CYCLONE MITIGATION
- **NCRMP**: **World Bank**-assisted; cyclone shelters, early warning, last-mile connectivity; **Phase I = AP and Odisha** (cost **Rs 2,541.60 crore**).
- NCRMP-I built **219 multipurpose cyclone shelters (MPCS)** in AP (Odisha 316).
- **NDMA Cyclone Guidelines 2008**: structural – multipurpose shelters, raised platforms, cattle shelters, evacuation roads, **storm-surge barriers**; nature-based – **mangroves, bio-shields, saline embankments, sand-dune restoration**.
- Roof: **hipped roof** firmly anchored to walls beats a gable roof; **pyramidal roof** best reduces wind force.
- Tools: **Doppler radar** network; **SCATSAT-1** (ISRO scatterometer) for cyclone tracking; GIS mapping.
### 10. FLOOD MANAGEMENT
- **CWC** began scientific **flood forecasting in 1958** (APPSC 2025 key).
- **Rashtriya Barh Ayog**: set up **1976**, report **1980**; flood-prone area about **40 million ha**.
- **Model Flood Plain Zoning Bill**: **1975**. NDMA Flood Guidelines **2008**; Urban Flooding **2010**.
- Flood management = **State subject**; Centre helps via **FMBAP**.
- Urban Flood Risk Management Programme Phase II (Oct 2025): 11 cities incl. **Visakhapatnam**; 90:10.
- **City Flood Alert** (Jul 2026): **RTGS + APSDMA + IIT Gandhinagar**; first in **Vijayawada**; **48-hour** rain forecasts, **6-hour** flash-flood warnings.
| Measure | Category |
|---|---|
| Dams, embankments, detention basins, channel improvement | Structural (flood) |
| Forecasting, floodplain zoning, insurance | Non-structural (flood) |
| Check dams, farm ponds, irrigation | Structural (drought) |
| Drought-resistant crops, water conservation, PMFBY | Non-structural (drought) |
| Retaining walls, slope stabilisation | Structural (landslide) |
| Afforestation | Non-structural (landslide) |
| Seismic microzonation | Non-structural (earthquake); retrofitting (IS 1893) structural |
- Hazard mapping → risk zones; drainage planning → less urban waterlogging; flood forecasting → early warning.
#### 10.1 Godavari–Krishna Deltas
| Work | Year | Fact |

### 11. TSUNAMI AND INSTITUTIONS
- **2004 tsunami**: AP coast hit (Krishna, Prakasam, Nellore, Guntur, Godavari districts) but **Tamil Nadu worst-affected** (APPSC 2025 key).
- **INCOIS, Hyderabad**: Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre opened **Oct 2007**.
- Mangroves and coastal shelterbelts buffer surges.
- **APSDMA**: statutory, under **DM Act 2005 + AP DM Rules 2007**; chaired by the **Chief Minister**; **chief nodal agency**; runs early warning, coordinates districts.
- **ERSS – Dial 112** went live in AP on **19 Feb 2019**; AP's 112 control centre (PSAP) is at **Mangalagiri**. APSDMA control room helplines: **112, 1070, 1800-425-0101**.
- **NDMA** = policy and coordination; **NDRF/SDRF** = search, rescue, relief. SDRF/NDRF releases **Rs 479.01 crore** (2026-27); AP got NDMF support (Oct 2025).
- Debate: annual NDMA **State Disaster Vulnerability Index** for Finance Commission awards (Apr 2026).
