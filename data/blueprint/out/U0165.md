## U0165 | MIR JAFAR, MIR QASIM AND THE BATTLE OF BUXAR (1764); TREATY OF ALLAHABAD (1765) AND DIWANI
### 1. KEY TERMS – FARMAN, DASTAK, DIWANI, NIZAMAT
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Farman (1717)** | **Farrukhsiyar** let the Company trade in Bengal **without customs duties** for a fixed **Rs 3,000 a year**; the **"Magna Carta" of the Company** |
| **Dastak** | **Free pass** for duty-free trade; misused by Company servants for private trade → drained the Nawab's revenue; caused quarrels with Siraj and later Mir Qasim |
| **Diwani** | Right to collect **revenue** + administer **civil justice** |
| **Nizamat** | **Police, criminal justice**, law and order |
| Naib Subahdar | Company-nominated officer for defence + administration (**Muhammad Reza Khan**) |
### 2. NAWABS OF BENGAL IN SEQUENCE
| Nawab | Note |
|---|---|
| **Murshid Quli Khan** | Last Subahdar appointed by the Mughal emperor; asked the English to pay customs duties |
| Sarfaraz Khan | Killed by Alivardi |
| **Alivardi Khan** | Deputy Governor of Bihar; stopped both companies fortifying Calcutta and Chandannagar |
| **Siraj-ud-Daulah** (1756–57) | Alivardi's grandson; **last independent Nawab**; killed Shaukat Jang at **Manihari** |
| **Mir Jafar** (1757–60; 1763–65) | Puppet; deposed 1760, restored 1763 |
| **Mir Qasim** (1760–63) | Reformer; fought at Buxar as ex-Nawab |
| **Najm-ud-Daula** (1765) | Mir Jafar's son; Nizamat in name only |
### 3. BATTLE OF PLASSEY (1757) – BACKGROUND
- **23 June 1757**, **Palashi**, on the **Bhagirathi**, **Nadia district**, West Bengal.
- **Clive** (about 3,000) defeated **Siraj-ud-Daulah** (about 50,000), who had **French support**.
- **Causes:** misuse of dastaks; English **fortified Calcutta** without consent (the **French obeyed** his order to stop; the English did not); Company sheltered his enemies.
- **Black Hole (June 1756):** Siraj took Qasim Bazar → Calcutta (Fort William); Governor **Drake** (British side, with **Holwell**) **fled to Fulta**; British used it as a pretext for war.
- **Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757):** restored the 1717 farman privileges; the Company still plotted to remove Siraj.
| Side | Persons |
|---|---|
| Conspirators | **Mir Jafar (Mir Bakshi**, chief plotter), **Rai Durlabh**, **Jagat Seth**, **Amir Chand (Omichund)**, Manik Chand |
| Loyal to Siraj | **Mir Madan**, **Mohan Lal** |
!TRAP A statement listing **Mir Madan** among the traitors is WRONG.
- **Results:** Mir Jafar Nawab; paid **Rs 1.77 crore**; Company got the **zamindari of the 24 Parganas**, **duty-free trade in Bengal, Bihar, Orissa** and resources to beat the French.
### 4. MIR JAFAR – TWO TENURES
- **Tenure 1: 1757–1760** (after Plassey); concessions = Rs 1.77 crore + 24 Parganas.
- **Dutch intrigue:** plotted with the Dutch; **Clive beat the Dutch at Chinsura/Bidara (1759)** → Dutch left India.
- **Removed 1760:** could not meet the Company's demands.
- **Tenure 2: 1763–1765**, restored after Mir Qasim's fall; **Nawab of Bengal during Buxar**.
### 5. MIR QASIM (1760–63)
- **Son-in-law of Mir Jafar**; placed on the throne by Governor **Vansittart (1760)**; earlier **Fauzdar of Purnia and Rangpur**; most efficient successor of Alivardi.
- **Capital: Murshidabad → Monghyr (Munger), Bihar**, to escape constant interference.
- **Abolished all internal (inland) trade duties** (APPSC G2 2017) → Indian merchants on equal terms with the English; the **dastak advantage** was gone.
- **Conflict:** Company servants, angry at lost private-trade profits, went to war; defeated in **1763**, fled to **Awadh**, later an ally at Buxar.
### 6. BATTLE OF BUXAR (22–23 OCT 1764)
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Place | **Buxar (Bihar)** |
| British commander | **Major Hector Munro** |
| Allies | **Mir Qasim** (ex-Nawab), **Shuja-ud-Daulah** (Nawab of **Awadh/Oudh**), **Shah Alam II** (Mughal emperor; ruler of Delhi) |
| Outcome | Decisive English win; **Shuja fled**, sought **Rohilla + Maratha** help, then surrendered; Awadh + Allahabad occupied; Shah Alam joined the English |
- Eyre Coote won **Wandiwash**; Lally led the French there.
### 7. BUXAR vs PLASSEY – "MOST DECISIVE BATTLE"
| Point | Plassey (1757) | Buxar (1764) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Treachery**; "a transaction" | **Open military victory** |
| Enemy | One Nawab | Bengal + Awadh + Mughal Emperor |
| Result | Puppet Nawab, 24 Parganas | **Diwani** of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa; Awadh dependent |
| Significance | **Foundation** of British rule | British masters of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa + major power in North India |
- **Buxar = most decisive battle** for British supremacy (UPPSC 2016); it **confirmed the result of Plassey**.
- Order: **Ambur (1749)** (Second Carnatic War) → **Plassey (1757)** → **Wandiwash (1760)** (decline of French power) → **Buxar (1764)**.
### 8. TREATY OF ALLAHABAD (1765) AND THE DIWANI
#### 8.1 Terms
- Signed **August 1765** after Buxar, by **Clive** (back as Governor of Bengal for the **second** time, **May 1765**).
| Party | Term |
|---|---|
| **Shah Alam II** | Granted the **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** for **Rs 26 lakh a year**; got **Kora and Allahabad**; under Company protection, to live at Allahabad → Company **pensioner** |
| **Shuja-ud-Daulah** (Awadh) | Dominions back except Kora and Allahabad; war indemnity **Rs 50 lakh**; Awadh = **buffer state** |
| Nawab of Bengal | Kept the **Nizamat** |
#### 8.2 Grant of Diwani
- **Grantor:** Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II**; order dated **12 August 1765**; date = **1765** (APPSC G2 2017).
- **Recipient:** the Company under **Lord Clive**.
- **Mohd. Raza Khan** – Deputy Diwan of Murshidabad, appointed by Clive.
- **Khasis** – first mountain tribe the British contacted after the 1765 Diwani (trade, esp. limestone).
#### 8.3 Najm-ud-Daula – Company control
- 1765 treaty handed defence + administration to the Naib Subahdar **Muhammad Reza Khan**; Nawab kept the Nizamat in name only.
#### 8.4 Chronology
- **Plassey (23 June 1757) → Buxar (Oct 1764) → Clive returns (May 1765) → Allahabad / Diwani (Aug 1765) → Warren Hastings Governor (1772)**.
| Treaty | Year | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **Allahabad** | 1765 | Followed Buxar; Diwani |
| Madras | 1769 | With Haidar Ali |
| Salbai | 1782 | Ended First Anglo-Maratha War |
| Bassein | 1802 | Peshwa's subsidiary treaty |
| Sugauli | 1816 | Ended Anglo-Nepal War |
!TRAP Treaty after Buxar = **Allahabad**, not Salbai, Bassein or Sugauli.
### 9. ROBERT CLIVE AND DUAL GOVERNMENT (1765–72)
#### 9.1 Clive
- **"Founder of the British Empire in India"** (APPSC 2019); **"heaven-born general"** (William Pitt).
- **Governor of Bengal twice: 1758–60 and 1765–67**.
- **Reforms:** civil – banned gifts and private business, made internal tax compulsory; military – banned **double batta** (from 1766).
#### 9.2 Dual Government
- **Company = Diwani**; **Nawab = Nizamat**; Mughal emperor = nominal sovereign.
- **Naib diwans:** **Muhammad Reza Khan (Bengal)**, **Raja Shitab Rai (Bihar)** – collected revenue for the Company.
- **Criticism:** **"power without responsibility"**.
- **End: Warren Hastings (1772)** ended the Mughal pretence, ruled by right of conquest, stopped the tribute to Shah Alam.
### 10. BENGAL FAMINE OF 1770
- Dual Government separated power from responsibility; revenue was squeezed as famine spread → **1770 Bengal famine** (Governor **John Cartier**'s time).
