## U0537 | INFLATION – TYPES, CAUSES, MEASUREMENT (CPI, WPI, CORE), EFFECTS AND CONTROL
### 1. INFLATION – BASIC TERMS
- **Inflation**: a rise in the **general price level**; it **decreases the purchasing power of money** (APPSC 2025).
- When all prices rise, the purchasing power of money **decreases**.
- **Deflation**: a lasting **fall in the general price level** (negative inflation); linked with lower consumer spending, falling demand and slowdown.
- **Disinflation**: a **fall in the rate** of inflation that **stays positive** (e.g. 3% to 2%), not negative inflation.
- **Reflation**: inflation coming back after deflation – a sign of **recovery**.
- A fall in the inflation rate surely means only that **prices are rising more slowly** than before, not that prices fell.
- **Economic growth** usually comes with **moderate inflation** – rising demand pushes prices up.
!TRAP Disinflation = rate falls but stays positive; Deflation = prices fall.
#### 1.1 By Speed (Ascending)
| Type | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Creeping** | Below 3% |
| **Walking / trotting** | About 3-10% a year |
| **Galloping** | Above walking; comes before hyperinflation |
| **Hyperinflation** | Most severe |
### 2. HEADLINE, CORE AND FOOD INFLATION
#### 2.1 Headline vs Core
| Basis | Headline inflation | Core inflation |
|---|---|---|
| Represents | **Total inflation** | **Underlying trend** (long run, ignores short-term swings) |
| Food | Includes | **Excludes** volatile food |
| Fuel and energy | Includes | **Excludes** fuel and energy (and electricity) |
| Stability | **More volatile** | **More stable** |
| Shows | What consumers feel | Demand-side trend |
- **Headline inflation** = rate of change in the **CPI number** (standard basket of a typical family).
- Core does not include food and energy; headline does **not** exclude food.
- **Food inflation**: from monsoon and supply shocks; measured by the **Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI)** with **10 sub-groups** (cereals, meat and fish, egg, milk, oils, fruits, vegetables, pulses, sugar, spices).
- Policy debate: target headline or core? Core is stickier and responds more to policy.
### 3. TYPES OF INFLATION BY CAUSE
| Type | Cause / meaning |
|---|---|
| **Demand-pull** | Aggregate demand > aggregate supply (full-employment output); **expansionary policy, fiscal stimulus, public spending, loose money, higher purchasing power** – "too much money chasing too few goods" (UPSC 2021) |
| **Cost-push** | Rising input costs – **wages, raw materials, fuel, rupee fall** → supply falls; e.g. West Asia oil shock 2026 |
| **Bottleneck** | Supply falls sharply while demand stays same; structural shortages (farm productivity, storage, distribution); hard for government to tackle |
| **Stagflation** | **High inflation + slow growth + unemployment** |
| **Export-led** | Strong export demand drains domestic supply |
| **Skewflation** | Prices rise sharply in **a few items** (e.g. food) while overall inflation stays low |
| **Reflation** | Inflation returning after deflation |
!TRAP Demand-pull is NOT caused by inflation-indexing of wages or rising interest rates.
- **Cost-push pressure falls** with a **permanent fall in input costs**.
### 4. CAUSES OF INFLATION IN INDIA
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Supply shocks** (dominant) | Erratic **monsoon**, **El Nino** (vegetables), costlier **crude oil**, **rupee depreciation**; West Asia conflict raised fuel and input costs in 2026 |
| **Demand side** | Public expenditure, loose monetary policy, higher incomes |
| **Other** | **MSP hikes**, **hoarding**, **inflation expectations** |
- Better **storage and distribution efficiency** eases supply shocks.
- **Stagflation factors**: inflation from **higher government spending** + **fall in industrial production** and slow growth (APPSC G2 2025: a and b only; an older question took all four listed factors).
- Crude benchmarks: **Brent** (North Sea), **West Texas Intermediate (WTI)** and **Dubai** – three main benchmarks.
### 5. EFFECTS OF INFLATION AND DEFLATION
#### 5.1 Gainers and Losers
| Group | Who |
|---|---|
| **Gainers** | **Debtors** (repay money of lower real value), producers, businessmen, **real-asset holders** |
| **Losers** | **Creditors** (hurt most), **fixed-income groups** (pensioners, salaried without DA), **savers**, **bond-holders** |
- Rule: **debtors gain, creditors lose**.
- Fixed-income groups are exposed unless incomes are adjusted – **not automatically protected**.
- **Food inflation is regressive** – the poor spend a bigger share on food; food and beverages ≈ **37%** of the CPI basket.
- Price-hike: businessmen profit and consumers cut needs – both true, but the second does not explain the first.
- Other costs: **menu costs** (repeated re-pricing), uncertainty that hurts saving and investment.
#### 5.2 Effects of Deflation
- Purchasing power **rises**.
- **Deflationary spiral**: delayed purchases → lower profits → **higher unemployment** → heavier **real debt burden**.
### 6. PRICE INDICES – PUBLISHER, BASE YEAR, WEIGHTS
#### 6.1 Who Publishes What
| Index | Compiled by | Base year | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CPI-Combined** (Rural, Urban, Combined) | **NSO, MoSPI** | **2024** (replaced 2012 series; first release 12 Feb 2026 for Jan 2026) | **Headline inflation**; monetary policy target |
| **CPI-IW** | **Labour Bureau** (not NSO) | **2016** (from Sep 2020) | **DA** of industrial workers and Central Government employees; urban poverty |
| **CPI-AL** | Labour Bureau | **2019** (from June 2025; earlier 1986-87) | Farm labourers' wages; rural poverty |
| **CPI-RL** | Labour Bureau | **2019** (from June 2025; earlier 1986-87) | Rural labourers' wages |
| **WPI** | **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry** | **2011-12** (revised from 2004-05 in 2017); new series **2022-23** | Contract escalation, industrial analysis |
| **CFPI** | NSO | As CPI | Food inflation |
| **GDP deflator** | NSO | Series base 2022-23 | Widest measure |
- **CPI-IW base**: **1982 → 2001 → 2016** (w.e.f. September 2020).
- **CPI-AL linking factor**: **AP 10.72**, India 9.69 (CPI-RL 9.78).
- **Labour Bureau**: est. **1920**, **Chandigarh**, **Ministry of Labour and Employment**; also collects data on industrial disputes, closures, retrenchments, lay-offs.
- **Odd one out** among CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL, CPI-UNME: **CPI-UNME** (Urban Non-Manual Employees – not a Labour Bureau series).
- National income and GDP estimates – **NSO** under MoSPI (earlier **CSO**).
- **IIP** measures **industrial output, not prices**; NSO, monthly on the 28th, 14 source agencies.
!KEY CPI = NSO; CPI-IW/AL/RL = Labour Bureau; WPI = Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT.
#### 6.2 New CPI (Base 2024) Structure
- **12 divisions** under **COICOP 2018** (earlier 6 groups).
- **358 items** (308 goods, 50 services).
- Weights: **food and beverages 36.75%**, housing 17.67% (old 2012 series food ~46%).
- Added rural house rent and **12 online platforms** (Feb 2026).
- RBI now uses **CPI (Combined), base 2024 = 100**.
#### 6.3 WPI Structure
- **2011-12 series**: **697 items** – primary 117, **manufactured 564**, fuel and power 16; **no services**.
- **Manufactured products** = largest weight, **64.23%** (~64%).
- **New WPI (base 2022-23)**: **957 items**; weights on **Gross Value of Output**; runs with a new **Producer Price Index (PPI)** for 5 years, then WPI phased out.
- **PPI**: average change in prices of produced goods (output PPI: leaving production; input PPI: entering it).
- New WPI series on base 2004-05 started on **14 September 2010**.
- WPI was long called India's primary inflation measure.
### 7. CPI VS WPI VS GDP DEFLATOR
| Basis | CPI | WPI | GDP deflator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Measures | **Retail** prices | **Wholesale** (first-point bulk) prices | All goods and services produced |
| Services | Included | **Excluded** | Included |
| Publisher | NSO | Office of the Economic Adviser | NSO |
| Biggest weight | **Food** (more than WPI – UPSC 2020) | **Manufactured goods** | – |
- **GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100**; the **broadest** measure.
- A **rise in the GDP deflator** → **fall in purchasing power** of money (APPSC G2 2025).
- GDP deflator is **not** used for industrial workers' DA (that is CPI-IW).
- **Dearness allowance basis**: **CPI-IW**.
- India measures inflation on CPI, WPI and the labour cost-of-living indices; RBI now uses CPI (Combined).
### 8. HEADLINE MEASURE, INFLATION TARGETING AND COMMITTEES
#### 8.1 From WPI to CPI
- Earlier **WPI** = headline measure, the main index **till April 2014**.
- **April 2014**: RBI adopted **CPI (Combined)** – **Urjit Patel Committee** framework.
- Present headline inflation = **CPI-Combined (CPI-C)**; the most common measure in India.
#### 8.2 Flexible Inflation Targeting
| Act / step | Year | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Urjit Patel Committee | 2014 | CPI as nominal anchor; flexible inflation targeting |
| **Monetary Policy Framework Agreement** | **Feb 2015** | Led to RBI Act amendment |
| **RBI Act, 1934 amended** | **2016** | MPC; statutory flexible inflation targeting |
| **Section 45ZA** | – | **Target-setting** by the Centre with RBI every 5 years |
| **Section 45ZB** | – | **Six-member MPC** |
| **Section 45ZN** | First invoked **2022** | RBI reports reasons to Government when band is breached for **3 consecutive quarters** |
- **Target (1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2031)**: **4% CPI-Combined, ±2% (2-6%)**, kept by Gazette notification.
- **6%** is the **upper limit**, not the target; **WPI and core CPI are not the target index**.
- After flexible inflation targeting, average CPI fell from **6.8% (2012-16)** to about **4.9%**.
#### 8.3 Committees on Price Indices
| Committee | Subject |
|---|---|
| **Urjit Patel (2014)** | Monetary policy framework; CPI as anchor |
| **Abhijit Sen** | Working group on **WPI revision** (2007; report **May 2008**); base **2004-05** |
| **Saumitra Chaudhuri** | Working group on WPI revision (set up March 2012); base **2011-12** |
| Ramesh Chand (NITI Aayog) | Working group for WPI base **2022-23** (2025) |
!TRAP Abhijit Sen = 2004-05 WPI base; Saumitra Chaudhuri = 2011-12 base; Urjit Patel = CPI anchor, not WPI.
### 9. PHILLIPS CURVE
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **A.W. Phillips (1958)** | **Inverse** relation between **unemployment and (wage) inflation** – lower unemployment comes with higher inflation |
| **Friedman** | **Long-run** Phillips curve is **vertical**; no lasting trade-off |
- **India's curve is fairly flat** (2012-2026 IIP-CPI data): about **92% of workers informal** → weak wage bargaining.
- **Stagflation risk**: rate hikes cut output and jobs without lowering supply-driven inflation much.

### 11. CONTROL MEASURES AND INFLATION-INDEXED BONDS
#### 11.1 Tools
| Type | Tools |
|---|---|
| **Monetary (RBI)** | Raise **repo, CRR**; **OMO sale of securities**; tighten liquidity; anchor expectations; repo **5.25%** (Aug 2026), hike expected in October |
| **Fiscal (Government)** | Cut deficit and spending, **raise taxes**; lower indirect taxes on essentials |
| **Supply-side** | **OMSS (Domestic)** – **FCI sells wheat and rice**; **Price Stabilisation Fund** buffer of **pulses and onions**; **buffer-stock release** – 47,786 MT onion by NCCF and NAFED (to 8 Oct 2025) |
| **Subsidised sales** | **Bharat Atta, Bharat Rice, Bharat Dal** (Bharat Chana Dal from July 2023 at ₹60/kg) via **NAFED, NCCF, Kendriya Bhandar** |
| **Trade and stock** | Onion export ban lifted **4 May 2024** with 40% duty and US$550 MEP; MEP removed and duty cut to 20% on **13 Sep 2024**; stock limits |
- **OMO sale of securities** = **monetary**; **buffer-stock release** = **supply-side**; **taxes and spending** = **fiscal**.
- **Bharat Atta** helped ease cereal prices (APPSC G1 2024: key II and IV).
!TRAP Repo rate works on aggregate demand and liquidity; it **cannot directly fix supply shocks** (onion, crude oil).
!TRAP OMSS = wheat and rice by FCI; pulses and onions belong to the PSF buffer.
#### 11.2 AP Measures
- **State-level Price Monitoring Committee** headed by the **Chief Secretary**.
- **DES monitors 8 essential commodities daily** (groundnut and palm oil added Feb 2025).
#### 11.3 Inflation-Indexed Bonds (IIBs)
- **Principal and/or interest linked to an inflation index**; protects the **real return**.
- **RBI issued IIBs in 2013**.
- Investors: hedge against inflation.
- Government: cheaper borrowing when credible on inflation; signals commitment to low inflation.
