## U0312 | PARLIAMENTARY FORM OF GOVERNMENT – FEATURES, MERITS, DEMERITS; COMPARISON WITH PRESIDENTIAL SYSTEM
### 1. MEANING OF PARLIAMENTARY GOVERNMENT
- Also called **cabinet** or **responsible government**: stays in power while it has a **majority in the popular House**.
- Based on **cooperation and co-ordination** of legislature and executive (**fusion of powers**); presidential = **separation of powers**.
- India adopted the **British** model (influenced by the UK); parliamentary system at the **Centre and in the States**; both get power from the Constitution.
!KEY Defining test: India is parliamentary because the **executive is responsible to the legislature** (Council of Ministers headed by PM, to the Lok Sabha) – A and R both true, R explains A (UPPSC 2025).
### 2. FEATURES OF PARLIAMENTARY GOVERNMENT (INDIA)
1. **Nominal and real executive**: **President** = de jure head (Head of State); **PM + Council of Ministers** = real executive. Executive powers of the Union are vested in the President.
2. **Art. 74(1)**: President acts on **aid and advice**; advice **binding**.
3. **Majority party rule**: leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha becomes PM.
4. **Collective responsibility** to the Lok Sabha (**Art. 75**); ministers individually responsible to the **President**.
5. **Dual membership**: ministers must be MPs; a **non-member** must enter Parliament within **six months**, else stops being minister.
6. **Leadership of the PM**: heads Council of Ministers; **Leader of the Lok Sabha**; chief spokesperson.
7. **Dissolution** of the lower House possible.
8. Government can be **removed by Parliament** (no-confidence); ministers stay while they keep its confidence.
!TRAP The President is **not** the real head of the executive (APPSC G2 2017). India is **not** a presidential system.
!TRAP India is democratic and has a written constitution, but the written constitution does **not** explain democracy (UK is democratic without one).
### 3. PRESIDENTIAL SYSTEM – FEATURES
- President = **Head of State and Head of Government**; **single executive**; power centralised.
- **Directly elected**; **fixed term**; removable only by **impeachment**.
- **Not accountable** to the legislature; **cannot dissolve** the legislature.
- **Appoints his own Council of Ministers** (secretaries); US secretaries do not sit in the legislature.
!TRAP APPSC G2 2025 key: head of State, head of Government, directly elected = correct; "**accountable to the legislature**" = **wrong**.
#### 3.1 Parliamentary vs Presidential
| Parliamentary | Presidential |
|---|---|
| Nominal head + real head (PM) | One person is both heads |
| **Collective** executive (cabinet) | **Single** executive |
| **Fusion** of powers; ministers are legislators | **Separation** of powers |
| Removable by **no-confidence** | **Fixed tenure**; only impeachment |
| Lower House can be dissolved early | No dissolution |
### 4. ORIGIN COUNTRIES AND SEMI-PRESIDENTIAL SYSTEMS
| System | Origin | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Parliamentary** | **Britain** (Westminster model) | UK, Japan, Canada, India |
| **Presidential** | **USA** – oldest and purest form | USA, Brazil, most of Latin America |
| **Semi-presidential** | — | **France** (Fifth Republic, **1958**), **Russia**, **Sri Lanka** (**1978** Constitution) |
- **Semi-presidential**: **directly elected President with real powers** + **PM responsible to the legislature**; both may be from different parties.
- India's President is **indirectly elected** and **nominal** – India is not semi-presidential.
!PAIR Westminster | Britain | Parliamentary || Oldest presidential | USA | Presidential
### 5. INDIA VS BRITAIN
| Feature | India | Britain |
|---|---|---|
| Head of State | **Indirectly elected President**, 5 years | **Hereditary monarch** |
| Supremacy | **Constitutional supremacy** (written Constitution) | **Sovereignty of Parliament** |
| Constitution | Written | Unwritten (conventions) |
| Judicial review | Courts can strike down laws; **basic structure** | Courts cannot question Acts of Parliament |
| PM from | **Either House** | **House of Commons** |
| Ministers | Six months as non-member allowed | Must be MPs |
| **Shadow cabinet** / legal responsibility of ministers | No | Yes |
- **Latest:** UK PM **Keir Starmer** announced resignation as PM and Labour leader. UK: ruling party can replace the PM; India: removal needs loss of **Lok Sabha confidence**.
### 6. INDIA VS USA
| Feature | India | USA |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | Parliamentary | Presidential |
| **Residuary powers** | **Parliament (Union)** | **States** |
| Judicial review | Narrower – "**procedure established by law**" | Wider – "**due process of law**"; SC called a "third chamber" |
| Term limit | **None** for PM | **22nd Amendment (1951)** |
- **Common**: written Constitution, federal structure, independent judiciary with judicial review, Fundamental Rights / Bill of Rights.
- **Latest:** on **22 March 2026**, **Narendra Modi** completed **8,931 days** as head of an elected government, passing **Pawan Kumar Chamling** (8,930).
### 7. MERITS AND DEMERITS
| System | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| Parliamentary | **Responsible government** – executive **continuously accountable**; harmony; fresh elections break deadlock | Unstable coalitions; no fixed tenure; ministers not specialists; **cabinet dictatorship** with large majority |
| Presidential | **Stable**, fixed-term executive | **Executive-legislature deadlock**; **personality cult** |
!KEY Main advantage of parliamentary government: executive stays **responsible to the legislature** (collective responsibility, Art. 75).
### 8. CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY'S CHOICE
- **Familiarity** under the **1919 and 1935 Acts**.
- India's **vast size and diversity**.
- Avoid **US-style executive-legislature conflict** and a personality cult.
- **Dr B.R. Ambedkar**: a democracy cannot maximise both **stability and responsibility**; India chose **responsibility** over fixed tenure.
### 9. PRESIDENTIAL SYSTEM DEBATE IN INDIA
| Person / body | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Vasant Sathe** | Proposed a presidential form, **1970s-80s** |
| **NCRWC** (Venkatachaliah, 2000-02) | Advised **keeping** the parliamentary system |
- **One Nation, One Election**: amendments to Arts **83, 172, 327**; critics say fixed tenures graft presidential mechanics and weaken accountability.
- **Shamsher Singh (1974)**: Governor publicly criticising Cabinet policy runs "**counter to the Parliamentary system**"; Governor's discretion also rests on parliamentary logic.
