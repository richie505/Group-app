## U0188 | ANDHRA AND SOUTH INDIA IN 1857 – LIMITED RESPONSE; EARLIER LOCAL UPRISINGS (RAMPA, PALEGAR REVOLTS)
### 1. OVERVIEW – RESISTANCE IN ANDHRA BEFORE AND DURING 1857
- South India's revolts were **local and scattered**; the **Nizam's loyalty** kept the Deccan closed to 1857.
- It took the Company **50–60 years** to consolidate its hold on Andhra: **zamindars** on the coast + **palegars** in Rayalaseema.
- The 1846 Uyyalawada revolt was the **last major palegar rising** in Rayalaseema, a decade before 1857.

#### 2.1 Battle of Bobbili, 1757

### 3. VIZIANAGARAM RISING AND THE BATTLE OF PADMANABHAM, 1794
#### 3.1 Background
- **Chinna Vijayarama Raju** (ruled 1760–94) removed step-brother and Diwan **Sitarama Raju** for misrule (1784); Sitarama Raju bribed Council members and got his post back.
- Company demanded **Rs 3 lakh** tribute + **disbanding of troops**; the Raja refused.
- **2 Aug 1793**: British seized the **Vizianagaram fort**; estate split into **3 parts**; Raja ordered to live at **Machilipatnam** on a pension of **Rs 1,200**.
#### 3.2 The Battle
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Place | **Padmanabham**, between Bhimunipatnam and Vizianagaram |
| Date | **10 July 1794** |
| Parties | **East India Company** vs Vizianagaram zamindar **Pusapati Chinna Vijayarama Gajapati Raju II** |
| British commander | **Colonel Prendergast** (also spelt Pendergast) |
| Cause | British **revenue demands** |
| Result | British victory; the Raja and **about 1,000 Kshatriya warriors** killed; compared to **Culloden** |
#### 3.3 Outcome
- Vizianagaram reduced to an **ordinary zamindari**.
- Minor son **Narayana Babu** (hid at **Makkuva**) restored on accepting the **Permanent Settlement (1802)** + **Rs 6 lakh peshkash**.
!TRAP Bobbili 1757 = Vizianagaram + French vs Bobbili; **Padmanabham 1794 = Company vs Vizianagaram** – it decided the defeat of Vizianagaram, not Bobbili.

### 5. PALEGARS / POLIGARS AND THEIR REVOLTS
#### 5.1 Who Were the Palegars?
- **Poligars/Palegars** = **local military chiefs** and long-time **revenue farmers**; fortified chiefs with self-rule under the **Nizam's nominal suzerainty**.
- Dozens in **Kurnool, Cuddapah, Bellary, Anantapur**.
#### 5.2 Causes of Resistance in the Ceded Districts
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Cession of 1800 | Nizam ceded **Cuddapah, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary** under the **Subsidiary Alliance (1798)** |
| Revenue burden | Heavy revenue + **peshkash** |
| Estates | Estates taken / forfeited |
| Disarmament | **Thomas Munro** ordered palegars to **disarm and pay peshkash** |
- 1800–01: **About 80 palegars** resisted Munro.
- **1801, Taranikallu (Adoni division)**: Patel **Mutakuri Goudappa** attacked the Sub-Collector's office → hanged by Sub-Collector **Dhakar** (not Munro).
#### 5.3 Suppression
- Officer: **Thomas Munro, Principal Collector** (PYQ 2011) + military officer **Campbell**.
- Methods: proclamations to lay down arms → **18 months** of military action → **confiscation** of estates → small **pensions** for loyal heirs.
- Settlement afterwards: **Ryotwari** in the Ceded Districts.
!TRAP Settlement in the Ceded Districts = **Ryotwari**, NOT Permanent Settlement.
#### 5.4 Poligar Revolts in Tamil Nadu and Travancore
- The Company's **1781** deal letting the **Nawab of Arcot** manage Tirunelveli and Carnatic revenue hit poligars' rights.
| Leader | Area | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Veerapandya Kattabomman** | Panchalankurichi (Tirunelveli) | Hanged at Kayattar, **1799** |
| **Marudu brothers** | **Sivaganga** | **South Indian Rebellion**; executed at Tiruppathur, **1801** |
| **Velu Thampi Dalawa** | Travancore (Diwan) | Revolt **1808–09**; **Kundara Proclamation**, 11 Jan 1809 |
!TRAP Kundara Proclamation = **Velu Thampi**, not Munro.

### 8. HYDERABAD IN 1857
#### 8.1 Persons
| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| **Mubariz-ud-Daula** (Nizam's brother) | Led anti-British **Wahabi Movement (1839)**; arrested; jailed in **Golconda Fort** |

!KEY APPSC G2 2001: Nizam of 1857 = **Afzal-ud-Daula**. PYQ 2011: Wahabi Movement (1839) = **Mubariz-ud-Daula**.
#### 8.2 Risings in Order (APPSC G1 2023)
1. **Mubariz-ud-Daula** (Wahabi leader) imprisoned in **Golconda Fort**.
2. **June 1857, Aurangabad**: **1st Cavalry of the Hyderabad Contingent** disarmed; **Mir Fida Ali** revolted; suppressed by **Capt. Abbot**; Fida Ali hanged.
3. **Jamedar Chida Khan** arrested and held at Hyderabad.
4. **17 July 1857**: **Turrebaz Khan** with **Maulvi Allauddin** attacked the Residency to free Chida Khan; repulsed by **Resident Davidson**.
5. Turrebaz Khan escaped, was caught and **shot near Toopran (1859)**; Allauddin was sent to the **Andamans**.
#### 8.3 Rewards to Loyalists
- **Afzal-ud-Daula** got the **"Star of India"**; Salar Jung honoured separately.
- **Raichur Doab + Dharashiv (Osmanabad)** restored to Hyderabad; its **debt written off**.

### 10. LIMITED RESPONSE, REPRESSION AND CONSEQUENCES
#### 10.1 Reasons for the Limited Response
- **Nizam and Salar Jung loyal**; Madras Presidency quiet.
- **Zamindars gained** from the Permanent Settlement.
- People valued **Company stability** over the "earlier anarchy"; **loyalty meetings** at **Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Vijayawada**.
- Revolt lacked central leadership, a programme and a base in the south.
!TRAP APPSC G2 2025 A/R: A "Andhra was unaffected because it had **no local leaders**" = **FALSE** (Turrebaz Khan, Subba Reddy, Dandasena, Kadiri attempt); R "Afzal-ud-Daula pro-British" = **TRUE** → answer **(4)**.
#### 10.2 Who Did and Did Not Rebel
- **Nizam of Hyderabad did NOT rebel** against the Company.
- **Did rebel**: Raja of **Vizianagaram**, **Polygars** of Tamil Nadu, Diwan **Velu Thampi** of Travancore.
#### 10.3 Repression
- **Subba Reddy, Dandasena, Fida Ali hanged**; Sheikh Peer Sahib arrested; across India thousands executed after mock trials.
#### 10.4 Crown Rule in Madras Presidency
