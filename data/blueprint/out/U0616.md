## U0616 | HYDROLOGICAL DISASTERS – FLOODS, FLASH FLOODS, CLOUDBURSTS, DROUGHTS, TSUNAMIS, STORM SURGES
### 1. DISASTERS – MEANING AND CLASSIFICATION
- **Disaster = Hazard × Vulnerability.**
- Natural disasters cause most damage in **developing countries**; India is **not** disaster-free.
#### 1.1 Classification
| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Hydrological / hydro-meteorological** | Floods, flash floods, cloudbursts, storm surges |
| **Climatological** | Droughts, heatwaves, cold waves |
| **Geological** | **Earthquakes**, tsunamis, landslides, avalanches |
| **Man-made (chemical/industrial, nuclear)** | **Bhopal gas tragedy**, Chernobyl |
#### 1.2 Onset
| Rapid-onset | Slow-onset |
|---|---|
| Cyclone, **flood**, tsunami, flash flood, cloudburst | **Drought** – a **"creeping emergency / creeping hazard"** |
#### 1.3 Secondary Hazards
| Primary | Secondary hazard |
|---|---|
| Cloudburst | **Flash flood** |
| Flash flood | **Landslide** |
| River flood | Bank erosion |
| Flood | Water contamination, disease, crop loss |
| Coastal flood | **Salinity intrusion** |
| Dam failure | Downstream flash flooding |
| Urban flood | Infrastructure collapse |
### 2. INDIA'S VULNERABILITY PROFILE
| Hazard | Exposure | Region |
|---|---|---|
| **Earthquake** | ~**58.6%** of landmass (moderate to very high) | Himalaya, Kutch |
| **Flood** | >**40 million ha (~12%)**; Assam alone 31.05 lakh ha | Brahmaputra-Ganga plains |
| **Drought** | ~**68%** of cultivable area | Rajasthan, Deccan interior |
| **Cyclone/tsunami** | ~**5,700 km** of the ~7,500 km (old 7,516.6 km) coastline | East coast |
| **Landslide** | ~**12.6%** of land (~0.42 million sq km) | Himalaya, Western Ghats (not Thar) |
- **27 of 36** States/UTs are disaster-prone.
- Cyclones affect **13 coastal States/UTs** and **84 coastal districts**.
- **World Risk Index 2025**: India **2nd** most vulnerable, after the **Philippines**. (Global Climate Risk Index 2019: India 14th.)
- India loses about **0.4% of GDP** a year to disasters (1990–2024), mainly floods and storms; China and Indonesia face higher seismic risk.
- **2025**: ~331 days of climate impacts; **over 4,000 deaths**; heaviest in **Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**.
- Emerging Asia (OECD): ~100 disasters a year affecting ~80 million people. Water-related disasters worldwide (2025): ~5,000 deaths, 8 million displaced.
#### 2.1 Displacement Data (IDMC)
- **IDMC** (Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre, **Geneva**) publishes the **Global Report on Internal Displacement (GRID)**.
- IDMC (2020 to May 2021): India **seventh** worst climate-disaster-hit country.
- **GRID 2025**: India had **5.4 million** disaster displacements in **2024** (highest in 12 years; floods about two-thirds); over **2015–2024** India recorded **32.3 million**, **3rd** after China and the Philippines.
### 3. FLOODS – NATURE, EXTENT AND TYPES
#### 3.1 Nature and Extent
- Overflow of water onto normally dry land when a channel cannot contain it; **rapid-onset**; the **most frequent natural disaster** in India; can be **mitigated through early warning**.
- Of India's **329 mha**, more than **40 mha** is flood-prone (**Rashtriya Barh Ayog**); a recent estimate is about **49 mha**. About 10% of India is flood-affected.
- **Most flood-prone state: Bihar**; worst-hit **Assam and Bihar**, then **UP and West Bengal**. Lower Assam is flood-prone (not drought-prone).
- **Flood control is a State subject**; the Centre helps through **FMBAP**.
#### 3.2 Types of Flood
| Type | Cause | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Riverine** | Discharge exceeds channel capacity (monsoon) | Ganga, Brahmaputra, Kosi, Godavari; crop damage, bank erosion |
| **Flash** | Sudden intense rain, **cloudbursts** | Himalaya, steep catchments, cities |
| **Urban** | Rain + poor drainage, wetland encroachment | Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Delhi |
| **Coastal** | Storm surges, cyclones, high tides | East and west coasts; inundation, salinity |
| **GLOF** | Glacier retreat swells a **proglacial lake**, which bursts | Uttarakhand, Sikkim |
| **Dam failure** | Breach | Catastrophic downstream flooding |
### 4. FLOODS – CAUSES, MANAGEMENT AND COMMITTEES
#### 4.1 Causes
| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| Heavy / prolonged rainfall | Discharge rises fast |
| Cloudburst | Very intense rain over a small area |
| Snowmelt | Sudden rise in river flow |
| **Siltation** (from soil erosion) | Reduces channel depth and capacity |
| Embankment failure | Water escapes the channel |
| **Deforestation** | Increases runoff |
| Floodplain encroachment | Raises human exposure |
| **Poor drainage** | Water accumulates |
| **Dam failure** | Sudden downstream discharge |
!KEY APPSC 2025: Flood causes = **heavy rainfall, poor drainage, deforestation, dam failure**.
- **Dam failure** causes: weak foundation, design/material failure; extreme flood, landslides, earthquakes; **overtopping** (water spills over the crest); sedimentation reduces storage.
- Prolonged rain can cause landslides that block rivers: **Pareechu river (Tibet)**, August 2004 landslide lake – flood threat on the **Sutlej** in Himachal Pradesh.
#### 4.2 Measures
| Structural | Non-structural |
|---|---|
| **Dams and reservoirs**, channel and drainage improvement, diversion | **Flood insurance**, **flood-plain zoning**, flood-proofing, flood fighting |
- **Nature-based buffers**: wetlands store water (restoring them reduces urban floods); floodplains store water; **forests increase infiltration and interception**; mangroves buffer coasts; riparian plants hold banks.
- **Odisha Flood Hazard Atlas**: built from satellite images of floods **2000–2018**.
#### 4.3 Committees and Cooperation
| Body | Year | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Rashtriya Barh Ayog** (National Flood Commission) | Set up **1976**; report **1980** | Assessed ~40 mha flood-prone |
| Expert Committee for Scientific Assessment of Flood-Prone Areas | **2012** | Set up by **Ministry of Water Resources** |
| India-China Expert Level Mechanism on flood data | Apr 2025 | 16th meeting |
### 5. FLASH FLOODS, CLOUDBURSTS AND GLOFS
#### 5.1 Cloudburst
- **IMD definition**: at least **100 mm (10 cm) in one hour** over about **20–30 sq km**.
- Sudden, highly localised; mostly at **1,000–2,500 m**; causes **flash floods and landslides**; very hard to forecast (Doppler radar, nowcasting).
| Basis | Cloudburst | Ordinary heavy rain |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | Extreme | Varying |
| Timing | Sudden | Can be prolonged |
| Area | Highly localised | Wider |
- **Flash floods**: very rapid onset, short warning time; main danger is debris; also landslides, erosion. **IMD** gives flash-flood guidance for India + **Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal**.
- **HKH "monsoon paradox"**: uneven seasonal rain plus more short, intense cloudbursts.
#### 5.2 Vulnerable Areas
| Region | Areas | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Himalaya | Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, Ladakh, Sikkim | Steep slopes, orographic effect |
| **Western Ghats** | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra | Intense rain on the **windward side** |
| **AP Eastern Ghats agency areas** | Narrow valleys | Cloudburst flash floods |
#### 5.3 Events
| Event | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Leh** cloudburst | 2010 | Ladakh |
| **Kedarnath** | June 2013 | **Cloudburst + GLOF**; Chaurabari Glacier (north of the temple), lake **Gandhi Sarovar** |
| **Chamoli** glacier burst | 2021 | Uttarakhand |
| HP flash floods | 2023 | Unregulated slope construction |
| Mandi (HP) | 2025 | Nearly 20 times normal rain |
| **Dharali-Harsil** (Uttarkashi) | **5 Aug 2025** | Ice-patch collapse on the **Srikanta glacier** (ISRO); diverted the **Bhagirathi** |
| Nepal GLOF floods | 26 Aug 2026 | 26 GLOFs since the 1970s (UNDP) |
- Central Himalaya glacier area down **>20%** in three decades.
### 6. URBAN FLOODING AND MAJOR FLOODS
#### 6.1 Causes of Urban Flooding
- Intense rain, cloudbursts, cyclone rain; **encroached drains and lakes**, **wetland destruction**, impervious surfaces, poor storm drains; high river discharge, **high tides**; unplanned growth, low ground, floodplain construction.
#### 6.2 Major Floods
| Flood | Year | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Mumbai** | **26 July 2005** | **944 mm in 24 h**; drainage failure |
| **Bihar (Kosi)** | 2008 | **Embankment breach** |
| **Chennai** | Dec 2015 | Extreme rain; **wetland destruction** |
| **Kerala** | Aug 2018 | Heavy rain; **dam releases** |
- **AP**: **Vijayawada – Budameru rivulet**; also Visakhapatnam, Guntur. **Krishna-Godavari deltas** flood worst when Srisailam and Nagarjuna Sagar release water with heavy Godavari inflows.
- **AP City Flood Alert (July 2026)**: AI platform by **RTGS + APSDMA + IIT Gandhinagar**; 48-hour rain forecasts, 6-hour flash-flood warnings; satellite DEM + ~30 years of Budameru data; first in **Vijayawada**, later all ULBs.
- AP: significant crop losses from floods (true); the claim that PMFBY farmer coverage in AP has been increasing is **false** (PYQ key).
### 7. DROUGHTS
#### 7.1 Nature and Types
- Prolonged shortage of rain or moisture → water scarcity; **slow-onset**; hits agriculture, drinking water, industry.
| Type | Indicator | Time lag |
|---|---|---|
| **Meteorological** | Rain **≥ 25% below LPA** | Immediate |
| **Agricultural** | Soil-moisture deficit → crop failure | Intermediate |
| **Hydrological** | Fall in reservoirs, rivers, aquifers | Weeks to months |
| **Socio-economic** | Demand exceeds supply; water/food shortage hits livelihoods and markets | Long-term |
| **Ecological** | Ecosystem productivity collapses | — |
- **IMD**: deficiency **26–50%** of LPA = **moderate**; **over 50%** = **severe** drought.
#### 7.2 Triggers and Consequences
- Triggers: delayed/erratic monsoon, monsoon failure and breaks, high heat and evaporation; **El Nino** (6 of 10 El Nino years depressed Indian rain). Normal SW-monsoon rain: **868.6 mm** (1971–2020).
- Consequences: **groundwater depletion**, land degradation, **desertification**, **distress migration**, rural indebtedness, lower farm output, ecosystem damage.
#### 7.3 Drought-prone Regions
- **Western Rajasthan, Kutch-Saurashtra, Marathwada-Vidarbha, interior Karnataka, Rayalaseema (AP)**.
- **Rayalaseema**: monsoon failure, crop loss, distress migration; **deep borewells** worsen groundwater loss.
### 8. TSUNAMIS
#### 8.1 Nature and Causes
- Japanese **"tsu" = harbour + "nami" = wave**; a natural disaster; **not a "tidal wave"** (tides come from Moon and Sun gravity).
- Series of waves from **sudden vertical displacement of the ocean floor** – mainly **undersea earthquakes at subduction zones**; also submarine landslides, volcanic eruptions, explosions, asteroid/meteorite impacts.
- Can occur at any time of day or night. Warning signs: **sudden receding of the sea**, rapid sea rise, quake shaking.
#### 8.2 Behaviour
| Deep ocean | Near the coast |
|---|---|
| Very long wavelength; height **< 1 m**; speed **~700–800 km/h**; barely noticed | **Slows down**; height rises sharply (shoaling); massive inundation |
- Impact is less over the ocean, more near the coast – **speed depends on water depth**.
#### 8.3 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami
- **26 Dec 2004**; **Sumatra-Andaman earthquake, Mw ~9.1**; origin **Indian Ocean** (APPSC 2025); >12,000 deaths in India.
- Hit **Tamil Nadu, AP, Puducherry, Kerala, A&N**; **worst-hit state Tamil Nadu**, worst district **Nagapattinam** (APPSC 2025).
- **Mangroves** acted as a safety hedge – prop roots and pneumatophores resist uprooting.
#### 8.4 Risk Zones
| Risk | States/UTs |
|---|---|
| **Very high** | **Andaman & Nicobar** (near the Sunda subduction zone) |
| High | Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Odisha |
| Moderate | Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat |
- Sources: **Sunda-Andaman** subduction zone (east); **Makran zone** threatens the **west coast**.
- **Tsunami warning system** (MoES/INCOIS) warns **25 Indian Ocean countries within 10 minutes**; **NIOT Nellore's OMNI buoys** feed it.
### 9. STORM SURGES AND COASTAL HAZARDS
- **Storm surge**: abnormal sea rise pushed by **cyclone winds + low pressure**; a high tide raises the baseline; **sea-level rise** worsens surge flooding and inundates low deltas; brings **salinity intrusion**.
- **East coast** gets far more cyclones than the west. **AP** is the most cyclone-prone state; **coastal Odisha** is the most cyclone-prone area in one PYQ key (mangrove loss in the **Mahanadi delta**).
- **Coastal defence**: **mangroves**, shelter belts, dune stabilisation, seawalls; mangroves reduce cyclone impact.
#### 9.1 Coastline Remeasured (2023–24)
- By the **National Hydrographic Office** with **Survey of India**; reported by MHA, **Dec 2024**: **11,098.81 km**.
| State | Coastline | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| **Gujarat** | 2,340.62 km | 1 |
| **Tamil Nadu** | — | 2 |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | **1,053.07 km** | **3** |
!TRAP AP is now **3rd**, not 2nd; Tamil Nadu is second under the revised figures.
- **AP angle**: coastal belt – cyclones and storm surges; deltas – floods; **Rayalaseema – drought**.
### 10. LANDSLIDES AND AVALANCHES
#### 10.1 Landslides
- Mass movement of rock, debris or earth down a slope or riverbank under **gravity**; both **geological and hydro-meteorological**; mostly in **hilly terrain** (APPSC 2025); **heavy rainfall** is the main trigger in India.
| Region | Areas | Triggers |
|---|---|---|
| NW Himalaya | J&K, HP, Uttarakhand | Earthquakes, rain, **road cutting** |
| NE Himalaya | Sikkim, Arunachal, Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Meghalaya | Heavy rain, **jhum cultivation** |
| Western Ghats, Konkan | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Goa, **Nilgiris** | Monsoon rain, deforestation |
| **Eastern Ghats** | **Araku region (AP)** | Local heavy rain |
- Rising frequency: unregulated construction on unstable slopes, deforestation + road widening (**Char Dham**: 43 ha diverted, ~7,000 deodars approved for felling, 23 Jan 2026), cloudbursts, deglaciation. **Increased forestation is NOT a cause.**
- Institutions: **GSI** – nodal agency; **National Landslide Forecasting Centre**; National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping. **NDMA Guidelines (landslides and snow avalanches) 2009**; **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy 2019**; **TAC** for Landslide Mitigation formed by the **Ministry of Mines**. Recent event: **Wayanad, Kerala, 2024**.
#### 10.2 Avalanches
- Rapid flow of snow, ice and debris down a mountain slope; in high Himalaya – **J&K (most affected)**, Ladakh, **HP (Lahaul-Spiti, Kinnaur, Rohtang, Chamba)**, Uttarakhand (Garhwal, Kumaon), Sikkim, Arunachal.
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Slab** | Large cohesive slab breaks off – **most dangerous** |
| Loose snow (sluff) | Small dry slide |
| Ice | Ice masses from glaciers |
| Rock/debris | Rock, soil and snow |
- Causes: heavy snowfall, weak snow layers, slopes of **30°–45°**, wind loading, quakes, rapid warming, rain on snow; human – skiing, mountaineering, explosives, road building.
- **Nodal: Ministry of Defence**; **DGRE** (earlier **SASE**, DRDO) issues **daily winter bulletins**; **BRO** builds roads and protection structures.
- Measures: structural – snow nets, bridges, retaining walls, galleries; non-structural – **hazard zonation**, restricted movement, training.
