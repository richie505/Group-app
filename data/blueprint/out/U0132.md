## U0132 | JAHANGIR AND NUR JAHAN; SHAH JAHAN – DECCAN AND CENTRAL ASIAN POLICY, THE WAR OF SUCCESSION
### 1. JAHANGIR'S REIGN (1605-1627)
- Prince **Salim**, Akbar's eldest son (named after **Sheikh Salim Chishti** of Sikri); crowned **1605** as **Nur-ud-din Muhammad Jahangir**.
| Event | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Khusrau's revolt** | Eldest son (mother **Man Bai**) **1606**; blinded |
| **Guru Arjan Dev** (5th Guru) | Executed **1606** for backing Khusrau – political, not general persecution |
| **Mewar** | **Rana Amar Singh** submitted **1615** (**Treaty of Chittor**); Mewar and Chittorgarh returned; son **Karan Singh** made mansabdar |
| **Kandahar** | Lost to Persia (**Shah Abbas I**) in **1622** |
| **Khurram's revolt** | **1622-26**; Nur Jahan backed **Shahryar** (her son-in-law) as heir |
| **Mahabat Khan's coup** | **1626**: seized Jahangir on the **Jhelum** |
!TRAP APPSC G1 2022: Arjan Dev's execution, Mewar's submission and the English arrival = Jahangir. **Annexation of Kabul and Kandahar** = NOT Jahangir.
### 2. NUR JAHAN AND THE JUNTA
- **Mehrunnisa**, born **1577** at Kandahar; daughter of **Mirza Ghiyas Beg**; widow of **Sher Afghan**; childhood name per APPSC key **Bahu Begam**.
- Married Jahangir **1611**; titles **Nur Jahan** ("Light of the World"), later **Padshah Begum**; poet, pen name **"Makhfi"**.
- **Name on coins and farmans** – a privilege no other Mughal consort had.
- Built the **Tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah, Agra** (marble inlay; precursor of the Taj).
| Junta member (five) | Relation |
|---|---|
| Nur Jahan | Empress |
| **Asmat Begum** | Mother |
| **Ghiyas Beg – Itimad-ud-Daulah** | Father; Jahangir's **diwan** |
| **Asaf Khan** | Brother; Khan-i-Saman; Shah Jahan's father-in-law |
| **Prince Khurram** | Married her niece **Arjumand Banu Begum** (1612) |
!TRAP **Jahangir** and **Mahabat Khan** were NOT junta members.
| Emperor | Diwan |
|---|---|
| Akbar | Muzaffar Khan |
| Jahangir | Itimad-ud-Daulah |

| Person | Relation / title / fate |
|---|---|

### 5. SHAH JAHAN (1628-1658)
- Crowned at **Agra, 1628**. Early revolts: Bundela **Jujhar Singh**; **Khan Jahan Lodi**.
- **Hughli (1632)**: Portuguese defeated over trading abuses.
- Capital moved from Agra to **Shahjahanabad (Delhi)**; built **Lal Qila**, Jama Masjid, **Taj Mahal**.
- **Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus)** in the Diwan-i-Khas; carried away by **Nadir Shah, 1739**.
- Ended **Sijda** (begun by Balban). Slightly more orthodox; kept many Hindu nobles.
- Poet laureate: **Kaleem (Abu Jalih)**.
| Work | Author |
|---|---|
| **Padshahnama** | **Abdul Hamid Lahori** (official historian) |
| **Shahjahannama** | **Inayat Khan** (English: A.R. Fuller) |
| **Masir-i-Alamgiri** | **Saqi Mustaid Khan** (Aurangzeb) |
| **Adab-i-Alamgiri** | Aurangzeb's letters on the father-son money dispute |
| Fatawa-i-Alamgiri | Compiled under **Aurangzeb**, not Shah Jahan |
- **"Golden age"** of Mughal architecture.
- **Captivity**: Agra Fort, nursed by Jahanara, till death in **1666** (about **8 years**).
### 6. CENTRAL ASIAN POLICY – KANDAHAR AND BALKH
| Campaign | Commander | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Kandahar recovered, **1638** | Persian governor **Ali Mardan Khan** surrendered | Mughal gain |
| **Balkh-Badakhshan, 1646-47** | **Murad Bakhsh**, then **Aurangzeb** (not Dara) | Failed, heavy losses |
| Kandahar lost, **1649** | – | To Persia |
| Sieges **1649, 1652** | **Aurangzeb** | Failed |
| Siege **1653** | **Dara Shikoh** | Failed; Kandahar lost for good |
- Kandahar: frontier fortress between India, Persia, Central Asia.
### 7. DECCAN POLICY
- **1636**: **Ahmadnagar** (Nizam Shahi) annexed – Malik Ambar's son **Fath Khan** submitted.
- **1636 ahdnamas** with **Bijapur and Golconda**: Mughal suzerainty, tribute, Mughal arbitration.
- Golconda's **Inqiyad Nama** (Abdullah Qutb Shah): Shah Jahan's name in **khutba and coins**.
- Four Deccan subas: **Khandesh, Berar, Telangana, Daulatabad**.
- **Aurangzeb** as Deccan viceroy: **1636-44** and **1652-58**; attacked Golconda in **1656**, urged by **Mir Jumla**.
| Kingdom | Annexed | Last ruler |
|---|---|---|
| Bijapur | **1686** | **Sikandar Adil Shah** |
| Golconda | **1687** (21st suba) | **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** – exiled to **Daulatabad**, died 1699 |
- AP angle: Jahangir's envoy to Golconda **Mir Makki**; Tana Shah employed **Madanna and Akkanna**, helped Shivaji.
- Marathas: **Sambhaji** executed 1689; **Rajaram** from **Jinji**, then **Tarabai**; **Shahu** released by **Bahadur Shah I**; Aurangzeb died in the Deccan, 1707.
### 8. WAR OF SUCCESSION (1657-59)
| Prince | Post | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| **Dara Shikoh** | Heir, at court | Lost Samugarh, Deorai; betrayed by **Malik Jiwan of Dadar**; executed 1659; buried in **Humayun's Tomb** |
| **Shuja** | Bengal | Fled to **Arakan** |
| **Aurangzeb** | Deccan | Won; crowned 1658 |
| **Murad Bakhsh** | Malwa, Gujarat | Imprisoned in **Gwalior**, executed 1661 |
| Battle | Place | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Bahadurgarh** (Feb 1658) | Near **Banaras** | Sulaiman Shikoh beat Shuja |
| **Dharmat** (15 Apr 1658) | Near **Ujjain (MP)** | Aurangzeb + Murad beat **Jaswant Singh** |
| **Samugarh** (29 May 1658) | Near **Agra** | **Dara** beaten – decisive |
| **Khajwa** (Jan 1659) | Near **Fatehpur** | **Shuja** beaten |
| **Deorai** (Mar 1659) | Near **Ajmer** | Dara's final defeat |
- Aurangzeb took the throne by force – **no rule of primogeniture**.
