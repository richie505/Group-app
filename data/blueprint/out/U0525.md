## U0525 | MONEY SUPPLY – M0 TO M4, HIGH-POWERED MONEY, MONEY MULTIPLIER, VELOCITY
### 1. FUNCTIONS AND KINDS OF MONEY
#### 1.1 Functions of Money
| Class | Functions |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Medium of exchange**; **measure of value** (unit of account) |
| **Secondary** | **Store of value**; standard of deferred payments; transfer of value |
| **Contingent** | **Basis of credit**; distribution of national income; giving liquidity |
!TRAP APPSC 2025: **Production of goods is NOT a function of money**.
#### 1.2 Kinds of Money
| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Hard vs soft money | Hard = backed by gold/credible assets; soft = paper backed by government bonds |
| Bank money vs cash | Bank money = **secondary money**; cash = high-powered money |
| Legal tender vs near money | Legal tender must be accepted; near money is liquid but cannot directly pay for goods |
- **RBI notes = unlimited legal tender**; **coins = limited legal tender**.
- **Digital Rupee** = legal tender under the RBI Act (amended by **Finance Act 2022**).
- **Plastic money** (credit, debit, prepaid cash, smart and store cards; mainly issued by banks) = used in place of bank notes, but **NOT legal tender**.
- Not every highly liquid asset is legal tender.
### 2. MONEY TERMS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Seigniorage** | **Face value minus cost of production** of currency = issuer's profit; a ₹500 note costs about **₹4**; a source of RBI surplus |
| **Legal tender** | Money a creditor is **legally bound** to accept in settlement of claims (UPSC 2018) |
| **Fiat money** | Notes and coins with no intrinsic value; money by government order |
| **Near money** | Time deposits, **T-bills**, bills of exchange, bonds near redemption, savings certificates |
| **Token coin** | Face value **higher** than metal value (rupee coin) |
| **Velocity** | Average number of times a unit of currency changes hands in a period; ₹10 note used twice = **₹20** of transactions |
| Required Reserve Ratio | Fixed percentage of deposits every bank must keep as reserves |
- **Currency expansion** → value of money falls → higher prices.
- A rise in general prices can come from more money supply, lower output and higher effective demand (all three).
- **Money supply is NOT included in a country's wealth**.

### 4. CURRENCY ISSUE AND DEMONETISATION
#### 4.1 Issuing Authority
| Item | Issued by |
|---|---|
| ₹1 note and coins | **Government of India** (Ministry of Finance) |
| Remaining notes | **RBI** |
- Note issue rests on the **Minimum Reserve System** (replaced the proportional reserve system after 1956): ₹200 crore of gold and forex, of which **₹115 crore gold**.
#### 4.2 Demonetisation Episodes
| Year | Notes | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| January **1946** | High-denomination notes (₹1,000 and ₹10,000); by ordinance | Black money, tax evasion |
| **1978** | **₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000**; High Denomination Bank Notes (Demonetisation) Act, under **Morarji Desai** | Black money |
| **8 Nov 2016** | **₹500, ₹1,000** (effective midnight; some keys say 9 Nov 2016); new ₹500 and **₹2,000** issued | Black money, **counterfeit currency (FICN)**, corruption, terror financing |
- Legal basis: **Section 26(2), RBI Act 1934**, on recommendation of RBI's **Central Board**.
- SC upheld 2016 move **4:1** (2023). **₹2,000** withdrawn **May 2023**, still legal tender.
- **FICN**: NCRB Crime in India 2024 → **₹54.61 crore** fake currency seized (about 6 lakh ₹500 + over 1 lakh ₹2,000 notes).
- Debate: black-money gains unproven; cash use not cut; fake notes still seized ten years on.
### 5. HIGH-POWERED MONEY (M0)
- Names: **reserve money, monetary base, base money, central bank money**, high-powered money.
- **Basis of credit creation**; most important money indicator for RBI.
!KEY APPSC Group 2, 2025: **H = currency held by the public + cash reserves of commercial banks (with themselves and with RBI) + other deposits with RBI**. H does **not** include time deposits.
| Side | Components |
|---|---|
| **Uses side** | Currency with public + banks' cash in hand + banks' deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI |
| Sources side | Net RBI credit to Government + RBI credit to banks and commercial sector + net forex assets of RBI + Government's currency liabilities − net non-monetary liabilities of RBI |
- **Currency in circulation** = notes + rupee coins + small coins (held by public + banks' cash in hand).
- **"Other" deposits with RBI**: quasi-government and financial institutions (incl. primary dealers), foreign central banks and governments, international agencies such as the **IMF**.
### 6. MONETARY AGGREGATES M1 TO M4
#### 6.1 Second Working Group on Money Supply (1977)
| Aggregate | Components | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **M0** | Currency in circulation + bankers' deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI | Reserve money |
| **M1** | **Currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI** | **Narrow**, most liquid |
| **M2** | **M1 + post office savings bank deposits** | Narrow |
| **M3** | **M1 + time deposits with banks** | **Broad money** ("aggregate monetary resources") |
| **M4** | M3 + **all post office deposits** (excluding NSCs) | Broad, least liquid |
- **M3** = most commonly used broad money; = currency with public + demand deposits + time deposits + other deposits with RBI (all four).
- **M3 − M1 = time deposits** with banks. Time deposits are **not in M1**.
- **Liquidity order**: **M1 > M2 > M3 > M4**.
- Assets by liquidity: **currency > demand deposits > savings deposits > time deposits**; demand deposits can be withdrawn instantly, time deposits are locked.
- **Time deposits** = most important component of commercial banks' liabilities; then savings and demand deposits.
- Sources of broad money: net bank credit to Government + bank credit to commercial sector + net forex assets of banking sector + Government's currency liabilities − net non-monetary liabilities.
!TRAP **M2 = M1 + PO savings**; **M3 = M1 (not M2) + time deposits**.
#### 6.2 New Aggregates (1998)
- **Third Working Group** (1998), chaired by **Y. V. Reddy** (Money Supply: Analytics and Methodology of Compilation).
- **NM0–NM3**: residency-based; separate short-term from long-term deposits.
- **L1, L2, L3** = **liquidity aggregates**: add post-office deposits, term money and CDs of FIs, NBFC public deposits (**not RBI gold reserves**).
### 7. MONEY MULTIPLIER AND CREDIT CREATION
#### 7.1 Formula and Process
1. RBI creates reserve money (issues notes).
2. Banks keep CRR (with RBI) and SLR (with themselves).
3. They lend the rest; each loan creates a new deposit.
- **m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr)**; cdr = currency-deposit ratio, rdr = reserve-deposit ratio.
- **Money multiplier = M3 ÷ M0** (total money supply / base money).
- 10% reserves, no leakages: m = 1/0.10 = **10**; ₹100 supports about **₹1,000** of deposits.
| Money creator | Creates |
|---|---|
| RBI | Reserve money (notes) |
| Government | Coins and ₹1 note |
| Commercial banks | **Deposit money** by lending beyond CRR/SLR |
#### 7.2 What Moves Money Supply
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Higher CRR, SLR or cdr | **Lower multiplier**; less credit creation |
| Stronger **banking habit** | **Higher multiplier** (UPSC 2019) |
| RBI **buys G-secs** (OMO purchase); Government borrows from RBI; **deficit financing** | **Money supply rises** (injects reserve money) |
| RBI **sells G-secs** (UPSC 2012) | Money supply falls |
| Public deposits currency in banks | Does not raise money supply |
| **Cash withdrawal ₹1 lakh** from demand deposit | **Aggregate money supply unchanged** (demand deposits −₹1 lakh, currency with public +₹1 lakh) |
- **Raising SLR** = contractionary tool used in inflation; locks bank funds, reduces loan funds. An SLR cut frees funds.
- **CRR increase** → banks have less money to lend (more parked with RBI).
- High cash preference (high cdr) limits India's credit multiplier.
### 8. DEMAND FOR MONEY AND VELOCITY
#### 8.1 Theories
| Theory | Economist |
|---|---|
| Quantity theory **MV = PT** | **Irving Fisher** |
| Cash-balance **M = kPY** (k = 1/V) | **Marshall, Pigou** (Cambridge) |
| **Liquidity preference** (transactions, precautionary, speculative motives) | **J. M. Keynes** |
| Inventory model (square-root rule) | **Baumol (1952), Tobin (1956)** |
| Modern quantity theory, monetarism | **Milton Friedman** |
| IS-LM model | J. R. Hicks |
| **Pure Monetary Theory of Trade Cycle** | **R. G. Hawtrey** |
| Austrian cycle theory; free-market economics | Friedrich Hayek |
| Effective demand | Keynes |
- Value of transactions drives money demand; at **higher interest rates** money demand falls; speculative demand varies **inversely** with interest.
!KEY Money supply fixed + money demand **rises** → **interest rate rises**; demand falls → rate falls.
!PAIR MV = PT | Fisher | Transactions || M = kPY | Cambridge | Cash balance
