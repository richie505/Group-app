## U0548 | DEFICIT FINANCING - MEANING, METHODS AND CONSEQUENCES
### 1. MEANING - INDIAN VS WESTERN USAGE
| Usage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Indian** | Budget gap met by **borrowing from the RBI**; RBI creates **new money** (government "buys money") |
| **Western** | Any financing of a budget deficit, mainly borrowing from the **public/market** |
| Effect | **Increase in money supply**; creates extra paper currency |
- Deficit financing is **inflationary by nature**.
- **Fiscal deficit** (Union Budget) = budgetary deficit + net increase in internal and external borrowings and other liabilities.
!TRAP In India, deficit financing = borrowing from the **RBI**, not from the public.
### 2. PURPOSE IN INDIAN PLANNING
- Purpose: **raising resources for economic development**; a "pragmatic tool" to finance the **Five Year Plans**.
- Needed after 1947 because of a **low tax base**, poor compliance, weak private banking and **low domestic savings** (not a high savings rate).
#### 2.1 Phases and Plans
- Independence to **late 1960s**: little use, to fund plans.
- **Second Plan**: deficit financing about **₹1,100 crore** against ₹1,200 crore planned.
- **Third Plan**: reliance on deficit financing to be **strictly limited**.
- **1970s onwards**: heavy reliance (oil shocks, welfare spending) led to debt and inflation.
### 3. PUBLIC DEBT CLASSIFICATION
| Head | Items |
|---|---|
| Internal debt | **Market loans**, Treasury Bills, special securities to **NSSF** |
| Other liabilities (Public Account) | Small savings, State Provident Funds, reserve funds and deposits |
| External debt | Loans from foreign sources |
- **Market loans** = 75% of **AP's** debt.
### 4. METHODS OF DEFICIT FINANCING
- **Borrowing from the RBI** (monetising the deficit).
- **Ad hoc Treasury Bills** - automatic RBI financing, now abolished.
- **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** - temporary RBI advances from **1 April 1997**.
- Market borrowing; liquidity now managed by **OMOs**: sale of G-Secs **drains** liquidity, purchase **adds** it.
- 15 Sep 2026: RBI announced **OMO sales of ₹1 lakh crore** with 91/182/364-day T-bill auctions.
### 5. CONTROL OVER DEFICIT FINANCING
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| **Sep 1994** | RBI-Government agreement began phasing out ad hoc T-bills |
| **Mar 1997** | Agreement ended ad hoc T-bills; WMA from 1 April 1997 |
| **2003** | **FRBM Act** |
| **1 Apr 2006** | RBI barred from subscribing to **primary issues** of G-Secs |
| **2018** | Monetised borrowing only on **escape-clause** grounds |
- Escape clause: slip limited to **0.5% of GDP** a year.
### 6. CONSEQUENCES
| Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Demand-pull inflation** | Money supply and demand rise without more goods; hurts the **poor** most |
| **Crowding out** | Market borrowing raises interest rates, cuts private investment |
| **Debt trap** | Borrowing to pay interest; little room for health, education |
| Rating downgrades | Higher borrowing cost for government and firms |
#### 6.1 Inflationary Ranking
| Mode | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Creation of new money** | Most inflationary |
| Borrowing from banks | High |
| Borrowing from public | Lower (existing savings) |
| Repayment of debt | Not inflationary (withdraws money) |
#### 6.2 Current Data
- Interest = **20 paise** of every rupee spent (Budget 2026-27); about **40% of revenue receipts**.
- States borrowed ₹5.23 trillion till Oct 2025 (62% of FY26 plan); RBI wants a shift from subsidies to **capital expenditure**.
- **AP** fiscal deficit 2024-25: **5.05%** vs 4% limit.
