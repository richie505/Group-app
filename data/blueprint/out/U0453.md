## U0453 | PLAN MODELS – HARROD-DOMAR, MAHALANOBIS, WAGE-GOODS (VAKIL-BRAHMANANDA), GANDHIAN AND BOMBAY PLANS
### 1. MODELS COMPARED
| Model | Bottleneck | Priority | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harrod-Domar** (Roy Harrod, Evsey Domar) | **Limited savings** | Raise savings and investment | **First (1951-56)** |
| **Feldman-Mahalanobis** | **Capital goods** | Heavy industry (steel, machinery) | **Second (1956-61)** |
| **Wage-goods** (Vakil-Brahmananda, 1956) | **Food / wage goods** | Agriculture, employment | Alternative to Mahalanobis |
!KEY Bottleneck: Harrod-Domar = savings; Mahalanobis = capital goods; wage-goods = food.
### 2. PLAN → MODEL IT WAS BASED ON
| Plan | Model / strategy |
|---|---|
| **First (1951-56)** | **Harrod-Domar** (with modifications) |
| **Second (1956-61)** | **P.C. Mahalanobis** (Nehru-Mahalanobis-Feldman; modified Soviet model) |
| **Third (1961-66)**, "Gadgil Yojana" | **Sandy-Chakravarty** model; import substitution |
| **Fourth (1969-74)** | **Allen Manne – Ashok Rudra** model; Gadgil strategy (growth with stability) |
| **Fifth (1974-79)** | **Dhar** model |
- **Solow (Solow-Swan):** neoclassical model on a **Cobb-Douglas** production function; growth from technology + capital. **No Indian plan was based on it.**
- Second Plan distractors: **wage-goods, Gadgil, CELP, Tendulkar** – the key is **Mahalanobis**.
!TRAP Order 1-2-3-4-5 = Harrod-Domar – Mahalanobis – Sandy-Chakravarty – Manne-Rudra – Dhar.
### 3. HARROD-DOMAR MODEL
#### 3.1 Equation
- **g = s / k** (g = growth rate, s = savings rate, k = **capital-output ratio**).
- Growth **rises with savings**, **falls with a higher COR**.
- Chain: savings ↑ → investment ↑ (multiplier raises output and employment) → k sets output per unit of capital → growth.
#### 3.2 Capital-Output Ratio (COR)
| Basis | High COR | Low COR |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | More capital per unit of output; investment gives less output | Given capital gives more output |
| Capital productivity | Lower | Higher |
| Growth | Slower, even with high savings | Higher potential |
- ★ Low growth despite high savings and capital formation = **high capital-output ratio**.
- ★ Slow per-capita income growth in India = **high COR + high population growth**.
#### 3.3 First Plan
- Started **1 April 1951**; priority **agriculture** (primary sector).
- Faced refugee influx, food shortage, inflation; succeeded mainly due to **good harvests in its last two years**.
### 4. MAHALANOBIS MODEL
#### 4.1 Strategy and Sectors
- **Unbalanced growth**; priority to the **capital goods sector** ("heavy sector strategy").
- Idea: build machines at home → later self-sufficiency in consumer goods.
- **Four sectors:** (1) capital goods, (2) factory consumer goods, (3) household / small-scale consumer goods, (4) services.
- Feldman (USSR, **1928**) and Mahalanobis (**1953**) developed it independently.
#### 4.2 Included vs Not Included
| Included / emphasised | Excluded / neglected |
|---|---|
| **Import substitution**, self-reliance | **Foreign trade** – assumed a **closed economy** |
| **Dominant public sector** (socialistic pattern, IPR 1956) | **Consumer goods** left to small/cottage sector |
| Investment from **7% to 11%** of national income | **Exports / export promotion** |
| Heavy industry | **Agriculture** |
- **Criticisms:** shortages of food and capital; severe **food shortages** and **high inflation** by the late 1950s.
#### 4.3 Outcome – Second Plan
- Growth: target **4.5%**, achieved **4.27%**.
- Steel plants: **Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela**; **Chittaranjan** – locomotives; **Perambur** – coaches; Atomic Energy Commission.
!TRAP Mahalanobis neglected **agriculture, consumer goods and exports** – these are "NOT included" answers.

### 6. WAGE-GOODS MODEL
- Authors: **C.N. Vakil and P.R. Brahmananda** – book "**Planning for an Expanding Economy**" (**1956**).
- Real constraint = supply of **wage goods** (food, essential consumer goods), **not capital goods**.
- More wage goods → **disguised unemployed** put to productive work; stresses **employment**.
- Priority: **agriculture and wage goods** vs Mahalanobis's heavy industry.
### 7. NON-OFFICIAL PLANS → AUTHOR AND YEAR
#### 7.1 Full List
| Plan | Year | Author(s) | Core proposals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visvesvaraya Plan** ("Planned Economy for India") | **1934** | **M. Visvesvaraya** ("Father of Indian Planning") | First blueprint; **double national income in a decade** via industrialisation |
| **National Planning Committee** (Congress) | **1938** | Set up under **Subhas Chandra Bose**; chaired by **Nehru** | Precursor |
| **Bombay Plan** ("**A Plan of Economic Development for India**") | **1944** | **8 Bombay industrialists** incl. **J.R.D. Tata, G.D. Birla, Kasturbhai Lalbhai** | Big State role, public-sector dominance, licensing, **protection of infant industry**; **treble national income in 15 years** |
| **Gandhian Plan** | **1944** | **Shriman Narayan Agarwal** | Agriculture, cottage and village industries; self-contained villages; **Swadeshi, Sarvodaya, Trusteeship** |
| **People's Plan** | **1945** | **M.N. Roy** | Marxist socialism; basic necessities; **equal weight to agriculture and industry** |
| **Sarvodaya Plan** | **1950** | **Jayaprakash Narayan** | Gandhian constructive work and **Vinoba Bhave's** Sarvodaya |
- **Chronology:** NPC (1938) → Bombay and Gandhian Plans (1944) → People's Plan (1945) → First Plan (1951).
- Precursor: **Karachi Resolution (1931)**; **Dalal's Planning and Development Department (1944)**; **Advisory Planning Board (1946)** headed by **K.C. Neogi**.
- People's Plan echoed in the 1990s slogan "**reforms with a human face**".
- JP wrote the Sarvodaya Plan on Gandhian ideals; from **1954** committed to the Sarvodaya Movement.
!TRAP Bombay Plan was **not** by Rajaji and **Dhirubhai Ambani** was not an architect; it wanted a **big State role**, not a non-interventionist State.
!PAIR Visvesvaraya | 1934 | double in a decade || Bombay Plan | 1944 | treble in 15 years
#### 7.2 Gandhian Ideals – Books and Terms
- "**Gandhian Constitution for Free India**" – **Shriman Narayan Agarwal** (not M.N. Roy).
- "**India's Economic Policy: The Gandhian Blue Print**" – **Charan Singh**.
- **Trusteeship:** the wealthy act as trustees of wealth.
- **Ram Rajya:** rule on moral authority and justice for the poorest; **not a Hindu state**.
### 8. JANATA GOVERNMENT ALTERNATIVE STRATEGY
- Janata Party came to power in **1977**, during the Fifth Plan; **ended the Fifth Plan a year early (1978)**.
- Adopted the **Rolling Plan (1978-80)** – **Gunnar Myrdal's** concept.
- **Emphasis:** **agriculture and rural development, employment, small and cottage industries, minimum needs** – not heavy industry.
### 9. IMPERATIVE VS INDICATIVE CONTEXT
- **Stalin's USSR** began five-year plans in **1928** – **imperative** planning.
- India follows **indicative** planning.
