## U0519 | BALANCE OF PAYMENTS - STRUCTURE, CURRENT AND CAPITAL ACCOUNT, CONVERTIBILITY, FOREX RESERVES
### 1. MEANING OF BALANCE OF PAYMENTS
- **Balance of Payments (BoP)**: a **systematic and complete record of all economic transactions** between the residents of a country and the rest of the world during a period, **normally one year**.
- BoP has a **current account and a capital account** (PYQ 2025). The term relates to exports and imports.
- **IMF BPM6** uses **three accounts** (current, capital, financial); **RBI combines** capital and financial as "**Capital Account**".
- BoP includes **visible trade, invisible trade and borrowings**.
#### 1.1 Balance of Trade vs Balance of Payments
| Basis | Balance of Trade | Balance of Payments |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | **Goods only** (visible) | All transactions: goods, services, transfers, capital |
| Formula | Goods exports − goods imports | Whole external account, double-entry |
| Place | **Part of the current account** | Current + capital/financial account |
| Picture | Not the complete record | Better, complete picture |
- **BoT surplus**: goods exports > goods imports; **BoT deficit**: goods imports > goods exports.
!TRAP BoT = goods only; current account balance = goods and services + transfers (+ income).
### 2. STRUCTURE - CURRENT ACCOUNT VS CAPITAL ACCOUNT
| Current account | Capital (and financial) account |
|---|---|
| **Merchandise** exports and imports (visible trade; trade balance) | **FDI**, **FPI/FII**, ADRs/GDRs |
| Invisibles - services: software, travel, transport, banking, insurance | **ECB**, external assistance |
| Invisibles - **primary income**: interest, dividends, profits, wages | **NRI deposits** (e.g. FCNR(B)), banking capital |
| **Secondary income**: **remittances**, gifts, grants | Short-term trade credit; **change in forex reserves** |
#### 2.1 Current account
- Includes **goods, services, income and transfers**; it includes the Balance of Trade.
- **Invisibles** = services + primary income + transfers. Services = **non-factor** (shipping, banking, tourism, software) + factor income.
- **Private remittances** = current account → invisibles → private transfers.
- A **gift to foreigners** = **debit** in the current account.
#### 2.2 Capital account
- Cross-border capital movements: **foreign loans, FDI, portfolio investment**, purchase and sale of assets. **FDI is NOT current account.**
- Residents **buying foreign assets = debit**; **sale of Indian assets to foreigners = credit**.
| Item | BoP placement |
|---|---|
| Software services exports | Current account (services) |
| Foreign loans | Capital account |
| Remittances | Current transfers |
| Crude oil imports | Merchandise trade |
### 3. ACCOUNTING IDENTITY - WHAT ALWAYS BALANCES
- **Current Account + Capital Account + Financial Account + Net Errors & Omissions = 0**.
- BoP **always balances** in accounting terms: a surplus in one account = deficit in another. BoT need not balance.
- **Current account balance** = (exports − imports of goods and services) + net primary income + net secondary income (APPSC 2019: goods and services + net unilateral transfers).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Autonomous transactions** | Happen for their own sake |
| **Accommodating transactions** | Fill the gap: **reserve changes, official borrowing** |
| Financing a CAD | Capital account surplus (net inflow) or drawing down reserves; monetary authority = ultimate financier |
| Overall BoP | Positive → **adds to reserves**; negative → draws them down; reserves run short → **BoP crisis** |
### 4. CURRENT ACCOUNT DEFICIT - TREND
- **CAD**: payments exceed receipts from goods, services, transfers and net income (excess of total imports over total exports).
#### 4.1 CAD by period
| Period | CAD / current account |
|---|---|
| **1990-91** | **3.1-3.2% of GDP** (crisis); IMF drawn in 1991 |
| **2001-02 to 2003-04** | **Surplus three years running** (first surplus in 24 years in 2001-02); turned to deficit in 2004-05 |
| **FY13** | **Peak 4.8% of GDP** (taper tantrum) |
| Q4 2022-23 | $1.3 bn, 0.2% of GDP |
| **Q4 2023-24** | **Surplus** - surge in **services exports** (PYQ 2025) |
| 2024-25 | 0.6% of GDP |
| H1 2025-26 | 0.8% of GDP (lower than Brazil, Australia, UK) |
| Q3 FY26 (Dec qtr) | $13.2 bn, 1.3% (trade gap, lower exports to US) |
| **FY 2025-26** | **$25.2 bn (~0.6% of GDP)**; merchandise deficit $337.3 bn |
| June 2026 | $6.2 bn (surplus of $1.2 bn a year earlier); merchandise gap $30.2 bn |
| **Q1 2026-27** | **$4.2 bn, 0.5% of GDP** (vs $3.4 bn, 0.4%); trade gap $86.1 bn; net services $51.6 bn; net primary income outgo $10.5 bn |
!TRAP Peak CAD 4.8% (FY13) is higher than the 1990-91 crisis level of 3.1-3.2%.
- **FY24 external recovery**: moderation in merchandise export growth plus a **sharp fall in import growth** (G1 Prelims 2024). Services exports cushioned the deficit (up 4.85% to **USD 341.06 bn**, FY 2023-24).
- Goods trade: **14.2% of GDP (1990-91)** → almost three times by 2011-12; **30.4% (2023)**, 29.7% (2024).
#### 4.2 Causes of CAD
| Type | Factors |
|---|---|
| **Structural** | Oil (>80% imported) + gold; low export competitiveness; savings-investment gap |
| **Cyclical** | Commodity shocks, **temporary global slowdown**, rupee volatility |
### 5. INVISIBLES AND REMITTANCES - THE CUSHIONS
- **India = world's largest remittance recipient** (also in 2015): **$135.4 bn (3.5% of GDP) in FY25**; **USA** top source (**27.7%**).
- Workers' remittances (narrower measure) **$110.47 bn in FY26** (from $87.55 bn); Q4 FY26 $31.07 bn, a 13-year high.
- Remittances exceed gross FDI inflows; **counter-cyclical**; create **no debt**, nothing to repay.
| Top remittance recipients, 2024 (World Bank) | Amount |
|---|---|
| **India** | **$129 bn** |
| Mexico | $68 bn |
| China | $48 bn |
| Philippines | $40 bn |
| Pakistan | $33 bn |
- **Services surplus record $188.8 bn (FY25)** financed about **two-thirds** of the merchandise deficit; **software services** = largest part.
- **Net primary income** is an **outflow** ($10.5 bn in Q1 FY27).
- India's current-account trade is largest with the **USA**.

#### 6.2 Levels and cover

### 7. CAPITAL FLIGHT AND EXTERNAL VULNERABILITY
#### 7.1 Weak capital flows
- FY26: **FPIs pulled out $16.4 bn**; **net FDI only $6.9 bn** (down from $44.0 bn in 2020-21).
- Since mid-2024 capital inflows shrank, breaking the post-1991 pattern of a capital surplus covering the CAD.
- BoP position described as weak as the 2013 "Fragile Five" phase (12 May 2026).
#### 7.2 External shocks
- **US Fed rate hikes + strong dollar** → higher US yields → **hot money leaves** emerging markets → **rupee weakens**.
- **2013 taper tantrum**: **Morgan Stanley** named India one of the "**Fragile Five**".
- Morgan Stanley 1998 emerging markets: India **3rd** (Brazil 1st, Mexico 2nd).
#### 7.3 External debt (end-June 2026)
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Total | **$778.2 bn** (up $15.4 bn from March) |
| Debt-to-GDP | **20.8%** (from 20.9%) |
| Short-term debt | 19.7% of total; 23.0% of reserves |
| Currency | **US dollar 54.8%**, rupee 29.8%, yen 6.9%, SDR 4.1%, euro 3.5% |
| Debt service ratio | 5.6% of current receipts |
#### 7.4 Safeguards
- **Adequate reserves** (import cover), **low short-term debt**, less reliance on volatile **FPI** and debt flows. Relying on hot money raises risk.
- FCNR(B) and ECB inflows are **temporary**; **FDI** supports a self-sustaining capital surplus.
### 8. CORRECTING BoP DISEQUILIBRIUM
| Expenditure-switching | Expenditure-reducing / others |
|---|---|
| **Devaluation**/depreciation | **Tight fiscal policy**: cut government spending, raise taxes |
| **Import tariffs**, quotas, **import substitution** | **Tight monetary policy**: higher interest rates |
| **Export incentives**, export/tourist promotion | Foreign loans, IMF assistance, FDI; exchange control |
| Higher **gold import duty** | |
- **Devaluation** = classic expenditure-switching (home goods cheaper relative to foreign goods); used to fix the balance of trade.
- Tools used: devaluation, higher import tariffs, lower government spending.
- **NOT a correction measure**: **increasing government spending on domestic projects** - raises demand and imports (PYQ 2025).
- **Encouraging FDI** finances a BoP deficit; it is not a classical correction tool.
- **May 2026**: PM's appeal to cut import-linked consumption (gold, fuel) as the Iran conflict raised fuel and fertiliser prices + gold import duty hike = expenditure-switching steps.
- **Rekhi Committee (1992)** = indirect taxes, not export-import simplification.
### 9. IMF ROLE AND SDR
#### 9.1 IMF functions
- Core function: **surveillance** of global and member economies; main purpose: help solve **BoP problems**.
- Lends to members with BoP difficulties; **lender of last resort** when reserves cannot cover a deficit; loans meet **temporary BoP deficits**. India drew on the IMF in **1991**.
- Provides technical assistance.
#### 9.2 Facilities
| Facility | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Reserve tranche** | Member's **unconditional** drawing right from its **quota**; old name **gold tranche** |
| **Rapid Financing Instrument (RFI)** | Rapid help for **any member** with an urgent BoP need |
| **Rapid Credit Facility (RCF)** | **Concessional**, for **low-income countries** |
| Stand-By Arrangement / Extended Fund Facility | Regular lending windows |
!TRAP RBI swaps and Special Rupee Vostro Accounts are NOT IMF instruments.
#### 9.3 SDR
- **SDR** = IMF's international reserve asset, valued on a basket of **5 currencies** (weights from 1 Aug 2022):
| Currency | Weight |
|---|---|
| **US dollar** | **43.38%** |
| Euro | 29.31% |
| Chinese renminbi | 12.28% |
| Japanese yen | 7.59% |
| Pound sterling | 7.44% |
- Next review due by **end-July 2027**.
### 10. CONVERTIBILITY AND FOREX LAW
#### 10.1 Meaning
| Type | Freedom to convert for |
|---|---|
| **Current account convertibility** | Trade, income and transfers |
| **Capital account convertibility** | Investment and financial assets (FDI, portfolio, borrowing) without capital controls |
#### 10.2 Timeline
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1973 | **FERA** |
| **Sep 1975** | Rupee **delinked from sterling** |
| 1992-93 Budget | **LERMS**: dual rate - **40% official, 60% market** |
| **1 Mar 1993** | **Unified** exchange rate |
| **Aug 1994** | **Full current account convertibility** - accepted **Article VIII** of the IMF |
| 1999 / **1 June 2000** | **FEMA** enacted / in force |
- The rupee is still only **partially convertible on the capital account**.
#### 10.3 Committees
| Committee | Chair | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **High Level Committee on BoP (1991-93)** | **C. Rangarajan** | Sustainable CAD **about 1.6% of GDP**; **market-determined exchange rate** |
| Committee on Capital Account Convertibility (1997) | **S.S. Tarapore** | Three-phase path by 2000; preconditions: fiscal consolidation, inflation target, strong financial system; shelved after East Asian crisis |
| Committee on Fuller CAC (2006) | **S.S. Tarapore** | Revisited the roadmap |
#### 10.4 FERA vs FEMA
| Feature | FERA 1973 | FEMA 1999 |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Criminal** | **Civil** |
| Approach | Control of outflow | Management and regulation |
| Character | Strict, restrictive | Liberal |
- FEMA preamble objective: **facilitating external trade and payments**.
