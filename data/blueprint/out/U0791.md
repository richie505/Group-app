## U0791 | WATER POLLUTION – SOURCES, EUTROPHICATION, NAMAMI GANGE, SEWAGE TREATMENT, GROUNDWATER CONTAMINATION
### 1. SOURCES OF WATER POLLUTION
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Point** | Sewage outfalls, **industrial effluent pipes** |
| **Non-point** | **Agricultural runoff**, urban runoff |
| Sand mining | Raises turbidity, degrades river beds |
- Fertilisers pollute soil, water and air; **nitrates and phosphates** cause eutrophication.
- Ritual offerings and **dairy effluent** can have BOD far above sewage → deplete DO fast (15 Apr 2026).
- AP's main water polluters: **Pharma City (Vizag), tanneries, paper mills, aquaculture** (not coal mines or cement).
### 2. WATER QUALITY PARAMETERS
#### 2.1 Oxygen Demand
| Parameter | Measures | Clean-water value |
|---|---|---|
| **DO** | Oxygen dissolved in water | > 8.0 mg/L |
| **BOD** | Oxygen **bacteria need to decompose organic matter** | < 4.0 mg/L |
| **COD** | Oxygen to oxidise **all** organic matter (biodegradable + non-biodegradable) | < 5.0 mg/L |
- River pollution is measured by its **dissolved oxygen**: more pollution → **less DO**.
- **BOD** = standard criterion for pollution assay in aquatic ecosystems (mg/L). **High BOD** = sewage contamination; low BOD = good water.
#### 2.2 Other Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Coliform count | **Faecal contamination** (MPN/100 ml) |
| **Suspended solids** | Retained on a filter: **silt, clay** (APPSC 2023) |
| Dissolved solids / **TDS** | Salts passing the filter (sodium salts, nitrate) |
| Turbidity | Measured in **NTU** |
!TRAP Silt is a suspended solid; sodium salts and nitrate are dissolved.
### 3. WATER QUALITY STANDARDS
#### 3.1 CPCB Designated Best-Use Classes
| Class | Best use | Total coliform (MPN/100 ml) | DO (mg/L) | BOD (mg/L) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Drinking source **without conventional treatment, after disinfection** | **≤ 50** (APPSC 2024) | ≥ 6 | ≤ 2 |
| B | **Outdoor bathing** | ≤ 500 | ≥ 5 | **≤ 3** |
| C | Drinking after conventional treatment + disinfection | ≤ 5,000 | ≥ 4 | ≤ 3 |
| D | Wildlife and fisheries | – | ≥ 4 | – |
- Ganga **bathing standard**, faecal coliform: **2,500 MPN/100 ml**.
#### 3.2 BIS IS 10500 and WHO
| Parameter | BIS IS 10500 | WHO |
|---|---|---|
| Fluoride | **1.0 acceptable / 1.5 permissible** mg/L | 1.5 mg/L |
| Nitrate | **45 mg/L** | **50 mg/L** |
| Uranium | 30 ppb | – |
| TDS | **500 mg/L** (IS 10500-2012) | – |
- Drinking-water pollutants in parts of India: **arsenic, fluoride, uranium** (not sorbitol, formaldehyde).
### 4. EUTROPHICATION
- **Nutrient (N, P) enrichment** of a water body; the **natural ageing of a lake** by nutrient build-up.
- Triggers: fertiliser runoff, sewage, **detergents**, aquaculture effluent, airborne nitrogen (not a reduction of atmospheric nitrogen).
- Chain: **enrichment → algal bloom → algae decay → decomposition uses up DO → hypoxia, fish kills**.
- Stages: **oligotrophic → mesotrophic → eutrophic → hypereutrophic**.
- Harmful algal blooms in Indian seas: nutrient discharge from estuaries, monsoon land run-off, **upwelling** (all three).
- Blooms of pigmented **dinoflagellates** in estuaries = **red tides**.
### 5. POLLUTION-LINKED DISEASES
| Disease | Contaminant | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Minamata** | **Methyl mercury** | Japan, 1956; biomagnified through fish; neurological damage |
| **Itai-itai** ("ouch-ouch") | **Cadmium** | Japan; **mining wastewater used for irrigation** (APPSC 2024); bone, joint pain; first reported 1912, mass case 1950, **Toyama** |
| **Blue baby syndrome** | **Nitrate** | **Methaemoglobinaemia** in infants – blood cannot carry oxygen |
| **Fluorosis** | **Fluoride** | Dental (children under 8), skeletal, non-skeletal |
| **Knock-knee** (genu valgum) | Fluoride | Skeletal fluorosis; above **0.5 ppm over 5–10 years** |
| **Arsenicosis / blackfoot** | **Arsenic** | Skin lesions, skin and lung cancer |
- Fluorosis chemistry: fluoride replaces hydroxyl in **hydroxyapatite → fluorapatite**.
!PAIR Minamata | mercury | Itai-itai – cadmium || Blue baby | nitrate | Blackfoot – arsenic
### 6. GROUNDWATER CONTAMINATION
| Contaminant | Where |
|---|---|
| **Fluoride** | Above 1.5 mg/L at **9.04%** sites; **Rajasthan highest**, then Haryana, Karnataka (CGWB Ground Water Quality Report 2024); AP and Telangana also badly hit |
| **Arsenic** | Belt: **Ganga-Brahmaputra plains** – **West Bengal (maximum)**, Bihar, Assam; extends into **Bangladesh**; seven States: WB, Jharkhand, Bihar, UP, Assam, Manipur, Chhattisgarh; Kolkata associated |
| **Nitrate** | **56%** of districts exceed 45 mg/L; mainly fertiliser |
| **Uranium** | **Malwa (Punjab)**; > 30 ppb in **AP, Telangana** + 3 States (CGWB 2025) |
- Main contaminants: arsenic, fluoride, nitrate, uranium, radon.
- **Nalgonda technique** (fluoride removal): **alum + lime + bleaching powder**.
- AP (CGWB 2025, 12 Jan 2026): widespread contamination – uranium, nitrate, iron, fluoride alerts.
- National Fluorosis Day workshop: 9 Sept 2026.
### 7. SEWAGE AND EFFLUENT TREATMENT
#### 7.1 Treatment Gap
- Only **28%** (~28–30%) urban sewage treated; **72%** discharged untreated (2026).
- BOD discharge limit relaxed **10 → 30 mg/L** – criticised as letting in more organic pollution (6 Sep 2026).
#### 7.2 Technologies
| Technology | Function |
|---|---|
| Activated sludge | Conventional STP process |
| **SBR** (sequencing batch reactor) | Treats in batches in **one tank** |
| **MBR** (membrane bioreactor) | Activated sludge + **membrane filtration** → reuse-quality water |
| Activated carbon | Adsorption polishing |
#### 7.3 Hybrid Annuity Model and Reuse
- **HAM** approved for Namami Gange in **January 2016** (100% central funding); first use in **sewage** (earlier only highways); combines BOT-annuity and DBO.
- First HAM STP: **Haridwar** (Dec 2019). **One City One Operator**: Prayagraj, Kanpur, Howrah-Bally-Baranagar, Kamarhati.
- **National Framework on Safe Reuse of Treated Water: 2022**. Thermal plants within **50 km** of an STP must use treated wastewater.
- **AMRUT 2.0**: **20% reuse** of treated wastewater (not 50%).
#### 7.4 CETP and ZLD
- **CETP** = shared plant for clusters of **small-scale industries**; **India's first CETP: Andhra Pradesh, 1989**.
- AP runs **9 CETPs**; directed **Zero Liquid Discharge**; banned non-biodegradable effluent discharge (2024-25).
- ZLD example: **Tiruppur** textile cluster (Tamil Nadu).
### 8. RIVER AND LAKE CONSERVATION PROGRAMMES
| Plan | Year | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Central Ganga Authority** | **1985** (Rajiv Gandhi) | Ran GAP; renamed National River Conservation Authority, Sept 1995 |
| **GAP-I** | **1985** | Launched by Rajiv Gandhi at **Varanasi**; **25** main-stem towns |
| GAP-II | 1993 | **60** towns on Yamuna, Gomti, Damodar, Mahananda; merged into NRCP 1995 |
| **Yamuna Action Plan** | 1993 | Delhi stretch; **India–Japan** bilateral project |
| **NRCP** | **1995** | Rivers **other than the Ganga**; **38 rivers, 16 States** incl. **Godavari, Krishna**; Centre-State cost-sharing CSS |
| **NLCP** | 2001 | Urban lakes: **Bhoj (MP), Sukhna (Chandigarh), Pichola (Rajasthan)** – not Chilka |
| **NPCA** | 2013 | NLCP + wetlands programme merged |
- **NRCP objective**: improve water quality of rivers (other than the Ganga and tributaries) to prescribed standards by cutting pollution load (APPSC 2025).
- NRCP 2026: ₹595 crore central assistance; Singtam (Teesta, Sikkim) sewerage ₹39.03 crore, 3 MLD.
- Others: Clean Kaveri campaign; Krishna River conservation (AP, Telangana); Bhagirathi Eco-Sensitive Zone.
### 9. GANGA GOVERNANCE AND NAMAMI GANGE
#### 9.1 Governance Bodies
| Year | Body | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| 1985 | Central Ganga Authority | PM chair |
| **2009** | **NGRBA** | Under Environment Protection Act; PM chair; **river basin approach** |
| **2011** | **NMCG** | **Registered society**; NGRBA's implementing arm |
| **2016** | **National Ganga Council** | Replaced NGRBA; PM chair |
- Five tiers: **National Ganga Council (PM) → Empowered Task Force (Jal Shakti Minister) → NMCG → State Ganga Committees → District Ganga Committees (DMs)**.
- Legal base: **River Ganga (Rejuvenation, Protection and Management) Authorities Order, 2016** (7 Oct 2016).
- NMCG order (10 Aug 2026): flood-plain construction ban replaced by approval-based rules outside the "active flood plain".
#### 9.2 Namami Gange Programme
- Announced **2014-15**; integrated mission for Ganga and tributaries.
| Phase | Outlay |
|---|---|
| Phase I | **₹20,000 crore**, 2015–2020 |
| Namami Gange-II | Approved 2022; **₹22,500 crore**, 2021–2026 (to 31 March 2026) |
- Twin goals: **Nirmal Dhara** (unpolluted flow) + **Aviral Dhara** (continuous flow).
- **SDG 6** (clean water and sanitation) priority (APPSC 2022). Main problem: high faecal coliform.
- Pillars: sewerage/STPs, industrial effluent monitoring, riverfront/ghats, river-surface cleaning, biodiversity, afforestation, **Ganga Gram (2016)**, public awareness.
- Not a pillar: **River Aquifer Management**; uranium mining.
- Most sewerage projects (31 Dec 2021): **Bihar**.
- UN: among top 10 **World Restoration Flagships**.
!TRAP Nirmal = unpolluted; Aviral = continuous.
#### 9.3 Ganga-specific Measures
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Arth Ganga** | River-linked economy: natural farming, river tourism (dolphin safaris), treated-water reuse, artisans |
| **JALAJ** | Livelihood centres (NMCG + WII) |
| **Ganga Praharis** | Trained local volunteers for river biodiversity |
| **Ganga Utsav** | Annual river festival |
- Results: **143.8 lakh** carp fingerlings ranched since 2017; first **dolphin estimate 6,327** (March 2025).
- Criticism: capacity mismatch, top-down execution, little river-basin planning.
#### 9.4 Global River Cities Alliance
- Launched at **COP28, Dubai (Dec 2023)** – not New Delhi; **led by NMCG** (Jal Shakti).
- Members: river cities worldwide + partner countries, international funding agencies, knowledge partners (APPSC 2025).

### 11. ANDHRA PRADESH WATER BODIES UNDER STRESS
- **Godavari near Rajamahendravaram**: among most polluted stretches (CPCB 2025); unfit for drinking or bathing.
- Municipal corporation discharges **29.6 MLD** sewage through the **Nalla Channel**, then draws 65 MLD drinking water just downstream.
- Main polluters: **Andhra Paper Ltd** (31,934 KLD) + Nalla Channel; nitrate **119.6 mg/L** vs 45 at the APL lagoon.
- APPCB flag (27 Jun 2026): high BOD and coliform ahead of the **2027 Pushkarams**; Namami Gange model applied to Godavari basin.
- **Kolleru Lake**: severe **eutrophication** – lake bed turned into **aquaculture ponds** + farm runoff.
- Krishna stretches near cities also stressed.
