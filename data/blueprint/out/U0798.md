## U0798 | BIOTECHNOLOGY – NATURE, SCOPE, TOOLS; APPLICATIONS IN AGRICULTURE, MEDICINE AND INDUSTRY
### 1. NATURE AND SCOPE OF BIOTECHNOLOGY
- **CBD (1992)**: biotechnology = use of **biological systems** to make or modify products.
- Also: **industrial use of living organisms**, substances from them or their processes (e.g. **yeast for liquor**).
- Traditional biotech: curd, bread, wine, vinegar (ancient fermentation). Modern: rDNA, PCR, cloning, CRISPR.
| Colour | Field |
|---|---|
| **Red** | Medical |
| **Green** | Agricultural |
| **White** | **Industrial** – enzymes, biofuels, bioplastics, precision fermentation, green chemicals (NOT marine) |
| **Blue** | **Marine** |
- **Genetics** = study of genes, heredity and variation. **Enzymes** speed chemical reactions in living systems.
- **Bionics** = using biological systems' properties in the mechanical world; **bionomics** = organism and environment; **bionomy** = laws of life; **biometry** = statistical study; **anatomy** = body structures.
### 2. TOOLS AND GENOME TERMS
| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **PCR** | Key enzyme **DNA polymerase**; **denaturation → annealing → extension** |
| **Gel electrophoresis** | Separates DNA by **size** |
| **Restriction enzymes** | Cut DNA – **EcoRI, BamHI, HindIII** |
| **Ligase** | Only **joins** DNA |
| Plasmids | Vectors |
| **Bioreactors** | Large-scale culture; fastest ex-situ bioremediation |
| **CRISPR** | Genome editing |
- **Genome**: complete genetic information; zygote genome = DNA of **two gametes**. **Human genome ≈ 3 billion bases; over 99% shared**.
- **Transcriptome**: all expressed **mRNA**; **varies with environment**, unlike the genome.
- **DNA sequencing**: finds the base order (A, G, C, T) – genetic information depends on base order.
- DNA stores and passes information because of **complementarity** of strands (A–T, G–C).
- **Genetic code**: universal; triplet for 20 amino acids; non-overlapping, non-ambiguous, commaless; start and stop codons.
- **mRNA** carries the message for protein; **microRNA**: small non-coding RNA – silences target genes by **post-transcriptional regulation** (Ambros and Ruvkun).
- **Microsatellite DNA** (short tandem repeats): evolutionary relationships, genetic diversity.

### 4. DNA FINGERPRINTING
- **Basis: DNA polymorphism** – highly variable non-coding regions (**VNTRs**/repetitive DNA) differ between individuals.
- Tissues of the same person show the **same pattern**; polymorphisms are **inherited** (child gets half from each parent).
- Uses: **identity, paternity**, crime – **rape and assault** (trace hair, saliva, dried semen enough), conservation.
- Samples: **blood, bone cells, hair, saliva**, skin, sperm – all usable. **Biochips** = a technique, not a sample.
- First crime solved: **England**, murders of **1983 and 1986**.
- **CDFD** (Centre for DNA Fingerprinting and Diagnostics): **Hyderabad**, under DBT.

### 10. BIOREMEDIATION – MEANING AND METHODS
- **Bioremediation**: removal of pollutants from the biosphere by **biological processes** (APPSC-GS 2019); organisms (bacteria, fungi, algae, plants) or their **enzymes** restore a polluted environment.
- **Primary objective**: neutralising pollutants in **soil and groundwater** using micro-organisms (APPSC-G2 2025).
- Bacteria breaking down organic (municipal) waste = bioremediation (APPSC-GS 2022).
- Scope: soil, water, air; oil spills, heavy metals, pesticides, solvents; engineered microbes may be used.
| Term | Bioremediation? |
|---|---|
| Composting, bacterial oil clean-up, phytoremediation, constructed wetlands | **Yes** |
| **Burning contaminated soil / incineration** (thermal) | **No** (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| **Biomagnification** – rising DDT, mercury up food chain | No |
| **Eutrophication** – nutrient-driven algal blooms | No |
| Desalination, fossilisation, biofortification | No |
#### 10.1 In-situ vs Ex-situ
| Basis | In-situ | Ex-situ |
|---|---|---|
| Site | At original site, **no excavation** | Excavated, treated elsewhere |
| Techniques | **Bioventing, biosparging, bioaugmentation** | **Landfarming, composting, biopiles, bioreactors** |
| Cost | **Cheapest**, passive | Bioreactors costliest |
| Speed | **Slow** | **Bioreactors fastest** |
| Limits | Long time; metals complicate | Space, excavation |
#### 10.2 Technique → Target
| Technique | How / target |
|---|---|
| **Bioaugmentation** | Adds chosen degrading microbes – **oil spills** (APPSC-G2 2025), oily sludge |
| **Biostimulation** | Adds **N, P, oxygen** to native microbes – polluted lakes |
| **Bioventing** | Air/nutrients via wells into **soil** |
| **Biosparging** | Air injected **below the water table** – groundwater |
| Phytoremediation | Plants absorb, accumulate, detoxify |
| Mycoremediation | **Fungi** – organic pollutants |
| **Rhizofiltration** | Roots absorb/precipitate contaminants **from water** |
| Rhizoremediation | Roots stimulate rhizosphere microbes |
| Landfarming | Ex-situ land treatment (not slurry) |
#### 10.3 Phytoremediation and Hyperaccumulators
| Sub-type | Process |
|---|---|
| Phytoextraction | Stored in shoots, harvested – **water hyacinth** (heavy metals) |
| **Phytostabilisation** | Roots **immobilise** contaminants; cut leaching, erosion |
| Phytodegradation | Plant enzymes break organics |
| **Phytovolatilisation** | Released to **air** in volatile form (mercury, selenium) |
| Plant / microbe | Pollutant |
|---|---|
| **Pteris vittata** (fern) | **Arsenic** |
| **Indian mustard** | **Lead, cadmium, selenium** |
| **Thlaspi caerulescens** | **Zinc, cadmium** |
| **Alyssum** | **Nickel** |
| **Sunflower** | **Uranium**, radionuclides |
| **Eucalyptus** (sewage ponds) | Transpires surplus wastewater |
| **Bacillus flexus / Acinetobacter junii** | **Arsenate / arsenite** |
| **Ideonella sakaiensis** (PETase) | **PET plastic** |
| Engineered **Pseudomonas** | Oil spills (hydrocarbons) |
- Natural phytoremediators: **mangroves**, wetland vegetation; ponds: water hyacinth, duckweed.

#### 11.1 Advantages and Limits
| Basis | Bioremediation | Conventional (thermal, chemical) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Low | High |
| Side effects | No secondary pollution | Residues, emissions |
| Time | **Often slow** | Fast |
| Reliability | **Varies** with pollutant, organism, site, co-contaminants | Less site-dependent |
