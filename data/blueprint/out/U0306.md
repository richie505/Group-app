## U0306 | PARLIAMENTARY DEVICES – QUESTION HOUR, ZERO HOUR, MOTIONS (ADJOURNMENT, NO-CONFIDENCE, CENSURE, CALLING ATTENTION)
### 1. DEVICES OF PARLIAMENTARY CONTROL
- Parliamentary hours, motions and committees are the main means of **executive accountability** – controlling the Council of Ministers (flows from **Art 75(3)**).
- **Accountability triangle**: **Question Hour + Supplementary Questions + Adjournment Motions**.
- Tools: Question Hour, Zero Hour, half-an-hour and short-duration discussions; calling attention, adjournment, censure, no-confidence motions; committees (Government Assurances, Petitions, Subordinate Legislation, PAC, Estimates); Budget approval; **Motion of Thanks**.
- **Not a tool**: **judicial review** – it is the judiciary's job.
- Parliament controls administration mainly through **Parliamentary Committees**.
- State Legislatures use the same devices plus estimates and public accounts committees.
| Most effective device | Why |
|---|---|
| **No-confidence motion** | **Strongest / most powerful** weapon – whole CoM resigns (APPSC-G2 2025 key) |
| **Question Hour** | Most effective **day-to-day vigil** – Ministers must answer |
#### 1.1 Indian innovations
| Device | Year |
|---|---|
| **Short-duration discussion** (Rule 193) | **1953** |
| **Calling attention motion** | **1954** |
| **Zero Hour** | **1962** |
!TRAP 1953 – 1954 – 1962 = short-duration – calling attention – Zero Hour. Neither Zero Hour nor calling attention is in the Constitution.
### 2. QUESTION HOUR AND ZERO HOUR
| Basis | Question Hour | Zero Hour |
|---|---|---|
| Lok Sabha | **11 AM – 12 Noon** (first hour) | **12 Noon – 1 PM** (right after QH) |
| Rajya Sabha (since 2014) | **12 Noon – 1 PM** | **11 AM – 12 Noon** |
| Nature | **Formal**, in the Rules of Procedure | **Informal** Indian innovation (1962); **not in the Rules** |
| Notice | **15 clear days** (ordinary questions) | **No prior notice** – member intimates the Presiding Officer the **same day**, who decides whom to allow |
| Reply | Ministers must answer | Ministers **not bound to reply** |
| Duration | One hour | No time fixed in the Rules; at most one hour |
- Question Hour aim: **accountability of the Council of Ministers**; maximum attendance of members.
- Zero Hour: urgent matters of public importance; in political jargon "matters that cannot be delayed". Chair's directions go through the **Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs** to the ministry concerned.
- **Interpellation**: formal right of asking questions of the Executive.
!KEY APPSC-GS 2025; G2 2023, 2025: "time after Question Hour when members raise matters without prior notice" = **Zero Hour**.
!TRAP UPPCS 2021 key called Zero Hour "the first hour of the Session" – in the LS it follows Question Hour.
### 3. TYPES OF QUESTIONS
| Basis | Starred | Unstarred | Short Notice | To a private member |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mark | **Asterisk** | No asterisk | Shorter notice | – |
| Answer | **Oral** | **Written** (laid on Table) | **Oral** | Oral/written |
| Supplementaries | **Allowed** | **Not allowed** | Allowed | – |
| Subject | General public importance | General public importance | **Urgent** public importance | Own bill/resolution |
| Daily limit | **20** for oral answer | No limit | As Speaker/Chairman decides | – |
| Paper | **Green** | **White** | **Light pink** | **Yellow** (not blue) |
- Short notice: **less than 10 days'** notice. All three are taken up in **Question Hour**.
- No "adjournment question" – adjournment is a **motion**.
- **White Paper** – official Government document explaining policy on a subject; gathers views before a bill.
!PAIR Starred | Oral | Green || Unstarred | Written | White
### 4. RULES OF PROCEDURE – DEVICES AND RULE NUMBERS
- LS work runs mainly by the **Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business**; the Constitution gives only the framework. **No-confidence motion, Zero Hour, Rule 377** are **not in the Constitution**.
| Rule | Device |
|---|---|
| **Rule 184** (LS) | Discussion with a formal motion **and vote** – governments dislike it, losing may cost power |
| **Rule 193** (LS) | **Short-duration discussion**, **no vote**, max **2 hours** |
| **Rule 377** (LS) | Matters not raisable under any other device; **written notice** to Secretary-General + **Speaker's consent**; **20 → 30 a day from 27 July 2015** |
| **Rule 180A–E** (RS) | **Special Mentions** |
| **Rule 198** (LS) | **No-confidence motion** |
| **Rule 222** (LS) / **Rule 187** (RS) | **Privilege motion** |
| Rules 200–203 (LS) | Removal of Speaker |
- **Half-an-hour discussion**: matter already debated enough but needing **factual elucidation**; no formal motion or vote.
!TRAP 193 = **no vote**; 184 = **vote**.
### 5. CALLING ATTENTION VS ADJOURNMENT MOTION
| Basis | Calling Attention | Adjournment Motion |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | **Indian innovation (1954)**, in the Rules | Traditional device |
| House | **Either House** | **Lok Sabha only** |
| Requirement | Presiding Officer's **permission** | Presiding Officer's consent + **50 members** |
| Objective | **Authoritative statement** from a Minister on an urgent matter | Discuss a **definite matter of urgent public importance**; sets aside business |
| Business | Not disrupted | Suspended; debate at least **2 h 30 min** |
| Censure | **No** censure | **Involves censure** of Government |
- Adjournment motion conditions: single, definite, specific matter of **recent occurrence**; **no privilege question**; **one per sitting**; only when other tools (e.g. short-duration discussion) would be **too late**.
- **Calling attention notices narrowed the scope of the Adjournment Motion** – not of Question Hour or Zero Hour.
!TRAP Adjournment and no-confidence: both **LS only**, both need **50 members**.
### 6. NO-CONFIDENCE, CONFIDENCE AND CENSURE MOTIONS
#### 6.1 No-confidence motion (Rule 198)
- Moved by the **Opposition**, **Lok Sabha only**, against the **entire Council of Ministers** – not one minister.
- Written notice to the **Secretary-General**; **at least 50 members** for leave; **no reasons** needed.
- **Not in the Constitution** – flows from **Art 75(3)** (collective responsibility to LS).
- If passed, the **whole CoM resigns**.
- **Speaker's role**: decides **admissibility** – discretion to admit or reject (APPSC-GS 2025 key); does not resign or dissolve the House.
- A PM from the RS can take part in LS work but **cannot vote** there, even on a no-confidence motion.
#### 6.2 Comparison
| Motion | Moved by / where | Reasons | Against | Effect if passed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **No-confidence** | Opposition; LS; 50 members | **Not needed** | Entire CoM | **Must resign** |
| **Confidence** | **Government**, to prove majority; LS only | – | – | If **defeated**, Government falls |
| **Censure** | LS | **Must be stated** | A minister, a group or the CoM | **Not bound to resign** – disapproval of policy |
- Censure differs from no-confidence in aim and procedure; no separate "leave of the House".
#### 6.3 Instances
- **First no-confidence motion**: **J.B. Kripalani** against **Nehru** (**1963**).
- **AP angle – Andhra State, Nov 1954**: **Gouthu Latchanna's** no-confidence motion passed **69–68** – **Prakasam ministry fell by one vote** after 13 months; President's Rule 16 Nov 1954 – 27 Mar 1955.
- **G.V. Mavalankar** – first Speaker to face a removal motion (15 Dec 1954, by Vigneshwar Missir and J.B. Kripalani); rejected 18 Dec 1954.
- **Mar 2026**: resolution to remove Speaker **Om Birla** dismissed by **voice vote** – removal under **Art 94(c)** needs an **effective majority**, 14 days' notice and 50 members' support.
### 7. PRIVILEGE AND CUT MOTIONS
- **Privilege motion**: against a Minister **withholding or distorting facts**; **either House**; Presiding Officer's consent; referred to the **Committee of Privileges**; does not force resignation. **May 2026**: privilege notice in RS against the Union Education Minister.
- **Cut motions** (LS, on **demands for grants** – Union Budget):
| Cut | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy cut** | Reduce to **Re 1** | Disapproval of policy |
| **Economy cut** | By a **specified amount** | Economy |
| **Token cut** | By **Rs 100** | Specific grievance |
### 8. RESOLUTIONS AND THE WHIP
#### 8.1 Resolutions
- Self-contained proposal; **always a substantive motion**; **must be put to vote**; mover cannot withdraw without **leave of the House**; same subject **barred for one year**.
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Private member's** | **Alternate Fridays**, afternoon |
| **Government** | Moved by a minister – e.g. approving treaties |
| **Statutory** | Under a constitutional/statutory provision |
- **AP angle**: the AP Legislature uses the same devices – **Feb 2026**, AP Legislative Council adjourned amid a Question Hour / short-duration discussion row on the **Tirumala laddu** issue; **biometric attendance** for MLAs from the Budget Session 2026.
#### 8.2 Office of Whip
- **Not in the Constitution, the Rules or any statute** – rests on **parliamentary conventions** (APPSC-G1 2024 key).
- Each party appoints a whip/chief whip to secure attendance and party-line voting; the **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs** keeps contact with Chief Whips in both Houses.
- Defying the whip on a vote → **Tenth Schedule** disqualification.
| Whip | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **One-line** | Informative |
| **Two-line** | Direction to attend |
| **Three-line** | **Strict voting** direction |
### 9. CLOSURES, POINT OF ORDER, YIELDING THE FLOOR
| Device | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Closure motion** | Cuts short debate and puts the matter to vote – **four kinds** |
| **Simple closure** | "Matter sufficiently discussed; be put to vote" |
| **Closure by compartments** | Clauses grouped; debate and vote part by part |
| **Kangaroo closure** | Only **important clauses** debated; others taken as passed |
| **Guillotine** | Allotted time ends – **all remaining clauses/demands, even undiscussed**, put to vote (LS) |
| **Point of order** | Raised when proceedings depart from rules/Constitution; **no debate**; **Presiding Officer** rules |
| **Yielding the floor** | Member speaking **makes way** for another member or the Chair; the **Speaker can direct** it (convention) |
!TRAP Kangaroo = only important clauses debated; guillotine = undiscussed clauses voted when time ends.
### 10. FINANCIAL COMMITTEES
| Basis | PAC | Estimates Committee | Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Members | **22 = 15 LS + 7 RS** | **30, LS only** – **largest** | **22 = 15 LS + 7 RS** |
| Chairman (appointed by Speaker) | **Opposition**, by convention since **1967** (ruling party till 1966–67) | **Ruling party** | Speaker appoints |
| Work | **CAG audit reports**, appropriation accounts, waste; spending "**within the scope of the demand**"; not policy | Budget estimates; **economy and efficiency**; alternative policies – "**continuous economy committee**" | **PSU accounts** and CAG reports on PSUs; commercial soundness |
| Origin | **1921** (GoI Act, 1919) | 1921; present form **1950** (**John Mathai**) | **1964** (**Krishna Menon** Committee) |
- All three: **PR-STV**, term **one year**; **no minister** (also barred from Petitions, Subordinate Legislation).
- PAC recommendations **not binding**; PAC reports to the **Lok Sabha** (via Speaker; copy on RS Table).
- **18th LS (Aug 2024)**: PAC headed by **K.C. Venugopal** (also for 2026–27); Baijayant Panda did not head Estimates.
- **Joint Committee on Salaries and Allowances of MPs** is **not** a financial committee.
!KEY No RS member = **Estimates**; CAG reports = **PAC** (COPU for PSUs).
### 11. OTHER COMMITTEES – NICKNAMES, COMPOSITION, DRSCs
#### 11.1 Nicknames and roles
| Committee | Role |

| **Estimates** | "Continuous economy committee" |
| PAC, Estimates, COPU, Subordinate Legislation, Assurances, DRSCs | "**Watchdogs**" over the executive |
| **Subordinate Legislation** | Rules, regulations, bye-laws within limits; **15 in each House**; Ministers barred |
| **Government Assurances** | Ministers' promises till implemented |
| **Papers Laid on the Table** | Papers by ministers (other than statutory orders) |

#### 11.3 DRSCs and ad hoc committees
- **DRSCs**: set up **1993**; **24 = 16 under LS + 8 under RS**; **31 members = 21 LS + 10 RS**; quorum **11**; chairpersons appointed by the **Speaker / Chairman**.
- **Standing** committees are permanent (Business Advisory, Petitions, Privileges, Rules); **ad hoc** end when the task is done – inquiry committees (e.g. MPLADS, Railway Convention) and committees on Bills.
- **Select Committee** = one House; **Joint Committee** = both Houses, formed on a motion.
- **2G JPC (2011)**: **30 = 20 LS + 10 RS**; chairman **P.C. Chacko**; LS passed proposal 24 Feb 2011, RS 1 Mar 2011; report to Speaker Meira Kumar.
- **Consultative Committees** (incl. Railway Zones): set up by the **Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs** – not parliamentary committees.
- **Aug 2026**: FCRA (Amendment) Bill, 2026 sent to a JPC.
!TRAP DRSC 31 = 21 + 10; SC/ST Welfare 30 = 20 + 10; PAC 22 = 15 + 7.

### 12. FUNCTIONING – DATA AND DEBATES
- **17th LS**: only **16%** of Bills went to committees vs **71%** in the 15th; Question Hour ran only **60%** of its time.
- UK: every Bill examined by committees of both Houses; India refers under one-fifth.
- Related numbers: session gap max **6 months** (Art 85) → at least two sessions; **quorum one-tenth** (Art 100); first LS elections 25 Oct 1951 – 21 Feb 1952, **489 seats**; first Speaker **G.V. Mavalankar**.
