## U0769 | INTERNATIONAL ENVIRONMENTAL TREATIES – STOCKHOLM 1972, RIO 1992, RAMSAR, CITES, CBD AND ITS PROTOCOLS

| Year | Treaty / Conference | Key point |

!KEY Rio Conventions = **UNFCCC, CBD and UNCCD** – climate, biodiversity and land.
### 2. STOCKHOLM CONFERENCE, 1972
#### 2.1 Basics and Outcomes
- Official name: **UN Conference on the Human Environment**, Stockholm, Sweden, **5–16 June 1972**.
- Outcomes: **Stockholm Declaration (26 principles)** + Action Plan; **UNEP** set up in 1972 (HQ **Nairobi, Kenya**); **5 June** became **World Environment Day** (first observed **1973**).
#### 2.2 India at Stockholm
- PM **Indira Gandhi** attended – the **only visiting head of government**; she linked **poverty and environment**.
- India: a "cautious participant" at Stockholm → leader by Paris 2015 and KMGBF 2022.
- Stockholm decisions implemented in India through the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
- World Environment Day: **China hosted 2019**; WED 2022 theme **"Only One Earth"**.
### 3. RIO EARTH SUMMIT, 1992 AND FOLLOW-UPS
#### 3.1 Rio 1992
- Official name: **UN Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED)**, "Earth Summit" – the **first international Earth Summit**.
| Rio output | Key point |
|---|---|
| **Agenda 21** | Action plan for sustainable development at all levels |
| Rio Declaration | Principles on environment and development |
| **Forest Principles** | **Non-binding** statement on forests |
| **UNFCCC and CBD** | Opened for signature at Rio (CBD at Rio, **not Stockholm**) |
| UNCCD | Adopted **1994** as follow-up |
- **UNFCCC**: in force **21 March 1994**; aim – stabilise greenhouse-gas concentrations. UNFCCC is linked to UNCED.
- **UNCCD**: desertification and land degradation; bottom-up, local-community approach; linked to **SDG 15** and Land Degradation Neutrality. **COP-17: Mongolia**, theme "Restoring Land. Restoring Hope"; 197 parties; land degradation affects up to 40% of land. India hosted **COP-14 (2019)**.
#### 3.2 Global Environment Facility (GEF)
- Pilot GEF agreed **1990**, set up on the eve of Rio 1992; first implementing partners **UNDP, UNEP, World Bank**.
- Financial mechanism for **CBD, UNFCCC, UNCCD, Stockholm Convention, Minamata Convention**; grants for biodiversity, REDD+, climate change, land degradation, ozone layer.
#### 3.3 Follow-up Summits
| Summit | Year / venue | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Rio+5** | 1997, UNGA special session, **New York** | Reviewed Agenda 21 |
| Rio+10 (WSSD) | 2002, **Johannesburg** | Johannesburg Plan of Implementation |
| **Rio+20** (UNCSD) | 2012, Rio | **"The Future We Want"**; launched SDG process; High-Level Political Forum; set up **UNEA** (193 members, biennial, Nairobi) |
- **UNEA-4**: 11–15 March 2019, Nairobi; theme "Innovative solutions for Environmental Challenges and Sustainable Consumption and Production".
!TRAP UNEP (Stockholm 1972) and UNEA (Rio+20, 2012) are both in Nairobi – do not mix their origin.
- **Global 500 awards**: for protection of environment; launched **1987 by UNEP**.
### 4. RAMSAR CONVENTION, 1971
#### 4.1 Convention Basics
- **Convention on Wetlands**; signed **2 February 1971**, **Ramsar, Iran** (now World Wetlands Day); secretariat **Gland, Switzerland**; India a Party since **1982**.
- Obligations ("three pillars"): designate **wetlands of international importance**; **wise use** of all wetlands; **international cooperation**.
- **Wise use** = conserve through an ecosystem approach while allowing sustainable use.
- **Montreux Record**: sites where changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring or are likely, from **human interference** (technology, pollution). Indian sites: **Keoladeo NP + Loktak Lake**; **Chilika removed** after restoration.
#### 4.2 India's Ramsar Sites
- First Indian sites (**1 October 1981**): **Chilika Lake** and **Keoladeo NP**.
- India reached **101** sites with **Glaw Lake** (Arunachal Pradesh, 3 Aug 2026) – most in Asia.
- **16 October 2026**: tally **103** with **Sukhna Lake** (Chandigarh) and **Sauj Jheel** (Mainpuri, UP).
- **Udaipur and Indore** = India's first accredited **Wetland Cities** (January 2025).
- Ramsar **CoP-15** (30 Jul 2025): India's resolution on sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands adopted. **Amrit Dharohar** strategy (Jun 2023).
| Wetland | State |
|---|---|
| Chilika | Odisha |
| Keoladeo, Sambhar | Rajasthan |
| Wular | J&K |
| Loktak | Manipur |
| **Kolleru** | **Andhra Pradesh** |
| Harike | Punjab |
| Kabartal (Kanwar Lake) | Begusarai, Bihar |
| East Kolkata, **Sundarban** | West Bengal (Sundarban **not Odisha**) |
#### 4.3 AP Angle
- **Kolleru Lake** (designated **19 August 2002**), between Godavari and Krishna deltas, is **AP's only Ramsar site**.
- Visakhapatnam wetlands under **Operation Lungs 2.0**.
### 5. CITES, 1973
#### 5.1 Basics
- Full form: **Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora**; other name **Washington Convention**.
- Adopted **3 March 1973**, Washington D.C.; in force **1 July 1975**; secretariat **Geneva** (administered by **UNEP**); **184 Parties**.
- Covers about **35,000 species**: bans commercial trade for ~3%, regulates ~97%.
- **World Wildlife Day = 3 March** (CITES signing day; proclaimed by UNGA in 2013).
- Legally **binding** on Parties but does not replace national laws.
#### 5.2 Appendices
| Appendix | Listing criterion | Trade | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Threatened with extinction | **Commercial trade prohibited**; only exceptional non-commercial (research) with import + export permits | Tiger, snow leopard, rhino, clouded leopard, Indian and Chinese pangolins |
| **II** | Not necessarily threatened, but could be without control | Allowed with **export permit** | Corals, most sharks and rays, red sanders |
| **III** | Protected in at least one Party that asks others' help | Permits / **certificates of origin** | **Walrus (Canada)** |
- Species move between Appendices **only by COP vote**.
#### 5.3 CITES COPs
- COP meets **every 2–3 years**.
| COP | Venue / year | Highlight |
|---|---|---|
| **CoP3** | **New Delhi, 1981** | Only CITES COP hosted by India |
| CoP18 | 2019 | Indian star tortoise uplisted to App I on India's proposal |
| CoP19 | **Panama, 2022** | 54 shark and ray species to App II; rosewood, sea cucumbers |
| **CoP20** | **Samarkand, Uzbekistan, 2025** | **First in Central Asia**; 50 years of CITES; 77 species added; whale shark, oceanic whitetip, manta rays → App I |
- CoP20: India **defeated an EU proposal** on **Guggul** (Commiphora wightii).
- India's proposals: rosewood, otters, tortoises, Tokay gecko, wedgefish.
### 6. CITES IN INDIA AND INDIAN SPECIES
#### 6.1 Accession, Law and Bodies
- India a Party since **1976**; implementing law **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**.
- **WPA Amendment 2022**: CITES chapter + new **Schedule IV** for CITES-listed specimens; Management Authority (permits) + Scientific Authority (advice).
!TRAP Schedule IV is for CITES-listed specimens, not invasive alien species (IAS are under a separate section).
- **ZSI** hosts a CITES Cell; analysed 1,545 confiscated specimens of 105 species in 2025.
- **WCCB**: statutory body under MoEFCC, set up **2007** (WPA 2006 amendment); assists **Customs** in inspecting consignments under CITES and EXIM policy; keeps the wildlife-crime database.
- **CITES Trade Database**: UNEP-WCMC. **MIKE**: monitors elephant poaching.
#### 6.2 Species → Appendix
| Species | Appendix |
|---|---|
| Tiger, clouded leopard | I |
| Indian and Chinese pangolins | I (500,000+ seized globally 2016–2024) |
| **Indian star tortoise** | I (CoP18, 2019) |
| **Red sanders** | **II** |
| Rosewood (**Dalbergia** – legume family; D. latifolia, D. sissoo) | II |
| Striped hyena | II |
| **Golden gecko** (AP endemic) | II |
| Sharks and rays | Mostly II; whale shark, oceanic whitetip, manta rays → I |
#### 6.3 Red Sanders of Seshachalam (AP)
- **Pterocarpus santalinus**, endemic to **Seshachalam** and southern Eastern Ghats; **IUCN Endangered**; CITES **Appendix II**.
- AP's Red Sanders Protection Area: **5,300.97 sq km** across 7 districts.
- India was under CITES **Review of Significant Trade** for red sanders since 2004; **removed** at the 77th Standing Committee, Geneva, **November 2023**.
- Gives **45% (Rs 120 crore)** of India's ABS income – India's single largest ABS earner.
### 7. CMS AND WILDLIFE BODIES
- **CMS = Bonn Convention (1979)**: migratory species; **binding treaty under UNEP**.
- Appendix I: endangered migratory species (strict protection); Appendix II: species needing cooperation agreements.
- **CMS COP-13, Gandhinagar, Feb 2020** (hosted by India): **Great Indian Bustard, Asian elephant, Bengal florican** added to Appendix I.
- CMS COP15 (Campo Grande, Brazil): Global Initiative on the Taking of Migratory Species.
| Body | Mandate | Legal status |
|---|---|---|
| CITES (1973) | Regulate international wildlife trade | Binding treaty |
| CMS (1979) | Migratory species | Binding treaty under UNEP |
| **IUCN** (1948) – International Union for Conservation of Nature | **Red List**, field projects | Union of governments and NGOs, **not a UN organ**; no customs role |
| **TRAFFIC** (1976) | Wildlife trade not a threat to conservation | NGO network of **WWF and IUCN** |
!PAIR Bonn Convention | CMS | migratory species || Washington Convention | CITES | trade
### 8. CONVENTION ON BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY (CBD)
#### 8.1 Basics
- Adopted (text **22 May 1992**), opened at **Rio 1992**; **legally binding**; in force **29 December 1993**; secretariat **Montreal, Canada**; India ratified **1994**.
- Three objectives: **conservation**, **sustainable use**, **fair and equitable benefit-sharing** (not compulsory technology transfer).
- Financial mechanism: **GEF**. In-situ = in natural habitat; ex-situ = outside it.
#### 8.2 CBD COPs (every two years)
| COP | Year | Venue | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| COP1 | 1994 | Nassau, Bahamas | First COP |
| **COP10** | 2010 | **Nagoya**, Japan | Nagoya Protocol; Aichi Targets |
| **COP11** | 2012 | **Hyderabad**, India | Hosted by India |
| COP12 | 2014 | Pyeongchang | Reviewed Aichi progress |
| COP13 | 2016 | Cancun | – |
| COP14 | 2018 | Sharm el-Sheikh | Began post-2020 framework |
| **COP15** | 2021–22 | **Kunming and Montreal** | **KMGBF**; GBF Fund |
| **COP16** | 2024 | **Cali**, Colombia (resumed Rome, Feb 2025) | **Cali Fund** on DSI |
### 9. CARTAGENA AND NAGOYA PROTOCOLS
| Point | Cartagena Protocol | Nagoya Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | **Biosafety** – LMOs/GMOs from modern biotechnology | **Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)** of genetic resources |
| Adopted → in force | **29 Jan 2000 → 11 Sep 2003** | **2010 (COP10) → 2014** |
| Named after | Cartagena, Colombia | Nagoya, Japan |
| Parties | 173 | 137 |
| India | **MoEF(&CC)** nodal; **GEAC** approves GM | Ratified **2012**; 3,556 certificates |
#### 9.1 Cartagena
- **Advance informed agreement** procedure before first transboundary movement of LMOs.
- **Biosafety Clearing-House**: online platform for LMO information – **not a testing lab**.
- First COP/MOP-1: **February 2004, Kuala Lumpur**.
- **Nagoya–Kuala Lumpur Supplementary Protocol** on Liability and Redress (2010) – supplementary to **Cartagena**, hence CBD.
#### 9.2 Nagoya
- **Legally binding supplementary agreement** to CBD (not voluntary).
- "**Utilisation of genetic resources**" = **research and development** on genetic and/or **biochemical** composition.
- Covers plant, animal, microbial genetic resources + traditional knowledge; **excludes human genetic material**.
- Pillars: **prior informed consent**, **Mutually Agreed Terms**, compliance.
- India's **1st National Report**: **3,556 IRCCs**, over **60%** of global total.
!TRAP India ratified Nagoya in 2012; 2014 is the entry-into-force year. Nagoya is part of **CBD** – not UNFCCC, Ramsar or Paris. Carbon trading belongs to **Kyoto**, not Montreal Protocol.
### 10. CBD IMPLEMENTATION IN INDIA AND AP
- **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** (amended **2023**) + BD Rules 2024; NBA ABS regulations April 2025 incl. **Digital Sequence Information**.
| Tier | Role |
|---|---|
| **National Biodiversity Authority (Chennai)** | ABS approvals for foreign users, IPR, advice |
| State Biodiversity Boards | State-level regulation |
| **BMCs** | **People's Biodiversity Registers**; **levy collection fees** |
- ABS income over **Rs 266 crore** since 2008. NBA SOP (Sec. 38): **159 plant and 173 animal** threatened species notified.
- BD Amendment 2023 criticised: weakens ABS, biopiracy risk, reduced role of local communities.
- **AP State Biodiversity Board**: aim **zero loss of biodiversity and ecosystem services by 2050**; Local Biodiversity Fund, Biological Heritage Sites, SBSAP; **BIOMIS** and ABS End-to-End portals; City Biodiversity Index in four cities; plans **OECMs** for 30x30; chairman Neelayapalem Vijay Kumar.
### 11. AICHI TARGETS VS KUNMING-MONTREAL GBF
| Point | Aichi Targets | KMGBF |
|---|---|---|
| Adopted | **COP10, Nagoya, 2010** | **COP15, Montreal, Dec 2022** |
| Structure | **20 targets, 5 strategic goals** (Strategic Plan 2011–2020) | **23 targets (2030), 4 goals (2050)** |
| Deadline | 2020 | 2030 / 2050 |
| Result | **None fully met** (GBO-5) | Numeric targets, monitoring, finance |
- Sequence: **Nagoya 2010 → Kunming 2021 → Montreal Dec 2022 → Cali Oct 2024**. COP15 also = 10th MOP Cartagena + 4th MOP Nagoya.
!TRAP KMGBF was adopted at Montreal (not Kunming 2021, not COP16 Cali); Aichi at COP10 (not COP12).
#### 11.1 Goals and Headline Targets
| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal A | Cut extinction rate tenfold; halt human-induced extinction |
| Goal B / **C** / **D** | Sustainable use / **fair benefit-sharing** / **finance and technology** (close US$700 bn gap) |
| Target 1 | Loss of high-biodiversity areas close to zero |
| **Target 2** | **Restore 30%** of degraded land and water ecosystems |
| **Target 3 (30x30)** | **Conserve 30% of land and 30% of oceans** by 2030 |
| Target 6 | Cut invasive alien species by **at least 50%** |
| Target 7 | Halve excess nutrients and pesticide risk |
| Target 16 | Halve food waste |
| Target 18 | Cut harmful subsidies by **US$500 bn/year** |
| Target 19 | Mobilise **US$200 bn/year** by 2030, all sources |
| Target 22 | Full participation and rights of **IPLCs** |
- COP15 recognised the role of **indigenous peoples and local communities**; 30x30 must respect indigenous territories.
#### 11.2 30x30 and High Ambition Coalition
- **High Ambition Coalition for Nature and People**: co-chaired by **Costa Rica, France, UK**; goal – protect **30% of land and ocean by 2030**.
- India joined on **7 October 2021** – the **first BRICS** country.
!KEY APPSC G1 2024: 30x30 is a **biodiversity (area protection)** target, NOT renewable energy.
### 12. FINANCE, MONITORING AND INDIA'S NBSAP
- **GBF Fund** under the **GEF**.
- **COP16 Cali**: **Cali Fund** – large DSI users pay **1% of profits or 0.1% of revenue**; permanent subsidiary body on Indigenous peoples (Art. 8(j)).
- Monitoring: **headline indicators**; national reports under **Article 26** (about every four years). WDPA (UNEP-WCMC) shows ~10% of oceans protected, only 1.66% of high seas.
- **India's updated NBSAP 2024–2030**: **23 national targets**, released at **COP16 Cali (Oct 2024)**; Target 13 = ABS.
- **7th National Report** = first full review against 23 KMGBF targets.
!NUM US$200 bn = mobilise (T19); US$500 bn = subsidy cut (T18); US$700 bn = finance gap (Goal D)
