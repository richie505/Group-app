## U0643 | CLIMATIC CHANGE AND CLIMATIC REGIONS - WORLD, INDIA AND ANDHRA PRADESH
### 1. KOEPPEN CLIMATIC REGIONS OF INDIA
| Code | Meaning | Area |
|---|---|---|
| **Amw** | Monsoon, short dry season | West coast south of Goa |
| **As** | Monsoon, dry summer | Coromandel coast (Tamil Nadu) |
| **Aw** | Tropical savanna (wet and dry) | Most of the Peninsula; **AP** |
| **BShw** | Semi-arid steppe | Rain-shadow belts, parts of Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana |
| **BWhw** | Hot desert | Western Rajasthan |
| **Cwg** | Monsoon, dry winter | Ganga plain |
| **Dfc** | Cold humid winter, short summer | Arunachal Pradesh |
| **E** | Polar / tundra | High Himalaya, Ladakh |
- **Arid** regions cover about one-third of Earth, the largest share of any climate group.
### 2. TREWARTHA AND THORNTHWAITE
#### 2.1 Trewartha (modified Koeppen)
- Same **temperature-rainfall** logic, simpler for India.
| Trewartha | Type | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Am | Tropical rainforest | West coast, North-East |
| Aw | Tropical savanna | Most of the Peninsula |
| BS/BW | Steppe and desert | Rajasthan, rain-shadow Deccan |
| Caw | Humid subtropical, dry winter | Ganga plain |
!PAIR Trewartha Caw | = Koeppen Cwg | Ganga plain || Trewartha H | = Koeppen Dfc/E | Himalaya
#### 2.2 Thornthwaite (moisture-based)
- Basis: **moisture index** (precipitation effectiveness) and **potential evapotranspiration** (thermal efficiency).
- Order: perhumid (A) > humid (B) > sub-humid (C) > semi-arid (D) > arid (E).
- Perhumid: Western Ghats-west coast, North-East; semi-arid: rain-shadow interior Deccan; arid: western Rajasthan (Thar) and Kutch.
### 3. CLIMATE OF ANDHRA PRADESH
- **Tropical Monsoon** (APPSC-GS 2018: "Tropical"); Koeppen **Aw**; latitude **12°41'N to 19°07'N**.
| Region | Districts | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Humid coastal | Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, Kakinada | Heavy rain |
| Deltaic | Konaseema, East/West Godavari, Krishna | Fertile, river-fed |
| Semi-arid Rayalaseema | Anantapur, Sri Sathya Sai, Kurnool, Nandyal, YSR Kadapa | Droughts, extreme heat |
| Hill | Araku, Lambasingi, Horsley Hills | Cool |
- Rayalaseema = **rain-shadow** of SW monsoon; peak rain **Oct-Nov** (NE monsoon); coast peaks July. Average ~966 mm.
- Rayalaseema droughts come from low, uneven rain; low population density is a result, not a cause.
- **Six agro-climatic zones**; **Scarce Rainfall Zone**: Kurnool, Anantapuramu.
| Rainfall zone | Annual rain |
|---|---|
| Srikakulam-Visakhapatnam-Agency | 125-200 cm |
| Kadapa-Kurnool | 75-100 cm (dry deciduous belt) |
| Rayalaseema interior | under 70 cm |
| Anantapur-Hagari valley | under 51 cm, **lowest** |
| Extreme | Place |
|---|---|
| Wettest | **Sileru-Sabari** valleys (140-152 cm); district Visakhapatnam |
| Driest | **Hagari** valley; district Anantapur |
| Hottest | **Rentachintala** (Palnadu) |
| Coldest | **Lambasingi** (ASR) |
### 4. CLIMATE CHANGE SIGNALS
- **2024** = India's warmest year since **1901**; **446** heatwave days (highest since 2000); IMD anomaly +0.65°C (2025: +0.28°C).
- WMO 2025: ~**1.43°C** above pre-industrial; 2015-2025 the 11 warmest years.
- 2026: IMD forecast below-normal monsoon for most of AP, citing **El Nino**.
### 5. IMPACTS ON AP
- Coastal erosion at **Uppada** (Kakinada) and Krishna coast: rising seas, cyclonic waves.
- **Godavari delta sinking**: land subsidence exceeds regional sea-level rise.
- Coastline now **1,053.07 km** (3rd longest), 28 districts; earlier 974 km, 26 districts.
- Heatstroke cases 833 (2023) to 5,154 (2025); deaths 3 to 0.
### 6. ACTION PLANS AND VULNERABILITY
- **NAPCC**: 30 June 2008; **8 missions**: Solar, Enhanced Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Habitat, Water, Himalayan Ecosystem, Green India, Sustainable Agriculture, Strategic Knowledge. No nuclear mission.
- SAPCCs: **Odisha first (2010)**; mitigation (2010-15) to district-level adaptation (2020 on). Adaptation spending 5.6% of GDP (FY22).
- **AP SAPCC 2025-30**: 8 energy-sector adaptation measures; **1 lakh EV charging stations**.
- DST vulnerability report (2021): 8 highly vulnerable states, Jharkhand, Mizoram, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Assam, Bihar, Arunachal Pradesh, West Bengal; **Jharkhand** most vulnerable.
