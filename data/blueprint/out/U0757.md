## U0757 | OTHER RENEWABLES – BIOMASS, BIOFUELS, SMALL HYDRO, GEOTHERMAL, TIDAL, WASTE-TO-ENERGY
### 1. MNRE CLASSIFICATION OF RENEWABLE SOURCES
- **MNRE renewables:** solar, wind, **bio-power** (biomass, bagasse cogeneration, waste-to-energy), **small hydro** and **large hydro** (counted as renewable **since 2019**).
- Under development: **geothermal, tidal, OTEC**.
- **Nuclear** = non-fossil but **not renewable**; counted separately (8.78 GW).
- Significance: geothermal gives **24x7 baseload** (underground heat does not depend on sunlight or wind); bioenergy links waste management, rural income and energy security.
!TRAP Large hydro is renewable (since 2019); nuclear is non-fossil but NOT renewable.
### 2. BIOMASS AND BIOGAS
#### 2.1 Biomass Energy
- **Biomass energy** = solar energy stored by plants through **photosynthesis**; burned directly or converted to biogas/biofuels.
- **Gobar gas = biomass energy** (from cattle dung) (APPSC 2019).
- Biomass from waste: agricultural waste (stubble, paddy husk, manure, dairy waste), **food-processing residues, human sewage, biogenic municipal solid waste** (all three are biomass).
#### 2.2 Biogas
| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Composition | **Methane (~50–75%)** + CO2 + traces (piped natural gas is 85–95% methane) |
| Process | **Anaerobic digestion / fermentation** of organic matter |
| Microbes | **Methanogens** (methanogenic archaea/bacteria), in absence of oxygen |
| By-product | Slurry = good organic manure |
| Gobar gas / cooking gas | Biogas from cattle dung; main component **methane** |
- Pioneer: floating-drum **"Gramalakshmi"** plant of **Jashbhai Patel (1950s)**, spread by **KVIC**.
- **CBG (Compressed Biogas)** = purified, compressed biogas, chemically identical to **CNG**; renewable, carbon-neutral.
- **Bagasse cogeneration:** sugar mills burn bagasse → steam + power together.
#### 2.3 Gasification and Microbial Fuel Cells
- **Biomass gasification:** heating with **limited (controlled) oxygen** → syngas (**CO, H2, CO2**) for engines/power.
- **Producer gas** = CO + H2 + CO2 + methane.
- **Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC):** microbes act as **catalysts**, oxidise organic matter and pass electrons to an electrode; **cleans wastewater and makes electricity together**.
### 3. BIOFUEL GENERATIONS AND ALGAL FUELS
| Generation | Feedstock | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1G** | Food crops: sugarcane, molasses, corn, cassava, vegetable oils | Bioethanol (yeast **Saccharomyces cerevisiae**), biodiesel |
| **2G** | Non-food **lignocellulosic** residue: rice straw, husk, wood chips, bamboo, non-edible oils | Cellulosic ethanol |
| **3G** | **Algae / algal biomass** (APPSC 2023) | Algal biodiesel, biobutanol |
| **4G** | **Genetically modified** algae, yeast, fungi, cyanobacteria | Carbon-negative fuels |
- **Biobutanol** (Clostridium acetobutylicum): higher energy density than ethanol.
- 1G = food-fuel conflict; 2G avoids it but needs complex conversion.
- **Algal biofuel advantages:** high yield per area; uses wastewater and CO2; grows on land unsuitable for agriculture and also on land (ponds, lakes, tanks), not only seas.
- **Limitations:** high cultivation/harvest cost, **energy-intensive lipid extraction**, contamination, scale-up; needs high expertise, capital and large-scale facilities (a drawback for developing countries); not cost-effective.
!TRAP 3G = algae; 4G = genetically modified algae/microbes.
### 4. BIODIESEL, GASOHOL AND POWER ALCOHOL
#### 4.1 Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Biodiesel** | Methyl/ethyl esters of fatty acids by **transesterification** of vegetable oils/animal fats with methanol or ethanol |
| **Gasohol** | **Petrol (gasoline) + ethanol**, typically ~10% ethanol |
| **Power alcohol** | Fuel-grade (anhydrous) ethanol, mainly from **molasses** (sugar-industry by-product) |
| Bioethanol | Fermentation of sugar/starch by yeast |
#### 4.2 Feedstock Plants
| Crop | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Jatropha curcas** (ratanjyot) | Biodiesel plant (APPSC 2019); seeds ~40% oil; non-edible; grows in harsh conditions |
| **Pongamia pinnata** (Karanja) | Biodiesel plant; non-edible oil rich in oleic acid |
| Mahua, neem, sunflower | Other biofuel sources |
| **Sugarcane** | Ethanol from fermented juice and molasses |
| **Maize** | Starch-rich energy crop for ethanol |
- India has **400+** tree species with non-edible oilseeds.
- **Palm stearin oil** = feedstock of about **94%** of India's biodiesel (mostly imported), not jatropha.
- **RUCO** (FSSAI) = Repurpose Used Cooking Oil for biodiesel.
- Others: rapeseed, soybean, animal tallow, acid oil, algae.
- Biodiesel blend still **below 0.5%**; target **5% by 2030**.
- **April 2023:** Belgian firm **VITO** launched a 2-year biodiesel project at **Ayodhya**.
### 5. NATIONAL POLICY ON BIOFUELS
#### 5.1 NPB 2018
- Aim: cut import dependence, domestic feedstock, energy security, jobs; does **not** discourage advanced biofuels.
- Targets: **E20 + B5 by 2030**.
| Category | Fuels |
|---|---|
| **Basic biofuels** | 1G bioethanol, biodiesel |
| **Advanced biofuels** | 2G ethanol, drop-in fuels from MSW, 3G biofuels, bio-CNG |
- Ethanol feedstock widened beyond molasses: **sugarcane juice, B-molasses, surplus FCI rice, maize, damaged foodgrains (wheat), cassava, rotten potatoes, sugar beet**, grasses, agri residues (rice straw, cotton stalk, corn cobs, saw dust, bagasse).
- Biodiesel feedstock: non-food oilseeds (jatropha, pongamia) + algae.
- **Surplus food grains** for ethanol only with **National Biofuel Coordination Committee (NBCC)** approval (APPSC-G2 2025).
- **NBCC** is chaired by the **Minister of Petroleum and Natural Gas**; it approved surplus FCI rice (April 2020) and surplus maize (November 2020).
#### 5.2 NPB 2022 Amendment
| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| E20 target | Advanced from 2030 to **ESY 2025-26** |
| Feedstock | More feedstocks allowed |
| Advanced fuels | Boost to 2G/advanced biofuels |
| NBCC | **New members may be added** |
#### 5.3 Enablers
- **Motor Vehicles Act, Section 52**: engine conversion to biofuels.
- **100% FDI (automatic)** for biofuel technology for domestic use; plantations barred.
- VGF, tax credits, differential pricing for advanced biofuels.
### 6. ETHANOL BLENDING PROGRAMME
#### 6.1 Milestones
| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| Pilot | 2001 |
| **E5** in 9 States + 4 UTs | **1 January 2003** |
| **E10** achieved | **June 2022** (ahead of Nov 2022 target) |
| **E20 mandatory** (minimum **RON 95**) | **1 April 2026** |
| BIS **IS 19850:2026** (E22, E25, E27, E30) | May 2026 |
| Next goal **E30** | 2030 |
#### 6.2 Pricing and Gains
- EBP run by **MoPNG**; OMCs pay feedstock-wise prices: **maize > molasses > rice**; maize price CAGR 11.7% (FY22-25).
- Sugar mills may now make ethanol directly from cane juice; **Ethanol Interest Subvention Scheme** funds distillery capacity.
- Debates: food vs fuel (sugar prices, Sep 2026); maize-ethanol pulled land from **pulses and oilseeds**; 1–2% mileage loss with E20 (NITI roadmap 2021).
#### 6.3 Global Producers
- **USA** (maize) = world's **largest producer and exporter** of fuel ethanol; **Brazil** (sugarcane) second; together ~80% of world ethanol (UPSC 2025).
- **Brazil:** mandatory blending since **1976**; ~25% ethanol since 2007.
### 7. AVIATION BIOFUELS AND GLOBAL BIOFUELS ALLIANCE
- **First biofuel flight in India:** **SpiceJet**, **Dehradun–Delhi, 27 August 2018** (Bombardier Q-400), jatropha biojet from **CSIR-IIP**.
- **SAF targets** (international flights): **1% by 2027, 2% by 2028, 5% by 2030**; aligned with ICAO net zero 2050.
- SAF = **drop-in** synthesised hydrocarbons (not direct ethanol), blendable up to **50%** with jet fuel.
- SAF feedstocks: **agri residues, corn grain, wastewater sludge, wood-mill waste** (all four, UPSC 2024).
- **IOCL Panipat** = first Indian ISCC CORSIA-certified SAF producer (Aug 2025); Air India-IOCL SAF MoU.
- **BioE3 Policy 2024** backs bio-jet fuel.
- **Global Biofuels Alliance (GBA):** launched **9 September 2023** at the **G20 Summit, New Delhi**; core founders **India, USA, Brazil**; Secretariat in **New Delhi** (host agreement Oct 2024).

| Scheme | Year / Body | Feature |

!TRAP SATAT = MoPNG; National Bioenergy Programme = MNRE; co-firing = Ministry of Power (SAMARTH).
### 9. WASTE-TO-ENERGY AND SMALL HYDRO
#### 9.1 WtE Routes
| Route | Principle |
|---|---|
| Incineration | Direct burning of MSW |
| **Pyrolysis** | Heating **without oxygen** → char, bio-oil, syngas |
| **Gasification** | **Partial oxidation** (controlled oxygen) → syngas |
| Biomethanation | Microbes convert wet waste → biogas |
| Landfill gas | Methane captured |
| **RDF** | Refuse-derived fuel = dry combustible part of MSW |
- Plants: **Okhla**, Ghazipur, Narela-Bawana (Delhi); national WtE capacity 0.88 GW; bio-power 11.75 GW (Aug 2026).
#### 9.2 Small Hydro
| Class | Capacity |
|---|---|
| Micro | up to **100 kW** |
| Mini | 101 kW – **2 MW** |
| Small | 2 – **25 MW** |
- Sites: hill streams (run-of-river), **canal falls, toes of dams**; ~half potential in Arunachal, Himachal, J&K, Uttarakhand.
- Installed 5.18 GW (~25% of potential).
### 10. GEOTHERMAL ENERGY
- Source: **Earth's internal heat**, largely from **radioactive decay**; surface signs: hot springs, geysers, steam, **volcanoes** (APPSC 2018).
- Potential: **~10,600 MW from 381 hot springs** (GSI).
- Provinces: **Himalaya, Godavari valley, Cambay, SONATA, Mahanadi, West Coast, Andaman-Nicobar** (not Indo-Gangetic Plain / Ganga Delta).
| Field | State / UT |
|---|---|
| **Puga Valley, Chumathang** | Ladakh – highest potential |
| Tattapani | Chhattisgarh |
| Manikaran | Himachal Pradesh |
| **Tapovan** | Uttarakhand |
| **Surajkund** | Jharkhand |
| **Manuguru** (old Khammam), Godavari valley | Telangana (earlier AP) |
- **India's first geothermal project: Puga, Ladakh** (ONGC; APPSC 2021); world's highest (~14,000 ft); ONGC MoU extended 5 years (26 May 2026).
- **World's first geothermal power: Larderello, Italy (1904)**.
- **National Policy on Geothermal Energy** – India's first, MNRE, **15 September 2025**; **five pilot projects**; Net Zero 2070.
- **EGS** = artificially stimulated reservoirs. Challenges: uneven reservoirs, high drilling cost, subsidence, water pollution.
### 11. TIDAL, WAVE AND OTEC
| Site | State | Tidal range |
|---|---|---|
| **Gulf of Khambhat** | Gujarat | 5–10 m; **~7,000 MW** – best site (APPSC 2025) |
| Gulf of Kutch | Gujarat | 5–8 m |
| Sundarbans (Ganga delta) | West Bengal | ~5 m |
- Criterion: tidal range ~**5 m or more** in a narrow gulf/estuary; coastline ~7,517 km.
- Pilots proposed (not commissioned): **Durgaduani Creek** (Sundarbans), Gulf of Kutch.
- **First wave plant: Vizhinjam, Kerala (1991)**.
- **OTEC:** warm surface vs cold deep water, ~**20°C** gap, 30°N–30°S; sites **Lakshadweep, A&N**; power + desalination.
- **NIOT, Chennai (MoES)** = lead ocean-energy body; first desalination plant **Kavaratti, Lakshadweep (2005)**, LTTD, 1 lakh L/day.
!TRAP Gulf of Mannar = offshore wind zone, not the tidal answer.
### 12. ANDHRA PRADESH ANGLE

- **Reliance** laid the foundation of its **first CBG plant at Kanigiri, Prakasam** (2 April 2025; ₹139 crore; Napier grass on barren land) – first of **500 CBG plants, ₹65,000 crore**.
