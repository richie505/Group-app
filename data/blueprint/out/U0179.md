### 3. STEPS INTRODUCING ENGLISH EDUCATION
- **Charter Act 1813:** Rs 1 lakh a year for **revival of Indian literature** and **promotion of science**; also ended the India trade monopoly and admitted missionaries.
- **GCPI 1823:** handled the grant; the debate inside it led to English education.
- Led to English education: **Charter Act 1813, GCPI 1823 and the Orientalist–Anglicist controversy**.
- **English Education Act 1835** (Bentinck, on Macaulay's Minute): **English = medium of higher education**.
- English education introduced under **Lord William Cavendish Bentinck**.
- 1835 resolution was a **fatal blow** to Madras district/tahsildari vernacular schools.
### 4. ORIENTALIST–ANGLICIST DEBATE
| Orientalists | Anglicists |
|---|---|
| Wanted the grant for **Sanskrit, Arabic, Persian** learning | Wanted **Western science and literature through English** |
| **Warren Hastings**, **Sir William Jones**, Jonathan Duncan, **H.T. Colebrooke**, **H.H. Wilson** | **T.B. Macaulay** (leader), **Bentinck**, **Charles Trevelyan** |
| In Madras, **Thomas Munro** backed pathashalas and vernacular schooling | Western science and English literature |
- Result: Anglicists won in **1835**.
- **Munro's Survey of Indigenous Education (1826)** found a wide network of **vernacular village schools** in the Madras Presidency.
- Munro's collectorate and tahsildari schools (2 in Kadapa, 1 Nellore, 2 Vizag by 1830) were **closed in 1836** after the 1835 resolution.
!TRAP Thomas Munro supported **vernacular** schooling, not English-only education.
### 5. MACAULAY'S MINUTE, 1835
- Date: **2 February 1835**; Governor-General **William Bentinck**.
- Written by **Macaulay** as **Law Member** and **President of the GCPI**; approved by Bentinck, the **"Father of Modern Western Education in India"**.
- Called the **foundation of modern education** in India.
- **Recommendation:** education funds **exclusively** for **English-medium teaching of Western science and literature**; higher education put before mass primary schooling.
- **Famous remark:** "a **single shelf of a good European library** was worth the whole native literature of India and Arabia."
- **Aim:** a class of Indians to act as **interpreters** between the rulers and the ruled.
- **Downward Filtration Theory:** education to flow from the **higher classes to the masses**.
- Criticism: downward filtration **neglected mass literacy**; Hunter and Hartog kept returning to **primary education**.
!TRAP **1835 = Bentinck + Macaulay**; **1854 = Charles Wood + Dalhousie**.
### 6. WOOD'S DESPATCH, 1854
- Issued **19 July 1854** by **Sir Charles Wood**, **President of the Board of Control**, under Governor-General **Dalhousie**.
- Epithet: **"Magna Carta of English Education in India"**; the most important education document.
- **Aim:** spread of **Western culture**; foundation of modern education.
#### 6.1 Provisions
- **Graded hierarchy:** vernacular primary schools → Anglo-vernacular high schools → affiliated colleges → universities.
- **Grants-in-aid** to private (mostly **missionary**) enterprise.
- **English for higher** education; **vernacular for lower**.
- Universities in the presidency towns; government supervision of vernacular education; **teacher training**.
- Stressed **female education** and **vocational training**.
- Asked the Company to take up **mass instruction** – it **rejected downward filtration**.
#### 6.2 Three Universities of 1857
| University | Place | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Calcutta | Calcutta (Lord Canning) | 1857 |
| Bombay | Bombay | 1857 |
| Madras | Madras – **5 September 1857** | 1857 |
- Model: **University of London** – affiliating and examining universities.
### 7. LATER COMMISSIONS AND PLANS
| Body | Year | Viceroy / head | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hunter Commission** | **1882** | **W.W. Hunter**; Viceroy **Ripon** | **Primary** and secondary education; schools to **local boards and municipalities**; private enterprise; education of backward classes (esp. Muslims) and women; reviewed progress since 1854 |
| **Universities (Raleigh) Commission** | **1902** | **Curzon**; head **Sir Thomas Raleigh**; Indian members **Syed Hussain Bilgrami**, **Justice Gurudas Banerjee** | → **Indian Universities Act 1904** (more government control) |
| **Resolution on Education Policy** | **1913** | **Hardinge II** | Answer to **Gokhale's** demand for compulsory primary education |
| **Sadler (Calcutta University) Commission** | **1917–19** | **Dr M.E. Sadler** (VC, Leeds); member **Ashutosh Mukherjee**, Zia-ud-din Ahmad; Chelmsford | **12-year school**, intermediate stage, **3-year degree** |
| **Hartog Committee** | **1929** | Philip Hartog; **Irwin** (not Wavell) | **Wastage and stagnation** in primary education |
| **Sargent Plan** | **1944** | **Wavell** | **Free compulsory education, 6–14**; vocational and scientific outlook |
- Hunter recommendations took effect in **1883–84**.
- **W.W. Hunter** also compiled the **Imperial Gazetteer of India**.
!TRAP Hunter Commission 1882 did **not** deal with famine (Strachey did). The **Hunter Committee 1919** (Disorders Inquiry, **Lord William Hunter**) = **Jallianwala Bagh** and Punjab disturbances.

### 9. EDUCATION IN ANDHRA AND MADRAS PRESIDENCY
#### 9.1 Missionaries
- Western education in Andhra was **first spread by Christian missionaries** (contributed most).
| Mission | Andhra centre |
|---|---|
| **London Missionary Society** | **First** (from **1805**); **Kadapa** centre (1824) |
| **North German Society** | **Rajahmundry and Eluru** (1843) |
| **Lutherans** (Father Heyer) | **Guntur** |
| **American Baptists** | **Ongole, Nellore**, Rayalaseema |
| **Church Missionary Society** | **Machilipatnam** (Rev. Robert Noble) |
#### 9.2 Coastal Andhra Schools and Colleges
- **Central Collegiate Institute, Madras (1841)** – forerunner of **Presidency College**.
- **Noble School, Machilipatnam (1843):** **Rev. Robert Turlington Noble** of the CMS; college by **1864** (today **Noble College**) – not Rajahmundry.
- **G.N. Taylor**, Sub-Collector of Godavari: **Anglo-Vernacular School, Narsapur (1852)** with branches at **Palakollu, Penugonda, Achanta**; drafted the **Godavari District Education Scheme (1854)**.
- **Rajahmundry:** Elphinstone's scheme (**1839**) → regional school (**1854**) → second-grade college (**1873**) → degree college (**1877**).
- **Result-based grants-in-aid** to primary schools from **1877–78**.
- Hindu College, Visakhapatnam (1878) – now **Mrs A.V.N. College**; **Khallikota College** (1879) – **Berhampur**.
- **Order:** Central Collegiate Institute (1841) → Noble School (1843) → Madras University (1857) → result-based grants (1877–78).
!TRAP Noble = **Machilipatnam (1843)**; G.N. Taylor = **Narsapur (1852)**.
