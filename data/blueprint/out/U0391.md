## U0391 | COMMUNALISM – MEANING, CAUSES, FORMS AND GROWTH IN INDIA
### 1. MEANING AND DEFINITIONS
#### 1.1 Indian Meaning
- In India, communalism is most often seen as **religious difference** between groups that leads to tension and rioting.
- A group promotes its own interests **at the expense of others**.
- Hindu-Muslim communalism is seen as the single largest threat to **secular ideals**.
#### 1.2 Definition → Scholar
| Scholar | Definition / view |
|---|---|
| **Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe** | "The **political assertiveness of a community** to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation" (asked in **APPSC G2 2025**) |
| **Bipan Chandra** | The belief that because a group follows one religion, it also has **common social, political and economic interests**; gave the **three stages** |
| **Ram Ahuja** | An **ideology** that society is divided into religious communities whose **interests differ and often oppose** each other; "communal persons" do politics through religion |
| **T.K. Oommen** | **Six dimensions** of communalism (assimilationist to secessionist) |
| **W.C. Smith** (*Modern Islam in India*) | Ideology that treats each religious group as the basic **social, political and economic unit** and stresses antagonism between such groups |
| **Asghar Ali Engineer** | Leading scholar of communalism and communal harmony; *Communalism in India* |
!TRAP The name is **T.K. Oommen**, not "T.R. Oommen". Oommen = six dimensions; Bipan Chandra = three stages.
### 2. BIPAN CHANDRA'S THREE STAGES
| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| 1. **Communal nationalism** | Followers of one religion share common secular (political, economic) interests; still **nationalist** in outlook |
| 2. **Liberal communalism** | Interests of one community seen as **different but reconcilable** within one nation |
| 3. **Extreme communalism** | Interests seen as **mutually antagonistic**; politics of **fear, hatred and violence** |
- After the **1937 elections**, the Muslim League turned to extreme communalism "based on fear, hatred and violence"; echoed by the **Hindu Mahasabha and RSS**.
!KEY Order: communal nationalism → liberal communalism → extreme communalism.
### 3. CAUSES OF COMMUNALISM
| Type | Causes |
|---|---|
| Historical / colonial | British **divide and rule**; **separate electorates**; caste and religion recorded in the **first census (1871-72)**; 19th-century **revivalist movements** |
| Political | **Vote-bank** and identity politics; religion-based parties |
| Economic | **Poverty**, unemployment, lopsided development, relative deprivation |
| Legal / administrative | **Delayed justice**, lenient punishment, police inaction, biased intelligence |
| Social / media | **Fake news** and hate messages on social media; media amplifying communal narratives |
| Psychological / international | Prejudices, rumours; foreign funding |
- **1920s:** Arya Samaj **Shuddhi and Sangathan** vs Muslim **Tabligh and Tanzim**.
!PAIR 1920s movements | Shuddhi, Sangathan | Arya Samaj || Tabligh, Tanzim | Muslim bodies | Counter-movement
### 4. GROWTH IN COLONIAL INDIA (1887-1940)
#### 4.1 Lord Minto and the 1909 Act
- **Oct 1906 – Simla Deputation** led by the **Aga Khan** to **Lord Minto** demanded separate electorates.
- **Indian Councils Act, 1909** (Morley-Minto Reforms) gave the **first legal separate electorate** for Muslims.
- Lord Minto is called the "**Father of the Communal Electorate**".
#### 4.2 Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1887 | Lord **Dufferin** used **Syed Ahmad Khan** to keep Muslims away from Congress |
| Oct 1906 | Simla Deputation to Lord Minto |
| 1906 | **Muslim League** founded at **Dacca** under the Aga Khan and **Nawab Salimullah**; first communal party |
| 1909 | First legal separate electorate |
| 1915 | **All India Hindu Mahasabha** founded under the aegis of the **Maharaja of Kasim Bazar**; associated with **Madan Mohan Malaviya** |
| 1916 | **Lucknow Pact**: Congress accepted separate electorates and weightage |
| 1928 | **Nehru Report** |
| 1929 | **Jinnah's Fourteen Points** (reply to Nehru Report) |
| 1930 | **Allama Iqbal** proposed a consolidated Muslim state |
| 16 Aug 1932 | **Communal Award** of British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** |
| 1932 | **Poona Pact** (Gandhi and Ambedkar, Yerwada Jail) |
| 24 Mar 1940 | **Lahore (Pakistan) Resolution** adopted (moved 23 March) |
- Lucknow Pact gave short-term Hindu-Muslim unity but **legitimised communal politics**, laying groundwork for Partition.
- **23 March 1956** – Pakistan became a republic.
!TRAP 1906 = first communal party (Muslim League); 1909 = first legal separate electorate.
### 5. COMMUNAL AWARD AND TWO-NATION THEORY
- **Syed Ahmad Khan** = ideological father of the **two-nation theory** (Hindi-Urdu controversy; advised Muslims to stay out of Congress).
- **Communal Award (1932):** separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians and **Depressed Classes** (for **20 years**); **71 seats** for depressed classes.
- **Poona Pact:** replaced Dalit separate electorates with reserved seats in **joint electorates**; **71 → 148** reserved seats in provincial legislatures.
!NUM 71 = Communal Award seats; 148 = Poona Pact reserved seats; 20 years = Award's separate electorates
### 6. FORMS – OOMMEN'S SIX DIMENSIONS
| Dimension | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Assimilationist** | Small religious groups absorbed into a larger one | Claiming STs, Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are Hindus |
| **Welfarist** | Works for one community's welfare only | Christian or Parsi associations |
| **Retreatist** | Small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community |
| **Retaliatory** | Tries to harm members of other communities | Riots, revenge cycles |
| **Separatist** | Separate territorial state **within India** | Gorkhaland, Bodoland |
| **Secessionist** | Independent state **outside India** | Khalistan, "Azad Kashmir" |
- Other forms: **Political** = uses religion for votes; **Economic** = grows from competition for jobs and resources.
!TRAP Separatist = within India; Secessionist = outside India.
### 7. RELIGIOUS FUNDAMENTALISM VS COMMUNALISM
- **Fundamentalism:** strict adherence to religious beliefs; often rejects modern interpretations.
- Features: **literal reading of scripture**, conservatism, resistance to change, **group exclusiveness**.
- Causes: **identity crisis**, globalisation, **social insecurity**, political mobilisation, economic inequality.
| Religious fundamentalism | Communalism |
|---|---|
| Return to "fundamentals"; mainly **doctrinal** | **Political mobilisation** of religious identity |
| Reforms one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed **against other communities**; seeks power and resources |
| Can exist **without inter-group conflict** | Always "us vs them" |
!TRAP Group exclusiveness is a **feature** of fundamentalism, not a cause.
### 8. SECULARISM AND SAFEGUARDS
| Provision | What it does |
|---|---|
| Preamble | "**Secular**" added by **42nd Amendment (1976)** |
| Art. 14-16 | Equality |
| Art. 25-28 | Freedom of religion |
| Art. 29-30 | Minority rights |
| Art. 51A(e) | Promote harmony and common brotherhood |
| Sec. 123(3), RP Act 1951 | Appeal on ground of religion = **corrupt practice** |
| **Religious Institutions (Prevention of Misuse) Act, 1988** | Bars religious premises for political activity, hiding criminals or arms |
| **Places of Worship (Special Provisions) Act, 1991** | In force 11 July 1991; freezes religious character as on **15 August 1947** (with statutory exceptions) |
- Bodies: **National Integration Council**, **National Foundation for Communal Harmony**, MHA communal harmony guidelines.
- Other amendments (distractors): **24th** = Parliament's amending power; **44th (1978)** = undid parts of 42nd; **52nd (1985)** = anti-defection.
- APPSC GS 2007 key treated the **Shah Bano case** as a development that "contributed to the spirit of communal harmony".
- **16 May 2026:** "minority vs majority communalism" debate; parties accused of selective opposition.
- **16 Aug 2026:** Kerala's V.D. Satheesan called for unity against communalism, linking the Haldwani "purification" row to secular values.
