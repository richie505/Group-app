## U0158 | ADVENT OF EUROPEANS IN ANDHRA – PORTUGUESE, DUTCH, FRENCH AND ENGLISH
### 1. WHY EUROPEANS CAME TO ANDHRA
- **Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam)** on the Krishna delta coast was the chief port of the **Qutb Shahi** kingdom of Golconda.
- Its **chintz** (painted/printed cotton) and **diamond** trade drew all four powers: Portuguese, Dutch, English and French.
- **Hyderabad** was founded in **1591** by **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**; under him the Dutch (1605) and the English (1611) got their Machilipatnam factories.
- Early travellers: **Marco Polo** visited **Motupalli**; **Tavernier**, a **17th-century** trader, visited the Andhra mines in search of the best diamonds and precious stones.
- **Madras (1639)** became the English base that later controlled Andhra.
#### 1.1 Order of Arrival in India
- Sequence: **Portuguese (1498) → Dutch (1605) → English (1608) → French**.
- The **French** were the **last Europeans to come to India as traders**.
!KEY Portuguese came first to India (1498), but their Machilipatnam factory came last (1670).
### 2. ORDER OF FACTORIES AT MACHILIPATNAM

|---|---|---|---|

### 3. THE ENGLISH IN ANDHRA
#### 3.1 The Ship Globe (1611)
- The English ship **Globe** reached Machilipatnam in **1611**, under **Captain Hippon**, with **Peter Floris** (a Dutchman in English service) as merchant.
- The English set up their **first factory on the east coast** at Machilipatnam in **1611**, with permission from **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**.
- The Masulipatnam factory (1611) marks the start of the East India Company's expansion in the south.
#### 3.2 Golden Farman (1632)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Granted by | **Abdullah Qutb Shah**, Sultan of Golconda |
| Year | **1632** |
| To | English East India Company |
| Privileges | Trade freely in the ports of the Golconda kingdom; reopen the **Masulipatnam** factory |
!TRAP Golden Farman (1632) came from the **Sultan of Golconda**, not from the Mughal emperor.
#### 3.3 English Trade Practice
- English used **Dadni** advances to weavers through **dubashes** (local intermediaries).
- English **paid weavers less** while demanding **high quality**, so weavers avoided them.
- By comparison, the Dutch, Portuguese and French advanced money to weavers and paid fairly.
### 4. THE DUTCH IN ANDHRA
#### 4.1 Dutch Settlements
| Dutch settlement | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Machilipatnam** | **1605** | **First Dutch factory in Andhra**; first European factory in Andhra |
| Nizampatnam (Petapoli) | 1606 | Second Dutch factory on the Coromandel coast |
| Pulicat | 1610 | Later Fort Geldria (1613) |
| Palakollu | 1613 | Godavari delta |
| Draksharama | 1633 | Godavari delta |
| Bheemunipatnam | 17th century | North coastal Andhra |
#### 4.2 Dutch Features
- Followed a **spice monopoly**, minted **their own coins**, and traded **without middlemen** for profit.
- **Dutch = first to get the right to mint coins** in Andhra (granted by local rulers).
!KEY Dutch first factory in Andhra: **Masulipatnam, 1605**.
### 5. THE PORTUGUESE IN ANDHRA
#### 5.1 Policy and Decline
- **Blue Water Policy** of **Francisco de Almeida**: control the **sea**, not land.
- Held the **Arabian Sea trade monopoly** until the **17th century**; decline began with the **Dutch arrival (1605)**.
- Used **Hooghly** as a base for **piracy** in the Bay of Bengal.
- Factory at Machilipatnam only in **1670** (permission of Abdullah Qutb Shah).
#### 5.2 Crops and Fruits Introduced by the Portuguese
| Crop / fruit | Note |
|---|---|
| **Tobacco** | Early 17th century |
| **Pineapple** | About 1548 |
| **Cashew** | Brought to Goa to check soil erosion |
| **Chilli** (red pepper) | 16th century, from the Americas |
| **Potato**, sweet potato | From the Americas |
| **Groundnut** | From the Americas |
| **Papaya**, **guava** | 16th century |
!TRAP Tobacco, chilli and cashew came with the **Portuguese**, not with the Dutch or English.

### 7. THE FRENCH IN ANDHRA
#### 7.1 Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1664** | French East India Company founded (**Colbert**) |
| **1668** | First French factory at **Surat**, soon its HQ |
| **1669** | **Machilipatnam** factory – by **Marcara**, with **Abdullah Qutb Shah's** permission |
| 1673-74 | **Pondicherry** (Francois Martin) |
| **1723** | **Yanam** – the **only French territorial possession in Andhra** |
| **1753** | **Bussy** gets the **Northern Circars** from **Salabat Jung** |
#### 7.2 Bussy's Role
- **Bussy** secured the **Northern Circars** in **1753** by a grant from **Salabat Jung** (not Muhammad Ali), for the upkeep of French troops in the Nizam's service.
- In the **Bobbili War (1757)**, **Vijayarama Raju** of **Vizianagaram** took **French (Bussy's) help** against Bobbili (APPSC 2019).
!TRAP The Circars grant of 1753 came from **Salabat Jung**, not from Muhammad Ali (the British-backed Nawab of the Carnatic).
#### 7.3 Four Powers at a Glance
| Feature | Portuguese | Dutch | English | French |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reached India | 1498 (first) | 1605 | 1608 | Last (company 1664) |
| Machilipatnam factory | 1670 (4th) | 1605 (1st) | 1611 (2nd) | 1669 (3rd) |
| Permission | Abdullah Qutb Shah | Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah | Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah | Abdullah Qutb Shah |
| Policy / method | Blue Water Policy | Spice monopoly, own coins, no middlemen | Dadni advances, dubashes | Political alliances (Bussy) |
| Other Andhra posts | – | Nizampatnam, Pulicat, Palakollu, Draksharama, Bheemunipatnam | Armagaon (Nellore), later Madras base | Yanam (1723) |
| Treatment of weavers | Fair advances | Fair advances | Paid less, demanded high quality | Fair advances |
| End in Andhra | Decline after 1605 | Lost ground to English | Gained Northern Sarkars | Defeated at Chandurthi (1758) |
- The English position grew in three steps: **factory (1611) → Golden Farman (1632) → Northern Sarkars after Chandurthi (1758-59)**.
- The French gained their greatest Andhra influence not by trade but by **Bussy's alliance with the Nizam** (Salabat Jung); it ended when Forde took Masulipatnam (1759).
### 8. ANGLO-FRENCH RIVALRY IN ANDHRA
#### 8.1 Events → Year → Outcome
| Event | Year | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Second Carnatic War** | **1749-1754** | British installed **Muhammad Ali** as Nawab of the Carnatic |
| Circars to the French | **1753** | Bussy gets the Northern Circars from Salabat Jung |
| **Bobbili War** | **1757** | French supported **Vizianagaram** |
| **Battle of Chandurthi** | **1758** | **Col. Forde** (sent from **Bengal**), aided by **Ananda Gajapati**, defeated the French under **Conflans**; led to surrender of the Northern Sarkars to the British |
| Fall of Masulipatnam | **1759** | Masulipatnam to Col. Forde; **Salabat Jung** gave **Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar** and part of **Kondapalli** to the British |
| **Wandiwash** | **1760** | **Eyre Coote** defeated **Count de Lally**; end of French power in India |
- Order: **Circars grant (1753) → Bobbili (1757) → Chandurthi (1758) → fall of Masulipatnam (1759) → Wandiwash (1760)**.
!KEY Battle of Chandurthi, 1758: Forde (English) beat Conflans (French) – the turning point in Andhra.
