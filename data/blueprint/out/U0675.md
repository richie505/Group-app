## U0675 | INDIA – INDUSTRIAL REGIONS AND BELTS; FACTORS OF LOCALISATION
### 1. FACTORS AND ORIENTATION
- Factors: raw material, power, market, labour, transport, **water** (it matters), government policy, clustering.
| Orientation | Examples |
|---|---|
| Raw-material (weight-losing) | Iron and steel (~4 t input per t), sugar (cane perishable), **cement (limestone)** |
| Power | **Aluminium** (14,000–15,000 kWh/t) |
| Market (weight-gaining, fragile) | **Bakery**, soft drinks |
| Footloose | **Software**, diamond polishing, electronics assembly (APPSC 2019) |
- **Weber (1909)**: MI above 1 (iron and steel, sugar) → near raw material; ubiquitous inputs → market.
- **Cotton** = pure material (no weight loss), so cotton textiles spread all over India.
- **Electronics**: raw material not the main factor. Footloose clusters: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Noida.

### 5. SEZs, NIMZs AND PARKS
- **SEZ Act 2005**, in force **10 Feb 2006**; 368 notified, **276 operational** (31 Mar 2025). Examples: Vallarpadam, Nanguneri.
- **NIMZ**: National Manufacturing Policy **2011** – manufacturing **25% of GDP**, **100 million jobs**; townships bigger than SEZs, different governance, exit and tax rules.
- **BHAVYA**: 100 plug-and-play parks; ₹33,660 crore, 2026-27 to 2031-32; challenge mode; run by NICDC.
- **APIIC**: nodal, about **571** parks in AP. Manufacturing ~16–17% of GDP; target 25% by 2047.
### 6. REGIONAL IMBALANCE
- **IPR 1956**: balanced regional growth; PSUs in backward regions. **Fourth Plan (1969-74)**: attract industry to backward areas.
- Steel plants at **Salem, Visakhapatnam, Vijayanagar** placed away from raw materials.
- South and West lead; East, North-East lag. Economic Survey 2025-26: clusters too small, regulatory rigidities.
