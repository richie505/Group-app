## U0352 | CENTRALIZATION VS DECENTRALIZATION – CONCEPTS, ARGUMENTS AND INDIAN EXPERIENCE
### 1. CONCEPTS
- **Centralisation**: decisions concentrated at the apex (Union, State capital, department HQ). **Decentralisation**: authority transferred downward.
- **Democratic decentralisation** = **community participation** and **local accountability**; not central control.
- Features: **autonomy, people's participation, empowerment**; "instilling non-confidence" is NOT a feature (BPSC 2020).
- Local government attributes: **statutory status, community participation, power to tax**; central control is NOT one.
- Local functions: public health, sanitation, water supply, rural development – **not** public order or judicial review (higher courts).
#### 1.1 Forms – Weakest to Strongest
| Form | What happens | Control |
|---|---|---|
| **Deconcentration** | Work shifted to field offices of the same government | Centre keeps full control – **weakest** |
| **Delegation** | Tasks given to **semi-autonomous bodies** | **Can be withdrawn at will** |
| **Devolution** | Powers to **elected local governments** with own mandate and revenue | Not withdrawable at will – **strongest** (73rd, 74th) |
!TRAP Power taken back at will = delegation, not devolution.
#### 1.2 Dimensions
| Dimension | Transfers |
|---|---|
| **Political** | Power to decide – elected local bodies |
| **Administrative** | Power to implement – staff, functions |
| **Fiscal** | Power to **spend and raise** funds and taxes |
### 2. ARGUMENTS
| For centralisation | For decentralisation |
|---|---|
| Nation-building after Partition, unity, security | Proximity: local bodies know local needs |
| Planned heavy industry, food security | Participation turns subjects into citizens |
| Weak local capacity | Accountability and speed |
| **Elite capture** by dominant castes | Political education of leaders |
### 3. VIEWS ON THE VILLAGE
| Person | View |
|---|---|
| **Gandhi** | **Gram Swaraj**: republic of self-sufficient villages; cottage industry, swadeshi, sarvodaya, trusteeship |
| **Sriman Narayan Agarwal** | **Gandhian Plan (1944)** |
| **Nehru** | **Centralised planned state**, heavy industry (National Planning Committee, Bombay Plan) |
| **Ambedkar** (**4 Nov 1948**) | Village = seat of **caste domination**, "a **sink of localism, a den of ignorance**, narrow-mindedness and communalism"; the **individual** as unit |
### 4. ARTICLE 40 AND THE AMENDMENTS
- **Article 40 (DPSP, Part IV)**: State shall organise **village panchayats** as **units of self-government**; non-justiciable – a **compromise** between Gandhi and Ambedkar.
- **64th Amendment Bill (1989)**, Rajiv Gandhi: passed Lok Sabha, **failed in the Rajya Sabha** (fear of centralisation).
- **73rd Amendment** gave Article 40 practical shape; **Articles 243A, 243B, 243G** came then.
- Article 33 = Parliament's power to modify Fundamental Rights (a distractor).
### 5. INDIAN EXPERIENCE
- **CDP (1952)** run top-down; **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** appointed **January 1957**.
- Nehru launched Panchayati Raj at **Nagaur, Rajasthan, 2 October 1959**; **AP followed 1 November 1959**.
- 1960s: planning more centralised; participation diluted.
- Outcome: **political** power passed, **some administrative**, **fiscal least** – the decentralisation gap.
- **AP**: **12-Ayagar** village system of the **Kakatiyas and Vijayanagara**.
- 13 May 2026: critics say **SWM Rules, 2026** (in force 1 Apr 2026, under EPA 1986) centralise local-body matters.
### 6. SUBSIDIARITY AND THE THREE Fs
- **Subsidiarity**: function at the **lowest competent level**; moves up only if that level cannot do it; invoked by the **2nd ARC**.
- **3 Fs: Funds, Functions, Functionaries** – incomplete devolution keeps PRIs **implementing agencies**, not planning units.
