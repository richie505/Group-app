## U0627 | INDIAN CLIMATE – MONSOON MECHANISM, THEORIES AND SEASONS
### 1. MONSOON – MEANING AND CHARACTERISTICS
- "Monsoon" from Arabic **Mausim** = season.
- **Seasonal (secondary)** winds that **reverse direction** with the season – sea → land in summer, land → sea in winter; NOT primary (planetary) winds.
- Confined to the tropics between **20°N and 20°S**.
- Distribution is controlled mainly by **relief**; rainfall is **not steady**, **unequally distributed** and variable.
- Sudden onset = "**burst**" (first week of June).
- **Breaks**: the monsoon trough shifts to the **Himalayan foothills** → plains dry, hills get heavy rain.
- India is a "**thirsty land**" despite heavy rain: almost all rain comes in June–September and runs off or evaporates, leaving shortage in the other 8 months.
!TRAP APPSC: "Monsoons are primary winds" – FALSE. "Monsoon rain is equally distributed" – NOT a feature.
#### 1.1 Controls of Indian Climate
- Main controls: **latitude, altitude, pressure and wind system, distance from the sea (continentality), ocean currents, relief**.
- Altitude: **Ooty** (~2,240 m) is far cooler in summer than **Coimbatore** (~411 m).
- Continentality: at a similar latitude, **Mumbai** has moderate temperatures while inland **Nagpur** has hot summers and cold winters.
### 2. THEORIES OF THE MONSOON MECHANISM
| Theory / driver | Core idea |
|---|---|
| **Thermal / differential heating** – **Sir Edmund Halley, 1686** | Land heats faster than sea → summer low over land draws moist sea winds; reversed in winter |
| **Dynamic / ITCZ shift** | ITCZ moves north over the Ganga plain as the **monsoon trough**; **SE trades cross the equator** and turn **south-westerly** |
| **Jet streams** | **Subtropical westerly jet (STJ)** shifts **north** of the Himalayas → onset; returns **south** → withdrawal. **Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)** (South China Sea → East Africa) coincides with the SW monsoon |
| **Tibetan Plateau heating** | Plateau (~**5,000 m**) = summer **heat island** → low pressure, **upper-air anticyclone**, stronger TEJ |
| **ENSO / IOD / MJO** | **El Nino weakens**, **La Nina aids**; **positive IOD** can offset El Nino; an unfavourable **MJO** phase stalls the monsoon |
- **Al Masudi** (10th c.) noted the seasonal winds.
- **Dust aerosols** over the Indo-Gangetic Plain heat the mid-troposphere and help pull the monsoon inland.
- Winter jet blows north of the Himalayas, parallel to the Tibetan highlands; the Tibetan highland **splits it** into north and south branches.
!KEY Onset = STJ north of the Himalayas; withdrawal = STJ back south of the Himalayas (not north).
#### 2.1 Mascarene High and Somali Jet
| Term | Role |
|---|---|
| **Mascarene High** | Permanent high in the south Indian Ocean (near **Madagascar**) → drives **cross-equatorial flow** to the subcontinent |
| **Somali jet** | Low-level jet along the East African coast; with the **Somalia Current** it strengthens monsoon winds |
### 3. SEASONS OF INDIA
#### 3.1 IMD Seasons
| IMD season | Months |
|---|---|
| Winter | January–February |
| Pre-monsoon / hot weather | **March–May** |
| **South-west monsoon** | **June–September** (~**75%** of annual rain) |
| Post-monsoon / NE monsoon | **October–December** |
- Hottest in NW/central India: **April–June**; **heat waves peak May–June**; **dust storms** commonly in **May**.
- **Nautapa** – traditional nine-day peak of summer heat in north India, just before the monsoon.
#### 3.2 Six Traditional Seasons
| Season | Months |
|---|---|
| Vasanta (Spring) | March–April |
| Grishma (Summer) | May–June |
| Varsha (Rainy) | July–August |
| Sharad (Autumn) | September–October |
| Hemanta (Pre-winter) | November–December |
| Shishira (Winter) | January–February |
!TRAP "Pravaha" is NOT one of the six seasons.
### 4. HOT-WEATHER LOCAL STORMS AND WINDS
| Phenomenon | Region | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Mango showers** (April rains, summer showers) | **Kerala, coastal Karnataka** (also Tamil Nadu; AP in Apr–May) | Early ripening of mangoes; thunderstorms over the Bay |
| **Blossom (coffee) showers** | Kerala and Karnataka coffee areas | Coffee flowering |
| **Kaal Baisakhi / Nor'westers** | **West Bengal**, Odisha, Assam, NE | Violent evening thunderstorms; useful for **tea, jute, rice** |
| **Bardoli Chheerha** / "tea showers" | Assam | Assam's name for Kaal Baisakhi |
| **Loo** | N and NW plains, **Punjab to Bihar**; Rayalaseema locally | Hot, dry, dusty afternoon wind |
| **Andhi** (dust storms) | Thar → Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, W UP | March–June |
| Karaburan | Central Asia | Hot dusty wind |
- Local convective storms give about **10%** of annual rain.
#### 4.1 Tornadoes and Hailstorms
- **Tornadoes**: mainly **eastern/NE India** (WB, Odisha, Jharkhand, Bihar, Assam), **March–May** with the Nor'westers. Deadliest: **24 March 1998, Belda** (WB–Odisha border), ~250 deaths. Others: New Delhi (1978), Berhampore (1993), Kendrapara (2009).
- **Hailstorms**: mainly **pre-monsoon (March–May)** and winter; rare in the monsoon; **not** mainly Oct–Dec. Most prone: **Maharashtra – Vidarbha and Marathwada**; also MP, Chhattisgarh, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, UP, HP.
### 5. ONSET, ADVANCE AND WITHDRAWAL
| Stage | Fact |
|---|---|
| A&N Islands | ~**25 May** |
| **Kerala** – first state | **1 June** |
| Bhopal / Delhi | ~15 June / ~**25 June** |
| Full coverage | **8 July** (IMD revised normal) |
| Withdrawal | Begins from the **NW in October**, complete by **end-November** |
- IMD declares onset after **10 May** when **60% of 14 stations** get **2.5 mm** for two days and westerlies reach ~600 hPa; uses 5-day pentads.
- Duration: **< 75 days** in W Rajasthan to **> 120 days** in the SW peninsula.
- **2026**: Kerala onset **4 June** (3 days late) under a strengthening El Nino; reached AP on 6 June. The forecast **26 May ± 4 days** failed – first miss since 2015.
### 6. BRANCHES OF THE SW MONSOON
| Branch | Strikes / covers | Missed / weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Arabian Sea** | Western Ghats and Malabar first; heaviest at **Agumbe, Hulikal**; up the west coast, then via Narmada–Tapi to central India | Rain-shadow interior; **Rajasthan** (runs **parallel to the Aravallis**); **Chhattisgarh Basin** little affected |
| **Bay of Bengal** | Myanmar coast and **NE hills** (Meghalaya – Mawsynram); turned **west** by the Himalayas along the Ganga plain | Rain decreases **E → W/NW**; Punjab plains ~600 mm |
- After the Ghats the Arabian Sea branch splits into two; one moves parallel to the Aravallis → scanty rain.
- **Meghalaya**: Bay branch pushed up the **funnel-shaped** Garo, Khasi, Jaintia hills → orographic rain.
- **West coast** gets more rain (narrow plain, high Ghats), but the **east coast** produces more rice (climate, soil).
!KEY APPSC 2024: "Kerala receives the SW monsoon first" and "the Bay branch soaks Meghalaya" – both correct.
### 7. RAINFALL DISTRIBUTION AND EXTREMES
| Extreme | Place |
|---|---|
| **Wettest** | **Mawsynram** (Khasi Hills), ~**1,187 cm** (467.4 inches); then **Cherrapunji (Sohra)**, East Khasi Hills |
| **Lowest rainfall** | **Leh** (56.967 mm, Rainfall Statistics 2024); Jaisalmer 451.888 mm; Bikaner 457.611 mm; Cherrapunji 10,207 mm |
| Highest / lowest state | **Meghalaya / Rajasthan** |
- **Below 60 cm**: Thar, Punjab–Haryana, Kutch, Ladakh–Karakoram, interior peninsula (not Khasi Hills).
- **Rain shadow** (lee of Western Ghats): interior Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, **Rayalaseema**, Tamil Nadu; **Ladakh** – rain shadow of the Himalaya.
- **Konkan coast** – windward, heavy rain.
- **Rajasthan dry**: Arabian Sea branch parallel to the Aravallis (no uplift); Bay branch arrives exhausted; far from the sea.
#### 7.1 AP Angle
- Wettest: **Sileru–Sabari** valleys (140–152 cm); driest: **Hagari valley** (< 51 cm).
- Hottest **Rentachintala** (Palnadu); coolest **Lambasingi**.
- 2026: 17 of 28 districts deficient by 14 June; **51.2%** deficit (1 Jun–25 Aug), all 28 districts deficient; Kurnool promoted dry sowing on 92,000 acres.
### 8. RETREATING MONSOON, NE MONSOON AND WESTERN DISTURBANCES
| System | Origin | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Retreating monsoon** (Oct–Nov) | Sun moves south; NW low vanishes; TEJ disappears | Clear skies, "**October heat**" (hot, humid); Bay cyclones → **bimodal** rain in the south |
| **NE monsoon** (Oct–Dec) | Retreating winds pick up Bay moisture | **Coromandel (Tamil Nadu)**, Rayalaseema, Nellore, Prakasam |
| **Western disturbances** | **Mediterranean** temperate cyclones via Iran–Afghanistan–Pakistan | NW India **10–15 cm** winter rain for **rabi**; snow in J&K, HP, Uttarakhand |
- **Tamil Nadu coast is dry in the SW monsoon** – rain-shadow side and winds parallel to the coast; its winter rain comes from the **NE monsoon**, not western disturbances.
- **Northern plains winter rain** = western disturbances; the NE monsoon is active then but is not the cause.

### 10. MONSOON RESEARCH AND FORECASTING
- IMD is under the **Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES)**.
| Mission / model | Year | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **National Monsoon Mission** | **2012**, MoES via **IITM Pune + IMD** | Phase 1 2012–17; Phase 2 from 2017–25; ~Rs 1,000 crore → audited return Rs 50,000 crore |
| Monsoon Mission Coupled Forecasting System | 2012 | Dynamic ocean–atmosphere–land model |
| **Mission Mausam** | **14 Jan 2025**, with IMD Vision 2047 | **Rs 2,000 crore**; IMD, NCMRWF, IITM; **Bharat Forecast System** (6 km, 10-day rain) |
| Block-level model | May 2026 | AI + nearly a century of data |
- **LPA** of SW monsoon rain (1971–2020) = **868.6 mm** (~87 cm).
- **Ocean Mean Temperature (OMT)** – upper Indian Ocean down to the **26 °C isotherm**, measured Jan–Mar; predicts rain better than **SST**.
- 2026: April forecast **92%** of LPA (first below-normal April forecast in 11 years), revised to **90%** (Jun); June deficit **43%** → contingency plans; WMO ~80% El Nino chance; hottest August; four Bay lows lasted 26 days (normal 16.3).
