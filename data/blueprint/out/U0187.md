## U0187 | CONSEQUENCES OF 1857 – QUEEN'S PROCLAMATION, GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT 1858, ARMY REORGANISATION
### 1. AFTERMATH OF 1857 – KEY DATES
| Year / Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1833 | **Charter Act**: GG of Bengal became **Governor-General of India** (first: **William Bentinck**); Company made purely administrative |
| 10 May 1857 | Meerut revolt |
| 20–21 Sept 1857 | British recaptured **Delhi** |
| 18 June 1858 | **Rani Lakshmibai** died |
| Aug 1858 | **Government of India Act, 1858** passed |
| **1 Nov 1858** | **Queen's Proclamation**, Allahabad Durbar (read by **Canning**) |

- The Revolt gave a **death blow to Company rule**; the reign passed to **Queen Victoria**.
### 2. GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1858
#### 2.1 Core Change
- Officially the **Act for the Good Government of India**; also called the **Act of Administrative Reforms in India**.
- **Abolished Company rule**: government, territories and revenues of India moved from the Company to the **British Crown**; Company territories now governed by the Queen.
- **1858 = watershed**: start of Crown rule (1858–1947).
#### 2.2 Which Act Transferred Power? (option trap)
| Act | Year | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| Pitt's India Act | 1784 | Left the Company in place (dual control) |
| Charter Act | 1833 | **GG of India** (Bentinck); Company purely administrative |
| Charter Act | 1853 | Company still in place |
| **GoI Act** | **1858** | **Company rule ended; Crown rule via SoS and Viceroy** |
| Indian Councils Act | 1861 | Came after the transfer |
| Royal Titles Act | 1876 | Victoria became Empress of India (proclaimed 1877) |
| GoI Act | 1935 | Abolished the Council of India |
!TRAP Power to the Crown = **1858 Act** (APPSC 2018, 2019), not ICA 1861. GG of Bengal → GG of India = **Charter Act 1833** (APPSC G2 2024, 2025).
#### 2.3 Offices Created in 1858
| Office | Role |
|---|---|
| **Secretary of State for India** | British **cabinet minister** and member of Parliament, **responsible to Parliament**; Crown used its powers through him; could issue orders to the Viceroy; first = **Lord Stanley** |
| **Council of India** | **15 members**, in **London**; advised the SoS; abolished by the **1935 Act** |
| **Viceroy** | GG got the additional title **Viceroy and Governor-General** (Crown's representative); first = **Lord Canning** |
- Same office-holder: **Viceroy** for the **princely states**, **Governor-General** for **British India**.
- **Lord Canning (Charles John Canning)**: GG during the Revolt (1856–58), first Viceroy (1858); made **Allahabad** the emergency headquarters in 1857.
### 3. QUEEN'S PROCLAMATION, 1 NOVEMBER 1858
#### 3.1 Basics
- Read by **Lord Canning** at a **Durbar at Allahabad (Prayagraj)** on **1 November 1858**.
- Addressed to "**the Princes, Chiefs and Peoples of India**".
- Called the **"Magna Carta of the Indian people"** (promises on rulers, religion, public service, property, land). Gandhi too cited it as a "Magna Carta" (12 May 2026, on Magna Carta's legacy).
- **Objects (UPSC 2014):** disclaim annexation of Indian States + place administration under the Crown; **not** to regulate Company trade.
#### 3.2 Promises – Kept vs Broken
| Promise | Detail | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| Princes | **No further annexation**; rights, dignity, honour respected; **Company treaties honoured**; **adoption allowed** | **Kept** |
| Religion | **No interference** in religion and social customs; no forced conversions | **Kept** |
| Public service | Appointments by **merit and qualifications**, without discrimination of **race or creed** | **Broken** – ICS stayed European-dominated |
| Laws and property | Ancient rights, usages, customs respected; property and succession protected; peasants' land rights while revenue was paid | Partly |
- SoS **Kimberley**: an adequate number of civil servants "**shall always be Europeans**".
- **1858–1885 = "Era of Broken Promises"**.
!TRAP **Allahabad 1858** = Proclamation (Canning); **Delhi 1877** = Empress of India (Lytton).
### 4. POLICY TOWARDS PRINCES
- Dalhousie's **annexation policy abandoned** – the promise actually kept.
- **Lord Canning withdrew the Doctrine of Lapse**; **adoption** by childless rulers recognised (right restored by the 1858 announcement).
- Princes kept their states subject to **British paramountcy** (Crown as ultimate sovereign); became **subordinate allies** and props of British rule, having stayed loyal in 1857.
- Canning called loyal princes "**breakwaters to the storm**".
### 5. ARMY REORGANISATION
#### 5.1 Peel Commission (1859)
- Before: three Company **presidency armies** – Bengal, Madras, Bombay.
| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| European : Indian ratio | **Bengal 1:5 → 1:2**; **Madras and Bombay 2:5**; European share raised |
| Artillery | **Exclusively European**; Indians barred from heavy weapons; key posts for Europeans |
| Regiments | **"Divide and counterpoise"** – regiments deliberately mixed by caste and region (Sikh, Gurkha, Rajput units) to prevent fraternisation |
| Recruitment | Shift to **Gurkhas, Sikhs, Pathans** ("martial races": Punjab, NWFP) who stayed loyal, rather than **Awadh and Bihar** |
#### 5.2 Eden Commission (1879)
- Recommended abolishing the separate presidency armies → single **Indian Army** (1895) with **four commands** (not three).
!NUM 1:2 = Bengal Army; 2:5 = Madras and Bombay; 4 = commands in 1895
### 6. DIVIDE AND RULE AND RACIAL ATTITUDES
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Divide and rule** | **Muslims** first blamed and targeted; later cultivated against the educated **Hindu middle class**; communities played off |
| Landed class | **Awadh zamindars and taluqdars** restored to proprietary rights → conservative, loyalist class |
| Non-interference | Reforming zeal abandoned; state stepped back from missionary activity |
| Racial attitude | Racial arrogance grew; English-educated "**Babus**" viewed with suspicion |
### 7. INDIAN COUNCILS ACT, 1861 – LESSON OF 1857
- Lesson of 1857: associate Indians with law-making.
- Gave statutory recognition to Canning's **portfolio system** (introduced 1859): each member in charge of a department.
- Viceroy could issue **ordinances** without the council, valid **six months**.
- Additional legislative members: at least **half non-official**.
- **1862**: Canning nominated the first **three Indians** – **Raja of Benares**, **Maharaja of Patiala**, **Sir Dinkar Rao**.
### 8. END OF THE MUGHALS AND THE COMPANY
- **Bahadur Shah II**: proclaimed **Shahenshah-e-Hindustan** by rebels; tried, exiled to **Rangoon**, died **1862** → Mughal rule formally ended. LAST Mughal emperor.
- **East India Company**: chartered by **Elizabeth I**, **31 Dec 1600**; lost rule **1858**; dissolved **1874**.
#### 8.1 AP Angle
- Andhra came under direct Crown rule: **Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema** stayed in the **Madras Presidency**.
- **Telangana** stayed under the **Nizam**, subject to British suzerainty (not run from London).
- **Madras Native Association (1852)**, founded by **Gazula Lakshmi Narasu Chetty** = first to petition against non-implementation of the Allahabad Proclamation.

### 11. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS

2. Proclamation at **Allahabad** (1858), not Delhi; Empress title at **Delhi** (1877).
3. Reader = **Canning**, not Lytton.
4. Objects did **not** include regulating Company trade.
5. Bengal **1:2**; Madras/Bombay **2:5**.
6. Regiments were **mixed** by caste and region, not organised without regard to them.
7. Company **dissolved 1874**, not 1858.
8. Equal employment promise was **broken**; no-annexation was **kept**.
9. 1895 Indian Army had **four** commands.
