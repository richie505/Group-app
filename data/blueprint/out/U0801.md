## U0801 | GENETIC ENGINEERING – rDNA, GM CROPS, GENE EDITING AND ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT OF TRANSGENICS
### 1. BASICS – GMO, TRANSGENIC, APPLICATIONS
- **GMO**: genome altered by genetic engineering; genes moved in ways that **would not occur naturally**.
- **Transgenic organism**: carries a **foreign gene from another species** for a desired characteristic or product.
| Field | Uses |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | GM crops; drought tolerance, higher nutritive value |
| Medicine | Drugs, vaccines, **genetic diagnostics**, gene therapy |
| Industry | **Microbial production** of useful products |
| Research | **Genetic library**; **biochips** (DNA, RNA, protein) for drug discovery |
- Achieved by genetic engineering: disease resistance, growth promotion, animal cloning; **NOT human cloning** (banned).
- Mouse grown **eight times** its size with a human growth hormone gene = genetic engineering.
- **Human Genome Project**: mapping human genes; **paternity** = **DNA fingerprinting**.
### 2. rDNA TECHNOLOGY – STEPS AND TOOLS
1. Isolate DNA
2. Cut at specific sites by **restriction endonucleases**
3. Amplify fragment (**PCR**)
4. Join to vector with **DNA ligase** (rDNA)
5. Insert into host (**E. coli**)
6. Extract product → downstream processing
| Tool | Fact |
|---|---|
| Restriction enzyme | **Molecular scissors**; cuts at specific recognition sites, not at random |
| DNA ligase | **Molecular glue / stitcher**; joins fragments |
| Plasmid | Extranuclear, usually **circular double-stranded** DNA; replicates independently |
| **pBR322** | Early E. coli vector; p = plasmid, **BR = Bolivar and Rodriguez** |
| *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* | **"Nature's genetic engineer"**; disarmed **Ti plasmid** carries **T-DNA** |
| PCR | **Amplifies DNA**; for diagnostics, research |
| RNAi | **Gene silencing** |
- Recombinant plasmid enters bacteria by **transformation**; bacteria multiply to give product.
- **E. coli and Agrobacterium** = two most useful bacteria in genetic engineering.
- **Stem cells**: somatic and embryonic; **not only from embryos** or mammals; used to screen new drugs.

### 4. Bt TECHNOLOGY AND Bt COTTON
- Gene from ***Bacillus thuringiensis***: **Gram-positive soil bacterium**; microbial insecticide sprayed since **1920**; used in organic farming.
- Crystal **delta-endotoxins (Cry)**: protoxin activated in the larva's **alkaline gut** → binds midgut → **pores** → death. Trait = **insect resistance**.
| Version | Year | Genes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bollgard I** | 2002 | **Cry1Ac** (single gene) |
| **Bollgard II** | 2006 | **Cry1Ac + Cry2Ab** |
- Bt cotton approved **March 2002**: three **MAHYCO** hybrids; against **American bollworm**; ~**90%** of cotton area (~10.8 m ha); **first and only** commercial GM crop.
- Yield **190 → 480 kg/ha** (2002-2023); pesticide use cut **40-50%**.
- Problems: **pink bollworm resistance**; rise of sucking pests (**whitefly, mealybug**).
- **Maharashtra** = largest Bt cotton producer (APPSC G2 2017).
- Bt proteins **degrade in soil** (lasting persistence not proven).
!TRAP Bt cotton approval = 2002 (Bollgard I); 2006 is Bollgard II.

### 6. GENOME EDITING – CRISPR
- **CRISPR** = Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats; a natural **bacterial/archaeal defence**.
- **Cas9** = molecular scissors; **guide RNA** = navigator.
- **Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier** (2012) → **Nobel Chemistry 2020**.
| Category | Nature | India (2022) |
|---|---|---|
| **SDN-1** | No template, no foreign DNA | **Exempt** |
| **SDN-2** | Template, no foreign DNA | **Exempt** |
| **SDN-3** | Inserts foreign DNA | Regulated as GM |
| Tool | Fact |
|---|---|
| **TnpB (ICAR)** | Indigenous, smaller alternative to Cas9 |
| **Base editing** | One base → another, **no double-strand break** |
| **CoE-CIT** | CRISPR centre, **Bengaluru** |

### 7. GENETIC MEDICINE
- **Gene therapy**: alter, replace or correct defective genes; CDSCO rules **2023**.
- **Genetic medicines**: carriers = **engineered viruses, lipid nanoparticles**.
- **Personalised medicine**: built on genetic make-up = **CRISPR**; **pharmacogenomics** = genes and drug response.
- **Mitochondrial replacement therapy**: **"three-parent baby"**; concern = germline editing, designer babies.
- **Recombinant vaccines**: Hepatitis B, HPV, COVID-19.
- **Synthetic biology**: insulin, artemisinin; **semaglutide in baker's yeast**.
### 8. GOLDEN RICE, BIOFORTIFICATION, TRANSGENIC ANIMALS
- **Golden Rice**: **beta-carotene** (pro-vitamin A) against **Vitamin A deficiency** (APPSC 2025); **phytoene synthase from daffodil** + **crtI from *Erwinia uredovora***; **Ingo Potrykus and Peter Beyer**.
!PAIR Nutrition: Biofortification | Conventional breeding | No foreign gene || GM crop | Genetic engineering | Foreign gene
- ICAR: **34 biofortified** varieties in one year.
- **Mutation breeding** (non-GMO): **71 Trombay** varieties.
- **GM mosquitoes / gene drives**: >90% offspring inherit (normal 50%); **suppression** (sterile females) vs **modification** (parasite cannot develop); **no simple recall**.
- **GM fish**: AquAdvantage salmon; escapees may out-compete wild stock.
### 9. GLOBAL GM STATUS AND CARTAGENA
- Top GM area: **USA**, Brazil, Argentina, Canada, **India (5th)** – top five >90% of world area.
- **Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety**: adopted **2000** (Montreal), in force **2003**; transboundary movement of **LMOs**; India a party.
### 10. ENVIRONMENTAL AND BIOSAFETY RISKS
- **Gene flow** = **primary environmental concern**: transgenes move by **pollen** into non-GM crops and wild relatives → **superweeds**, altered gene pools, risk to endangered relatives.
- Highest risk at a crop's **centre of origin/diversity**: India = centre of diversity of **brinjal**.
- **Non-target harm**: pollinators, natural enemies, soil nematodes/microbes, aquatic life.
- **Genetic homogenisation**: uniform varieties → less agro-biodiversity, more vulnerability.
- **Genetic modification** = technology flagged for **biodiversity impact** (NOT solar, wind, rainwater harvesting).
| Biosafety risk | Case |
|---|---|
| **Allergenicity** | Soybean with **Brazil-nut** methionine gene – never released |
| **Horizontal gene transfer** | Genes between unrelated organisms |
| **Antibiotic-resistance markers** | Fear of spreading resistance to pathogens |
| **Mon 863** | Monsanto pest-resistant maize (**corn rootworm**); feeding-study debate |
- Claimed benefits: less pesticide; drought/stress tolerance.
#### 10.1 GURT and Safeguards
| Type | Mechanism | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **V-GURT (Terminator)** | **Sterile seeds** | Banned under **PPV&FR Act, 2001** |
| T-GURT (Traitor) | Trait switched by a chemical | Research stage |
- Terminator ends **seed saving** → farmers must **buy seed each season**.
- **PPV&FR Act** protects landraces; **National Gene Fund** for benefit sharing.
