## U0362 | E-GOVERNANCE – MODELS, TYPES OF INTERACTION, NeGP, DIGITAL INDIA AND GOVERNANCE OUTCOMES
### 1. E-GOVERNANCE – MEANING AND SIGNIFICANCE
- E-governance = use of ICT to deliver government services, information and transactions; the aim is **faceless, paperless, cashless** governance.
- Significance (APPSC 2025 key): **citizen engagement** and **better service delivery**.
- Other gains: **fewer face-to-face contacts** (less scope for bribes) and **audit trails**.
!TRAP E-governance does NOT remove audit trails; digital systems **strengthen** them.
#### 1.1 Why It Matters for Governance Outcomes
- **Transparency**: rules, fees, timelines and file status are visible online, so discretion falls.
- **Accountability**: every transaction leaves a digital record that can be audited later.
- **Speed and convenience**: services reach citizens through a single window, a mobile app or a village CSC instead of many office visits.
- **Participation**: platforms such as MyGov let citizens give suggestions on policy.
- **Cost**: fewer visits and less paper lower costs for both government and citizen.
### 2. MODELS OF E-GOVERNANCE – ARIE HALACHMI
#### 2.1 Five Models
| Model (Arie Halachmi) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Broadcasting / wider dissemination** | Government information put in the **public domain** |
| **Critical flow** | Critical information channelled to a **targeted audience** (e.g. corruption data → **ombudsman**) |
| **Comparative analysis** | Learning from **best practices** elsewhere |
| **E-advocacy / mobilisation and lobbying** | **Online civil-society networks** influence decisions |
| **Interactive service** | **Two-way G2C** services (filing, payments, feedback) |
- Theorist: **Arie Halachmi** – exactly **five** models.
!TRAP Corruption data sent to an ombudsman = **critical flow**, not broadcasting.
### 3. TYPES OF INTERACTION – G2C, G2B, G2G, G2E
| Type | Meaning | Indian examples |
|---|---|---|
| **G2C** | Government to Citizen | Online birth, income, caste certificates; **UMANG**, **MeeSeva** |
| **G2B** | Government to Business | **MCA portal**, **GSTN**, **e-Procurement** |
| **G2G** | Between departments/governments | **NIC** solutions, **SWAN**, **e-Office** |
| **G2E** | Government to its Employees | Online HR and administrative tools |
!PAIR GSTN | G2B | business tax || NIC, SWAN, e-Office | G2G | inter-government
- **How to identify the type**: ask who receives the service. A citizen asking for a certificate = G2C; a company filing returns or bidding for tenders = G2B; one department sending files to another = G2G; an employee using an HR tool = G2E.
- In AP, **MeeSeva** and **eSeva** are G2C; **e-Procurement** is G2B; **APSWAN** is the G2G backbone.
### 4. NATIONAL e-GOVERNANCE PLAN (NeGP) AND e-KRANTI
#### 4.1 NeGP, 2006
- Approved by the Government on **18 May 2006** with **27 Mission Mode Projects (MMPs)**.
- In **2011**, **4** MMPs added – **Health, Education, PDS, Posts** – taking the total to **31**.
- Core infrastructure: **SWAN** (State Wide Area Network), **State Data Centres**, **CSCs**.
- MMPs are of three kinds: **Central** (run by Union ministries), **State** (run by state governments) and **Integrated** (cutting across several departments and levels).
#### 4.2 e-Kranti (NeGP 2.0)
- Approved by the Union Cabinet on **25 March 2015**; it is a **pillar of Digital India**.
- Vision: **"Transforming e-Governance for Transforming Governance"**.
- Expanded MMPs from **31 to 44**: **15 Central, 17 State, 12 Integrated**.
| Feature | NeGP | e-Kranti |
|---|---|---|
| Approved | 18 May 2006 | 25 March 2015 |
| MMPs | 27 (31 from 2011) | 44 |
| Status | NeGP 1.0 | NeGP 2.0, part of Digital India |
!NUM 27 (2006) → 31 (2011) → 44 (e-Kranti 2015) MMPs; 15 + 17 + 12 = 44
### 5. DIGITAL INDIA PROGRAMME
#### 5.1 Basics
- Launched **1 July 2015**; implementing ministry: **Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY)**.
- Aims: digital infrastructure as a core utility to every citizen, governance and services on demand, and **digital empowerment** of citizens.
#### 5.2 Nine Pillars (full list)
1. **Broadband Highways**
2. **Universal Access to Mobile Connectivity**
3. **Public Internet Access Programme**
4. **e-Governance**: reforming government through technology
5. **e-Kranti**: electronic delivery of services
6. **Information for All**
7. **Electronics Manufacturing**
8. **IT for Jobs**
9. **Early Harvest Programmes**
!TRAP "Which is NOT a pillar": items such as a satellite launch or a defence programme are not among the nine; e-Kranti IS a pillar.
- **2026**: CM Naidu declared 2026 the **Technology-Driven Decision Making Year** in AP.
### 6. NATIONAL DIGITAL PLATFORMS
| Platform | Expansion / nature | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **UMANG** | **Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance** | Launched **November 2017**; single app for central and state services (tax, EPF, gas, passport) via **web, SMS, IVR** |
| **DigiLocker** | Cloud locker for documents and certificates | Key Digital India initiative; **72.43 crore users** (Aug 2026) |
| **MyGov** | Citizen-engagement platform | **Participative governance** |
| **GI Cloud – MeghRaj** | Government of India cloud | Hosts government applications |
| **ERSS** | **Emergency Response Support System** | Single emergency number **112** |
| **CSC** | **Common Service Centres** | Village-level service outlets; grievances can be filed at **5.1 lakh CSCs** (2026) |
| **Aadhaar** | Biometric identity | Launched **2009**; **Aadhaar Act 2016**, amended **2019** |
| **UPI** | Unified Payments Interface (**NPCI**) | **BHIM** app on UPI launched **30 Dec 2016** |
!TRAP ERSS is linked to **112**; CSCs are unrelated to the ERSS–112 link.
- **Oct 2024**: DigiLocker was **integrated with UMANG**, so stored documents can be used inside UMANG services.
- **UMANG at launch (Nov 2017)**: **162 services** from **33** departments and **4** states.
!KEY Aadhaar: launched 2009 → Act 2016 → amended 2019.
### 7. INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY ACT, 2000 – E-GOVERNANCE PROVISIONS
| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Section 4** | **Legal recognition of electronic records** (equal to paper documents) |
| **Section 5** | **Legal recognition of electronic (digital) signatures** – same as handwritten signatures |
| **Section 6** | **Use of electronic records and signatures in Government** – online filing, electronic licences, digital receipt/payment |
| **Section 7** | **Retention of electronic records** – a legal duty to keep records is met by electronic form |
| **Section 9** | Sections 6–8 give **no right to insist** that a document be accepted in electronic form |
!PAIR Section 4 | Electronic records | Recognition || Section 5 | Digital signatures | Recognition

### 10. DIGITAL DIVIDE AND GOVERNANCE OUTCOMES
| Initiative | Purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **BharatNet** | Optical fibre broadband to **Gram Panchayats** | **2.15 lakh GPs** connected (Mar 2026); Amended BharatNet (Aug 2023) targets **2.64 lakh GPs** in ring topology |
| **PM-WANI** | Public **Wi-Fi hotspots** (Department of Telecom) | Launched **December 2020**; **4.09 lakh** hotspots (Mar 2026) |
| **PMGDISHA** | **Rural digital literacy** | **6.39 crore individuals** trained (31 Mar 2024) |
- Outcomes: transparency, speed, lower corruption; risks: **digital divide**, cyber security, data privacy.
- **Digital divide** = the gap between people who have internet access and digital skills and those who do not (rural poor, elderly, women, remote areas). BharatNet closes the **infrastructure** gap, PM-WANI the **access** gap and PMGDISHA the **skills** gap.
- The **Visakhapatnam Declaration (2025)** also targets this gap by extending NeSDA to low-connectivity regions such as Ladakh and the North-East.
