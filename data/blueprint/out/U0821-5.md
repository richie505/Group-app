### 1. WITHDRAWALS FROM THE INTERNATIONAL CRIMINAL COURT
#### 1.1 Sahel States Leave the ICC
- Military-led **Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger** (coups **2020–2023**) withdrew from the **ICC**, calling it a **"neo-colonial instrument"**.
- Grouping: **Alliance of Sahel States (AES)**; moving closer to **Russia**, away from **France**.
- Under the **Rome Statute**, withdrawal takes effect **one year after notice to the UN Secretary-General**.
#### 1.2 ICC – Background
- Established **2002**; seat **The Hague, Netherlands** – the **first permanent international criminal court**.
- Crimes: **war crimes, crimes against humanity, genocide, aggression**; works on the **principle of complementarity**.
- **Vladimir Putin** under an ICC arrest warrant since **March 2023**.
!TRAP ICC withdrawal is effective **one year** after notice to the **UN Secretary-General**, not immediately.
### 2. RECOGNITION OF PALESTINE
#### 2.1 European Recognitions
- Recognised: **Spain, Ireland, Norway**; **France** signalled readiness.
- Reluctant: **Germany, Austria** (Holocaust legacy).
- **Norway** hosted the **Oslo Accords (1993)**.
- EU's **Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP)** needs **unanimity** – so recognition is by **individual states**.
- **ICJ:** **South Africa's genocide case** against Israel.
- **Eurobarometer 2023:** **72%** of Europeans back upholding international law.
#### 2.2 Wider Recognition at UNGA (September 2025)
- About **10** countries moving to recognise – **Britain, France, Canada, Australia, Belgium**.
- **PLO** declared statehood in **1988**; Palestine is a UN **observer**.
- **147 of 193** UN members recognise Palestine (latest **Mexico, January 2025**).
- Governance: **Palestinian Authority** – parts of the **West Bank**; **Hamas** has run **Gaza since 2007**; about **40** countries have consulates in **Ramallah/East Jerusalem**.
!TRAP Spain, Ireland and Norway had **already recognised** Palestine; Britain, France, Canada, Australia, Belgium were **moving to recognise** at the UNGA, September 2025.
### 3. UNESCO AND CULTURAL HERITAGE
#### 3.1 Gaza – Destruction of Cultural Heritage
- Conflict escalated after **7 October 2023**; deaths **67,160** (Palestinian Health Ministry).
- **UNESCO assessment (August 2025):** **110** cultural sites damaged – **13 religious, 77 historic buildings, 9 monuments, 1 museum, 7 archaeological, 3 depositories**.
- Governing treaty: **1954 Hague Convention** for the Protection of Cultural Property in Armed Conflict (**Israel a signatory**); UNESCO term **"urbicide"**.
| Site | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Great Omari Mosque** | **7th century** – **second-oldest mosque in Palestine** |
| **Al-Qissariya (Gold) Market** | **Mamluk**, 14th century |
| **Church of Saint Porphyrius** | **Greek Orthodox**, **5th century** |
| **Pasha's Palace (Qasr al-Basha)** | **17th century**; **Radwan Dynasty** castle |
| **Hamam al-Sammara** | Gaza's **only surviving Turkish bath** |
| **Rashad Al-Shawa Cultural Center** | Built **1985**; hosted **Arafat–Clinton** talks |
| **Bureij Mosaic** | **Byzantine**; found **2022**; **500 sq m** |
#### 3.2 India's New World Heritage Site (July 2025)
- **Maratha Military Landscapes of India** inscribed on **11 July 2025** at the **47th session** of the World Heritage Committee, **Paris** – India's **44th** World Heritage property.
- A **serial nomination of 12 forts**: **11 in Maharashtra** (including **Raigad, Pratapgad, Sindhudurg, Vijaydurg**) and **1 in Tamil Nadu** (**Gingee Fort**).
### 4. WHO DEVELOPMENTS
#### 4.1 Nepal Eliminates Rubella
- **18 August 2025:** WHO declared **Nepal** has **eliminated rubella** as a public health problem.
- Nepal was **first in the WHO South-East Asia Region** to adopt a **laboratory algorithm** for rubella surveillance.
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 2012 | Rubella vaccine campaign (children **9 months–15 years**) |
| 2016 | **Second dose** in routine immunisation |
| Early 2024 | Over **95%** of children got at least one dose |
- **Rubella = German measles**; can cause **Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS)** – about **1,00,000** infants a year globally.
- **MMR vaccine: two doses** (not three).
- **2022:** **17,865** rubella cases in **78** countries.
- **2024:** **14.3 million** children missed all vaccines; **DTP3 85%**; measles first dose **84%**.
!TRAP MMR is given in **two** doses; rubella is **German measles**, not "measles".
#### 4.2 WHO Pandemic Agreement (May 2025)
- Adopted by the **World Health Assembly** on **20 May 2025** (Geneva); the committee vote the day before was **124 in favour, 0 against, 11 abstentions**.
- Open for signature only after the **PABS (Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing) Annex** is adopted; **60** countries needed for entry into force.

### 6. VISAS, MIGRATION AND THE ILO
#### 6.1 US H-1B Fee
- **September 2025:** fee of **$100,000** for **new H-1B applicants** – about **six times** the earlier fee (Trump administration).
- Quota: **85,000** a year since **2004** (incl. **20,000** for US advanced degrees); **lottery-based**.
- Indians: about **71%** of H-1B recipients; about **60%** earn below **$100,000**.
#### 6.2 China's K Visa
- **K Visa:** for young foreign **STEM** researchers, innovators, entrepreneurs – **no employer sponsorship** needed.
- Announced **weeks before** the US raised the H-1B fee.
| Visa | Target | Sponsorship |
|---|---|---|
| **R Visa** | High-level foreign experts | Employer sponsorship **mandatory** |
| **K Visa** | Young STEM talent | **Not required** |
- Xi Jinping's slogan: **"New Quality Productive Forces"**; **Thousand Talents Programme**.
- China needs **700,000** semiconductor engineers by **2026** (has ~**450,000**); employs **4–6%** of global STEM talent.
#### 6.3 India–ILO MoU on IRCO
- India and the **ILO** to develop the **International Reference Classification of Occupations (IRCO)**.
- Signed in **Geneva** by Ambassador **Arindam Bagchi** and ILO DG **Gilbert F. Houngbo**; **Mansukh Mandaviya** attended virtually; Labour Secretary **Vandana Gurnani**.
- Origin: **G20 (India presidency 2023)** endorsed **skills-based migration pathways**.
- Domestic: **PM Viksit Bharat Rozgar Yojana** – **35 million** formal jobs in **2 years**; **NCS** and **e-Shram** portals.
!TRAP The India–ILO MoU **does not mandate migration**.
#### 6.4 Migration Patterns
- **Skilled migration** since the **1990s** (e.g. US H-1B); **GCC** countries host mainly **temporary labour migrants**.
- **Climate change** expected to drive large forced migration; agencies – **IOM, UNHCR**.
### 7. INDIA'S DIASPORA
- Indian diaspora: **32 million+** (PIOs + NRIs).
- Incidents: **Edmonton (Canada)** Deepavali fires; **anti-immigrant protests in Australia**.
- **Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2003** – **Sections 7A–7D** created **OCI**; **PIO–OCI merger 2015**.
- **India does not permit dual citizenship**.

- Platforms: **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**, **Know India Programme**, **IDF-OI**.
### 8. INDUS WATERS TREATY AND SAWALKOTE PROJECT
- India **suspended the Indus Waters Treaty (1960)** after the **Pahalgam attack**; this gave momentum to **Sawalkote** and the **Wullar Barrage**.
- **Sawalkote:** **1.8 GW**, on the **Chenab** (**J&K**), by **NHPC**.
- Reservoir **>50,000 crore litres** – termed **run-of-river** but closer to a **storage** project.
- Forest diversion **847 ha**; about **1,500 families** displaced; rehabilitation only **0.6%** of cost; cost up **₹9,000 crore**.
- Other Chenab projects: **Dulhasti, Baglihar, Salal**.
### 9. INDIA–US CORN TRADE DISPUTE
- India's corn imports **2024–25: 0.97 mt** – **Myanmar 0.53 mt**, **Ukraine 0.39 mt**, **US only 1,100 t**.
- **Tariff-rate quota:** **0.5 mt at 15%** duty; beyond that **50%**.
- India **bans GM corn imports**; about **94%** of US corn (2024) is GM; NITI Aayog proposal for **GM maize for ethanol withdrawn**.
- US: **largest producer/exporter** – production **377.63 mt**, exports **71.70 mt** (2024–25).
- China's US corn purchases: **$5.21 bn (2022) → $331 mn (2024)**.
- Prices: US ~**₹15/kg**; Indian wholesale **₹22–23/kg**; **MSP 2025–26 ₹24/kg**.
- Top maize states: **Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar**.

### 12. WTO, IMF AND WORLD BANK
#### 12.1 WTO Ministerial Conferences
| Conference | Venue | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| **MC12 (2022)** | **Geneva** | First fisheries subsidies deal – bans subsidies to **illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU)** fishing |
| **MC13 (26 Feb–2 Mar 2024)** | **Abu Dhabi (UAE)** | **E-commerce moratorium** extended two years; no second-wave fisheries deal (India's stand on **special and differential treatment**) |
| **MC14 (26–30 March 2026)** | **Yaoundé (Cameroon)** | Ended in **deadlock**; **e-commerce moratorium lapsed** on **30 March 2026** – first time since **1998** |
- **Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies** entered into force on **15 September 2025**, after acceptances by **two-thirds (111)** WTO members; the WTO's **first agreement with environmental sustainability at its core** (bans subsidies to IUU fishing and overfished stocks).
- After MC14, **66** members (about **70%** of world trade) agreed interim implementation of a **plurilateral E-Commerce Agreement**.
!TRAP MC14 was held in **Yaoundé, Cameroon** (March 2026), not Geneva; the e-commerce moratorium **lapsed** there.
#### 12.2 WTO Subsidy Boxes (Agreement on Agriculture)
| Box | Meaning | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Amber** | **Trade-distorting** support – price support (e.g. MSP), input subsidies | **Capped**; **de minimis 10%** of production value for developing, **5%** for developed countries |
| **Blue** | Amber support with **production-limiting** conditions | Exempt from reduction |
| **Green** | **Minimal trade distortion** – research, infrastructure, decoupled income support | **Unlimited** |
- **Bali (2013):** interim **peace clause** – developing countries not challenged for breaching de minimis limits on **public stockholding for food security**.
#### 12.3 Bretton Woods Institutions
- **IMF:** **191** members – **Liechtenstein** became the **191st** on **21 October 2024** (quota **SDR 100 million**); MD **Kristalina Georgieva**.
- **IBRD:** **189** member countries.
- **IMF World Economic Outlook (October 2026):** India's growth **7.0%** for **2026** (raised **0.6** point from July) and **7.0%** for 2027; world growth **3.1%** (2026).
- **World Bank Logistics Performance Index 2023** (7th edition): India **38th of 139** (44th in 2018; 54th in 2014).
#### 12.4 Multilateral Loans to Andhra Pradesh – Amaravati
| Lender | Loan | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **World Bank** | **$800 million** | Approved **19 December 2024**; **29 years** incl. **6-year grace**; **Amaravati Integrated Urban Development Program** |
| **ADB** | **$788.8 million** | Approved **December 2024**; results-based loan |
- Together about **$1.6 billion** (~**₹13,600 crore**) for **Amaravati capital Phase-I**.
