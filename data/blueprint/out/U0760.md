## U0760 | ENERGY EFFICIENCY – BEE, ENERGY CONSERVATION ACT, PAT, STAR LABELLING, UJALA
### 1. ENERGY CONSERVATION ACT
#### 1.1 Energy Conservation Act, 2001
- Set up the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)**; BEE operational from **1 March 2002**.
- **Designated consumers**: mandatory **energy audits** and norms; **penalties** for non-compliance.
- EE norms for appliances, equipment and buildings with connected load **>100 kW** or contract demand **>120 kVA**.
#### 1.2 Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022
- **Lok Sabha August 2022 → Rajya Sabha December 2022** (APPSC 2021).
| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme** | Legal basis for a carbon market – **Section 14AA** |
| Non-fossil obligation | Designated consumers to use a minimum share of non-fossil energy |
| Building codes | Extended to **large residential buildings** (ECSBC) |
| Vehicles and vessels | Efficiency norms can be set |
!TRAP EC Act = 2001; BEE operational 1 March 2002; amendment passed December 2022.
### 2. ORGANISATIONS
| Body | Status / Role |
|---|---|
| **BEE** | **Statutory** (EC Act 2001), **Ministry of Power**; cuts energy intensity; runs PAT, S&L, ECBC, SEEI, CAFE, NECA |
| **EESL** | JV of Ministry of Power PSUs; runs **UJALA, SLNP**, National Efficient Cooking Programme |
| **PCRA** | Petroleum Conservation Research Association; driving advice; runs **SAKSHAM** (not BEE) |
| NITI Aayog + BEE | **State Energy Efficiency Preparedness Index (2018)** |
| BEE + AEEE | **State Energy Efficiency Index (SEEI)** |
- **NMEEE** = one of the **8 missions of NAPCC (2008)**.
- India imports ~**80%** of its oil, so efficiency raises energy security.
### 3. PAT SCHEME
#### 3.1 Mechanism
1. **Market mechanism under NMEEE**: energy-reduction targets for **Designated Consumers**.
2. Over-achievers earn **ESCerts**.
3. Under-achievers buy them.
4. Traded on **power exchanges (IEX)**.
- **PAT Cycle I (2012-15): 478 units in 8 sectors** – thermal power, iron & steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, **chlor-alkali**, pulp & paper, **textiles**.
- Added later: **railways**, DISCOMs, refineries, petrochemicals, commercial buildings.
- After CCTS, PAT keeps 4 sectors: **thermal power, railways, DISCOMs, commercial buildings** (Jun 2026).
#### 3.2 PAT vs CCTS
| Feature | PAT | CCTS |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate | **ESCerts** (energy intensity) | **CCCs** (emission intensity) |
| Start | 2012 | Notified **2023** – India's **first mandatory compliance carbon market** |
| Coverage | 478 units, 8 sectors (Cycle I) | **9 heavy industries, 208 units** |
| Administrator / Regulator | BEE | **BEE / CERC** |
- CCTS: compliance + voluntary offset; supports **45% cut in GDP emission intensity by 2030** (vs 2005).
- **Sep 2026:** UK recognised CCTS under its **CBAM** (from **1 January 2027**; aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, iron and steel).
### 4. STANDARDS AND LABELLING
- Launched **2006**; BEE under Ministry of Power; **40+ equipment**; **1–5 star**; each extra star saves **~10–15%**.
| Mandatory | Voluntary |
|---|---|
| Refrigerators, deep freezers, ACs, **ceiling fans**, TVs, washing machines, **geysers (storage water heaters)**, LED lamps, **tubular fluorescent lamps**, induction hob, solar inverter, LPG stove, distribution transformers, chillers | Industrial motors, computers, microwave ovens |
- **From 1 January 2026:** labels mandatory for fridges, TVs, LPG stoves; makers **penalised if actual use deviates** from the label.

### 7. LIGHTING
| Lamp | Features |
|---|---|
| **LED** | Highest efficacy, longest life, **no mercury**; **gallium arsenide** main emitter |
| **CFL** (Compact Fluorescent Lamp) | ~75% less energy than bulbs; contains **mercury**; shorter life than LED |
| Incandescent | Energy lost mainly as **heat** |
| **Sodium vapour** | **Monochromatic yellow** (does not split through water droplets); poor colour rendering |
| Mercury vapour | **White** light |
- **Fluorescent tube:** low-pressure **mercury vapour + argon** (sometimes xenon, neon, krypton); mercury gives **UV**, which hits the **phosphor** coating → visible light.
- LED lasts longer than CFL because it is more energy efficient.
- A-R (APPSC 2021): replace bright and CFL bulbs with LEDs because LEDs use less energy – A and R true, R explains A.
### 8. EVERYDAY CONSERVATION
- Efficiency **reduces the need for new energy sources** (lowers demand) – sustainable development link (APPSC 2025).
- Practices: LEDs, star-rated appliances, switch off idle loads, avoid AC overuse; induction stoves, lids, pressure cookers; PCRA driving tips.
