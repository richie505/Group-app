## U0285 | ARTICLES 20, 21, 21A AND 22 – PROTECTION IN CONVICTION, LIFE AND LIBERTY, EDUCATION, PREVENTIVE DETENTION
### 1. THE FOUR ARTICLES AT A GLANCE
| Article | Subject | Available to |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Protection in respect of **conviction for offences** | Citizens, foreigners and **legal persons (companies)** |
| 21 | Protection of **life and personal liberty** | Citizens and non-citizens |
| 21A | **Right to education** (6-14 years) | Children |
| 22 | Protection against **arrest and detention** | Not enemy aliens, not preventive detainees (for 22(1)-(2)) |
### 2. ARTICLE 20 – THREE PROTECTIONS
| Clause | Protection | Scope / NOT covered |
|---|---|---|
| **20(1)** | **No ex post facto criminal law** – no conviction except for a law in force at the time; no heavier penalty | Only criminal laws; not retrospective **civil or tax** laws; bars conviction/greater penalty, **not the trial** |
| **20(2)** | **No double jeopardy** – not prosecuted and punished twice for the same offence | Only prosecution and punishment by a court/judicial tribunal; **not departmental proceedings** |
| **20(3)** | **No self-incrimination** – accused not compelled to be a witness against himself | Criminal cases; oral and documentary testimony; **not physical evidence** like fingerprints/specimens (**Kathi Kalu Oghad, 1961**) |
- **Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010)**: **narco-analysis, polygraph, brain-mapping without consent** violate 20(3).
- Art. 20 does **not** cover **preventive detention**. **Prohibition against torture** is not in Art. 20; protection from torture is under **Article 21**.
- Double jeopardy: CrPC Sec. 300 → **Sec. 337 BNSS**.
!TRAP Art. 20 protects against double jeopardy and self-incrimination, but not departmental proceedings or physical evidence.
### 3. ARTICLE 21 – PROCEDURE ESTABLISHED BY LAW vs DUE PROCESS
| A.K. Gopalan (1950) | Maneka Gandhi (1978) |
|---|---|
| "**Procedure established by law**" = any procedure enacted by legislature (from **Japan**) | Procedure must be **just, fair and reasonable** |
| Arts. 19, 21, 22 **separate** | Arts. **14, 19, 21 linked** – the **golden triangle** |
| Protects only against the **executive** | Also against **arbitrary legislation** – in effect **American due process** |
- Article 21's text uses "procedure established by law"; there is no due-process clause, and Gopalan said courts could not test reasonableness.
- **Due process of law** in essence = **natural justice**. "Procedure established by law" (Japan) is more definite than "due process" (USA).
- **Unguided, uncontrolled executive discretion** breaks **Article 14** (arbitrary, weakens the Rule of Law).
- ★ **Capital punishment** is not barred by Article 21.
!PAIR Procedure established by law | Japan | Gopalan || Due process | USA | Maneka (in effect)

### 5. RIGHT TO PRIVACY
- **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India**, **24 Aug 2017**: **nine-judge** bench, unanimous (**9:0**) – privacy is a Fundamental Right, mainly under **Article 21** (with Part III).
- **Overruled**: **M.P. Sharma** and **Kharak Singh** (to the extent they denied privacy as a Fundamental Right).
- Test for limiting privacy: **legality** (a law), **legitimate aim**, **proportionality**.
### 6. RIGHT TO DIE AND EUTHANASIA
| Case | Year | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Maruti Shripati Dubal (Bombay HC) | 1987 | Right to die part of Art. 21; Sec. 309 IPC unconstitutional |
| **P. Rathinam** | 1994 | **Sec. 309 IPC** (attempted suicide) unconstitutional |
| **Gian Kaur v. Punjab** | 1996 | Overruled Rathinam: **no right to die**, but **right to die with dignity**; Sec. 309 valid |
| **Aruna Shanbaug** | 2011 | Plea rejected; **passive euthanasia** allowed under **High Court supervision** |
| **Common Cause** | 2018 | Dying with dignity (passive euthanasia) an FR under Art. 21; **living will / advance directive** upheld |
- **Mental Healthcare Act, 2017**: attempted suicide effectively not a crime.
- **BNS, 2023** (from **1 July 2024**): **no general offence of attempted suicide**; **Sec. 226 BNS** punishes only an attempt to compel or restrain a public servant.
- 16 Mar 2026: Harish Rana (PVS for 13 years) – parents sought withdrawal of life support in the SC.
!TRAP Rathinam (1994) struck Sec. 309; Gian Kaur (1996) restored it.
### 7. ARTICLE 21A AND THE 86TH AMENDMENT
- **Art. 21A**: State shall provide **free and compulsory education** to all children of **6 to 14 years** "in such manner as the State may determine". Only **elementary** education is an FR.
- Inserted by the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** (passed as the **93rd Amendment Bill**); in force with the RTE Act on **1 April 2010**.
- The **RTE Act, 2009 only gives effect** to Art. 21A – an Act cannot add an Article. Added by the **86th, not 91st**; **property** was removed by the **44th (1978)**, not the 86th.
- Nature: an **FR** (PYQ 2018), an **extension of the right to life** (PYQ 2019); education essential to the right to development.
| 86th Amendment changed | New content |
|---|---|
| Art. 21A (FR) | Free, compulsory education **6-14 years** |
| Art. 45 (DPSP) | **Early childhood care** and education **below 6** |
| Art. 51A(k) (FD) | Parent/guardian to provide education to child **6-14** – the **11th duty** |
- Free and compulsory education was earlier a **Part IV** (DPSP) provision.
#### 7.1 RTE Act 2009 and Earlier Cases
- **Sec. 3**: every child 6-14 has a right to free, compulsory education in a **neighbourhood school** till elementary education is complete.
- **25%** entry-level seats in **unaided private schools** for weaker groups; **not minority schools**; schools reimbursed.
- Amendments: 2012, 2017, 2019 (assessment and detention allowed at certain stages), 2022. Aim: **Education for All**.
| Case | Year | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| **Mohini Jain v. Karnataka** | 1992 | Right to education flows from Art. 21 |
| **Unnikrishnan v. State of AP** | 1993 | **AP angle**: free education **up to 14 years**; beyond that subject to State's economic capacity |
- Sequence: Mohini Jain (1992) → Unnikrishnan (1993) → 86th Amendment (2002) → RTE Act (2009).
### 8. ARTICLE 22 – RIGHTS OF ARRESTED PERSONS
- **22(1)-(2)**: be **told the grounds** of arrest; **consult and be defended by a lawyer of choice**; produced before the **nearest magistrate within 24 hours** (excluding journey time).
- **22(3) exclusions**: **enemy aliens** and persons held under **preventive detention** law.
- Also outside: arrest under court orders, civil arrest, deportation of aliens.
- **Not guaranteed**: **bail as of right**, **trial by jury**.
- CrPC Secs. 50, 57 → **BNSS Secs. 47 and 58**.
- **Vihaan Kumar v. State of Haryana (2025)**: grounds of arrest must be meaningfully communicated to every arrestee.
### 9. PREVENTIVE DETENTION
- **Preventive** = without trial, on suspicion of future acts; **punitive** = after conviction. Justified for **public order**, not routine **law and order** (SC, Jun 2026).
- **22(4)**: no detention beyond **3 months** unless an **Advisory Board** (HC judges or persons qualified to be) reports sufficient cause.
- **22(5)**: grounds communicated + **earliest opportunity of representation**. **22(7)**: **Parliament** fixes the maximum period.
- **44th Amendment (1978)** cut the period to **2 months**, but the clause was **never notified** → **3 months** still applies (16 Jun 2026: demands to notify it).
| Who legislates | Subjects |
|---|---|
| Parliament alone | Defence, foreign affairs, security of India |
| Parliament and States | Public order, security of a State, essential supplies |
#### 9.1 Preventive Detention Laws
| Law | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Preventive Detention Act | 1950 | First such law; lapsed 1969 |
| **UAPA** | 1967 | Anti-terror law; SC (6 Jan 2026): delay alone not a "trump card" for bail |
| **MISA** | 1971 | Used in the Emergency; repealed 1978 |
| **COFEPOSA** | 1974 | **Smugglers** and foreign-exchange manipulators |
| **NSA** | 1980 | Detention up to **12 months** without charges |
| J&K **PSA**, Kerala KAPA | — | State detention laws |
- Roots: **Regulation III of 1818** and the **Rowlatt Act**. NCRB: over **1.2 lakh** detentions a year, about **half quashed** by HCs (Jun 2026).
### 10. ARTICLES 20 AND 21 DURING EMERGENCY
- **44th Amendment (1978)**: enforcement of **Arts. 20 and 21 cannot be suspended** under Art. 359 even in a National Emergency.
- Puttaswamy (2017) reaffirmed 20 and 21 as non-derogable.
