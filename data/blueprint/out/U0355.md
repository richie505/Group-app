## U0355 | 73RD AMENDMENT – PART IX, ELEVENTH SCHEDULE, THREE-TIER STRUCTURE, GRAM SABHA, RESERVATIONS, STATE ELECTION COMMISSION
### 1. THE 73RD AMENDMENT – WHAT IT ADDED AND WHY IT MATTERS
#### 1.1 73rd vs 74th Amendment
| Item | 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 (rural) | 74th Amendment Act, 1992 (urban) |
|---|---|---|
| Bodies | Rural **Panchayats** | Urban local bodies (Municipalities) |
| Part | **Part IX** "The Panchayats" | **Part IXA** "The Municipalities" |
| Articles | **243 to 243O** (16 Articles) | **243P to 243ZG** (18 Articles) |
| Schedule | **Eleventh Schedule – 29 subjects** | **Twelfth Schedule – 18 subjects** |
| In force | **24 April 1993** (National Panchayati Raj Day) | **1 June 1993** |
| Exemption Article | **243M** | **243ZC** |
- Gave **constitutional status** to PRIs and put **Article 40** (DPSP – organise village panchayats) into effect.
- Other DPSP distractors: 39(b) material resources, 44 Uniform Civil Code, 48 agriculture and animal husbandry.
#### 1.2 Significance
- Brought PRIs into the **justiciable part** of the Constitution – key features became legally enforceable.
- States are now **constitutionally obliged** to hold elections; not free to choose.
- Shift from **representative to participatory** (grassroots) democracy; rejuvenated rural local bodies and reduced political interference in them.
- Clearest achievements: **regular elections** and near-**50% women** representation.
!KEY APPSC 2025 Assertion-Reason: "73rd Amendment is a landmark for grassroots democracy" (A) because "it brought PRIs under the justiciable part" (R) – both true, R explains A.
- Keyed answer (APPSC): 73rd gives regular elections (243K) and women's reservation of at least one-third at all levels (243D); the key also added mandatory transfer of funds suggested by the SFC.
- **Ministry of Panchayati Raj** – created **27 May 2004**; handles Part IX, PESA and District Planning Committees.

### 3. COMPULSORY VS VOLUNTARY PROVISIONS
| Compulsory | Voluntary (State's choice) |
|---|---|
| **Gram Sabha** in every village or group of villages | Representation of **MPs and MLAs** in panchayats and their voting rights |
| **Three tiers** (middle optional if population is 20 lakh or less) | **Reservation for backward classes** |
| **Direct election** to all seats at all levels | **Devolving the 29 subjects** of the Eleventh Schedule |
| Indirect election of intermediate and district chairpersons | **Financial powers** – taxes, grants, funds |
| Minimum age **21** | – |
| SC/ST reservation in proportion to population | – |
| **One-third** seats and chairpersons for **women** | – |
| **5-year term**; fresh polls within **six months** | – |
| **SEC** (243K) and **SFC** every 5 years (243I) | – |
- Way of choosing the **village chairperson** – left to **State law**.
- Voluntary devolution of the 29 subjects is the main cause of **uneven devolution** of funds, functions and functionaries.
!TRAP Wrong statements: "BC reservation is compulsory" (SC/ST mandatory, BC discretionary); "18 functional items for Panchayats" (it is 29).
- APPSC G2 2025 mandatory-list question: Gram Sabha, three tiers and SEC – answer "All of the above".
### 4. THREE-TIER STRUCTURE, MEMBERSHIP AND TENURE
#### 4.1 Tiers and elections (Articles 243B, 243C)
- Tiers: **village – intermediate – district**. A Gram Panchayat can cover **more than one village**; the **Gram Panchayat** is the elected executive body.
| Who | How elected |
|---|---|
| Members at all three levels | **Directly** elected from territorial constituencies |
| Intermediate and district chairpersons | **Indirectly**, by and from among elected members |
| Village chairperson | As **State law** provides |
!TRAP Follow the Article as enacted: only members are directly elected; intermediate/district chairpersons are indirect.
#### 4.2 Qualification (Article 243F)
- Minimum age **21 years** (not 25, not 30); other disqualifications as State law provides.
#### 4.3 Term and dissolution (Article 243E)
- Term **5 years from the date of its first meeting**.
- After dissolution: elections within **six months** (not three); none needed if **less than six months** of term remain.
- Panchayat formed after early dissolution serves only the **remainder** – e.g. first meeting 10 February 2014 → term ends **10 February 2019**.
- Gram Sabha meetings are held in each village (by rotation); Gram Panchayat meetings at the GP headquarters.
#### 4.4 Names of tiers across States
| Intermediate tier / body | State |
|---|---|
| **Mandal Parishad (Mandal Praja Parishad)** | **Andhra Pradesh** |
| Panchayat Samiti | Most States, e.g. Rajasthan, Maharashtra |
| **Kshetra Panchayat** (area decided by State Government) | **Uttar Pradesh** |
| **Janpad Panchayat** | **Madhya Pradesh** |
| **Anchalik Panchayat** | **Assam** |
| **Taluk Panchayat** | **Karnataka, Gujarat** |
| Taluka Parishad / Anchalik Parishad (PYQ pairing) | Gujarat / West Bengal |
| Zila Parishads at sub-divisional level | Assam |
| Tribal councils; no Panchayat system | Meghalaya |
| No village panchayats | Mizoram |
- **AP tiers:** Gram Panchayat – **Mandal (Praja) Parishad** – Zilla Parishad.
### 5. GRAM SABHA AND PESA
#### 5.1 Definition and powers
- **Article 243(b):** body of all persons **registered as voters** in the electoral rolls of a village within the Panchayat area.
- **Article 243A:** exercises such powers as the **State Legislature** provides by law.
- Example of **direct democracy**; approves plans and budgets.
#### 5.2 Planning campaigns
- **People's Plan Campaign "Sabki Yojana Sabka Vikas"** – from **2 October 2018**; prepares **Gram Panchayat Development Plans (GPDP)** through Gram Sabhas.
- GPDP – GP-level plan for economic development and social justice.
- **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans** – linked to **PM Gati Shakti**.
#### 5.3 PESA Act, 1996
- From the **Bhuria Committee (1995)**; extends Part IX with exceptions to **Fifth Schedule Areas** (not Sixth) under **Article 243M(4)(b)**, in **ten States including AP**.
- Gram Sabha is supreme, not merely advisory: owns **minor forest produce**, controls **village markets and liquor**, is **consulted before land acquisition**, approves plans and identifies beneficiaries.
- **2026:** 30 years of PESA marked.
### 6. EXEMPTED AREAS – ARTICLE 243M
| Exempted area | Detail |
|---|---|
| States | **Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram** |
| Scheduled and tribal areas | Fifth Schedule areas (until PESA) and Sixth Schedule areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram |
| Manipur | **Hill areas** with District Councils |
| West Bengal | **Darjeeling** hill council area |
- **Arunachal Pradesh:** SC reservation does not apply (no SC population).
### 7. RESERVATION – ARTICLE 243D
#### 7.1 Rules
- SC/ST seats in proportion to population; **at least one-third** seats and chairperson posts for **women** (243D(4)) – a floor, not a ceiling; reserved seats go by **rotation**.
- BC reservation – optional (243D(6)).
#### 7.2 Women's 50% States and data
- **Bihar** – first to give **50%** to women (**2006**). By 2010: Bihar, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh. Many States now give 50%, including **AP**.
- **MoPR, April 2026:** **12.14 lakh of 24.41 lakh** elected PRI representatives are women (**49.75%**).
| Meet 50% exactly | Short of 50% |
|---|---|
| AP, Himachal Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Sikkim | Gujarat 49.9%, Haryana 42.12%, Tripura 45.25%, DNH & DD 31.97% |
- **Jun 2026:** **Sashakt Panchayat Netri Abhiyan** (training women representatives) and **Model Women-Friendly Gram Panchayats** (one per district) – against token representation.
#### 7.3 OBC reservation cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **K. Krishna Murthy v. Union of India** | 2010 | Upheld 243D(6)/243T(6); **political backwardness ≠ social/educational**; total vertical reservation normally within **50%** |
| **Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. State of Maharashtra** | 2021 | **Triple test**; no automatic OBC quota |
- Triple test: (1) dedicated commission with empirical inquiry; (2) local-body-wise proportion on its findings; (3) **SC+ST+OBC within 50%**.
- AP's **34% BC quota** plus SC/ST shares must pass Gawali's 50% ceiling.
### 8. ELEVENTH SCHEDULE – 29 SUBJECTS
- **29 subjects** (Article 243G) – a **menu**: the State "may" devolve them.
| Group | Items |
|---|---|
| Agriculture and land | Agriculture incl. extension; **land improvement, land reforms, regulation of land use**, soil conservation; **minor irrigation**, watershed; animal husbandry, dairying, poultry; **fisheries**; social and farm forestry; minor forest produce |
| Industry and energy | Small-scale industries; khadi, village and cottage industries; **rural electrification**; **non-conventional energy**; **fuel and fodder** |
| Infrastructure | **Rural housing**; **drinking water**; roads, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways |
| Education and culture | Primary and secondary education; technical and vocational training; adult and non-formal education; libraries; cultural activities; markets and fairs |
| Health and welfare | Health and sanitation; family welfare; women and child development; social welfare incl. handicapped; weaker sections, SCs and STs; **poverty alleviation programme**; **public distribution system**; maintenance of community assets |
!TRAP NOT in the Eleventh Schedule: **Rural sports**, **public order**, police; **slum improvement, cattle pounds, fire services, urban planning, burial grounds, slaughterhouses** = Twelfth Schedule.
### 9. STATE ELECTION COMMISSION AND STATE FINANCE COMMISSION
#### 9.1 State Election Commission (Article 243K)
- Superintendence of electoral rolls and conduct of **all Panchayat elections** (municipal elections under 243ZA).
- **State Election Commissioner** – appointed by the **Governor**; removed only like a **High Court judge**; conditions of service cannot be varied to his disadvantage.
- SFC does **not** appoint the State Election Commissioner.
- **AP SEC:** formed **17 June 1994** under the AP Panchayat Raj Act, 1994; Commissioner must hold or have held a post not below **Principal Secretary**. A **2020 ordinance** cutting the term from **five to three years** was struck down by the High Court.
- **2026:** AP local body polls under SEC **Anil Chandra Punetha**; ULB elections for 100 of 123 ULBs (Arts 243K, 243ZA).
#### 9.2 State Finance Commission (Article 243I) vs Union FC
| Basis | Union Finance Commission | State Finance Commission |
|---|---|---|
| Article | **280** (Part XII) | **243I** (Panchayats) and **243Y** (Municipalities) – same Commission |
| Appointed by | **President** | **Governor** |
| Periodicity | Every 5 years or earlier | **Every 5 years**; first within **one year** of 24 April 1993 |
| Divides | Union ↔ States | State ↔ local bodies |
| Report | President, then Parliament (Art. 281) | Governor, then State Legislature with **action-taken memorandum** |
- SFC is a **constitutional** body; recommendations **advisory**; State Legislature decides its composition.
- SFC recommends: sharing **net proceeds of State taxes**, duties, tolls and fees; taxes assigned to Panchayats; **grants-in-aid** from State Consolidated Fund; measures to improve finances; any matter the Governor refers.
- **Article 280(3)(bb)** (Panchayats, 73rd) and **280(3)(c)** (Municipalities, 74th): Union FC suggests measures to augment a **State's Consolidated Fund** on SFC recommendations.
- Central tax share goes to States; Panchayats get **FC grants** routed through the State. Panchayat funds: taxes, grants, **loans from State Government** (valid source).
- Union FC also handles any matter the President refers; it does not settle Article 131 disputes (Supreme Court does).
!TRAP "Union FC has no role in local body assistance" is FALSE (UPPSC 2011).
#### 9.3 AP State Finance Commissions
- **Part VI** of the AP Panchayat Raj Act, 1994 gives effect to Article 243I.
- **4th SFC** report – October 2019. **5th SFC** constituted **20 March 2023** after a PIL over delay; report for **2025–30** submitted 31 December 2024 (chair Prof. S. Rathna Kumari).
- **Aug 2026:** 16th Finance Commission grants require duly constituted local bodies; AP stands to lose **₹12,150 crore** if polls are not completed by October 2026.
