### 2. KEY RPA 1951 SECTIONS
| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| 8 | Conviction with **2+ years** → disqualified + **6 years** after release; **8(4)** struck down |
| 29A | Registration of parties with the ECI |
| 33(7) | Contest from at most **two constituencies** (added 1996) |
| 33A | Disclosure of **criminal antecedents** |
| 34 | Security deposit: **₹25,000** (LS), **₹10,000** (Assembly); SC/ST half |
| 61A | Statutory backing to **EVMs** – inserted **1989** (after the 1982 trial was challenged) |
| 70 | Vacate one of two seats within **14 days** |
| 77 / 78 | Keep expense account / file with the **DEO** (not RO) within **30 days** |
| 81 | Election petition in High Court within **45 days** |
| 100 | Grounds for declaring election void |
| 123 | **Corrupt practices** – bribery, undue influence, appeal on religion/caste |
| 125 / 126A | Promoting enmity at elections / **ban on exit polls** |
| 135B / 135C | Paid holiday / **liquor ban** 48 hours before poll |
| 151A | By-election within **6 months** |
- Parliament bars convicted persons, through **Section 8(3)** – a law of Parliament.
- Deposit lost on failing to get **more than one-sixth** of valid votes; a winner losing deposit means very many candidates stood.
!NUM 14 days = vacate seat (70); 30 days = accounts (78); 45 days = petition (81).
### 3. CONTESTING MORE THAN ONE SEAT AND THE 1996 AMENDMENT
- Cap of **two** came in **1996** after **Devi Lal** contested **three** Lok Sabha seats in **1991**; before 1996 no cap.
- Reforms: **one candidate, one seat** (Law Commission 255th); **vacating candidate pays** by-poll costs (ECI).
- **RP (Amendment) Act, 1996**: two-seat cap; **higher deposits**; more proposers for independents/unrecognised parties; countermanding only on death of a **recognised-party** candidate (party may name a replacement); paid holiday (135B); liquor ban (135C); by-polls in 6 months (151A); disqualification for offences under the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971**.
- **Rajya Sabha** (2003 amendment): any registered elector in India can contest from any State; upheld in **Kuldip Nayar (2006)**.
- Most Lok Sabha/Assembly elections were held in the **1990s** (frequent government changes).
### 4. CRIMINALISATION OF POLITICS
#### 4.1 Cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **ADR v UoI** | 2002 | Disclose criminal cases, **assets, liabilities, education** |
| **PUCL v UoI** | 2003 | Voter's **right to know** under Art. 19(1)(a); struck down **Sec. 33B** |
| Ramesh Dalal | 2005 | Sitting MP/MLA convicted 2+ years barred |
| **Lily Thomas** | July 2013 | Struck down **Sec. 8(4)** (3-month appeal buffer); disqualified **immediately** on conviction |
| Krishnamurthy v Sivakumar | 2015 | Hiding serious cases vitiates nomination |
| **Public Interest Foundation** | 2018 | 5-judge bench; did **NOT** disqualify on framing of charges – **left to Parliament**; parties must publish criminal records |
- Tenth Schedule disqualification: by **Speaker/Chairman**, subject to judicial review (SC, 1992).
#### 4.2 Data and Steps
- **ADR**: serious charges against **31% MPs, 29% MLAs**; MPs **14% (2009) → 31% (2024)**.
- **12 special courts** for MP/MLA cases; PIL for **lifetime ban** pending.
- ECI (Sep 2025): parties must publish candidates' records and **reasons for selection** (withdrawal not needed).
- "**Bahubali culture**" – muscle power weakening trust in legislatures (Sep 2025).
### 5. MONEY POWER AND EXPENDITURE LIMITS
| Category | Lok Sabha | Assembly |
|---|---|---|
| Larger States/UTs | **₹95 lakh** (earlier ₹70 lakh) | **₹40 lakh** (earlier ₹28 lakh) |
| Smaller States/UTs | ₹75 lakh (earlier ₹54 lakh) | ₹28 lakh (earlier ₹20 lakh) |
- Basis: **Rule 90, Conduct of Election Rules 1961**; raised by the **Conduct of Elections (Amendment) Rules, 2022**. Older keys: ₹40 lakh, ₹70 lakh, ₹1 crore.
- Limits apply to **candidates, not parties** (Sec. 77 loophole); travel of 20 star campaigners excluded (Sep 2026). Failure to file accounts → ECI can **disqualify**.
- Party tax exemption: **no cash donation above ₹2,000**; **records above ₹20,000** – now **Sec. 12, Income Tax Act 2025** (earlier Sec. 13A, 1961 Act).
- ADR: **60% (₹19,083 cr)** of party funds in 2004-23 from undisclosed sources.
- RUPPs: over **2,800**, only ~750 contested 2024; ECI delisted **808**.
### 6. ELECTORAL BONDS
- Scheme notified **2 January 2018** (via Finance Act 2017); aim stated: transparency.
- Sold **only by SBI**; **bearer** bonds; **₹1,000 – ₹1 crore**; valid **15 days**.
- Buyer: a **citizen of India** or a body incorporated in India – alone or jointly.
- Cashable only by a registered party with **at least 1% votes** in the last election.
- **ADR v Union of India, 15 Feb 2024**: **5-judge**, **unanimous** – scheme **unconstitutional**; violated voters' **right to information, Art. 19(1)(a)**; struck down removal of the **corporate donation cap** (Companies Act); **SBI** to give donor data to the **ECI**.
!TRAP 2024 = bonds **struck down**, not introduced.
### 7. STATE FUNDING OF ELECTIONS
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Partial | State meets some costs, cash or **in kind** (airtime, fuel, rallies) |
| Full | State bears all fair costs; **private donations banned** |
- Recommended by **Indrajit Gupta (1998)**, **Dinesh Goswami (1990)** (government funding of Parliamentary polls), **2nd ARC** (partial).
- **170th Report**: full funding desirable only if parties are barred from other sources.
- Countries: **Germany, Sweden, Japan, Canada**.
| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Curbs black money, quid-pro-quo | Heavy fiscal burden |
| Level field (**Art. 14**) | Does not stop off-book cash |
- **State of Karnataka v Prathik Parasrampuria (2026)**: removing black money = core ECI duty; ECI still cannot deregister parties for financial non-compliance.
- Idea (Jan 2026): **National Election Fund** – pooled corporate donations shared by vote share.
### 8. EVM AND VVPAT
#### 8.1 Firsts
| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **19 May 1982** | First EVM use – **70-Parur (Kerala)**; A.C. Jose v Sivan Pillai |
| 1989 | **Sec. 61A** – statutory backing |
| 1998 | 16 Assembly seats in MP, Rajasthan, Delhi |
| **1999** | **Goa** – first whole-State Assembly poll on EVMs (APPSC-G2 2025: true) |
| **2004** | 14th Lok Sabha – first all-EVM national election |
| **4 Sep 2013** | **VVPAT** first – **51-Noksen (Nagaland)** by-election |
- Not Gujarat; Goa was NOT the first VVPAT.
#### 8.2 Machines
- Makers: **BEL (Bengaluru)**, **ECIL (Hyderabad)** – ECIL is AP/Telangana's link.
- **M3**: up to **2,000 votes**; one control unit takes **24 ballot units = 384 candidates incl. NOTA**; tamper detection, self-diagnostics. **M2**: 4 BUs, 64 candidates. VVPATs: M1, M2, M3.
- **VVPAT** slip (serial no., name, symbol) seen through a window for **7 seconds** – not handed over. EVMs show **colour photos**.
#### 8.3 Courts and Rules
- **Subramanian Swamy v ECI (2013)**: VVPAT needed for free and fair polls, in phases.
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu (2019)**: VVPAT tally **1 → 5 polling stations** per Assembly segment.
- **ADR v ECI (April 2024)**: no 100% slip count, **no paper ballots**; **symbol-loading units sealed 45 days**; **2nd/3rd-placed** candidates may seek a check of **5% of EVMs** within **7 days**.
- New ECI rules: VVPAT slips counted wherever EVM data ≠ **Form 17C**. Counting order: **postal ballots → penultimate EVM round → final round**.
- ECI admitted EVMs "are still a subject of frequent public questioning" (2 Sep 2026).
#### 8.4 RVM and Totaliser
- **Remote EVM (RVM)**: for **domestic migrants**; up to **72 constituencies** from one remote station; shown to parties **Jan 2023**.
- **Totaliser**: pools several control units to **hide booth-wise trends**; Law Commission 255th; SC sought Centre's response (Sep 2026); ECI: **no legal framework**.
### 9. NOTA AND REFORMS CHRONOLOGY
- **NOTA**: **PUCL v UoI, 27 Sep 2013** – right to reject from Art. 19(1)(a); symbol by **NID Ahmedabad**; first used in **2013 Assembly polls** (Chhattisgarh, Mizoram, Rajasthan, MP, Delhi); first Lok Sabha **2014** (1.1%).
| Year | Reform |
|---|---|
| 1993 | **EPIC** under CEC **T.N. Seshan** – number valid for life; not from the 61st Amendment |
| 2010 | Overseas electors (in force Feb 2011) |
| 2021 | **Election Laws (Amendment) Act**: **Aadhaar-roll linking**, multiple qualifying dates, gender-neutral words, requisition of premises |
| 2023 | **CEC and ECs Act**: panel = **PM, a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by PM, LoP** – CJI dropped |
| 2025 | **ECINET** (40+ apps), 100% webcasting, SIR of rolls |
- Order: **EVM → EPIC → VVPAT → NOTA** (UPPSC 2025). VVPAT came **before** NOTA in 2013.
- Criticism: 2023 Act gives the executive a majority on the panel.
- AP angle: **ASR district** – 100% digitisation of SIR forms (Jul 2026).
- ECI-SEC declaration (26 Feb 2026, CEC **Gyanesh Kumar**): share EVMs, rolls, ECINET with SECs.
### 10. ONE NATION ONE ELECTION
- Polls held together **1951-52 to 1967**; backed by Law Commission **170th (1999)** and **2018 draft report**.
- **High-Level Committee**: set up **2 Sep 2023**, chair **Ram Nath Kovind**; members **Amit Shah, Ghulam Nabi Azad, N.K. Singh, Subhash C. Kashyap, Harish Salve, Sanjay Kothari**.
- Phases: Lok Sabha + Assembly first, **local bodies within 100 days**. Safeguard: German **constructive no-confidence**.
- **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024** (Dec 2024 → **JPC**): inserts **Art. 82A**; amends **83, 172, 327** (not 280). JPC still examining (Feb 2026).
- Debate also names **83, 85, 172, 174, 356**; PYQ key: ONOE needs amending **Art. 356** (President's Rule, not CEC appointment).
- Former CJI **B.R. Gavai** (JPC, 2026): Bill does not violate Basic Structure.
