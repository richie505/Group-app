## U0283 | RIGHT TO EQUALITY – ARTICLES 14-18; REASONABLE CLASSIFICATION, RESERVATION JURISPRUDENCE
### 1. RIGHT TO EQUALITY – ARTICLES 14 TO 18
- The Right to Equality has **five Articles, 14 to 18** (Article 13 is not among them).
- Equality in India is **not absolute**: it allows **reasonable classification**.
#### 1.1 Article-wise Content
| Article | Content | Available to |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | "The State shall not deny to any person **equality before the law** or the **equal protection of the laws** within the territory of India" | **All persons** (citizens and foreigners) |
| 15 | **Prohibition of discrimination** only on grounds of **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth** | **Citizens only** |
| 16 | **Equality of opportunity in public employment** | Citizens only |
| 17 | **"Untouchability" abolished**; its practice in any form forbidden; enforcing any disability from it is an **offence punishable by law** | All |
| 18 | **Titles abolished**, except **military and academic** distinctions | — |
- APPSC 2025: "Right to Equality" is guaranteed by **Article 14**. PYQ 2018: "Art. 15 is available only to citizens" is correct.
- Right to equal protection of the law (Art. 14) is open to all persons; Arts. 15, 16, 19(1)(a) and cultural rights are not.
#### 1.2 Clauses of Articles 15 and 16
- **15(1)** grounds: religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth **only**; **descent is not among them** (it is in 16(2)).
- **15(2)**: access to shops, restaurants, **hotels**, wells, tanks, bathing ghats – binds **private persons** as well as the State.
- **16(1)** and **16(2)**: equal opportunity for all citizens in public employment; 16(2) bars religion, race, caste, sex, **descent**, place of birth, **residence**.
- **16(3)**: **Parliament only** may prescribe a residence requirement for certain jobs.
- **16(5)**: posts in a religious or denominational institution may be kept for persons of that religion/denomination.
!TRAP Descent and residence are barred grounds only in 16(2), not in 15(1).
### 2. SPECIAL PROVISIONS – ARTICLES 15 AND 16
#### 2.1 Clauses → Beneficiaries → Amendment
| Clause | Beneficiaries / subject | Added by |
|---|---|---|
| 15(3) | **Women and children** | Original |
| 15(4) | **SEBCs (OBCs), SCs, STs** – special provisions | **1st Amendment, 1951** (after Champakam Dorairajan) |
| 15(5) | SEBC/SC/ST **admission** in educational institutions, **including private aided or unaided**; **excludes Art. 30 minority institutions** | **93rd Amendment, 2005** |
| 15(6), 16(6) | **EWS, up to 10%** in education and public employment | **103rd Amendment, 2019** (passed as the **124th Amendment Bill** in Lok Sabha) |
| 16(4) | Backward classes not adequately represented in services | Original |
| 16(4A) | **SC/ST reservation in promotion**; later with **consequential seniority** (nullified the "catch-up" rule) | **77th (1995)**; **85th (2001)** |
| 16(4B) | **Backlog (carried-forward) vacancies** treated as a separate group, outside the 50% ceiling | **81st (2000)** |
- **OBC reservation**: Articles **15(4) and 16(4)**. 15(4) and 16(4) cover SCs, STs or OBCs.
- 15(3): women and children; 15(4): SCs, STs, OBCs; 15(5): educational advancement; 16(4): reservation in jobs.
- **105th Amendment (2021)** returned States' power to identify SEBCs (reversed the Maratha-case reading of the **102nd**).
#### 2.2 Who is NOT covered
- Article 15 special provisions cover women, children, SEBCs, SCs, STs, EWS [15(6)]; **aged persons are NOT covered** (APPSC GS 2023).
- Art. 29: cultural and educational rights of any section; Art. 30: minority educational institutions.
#### 2.3 Related Reservation Articles
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| 340 | President appoints a commission on conditions of SEBCs |
| 338B | **NCBC** – constitutional body (102nd Amendment) |
| 342A | President specifies SEBCs for the **Central List**; States keep their own lists (102nd, 105th) |
!KEY APPSC 2022: Amendments giving reservation in jobs/admissions → all of **77th, 81st, 93rd**.
### 3. TYPES OF EQUALITY
| Type | Articles |
|---|---|
| Social | **15, 17, 18** (PYQ 2017: Arts. 17-18 = social equality) |
| Economic | **DPSP 39(b), 39(c), 39(d)** |
| Political | **325, 326** – no exclusion from rolls, adult suffrage |
- **Economic equality is NOT part of the FR to equality.** Included: equality before law (14), social equality (15), equal opportunity (16).
- Preamble promises "**equality of status and of opportunity**".
- **Ambedkar**: liberty, equality, fraternity = an **inseparable trinity**.
- Articles 14-18 lay the "equality floor" for fraternity.
### 4. ARTICLE 14 – TWO FACES AND RULE OF LAW
#### 4.1 Equality before Law vs Equal Protection
| Equality before law | Equal protection of the laws |
|---|---|
| **British** origin (Dicey) | **American** origin (US 14th Amendment) |
| **Negative**: no special privilege; no one above law | **Positive**: like treated alike in equal circumstances |
| All equally subject to ordinary law and ordinary courts | Permits **reasonable classification** |
!PAIR Equality before law | British | Negative || Equal protection | American | Positive
- State can treat unequals differently for a level playing field; among equals the law should be equal.
#### 4.2 Rule of Law – Dicey
- **A.V. Dicey** (British jurist), *Introduction to the Study of the Law of the Constitution* (**1885**); borrowed from Britain like Cabinet System, Parliamentary Government, Single Citizenship, Prerogative Writs.
- **Three elements**: (1) **absence of arbitrary power** (limitation of powers) – regular law supreme, no punishment except for a distinct breach of law; (2) **equality before law**; (3) **primacy of individual rights** (liberty and civil rights) – rights from judicial decisions.
- **Not an element**: "people's responsibility to the Government". "The State has inherent unlimited police powers" – false.
- Dicey opposed the French **Droit Administratif**: officials are not subject only to special administrative tribunals.
- **India accepts (i) and (ii) but reverses (iii)**: rights flow from the Constitution.
- Rule of Law in India rests on **Article 14**; it is a **basic feature** – **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** and **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**.
- ★ Answer key: Indian Constitution is similar to **America** in the Rule of Law.
- **Rule of Law Index**: released by the **World Justice Project** (measuring since 2008; eight areas).
- Authoritarian government is not a feature of Indian polity.
| Rule of Law | Rule by Law |
|---|---|
| Government **subordinate** to law | Ruler **above** law; law as a weapon of state control |
| Laws just, fair, non-arbitrary | Governs citizens only |
### 5. REASONABLE CLASSIFICATION AND ARBITRARINESS
- Art. 14 forbids **class legislation**, not reasonable classification.
- **Twin test**: **intelligible differentia** + **rational nexus** with the law's object.
- **E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)**: "Equality and arbitrariness are **sworn enemies**" / equality is **antithetic to arbitrariness**.
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**: any arbitrary State action breaks Art. 14; **unguided, uncontrolled discretionary power** may be struck down.
- Article 14 puts a **limit on legislative power**.
- 11 Apr 2026: SC held the State cannot treat **pensioners** differently from serving employees on **dearness allowance** without valid classification (Kerala/KSRTC).
- Feb 2026: SC questioned the caste-discrimination clause of the **UGC equity regulations** (**Mritunjay Tiwari v. Union of India**).
### 6. EXCEPTIONS TO EQUALITY
| Provision | Immunity / special status |
|---|---|
| **Art. 361** | President and Governors not answerable to courts; **no criminal case during term**; civil case only after **two months' notice** |
| **Art. 361A** (44th Amendment, 1978) | No liability for a true, non-malicious report of proceedings of Parliament/State legislatures |
| **Arts. 105 / 194** | MPs / MLAs: immunity for anything said or vote given in the House |
| International law | **Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors, diplomats** and **UN agencies** immune from civil and criminal proceedings |
### 7. ARTICLE 17 – UNTOUCHABILITY
- Art. 17 secures **social equality**; "untouchability" is not defined in the Constitution.
- **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955** → renamed **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** by the 1976 amendment (effective **19 Nov 1976**).
- **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** – further law against caste atrocities.
!TRAP The renamed Act keeps the year 1955: "Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955", not 1976.
### 8. ARTICLE 18 – TITLES AND AWARDS
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| 18(1) | State confers no title except **military or academic** |
| 18(2) | A **citizen** shall not accept a foreign title **at all** – no consent route |
| 18(3) | A **non-citizen** holding an office of profit or trust needs the **President's consent** to accept a foreign title |
| 18(4) | No office-holder may accept a present, emolument or office from a foreign State without the **President's consent** (PYQ 2016) |
- Art. 18 helps **social equality** by ending artificial distinctions.
- **Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (1996)**: **Bharat Ratna and Padma awards are not titles**, but must **not be used as prefixes or suffixes**.
- Highest civilian award: **Bharat Ratna**; started with **Padma Vibhushan in 1954**.
### 9. RESERVATION JURISPRUDENCE
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Champakam Dorairajan** | 1951 | Madras **Communal G.O.** struck down under **Art. 29(2)**; DPSPs subordinate to FRs → **1st Amendment, Art. 15(4)** |
| **M.R. Balaji v. State of Mysore** | 1962 | **Caste not the sole test**; reservation reasonable, **generally below 50%** |
| Janki Prasad Parimoo v. J&K | 1973 | Caste alone cannot be sole determinant of backwardness under 15(4) |
| **Indra Sawhney** (Mandal) | 16 Nov 1992 | 9 judges, **6:3**; upheld **27% OBC**; **creamy layer** excluded; **50% ceiling** save extraordinary cases; **no reservation in promotions**; ordered a permanent body (NCBC) |
| Samatha v. State of AP | 1997 | No **tribal land** leases in Schedule V areas to non-tribals/private parties |
| **M. Nagaraj** | 2006 | 5 judges upheld **77th, 81st, 85th** Amendments with conditions: **quantifiable data** on backwardness, inadequate representation, **efficiency (Art. 335)** |
| Ashoka Kumar Thakur | 2008 | Upheld 93rd Amendment; 27% OBC in central institutions; creamy layer excluded |
| Niyamgiri (Orissa Mining Corp.) | 2013 | Gram Sabha's role under Forest Rights Act, 2006; Dongria Kondh rights |
| **Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta** | 2018 | **No proof of backwardness** for SC/ST promotions; **creamy layer** extended to SC/ST promotions |
| Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil (Maratha) | 2021 | Maratha quota struck for **crossing 50%**; read 102nd as Central list only |
| **Janhit Abhiyan** | 2022 | Upheld **103rd Amendment, 10% EWS**, **3:2**; economic criteria alone valid |
| **State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh** | 1 Aug 2024 | **7-judge** bench, **6:1**; overruled **E.V. Chinnaiah (2004)**; **sub-classification of SCs/STs** allowed on **empirical data** (Arts. 15(4), 16(4)); recommended creamy layer identification |
- Order: Champakam → Balaji → Indra Sawhney → Nagaraj.
#### 9.1 Creamy Layer and the 50% Ceiling
- Creamy layer and 50% cap **originate in Indra Sawhney (1992)**; OBC income limit **₹8 lakh a year (2017)**.
- **AP angle (Apr 2026)**: Andhra Pradesh matched its OBC creamy layer with the central income limits.
- Exceptions to 50%: **carried-forward vacancies (81st)**; **EWS 10%** (Janhit Abhiyan). No State legislature has free power to cross the ceiling.
- Aug 2026: Centre opposes an income-based creamy layer for **SCs and STs**; calls Nagaraj's reference a "general observation".
!TRAP Nagaraj needed quantifiable data; Jarnail Singh removed the need to prove backwardness.

### 12. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Art. 14** = all persons; **Art. 15, 16** = citizens only.
2. **Equality before law** = British, negative; **equal protection** = American, positive.
3. **Descent/residence** appear in **16(2)**, not 15(1).
4. **18(2)**: a citizen can never accept a foreign title; consent applies only to 18(3)/(4).
5. Bharat Ratna/Padma are **not titles** (Balaji Raghavan, 1996), but no prefix/suffix.
6. **103rd Amendment** passed as the **124th Bill**.
7. Indra Sawhney **6:3**, Janhit Abhiyan **3:2**.
8. **Economic equality** is not part of the FR to equality (it is in DPSP 39).
9. "People's responsibility to the Government" is **not** a Rule of Law element.
10. **Aged persons** are not covered by Art. 15 special provisions.
