## U0763 | ENVIRONMENT, ECOLOGY AND ECOSYSTEM – COMPONENTS, FOOD CHAINS, ENERGY FLOW AND ECOLOGICAL PYRAMIDS
### 1. ENVIRONMENT, ECOLOGY AND LEVELS OF ORGANISATION
#### 1.1 Environment – Meaning and Categories
- **Environment**: the surroundings and conditions in which organisms live; biotic and abiotic factors interact; it is never static.
- **Composite nature**: physical (nutrients, water, air) + biological (organisms) + chemical interactions (**carbon and nitrogen cycles**).
| Category | Includes |
|---|---|
| Physical | **Lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere** |
| Biotic | Plants, animals, **microbes** (microbes are biotic, not physical) |
| Cultural | Man-made – social, economic, political institutions |
- **Primary functions**: provide resources, **assimilate wastes**, habitat for biodiversity.
!TRAP APPSC 2024: "Generating synthetic chemicals" is NOT a function of the environment.
- A **habitat always has life**; an environment may exist without life.
#### 1.2 Ecology and Levels of Organisation
- **Ecology**: study of relationships between organisms and their environment; term coined by **Ernst Haeckel (1866)**.
- Ecological order: **organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere**.
| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Population | Individuals of **one species** in an area |
| Community | All populations of different species in an area (organisms interacting with one another) |
| Ecosystem | Community + its non-living environment; **basic functional unit of ecology** |
| Biome | Large region defined by **climate + dominant vegetation** (rainforest, tundra, desert) |
| Biosphere | All ecosystems together; Earth inhabited by living organisms; an **open system** |
- **Biodiversity**: flora and fauna of an area; measured as **alpha, beta, gamma**; "biological diversity" coined by **Dasmann**.
- **Biodiversity hotspot** (Myers; Conservation International): at least **1,500 endemic vascular plants** and lost at least **70%** of original habitat.
- **Sere** = entire sequence of communities in succession; **habitat fragmentation** = continuous habitat broken into isolated patches; **anthrome** (anthropogenic biome) = human-altered terrestrial biosphere, e.g. cropland, crop field, park, aquarium, garden.

### 2. HABITAT, NICHE AND POPULATION
#### 2.1 Habitat vs Niche
| Habitat | Niche |
|---|---|
| Physical place where an organism lives – its "**address**" | Functional role of a species – its "**profession**" (food, space, time, conditions used) |
| One habitat holds many species | **Unique to each species** – no two species share one niche |
- Niche is not only physical space but also the functional role in the community; types: habitat niche (where it lives), food niche (what it eats).
- **Fundamental niche**: full range of biotic and abiotic conditions and resources a species could use **free from other species** (APPSC 2021). **Realised niche**: actual range used under competition.
- **Niche breadth**: specialists (**koala** – only eucalyptus) vs generalists.
- **Resource partitioning**: competing species coexist by using different parts of a shared resource (e.g. pests feeding on different parts of one plant).
!KEY APPSC 2022: "niche is unique to a species" and "many species share a habitat" – both correct.
#### 2.2 Population Attributes
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Natality** | Births; rate per unit population per unit time (PYQ 2025) |
| Mortality | Deaths per unit time |
| Density | Individuals per unit area |
| Others | Age structure, sex ratio (NOT dominant vegetation) |
| **J-curve** | Exponential growth, unlimited resources (dN/dt = rN) |
| **S-curve** | Logistic growth levelling at carrying capacity K (dN/dt = rN(K−N)/K) |
- Age pyramids: **triangular = expanding; bell = stable; urn = declining**.
- **Carrying capacity**: largest population an environment can support for ever; resource extraction not above regeneration rate, wastes within assimilating capacity.
| r-selected | K-selected |
|---|---|
| Many small offspring, little care, short life, unstable habitats – **insects, weeds** | Few large offspring, much care, long life, near K – elephants, humans |
### 3. ECOSYSTEM – CONCEPT, STRUCTURE AND COMPONENTS
#### 3.1 Concept
- **Ecosystem**: functional unit of nature – biotic community + its non-living surroundings, linked by **energy flow and nutrient cycling**; term by **Tansley, 1935**.
- It is an **open system**, has its own productivity and **no fixed universal boundary**.
- Ecosystems are open for energy and matter; **the Earth is open for energy but nearly closed for matter**.
| Structure (what is there) | Function (what it does) |
|---|---|
| Species composition, trophic levels, abiotic materials | Energy flow (one-way), nutrient cycling, primary and secondary production, self-regulation |
- **Biogeochemical (nutritional) cycle**: movement of chemical elements (C, N, P) through biotic and abiotic components; **energy does not cycle**.
- Carbon cycle: **photosynthesis removes CO₂**; respiration, decay, fossil-fuel burning and **volcanic action** add it. Water cycle is driven by solar energy; phosphorus cycle begins with **rock weathering**.
#### 3.2 Abiotic Factors
- **Abiotic factor**: any non-living component affecting organisms (APPSC 2018); can be limiting or enabling.
| Type | Factors |
|---|---|
| Climatic | Light, temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind |
| **Edaphic** | **Soil** – texture, pH, minerals, drainage |
| Topographic | Altitude, slope, aspect |
- **Liebig's Law of the Minimum**: growth limited by the nutrient in shortest supply. **Shelford's Law of Tolerance**: both too little and too much limit.
- **Xeric condition**: low humidity, scarce water (arid, extremely cold habitats, salt marshes).
#### 3.3 Biotic Components
- Biotic list: green plants, non-green plants, decomposers, **parasites, symbionts**, animals, man.
| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| Producers (autotrophs) | Green plants; ocean: diatoms, cyanobacteria; vents: chemosynthetic bacteria |
| Primary consumers | Cow, rabbit, zooplankton, **copepod** |
| Secondary consumers | Wolves |
| Tertiary consumers | **Lion** (when eating wolves – not primary) |
| Omnivores | Humans |
| Decomposers (saprotrophs) | Bacteria and fungi |
| Detritivores | **Earthworms, millipedes, woodlice**, nematodes |
- **Producers** are intermediaries between biotic and abiotic components; **decomposers link** biotic and abiotic parts.
- Macroconsumers = phagotrophs; microconsumers = saprotrophs.
- Unusual herbivores: **green turtle, parrot fish, dugong**; green anaconda is a viviparous snake.
- Cold-blooded: frogs, snakes, lizards, fish, crocodile. APPSC 2023 match: humans = omnivores; saprotrophs = dead matter; reptiles = cold-blooded; camel = desert-adapted.
#### 3.4 Producers and Autotroph Types
| Type | Energy source – examples |
|---|---|
| Photoautotrophs | Sunlight – green plants, algae, cyanobacteria |
| Chemoautotrophs | Chemical oxidation – bacteria in deep ocean trenches oxidising **hydrogen sulphide**; nitrifying bacteria; many **archaea** (methanogens) |
- Aquatic producers: **phytoplankton, algae, submerged and floating plants**; hydrothermal vent chains rest on chemosynthetic bacteria, not sunlight.
- Some bacteria (green sulphur bacteria) photosynthesise with invisible (infrared) light; marine algae can use moonlight.
- Most of Earth's oxygen comes from algae: about **90%** of photosynthesis by aquatic plants and algae (**85% in sea**); marine phytoplankton and photosynthetic bacteria give about **50% of world's oxygen**.
- **Chlorella** – highest efficiency in turning solar to chemical energy.
#### 3.5 Decomposition
- Breakdown of organic matter by bacteria and fungi into simple inorganic nutrients reused by producers.
- Steps: **fragmentation → leaching → catabolism → humification → mineralisation**.
| Factor | Rate |
|---|---|
| Detritus rich in nitrogen and sugars; warm, moist | Fast |
| Lignin, chitin, low temperature, anaerobic | Slow |
- Useful microbes: Lactobacillus – curd; Rhizobium, Anabaena, Azotobacter (free-living) – nitrogen fixation.
### 4. FOOD CHAINS, TROPHIC LEVELS, FOOD WEBS AND BIOMAGNIFICATION
#### 4.1 Grazing vs Detritus Chain
| Chain | Starts with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Grazing** | **Living green plants** | Grass → goat → human; grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle |
| **Detritus** | **Dead organic matter** | Leaf litter → earthworm → bird |
| Pond / aquatic | Phytoplankton | → zooplankton → small fish → large fish; grassland: grass → grasshopper/rabbit → snake/hawk |
| Marine | Diatom | → crustaceans → herrings |
!TRAP PYQ 2025: "Grazing chain begins with dead leaves" = FALSE; "autotrophs capture energy and pass it to herbivores" = TRUE.
#### 4.2 Trophic Levels
| Level | Organisms |
|---|---|
| I | Producers – green plants, **phytoplankton, seaweeds**, chemosynthetic bacteria (vents, active volcanoes) |
| II | Herbivores, **zooplankton** |
| III | Carnivores |
| IV | Top carnivores |
| None single | Detritivores and decomposers recycle all levels |
- One species can occupy more than one level; chains rarely exceed **4-5 levels**.
!KEY Missing-level question: "tiger, grass, snake, frog" lacks the **herbivore (second level)**.
#### 4.3 Food Web and Stability
- **Food web**: network of interlinked food chains; **more complex → more stable** (alternative pathways). Fewer trophic levels do not increase biodiversity.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Resistance** | Withstand a disturbance |
| **Resilience** | Recover after disturbance (APPSC 2024) |
| Homeostasis | Self-regulation up to a limit through feedback |
- Diverse systems (rainforest, coral reef) more stable than tundra, desert, monoculture; **marine ecosystem** – most stable overall (unchanged for long periods).
- Warming and ocean acidification weaken calcifying plankton and corals, disrupting marine food webs.
#### 4.4 Bioaccumulation vs Biomagnification
| Bioaccumulation | Biomagnification |
|---|---|
| Build-up inside **one organism** over its life | Concentration rises at **each higher trophic level** |
- Only persistent, fat-soluble, non-biodegradable pollutants (DDT, mercury, PCBs) biomagnify.
- DDT (chlorinated hydrocarbon, organic halogen, harms nervous system): food crop → rat → snake → **hawk** – highest in hawk; causes **eggshell thinning**, seabird crashes.
- **Methylmercury** – Minamata disease (Japan); peaks in dolphins, orcas.
- Pollutants: biodegradable = sewage; non-biodegradable = DDT, plastic, asbestos; primary = CO, SO₂, NOx; secondary = O₃, **PAN**, smog.
### 5. ENERGY FLOW AND PRODUCTIVITY
#### 5.1 Energy Flow
- **Unidirectional**: sun → producers → herbivores → carnivores; not cycled. Primary source and driving force = **Sun**.
- Obeys thermodynamics: first law – energy transformed, not destroyed; second law – each transfer loses heat.
- Under 50% of sunlight is **PAR**; plants fix about **2-10% of PAR**; green plants capture about 1% of sunlight falling on leaves.
- **Total primary production efficiency** = share of incoming solar energy used in photosynthesis – about **1-2%** (APPSC 2024).
#### 5.2 Ten Percent Law
- **Raymond Lindeman (1942)**: about **10%** passes to the next level; about **90%** lost (respiration, excretion, locomotion, heat); hence 4-5 levels.
#### 5.3 Productivity
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| GPP | Total rate of solar energy fixed by producers |
| **NPP** | **GPP − producer respiration**; energy for consumers |
| Primary productivity | Rate solar energy is stored in green plants as food |
| Secondary productivity | Rate consumers form new organic matter |
- Highest NPP per unit area: **tropical rainforest** (APPSC 2025 key); estuaries, coral reefs, swamps, mangroves also high. Lowest: **deserts and open ocean** (ocean total still large by area).
- Decreasing order: **mangroves > grasslands > lakes > oceans**; ecotones (mangroves, estuaries) more productive than surroundings.
!TRAP 1-2% = solar energy captured; 10% = passed between levels.
### 6. ECOLOGICAL PYRAMIDS
- Show trophic levels; base = producers, apex = top consumers; **three types**.
| Pyramid | Upright | Inverted |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | Grassland, pond | **Tree ecosystem; parasitic chain** |
| Biomass | Forest, grassland | **Pond, marine** (small fast-turning phytoplankton crop) |
| **Energy** | **Always** | **Never** |
- **Biomass** = total weight of organisms per unit area or volume.
- Limitations: assume simple chain, ignore food webs; a species cannot be placed at two levels; **saprophytes and decomposers get no place**; size ignored.
!KEY G2 Mains 2025: energy pyramid can never be inverted.
### 7. BIOTIC INTERACTIONS AND SPECIES ROLES
#### 7.1 Interactions
| Interaction | Sign | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mutualism** | +/+ | **Lichen**, fig–fig wasp, mycorrhiza |
| **Commensalism** | +/0 | Orchid epiphyte on mango; **cattle egret** near cattle; barnacles on whales |
| Parasitism | +/− | **Cuscuta**, ticks, lice |
| Predation | +/− | **Tiger–deer**; Pisaster eating mussels |
| Competition | −/− | Same limited resource; Gause's exclusion |
| **Amensalism** | −/0 | Large tree shading small plant; allelopathy; **Penicillium** |
| Neutralism | 0/0 | Neither affected |
#### 7.2 Symbiotic Associations
| Association | Partners | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Mycorrhiza | Fungi + roots | Absorb water, minerals (**phosphorus**); plant feeds fungus |
| Lichen | Fungus + alga/cyanobacteria | Alga gives food; fungus gives shelter, water; pioneers on bare rock |
| Fig–fig wasp | Fig + wasp (Agaonidae) | Obligate coevolved pollination |
| Ant–fungus | Attini (leafcutter) ants | Ants farm fungus as food |
- Coevolved pollinator pairs: fig–fig wasp; **yucca–yucca moth**; **Ophrys orchid–bee** (sexual deceit).

#### 7.3 Species Roles
| Role | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Keystone** | Effect far larger than its numbers | Pisaster sea star (Paine), sea otter, fig, tiger |
| **Foundation** | Creates or shapes habitat | Corals, kelp, dominant trees |

### 8. ECOTONES, ECOGEOGRAPHIC RULES AND PLANT ADAPTATIONS
#### 8.1 Ecotone and Edge Effect
- **Ecotone**: transition zone where two communities meet; conditions intermediate.
| Ecotone | Between |
|---|---|
| Mangroves | Marine and terrestrial |
| Estuary | Fresh and salt water |
| Grassland | Forest and desert |
| Riverbank / marshland | Dry and wet |
- **Edge effect**: more species and higher density of some species in the ecotone; they are **edge species**.
!TRAP A uniform stand of one forest type is a single community, NOT an ecotone.
#### 8.2 Ecogeographic Rules
| Rule | Trait |
|---|---|
| **Bergmann** | Body size – colder = larger |
| **Allen** | Colder = shorter ears and limbs |
| **Jordan** | Fish vertebrae – colder = more |
| **Gloger** | Colouration – warm-humid = darker |
| Rensch | Sexual size dimorphism (shorebirds) |
#### 8.3 Plant Adaptations
| Group | Habitat | Adaptation – example |
|---|---|---|
| **Xerophytes** | Dry | Thick cuticle, sunken stomata, spines, succulence, **CAM**, long roots seeking water – Opuntia, madar, nerium |
| **Hydrophytes** | Water | **Aerenchyma**, reduced roots, thin cuticle, floating leaves – **Hydrilla**, lotus |
| **Halophytes** | Saline | **Pneumatophores, vivipary**, salt glands – **Rhizophora** |
| Epiphytes | On other plants | Support only, not parasitic – orchids, mosses |
| Phreatophytes | Groundwater | Deep roots |
| Lithophytes | Rocks | Epilithic |
| Cryptophytes | Ice surface | – |
- **Cuscuta (Amarbel, dodder)**: parasitic angiosperm; **haustoria** reach host xylem and phloem.
- **Nepenthes** (pitcher plant): insectivorous; pitcher = modified leaf; nitrogen-poor marshy acidic soil.
- Desert leaves: waxy, tiny or none, thorns; camel tolerates long water deprivation, broad foot pads.

### 9. TERRESTRIAL ECOSYSTEMS AND BIOMES
#### 9.1 Classification
- **Natural**: forests, grasslands, oceans, lakes, deserts. **Artificial**: crop fields, parks, villages; **city** = extreme example.
- Terrestrial: tundra, forest, grassland, desert, mountain. **Estuary, marine, wetlands are aquatic**.
#### 9.2 Forests
- Types: tropical rainforest, tropical deciduous, temperate deciduous, **taiga** (coniferous).
- **Rainforest**: highest land species richness; fast decay → thin, poor soil; regrows slowly once removed.
- **Taiga**: slow decay, thick litter, acidic soil.
- **Mature forests** give little net oxygen (respiration balances photosynthesis) but are carbon sinks.
#### 9.3 Deserts
- Hot: **Thar** (western Rajasthan, Kutch), Sahara. Cold: **Ladakh, Gobi**.
- Plants: deep roots, small or spiny leaves, waxy cuticle, succulents (NOT broad thin leaves).
#### 9.4 Grasslands
| Name | Region |
|---|---|
| Prairie | North America |
| Pampas | Argentina |
| Steppe | Eurasia |
| Veld | South Africa |
| Downs | Australia |
| Savanna | Tropical wet-dry, Africa |
| **Shola** | Western Ghats – Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu |
| Terai | Himalayan foothills |
| **Banni** | Kutch, about 2,600 sq km; threat **Prosopis juliflora** |
- India's first guide to **Open Natural Ecosystems** launched at **UNCCD COP17, Ulaanbaatar** (Aug 2026): over 10% of India, under 5% protected.
### 10. AQUATIC ECOSYSTEMS, WETLANDS AND AP ECOSYSTEMS
#### 10.1 Freshwater
| Class | Salinity | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Freshwater | < 5 ppt | **Lotic** (flowing): springs, rivers; **Lentic** (still): ponds, lakes, swamps |
| Brackish | 5-35 ppt | Estuaries, salt marshes, mangroves |
| Marine | ≥ 35 ppt | Open ocean, shallow seas, coral reefs |
- Freshwater covers about **0.8%** of Earth's surface, holds 0.009% of its water.
| Lake zone | Feature |
|---|---|
| Littoral | Shallow; light reaches bottom; rooted plants |
| **Limnetic** | Open sunlit water; **phytoplankton abound** |
| Profundal | Deep, dark; decomposers |
- Trophic status: **oligotrophic** = low nutrients, clear; mesotrophic = intermediate; **eutrophic** = nutrient-rich, algal blooms, low oxygen at depth.
#### 10.2 Marine
- **Ocean = largest ecosystem**, about **71%** of Earth's surface.
| Zone | Feature |
|---|---|
| Photic | Upper ~200 m; photosynthesis |
| Disphotic | ~200-1,000 m twilight; too little light |
| Aphotic | Below ~1,000 m; dark |
| Pelagic | Open water column |
| Benthic | Sea floor |
- **Coral reefs** = "rainforests of the sea"; break storm waves, prevent erosion (A-R: both true, II does not explain I).
- Marine includes coral reefs, **hydrothermal vents**, tidal zones; rivers are NOT marine.
#### 10.3 Estuaries and Mangroves
- Estuary salinity rises from near-fresh upstream to about **35 ppt** at the mouth.
- Mangroves buffer cyclones and surges, cut erosion, store carbon.
- Estuaries – East: Hooghly, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery; West: **Narmada, Tapi**.
