## U0764 | BIOGEOCHEMICAL CYCLES, ECOLOGICAL SUCCESSION AND ECOSYSTEM SERVICES
### 1. FUNCTIONS OF THE ENVIRONMENT
#### 1.1 Four Functions
| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Supplies resources | **Both renewable AND non-renewable** raw materials (first function) |
| Assimilates waste | **Biodegradation** – microorganisms break organic waste into nutrients for reuse |
| Sustains life | Genetic diversity, biodiversity, **ozone layer UV protection** |
| Aesthetic services | Scenery, recreation |
!TRAP APPSC 2022: "Environment provides renewable resources only" is the INCORRECT statement.
!TRAP "Assimilating wealth" is NOT a vital function of the environment.
#### 1.2 Function → Description (APPSC 2023 match)
| Function | Description |
|---|---|
| **Waste decomposition** | Breakdown of organic waste into **simpler compounds** |
| Cultural heritage | Source of traditional practices, knowledge and identity |
| Recreation and aesthetics | Scenic and leisure value |
| Medicinal resources | Supply of drugs and herbal remedies |
- **Ecosystem functions**: ecological succession, nutrient cycling, energy flow through food chains, **homeostasis** (maintenance of ecological balance).
- Nutrients are recycled; **energy flows through and is not recycled**.
### 2. ECOSYSTEM SERVICES AND VALUATION
#### 2.1 Millennium Ecosystem Assessment (MEA, 2005, UN)
| MEA category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Provisioning** | Tangible goods – food, fresh water, timber, fibre, medicinal resources |
| **Regulating** | Climate, flood, disease regulation, water purification, **pollination**, waste decomposition |
| **Cultural** | Recreation, aesthetics, cultural heritage, spiritual value |

#### 2.2 Forest Services
| Economic products | Ecological services |
|---|---|
| Timber, bamboo, fuelwood, fodder, NTFPs | Carbon sequestration, soil and water conservation, **groundwater recharge, flood moderation**, air purification |
- **AP angle**: AP forests give intangible services – carbon sink, water-flow regulation, soil and moisture conservation. AP has the **second-largest mangrove ecosystem** in India (Godavari and Krishna estuaries).

### 3. BIOGEOCHEMICAL CYCLES – BASICS AND TYPES
- Nutrients are recycled indefinitely between organisms, air and the Earth's crust; driven by energy flow; **all cycles are linked to the water cycle**.
- Organisms need at least **27 elements** (15 metals). Macronutrients: potassium, sodium, magnesium, calcium, sulphur, phosphorus, chlorine.
| Type | Cycles | Main reservoir |
|---|---|---|
| **Gaseous** | **Carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, water** | Atmosphere / ocean |
| **Sedimentary** | **Phosphorus, sulphur, calcium, potassium, iron** | Earth's crust – soil and rocks |
!TRAP Hydrogen (H₂) is NOT a gaseous cycle in exam lists.
!KEY APPSC 2025: Removing decomposers blocks mineral movement – nutrients locked in dead matter never return to the soil.
#### 3.1 Phosphorus Cycle
- **No atmospheric phase**; natural reservoir = **rock (phosphates)**; weathering of rocks is the main source; much ends in **ocean sediments**.
- P is used in membranes, nucleic acids, energy transfer, shells, bones, teeth.
### 4. CARBON CYCLE
| Adds CO₂ | Removes CO₂ |
|---|---|
| Respiration, decomposition, fossil-fuel combustion, **volcanic activity**, deforestation | **Photosynthesis** (plants, algae, some bacteria), dissolving in oceans, carbonate sedimentation, silicate weathering |
- Reservoirs and sinks: atmosphere, oceans, forests, soils; long-term – carbonate rocks and fossil fuels. Forests = vital carbon sinks.
!TRAP Photosynthesis does NOT add CO₂ – it removes it.
### 5. NITROGEN CYCLE
#### 5.1 Reservoir and Processes
- Main reservoir = **atmosphere (N₂ about 78% of air)**, NOT soil (APPSC G2 2025).
- Plants take nitrogen as **nitrate**; atmospheric N₂ is not directly usable.
| Process | What happens | Agents |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixation** | N₂ → ammonia/nitrate (enzyme **nitrogenase**) | Rhizobium, Azotobacter, cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc), **lightning**, industrial Haber process |
| **Ammonification** | Organic N in dead matter → ammonia | Decomposer bacteria and fungi |
| **Nitrification** | Ammonia → nitrite → nitrate | **Nitrosomonas** (to nitrite), **Nitrobacter** (to nitrate) |
| **Denitrification** | Nitrate → N₂ gas, returned to air | **Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus** |
- Fixation, ammonification and nitrification **add** usable N to soil; denitrification **removes** it.
!PAIR Nitrification | Nitrosomonas → Nitrobacter | adds nitrate || Denitrification | Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus | returns N₂
#### 5.2 Legumes and Root Nodules
- **Rhizobium** – symbiotic, in **legume root nodules** (pulses, pea); **Rhizobium japonicum** – soybean.
- **Leghaemoglobin**: **pink/red** pigment in nodules (alfalfa, soybean); binds oxygen to keep it low, since **nitrogenase is destroyed by oxygen**.
- **Boron** is necessary for nodule formation.
- Pea family (Fabaceae/Leguminosae): groundnut, horse-gram, soybean; N-fixing plants: alfalfa, chickpea, clover.
#### 5.3 Biofertilisers
| Organism | Nature | Crop / role |
|---|---|---|
| Rhizobium | Symbiotic, legume nodules | Pulses, pea |
| **Azotobacter**, Azospirillum | **Free-living** N fixers | Cereals, millets, non-leguminous upland crops |
| Blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) | Photosynthetic N fixers; **heterocysts** | **Rice / paddy** |
| **Azolla–Anabaena** | Water fern Azolla + Anabaena in its leaves | Water-logged rice fields (Azolla pinnata) |
| **Frankia** | Actinomycete, **non-legumes** | **Alnus, Casuarina** |
| **VAM (mycorrhiza)** | Fungus–root association | **Phosphorus** and water uptake |
| PSB | Bacillus megaterium, Pseudomonas striata | Solubilise phosphate |
!TRAP Azotobacter is NOT partnered with Anabaena; symbiotic fixation in legume nodules is by Rhizobium, not cyanobacteria; VAM does not denitrify.
### 6. WATER CYCLE REGULATION – WETLANDS AND FORESTS
- Water carries elements through the ecosystem; cycles would stop if water did not cycle.
- **Wetlands as sponges**: store floodwater, recharge aquifers, keep dry-season flow; also control erosion, retain sediment, support nutrient cycling and biodiversity.
- **Wetlands as "kidneys" of the Earth**: aquatic plants absorb heavy metals and excess substances.
- **Microclimate regulators**: stored water evaporates and holds heat, steadying local temperature and humidity.
- **Forests**: regulate water flow, conserve soil and moisture, recharge groundwater, moderate floods.
### 7. HUMAN DISRUPTION OF CYCLES
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Fossil fuels + deforestation | Raise atmospheric CO₂ → climate change |
| Chemical pesticides | Alter soils and microbial networks; may **weaken** resilience (not always increase it) |
| **Nitrogen overload** | Human activity has at least doubled nitrogen available to plants |
| Fertiliser runoff | Algal blooms; decay uses up dissolved oxygen → **dead zones** |
| N fertiliser in soil | Microbes release **nitrous oxide**, a greenhouse gas |
- **Prosopis juliflora** (invasive) called an "ecological first responder" – a pioneer spreading where hydrology and soil chemistry have changed (7 May 2026).
### 8. ECOLOGICAL SUCCESSION – TERMS AND TYPES
#### 8.1 Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Succession** | Gradual, orderly replacement of one community by another in an area over time |
| **Pioneer species** | First hardy colonisers – lichens, mosses |
| **Sere / seral stage** | Whole sequence = sere; each step = seral stage (seral communities in between) |
| **Climax community** | Final stable community in balance with climate (forest, grassland, coral reef) |
| Seasonal succession | Cyclic, driven by environmental rhythms; NOT caused by disturbance |
#### 8.2 Primary vs Secondary
| Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|
| Area that never had life – lava, bare rock, volcanic island; no soil | After fire, flood, logging; **soil, seeds, roots remain** |
| Pioneers: lichens and mosses | **Faster** than primary |
- On a surface without soil, **lichens and mosses** survive; **ferns need soil**.
- Grasslands do not turn into forest because of **water limits and dry-season fire**.
#### 8.3 Seres by Starting Medium
| Sere | Starts on |
|---|---|
| **Hydrosere** | Water (pond, lake) |
| **Xerosere** | Dry land |
| **Lithosere** | **Bare rock** (sub-type of xerosere) |
| **Psammosere** | **Sand** (sub-type of xerosere) |
| **Halosere** | **Saline** soil or water |
### 9. SUCCESSION – PHASES, SEQUENCES AND THEORIES
#### 9.1 Clements' Phases (1916)
| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. **Nudation** | Bare area forms (erosion, landslide, volcano, fire) |
| 2. **Invasion** | Seeds/spores arrive; **ecesis** (successful establishment), aggregation |
| 3. Competition and co-action | Unfit species eliminated |
| 4. **Reaction** | Organisms modify soil, water, light, temperature – **most important phase** |
| 5. **Stabilisation** | Climax community |
!KEY Invasion comes right after nudation; ecesis belongs to the invasion phase.
#### 9.2 Sequences
- **Xerosere**: crustose lichens → foliose lichens → mosses → herbs and grasses → shrubs → forest. **Lichens** (algae + fungi) start succession on bare rock.
- Pioneer trees (birch, oak) need high light; shade-tolerant species form the climax.
- **Hydrosere**: phytoplankton → submerged rooted (Chara) → floating (Lemna, Pistia) → reed-swamp → sedge-meadow → woodland → forest; ends in wet woodland (**fen carr**).
- Hydroseres are better studied – **lake sediments keep a permanent record**.
#### 9.3 Ecologists and Climax Theories
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **F.E. Clements** | Phases of succession (1916); **monoclimax** – one climax per region, set by climate |
| **Tansley** | **Polyclimax**; coined "ecosystem" |
| **Whittaker** | **Climax-pattern** |
| E.P. Odum (1971) | Succession = orderly community change within a unit area |
| H.C. Cowles | Succession on sand dunes |
| Elton / Lindeman | Food chains, pyramid of numbers / 10% law |
