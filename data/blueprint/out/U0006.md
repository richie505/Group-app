## U0006 | INDUS VALLEY CIVILIZATION – DISCOVERY, CHRONOLOGY, GEOGRAPHICAL EXTENT, BOUNDARY SITES
### 1. IVC – BASIC FACTS
- **Largest Bronze Age civilisation by area** (larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia); India's **first urbanisation**.
- Extent: about **13 lakh sq km (1.3 million sq km)**; population about **5 million**.
- Most sites lie on the **Ghaggar–Hakra** system: about **250 sites**, of which **6 were cities**.
- Main source of knowledge: **seals** (more than **3,000** found) and excavations; the **script is not yet deciphered**.
- First **silver** in India: Harappan culture (Mohenjodaro, Harappa).
- IVC rivers: **Sindhu, Chenab, Jhelum, Ravi, Sutlej** – **not the Ganga**; spread from the Jhelum to the **Hindon** (Alamgirpur).
### 2. NAMES AND OTHER NAMES
#### 2.1 Names of the Civilisation
| Name | Basis |
|---|---|
| "Indus Valley Civilisation" | Coined by **John Marshall**; now thought too narrow |
| "**Harappan**" | Preferred – **Harappa was the first site found** and the culture went beyond the Indus valley |
| "Sindhu-Sarasvati" | Used by scholars stressing Ghaggar-Hakra sites |

### 3. DISCOVERY AND EXCAVATION
#### 3.1 Early Reports
- **1826**: **Charles Masson** first reported the Harappa mounds; described the ruins 1826–38.
- **1853 and 1856**: **Alexander Cunningham** – first to dig at Harappa; saw pottery, tools, a seal but **missed their value**.
- **1857**: Harappan bricks used as ballast for the **Karachi–Lahore railway**.
- **1912**: **J. Fleet** published Harappan seals.
- **Sir John Marshall**: DG of ASI (**1902–1928**); led the work that found Harappa and Mohenjodaro; appointed **Daya Ram Sahni**.

### 4. CHRONOLOGY AND ORIGIN
#### 4.1 Phases
| Phase | Dates | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Early Harappan | 3300–2600 BCE | Kot Diji, Amri, Sothi-Siswal; villages |
| **Mature Harappan** | **2600–1900 BCE** | Planned cities, **1:2:4 bricks**, script, uniform weights |
| **Late Harappan** | **1900–1300 BCE** | De-urbanisation; OCP and Cemetery-H cultures |
#### 4.2 Dating and Origin Theories
| Scholar / method | View |
|---|---|
| **John Marshall** | **3250–2750 BCE**, by **Mesopotamian cross-dating**; indigenous growth |
| **D. P. Agrawal** (radiocarbon) | About **2500–1750 BCE** |
| **E. J. H. Mackay** | **Migration from Sumer** |
| **Mortimer Wheeler** | **Diffusion of ideas** from West Asia |
| Current consensus | **Regional growth from the Mehrgarh** tradition |
- **Dholavira**: inhabited about **3000–1800 BCE** (APPSC 2018 key), in seven stages.
#### 4.3 Non-Aryan Character
- IVC was **non-Aryan / pre-Vedic**: Harappan **urban** vs Rigvedic **pastoral/rural**; a picture script; **no horse, chariot or iron** in the Mature phase.
| Period | Economy |
|---|---|
| Indus Valley | Urban |
| Rigvedic | Pastoral |
| Later Vedic | Agrarian |
| Medieval | Landlordism |
- Assertion-reason: Harappa and Mohenjodaro are both "dead" cities and found by digging – both true, but the second does not explain the first.

#### 6.3 Uttar Pradesh Sites
- **Alamgirpur** (Meerut, Hindon): eastern limit; gold-coated terracotta bead, cloth evidence.
- **Hulas** (**Saharanpur**, not Meerut): Late Harappan; pottery, chert blades, bone points.
- **Mandi** (**Muzaffarnagar**): Late Harappan **jewellery/gold hoard**, found **June 2000**.
- **Sinauli** (Baraut, **Baghpat**): **125 graves**, mostly primary, north–south.

### 8. SITE-SPECIFIC FINDINGS
| Site | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Harappa** | Two rows of **six granaries**, **Cemetery H**, red sandstone male torso |
| **Mohenjodaro** | **Great Bath**, Great Granary, citadel, assembly hall, bronze **Dancing Girl** (~10.5 cm, lost-wax), **Priest-King**, **Pashupati seal**, **humped bull seal**, skeletons in wells, cotton cloth |
| **Lothal** | **Dockyard** (world's first tidal port), **bead factory**, rice husk, **fire altars**, **ship model**, **copper dog**, **Persian Gulf seal**, **ivory scale**, **three double burials** |
| **Kalibangan** | **Ploughed field**, **seven fire altars**, **earthquake** evidence, camel bones, black bangles, wooden drain, toy-cart wheels |
| **Dholavira** | **Water reservoirs** and dams fed by two seasonal streams; **ten-sign signboard**; **three-part town** (Citadel, Middle, Lower); rock-cut architecture |
| **Banawali** | **Terracotta toy plough**, **Mother Goddess** figurines, barley, oval plan, **radial streets** |
| **Chanhudaro** | **Bead-making and bangle factory**, furnace, **no citadel**, ink-pot, lipstick, cart with seated driver |
| **Rakhigarhi** | Granary, cemetery, drains; **4,500-year-old skeleton**; ancient DNA study |
| **Ropar** | **Dog buried with humans**, steatite seal, oval pit burials |
| **Rangpur** | Late Harappan (**Rojdi** culture); rice, bajra, sorghum |
| **Kot Bala** | **Earliest furnace** |
| **Amri** | Rhinoceros remains |
| **Balu** | **Earliest garlic** |
| Nageshwar / Kerala-no-dhoro | Shell centre / salt centre |
| **Kuntasi** | Double burial |
| Bhagwanpura | Late Harappan overlapping **Painted Grey Ware** |
- Signs of Harappan cultivation: **Lothal and Rangpur** (rice); ploughed field at **Kalibangan**.
!PAIR Ploughed field | Kalibangan | actual field || Plough model | Banawali | terracotta replica
!TRAP Great Bath = Mohenjodaro, not Lothal; seven fire altars = Kalibangan, not Rangpur; dockyard = Lothal; signboard = Dholavira.
### 9. LARGEST SITES, UNESCO AND RECENT DEVELOPMENTS
- **Rakhigarhi** = **largest Indian site**; **Mohenjodaro** largest in Pakistan; **Dholavira** = **second-largest in India**, fifth-largest IVC city.
- **Dholavira**: UNESCO WHS **2021**; India's **40th**, **Gujarat's 4th**; **first IVC site in India** on the list; helped by absence of encroachment.
- **Mohenjodaro**: UNESCO WHS since **1980**.
- **Rakhigarhi**: on UNESCO **Tentative List** (Jun 2026).
- Budget 2020 five iconic sites: **Rakhigarhi, Hastinapur, Shivsagar, Dholavira, Adichanallur**.
- Jun 2026: Haryana protected **Tighrana and Mitathal** (Bhiwani, ~4,400 years old) under the **Haryana Ancient and Historical Monuments Act, 1964** → **seven** protected sites (Rakhigarhi, Banawali, Bhirrana, Kunal, Siswal, Mitathal, Tighrana).
- 23 Jun 2026: ASI transferred Rakhigarhi's **56 skeletons** (Mound 7, incl. a ~4,600-year-old woman) to the **Anthropological Survey of India** under an MoU.
