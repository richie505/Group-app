## U0387 | PROCESSES OF SOCIAL CHANGE – SANSKRITISATION, WESTERNISATION, MODERNISATION, SECULARISATION
### 1. FACTORS AND SOURCES OF SOCIAL CHANGE
| Factor | Example |
|---|---|
| **Technology** | **Industrial Revolution** (**Watt, Hargreaves**); internet and social media – 820 million+ internet users (Dec 2025) |
| **Education** | Spread of **modern education** |
| **Law** | **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955**; widow remarriage and sati laws |
| **Media** | Behaviour-change communication (Economic Survey 2025-26, SBCC); street plays in **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** |
| **Migration / urbanisation** | Industrialisation and urbanisation |
| **Planning** | Green Revolution (3rd Plan); inclusive growth (11th Plan); technology-led modernisation from the 7th Plan |
- **Media** is a modern social institution: spreads information, **creates awareness, forms public opinion**, drives change and **cultural diffusion**; types – print, electronic, social.
- Dalit change came through **state policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation and Westernisation**.
- New development model for developing societies stresses **non-material indicators** (quality of life, equity, wellbeing).
- **ILO (2025)**: India's social protection coverage **64.3%**, up from **19% (2015)**; 25 crore → 100 crore people.
!TRAP **Seasonal migration of nomads** is a routine cycle – **NOT** a source of social change (APPSC 2019).

### 3. DIFFUSION PROCESSES AND CULTURAL TERMS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Acculturation** | Change through **sustained contact** with another culture; influenced but **stays distinct** (APPSC 2019) |
| **Assimilation** | **Absorption** into the dominant culture; minority **loses its identity** |
| **Integration** | Participation while **retaining identity** |
| **Cultural diffusion** | Spread of culture |
| **Cultural relativism** | Understanding a culture **on its own terms** |
| **Ethnocentrism** | Own culture seen as superior |
| **Cultural pluralism / Multiculturalism** | Coexistence of different cultures |
| **Ethnic group** | Group sharing a common culture (clan, lineage, sapinda are kinship groups) |
| **Xenophobia** | Fear of foreigners |
| **Regionalism / Communalism / Casteism** | Loyalty to a region / religious group / caste |
- Ghurye's *The Aborigines So-Called and Their Future* argued for **tribal assimilation**; *The Tribal World of Verrier Elwin* – Elwin, on tribes.
!TRAP Acculturation = influenced but distinct; assimilation = distinct identity lost.
### 4. SANSKRITISATION (M.N. SRINIVAS)
- **Coined by M.N. Srinivas** in *Religion and Society among the Coorgs of South India* (**1952**) (APPSC 2019); developed in *Social Change in Modern India*.
- First called **"Brahminisation"**.
- Meaning: a **low caste or tribe** adopts the customs, rituals and way of life of a higher, often **twice-born**, caste to claim higher rank.
- **Reference groups**: **Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas** or the local **dominant caste**.
- Brings **positional, not structural** change – mobility for a caste, but the **hierarchy stays intact**.
- **De-sanskritisation**: rejecting Sanskritic norms and stressing own identity (e.g. **non-Brahmin / Dravidian movement**).
- Srinivas's set: **Sanskritisation + Westernisation + Secularisation + Dominant Caste**; urbanisation and education change authority patterns but do **not end the joint family**.
### 5. WESTERNISATION
- Srinivas: changes from **150+ years of British rule**.
- **Levels**: **technology, institutions, ideology, values**.
| Westernisation | Modernisation |
|---|---|
| **Source-specific** – West/British | **Universal**, any source |
| **Ethically neutral** (Srinivas's preference) | **Value-loaded** ("progress") |
### 6. MODERNISATION
- **Daniel Lerner**, *The Passing of Traditional Society* (**1958**): sequence **urbanisation → literacy → media exposure → participation**; linked **"empathy"** with **psychic mobility**.
- **Yogendra Singh**, *Modernization of Indian Tradition* (**1973**): studies Sanskritisation, Islamisation, Westernisation and modernisation at **macro and micro** levels.
- **Milton Singer**, *When a Great Tradition Modernizes*: **tradition and modernity coexist** (Madras temple culture).
- **Indicators**: rationality, scientific temper, industrialisation, urbanisation, literacy, mass media, democratic participation.
### 7. CULTURAL LAG (OGBURN)
- **W.F. Ogburn**, *Social Change* (**1922**) – originator.
- **Material culture** (technology) changes faster; **non-material culture** (beliefs, norms) trails = **delay in adjusting** to change.
- **Lagging parts**: **religion, law, tradition** – **not politics** (UPPSC 2014).
- Example: fast digital spread vs slow rules on children's online safety – **DPDP Act, 2023** (parental consent under 18).
### 8. SECULARISATION
- **Sociological secularisation**: decline of religion's hold on social life; **changing beliefs and practices**; differentiation and **rationalisation**.
- **Max Weber**: **"disenchantment of the world"**.
| Sociological secularisation | Constitutional secularism |
|---|---|
| Change in **society** | Duty of the **state** |
| Beliefs and practices change | **State neutrality and equal respect** (Arts. **25-28**) |
