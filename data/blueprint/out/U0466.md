## U0466 | NATIONAL INCOME CONCEPTS – GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP, FACTOR COST VS MARKET PRICE, GVA, DEFLATOR
### 1. GDP – DEFINITION AND ELEMENTS
- **GDP** = market value of **all final goods and services** produced **within the geographical boundaries** of a country **during a year**, **whoever produces them** (GS 2025 key).
- Three elements: **final** goods only (no intermediate goods), **domestic territory**, **time period** (one year).
- **Gross** = before deducting depreciation; **Domestic** = within the country irrespective of nationality.
- GDP = **Q x P**; counts production by both residents and non-residents inside the territory.
- GDP is **place-based**; it does **NOT include NFIA**.
- **National income** = money value of final goods and services produced (**no intermediate goods**).
- **Income method**: GDP = domestic factor incomes − transfer payments.
- **Hindu growth rate** is related to **GDP**.
### 2. GNP AND GDP VS GNP
#### 2.1 GNP
- **GNP (Gross National Product)** = total money value of final goods and services produced by the **normal residents** of a country during a period, **inside and outside** the country's boundaries.
- GNP is **nationality/residence-based**.
#### 2.2 Comparison
| Basis | GDP | GNP |
|---|---|---|
| Based on | **Domestic territory** | **Normal residents / nationals** |
| Measures | Production within the country | Production by residents within and outside the country |
| Nature | **Location-based** | **Residence-based** |
| Indians earning abroad | Excluded | **Included** |
| Link | GDP = GNP − NFIA | **GNP = GDP + NFIA** |
- **Main difference** between GDP and GNP = **net factor income from abroad (NFIA)**.
- **NFIA** = factor income **received from abroad − factor income paid abroad**.
- NFIA form: **GNP = GDP + X − M** (X = income of nationals abroad; M = income of foreigners in India).
#### 2.3 When one exceeds the other
| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| NFIA **positive** (residents earn more abroad than foreigners earn at home) | **GNP > GDP** |
| NFIA **negative** | **GNP < GDP** |
| NFIA zero | GNP = GDP |
- **India**: NFIA usually **negative** -> **GNP lower than GDP**.
- An Indian resident's factor income earned in **Saudi Arabia** counts in India's **GNP only**, not GDP.
!TRAP GDP is NOT equal to GNP – GNP considers nationality (factor income from abroad).
### 3. AGGREGATES AND CONVERSIONS
#### 3.1 Formula table
| Aggregate | Formula |
|---|---|
| **GNP** | GDP + NFIA; also NNP + depreciation |
| **NDP** | GDP **minus** depreciation (not plus) |
| **NNP** | NDP + NFIA; also GNP − depreciation |
| **NNP at MP** | GNP at MP − depreciation |
| **NNP (FC)** | NNP (MP) − net indirect taxes |
| **National Income** | **NNP at factor cost** = NDP at FC + NFIA |
| National Income (long form) | GNP at MP − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies |
| National Income (from NNP) | NNP at MP − (indirect taxes − subsidies) |
| GDP at MP | GNP at MP − net income from abroad |
| Shortcut | **NNP = GDP + NFIA − depreciation** |
#### 3.2 Three bridges
| Bridge | Item | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic -> National | **NFIA** | GNP = GDP + NFIA |
| Gross -> Net | **Depreciation** | NDP = GDP − Dep; NNP = GNP − Dep |
| Market price -> Factor cost | **Net indirect taxes** | FC = MP − (indirect taxes − subsidies) |
- **Depreciation** (consumption of fixed capital) is removed because output that only replaces worn-out capital is **not new output**.
#### 3.3 Path from GDP (MP) to National Income
1. GDP (MP)
2. + NFIA -> GNP (MP)
3. − depreciation -> NNP (MP)
4. − net indirect taxes -> **NNP (FC) = National Income**
!KEY National Income = **NNP at factor cost** – the standard measure.
### 4. FACTOR COST VS MARKET PRICE; GVA AT BASIC PRICES
#### 4.1 Factor cost vs market price
- **MP = FC + indirect taxes − subsidies**; **FC = MP − indirect taxes + subsidies**.
- GDP at MP and GDP at FC differ by **indirect taxes and subsidies – not direct taxes** (APPSC-G2 2025).
!TRAP Subsidies are **added back** when going from MP to FC; "− subsidies" in that step is the wrong sign.
#### 4.2 Production taxes vs product taxes
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Production taxes** | Levied **irrespective of output** | Land revenue, **stamp duty**, **property tax** |
| **Product taxes** | Levied **per unit** of product | **Excise, sales tax/GST** |
#### 4.3 Three price levels
| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| GVA at factor cost + **net production taxes** | **GVA at basic prices** |
| GVA at basic prices + **net product taxes** | **GVA at market prices = GDP** |
- **GDP = GVA + taxes on products − subsidies** (wider measure).
- **GVA vs NVA**: GVA **includes** depreciation; NVA **excludes** it.
### 5. GVA – MEANING AND HEADLINE MEASURE
- **GVA** = value added by producers and sectors – a **production-side** measure; GDP = broader measure after net taxes on products.
- **Real GVA** at constant prices (base year **removes the effect of price changes**); **nominal GVA** at current prices.
- **GVA at basic prices** = headline sectoral measure since the **2011-12 series**; still used in the **2022-23 series**; NSO headline growth is on GVA at basic prices.
- **Sep 2026**: Q1 2026-27 real GDP **Rs 81.36 lakh crore** vs real GVA **Rs 73.82 lakh crore** (difference = net product taxes).
- CEA projected a rise in household disposable income in 2025; GDP growth **7.8% in Q1 FY26**.
### 6. INCLUSIONS AND EXCLUSIONS
| Included | Excluded |
|---|---|
| Wages and salaries, **rent**, **profits**, dividends, **interest on productive loans** | **Transfer payments** – pensions, scholarships, unemployment allowance, prizes (no productive service in return) |
| **Value added at each stage** | **Intermediate goods** (double counting) |
| **Commission on resale** (payment for a productive service; NOT a transfer payment) | **Second-hand sales** (already counted when produced) |
| | **Interest on public debt**, **tax revenue** (transfers, not factor payments) |
| | **Transfer earnings** (not payments for factor services) |
- **Factor income** = wages, rent, interest, profit earned by the factors of production.
- **National income at factor cost** = wages, rent, interest and profits of the country's **normal residents**.
- Transfer payments are **added back** when moving from NI to personal income.
!KEY APPSC-G2 2025 A-R: Transfer earnings are excluded – both A and R true, R explains A.
### 7. PERSONAL, DISPOSABLE AND PER CAPITA INCOME
#### 7.1 Derivation steps
| Measure | Derivation |
|---|---|
| **Personal Income** | NI − undistributed corporate profits − social security contributions − corporate tax + government and business **transfer payments** + net interest paid by government |
| **Personal Disposable Income** | Personal income − **personal direct taxes** − **non-tax payments (fines, fees)** = what individuals can spend or save |
| **National savings** | National disposable income − consumption |
| **Per Capita Income** | **National Income ÷ Population** (APPSC 2025; GS 2025 key) |
- **Per capita income** = average income of a country; it rises **only when NI grows faster than population**.
#### 7.2 Flow vs stock
- **National income = flow** (over a period); **national wealth = stock** (point of time).
#### 7.3 Growth measure
- **Sign of economic growth** = sustained increase in **real per capita income**.
- **Most appropriate measure** of economic growth = **per capita real income** (per capita product / per capita NNP).
- Considered in growth: GDP, GNP, per capita GNP; **NOT considered: growth in financial aid from World Bank**.
### 8. NOMINAL VS REAL GDP
| Basis | Nominal GDP | Real GDP |
|---|---|---|
| Prices | **Current-year** prices | **Constant / base-year** prices |
| Includes price change | Yes | No |
| Better indicator of output growth | No | **Yes** |
- **Real GDP** = value of production at prices of a **single (base) year**.
- **Base year now 2022-23** (earlier 2011-12).
- Example: nominal GDP 1,000 -> 1,200 while price index 100 -> 120 -> **real GDP unchanged**.
- **Prices and money income both doubled** -> **real income unchanged**.
- Nominal growth exceeds real by roughly the **inflation rate**; 2025-26: nominal **8.0%** vs real **7.4%** (estimates, old series).
- Per capita income grows faster at **current** prices than at **constant** prices, because constant prices allow for the rise in price level.
### 9. GDP DEFLATOR AND GNP DEFLATOR
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| **GDP deflator** | **Nominal GDP / Real GDP x 100** = GDP at current prices / GDP at constant prices x 100 (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| **GNP deflator** | Ratio of **nominal GNP to real GNP**, as a percentage |
| Why most comprehensive | Covers **all goods and services in GDP** (consumption, investment, government, exports); WPI, CPI, CPI-IW use **fixed baskets** |
| Rising deflator | Higher prices -> **purchasing power of money falls** |
- GDP deflator = **most comprehensive measure of inflation** (G2 2025 key).
- **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): deflates **output and input prices separately** for manufacturing and agriculture, replacing single deflation.
- Sectoral deflators (2025-26): agriculture grew fastest -> services -> industry -> manufacturing; so manufacturing's nominal share fell while its real share stayed about **17-18%**.
!PAIR GDP deflator | Nominal / Real GDP x 100 | all goods || CPI / WPI | Fixed basket | limited goods
### 10. NEW GDP SERIES, PPP AND INDIA'S RANK
#### 10.1 New series
- **27 Feb 2026**: NSO released the new GDP series (**base 2022-23**) with **double deflation** and **Supply-Use Tables**.
- GDP at current prices: **Rs 261.18 / 289.84 / 318.07 lakh crore** for 2022-23 / 2023-24 / 2024-25.
#### 10.2 Purchasing Power Parity
- **PPP** compares GDP by pricing a **common basket** at each country's own prices -> removes price-level differences.
- Estimated under the **International Comparison Program (ICP)** of the **UNSD**; **World Bank** = global implementing agency; **NSO** = India's nodal agency.
- India's **PPP GDP is much larger** than market-rate GDP because **prices are lower in India**.
#### 10.3 India's rank
| Measure | India's rank |
|---|---|
| Nominal GDP, 2025 | **4th** (overtook Japan; about **USD 4.18 trillion**) |
| Nominal GDP, IMF WEO April 2026 | **6th** (USD 4.15 trillion) |
| PPP GDP | **3rd**, after USA and China (ICP 2021) |
- Older facts: **Japan** was the third largest economy (UPPCS 2007); highest per capita income in World Development Report 2000 = **Luxembourg ($44,640)**; in 2024 **Singapore** had the highest per capita GNI (PPP).
### 11. STATE DOMESTIC PRODUCT AND AP
- **State Domestic Product** (APSES wording, asked word for word in APPSC-GS 2025) = aggregate economic value of all goods and services produced **within the State's boundaries**, **counted without duplication**, during a specified period, usually a year.
- **AP per capita income** is computed on **NSDP**: **Rs 2,68,653** vs India **Rs 2,00,162** (2024-25 FAE).
- Per capita GSDP = gross; per capita income = net -> compare gross with gross, net with net.
- **Goa** = highest per capita income among Indian States; **Bihar** had the highest GSDP growth in 2010-11 among the States given.
### 12. INVESTMENT CONCEPTS – SAVING, MULTIPLIER, ACCELERATOR
| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Autonomous investment** | Independent of income; driven by outside factors – new product, new technique, new market |
| **Induced investment** | Rises with income / demand for consumer goods |
| **Gross vs net investment** | **Net = gross − depreciation**; net addition to capital stock |
| **Planned (ex-ante) vs unplanned** | Unplanned = unintended change in inventories; **realised = planned + unplanned** |
| **Saving-investment link** | Equilibrium income where **planned saving = planned investment** |
| **Multiplier (k)** | **k = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS** |
| **Accelerator** | Induced investment ÷ **change** in income; linked to **J.M. Clark** |
- If planned saving > planned investment, unsold goods pile up, output and income fall until saving = investment.
- Gross investment that only covers depreciation -> **net investment zero**.
!TRAP Multiplier works on **MPC/MPS**; accelerator works on the **change in income**, not its level.
