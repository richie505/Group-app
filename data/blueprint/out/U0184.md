## U0184 | REVOLT OF 1857 – OUTBREAK, SPREAD, CENTRES AND LEADERS
### 1. OUTBREAK – BARRACKPORE TO DELHI
#### 1.1 Event Chronology
| Event | Date | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Barrackpore** (near Calcutta) – first outbreak | **29 March 1857** | **Mangal Pandey** of the **34th Bengal Native Infantry** attacked **Lt Baugh** and **Sgt-Major Hewson** |
| Mangal Pandey hanged | **8 April 1857** | 34th BNI disbanded (dissolved) |
| Meerut sentence | 9 May 1857 | **85 sepoys of the 3rd (Bengal Light) Cavalry** sentenced to 10 years for refusing cartridges |
| **Meerut – real start** | **10 May 1857** | 3rd Bengal Light Cavalry and the 20th regiment revolted; shot officers, freed the 85, marched with **"Dilli Chalo"** |
| **Delhi** | **11 May 1857** | Rebels reached Delhi, entered the **Red Fort**, killed Commissioner **Simon Fraser** |
| Proclamation | 11-12 May 1857 | **Bahadur Shah II** proclaimed **"Shahenshah-e-Hindustan"** |
| Bakht Khan | **3 July 1857** | Reached Delhi from Bareilly |
- British commanding officer at Barrackpore: **Lt Gen Sir John Bennet Hearsey**.
- **Governor-General** during the revolt: **Lord Canning**; as **first Viceroy** he read the **Queen's Proclamation (1 Nov 1858)** at **Allahabad**.
- Symbols of the revolt: **lotus and chapati**.
- The first event of the revolt proper = the sepoys' **march to Delhi's Red Fort**.
!TRAP **Barrackpore** = first outbreak (Mangal Pandey); **Meerut** = real start of the revolt (10 May 1857).
!KEY APPSC-GS 2024: the revolt was widespread but failed; it began at Meerut – all statements true.
#### 1.2 Mangal Pandey
- Born **19 July 1827**; called the **first martyr** of 1857.
- 21 Jul 2026: the PM paid tribute to him on his birth anniversary.

### 6. BAHADUR SHAH ZAFAR – SYMBOLIC LEADER
- Proclaimed **Shahenshah-e-Hindustan** by the rebels at Delhi.
- His proclamations stressed **Hindu-Muslim unity** (ban on cow slaughter, joint commands) and restoration of the Mughal order.
- Pension: **one lakh rupees per month** (Rs 99,000 in Delhi + Rs 1,000 in Lucknow).
- **Fate:** captured after Delhi fell (**Sep 1857**), tried, **exiled to Rangoon (Yangon, Myanmar)**; died there in **1862**.
- Hindu-Muslim unity of 1857 was later cited against the **two-nation theory**.
!TRAP Exile place = **Rangoon**, not Andaman or Mandalay.

### 11. SIGNIFICANCE AND WEAKNESSES
- First great direct threat to British rule; heroes invoked in the Quit India and INA phases.
- **Government of India Act 1858**: power to the **Crown**; **Secretary of State** created; Governor-General made **Viceroy**.
- **Weaknesses:** lack of **coordination, central leadership**, programme and resources; narrow base; **big princes and urban elites stayed away**.
- Largest number of soldiers came from **Oudh** (over 75,000 sepoys).
