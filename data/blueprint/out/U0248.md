## U0248 | INTEGRATION OF PRINCELY STATES – INSTRUMENT OF ACCESSION, JUNAGADH, HYDERABAD (OPERATION POLO), KASHMIR
### 1. LEGAL FRAME AND INSTRUMENT OF ACCESSION
#### 1.1 Indian Independence Act, 1947
- Lapse of British paramountcy: a princely state could **join India, join Pakistan or remain independent**.
- **Hold-outs on 15 August 1947:** all states except **Kashmir, Hyderabad and Junagadh** had signed (Travancore is sometimes listed as a fourth, but it joined before 15 August).
#### 1.2 Instrument of Accession (IoA)
- Ceded only **three subjects** to the Dominion: **Defence, External Affairs, Communications**.
- **Internal administration stayed with the rulers**; in return rulers got **privy purses** and internal autonomy.
!KEY IoA = Defence + External Affairs + Communications. Nothing else was surrendered at first.
#### 1.3 Three Hard Cases, Three Methods
| State | Resolved by |
|---|---|
| **Junagadh** | **Plebiscite** (February 1948) |
| **Hyderabad** | **Police action** (Operation Polo, September 1948) |
| **Kashmir** | **Instrument of Accession, followed by war** |
!TRAP States that had not acceded by 15 August 1947 and where the ruler's religion differed from the majority: **Junagadh, Kashmir, Hyderabad**. **Bhopal** is the odd one out (it delayed but is not in this group).
### 2. INTEGRATION MACHINERY – STATES DEPARTMENT, PATEL AND V.P. MENON
#### 2.1 Who Did What
| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sardar Patel** (Deputy PM, Home Minister) | Head of the **States Department** (Ministry of States); integration led by him – "Iron Man of India" |
| **V.P. Menon** | **Secretary and chief negotiator**, States Department |
- Method: **persuasion**, patriotic appeals, threat of popular uprisings or force; **privy purses + internal autonomy** offered.
#### 2.2 Menon's Account and Four Phases
- Book: **"The Story of the Integration of the Indian States"** – **V.P. Menon**.
1. **Extinction** – **216 small states** merged into provinces.
2. **Consolidation into unions** – **Saurashtra** (Kathiawar states), **Rajasthan Union**, **PEPSU**, **Madhya Bharat**.
3. **Democratisation**.
4. **Abolition of privy purses** (1971).
#### 2.3 Part A, B, C, D States (1950)
| Category | Covered |
|---|---|
| **Part A** | Former **Governor's provinces** |
| **Part B** | Former princely states/unions with a **Rajpramukh** |
| **Part C** | Former **Chief Commissioner's provinces** and some princely states |
| **Part D** | **Andaman and Nicobar Islands** |
### 3. STATES' PEOPLE'S MOVEMENT AND CONGRESS POLICY
#### 3.1 All India States Peoples' Conference (AISPC)
- Founded **1927**, **Bombay** (December 1927); organisers **Balwantrai Mehta**, **Maniklal Kothari**, **G.R. Abhyankar**; first session presided by **Diwan Bahadur Ramachandra Rao**.
- Aim: coordinate people's movements in princely states against autocratic rule.
- Presidents: **Jawaharlal Nehru** (from **1939**, till 1946); **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** later.
- Dissolved on **25 April 1948**; its units merged into the Congress.
- **Praja Mandal movement** – the parallel movement in the princely states (Kashmir, Hyderabad, Travancore); also called the **State Peoples Conference**.
#### 3.2 Praja Mandal Leaders → State
| Leader | State / movement |
|---|---|
| **Vijay Singh Pathik** | **Rajasthan** (Bijolia; Rajasthan Seva Sangh) |
| **Sewa Singh Thikriwala** | **Punjab** (Patiala; Riyasti Praja Mandal) |
| **Sarangadhar Das** | **Orissa** (Dhenkanal Praja Mandal) |
| **Swami Ramananda Tirtha** | **Hyderabad State Congress** (1938) |
#### 3.3 Congress Policy on the States
| Year / session | Policy |
|---|---|
| 1920s | **Non-interference** in internal affairs of states |
| **Haripura (1938)** | **Purna Swaraj covered the princely states as well**, but struggles to be waged by the states' own people |
| **Tripuri (1939)** | Dropped non-interference; **openly backed** state movements; Nehru AISPC president |
!NUM 1927 = AISPC founded and Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen emerged; 1938 = Hyderabad State Congress + Haripura; 1939 = Tripuri

### 6. HYDERABAD STATE – DYNASTY, NIZAM AND DEFIANCE
#### 6.1 The State
- Ruling dynasty: **Asaf Jahi (Nizams)**, founded by **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I, 1724**.
- **Largest and richest** princely state; **first in population** (1,63,38,534 in 1951).
- Last Nizam: **Mir Osman Ali Khan**, the **7th Nizam**; wanted **independence or accession to Pakistan**; sought an independent "Dominion of Hyderabad".
!TRAP Asaf Jahi (Nizams) ≠ Qutb Shahi (Golconda).
#### 6.2 Standstill Agreement
- Signed **29 November 1947** by **Nizam Mir Osman Ali Khan** and **Lord Mountbatten**, for **one year**; kept existing arrangements during talks.
- Nizam's defiance continued: banned Indian currency, imported arms from Pakistan; India suspected secret deals with Pakistan.
- Hyderabad stayed outside India about **13 months** (15 Aug 1947 – 17 Sept 1948).
#### 6.3 Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen and the Razakars
- **Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen (MIM)** emerged in **1927**.
- **Razakars** – militant volunteer force of the **Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen**, led by **Kasim (Qasim) Razvi**.
- Their violence against the population forced military action; they also targeted the Telangana armed struggle.
#### 6.4 Hyderabad Who's Who

| **Nawab Ali Yavar Jung** | Wrote **"Hyderabad in Retrospect"** (1949) |

| **Maj. Gen. El Edroos** | **Nizam's army chief** |

| **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** | **First elected CM** of Hyderabad State (**1952**) |
### 7. OPERATION POLO – THE POLICE ACTION
#### 7.1 Road to Polo
1. Standstill Agreement, 29 November 1947 →
2. Nizam bans Indian currency, imports arms →
3. Razakar violence →
4. **13–17 September 1948:** Operation Polo; **Nizam surrenders and accedes** →
5. **Military Governor J.N. Chaudhuri**; Part B state with the Nizam as **Rajpramukh**.
#### 7.2 Operation Facts
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Code name | **Operation Polo** ("Hyderabad Police Action") |
| Dates | **13–17 September 1948** (about four to five days) |
| Ended | **17 September 1948** |
| Indian commander | **Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri** (later Military Governor) |
| Nizam's side | **Maj. Gen. El Edroos** (army chief); Razakars under Kasim Razvi |
| Result | Nizam's forces + Razakars defeated; **Nizam's accession** in 1948 |
!PAIR Operation Polo | Hyderabad | 1948 – J.N. Chaudhuri || Operation Vijay | Goa | 1961 – K.P. Candeth
### 8. HYDERABAD'S UN APPEAL, THE "JOIN INDIAN UNION" MOVEMENT AND ACCOUNTS
- **'Join Indian Union' movement (1947)** – **Swami Ramananda Tirtha** (Theertha), through the **Hyderabad State Congress**.
- **'The Saga of Hyderabad Struggle in India's Freedom Movement'** – Swami Ramananda Tirtha; released by **Vice-President Krishna Kant, May 2002**.
- Nizam's appeal to the **UN Security Council (1948)** – delegation led by **Nawab Moin Nawaz Jung**.
- **"Hyderabad in Retrospect" (1949)** – **Nawab Ali Yavar Jung**; first published **anonymously** ("an ex-official of Hyderabad") as articles in the **Times of India**.
!TRAP "Hyderabad in Retrospect" = **Ali Yavar Jung**; the APPSC (Group 2, 2012) key gave **Laik Ali**, the last PM.
### 9. TELANGANA ARMED STRUGGLE (1946–51)
- Peasant revolt led by the **Communists** and the **Andhra Mahasabha** against the Nizam, the **deshmukhs** and later the Razakars.
- Trigger: killing of **Doddi Komaraiah (1946)**.
- Link to integration: unrest + Razakar violence → Operation Polo (1948); the Indian Army then put down the armed squads; struggle **withdrawn October 1951**.
### 10. AFTER 1948 – RAJPRAMUKHS, PRIVY PURSES AND DISSOLUTION OF HYDERABAD
#### 10.1 Hyderabad as a Part B State
- **Part B state (1950–56)**; **Nizam as Rajpramukh**.
- Dissolved **1 November 1956** (States Reorganisation):
| Region | Went to |
|---|---|
| **Telugu districts** | **Andhra Pradesh** |
| **Marathwada** | **Bombay State** |
| **Raichur and Gulbarga** | **Mysore** |
- **AP angle:** 17 September 1948 ended Nizam rule over Telangana. **T. Prakasam** – first CM of **Andhra State (1953)**; Burgula Ramakrishna Rao – first elected CM of Hyderabad (1952).
#### 10.2 Privy Purses and Rajpramukhs
| Term / Act | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Privy purses** | Guaranteed annual payments to rulers for accession |
| **Rajpramukhs** | Heads of the **Part B states** (former princely unions) |
| **7th Amendment, 1956** | **Ended the office of Rajpramukh** |
| **26th Amendment, 1971** (Indira Gandhi) | **Privy purses and titles abolished**; **removed Articles 291 and 362** |
!TRAP 26th (1971) = privy purses; 7th (1956) = Rajpramukhs.
### 11. FRENCH AND PORTUGUESE POSSESSIONS
#### 11.1 French Enclaves – Pondicherry, Yanam
- **Pondicherry, Karikal, Mahe, Yanam** passed to India **de facto on 1 November 1954** (after the **Kizhur** referendum, October 1954); **de jure on 16 August 1962**.
- **Chandernagore** passed de facto in **1950**; merged into **West Bengal** in **1954**.
- **Yanam** – French-origin enclave in AP's **Godavari delta**; part of **Puducherry UT**.
#### 11.2 Goa, Daman and Diu – Operation Vijay
| Power | Possessions after 1947 | How freed |
|---|---|---|
| **Portugal** | Goa, Daman, Diu; Dadra and Nagar Haveli | **Operation Vijay, December 1961** (Goa, Daman, Diu) |
- Commander: **Maj. Gen. K.P. Candeth**.
- **Goa** was under Portuguese control after 15 August 1947; the **Portuguese surrender (December 1961)** = end of European colonial rule in India.
