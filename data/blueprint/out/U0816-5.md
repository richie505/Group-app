### 1. FOUR LABOUR CODES (IN FORCE 21 NOVEMBER 2025)
#### 1.1 The Codes
- From **21 November 2025**, **4 Labour Codes replaced 29 Central labour laws**.
- Labour is in the **Concurrent List** (List III, **Entries 22–24 and 35**).
| Code | Laws merged | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| **Code on Wages, 2019** | **4** | National Floor Wage; max 48 hrs/week |
| **Code on Social Security, 2020** | **9** (EPF, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit) | Gig and platform workers defined |
| **Industrial Relations Code, 2020** | **3** (Trade Unions, Industrial Disputes, Industrial Employment) | Layoff permission 100 → 300 |
| **OSH Code, 2020** | **13** (factories, mines, contract labour, construction) | Factory licence 10 → 20 |
#### 1.2 Code on Wages
- Applies to all employees; **National Floor Wage** – States cannot fix minimum wage below it.
- Wage = Basic + DA + Retaining Allowance; **deductions capped at 50%**.
- Hours 8–12/day, **max 48/week**; **overtime at least twice** the normal rate.
- Payment monthly within 7 days; on resignation/termination within 2 days; wage slips mandatory.
- **Anoop Satpathy Expert Committee (2019)** – national minimum wage **₹375 per day**.
#### 1.3 Code on Social Security
- **First legal definition of gig and platform workers** (this Code, not the Code on Wages); Social Security Fund.
- Aggregators contribute **1–2% of annual turnover**, capped at 5% of amount payable to gig workers.
- **Fixed-term employees: gratuity after 1 year** (5 years applies to permanent employees).
- EPFO: establishments with 20+ employees; ESIC pan-India, mandatory even with 1 worker in hazardous work (earlier 10); inspector-cum-facilitators replace inspectors.
#### 1.4 Industrial Relations Code
- Layoff/retrenchment/closure permission threshold **100 → 300** workers (not 500).
- Merged the **Industrial Disputes Act, 1947** and **Trade Unions Act, 1926**.
- Strike: notice within 60 days before; no strike within 14 days of notice; mass casual leave by >50% workers = strike.
- Negotiating union: **51%** membership; council – unions with ≥20%.
#### 1.5 OSH Code
- Factory licence: with power **10 → 20** workers; without power **20 → 40**.
- Contract labour law applies when contractor has **50+** (earlier 20).
- **Women may work before 6 am / after 7 pm** with consent and safety measures.
- **Appointment letters** and **annual free health check** mandatory.
- Safety Committees: ≥500 factory, ≥250 construction, ≥100 mine workers.
!TRAP Gig workers are defined in the **Social Security Code**, not the Wages Code; IR layoff threshold is **300**, not 500.
### 2. DATA PROTECTION AND RIGHT TO INFORMATION
#### 2.1 DPDP Rules, 2025
- Notified **14 November 2025** (draft **January 2025**) – operationalise the **DPDP Act, 2023** (passed August 2023).
- **Immediate effect**: formation of the **Data Protection Board of India (DPBI)** + the RTI amendment; most provisions after **12–18 months** (consent, erasure, DPOs, audits, breach reporting – 2026–27).
- Citizen rights: **erasure, correction**, grievance redressal, **digital nominee** (after death/incapacity).
- Consent: informed; **withdrawal as easy as giving**; Consent Managers.
- Children: targeted ads restricted; limited exemption for parents to track child's location.
- **Significant Data Fiduciaries** – graded obligations.
- DPBI: **"digital by design"**, online complaints, can drop proceedings on a voluntary undertaking; it is a **subordinate office of MeitY** (criticised for lack of independence).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Data Principal** | Individual whose data is processed |
| **Data Fiduciary** | Entity deciding **purpose and means** of processing |
- Puttaswamy (2017) – privacy a fundamental right (Art 21); **Srikrishna Committee** draft – 2018.
!TRAP **RTI Act Section 8(1)(j)** amended – the **public-interest override** for personal information **removed**.
#### 2.2 20 Years of the RTI Act

### 3. CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT BILLS AND PARLIAMENT BILLS
#### 3.1 Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill, 2025
- Introduced in Lok Sabha by the **Union Home Minister**; amends **Article 75**.
- **PM, CMs, Union and State Ministers** arrested and detained for **30 consecutive days** (without bail) on charges punishable with **at least 5 years** – removed by President (PM/Union) or Governor (CM/State) on advice **by the 31st day**; if no advice, **automatically ceases**.
- **No permanent disqualification** – re-appointment possible after release (Art 75(1)).
- Not limited to Union Ministers – applies to State Ministers too; UTs through amendments to **Government of UTs Act, 1963** and **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019**.
- Backdrop: **V. Senthil Balaji case (Tamil Nadu, 2023)**.
- Introduced with Govt of UTs (Amendment) Bill, J&K Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill and the Online Gaming Bill; Lok Sabha **Rules 19A and 19B waived** (Monsoon Session ending 21 August 2025); critic Abhishek Singhvi.
- **JPC: 31 members – 21 LS + 10 RS**; JPC is an **ad hoc** body, recommendations not binding. Later: JPC deadline extended, Bill left out of the **July 2026 Monsoon Session** agenda.
- Concerns: presumption of innocence, separation of powers, misuse.
| Case | Point |
|---|---|
| **Manoj Narula v UoI (2014)** | PM's advice binding unless constitutionally disqualified |
| **Ram Jawaya Kapur v Punjab (1955)** | Parliamentary accountability |
| **S.R. Bommai (1994)** | Floor test |

#### 3.3 Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025
- Passed Lok Sabha by voice vote (Monsoon Session); **assent 22 August 2025**; Speaker Om Birla, IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, IT Secretary S. Krishnan.
- **Bans online money games** (offering, operation, advertisement, participation); **promotes e-sports and social games**.
| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **E-sports** | Recognised under National Sports Governance Act, 2025; entry fee allowed, performance prizes |
| **Online social games** | Casual/educational; **no betting**; promoted |

- Offering RMGs: up to **3 years + ₹1 crore**; advertising: up to **2 years + ₹50 lakh**; offences **cognisable and non-bailable**.
- **Players not penalised** – only operators/advertisers; blocking by CERT-In; Interpol for foreign operators.

- Losses ~₹15,000 crore a year; 32 gaming suicides in Karnataka in 31 months; ₹400 crore FIEWIN fraud; no explicit child-protection provisions.

### 4. GOVERNORS, BILLS AND THE PRESIDENTIAL REFERENCE
#### 4.1 Sequence
| Step | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | TN Governor **R.N. Ravi** sat on **10 Bills** (since 2020) |
| 2 | **8 April 2025**: SC (Justices **J.B. Pardiwala, R. Mahadevan**) held delay illegal; set timelines under **Art 142** (three months); pocket veto unconstitutional – **first time** enforceable timelines on Governors and President |

#### 4.2 Article 200 Options
- Assent; withhold assent; return for reconsideration (not Money Bill); **reserve for the President** (not the Supreme Court).
- Art 200 says **"as soon as possible"** – no timeline; mandatory reservation e.g. a Bill reducing HC powers.
- **Art 201** – President's assent to reserved Bills; **Art 163** – aid and advice; **163(2)** – Governor's discretion not questioned in court.
- Centre: Governor not a **"postman"**; AG R. Venkataramani – timelines = judicial amendment. States: reference relitigates verdict (Singhvi, Gopal Subramanium).
- GoI Act 1935: Governor had absolute veto. Cases cited: **Nabam Rebia (2016)**, K.M. Singh (2020).
| Commission | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Sarkaria (1988)** | Discretion only in rare cases; President to dispose of reserved Bills in **6 months** |
| **Punchhi (2010)** | Governor to decide on Bills within **6 months** |
#### 4.3 Article 143
- **143(1)**: question of law or fact of public importance – opinion **advisory, not binding** (persuasive).
- **143(2)**: disputes on pre-Constitution treaties.
- In a Reference the Court **cannot overrule its precedent** – only clarify or suggest a larger bench.
### 5. LANDMARK JUDGMENTS
#### 5.1 Rejanish KV v K Deepa – District Judges (9 October 2025)
- Five-judge Bench led by **CJI B.R. Gavai**: **serving judicial officers can be directly recruited as district judges** (earlier only advocates with 7 years' practice).
- **Overruled Satya Narain Singh (1984) and Dheeraj Mor v HC of Delhi (2020)**; **Justice Sundresh** – absolute bar violates **Art 14**.
- Directions: **7 years** experience (advocacy + judicial service combined); uniform minimum age **35**.
- **Art 233** – district judges by Governor in consultation with HC; other officers – Governor after consulting State PSC and HC; Arts 233–237 (Part VI).
- CJM – offences up to 7 years; JM – up to 3 years; death sentence by Sessions Judge needs HC confirmation.
#### 5.2 Seniority in Higher Judicial Services
- Hearings from **28 October 2025** – five-judge Bench (CJI Gavai): promotees vs direct recruits; **promotees ≈ three-fourths** of the cadre.
- Bihar: 91 Principal District Judges – 86 direct; UP 70 – 58 direct; **AP HC recommended 50%** of HC appointments from service be those who began as Civil Judges.
- **All India Judges Association (2002)**: **75% promotion (50% merit-cum-seniority + 25% LDCE), 25% direct**; 40-point roster (R.K. Sabarwal, 1995).
- **May 2025 SC**: LDCE 25% restored; qualifying service **5 → 3 years**; **3 years' practice** for Civil Judge entry.
- District judiciary handles **~90%** of litigation.
- **NALSA**: Patron-in-Chief **CJI**; Executive Chairman **second senior-most SC judge**; LSA Act 1987; Art 39A.

### 6. ELECTIONS – SIR, ECINET AND VICE-PRESIDENT
#### 6.1 Bihar SIR and Pan-India SIR
- **SC order 10 July 2025**: framed whether ECI can conduct SIR; **did not stay** Bihar SIR; ordered draft-roll publication.

#### 6.2 Roll Integrity and ECINET
- **Aland (Karnataka)**: fraudulent **Form-7** applications to delete ~6,000 voters (2023); **Form-7 = objection to inclusion/deletion**; The Hindu's probe; allegations by LoP Rahul Gandhi.
- ECI response: **e-sign** on ECINet – **Aadhaar-linked mobile verification** for online registration, deletion, correction; Aadhaar **not needed to vote**.
- **ECINET** (Election Commission Integrated Network) – announced **4 May 2025**; integrates **40+ apps**; **first used in 19 June 2025 bypolls** (Kerala, Gujarat, Punjab, West Bengal); turnout uploaded by **presiding officers** (not returning officers) before leaving the station.

#### 6.3 Vice-Presidential Election 2025
- **C.P. Radhakrishnan (NDA)** defeated **B. Sudershan Reddy** (joint opposition) – September 2025; 98%+ MPs voted; **Returning Officer P.C. Mody** (RS Secretary General).
- **Art 66**: electoral college of **elected + nominated members of both Houses** – **State MLAs do not vote** (President's college includes elected MLAs).
- STV proportional representation, secret ballot; citizen, **at least 35**, no office of profit; 5-year term, **re-election allowed**; ex-officio **Chairman of Rajya Sabha** (not Lok Sabha).
#### 6.4 Freebies vs Welfare
- Bihar 2025: promises over ₹8 lakh crore/year; ₹10,000 to 75 lakh women (₹7,500 crore pre-poll scheme).
- **S. Subramaniam Balaji v TN (2013)** – manifesto promises **not "corrupt practice"** under **Sec 123, RPA**; SC (Nov 2023) admitted plea on freebies.
- Good welfare: **NTR ₹2/kg rice (AP, 1980s)**; Bihar girls' bicycle scheme; Punjab debt-to-GSDP 47.2%.
### 7. CITIZENSHIP, IMMIGRATION AND REFUGEES
#### 7.1 Immigration and Foreigners Act and Orders, 2025
- **Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025** (April 2025) replaced the **Foreigners Act 1946**, Registration of Foreigners Act 1939, Passport (Entry into India) Act 1920; **absorbed** Immigration (Carriers' Liability) Act 2000.
- **Immigration and Foreigners Order, 2025** (MHA, **1 September 2025**) – replaced the **Foreigners (Tribunal) Order, 1964**; **first clear legal basis for detention**.
  - Foreigners Tribunals: powers of **Judicial Magistrate First Class**; max 3 members; ex-parte orders reviewable within 30 days.
  - **FTs only in Assam (100)**; order applies across India; employment barred in power, defence, space, nuclear etc.
- **Exemption Order, 2025** – frees from passport/visa rules: citizens of Nepal, Bhutan; Tibetan refugees; six minorities from Afghanistan, Pakistan, Bangladesh; **Sri Lankan Tamils who sheltered on or before 9 January 2015**; **does not confer citizenship**.
- **IMDT Act, 1983** struck down **2005** – 11 IMDTs became FTs; Assam NRC 2019 excluded 19 lakh.
#### 7.2 Refugee Policy
- India **not a signatory** to the **1951 Refugee Convention or 1967 Protocol**.
- Tibetans ~63,000 (rehabilitation policy 2014); Sri Lankan Tamils ~90,000 (excluded from CAA).
- **CAA 2019**: six minorities from Pakistan, Afghanistan, Bangladesh who entered **before 31 December 2014**.
