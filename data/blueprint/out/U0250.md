### 2. DIRECT ACTION DAY, 16 AUGUST 1946
- League withdrew its acceptance of the **Cabinet Mission Plan** and on **29 July 1946** called for **"Direct Action"**; Jinnah also condemned Wavell's invitation to Congress to form the interim government.
- Observed **16 August 1946** to press for **Pakistan** ("bandh" call).
- Riots began in **Calcutta** – the **"Great Calcutta Killings"** → spread to **Noakhali** (East Bengal), **Bihar** and north India.
!TRAP 29 July 1946 = call; 16 August 1946 = Direct Action Day.
### 3. GANDHI IN 1946–48
| Date | Gandhi's whereabouts / event |
|---|---|
| **Nov 1946 – March 1947** | **Noakhali** peace mission, walking village to village |
| **March 1947** | **Bihar** – to calm anti-Muslim riots |
| **15 August 1947** | **Beliaghata, Calcutta** – fasting and praying; took part in **no** Independence Day celebration, saddened by partition |
| **30 January 1948** | Assassinated by **Nathuram Godse**, New Delhi |
- Outcome at Noakhali: he **failed to stop the enmity and violence**.
- **Mountbatten** called Gandhi the **"one-man boundary force"** (50,000 soldiers in Punjab vs one man in Bengal).
### 4. ATTLEE'S STATEMENT AND ALTERNATIVE SCHEMES
#### 4.1 Attlee's Statement, 20 February 1947
- **Clement Attlee** – **Labour Party**; British PM **1945–1951**; PM at independence.
- Declared in the **House of Commons** that British rule would end by **30 June 1948**, even without agreement among Indian parties.
- Power could go to **provincial governments** (hint of Balkanisation); appointed **Mountbatten** in place of **Wavell**.
- Attlee quotes: "**Imperialism is dead as Dodo**"; "The British Government is **not responsible** for the partition of India".
#### 4.2 Alternative Schemes
| Scheme | Proponent | Proposal and fate |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan Balkan / "Dickie Bird Plan"** (May 1947) | **Mountbatten** | Power to provinces (Bengal, Punjab) to join India, Pakistan or stay independent; **Nehru rejected** it as Balkanisation; soon dropped |
| **United Bengal plan** (1947) | **Sarat Chandra Bose + H.S. Suhrawardy** | Sovereign undivided Bengal; **rejected by both Congress and League** |
| Dominion status first | **V.P. Menon** | Immediate transfer on **dominion status**, without waiting for a new constitution |
- Mountbatten's instructions: **keep India united if possible**.
### 5. MOUNTBATTEN PLAN (3 JUNE PLAN), 1947
- **Mountbatten** became Viceroy in **March 1947**; plan announced **3 June 1947** – basis of independence and of partition.
- Rationale: accepted the Pakistan demand to avoid civil war and administrative collapse.
#### 5.1 Provisions
- **Punjab and Bengal:** Legislative Assemblies to meet in **two groups** (Hindu-majority / Muslim-majority districts) and vote by simple majority on partition → **both voted to divide**.
- **Sindh:** to decide on its own.
- **Referendums:** **NWFP** → joined Pakistan despite the **Khudai Khidmatgar** boycott (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan); **Sylhet** (Assam) → joined East Pakistan.
- **Boundary Commission** for Punjab and Bengal.
- **Princely states:** paramountcy to lapse; join India or Pakistan – **no third option** of remaining independent.
- Common Governor-General permitted, but Pakistan chose **Jinnah**.
- Armed forces divided; power transferred on the basis of the **GoI Act 1935**.
- **Advanced the transfer date: June 1948 → 15 August 1947**.
#### 5.2 Congress's Acceptance
- To avoid **Balkanisation**, secure a **strong Centre** and end **communal bloodshed**.
- Congress accepted the plan but **never accepted the Two-Nation Theory**.
- **AICC, Delhi (14/15 June 1947)** accepted the partition resolution, moved by **Govind Ballabh Pant**, under President **J.B. Kripalani**.
!TRAP Attlee deadline = 30 June 1948; Mountbatten advanced it to 15 August 1947.
### 6. INDIAN INDEPENDENCE ACT, 1947
#### 6.1 Stages
| Stage | Date |
|---|---|
| Bill introduced in the **House of Commons** by **Attlee** | **4 July 1947** |
| Passed by Parliament | July 1947 |
| **Royal assent** (Act enacted) | **18 July 1947** |
| Came into effect | **15 August 1947** |
- Purpose: gave **legal force to the Mountbatten Plan**.
!TRAP 4 July = introduced (not passed); 18 July = royal assent.
#### 6.2 Provisions
- **Two independent dominions**, India and Pakistan, with the right to leave the Commonwealth.
- **Offices ended:** **Viceroy** and **Secretary of State for India**.
- Each dominion's **Governor-General** acts on its cabinet's advice (constitutional head); a common GG allowed.
- **Constituent Assemblies made sovereign** – could frame any constitution and **repeal any British law, even this Act**; also acted as legislatures. The Constituent Assembly (as Provisional Parliament) took sovereign power at **midnight of 14/15 August 1947**.
- **Paramountcy lapsed** → the **565 princely states** free to accede to India or Pakistan.
- Armed forces divided; **Boundary Commissions** for Punjab and Bengal.
- **Interim law:** **GoI Act 1935, as adapted**, until new constitutions.
- British Government to have **no control** over either dominion from 15 August 1947; yet the **British sovereign did appoint** the Governor-General of free India.
!KEY Most distinctive feature: an **"Act without a constitution"** – transferred power but left constitution-making to Indians. Dominion status let India and Pakistan become free before framing constitutions.

### 8. RADCLIFFE BOUNDARY COMMISSIONS
- Formed by **Mountbatten** on **30 June 1947**; two commissions – **Punjab** and **Bengal**.
- Each = **2 Congress + 2 League** nominee judges; **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**, a British lawyer with **no knowledge of Indian conditions beyond the map**, chaired **both**.
- The **Radcliffe Line** award was ready on **12 August** but published on **17 August 1947**, after independence, so Britain would not be blamed for the violence.
### 9. CONSEQUENCES OF PARTITION
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Assets** | **Partition Council** divided assets and liabilities; **Pakistan took 17.5%** |
| **Armed forces** | Forces, services and assets divided between the two dominions |
| **Migration** | An estimated **10–15 million** crossed the borders; hundreds of thousands killed |
| **Rehabilitation** | **Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation** – refugee camps, resettlement, evacuee property |
| **Kashmir** | UN ceasefire **1 January 1949** |
| **Goa** | Struggle continued against the Portuguese after 1947; **Operation Vijay** |
- Criticism: the **hasty advanced date** and the **late Radcliffe award** worsened the violence.
