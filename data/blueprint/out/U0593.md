## U0593 | SEASONS – INCLINATION OF THE AXIS, SOLSTICES AND EQUINOXES, DISTRIBUTION OF INSOLATION
### 1. WHY SEASONS OCCUR
- Earth **revolves** round the Sun in **365.25 days** with its axis tilted **23½° from the perpendicular** (**66½° to the orbital plane**) – the key reason (**APPSC 2018**).
- **Parallelism**: the axis keeps pointing the same way (towards the Pole Star) during revolution.
- Seasons come from **axial inclination + parallelism + revolution + spherical shape** → changing **angle of insolation** and **day length**.
- Hemispheres tilt alternately towards (summer) and away (winter) from the Sun; so seasons in the two hemispheres are **opposite**.
- Tilt + revolution also give changing day length, solstices and equinoxes, six-month polar day and night.
!TRAP Rotation causes day and night, not seasons. Revolution alone is not enough – it needs axial tilt.
### 2. DISTANCE IS NOT THE CAUSE
| Point | Date | NH season |
|---|---|---|
| **Perihelion** (nearest) | ~**3 January** | **Winter** |
| Aphelion (farthest) | early July | Summer |
- Earth is nearest the Sun during **northern winter**, so distance cannot cause seasons; **tilt** does (solar angle + day length).
!KEY Seasons = axial tilt, not Earth–Sun distance.
### 3. SOLSTICES AND EQUINOXES
| Date | Event | Sun overhead at |
|---|---|---|
| **21 March** | Vernal (spring) equinox | **Equator** |
| **21 June** | Summer solstice (NH) | **Tropic of Cancer** |
| **23 September** | Autumnal equinox | **Equator** |
| **22 December** | Winter solstice (NH) | **Tropic of Capricorn** |
- Sun overhead at the Equator **twice a year**: 21 March and 23 September; day and night equal everywhere.
- **21 March – 23 September**: Sun between Equator and Tropic of Cancer → **NH summer**.
- **21 June** = **International Day of Yoga** (2026 theme "Yoga for Healthy Ageing") – marks the summer solstice.
### 4. DAY LENGTH BY LATITUDE
| Latitude | 21 June | Equinoxes | 22 Dec |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equator** | 12 h | 12 h | 12 h |
| Tropic of Cancer | ~13½ h | 12 h | ~10½ h |
| **Arctic Circle** | 24 h day | 12 h | 24 h night |
| North Pole | Continuous day | Sunrise/sunset | Continuous night |
- Place with **equal day and night all year**: the **Equator**.
- On **21 June** the Sun **does not set at the Arctic Circle** (makes a full circle above the horizon).
- **Midnight sun** = Sun visible above the horizon at midnight.
### 5. SUMMER VS WINTER
| Basis | Summer | Winter |
|---|---|---|
| Hemisphere | Tilted **towards** Sun | Tilted **away** |
| Rays | More **direct** | More **oblique** (slanting) |
| Energy | Concentrated, more heating | Spread over larger area, less heating |
| Days | Longer | Shorter |
| Sun altitude / shadow | Higher / **shorter** | Lower / **longer** |
- Noon is hotter in summer at a place like Delhi because in winter the rays fall **more slanting**.
- Example: NH tilted towards the Sun → NH summer.
### 6. INSOLATION – FACTORS AND DISTRIBUTION
- Factors: **angle of incidence** (vertical rays heat a smaller area), **duration of day**, **transparency of atmosphere**, **land configuration**.
!TRAP Direction of ocean tides is NOT a factor of insolation.
- Insolation **falls from the tropics to the poles**.
- Maximum over **cloudless subtropical deserts**, not the Equator.
### 7. SEASONS BY HEMISPHERE AND LATITUDE ZONE
| Zone | Seasonal feature |
|---|---|
| **Low latitudes** | Small temperature contrast; **wet and dry** seasons |
| **Subtropics** | Hot summers, mild winters |
| **Mid-latitudes** | **Four distinct seasons**; large summer–winter temperature gaps |
| **High latitudes** | Largest swings in day length and insolation |
- Greatest seasonal contrast: **high latitudes** (**APPSC-G1 2023**); another key gave **mid-latitudes** for the biggest summer–winter temperature gap.
- **Southern Hemisphere**: longest day ~**22 December**, shortest ~21 June; summer in **January** (its hottest month).
- **Christmas in Australia falls in summer** – Australia is in the Southern Hemisphere.
### 8. SHADOWS AND PLACES IN INDIA
- Vertically **overhead Sun → no noon shadow**. Higher Sun → shorter shadow.
- **21 June**: no noon shadow on the **Tropic of Cancer**; north India's noon shadow is **smallest on 21 June** and longest in December.
- Equator: no noon shadow in March and September.
- Overhead Sun only **between the Tropics**, twice a year (**zero-shadow days**). **Delhi, Srinagar** (north of 23½°N) **never** get it.
- **AP** (12°41'N–19°07'N) lies between Equator and Tropic of Cancer → overhead noon Sun **twice a year**, high temperatures all year.
- **21 June day length grows northward**: **Srinagar > Delhi > Hyderabad > Chennai > Kanyakumari**. Longest summer sunlight: **Srinagar**.
#### 8.1 Indian and AP Seasons
- Hot weather season **March–May** (Sun's northward march): ~**50°C at Ganganagar (1935)**; ~10% rain pre-monsoon.
- **IMD's four seasons**: winter (Jan–Feb) → pre-monsoon (Mar–May) → South-West monsoon → post-monsoon. Traditional: six **ritus** (Vasanta to Shishira).
| AP season | Months |
|---|---|
| SW monsoon | June–September (~2/3 of rain) |
| Winter | October–February; **Lambasingi** coldest (~2°C) |
#### 8.2 Related One-Liners
- "Yellow Sea is the coastless sea of the world" is a **wrong** statement.
- **Stratosphere** holds the ozone layer – the **Ozonosphere**.
