## U0591 | MOTIONS OF THE EARTH – ROTATION AND REVOLUTION; EFFECTS (DAY AND NIGHT, CORIOLIS FORCE, TIDES)
### 1. ROTATION
- Earth spins on its **axis** (line through both poles) **west to east** – counter-clockwise seen from the North Pole. Only **Venus and Uranus** spin the other way.
- Axis tilted **23.5° (23°30')** from the ecliptic axis.
- Angular speed **15° per hour** (1° in 4 minutes). Linear speed ~**1,670 km/h at the Equator**, **zero at the poles**.
- Not felt: uniform motion; atmosphere moves along.
#### 1.1 Sidereal vs Solar Day
| Day | Measured against | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Sidereal** | A distant star | **23 h 56 min 4 s** |
| **Solar** | The Sun | **24 h** (~4 min longer, as Earth also moves in orbit) |
#### 1.2 Changes in Rotation
- Ice melt moves mass towards the Equator, raising the moment of inertia: days longer by **~1.33 ms per century**.
- Axis shift is mainly from mass redistribution (ice, groundwater loss) – **UPSC 2025**; solar flares only disturb the upper atmosphere.

### 3. DAY AND NIGHT
- Caused by **rotation** – not revolution, not tilt alone.
- **Circle of illumination** = terminator = "**grey line**" / twilight zone (**APPSC 2019**); passes through both poles **only at the equinoxes**.
- Unequal day lengths: **tilt + revolution**.
- **Equator**: ~12 h day all year; seasons marked by wet and dry periods. Variation grows with latitude; **poles: ~6 months day + 6 months night** (revolution with tilt).
- **Midnight Sun**: beyond the Arctic and Antarctic Circles near the summer solstice; **Norway** = "Land of the Midnight Sun".
#### 3.1 Twilight
- **Shortest at Equator** (Sun rises and sets almost vertically); **longest at high latitudes** (oblique path; weeks near poles).
### 4. CORIOLIS AND CENTRIFUGAL EFFECTS
- **Coriolis** = apparent deflection of moving air/water caused by **rotation** (APPSC-G1 2019; BPSC 2022).
- **Ferrel's law**: deflection to the **right in NH**, **left in SH**.
- Force = **2Ωv sin(latitude)**: **zero at Equator**, maximum at poles; grows with latitude and wind speed (UPSC 2024).
- Cyclones rarely form within **5° of the Equator**; NH cyclones spin **anticlockwise**.
- AP angle: **Cyclone Montha (2025)** was pushed north-northwest (steering winds, trade winds, Coriolis) to the **Kakinada** coast.
- **Centrifugal force**: maximum at Equator, zero at poles → equatorial bulge, polar flattening (**oblate spheroid**); **weight least at Equator**.
### 5. TIDES
| Tide | Position |
|---|---|
| **Spring** | Sun–Earth–Moon in line (new and full moon) |
| **Neap** | Sun and Moon at right angles (~7 days later) |
- Each twice a month. Two high, two low tides daily (~**12 h 25 min** apart).
- High tide comes **~52 min later** each day (lunar day 24 h 52 min): 8:00 am → 8:52 am.
### 6. REVOLUTION AND LEAP YEAR
- Slightly **elliptical** orbit; period **365.25 days** (365 d 6 h 9 min 9.51 s); mean speed **29.78 km/s**.
- Perihelion early January (**3 Jan in 2026**); aphelion early July (**6 July in 2026**). Earth–Sun distance is not constant.
- **Sun's gravity** = centripetal force.
- Gives the year, **seasons** (with tilt), solstices and equinoxes. Sun overhead at Equator on **21 March and 23 September**.
- Extra quarter day → **leap year** of **366 days** with **29 February**; century year only if divisible by **400** (**2000 yes, 1900 no**).
- Rotation gives **day and night, time zones, Coriolis**; revolution gives the year and seasons.
