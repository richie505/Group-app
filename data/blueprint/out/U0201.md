## U0201 | MODERATE PHASE (1885-1905) – LEADERS, METHODS, DEMANDS AND ACHIEVEMENTS; ECONOMIC CRITIQUE OF COLONIALISM
### 1. FOUNDING BACKGROUND
- **A.O. Hume** – regarded as the **founder of the INC**; Naoroji was a founding figure.
- **W.C. Bonnerjee** – **first INC President**, Bombay, **1885**; **Naoroji presided in 1886**.
- **Safety Valve Theory** – INC seen as a way of channelling political discontent.
!TRAP APPSC 2019 keyed "Naoroji founded the INC with Hume and Wacha" as **wrong** – Hume is the founder.

### 3. DADABHAI NAOROJI
- INC president **three times (not four)**: **Calcutta 1886, Lahore 1893, Calcutta 1906**.
- **Calcutta 1906** – first demand for **Swaraj** as the goal (APPSC 2025).
- **First Indian member of the British Parliament** – House of Commons, **1892**, **Liberal Party**, **Central Finsbury**.
- Founded the **London Indian Society (1865)** and **East India Association (1866, London)**.
- Backed the Indian Parliamentary Committee (1893); served on the **Welby Commission (1895)**.
- Wrote **Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901)** – called British rule "**Un-British**"; **estimated India's national income**.
- Said Britain was "**bleeding India white**".
!PAIR Lal Mohan Ghose | first Indian to **contest** a Commons election | 1885 || Dadabhai Naoroji | first Indian **elected** | 1892, Central Finsbury
### 4. G.K. GOKHALE (1866-1915)
- **Political guru of Gandhi**; Gandhi returned to India (**1915**) on Gokhale's advice (recalled in a Feb 2026 profile).
- Gandhi wrote **"Gokhale – My Political Guru"** (first ed. Ahmedabad, Feb 1955).
- **M.G. Ranade** = Gokhale's political and intellectual guru.
- President, **21st INC session, Banaras 1905** – condemned the **partition of Bengal**, backed **Swadeshi**.
- **Refused a knighthood** and a seat in the **Council of the Secretary of State for India**.
- Constitutional methods; pushed social and educational reform.
!KEY Guru chain: **Ranade → Gokhale → Gandhi**.

### 6. ECONOMIC CRITIQUE – DRAIN OF WEALTH
- **Naoroji, R.C. Dutt and Dinshaw Wacha** showed Britain drained India's wealth through:
1. **Home Charges**
2. **Salaries and pensions**
3. **Unequal trade** + British monopoly
4. India's wealth transferred to Britain
- **Greatest Moderate contribution** – broke the myth of British benevolence; later used by the Extremists and Gandhi.
- **Economic critics of colonialism**: Naoroji, **G. Subramania Iyer**, R.C. Dutt (all three).
!TRAP **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan rejected** Naoroji's Drain Theory; saw British rule as modernising.

### 7. DEMANDS OF THE MODERATES
| Area | Demands |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional** | **Larger councils**, **elected members**, **control over the budget**; "No taxation without representation" |
| **Administrative** | **Simultaneous ICS exams** (India and England); **higher ICS age limit**; **Indianisation** of services; **separation of judiciary from executive** |
| **Economic** | **Lower land revenue**; **end salt tax**; **cut military spending** (Afghan, Burma wars); **tariff protection** for industry; irrigation |
| **Civil rights** | Free speech, press, association; protested **Tilak's arrest** and deportation without trial of the **Natu brothers** |
### 8. METHODS AND LIMITATIONS
#### 8.1 Beliefs and Methods
- British rule seen as "providential"; British just but ill-informed → **faith in British justice and fair play**.
- **3 Ps: Prayers, Petitions, Protests**; resolutions, meetings, pamphlets – **strictly constitutional agitation**.
- Two-pronged: build opinion in India + persuade the British public.
- **Distrusted mass action**; no mass civil disobedience.
#### 8.2 Limitations
- Annual session lasted only about **three days**; **no machinery** between sessions.
- **Narrow urban, educated base**.
- **Partition of Bengal (1905)** exposed their "**naive faith**" in British justice.
- Moderates **supported social reform** (e.g. **Age of Consent Bill**); **Tilak opposed** it.

### 10. ACHIEVEMENTS
#### 10.1 Indian Councils Act, 1892
- Passed under Viceroy **Lansdowne** (20 June 1892).
- **More non-official members**, filled by **recommendation** of municipal/district boards, universities, chambers of commerce – **indirect election**; the word "election" avoided.
- Members could **discuss the budget** but **not vote or amend** it; could **ask questions** (no supplementaries).
- Weak: **no direct vote**, **official majority** kept; INC found it inadequate.
#### 10.2 Other Gains
- **Welby Commission (1895)** – on Indian expenditure.
- Pan-Indian **national identity**; **economic critique**; training in **democratic politics** and civil liberties – NOT complete independence.
- Moderates **backed Europe's democratic and scientific culture** and **sharply criticised the economic side** of imperialism (both true).
- Goal: **self-government within the Empire**.
