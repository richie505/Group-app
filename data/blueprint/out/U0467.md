## U0467 | METHODS OF MEASURING NATIONAL INCOME – PRODUCT, INCOME AND EXPENDITURE METHODS; DIFFICULTIES
### 1. THREE METHODS OF MEASUREMENT
| Method | Also called | What is summed |
|---|---|---|
| **Production / Product** | **Value added** method; output method; **net product / final product** method | Sum of value added by every producing unit (output − intermediate inputs) = total final product = GDP |
| **Income** | **Factor income / factor payment / distributed share** method | **Compensation of employees + operating surplus (rent, interest, profit) + mixed income** |
| **Expenditure** | **Consumption-and-investment** method; **final expenditure**; consumption / outlay method | **C + I + G + (X − M)** – final spending on output |
!TRAP "**Cost and Accounting method**" is NOT a method of measuring national income (APPSC-G2 2025). "**Diminishing cost method**" is NOT a method to calculate GDP.
#### 1.1 Product (value added) method – steps
- **Value added = value of output − intermediate consumption**.
- Add value added at every stage; the sum equals the value of the final product.
- **Change in inventories = production − sales**; included (unsold production counts as output).
#### 1.2 Income method – formula
- Economic Survey formula: **GDP = Compensation of employees + Consumption of fixed capital + (Taxes on production − subsidies on production) + Gross operating surplus**.
- **Factor income** = income earned by a factor of production for services rendered to the production unit.
#### 1.3 Expenditure method – components
| Component | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Private consumption (C)** | **Largest** share – about **59% of GDP**; 11th Plan (2007-12) average = 59% (APPSC-GS 2019); makes India **less exposed to global swings** |
| **Business investment (I)** | **Most volatile** component |
| Government spending (G) | Final spending of government |
| **Net exports (X − M)** | Last; the trade effect |
- **Q1 2026-27**: GFCF **34.3% of GDP** (from 31.4%); real GFCF grew **11.9%**; private consumption grew **7.1%** (1 and 17 Sep 2026).
#### 1.4 Fixed vs working capital
| Fixed capital | Working capital |
|---|---|
| Long-term assets used over many years – **farmer's plough, computer** | Used up in one cycle – **yarn, petrol** |
### 2. DOUBLE COUNTING AND VALUE ADDED
- **Double counting** = counting the same value more than once by adding sales at each stage.
- Example: **cotton farmer -> weaver -> tailor**; counting all three sales counts cotton three times (cotton -> cloth -> garment).
- Solution: count **only final goods**, or add the **value added at each stage**.
- **Intermediate goods** are excluded.
- **Double deflation** (2022-23 series): **Real GVA = Real output − Real intermediate consumption**; output and input prices deflated **separately**.
!KEY Final product method and value added method give the same total – both avoid double counting.
### 3. METHOD -> SECTORS IN INDIA
- India uses a **combination of the production and income methods**.
| Method | Sectors |
|---|---|
| **Production (value added)** | **Agriculture and allied, mining, manufacturing** |
| **Income** | Services: **trade, transport, banking, real estate, public administration** |
| **Expenditure (commodity flow)** | In part for **construction**; expenditure data (PFCE) serves as a **cross-check** |
- **NSO** compiles national accounts.
- **2026**: New 2022-23 series aligns **Supply and Use Tables** with national accounts -> cuts discrepancies between production- and expenditure-side GDP; improves real GVA with **double deflation + volume extrapolation**.
### 4. INCLUSION AND EXCLUSION TEST
| Item | In NI? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Wages and salaries, **rent**, **dividends**, corporate profits | **Yes** | Factor payments |
| **Interest on a productive loan** | **Yes** | Factor payment |
| **Imputed rent of owner-occupied houses** | **Yes** | Imputed service |
| **Self-consumed farm output** | **Yes** | Production |
| **Change in inventories** | **Yes** | Part of output |
| Production for self-use (fixed capital) | **Yes** | Production |
| **Transfer payments** (pensions, scholarships, gifts) | No | No service in return |
| **Transfer earnings** | No | Not payments for factor services (G2 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A) |
| **Interest on public debt** | No | Treated as transfer (APPSC-G1 2022, 2023) |
| **Second-hand sales** (car via dealer) | No | Only the **dealer's/broker's commission** is included |
| **Tax revenue** as such | No | Transfer to government |
| Shares, bonds (financial transactions) | No | No new production |
| **Housewives' unpaid services** | No | Not marketed |
!TRAP Imputed rent = IN; interest on public debt = OUT.
### 5. DEFINITIONS OF NATIONAL INCOME BY ECONOMISTS
| Economist | Definition's core |
|---|---|
| **Alfred Marshall** | **"National dividend"** – net aggregate of commodities (material and immaterial) and services produced each year by **labour and capital acting on natural resources** |
| **A.C. Pigou** | The part of the community's income, **including income from abroad**, that **can be measured in money** |
| **Irving Fisher** | **Consumption-based** – only **services received by ultimate consumers** |
| **Simon Kuznets** | Net output of commodities and services flowing to **ultimate consumers** or into **net additions to capital stock** |
- Marshall's "net" = after deducting depreciation of machines; income from abroad is added.
- Pigou's definition suits mainly **developed countries**, where nearly everything is bought for money.
!PAIR Marshall | national dividend | production || Fisher | consumption | ultimate consumers
#### 5.1 Official wording
- **GDP** = value of all final goods and services produced within the country in a year.
- **GSDP / SDP** = value of all goods and services produced within the State's boundaries, counted without duplication, during a period.
### 6. CIRCULAR FLOW OF INCOME
#### 6.1 Two-sector flows
| Flow | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Real flow** – factor services | Households -> firms |
| **Real flow** – goods and services | Firms -> households |
| **Money flow** – factor payments | Firms -> households |
| **Money flow** – consumption spending | Households -> firms |
#### 6.2 Leakages vs injections
| Leakages (withdrawals) | Injections |
|---|---|
| **Saving (S)** | **Investment (I)** |
| **Taxes (T)** | **Government spending (G)** |
| **Imports (M)** | **Exports (X)** |
- **Open-economy equilibrium: S + T + M = I + G + X** (total leakages = total injections).
- Open-economy expenditure identity: **Y = C + I + G + (X − M)**.
!TRAP Investment, government spending, exports = **injections**; saving, taxes, imports = **leakages**.
### 7. DIFFICULTIES IN MEASURING NATIONAL INCOME IN INDIA
#### 7.1 Structural difficulties
| Difficulty | Example |
|---|---|
| **Non-monetised economy** | Subsistence output, **self-consumption** (farm output kept for home use) |
| **Informal sector** | Big unorganised sector **without proper accounts** |
| **No occupational specialisation** | One person as both **farmer and weaver** -> double counting risk |
| **Black money / parallel economy** | Unrecorded income |
| Valuation problems | Depreciation, inventories |
| Data gaps | Missing records |
#### 7.2 Current issues
- **IMF review (Dec 2025)**: persistent **gaps between production- and expenditure-side GDP** show only **partial capture of informal activity**.
- Fix: new series uses **Supply and Use Tables** to reduce the discrepancy.
- **Mar 2026**: household-sector GVA is extrapolated using indicators after the benchmark year; private corporate segment depends on the **MCA database**.
### 8. STATE INCOME – AP
#### 8.1 Terms
| Term | Meaning / formula |
|---|---|
| **Income originating** | Value produced **within the State's boundaries** (= GSDP); excludes residents' income from outside |
| **Income accruing** | State income + income of the State's residents earned in other States/abroad |
| **GSDP** | GVA (basic prices, industry-wise) + product taxes − product subsidies = GSDP (market prices) |
| **NSDP** | GSDP − depreciation; **AP's per capita income** is based on NSDP |
| Production taxes | Stamp duties + property taxes |
| Production subsidies | Apprentice + interest subsidies |
#### 8.2 Compilation
- **Directorate of Economics and Statistics (DES), AP** compiles GSDP; NSO compiles national accounts.
- States use the **production (income-originating) approach**, sector by sector.
- AP GSDP computed in **9 sectors** (APPSC-G2 2017) = old CSO classification:
1. Agriculture, forestry, fishing
2. Mining
3. Manufacturing
4. Electricity, gas, water
5. Construction
6. Trade, hotels
7. Transport, storage, communication
8. Finance, real estate
9. Community and social services
- **Base year: 2011-12** (APSES 2024-25 figures at 2011-12 prices).
- Manufacturing GVA used to be allocated to States by **ASI shares**; now **GST data** is also available (Mar 2026).
- **May 2026**: MoSPI uniform guideline for compiling **GSVA (base 2022-23)** for consistency with national accounts.

### 11. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Cost and Accounting** and **Diminishing cost** are NOT methods.
2. India uses **production + income** methods; expenditure is a cross-check.
3. **Marshall = national dividend**; Pigou = money measure + income from abroad; Fisher = consumption.
4. **Imputed rent** and **self-consumed output** are included.
5. **Interest on public debt**, tax revenue, housewives' services are excluded.

7. **Exports** are an injection, **imports** a leakage.
8. AP GSDP compiled by **DES, AP**, not NSO.
9. Income **originating** excludes residents' income from outside the State; that is income **accruing**.
10. Interest on a **productive** loan is a factor payment – included.
