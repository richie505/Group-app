## U0199 | GROWTH OF NATIONALISM – FACTORS; PRE-CONGRESS POLITICAL ASSOCIATIONS
### 1. FACTORS OF INDIAN NATIONALISM
#### 1.1 Unintended Results of British Rule
- **Political and administrative unification**: railways, telegraph, post and **uniform laws** tied India together.
- **Western education**: liberal ideas (liberty, equality, democracy); **English as a link language** across regions.
- Rise of a **new educated middle class** – lawyers, journalists, teachers – who led the early associations.
- **Press and literature** spread political ideas; history-writing revived pride in India's past.
- Inspiration from the **American, French and Italian** freedom struggles.
#### 1.2 Reactions to British Rule
- **Economic critique**: the **drain** of wealth and the **ruin of artisans**.
- **Racial arrogance**: Lytton's policies and the **Ilbert Bill** agitation.
!KEY Early associations trained the English-educated class in **petitions, memorials and all-India teamwork** – this led straight to the **INC in 1885**.
### 2. LYTTON'S RESENTED MEASURES (1876-80)
| Measure | Year | Why resented |
|---|---|---|
| **Delhi Durbar** | 1877 | Proclaimed **Queen Victoria Empress of India** while millions starved in the **1876-78 famine** |
| **Vernacular Press Act** | 1878 | Gagged the Indian-language press |
| **Arms Act** | 1878 | Disarmed Indians but **spared Europeans** |
| **Second Afghan War** | 1878-80 | Drained Indian revenue |
| **ICS age cut** | 1876 | Maximum age lowered from **21 to 19** (by Secretary of State **Salisbury**), shutting out Indians |
!TRAP "Which was NOT a measure of Lytton?" – the **Ilbert Bill (1883)** was **Ripon's**, not Lytton's.
- **Amrita Bazar Patrika** turned **English overnight in 1878** to escape the Vernacular Press Act.
### 3. ILBERT BILL CONTROVERSY (1883)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Viceroy | **Lord Ripon** |
| Drafted by | **Sir Courtenay Ilbert** (Law Member) |
| Provision | Allowed **Indian magistrates and sessions judges to try European British subjects** |
| Reaction | Strong **European protest** ("White Mutiny") |
| Compromise | A European accused could claim a **jury with at least half Europeans** |
| Passed | **25 January 1884**, in a weakened form |
- Lesson for Indians: **organised agitation works** – the Europeans' success pushed Indians towards an all-India body.

### 5. MADRAS AND ANDHRA ASSOCIATIONS
#### 5.1 Madras Native Association (26 February 1852)
- **First political body of Madras**; **first such organisation in South India**.
- **Founder and first president**: **Gazula Lakshmi Narasu Chetty**; secretary **Somasundaram Chettiyar**.
- Began as an **adjunct of the British Indian Association**, later independent.
- **Petition (1852)** to the British Parliament before the Charter renewal of 1853 – against the **ryotwari oppression**, **torture by revenue collectors** and heavy taxes such as **moturpha** (a tax on trades and occupations).
- Its agitation helped lead to the **Torture Commission (1854)**.
#### 5.2 Madras Mahajana Sabha (16 May 1884)
- **Second major body** of Madras.
- **President P. Rangaiah Naidu**; **secretary P. Anandacharlu**; other founders M. Viraraghavachariar, G. Subramania Iyer.
- Demanded **larger councils** and **more Indian representation**.
#### 5.3 Kakinada (Coconada) Literary Association
- Sent **memorandums (1884-85)** to **raise the ICS age limit** and hold **simultaneous examinations** in India.
#### 5.4 Krishna District Association (1892)
- Founded by **Ramaswamy Gupta**; **first district association in the Madras Presidency**.
- Formed on the **INC's advice** to set up district bodies.
- **First meeting at Machilipatnam** (as per the APPSC key).
- **Angalur conference (1900)** demanded **agricultural banks**.
- Its resolution of 1899 led to **Krishna Patrika** (first issue **1902**).
!NUM 1852 – 1884 – 1892 = Native Association – Mahajana Sabha – Krishna District Association; **1900 = Angalur conference**, not the founding.
| Body | Year | Exam-key point |
|---|---|---|
| Madras Native Association | 1852 | Correct pair (APPSC-GS 2025) |
| Madras Mahajana Sabha | 1884 | Correct pair (APPSC-GS 2019, 2025) |
| Krishna District Association | 1892 | "1900" is wrong (APPSC-GS 2021, 2025) |
- Stimulus in the South: **S.N. Banerjee's tour** for the Indian Association (**1878**).
### 6. SURENDRANATH BANERJEE
- **Passed the ICS (1869)** → disqualified over an age dispute → reinstated → **dismissed in 1874** on a minor irregularity.
- **Toured India** against the cut of the ICS maximum age from **21 to 19**.
- Founded the **Indian Association (1876)** with **A.M. Bose**.
- Convened the **Indian National Conference (1883, 1885)** → **merged with the INC in 1886**.
- Title: **"Rashtraguru"**; paper: **The Bengalee**.
- **INC president**: **Poona 1895** and **Ahmedabad 1902** (not Surat 1907).
!TRAP Banerjee's presidencies were Poona 1895 and Ahmedabad 1902 – **not Surat 1907**.

### 9. NATURE AND SIGNIFICANCE
- Early bodies were **regional** and led by **landlords or the urban elite**.
- They grew from local petitions to **all-India coordination** → INC 1885.
- **AP angle**: Madras Native Association (1852), Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884), Krishna District Association (1892).
