## U0734 | SPACE POLICY AND COMMERCIALISATION – INDIAN SPACE POLICY 2023, IN-SPACE, NSIL, PRIVATE LAUNCH SECTOR
### 1. SPACE SECTOR REFORMS 2020 AND FDI
- **June 2020 reforms** opened the space sector to **Non-Governmental Entities (NGEs)**.
#### 1.1 FDI limits by segment (automatic route)
| Segment | Automatic-route limit |
|---|---|
| **Satellite components and subsystems** manufacturing | **100%** |
| **Satellite manufacturing/operation, data products** | **Up to 74%** |
| **Launch vehicles and spaceports** | **Up to 49%** |
- Beyond the segment limits: **Government route**.
- Overall: FDI **up to 100%** in non-sensitive space segments.
!NUM 100 – 74 – 49 = components – satellites/data – launch vehicles/spaceports
!TRAP Launch vehicles get the LOWEST automatic limit (49%), not satellites.
### 2. INDIAN SPACE POLICY 2023
- **Approved by Union Cabinet: 6 April 2023**; **released by the Department of Space: 20 April 2023**.
#### 2.1 Roles assigned
| Entity | Role under the policy |
|---|---|
| **ISRO** | **Advanced R&D**, **deep space** and **human spaceflight**; to **move out of making operational and commercial satellites and rockets** over time |
| **IN-SPACe** | **Independent regulator** – promotes, **authorises** and supervises NGEs |
| **NSIL** | **Commercialises mature ISRO technology** through industry; launch and satellite services |
| **DoS** | Overall policy, international cooperation, **space diplomacy**, treaty compliance, coordination |
| **NGEs / private sector** | **End-to-end** space activity: satellites, rockets, launches, ground stations, services, data |
- **Sept 2026:** ISRO's nine employee associations sought clarity on outsourcing; ISRO and IN-SPACe said ISRO **"will not be privatised or reduced"** to a regulatory body (7-8 Sep 2026).
!TRAP The regulator is IN-SPACe, NOT ISRO.
### 3. ORGANISATIONS – ANTRIX, NSIL, IN-SPACE
| Body | Set up | Status / model | HQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antrix Corporation** | **September 1992** | Private limited company; **Miniratna (2008)**; **supply-driven** – markets ISRO products, transponder leasing, remote-sensing data, consultancy | — |
| **NSIL (NewSpace India Limited)** | **2019** | **CPSE under DoS**; **demand-driven** | **Bengaluru** |
| **IN-SPACe** | **2020** | **Autonomous single-window agency under DoS** | **Ahmedabad** |
!PAIR IN-SPACe | Ahmedabad | regulator || NSIL | Bengaluru | commercial arm
#### 3.1 IN-SPACe – authorisations
- **Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre**; gives NGEs access to DoS facilities.
- **Chairman: Dr Pawan Kumar Goenka** (Sep 2026).
- **834 applications** received, **499 processed** (294 satellites, 103 launch vehicles).
- Authorised the first private launch **Vikram-S (2022)** and **Agnikul's launch**.
- Approved the **PPP EOS constellation** led by **Pixxel** with **Dhruva Space, SatSure Analytics India, PierSight Space** (20 Jul 2026).
- Enabled **SSLV technology transfer** to private consortia (Feb 2026).
- Runs the **IN-SPACe Seed Fund Scheme** and the **Technology Adoption Fund**.
### 4. NSIL – DEMAND-DRIVEN MISSIONS
- **Demand-driven model:** NSIL **builds, owns and operates** satellites against **assured customer demand** (Antrix was supply-driven).
| Mission | Fact |
|---|---|
| **GSAT-24 (2022)** | **India's first demand-driven** satellite; launched from **Kourou, French Guiana**; DTH capacity rented to **Tata Play** |
| **GSAT-N2 (GSAT-20) (2024)** | **Fully funded and owned by NSIL**; Ka-band broadband and in-flight connectivity; **SpaceX Falcon 9** (Nov 2024) |
| **LVM3 (2022)** | **First dedicated commercial mission** – **36 OneWeb** satellites |
- **10** in-orbit communication satellites transferred to NSIL in 2022; now **11 satellites, ~300 transponders, 150+ leases**.
- Launches: **141 satellites (138 customer, 3 Indian)** as of July 2026.
- **70 Technology Transfer Agreements** (Dec 2025).
!KEY APPSC 2024: GSAT-N2 = first demand-driven satellite fully funded and owned by NSIL.
### 5. PRIVATE LAUNCH AND PROPULSION FIRMS
| Firm (base) | Product | FIRST / date |
|---|---|---|
| **Skyroot Aerospace (Hyderabad)** | **Vikram-S** (suborbital) | **Mission Prarambh, 18 Nov 2022** – **India's first private rocket launch**; 89.5 km, 155 s, 3 payloads |
| Skyroot Aerospace | **Vikram-1** – **4-stage**, up to **350 kg to LEO** | **Mission Aagaman, 18 Jul 2026** – **India's first private orbital rocket** |
| Skyroot Aerospace | **Kalam** series | Solid motors named after A.P.J. Abdul Kalam |
| **Agnikul Cosmos (Chennai, IIT Madras)** | **Agnibaan SOrTeD**; **Agnilet** | **World's first single-piece 3D-printed semi-cryogenic engine**; 2024 launch from India's first private pad **Dhanush**, Sriharikota |
| **Bellatrix Aerospace (Bengaluru)** | Indigenous **green propulsion system**; electric thrusters | Demonstrated on **PSLV-C58 (POEM-3, 1 Jan 2024)**; developed under the **Technology Development Fund (TDF)** of DRDO |
- With Vikram-1, India became the **3rd country (after US and China)** with private orbital launch capability.
- Vikram-1 carried Skyroot's SCOPE, Cosmoserve's Embrace and **SOLARAS** (built with **Grahaa Space**) bearing **VIT-AP University's VISWA-M** solar-spectrum payload, to 450 km LEO.
- Few private firms worldwide have reached orbit: SpaceX, Rocket Lab, Firefly, some Chinese firms.
!TRAP Prarambh = Vikram-S, suborbital (2022); Aagaman = Vikram-1, orbital (2026).
!TRAP Bellatrix is based in Bengaluru, not Hyderabad.

### 7. PRIVATE INFRASTRUCTURE AT SDSC-SHAR
- **Sriharikota (AP)** – **India's only operational spaceport**; Agnikul's private pad **Dhanush** and its own mission control are here.
- Tirupati Space City is a future plan that builds on Sriharikota.
### 8. SPACE LEGISLATION
| Instrument | Year | Provision / status |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft Space Activities Bill** | **2017** | First put out; **licensing** of private space activity |
| Space Activities Bill | **Finalised May 2025** | Boosts private participation; **statutory powers to IN-SPACe** |
| **Liability Convention** | **1972** | Liability on the **launching State** |
- Status: **India still has no Space Activities Act**; the sector runs on policies and guidelines.
- Gaps: no statutory framework on **liability, dispute resolution, risk-sharing**; **no sovereign-backed insurance** (Jun 2026); expected law to cover dual-use regulation and treaty compliance.
### 9. SPACE ECONOMY – SIZE, TARGETS, FUNDS
| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Current size | About **US$8.4 billion** (~2% global share) |
| Target (**IN-SPACe Decadal Vision**) | **US$44 billion by 2033** (~**8%** global share) |
| Other goals | **50 launches a year**; about **2 lakh jobs** |
| Downstream | Data services to give 70% of future revenue |
| **Venture Capital Fund** | **₹1,000 crore**, 5 years, managed through **SIDBI** |
| **Technology Adoption Fund** | Run by **IN-SPACe** |
| SpIN (2022) | First space-innovation platform |
| Space start-ups | **More than 440** (Dr Jitendra Singh, Aug 2026), from single digits in 2014 |
- Concerns: >90% of space-grade chips imported; 18 collision-avoidance manoeuvres in 2025 (LEO congestion).
### 10. ANDHRA PRADESH – SPACE POLICY 4.0 AND SPACE CITIES
- **AP Space Policy 4.0** with **dedicated space cities** for space manufacturing; targets **₹25,000 crore** investment (2025-35) and **5,000 direct + 30,000 indirect jobs**.
- **Tirupati Space City:** about **3,000 acres near Sriharikota** – launch-vehicle assembly, satellite manufacturing, avionics; aims at about **US$4 billion** investment (Nara Lokesh, SPIEF, 5 Jun 2026).
- **Lepakshi Space City:** the second space city, for R&D.
- **AP Space Tech Summit-2026:** **Vignan's University, Vadlamudi (Guntur)** (23 Jan 2026).
