## U0275 | INDIAN INDEPENDENCE ACT 1947 AND THE CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY
### 1. DEMAND FOR A CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY (1934–1946)
#### 1.1 Timeline of the Demand
| Year | Person / Event | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1934 | **M.N. Roy** | First put forward the **idea** of a Constituent Assembly |
| May 1934 | **Swaraj Party** (parliamentary wing of Congress), **Ranchi** Conference | **First formal demand**; claimed the right to self-determination |
| — | Congress, **Patna** | Congress officially demanded it |
| 1938 | **Jawaharlal Nehru** | Clear statement supporting a Constituent Assembly |
| 1940 | **August Offer** | British **accepted the principle** |
| 1942 | **Cripps Mission** | Final proposal for an **elected** Constituent Assembly |
| 1946 | **Cabinet Mission Plan** | Assembly actually **constituted** |
- The first explicit demand for Fundamental Rights came earlier, in the **Constitution of India Bill, 1895**.
!TRAP Idea first = M.N. Roy (1934); first formal demand = Swaraj Party (Ranchi, May 1934). Both answers appear in keys, so read the question word: "idea" or "demand".

### 2. COMPOSITION OF THE CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY
#### 2.1 Seats
| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Planned total | **389** |
| British India | **296** = 292 Governor's provinces + 4 Chief Commissioners' provinces |
| Princely States | **93** |
| Ratio | **One member per 10 lakh** population (about 1 per million) |
| After partition | **299** |
- Provincial seats were split among **Muslims, Sikhs and General** in proportion to population.
#### 2.2 Method of Election (July–August 1946)
- Elected **indirectly** by members of the **Provincial Legislative Assemblies** (set up under the 1935 Act; provincial elections January 1946).
- **Proportional representation** with **single transferable vote**.
- **No adult franchise**: restricted franchise of the **GoI Act 1935** (tax, education, property).
- Princely representatives were **nominated by rulers**; the 93 seats were at first empty. States began joining from the **third session, April 1947**; on **28 April 1947** six states joined (Baroda, Bikaner, Jaipur, Patiala, Rewa, Udaipur).
- Nature: **partly elected, partly nominated**.
!NUM 1946 results: Congress 208 | Muslim League 73 | Others 15
#### 2.3 Women Members
- **15** women, including Ammu Swaminathan, Annie Mascarene, Sarojini Naidu, Dakshayani Velayudhan, Durgabai Deshmukh, Hansa Mehta, Kamala Choudhury, Leela Roy, Malati Choudhury.
!TRAP Elections were July–August 1946, NOT June–July; and NOT on universal adult franchise.

### 4. INDIAN INDEPENDENCE ACT, 1947
#### 4.1 Main Provisions
- Ended **British rule** (British sovereignty); combined sovereignty with **partition**.
- Created **two Dominions**: **India** and **Pakistan**.
- India a **Dominion from 15 August 1947**, British monarch as **nominal head**.
- **Governor-General** (common or separate) to act on the **advice of Dominion ministers**.
- **Crown lost its veto** over dominion laws.
- **Paramountcy lapsed**: about **565** princely states theoretically free.
- Constituent Assembly given **dual powers**: constitution-making and legislative; the **Central Legislative Assembly ended**.
#### 4.2 Crown's Residual Role and End of Dominion Status
- Dominion status lasted until India became a **Republic on 26 January 1950**.
- **Last Viceroy and first Governor-General** of independent India = **Lord Mountbatten**.
- **Last and only Indian Governor-General** = **C. Rajagopalachari** (June 1948 – January 1950).
- Rajagopalachari assented to the **Madras Estates (Abolition) Act** on 2 April 1949 — the first law in independent India to get the GG's assent.
#### 4.3 Significance
- Congress accepted **dominion status** for a **quick, peaceful transfer** of power and a **strong Centre**.
!KEY Two Dominions + lapse of paramountcy + dual role of the Assembly = the three exam provisions of the Act.
### 5. PRINCELY STATES UNDER THE ACT
| Instrument | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Instrument of Accession** | Ceded **defence, external affairs and communications** to the dominion |
| **Standstill Agreement** | Existing administrative arrangements continued until final terms |
- In theory free; in practice expected to join **India or Pakistan**; no option of independence.
- **Kashmir** acceded **26 October 1947** after the tribal invasion; UN-arranged ceasefire **1 January 1949**.
- **Hyderabad** signed a Standstill Agreement in **November 1947**.
!TRAP The Instrument of Accession did NOT cede finance.

### 8. KEY DATES AND OTHER FUNCTIONS
#### 8.1 Assembly Chronology
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **9 Dec 1946** | **First meeting**, Constitution Hall, New Delhi; Sinha presided; 211 attended; **Muslim League boycotted** |
| 11 Dec 1946 | Rajendra Prasad elected President |
| **13 Dec 1946** | **Objectives Resolution** moved by **Nehru** (fifth meeting) |
| 23 Dec 1946 | First session ended |
| **22 Jan 1947** | Objectives Resolution **adopted unanimously**; its revised form is the **Preamble** |
| **22 Jul 1947** | **National Flag** adopted |
| 29 Aug 1947 | Drafting Committee constituted |
| 17 Nov 1947 | Sat as Dominion Legislature; Mavalankar Speaker |
| 21 Feb 1948 | Draft Constitution published |
| May 1949 | Commonwealth membership ratified |
| 14 Sep 1949 | **Munshi-Ayyangar formula** on Hindi (now Hindi Diwas) |
| 14 Nov 1949 | Third reading begins |
| **26 Nov 1949** | **Constitution adopted** (last day of 11th session) |
| **24 Jan 1950** | Final meeting: **National Anthem**, **National Song**, **Prasad elected first President**, signatures |
| **26 Jan 1950** | **Commencement**: Republic of India |
- **26 January** chosen to commemorate **Purna Swaraj** (Lahore Congress 1929, observed 1930).
- Provisions in force on **26 Nov 1949**: citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament, transitional articles (Arts. 5–9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391–393).
- **Signing**: at the last sitting, **24 Jan 1950** (**284** members signed); adoption was on **26 Nov 1949**.
#### 8.2 Symbols and Language
- Flag wheel: **Ashoka Chakra**, **24 spokes**, symbolises **Justice**.
- Anthem settled by **Prasad's statement**, not by a vote; formal rendition **52 seconds**.
- **Vande Mataram** (Bankimchandra) declared National Song, **honoured equally** with the Anthem; original language **Sanskrit**.
- **Munshi-Ayyangar formula**: Hindi in Devanagari; **15-year** transition with English continued; **14 other languages** in the Eighth Schedule.
- Latest (2026): Parliament amended the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act** to give the National Song the same statutory protection as the Anthem. **Art. 51A(a)** names flag and anthem, NOT the song.
!TRAP "Vande Mataram originally in Bengali" is incorrect; Munshi-Ayyangar formula did NOT end English immediately.
### 9. DUAL ROLE OF THE ASSEMBLY
| Role | Chaired by |
|---|---|
| Constitution-maker (fully sovereign) | **Dr Rajendra Prasad** |
| Dominion Legislature | **G.V. Mavalankar** |
- After 26 January 1950 it continued as **Provisional Parliament** until the 1951–52 elections; ceased **17 April 1952**.
### 10. TIME TAKEN AND SIZE
- Time: **2 years, 11 months, 18 days** (APPSC GS 2024 key; some notes say 17 days).
- **11 sessions**, **165 sitting days**; plus one meeting on 24 Jan 1950 for signatures = **12** in all.
- Draft considered **114 days**; amendments **7,635 proposed**, **2,473 discussed**.
| Stage | Size |
|---|---|
| B.N. Rau's draft | 243 Articles, 13 Schedules |
| Draft Constitution | 315 Articles |
| Original Constitution | **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules** |
| Today (2026) | about 470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules |
### 11. LINGUISTIC PROVINCES AND THE AP ANGLE
- Madras Assembly resolved, four months before independence, to divide the Presidency into **Andhra, Tamil, Karnataka, Kerala**.
- Nehru accepted the Andhra demand on **27 Nov 1947**; Andhra was dropped from the Draft of 21 Feb 1948.
- **Linguistic Provinces Commission** (17 June 1948): **S.K. Dar**, retired Allahabad HC judge.
- **Dar Commission** and **JVP Committee** both rejected linguistic states for the time being; JVP left room for Andhra → **Andhra State, 1953**.
### 12. REMARKS AND CRITICISMS
#### 12.1 Who Said What
| Statement | Speaker |
|---|---|
| Constitutional morality "has to be cultivated" (4 Nov 1948, from George Grote) | **Ambedkar** |
| "Grammar of anarchy"; "a life of contradictions" (25 Nov 1949) | **Ambedkar** |
| Constitution a sacred document | **Ambedkar** |
| "A Constitution, like a machine, is a lifeless thing" (26 Nov 1949) | **Rajendra Prasad** |
| "One-party body in an essentially one-party country"; "Congress was India" | **Granville Austin** |
| "A body of Hindus" | **Viscount Simon** |
| "Drifting Committee" | **Naziruddin Ahmed** |
| Represented "only one major community" | **Winston Churchill** |
- **Constitution Day** (Samvidhan Divas), 26 November, observed since **2015** (Ambedkar's 125th birth-anniversary year).
- Maulana Azad wanted adult franchise delayed 15 years; Prasad and Nehru opposed.
#### 12.2 Criticisms
- **Not representative** (no adult franchise); **not sovereign** (British creation); **time-consuming** (about 3 years vs about 4 months for the USA); **Congress-dominated**; **lawyer-politician and Hindu dominated**; **borrowed / "patchwork"**.
