## U0454 | FIVE YEAR PLANS I TO XII – OBJECTIVES, TARGETS, ACHIEVEMENTS AND FAILURES OF EACH
### 1. PLANS I TO V AND THE PLAN HOLIDAY (1951-79)
| Plan | Period | Model | Main objective / tagline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **1951-56** (launched **1 April 1951**) | **Harrod-Domar** | **Agriculture, irrigation, power** (Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud); food, Partition rehabilitation, price stability |
| **2nd** | **1956-61** (began 1956, year of IPR) | **Nehru-Mahalanobis** | Rapid **industrialisation**, heavy and basic industries; **socialistic pattern of society** |
| **3rd** | **1961-66** ("**Gadgil Yojana**") | Sandy-Chakravarty | **Self-reliant, self-generating economy** ("make the economy independent"); self-reliance with priority to agriculture |
| **Plan Holiday** | **1 April 1966 – 31 March 1969** | — | **Three Annual Plans**; **Green Revolution (1966-67)**; agriculture and industry equal weight |
| **4th** | **1969-74** (finally launched **1969**) | **Manne-Rudra** | **Growth with stability and progressive achievement of self-reliance**; 14 banks nationalised 1969 |
| **5th** | **1974-79** (ended **1978**) | **Dhar** (final draft by **D.P. Dhar**) | **Removal of poverty (Garibi Hatao)** and **self-reliance**; control of inflation; zero net foreign aid |
- "**Garibi Hatao**" – Indira Gandhi's slogan from the **1971 elections**; became the **Fifth Plan's** basic objective.
- **Second Plan** – the "Mahalanobis Plan" (two- and four-sector model); real planning began here; first proposed **land reforms through cooperative land management**.
- **Fourth Plan** – self-sustaining development first adopted; **Growth Centre Approach** for regional disparities.
- **Fifth Plan** – serious concern for **unemployment** begins; anti-poverty programmes first started.
### 2. PLANS VI TO XII (1980-2017)
| Plan | Period | Main objective / tagline |
|---|---|---|
| **6th** | **1980-85** | **Poverty eradication and technological self-reliance**; power significant |
| **7th** | **1985-90** | "**Food, Work and Productivity**"; employment thrust |
| **Annual Plans** | **1990-92** | Employment and social transformation (political instability) |
| **8th** | **1992-97** (April 1992 – March 1997) | **Human resource development**; human development at the core; **indicative planning**; **John W. Miller** model; shift from heavy industry to **infrastructure** |
| **9th** | **1997-2002** | "**Growth with Social Justice and Equity**" (United Front Government); final NDC approval **19 Feb 1999** |
| **10th** | **2002-07** (1 April 2002 – 31 March 2007; NDC approved Dec 2002) | **8% GDP growth**; **double per capita income in 10 years**; **first plan with monitorable targets** |
| **11th** | **2007-12** | "**Towards Faster and More Inclusive Growth**" |
| **12th** | **2012-17** (launched 1 April 2012) | "**Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth**"; the **last plan** |
!TRAP Eleventh = faster and **more inclusive**; Twelfth = faster, **sustainable** and more inclusive; Ninth = growth with social justice (not the Eleventh).
### 3. PLANS CUT SHORT OR TERMINATED EARLY
| Plan | Cause | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Third** | **1962 and 1965 wars** + **1965-66 drought** | **Plan Holiday (1966-69)** |
| **Fifth** | Emergency, elections; **Janata government** ended it a year early (1978) | **Rolling Plan (1978-80)** |
| **Eighth** | **1990-91 political instability + BoP crisis** | Postponed two years; **Annual Plans 1990-92** |
- **Rolling Plan** = the Janata government's Sixth Plan (1978); ended 1980; new Sixth Plan by Congress. It had a plan for **one year**, a plan for **3, 4 or 5 years** renewed every year, and a **perspective plan** for 10, 15 or 20 years.

### 6. GROWTH: TARGET VS ACHIEVED
#### 6.1 Plan-wise Table (%)
| Plan | Target | Achieved |
|---|---|---|
| I | 2.1 | **3.6** |
| II | 4.5 | 4.27 |
| III | 5.6 | **2.2 (lowest; biggest shortfall)** |
| Plan Holiday | — | 3.69 |
| IV | 5.7 | 3.3 |
| V | 4.4 | 4.8 |
| VI | 5.2 | 5.7 |
| VII | 5.0 | 6.0 |
| VIII | 5.6 | 6.8 |
| IX | 6.5 | 5.4 |
| X | 8.0 (cut to 7 at mid-term) | 7.6 |
| XI | **9.0** | **8.0 (highest)** |
| XII | 9 (Approach Paper) → **8** (NDC, Dec 2012) | — |
- **Plans that beat targets:** **I, V, VI, VII, VIII**.
- **Highest GDP and per-capita growth:** **Eleventh Plan** (per capita GDP ~**7.6%** a year).
- Third Plan agriculture grew **negatively (−1.0%** a year).
- Eighth Plan: target growth 5.6%, industrial growth **7.5%**; per capita output grew 3.9%; agriculture 3.6% (target 3.5%); industry 8.5%; maximum cumulative real GNP growth among plans asked; growth said to touch ~9% at peak.
- Tenth Plan: **investment rate 32.10% > savings rate 26.84%**.
- Ninth Plan: ICOR ~**4.3** envisaged.
- Higher average growth with rising saving ratio: Sixth 5.7%, Seventh 6.0%, Eighth 6.8%.
#### 6.2 GDP Record – Highest and Lowest Years
- **1950-51:** ~3.6%.
- **Lowest year since 1961 (excluding Covid):** **1979, −5.2%** (drought, second oil shock); 2020-21 **−5.8%**; other negative years 1965-66, 1972-73.
- 1990-91: **1.1%**; 1991-92 below 1%; 1992-94 5%; 1994-95 6.3%.
- 2000-02: about half the Tenth Plan target; 2002-03 ~4% (poor monsoon); 2003-04 **8.2%**.
- **Highest average period:** **2004-05 to 2012-13** (boom peak ~**8.5%** in 2003-08); 1980s ~5.1-5.2%.
- "**Decade of jobless growth**" – **1991-2000**; organised-sector jobs grew **0.36%** vs **2.6%** target.
### 7. SECOND PLAN – OUTLAY AND CRITICISM
- Planned public outlay **Rs 4,800 crore** (private Rs 3,100 crore); actual **Rs 4,600 crore** (Rs 3,650 crore investment + Rs 950 crore current).
- **Why pruned:** food and capital shortages, **inflation**, closed-economy assumption, **1957-58 foreign-exchange crisis** (not falling inflation).
| Factor | Criticism of Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | Neglected → **food shortages** |
| Assumption | **Closed economy** |
| Employment | Capital-intensive industry → **few jobs** |
| Prices | **High inflation** by late 1950s |
### 8. ELEVENTH AND TWELFTH PLANS
| Feature | Eleventh (2007-12) | Twelfth (2012-17) |
|---|---|---|
| Theme | "Towards Faster and More Inclusive Growth" | "Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth" |
| GDP target | **9%** (rising to 10% by end) | 9% → **8%** |
| Agriculture | **4%** | **4%** (agriculture, forestry, fisheries) |
| Health | **IMR 28**; **MMR 1 per 1,000** live births; **TFR 2.1** | **IMR 25** |
| Targets | **27 national** monitorable; **13 State-level** | 25 monitorable |
| Outlay | **Rs 36,44,718 crore** | **Rs 76,69,807 crore** |
- **Eleventh inclusive-growth strategy:** **continued reform and a competitive private sector** (not withdrawal of the private sector); more emphasis on agriculture; health, education, skills; infrastructure; investment **37% → 39%** of GDP.
- Eleventh: cut poverty head-count ratio by **10 points**; **7 crore** new jobs; largest job growth expected in **construction (8.27%)**; education theme = compulsory elementary education; social services highest allocation, then energy.
- **Twelfth:** reduce poverty and regional inequality; health, education, skills up by **1.2 points of GDP**; fewer CSS with a **flexi-fund**; social-sector PPPs; plan/non-plan distinction reviewed by the **Rangarajan Committee**.
- 11th Plan 9% target hit by infrastructure fund shortfall; 12th Plan IMR 25 target missed for lack of funds.
!TRAP **27** monitorable targets (XI) vs **25** (XII); inclusive growth does **not** mean withdrawing the private sector.
### 9. ACHIEVEMENTS OF PLANNING
- Average growth (I–XI) **~4.5%** vs **~1%** before Independence; agriculture 2-3% (vs 0.3%); industry 6-8%.
- **Industrial base:** **Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela** steel plants; **Chittaranjan** (locomotives), **Perambur** (coaches); **IITs**; Atomic Energy Commission.
- **Food self-sufficiency:** Green Revolution (1966-67), **FCI (1965)**, National Seeds Corporation.
- **Institutions:** **SBI** (from Imperial Bank, First Plan period), NABARD (1982).
- First Plan succeeded mainly due to **good harvests** in its last two years.
- Poverty fell from **48.3% (1977-78)** to **37.4% (1983-84)**.
### 10. FAILURES OF PLANNING
| Failure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Poverty** | Persistent poverty and inequality; Eighth Plan – "the poor became poorer" |
| **Unemployment** | Job growth lagged (Eighth: 2% vs 2.6%) |
| **Regional imbalance** | Across and **within** States |
| Industry | Below target in several plans |
| Centralisation | Diluted people's participation |
| Implementation | Eighth Plan public-sector share 34.3% vs 45.2% target; overlapping rural electrification schemes |
### 11. AP AND THE FIVE YEAR PLANS
#### 11.1 AP Projects and Programmes
| Project / programme | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Nagarjuna Sagar** | Foundation by **Nehru, 10 Dec 1955**; dam built 1955-67 |
| **Srisailam** | Construction began **1960** (power project); opened **1981** |

| **Jalayagnam** | **2004**; AP irrigation mission to complete major and medium projects |
| **RKVY** | 2007; Rs 1,862 crore allotted |
#### 11.2 AP in the Eleventh Plan
- **Social sector** first priority; **education** highest within it.
- GSDP target **9.5%**, achieved **5.28%**; industry **7.26%** vs **10%** target.
- Per capita income **Rs 68,970** (above national); agriculture and allied share of GSDP **21.27%**.
#### 11.3 AP Plan Finance
- State's own resources (current revenue balance, PSU surpluses, borrowings) + **Normal Central Assistance**.
- **Gadgil formula (1969)** → **Gadgil-Mukherjee formula (1991)**, basis from the Eighth Plan:
| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Population (1971)** | **60%** |
| Per capita income | 25% |
| Fiscal performance | 7.5% |
| Special problems | 7.5% |
- **Special Category** = **90% grant : 10% loan**; **General Category** = **30% grant : 70% loan** – **AP was General Category**.
#### 11.4 After the Plans
- **NITI Aayog** replaced the Planning Commission on **1 Jan 2015**; plans ended **2017**.
- **Swarna Andhra Vision 2047** launched **13 Dec 2024**; aligned with Viksit Bharat 2047; economic regions **Visakhapatnam, Amaravati, Tirupati** (not Kurnool).
- NABARD AP State Focus Paper 2026-27: credit plan **Rs 5.11 lakh crore** (up ~20%); Rs 2.55 lakh crore agriculture; Rs 1.64 lakh crore MSMEs (6 May 2026).
- Kerala still makes Annual Plans; declared extreme poverty ended Nov 2025.
