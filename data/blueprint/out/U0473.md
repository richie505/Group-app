## U0473 | POVERTY – CONCEPTS (ABSOLUTE, RELATIVE, CHRONIC), POVERTY LINE DEBATES
### 1. TYPES OF POVERTY
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Absolute** | Lack of **minimum resources for survival**; set by a **fixed consumption / calorie norm** (India's focus); defined by the UN at the **World Summit for Social Development, Copenhagen, 1995** |
| **Relative** | Income **well below the median** income of society; compares fractile groups; used in **developed economies** (persists in USA, UK) – NOT a fixed calorie norm |
| **Subjective** | A person's **own perception** of their position |
| **Chronic** | Lasts over **long periods or across generations** |
| **Transient / cyclical** | **Temporary** – shocks, seasons, cycles |
| **Culture of poverty** | **Oscar Lewis** (American anthropologist): poverty handed down as a **way of life** |
- Types asked: **absolute, relative, subjective** – NOT "functional".
- Grouping: by **base information** (objective / subjective) and by **threshold scale** (absolute / relative).
- **Amartya Sen**: **capability approach**.
- Absolute poverty is measured by the **World Bank line** and calorie norm (APSES 2024-25).
- **Unemployment leads to poverty**: lack of income raises the number below the line.
!TRAP Fixed calorie norm = absolute poverty, not relative.
### 2. POVERTY MEASURES – FGT FAMILY
| Measure | Captures | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Head Count Ratio** | **Incidence** – share below the line | HC = Nq / N |
| **Poverty Gap Index** | **Depth** – average shortfall | (1/n) Σ (Z − yi)/Z |
| **Squared Poverty Gap** | **Severity** – more weight to the poorest | (1/n) Σ [(Z − yi)/Z]² |
- All three = **Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT)** family.
- If the poor become poorer but their number is unchanged → **HCR unchanged; PGI changes**.
- **Engel's Law**: as income rises, the **share** spent on food **falls** (absolute amount may rise).
!KEY Incidence – depth – severity = HCR – PGI – squared poverty gap.
### 3. POVERTY LINE IN INDIA – BASIS
- **Survey**: **NSSO** household consumption expenditure survey (now **HCES**, MoSPI).
- Basis = **consumption expenditure (MPCE), not income** – incomes of self-employed and daily-wage workers are hard to judge and change with seasons.
- Poor = consumption below the line (APPSC-GS 2024: statements A and B correct).
- Poverty in India defined in terms of **calorie intake**: **2,400 kcal rural / 2,100 kcal urban** per capita per day (ICMR, Alagh).
- **Poverty Line Basket (PLB)**: socially accepted minimum basket of basic needs bought in the market.
- BPL = whether a person can obtain a **minimum prescribed food basket**.
- **Literacy is NOT used** to decide the poverty line.
- Lines differ across time and countries because **basic needs vary by region and over time** (UPPSC 2025: A and R true, R explains A).
- Roots of **urban poverty lie in rural areas**: low rural education → migrants get poor jobs.
- Official poverty 1999-2000 (Mixed Recall Period): about **26% (26.1)**.
### 4. SOCIAL INDICATORS OF POVERTY
- Poverty shows in **illiteracy**, lack of **safe drinking water**, poor **housing**, and lack of **jobs**; these are social indicators used to judge deprivation.
- The poverty line itself uses **consumption**, not literacy or other social indicators.
- Shift to **multidimensional measures (MPI)** covers health, education and living standards.
### 5. TENDULKAR VS RANGARAJAN (2011-12)
| Line (2011-12) | Rural | Urban | All-India poverty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tendulkar** | **₹816/month** (~₹27/day) | **₹1,000/month** (~₹33/day) | **21.9%** (rural 25.7, urban 13.7) |
| **Rangarajan** | **₹972/month (₹32/day)** | **₹1,407/month (₹47/day)** | **29.5%** |
- Rangarajan rural line **₹32 a day** (APPSC-GS 2025).
- NSS **68th round** (2011-12, Tendulkar method, Planning Commission): "33.35% below poverty line" is **not true**.
- **Recall periods**: Tendulkar moved from **URP to MRP**; **MMRP** (7-day recall for some food items) records 10-12% higher consumption → lower poverty; **first used by Rangarajan**.
- Tendulkar line criticised as **too low**.
!TRAP ₹816 / ₹1,000 = Tendulkar 2011-12 (not 2010-11, not Rangarajan).
### 6. POVERTY LINE DEBATES AND LATEST ESTIMATES
- **HCES 2022-23 and 2023-24** (MoSPI): first full MPCE update in over a decade; urban food share ₹48 → ₹39 per ₹100 (rural from ₹59).
- **Tendulkar-line poverty: 4.7% (2022-23) → 2.3% (2023-24)**.
- RBI updated the Rangarajan line with HCES 2022-23 using a PLB-weighted price index.
- Critique: subsistence lines hide **vulnerability just above** the threshold; debate on income vs consumption vs multidimensional measures.
- UNDP simulation: West Asia war could raise poverty 23.9% → 24.2%.
- **AP**: HCR **9.20%** (2011-12) vs India **21.92%**.
### 7. INTERNATIONAL POVERTY LINE – WORLD BANK
| Year set | Line (per person per day) | PPP base |
|---|---|---|
| 1990 | **$1** ("dollar a day") | 1985 PPP |
| 2008 | **$1.25** | 2005 PPP |
| **Oct 2015** | **$1.90** (APPSC 2018) | **2011 PPP** |
| 2022 | **$2.15** | 2017 PPP |
| **June 2025** | **$3.00** (latest) | **2021 PPP** |
- Lower-middle-income line: $3.65 → **$4.20**; India under $4.20: **57.7% → 23.9%** (World Bank Fall 2025 brief); share below it fell from over 50% to about 30%.
- Global ($2.15): about **9.2%** of the world (over **700 mn**) in extreme poverty.
- World Development Report 2005: **80%** of Indians earned less than US$2 a day.
!TRAP $2.15 was the 2022 revision; $3 came in 2025.
### 8. COUNTRIES WITH THE LARGEST NUMBER OF POOR
- **World Bank 2016 (APPSC 2016 key)**: **India** had the largest number below the international line.
- Old $1.25 line: India's poor **421 mn (1981) → 456 mn (2005)**.
- World Bank (2018) noted **Nigeria** may already have overtaken India as the country with the most extreme poor.
