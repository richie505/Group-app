## U0613 | OCEAN MOVEMENTS – WAVES, TIDES AND OCEAN CURRENTS OF THE WORLD
### 1. WAVES, TIDES AND CURRENTS – THE DIFFERENCE
| Basis | Waves | Tides | Ocean currents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Oscillations; transfer **energy, not water** | **Vertical** rise and fall, once or twice a day | Continuous **horizontal** flow |
| Driver | **Wind** | Gravity of **Moon and Sun** | Winds, density differences, **Coriolis force** |
- Currents affect climate, fisheries and navigation. **Storm surges** are irregular and weather-driven, not tides.
### 2. WAVES AND TSUNAMIS
#### 2.1 Parts and Types of Waves
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wavelength** | Crest to crest |
| **Wave height** | Crest to trough |
| Period | Time between crests |
| **Swells** | Slow, steady, long-period waves from distant storms |
| **Breakers** | Break when depth < **half the wavelength** |
| **Rip current** | Narrow, fast channel flowing **away from the shore** |
| **Kallakkadal** | Kerala swell surge from far southern storms |
- **Tsunami** (Japanese **"harbour wave"**): caused by sudden **displacement of the sea floor** (quake, landslide, volcano), not wind.
### 3. TIDES – CAUSES, TYPES AND TIMING
#### 3.1 Causes
- Gravitational pull of the **Moon (greater)** and Sun (lesser); **centrifugal force** counters gravity.
- **Direct tide** = bulge facing the Moon (gravity); **indirect tide** = opposite side (centrifugal force).
- Tidal water channelled into bays/estuaries forms **tidal currents**.
#### 3.2 Spring vs Neap Tides
| Basis | Spring tide | Neap tide |
|---|---|---|
| Sun–Earth–Moon | **In a line** | **Right angles** |
| Moon phase | **New / full moon** | **First / third quarter** |
| Tidal range | **Maximum** | **Minimum** (high tide lower, low tide higher) |
#### 3.3 Types and Timing
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Semi-diurnal** | Two highs + two lows a day (most common) |
| Diurnal | One high + one low a day |
| Mixed | Unequal heights |
- High tides recur about every **12 h 25 min**, so high tide comes about **50 minutes** later each day (a Chennai 8.00 am high tide comes later next day).
- **Perigean tides**: unusually high when the Moon is closest to Earth.
### 4. TIDAL RANGE PLACES AND TIDAL ENERGY
| Place | Tidal range / feature |
|---|---|
| **Bay of Fundy** (Canada) | **World's greatest tidal range** |
| **Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay)**, Gujarat | 5–10 m; ~**7,000 MW**; main tidal-energy area (**Bhavnagar**) |
| Gulf of Kutch, Gujarat | 5–8 m |
| Sundarbans (Ganges delta) | ~5 m |
| **Chandipur** (Odisha) | Sea recedes a few km at ebb tide |
- Gulfs with **wide fronts and narrow rears** get the highest tides (funnelling).
- Tidal energy: **tidal range** (barrage across an estuary) vs **tidal stream** (underwater turbines).
### 5. OCEAN CURRENTS – DRIVERS
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Planetary winds** | Main driver; **trades** → equatorial currents west; **westerlies** → drifts east |
| **Coriolis force** | Deflects **right in N**, left in S; coastlines and floor relief also steer |
| Solar heating | Water expands near equator → slope |
| Temperature, salinity | Cold, salty water sinks at poles, moves slowly to equator (**thermohaline**) |
- **Equatorial Counter Current** flows **west to east** between the N and S Equatorial Currents through the **doldrums**, because their convergence piles water in the west.
- **Warm** currents: equator → poles, on **east coasts** (low-mid latitudes); **cold**: poles → equator, on **west coasts**. Currents keep Earth's heat balance.
### 6. OCEAN CURRENTS OF THE WORLD
#### 6.1 Warm or Cold – Full List
| Current | Type | Ocean / region |
|---|---|---|
| **Gulf Stream** | Warm | N. Atlantic; starts in Gulf of Mexico as **Florida** current |
| North Atlantic Drift, Norwegian, Florida | Warm | N. Atlantic |
| **Kuroshio** | Warm | North Pacific (Japan) |
| **Brazil** | Warm | South Atlantic |
| **Agulhas**, Mozambique | Warm | Indian (SE Africa) |
| East Australian | Warm | South Pacific |
| **Labrador** | Cold | N. Atlantic (Canada) |
| **Canary** | Cold | N. Atlantic (NW Africa) |
| **Benguela** | Cold | **S. Atlantic** (Namibia) |

!TRAP Odd-ones: **Humboldt** not warm (APPSC 2019); **Benguela** not North Atlantic; **Canary** not Pacific.
#### 6.2 Gyres and the Sargasso Sea
| Gyre | Components |
|---|---|
| North Atlantic | N. Equatorial → Gulf Stream → N. Atlantic Drift → Canary |
| South Atlantic | S. Equatorial → Brazil → West Wind Drift → Benguela |
| North Pacific | N. Equatorial → Kuroshio → N. Pacific Drift → California |
| South Pacific | S. Equatorial → East Australian → West Wind Drift → Humboldt |
| South Indian | S. Equatorial → Agulhas → West Wind Drift → West Australian |
- Gyres: **clockwise in N**, anticlockwise in S. **Sargasso Sea** (North Atlantic): **only sea with no land boundary**; bounded by **Gulf Stream (W), North Atlantic Drift (N), Canary (E), North Equatorial (S)**; calm, warm, salty; **Sargassum** weed. **Canary** is not in the South Atlantic circle.
### 7. INDIAN OCEAN CURRENTS
- **North Indian Ocean** currents **reverse with the monsoon** (monsoon drift); the South Indian Ocean has a permanent anticlockwise gyre.
| Season | Bay of Bengal / Arabian Sea | WICC | EICC |
|---|---|---|---|
| SW monsoon (summer) | **Clockwise** | **Southward** | Northward |
| NE monsoon (winter) | Anticlockwise | **Northward, Nov–Feb** | Southward |
- **Findlater Current** (Somali Jet): reverses with the monsoon; upwelling off the Horn of Africa.
!KEY APPSC GS 2018: WICC flows **northward (poleward) in November–February**.
### 8. EFFECTS OF CURRENTS
| Place | Current(s) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| NW Europe, Norway, **Murmansk** | North Atlantic Drift | Mild winters; **ice-free ports** |
| **Atacama** | Humboldt | Desert |
| **Namib, Kalahari** | Benguela | Desert |
| **Sonoran** | California | Desert |
| **Grand Banks** (SE of Newfoundland) | Labrador (cold) + Gulf Stream (warm) | Fishing ground; **fog** |
| Off Japan | Kuroshio + Oyashio | Fishing ground |
| Peru / Somalia | Humboldt / Findlater | Upwelling – fisheries |
- **Best fishing grounds**: where warm and cold currents meet, mainly **40°–60°**.
### 9. ENSO AND INDIAN OCEAN DIPOLE
#### 9.1 El Nino vs La Nina
| Basis | El Nino | La Nina |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | **"Christ child"**; warm current off Peru | Cold phase |
| Trade winds | Weaken / reverse | Stronger |
| Eastern Pacific (Peru) | Warmer; upwelling suppressed | Cooler; strong upwelling |
| SOI (Tahiti − Darwin) | **Negative** | **Positive** |
| Indian monsoon | Delayed, weakened (drought) | Usually good rain |
- Recurs on average every **2–7 years**.
- **IMD criterion**: central equatorial Pacific at least **0.5°C** warmer for **five consecutive overlapping three-month periods**. **Super El Nino**: anomaly above **+2.0°C**.
- **Southern Oscillation** (atmospheric side of ENSO): pressure seesaw Tahiti vs Port Darwin, described by **Gilbert Walker (1920)**.
- **May 2026**: IMD warned a super El Nino could weaken the monsoon; **Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich** tracked an eastward **Kelvin wave**.
!TRAP El Nino and La Nina are **not storms** (RPSC 2024); they are climate patterns.
#### 9.2 Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)
| Phase | Western Indian Ocean | Eastern Indian Ocean | Monsoon |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Warmer | Cooler | Usually strengthens; can offset El Nino |
| **Negative** | Cooler | Warmer | Usually weaker rain |
