## U0067 | GUPTA ART, LITERATURE, SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY – KALIDASA, ARYABHATA, VARAHAMIHIRA, AJANTA, MEHRAULI PILLAR
### 1. THE "CLASSICAL AGE" LABEL
#### 1.1 Why it is called the Classical / Golden Age
- Gupta rule is called the **Golden Age** of ancient India for art and culture.
- Peak of **Sanskrit literature**, **mathematics and astronomy** (zero, decimal place value), **Ajanta painting**, **Sarnath sculpture** and the **first structural temples**.
- First images of Hindu gods (Vishnu, Shiva) became common; shift from **rock-cut to free-standing structural temples**.
- **Nalanda** drew students from China, Tibet, Korea and South-East Asia; **Balaputradeva of Srivijaya** funded a monastery there.
#### 1.2 Criticism of the label
- Spread of **vishti** (forced labour).
- **Land grants and feudatories** weakened central power.
- **Decline of trade and towns**; rigid caste; **falling status of women**.
- Varahamihira's Romaka and Paulisa Siddhantas show Greek input, so Indian science was not isolated.
### 2. KALIDASA
#### 2.1 Works – type-wise
| Type | Works |

#### 2.3 The five Mahakavyas
| Mahakavya | Author | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Raghuvamsha, Kumarasambhava | Kalidasa | Raghu dynasty; birth of Kumara |
| Kiratarjuniyam | **Bharavi** | Arjuna vs Shiva as a **kirata** (hunter) at **Indrakeeladri** hills |
| Shishupalavadha | **Magha** | Epic poem |
| Naishadhiyacharita | **Sriharsha** | **Nala and Damayanti** (12th century) |
- Themes from the **Mahabharata**: Naishadhiyacharita, Kiratarjuniyam, Shishupalavadha – **not** Dashakumaracharita.
- Order: **Bhasa → Kalidasa → Magha → Sriharsha**. Magha and Sriharsha are post-Gupta.

### 5. SCIENTISTS AND THEIR TEXTS
#### 5.1 Scientist – field – work – contribution
| Scientist | Field | Works | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aryabhata** | Maths, astronomy | **Aryabhatiya (499 CE)** | First to treat maths as a separate subject (**Ganitapada**); Earth spherical and **rotates on its axis** (day and night); scientific cause of eclipses; **pi = 3.1416**; used zero and place value |
| **Varahamihira** (505–587 CE, Avanti) | Astronomy, astrology, geography, botany, architecture | Panchasiddhantika, Brihat Samhita, Brihat Jataka | Improved sine tables; shifting of equinoxes; earthquake theories; **termites** as sign of underground water |
| **Brahmagupta** (c. 598–668 CE) | Maths, astronomy | **Brahmasphutasiddhanta**, Khandakhadyaka | Rules of arithmetic with **zero and negative numbers**; before Newton: all things gravitate to the earth; first to study **cyclic quadrilaterals** |
| **Bhaskara I** | Astronomy | **Commentary on Aryabhatiya** | Wrote decimal numbers with a circle for zero (629 CE) |
| **Bhaskara II** (12th c.) | Maths (Algebra) | **Siddhanta Shiromani** | Earth's circumference; Lilavati has ideas of calculus |
| Mahavira (9th c., Karnataka) | Maths | **Ganitasarasangraha** | – |
#### 5.2 Varahamihira's texts
- **Panchasiddhantika**: summary of **5 siddhantas** – Surya, **Romaka**, **Paulisa**, Vasishtha, Paitamaha; based on Greek (also Roman, Egyptian, Indian) astronomy.
- **Romaka Siddhanta**: deals with **astronomy**; Roman-Greek influence, like Paulisa.
- **Brihat Samhita**: encyclopaedia (astronomy, astrology, geography, botany, architecture).
- **Brihat Jataka**: standard work on **astrology**.
- Siddhanta Shiromani has four parts: **Lilavati**, **Bijaganita** (Algebra), Ganitadhyaya, Goladhyaya.
!TRAP Lilavati = Bhaskara II (12th c.), not Gupta. Amarakosha is by Amarasimha, not Varahamihira.
### 6. ZERO AND THE PLACE-VALUE SYSTEM
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Earliest epigraphic place-value use | **Mankani (Sankheda) plates**, Gujarat, **AD 595–596** (Kalachuri year 346) |
| Bhaskara I | Circle for zero in decimal numbers, 629 CE |
| Earliest zero written as a circle (inscription) | **Gwalior inscription, 876 CE** |
| Decimal place value credited to | **Aryabhata** |
| Zero arithmetic set by | **Brahmagupta** |
- Zero was discovered by an anonymous Indian (2nd c. BC); **Arabs** learned it from India (Arab use from 873 AD) and spread it to the **Islamic world and Europe**. Zero did not come from Europe.
!KEY Decimal system and zero = the lasting gift of the Gupta age to world mathematics.

### 9. SCULPTURE AND THE MEHRAULI IRON PILLAR
#### 9.1 Sculpture
- Gupta stone sculpture grew out of the **Mathura school**.
- **Sarnath school**: polished **Chunar (buff) sandstone**, little decoration, **Dharmachakra mudra**; model for Buddhist art across Asia.
| Image | Place | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Sultanganj Buddha** | Bhagalpur, Bihar | **Bronze**, over 2 m – finest Gupta metal image |
| Eran Varaha | Eran (MP) | Vaishnava presence |
- Apr 2025: the PM gifted a **Sarnath Buddha in Dhyan mudra** to Thailand's King.
#### 9.2 Mehrauli iron pillar
- Inscription names the king **"Chandra"** = **Chandragupta II**.
- Originally at **Udayagiri near Vidisha (MP)**; now at **Mehrauli, Delhi**.
- **Not rusted for over fifteen centuries** – **high phosphorus** iron forms a protective layer.
!PAIR Material check | Sarnath = Chunar sandstone | Sultanganj = bronze || Mehrauli | iron | Chandragupta II
### 10. AJANTA, BAGH AND UDAYAGIRI CAVES
#### 10.1 Ajanta basics
- **Buddhist**; **Aurangabad district, Maharashtra**; horseshoe gorge of the **Waghora river**, Sahyadri ranges.
- **30 caves** (29 numbered, plus 15A); 5 chaityas (9, 10, 19, 26, 29), rest viharas; 25 viharas in older counts.
- Found **1819** by British officer **John Smith** while hunting tigers; **UNESCO 1983** (with Ellora).
- Patrons: **Satavahanas** (early, 2nd–1st c. BCE) → mainly the **Vakatakas** (King **Harishena**, 5th c.).
#### 10.2 Cave and panel
| Cave | Panel / fact |
|---|---|
| 9, 10 | **Earliest paintings in India** – Satavahana period, Amaravati style |
| **10** | **Chhaddanta (Shwetagaja) Jataka** – first "completely evolved painting" |
| **1** | **Bodhisattva Padmapani** ("one who holds the lotus"), Vajrapani |
| 16 | Excavated by minister **Varahadeva** (of Harishena); **Dying Princess** |
| 17 | **Mother and Child**, **Bhava-chakra** (wheel of life), Brahmi inscription |
| 19 | Chaitya; standing Buddha with two Yakshas |
| **26** | **Mahaparinirvana** – a sculpture, not a painting |
- Themes: **Jataka tales**, secular life, Bodhi-tree worship, group dances.
- Technique: **tempera on dry lime plaster (fresco secco)**, not true fresco; outlines in **red** first; lapis-lazuli blue is used.
- Known for mural paintings: **Ajanta and Lepakshi** (not Sanchi Stupa). Ajanta and Mahabalipuram share only **rock-cut** form.
- 3 Apr 2026: Ashwin Prabhu's book on Ajanta's murals.
#### 10.3 Other caves
| Site | Place | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Bagh** | **Dhar district, MP**, Vindhya slopes, **Baghani river** | **9 Buddhist viharas**, late 4th–6th c.; **Hallisaka** painting = joyous folk dance; Cave 4 = **Rang Mahal** |
| **Udayagiri** | Near **Vidisha** (MP) | 20 caves (19 Hindu, 1 Jain) under Chandragupta II; colossal **Varaha** panel; **Virasena's** inscription on his conquests |
| Ellora | Aurangabad | Buddhist Caves 1–12; Rashtrakuta cave temples |
| Mandapeshwar | Mumbai | Buddhist, later Christian |
- AP angle: **Mogalrajapuram** – 5 caves, Nataraja; **Bhairavakonda** – 8 Kapalika caves; Vishnukundinas adapted the Gupta rock-cut style (4th–5th c.).
!TRAP Gupta Udayagiri is in **MP**, not Kharavela's Jain Udayagiri in Odisha.
### 11. GUPTA COINS
- Gold = **Dinara** (Roman influence; largest numbers); silver = **Rupaka** (from *rupa*).
- **Chandragupta I** = first Gupta coin issuer.
- Silver began under **Chandragupta II (Sakari)** after conquering the **Western Kshatrapas** of Gujarat, copied from Saka silver issues.
- Largest hoard: **Bayana (Bharatpur, Rajasthan)**.
| Coin type | Ruler |
|---|---|
| King-queen (with Kumaradevi) | Chandragupta I |
| **Veena-player**, tiger-slayer, Ashvamedha | **Samudragupta** |
| **Archer, lion-slayer**, silver | **Chandragupta II** |
| **King feeding peacock** (Kartikeya), Ashvamedha | **Kumaragupta I** |
| Bow, arrow, Garuda, Lakshmi | Skandagupta |
!TRAP Tiger-slayer = Samudragupta; lion-slayer = Chandragupta II; peacock = Kumaragupta I. Silver (not gold) was modelled on Saka coins.
