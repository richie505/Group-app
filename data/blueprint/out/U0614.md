## U0614 | OCEAN-ATMOSPHERE INTERACTION – EL NINO, LA NINA, ENSO, IOD, WALKER CIRCULATION
### 1. EL NINO – MEANING AND MECHANISM
- **El Nino** = abnormal warming of the **central and eastern equatorial Pacific**; the oceanic part of ENSO.
- A warm current flows south along **Peru**, replacing the cold **Humboldt (Peruvian)** current; cold nutrient-rich **upwelling stops** → fewer phytoplankton, fish catch collapses.
- Name: **"little boy / Christ child"** – appears around **Christmas**.
- Recurs every **2–7 years**; **not every year**.
- **Bjerknes feedback**: easterly trades weaken/reverse → warm western Pacific water shifts east → winds weaken further → air sinks over Indian Ocean (high pressure) → **late or weak Indian monsoon**.
!TRAP El Nino and La Nina are **climate patterns, not storms**.
### 2. EL NINO VS LA NINA
| Feature | El Nino | La Nina |
|---|---|---|
| Central/east Pacific SST | Warmer | Cooler (strong upwelling) |
| Trade winds | Weak / reversed | **Stronger than usual** |
| Indian monsoon | Weak, below normal (drought sign) | Normal to above normal |
| North India winter | — | **Colder, frequent cold waves** (e.g. January 2012) |
| Bay of Bengal | **Fewer** post-monsoon (Oct–Dec) cyclones (INCOIS) | More floods, severe cyclones |
| Australia/Indonesia | Drier | **Heavy rain, floods** |
| Walker circulation | **Weakens, shifts east** | **Strengthens** |
- **6 of 10** El Nino years brought depressed Indian rainfall; last global El Nino **2023-24**.
- **2025** = warmest La Nina year on record.
### 3. EL NINO TYPES
| Type | Maximum warming |
|---|---|
| Canonical (normal) | **Eastern Pacific**, near Peru |
| **El Nino Modoki** ("similar but different", Japanese) | **Central Pacific**; cooler on both sides (tripole) |
### 4. WALKER CIRCULATION
- Normal state: east-west (zonal) loop over the tropical Pacific – air **rises over warm western Pacific/Indonesia**, flows east aloft, **sinks over cool eastern Pacific**, surface **easterly trades** return west.
- El Nino: loop weakens and shifts east → less convection over India.
### 5. ENSO AND SOUTHERN OSCILLATION
- **Southern Oscillation**: atmospheric pressure **seesaw** between the Pacific and Indian Oceans – **Tahiti vs Port Darwin**; first described by **Gilbert Walker (1920s)**.
- **SOI** = Tahiti minus Darwin; sustained **negative SOI = El Nino**, positive = La Nina.
- ENSO is used for short-term climate prediction.
### 6. ENSO MONITORING
- **Nino 3.4 region**: **5°N–5°S, 120°W–170°W**, central equatorial Pacific.
- **NOAA**: El Nino when the **Oceanic Nino Index** (3-month mean SST anomaly, Nino 3.4) is **≥ +0.5°C** for at least **5 consecutive overlapping seasons**.
- **IMD April 2026**: monsoon forecast **92% of LPA (LPA 87 cm)**, below normal, due to a developing El Nino.
### 7. INDIAN OCEAN DIPOLE (IOD)
| IOD phase | West (Arabian Sea) | East (south of Indonesia) | India |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Warmer | Cooler | **More rain**; can offset El Nino |
| **Negative** | Cooler | Warmer | Reduced rainfall |
- Discovered **1999**; measured by **Dipole Mode Index (DMI)**. Late May 2026 DMI −0.34°C = neutral.
- **1997**: strong positive IOD saved India from drought despite a **Super El Nino**.
- IOD runs **independently of ENSO**, though positive IOD events often co-occur with El Nino.
### 8. MADDEN-JULIAN OSCILLATION (MJO)
- Identified by **Madden and Julian, 1971-72**; eastward pulse of clouds and rain from a warm pool in the tropical Indian Ocean.
- Recurs every **30–60 days**; convection up to **10 km**.
- Shifts heavy rain east → **active and break spells** of the monsoon.
- **AP angle**: cyclone swings and weak-monsoon years hit coastal AP and rain-fed **Rayalaseema** farming.
