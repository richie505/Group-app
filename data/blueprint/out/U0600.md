## U0600 | WEATHERING, MASS WASTING AND SOIL FORMATION; DENUDATION AND THE CYCLE OF EROSION
### 1. DENUDATION
- Order: **weathering** (first stage) → **mass wasting / erosion** → **transportation** → **deposition**.
- Agents: **water** (most powerful), wind, ice, gravity.
- Water = strongest cause of soil erosion (**sheet, rill, gully** – Chambal ravines); **wind** leads only in areas like the **Thar**.
### 2. WEATHERING PROCESSES
| Process | Nature | What happens / example |
|---|---|---|
| Physical (mechanical) | No chemical change | Frost wedging, heating-cooling (exfoliation), salt crystal growth |
| Solution | Chemical | Soluble minerals (rock salt, gypsum) dissolve |
| **Carbonation** | Chemical | CO₂ in rain forms **carbonic acid**, dissolves **limestone**; stores carbon as **bicarbonate** |
| **Hydration** | Chemical | Minerals **absorb water and swell**: **anhydrite → gypsum** |
| **Hydrolysis** | Chemical | Water reacts with silicates: **feldspar → clay**; **muscovite → vermiculite** (potassium replaced by hydronium) (APPSC 2024) |
| **Oxidation** | Chemical | Iron minerals **rust** → red or yellow soils |
| Biological | Organic | Roots, burrowing animals, lichens, micro-organisms |
- Weathering is **fastest in hot and wet** climates; higher temperature and more rain speed up chemical weathering.
- Rainwater holds **CO₂ in solution and atmospheric oxygen** – both help chemical weathering.
- **Freeze-thaw** breaks rocks in the high Himalaya. Glacier-fed Himalayan catchments release CO₂ through **pyrite oxidation** (13 Sep 2026).
!TRAP Hydration = swelling (anhydrite → gypsum); hydrolysis = reaction (feldspar → clay, muscovite → vermiculite).
### 3. WEATHERING LANDFORMS
| Landform | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Exfoliation domes** | **Onion-like peeling** of granite (from pressure or temperature change) |
| **Tors** | Residual granite blocks standing above the weathered surface where joints are widely spaced |
| Block disintegration | Physical break-up of jointed rock into blocks |
### 4. MASS WASTING
- Downslope movement of earth material under **gravity** = **landslide** (APPSC 2019).
| Type | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Rockfall** | **Free fall** of rocks from steep cliffs |
| **Slide** | Whole coherent mass slips along a surface |
| **Slump** | **Rotational** slip along a **curved** surface in weak, wet soils |
| Debris flow / **mudflow** | **Fast** water-soil-rock mix after heavy rain or snowmelt |
| **Creep** | **Very slow** movement from wetting-drying, heating-cooling |
- Conditions: slopes **above 30 degrees**, weak/fractured rock, bare slopes; clay and silt get saturated (landslide-prone).
- India's main trigger: **SW monsoon rainfall**, then **earthquakes**. Most at risk: **Himalaya and North-East**.
### 5. CYCLE OF EROSION
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **W.M. Davis (1899)** | Landscape = **structure + process + stage (time)**; youth → maturity → old age; ends in a near-flat **peneplain** with **monadnocks** |
| **Walther Penck** | Landform depends on ratio of **uplift to erosion** acting **simultaneously**, not time-stages; slopes retreat parallel |
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Peneplain** | Low, almost flat end-surface of the Davis cycle |
| **Monadnock** | Resistant residual hill on a peneplain |
| **Inselberg** | Residual island-hill of **arid/semi-arid** regions (wind and sheet-wash erosion) |
- **Peninsular plateau** of India = old peneplain, rejuvenated in places.
!PAIR Davis | Time-stages | Peneplain || Penck | Uplift vs erosion | Parallel slope retreat
### 6. SOIL-FORMING FACTORS
- Five classical factors: **parent material, climate, relief/topography, organisms, time**; **latitude is NOT** one.
| Active factors | Passive factors |
|---|---|
| **Climate**, **biological activity** (vegetation, micro-organisms) | **Parent material**, **topography** (relief), **time** |
- APPSC G1 2024: active factors = climate and biological activity (**III and IV only**) – they drive weathering and organic processes.
- **AP**: climate, altitude and **bedrock composition** shape soils; uneven rainfall and excessive heat give AP soils their character; soils mainly a mix of sand, silt and clay.
### 7. SOIL TEXTURE
| Particle | Diameter |
|---|---|
| Sand (fine sand) | **0.05–2 mm** |
| Silt | **0.002–0.05 mm** |
| **Clay** | **Less than 0.002 mm** |
- Water-holding: **clay > silt > sand**; clay holds **maximum** water (smaller particles); clay pore spaces are **small, not large**; sand drains easily.
- **Loam** contains all particle types (about **40% sand, 42% silt, 18% clay**).
- Clay binds organic carbon; sandy soils lose it by leaching (Nov 2025).
- **Rainforest soils are poor** despite lush forest: heavy rain **leaches** nutrients and dead matter decomposes rapidly.
### 8. SOIL PROFILE
| Horizon | Character |
|---|---|
| **O** | Surface **organic** layer of decaying plant matter |
| **A** | **Topsoil**: minerals + organic matter; most plant roots |
| E | Light-coloured leached layer (not in every profile) |
| **B** | **Subsoil**: leached minerals collect |
| **C** | Weathered **parent material**, little organic matter |
| R | Unweathered **bedrock** |
### 9. ZONAL, RESIDUAL AND TRANSPORTED SOILS
| Soil | Climate / vegetation |
|---|---|
| **Podzol** | **Cold taiga** (boreal conifer forests); thin, acidic, leached, humus-poor |
| **Laterite** | **Hot, heavy-rain tropics**; silica and lime leached, **iron and aluminium oxides** left; acidic |
| **Terra rossa** | **Mediterranean**, over **limestone**; red |
| **Chernozem** | **Temperate grasslands** (steppes, prairies); black, humus-rich |
| **Oxisols** | Equatorial-tropical surfaces **stable for very long** (APPSC 2019) |
| Type | Formation / region |
|---|---|
| **In-situ / residual** = **sedentary** (APPSC 2018) | Form where the rock weathered; mainly Peninsular India |
| Transported (ex-situ) | Moved by rivers, wind, ice; mainly North India; **alluvium ~45.6%** of India |
- **AP cultivated land (Jul 2026)**: **red ~60%** (Rayalaseema), **black (regur) ~25%** (one-fourth), deltaic alluvium ~6%, **laterite ~1%**.
- **FAO Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026** (75 countries, 3 Sep 2026): soil biodiversity loss ranked **4th of seven** threats, after erosion, loss of soil organic carbon and nutrient mismanagement.
