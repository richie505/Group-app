## U0416 | POPULATION PROBLEM, AGEING AND PROBLEMS OF THE ELDERLY
### 1. POPULATION THEORIES
#### 1.1 Malthusian Theory
- **T.R. Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**).
- Population grows in **geometric progression** (1, 2, 4, 8, 16); food grows in **arithmetic progression** (1, 2, 3, 4).
| Check | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive checks** | Raise the death rate | Famine, disease, war, misery |
| **Preventive checks** | Lower the birth rate | **Moral restraint**: late marriage, celibacy |
- Malthus opposed contraception; critics say he did not foresee advances in farm technology.
#### 1.2 Other Theories
| Theory | Propounder |
|---|---|
| **Demographic transition** | **Warren Thompson and Frank Notestein** |
| **Optimum population** | **Edwin Cannan** (rejected Malthus's view of an inherent tendency to overpopulation) |
### 2. DEMOGRAPHIC TRANSITION
- Stages: **high birth and death rates** → **death rate falls** → **birth rate falls** → **both low**.
- India now: **youth-bulge** stage.
- **Dependency ratio** = (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64).
!KEY Dependency ratio counts the young (0-14) and the old (65+) against the working age (15-64).
### 3. POPULATION GROWTH IN INDIA
#### 3.1 Growth Phases
| Period | Phase |
|---|---|
| 1911-21 | **Decline** |
| **1921** | **"Great Divide"** |
| 1961-71 | **Explosion** |
| 1981-2011 | **High but slowing** |
#### 3.2 Census 2011 and Age Structure
- Population about **121.09 crore**; **68.9% rural**; decadal growth (2001-11) **17.7%**.
- **AP**: 495.77 lakh (**4.09%** of India, **10th**) (2025-26).
- SRS 2024: working age **66.4%**; children **24%**.
- Next Census: **2027**.
### 4. FERTILITY (TFR)
| Source | India | Others |
|---|---|---|
| **NFHS-5** (2019-21) | **2.0** | **AP 1.7**; **Bihar 3.0** (highest) |
| **SRS 2024** | **1.9** (below 2.1 for the **5th** year) | Rural 2.1, urban 1.5; only six states above 2.1; **Delhi 1.2** (lowest) (24 May 2026) |
- Replacement level TFR = **2.1**.
!TRAP Highest TFR = Bihar 3.0 (NFHS-5); lowest = Delhi 1.2 (SRS 2024).
### 5. FAMILY PLANNING AND POPULATION POLICY
#### 5.1 Family Planning Programme
- India was the **first country** to launch a **national family planning programme** (**1952**); early approach was **clinic-based**, mainly in cities.
- **ICPD, Cairo 1994**: shift from population control to **individual well-being** and reproductive health.
- **April 1996**: **target-free approach** – method-specific contraceptive targets dropped.
#### 5.2 National Population Policy 2000
| Objective | Target |
|---|---|
| Immediate | Meet **unmet need** for contraception, health infrastructure and staff; integrated reproductive and child health services |
| Medium-term | **TFR 2.1** (replacement level) by **2010** |
| Long-term | **Stable population by 2045** |
- **National Commission on Population** (May 2000): chaired by the **Prime Minister**; CMs of all states/UTs members; reconstituted in 2005.
### 6. EFFECTS OF POPULATION GROWTH
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Economic | Jobs needed for about **9.7 million** new working-age people a year; **demographic dividend** only if employed (5 Aug 2026) |
| Environment and land | Pressure on resources; smaller land holdings |
- UPPSC 2023: "Population control is necessary for the environment" is **true**, but the reason given was **false**.
### 7. POPULATION CONTROL DEBATES
#### 7.1 Southern States' Concern
- States that controlled population lose out: population has **15-25%** weight in **Finance Commission** formulas; delimitation may cost **8-10 seats**.
#### 7.2 Two-Child Norm
- **Javed v. State of Haryana (2003)**: SC **upheld** Haryana's two-child norm for local body polls.
- States that used the norm include **AP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Karnataka, Gujarat**.
#### 7.3 AP Stance Change
- AP's two-child norm for local polls dated from **1994**; the AP Panchayat Raj and Municipal Laws (Amendment) Bills **2024** (Nov 2024) removed it.
- **Draft Population Management Policy (March 2026)**: from **control to management**; **Rs 25,000** for a second or third child.

| Category | Top |

### 9. PROBLEMS OF THE ELDERLY AND THE FAMILY
#### 9.1 Problems
| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| Health | 80+ face chronic disease; insurance age caps; elderly = **30-35%** of hospitalisation spending (Feb 2026) |
| Dependency | **70%** depend on others; **78%** have no pension (NITI, 25 Jun 2026); 70% of elderly women have no pension |
| Social | **Ageism**, loneliness, abuse, **feminisation of ageing** |
#### 9.2 Ageing and Family; Policies
- **Integrated Programme for Older Persons**: NGOs get up to **90%** of cost of **old age homes**, day care, mobile medicare.
- **NPOP 1999**, **Senior Citizens Act 2007**, **NPHCE 2010-11**, **NPSC 2011** (**ageing in place**; destitution among rural elderly).
- **AVYAY – SAGE**: equity up to **Rs 1 crore** for senior-care start-ups.
- **Kerala**: first State with a **Senior Citizens' Welfare Department**, Commission and Elderly Budget (10 Jul 2026).
