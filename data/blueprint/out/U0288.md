## U0288 | RIGHT TO CONSTITUTIONAL REMEDIES – ARTICLE 32; THE FIVE WRITS AND THEIR SCOPE
### 1. ARTICLE 32 – STATUS AND SIGNIFICANCE
- **Right to Constitutional Remedies** = **Article 32** (one Article): the right to move the **Supreme Court directly** to enforce Part III – **itself a Fundamental Right** (APPSC GS 2025).
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**: Art. 32 is "**the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it**" – the Article without which the Constitution would be a **nullity**.
- "A right without a remedy is a **worthless declaration**" – Art. 32 makes Part III effective, not only declaratory.
- **Part of the basic structure** – cannot be abridged even by constitutional amendment. **Kesavananda Bharati (13 judges)**: judicial review under Arts. **32, 226, 227** is part of the inviolable basic structure.
!TRAP G2 Mains 2025 key gave "heart and soul" as Art. 14 – the correct answer is Article 32.
### 2. CLAUSES OF ARTICLE 32
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| 32(1) | Right to move the SC **by appropriate proceedings** to enforce Part III rights |
| 32(2) | SC may issue directions, orders or **writs** – the **five** named: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto (wide original jurisdiction) |
| 32(3) | **Parliament** may empower **any other court** to use these powers locally – **no such law made yet** |
| 32(4) | Right **not suspended** except as the Constitution provides (not by ordinary law) |
- Scope: **only Fundamental Rights**, not statutory or other constitutional rights. Jurisdiction **original but not exclusive** – concurrent with HCs; aggrieved person may choose either.
- Writs began as **prerogative writs** of **English common law** (the King as "fountain of justice").
- **Before 1950**, only the **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** HCs could issue writs; Art. 226 now empowers all HCs.
- **Article 13**: power of judicial review to SC and HCs. SC has **no exclusive jurisdiction** on validity of Central laws; HCs also have it.
### 3. GUARDIAN OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS
- **Guardian of Fundamental Rights = the Judiciary** – the **Supreme Court** is their "**protector and guarantor**" and **Guardian of the Constitution** (APPSC 2021); not the President.
- Power to enforce FRs: **Supreme Court (Art. 32) and High Courts (Art. 226)**. Only SC and HCs issue writs; **both issue all five**.
### 4. THE FIVE WRITS
| Writ | Literal meaning | Issued against | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | "**To have the body** (of)" | **Public authorities and private individuals** | Release from **unlawful detention**; detained person **produced before court** |
| **Mandamus** | "**We command**" | Public officials, statutory bodies, inferior courts, tribunals, public corporations, govt | Compels performance of a **public legal duty** refused or neglected |
| **Prohibition** | "**To forbid**" / to restrain | **Judicial and quasi-judicial** bodies only | Higher court stops a lower court exceeding jurisdiction – **while pending** |
| **Certiorari** | "**To be certified** / informed" | Judicial, quasi-judicial and (**since 1991**) certain **administrative** bodies affecting rights | **Quashes an order already passed**; also **transfers** a pending case |
| **Quo Warranto** | "**By what authority** (or warrant)?" | Holder of a **substantive public office** created by statute or the Constitution | Stops **illegal usurpation**; tests legality of the claim to office |
- **Mandamus orders action; Prohibition orders inaction.** Mandamus = Judiciary orders the **Executive** to perform its duty.
- **Certiorari** = "**postmortem**": higher court calls the subordinate court's record for review. Grounds: **lack or excess of jurisdiction**, or **error of law on the face of the record**.
- Habeas Corpus, Mandamus etc. are writ jurisdiction; **review, advisory opinions and special leave** are other kinds of jurisdiction.
#### 4.1 Habeas Corpus
- The **only writ directly against private individuals** (illegal confinement).
- "**Bulwark of individual liberty**" – the **greatest safeguard of personal liberty**; quick remedy against arbitrary detention. A **High Court** can also issue it.
- Not for **lawful detention**, contempt of court/legislature, or detention outside the court's jurisdiction.
- APPSC 2021: producing a detained person before court = **habeas corpus**.
#### 4.2 Quo Warranto
- **Any public-minded citizen** / any interested person may seek it – petitioner **need not be aggrieved** (locus standi relaxed).
- Not for **ministerial or private** office.

### 5. LIMITS ON WRITS
#### 5.1 Mandamus Does NOT Lie Against
- **Private individuals/bodies** without public duties – but it **can** lie against a **government company or statutory corporation** performing a **public function**.
- **President or Governors** – **Art. 361**: not answerable to any court for official acts.
- **Discretionary** duty; **legislative** functions; **contractual** obligation; **non-statutory departmental instructions**; Chief Justice of a HC acting judicially.
- **Law and policy**: mandamus **cannot force the Government to make a law or policy**, but can enforce the terms of a policy **once laid down** (G2 Mains 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A).
#### 5.2 Other Writs
- **Prohibition**: not against administrative authorities, legislative bodies, private persons.
- **Certiorari**: not against **legislative bodies** or **private persons**.
### 6. PROHIBITION vs CERTIORARI
| Point | Prohibition | Certiorari |
|---|---|---|
| Stage | **While proceedings are pending** | **After an order is passed** (also to transfer a pending case) |
| Nature | **Preventive only** | **Preventive and curative** |
| Effect | Stops the lower body | **Quashes** the order |
| Nickname | — | "Postmortem" |
- Common: both (traditionally) against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies; both check excess of jurisdiction.
!TRAP Prohibition = pending, preventive only; Certiorari = after the order, curative (and preventive).
### 7. ARTICLE 32 vs ARTICLE 226
| Point | Art. 32 (SC) | Art. 226 (HC) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Itself a Fundamental Right** – SC cannot refuse | Constitutional, **discretionary** remedy |
| Scope | **Only FRs** (narrower) | FRs + "**any other purpose**" (legal rights) – **wider** |
| Territory | **Whole of India** (wider) | Own territory; beyond it if the cause of action arises inside (**15th Amendment, 1963**) |
| Writs | Five | Five |
- **HC wider in subject matter; SC wider territorially.** High Courts are in a better position to protect rights.
- **Res judicata**: HC dismisses a writ **on merits** → same plea under Art. 32 barred; remedy is appeal. If not on merits, Art. 32 may be used.
#### 7.1 Amendments and Cases
| Case / Amendment | Year | Point |
|---|---|---|
| **42nd Amendment** | 1976 | Inserted **Art. 32A** (no challenge to State laws under Art. 32); curbed Art. 226 |
| **43rd Amendment** | 1977 | Omitted Arts. **32A, 131A, 144A, 226A, 228A** |
| 44th Amendment | 1978 | Also restored Art. 226 |
| **L. Chandra Kumar** | 1997 | Judicial review under **Arts. 226 and 227** = basic structure |
- 20 Jan 2026: SC (Justices **Dipankar Datta and S.C. Sharma**) agreed to examine whether the **Enforcement Directorate** can invoke writ jurisdiction (appeals by **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** against a Kerala HC ruling).
- 24 Apr 2026: in the ED-West Bengal matter, Centre defended an Art. 32 petition vs a State's plea for an **Art. 131** suit.

### 8. PIL AND LOCUS STANDI
- **PIL** loosens **locus standi** (only the directly aggrieved may sue) – any public-spirited person may move the court for those unable to. PIL is **not itself an FR**.
- **S.P. Gupta (1981)** – key PIL case. **Justices P.N. Bhagwati** (CJ when PIL began) and **V.R. Krishna Iyer** linked with PIL.
- **Epistolary jurisdiction**: letters/postcards treated as writ petitions – **Sunil Batra** (prisoner's letter).
### 9. ARTICLE 32 DURING EMERGENCY; ARTICLES 33-35
- **Art. 359**: during a National Emergency the **President** may by order suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of named FRs – except **Arts. 20 and 21** (44th Amendment).
- **Art. 33**: **Parliament** (not State legislatures) may restrict or modify FRs of **armed forces, police, intelligence** personnel.
- **Art. 34**: restrictions on FRs while **martial law** is in force; Parliament may **indemnify** acts done under it.
- **Art. 35**: **Parliament alone** legislates on specified FR matters (e.g. under Arts. 32(3), 33, 34).
### 10. ARTICLES 29-30 – CULTURAL AND EDUCATIONAL RIGHTS
- **Art. 29(1)**: any section of citizens with a distinct **language, script or culture** may **conserve** it; **29(2)**: no denial of admission to State-aided institutions on religion, race, caste, language.
- **Art. 30(1)**: all minorities, **religious or linguistic**, may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.

#### FIRST / LAST / SPECIAL FACTS
- First HCs with writ power: **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras** (before 1950).
- Only writ against private persons: **Habeas Corpus**.
- Only FR that is a right to a remedy: **Art. 32**.
