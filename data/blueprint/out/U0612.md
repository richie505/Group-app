## U0612 | PHYSICAL AND CHEMICAL CHARACTERISTICS OF OCEAN WATER – TEMPERATURE AND SALINITY DISTRIBUTION
### 1. DISTRIBUTION OF EARTH'S WATER
| Store | Share | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| **Oceans** | About **97%** (96.5%) (**APPSC GS 2025**) | 1 |
| **Polar ice and glaciers** | About 2% (1.74%) | 2 |
| **Groundwater** | About 1.7% | 3 |
| Lakes, soil moisture, atmosphere | Tiny | 4 |
| **Rivers** | **0.0002%** (smallest) | Last |
- Descending order: **oceans > ice and glaciers > groundwater > rivers** (UPPSC 2025).
- **Largest store of fresh water = glaciers and ice caps** (over 68%); next = **groundwater** (a little over 30%); surface water about 0.3%.
!KEY Density of water is **maximum at 4°C**: a lake freezes at the top while the bottom stays liquid (anomalous behaviour).
### 2. SALINITY – BASICS
- **Salinity** = total dissolved salts in sea water, in **parts per thousand (‰, ppt)** or **PSU** (Practical Salinity Units).
- **Average 35‰** = 35 g of salt per 1 kg of sea water (by mass); open ocean 33–37‰.
- **Isohaline** = line joining places of equal salinity.
- **Main source of salts = land**: weathered rocks carried by **rivers**; volcanic / hydrothermal vents are a smaller source.
- **Salinity reduces evaporation** (**APPSC GS 2018**); evaporation raises salinity.
### 3. FACTORS CONTROLLING SALINITY
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Evaporation** | Raises |
| **Precipitation** | Lowers |
| **River inflow** | Lowers (Black Sea: Danube, Dnieper, Dniester) |
| **Freezing** | Raises |
| **Ice melting** | Lowers |

### 5. HORIZONTAL DISTRIBUTION OF SALINITY
| Region | Salinity | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Equator | Relatively lower | Heavy rain dilutes |
| **Subtropics (20°–40° N/S)** | **Highest** | Strong evaporation, low rain; maximum near the **Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn** |
| Polar seas | Lower | Low evaporation, ice melt, rivers |
!TRAP Highest salinity is in the **subtropics, NOT at the equator**.

### 6. TEMPERATURE DISTRIBUTION
- **Horizontal**: falls from equator to poles; enclosed tropical seas warmest; **NH oceans warmer** (more land).
- **Tropics: western side warmer** (trade winds pile warm water west; warm currents on east coasts of continents). **Temperate: eastern side warmer** (westerlies, North Atlantic Drift).
- **Annual range smaller in the Pacific** than the Atlantic (vast; heats and cools slowly).
- **Three layers**: warm surface (to ~500 m; 20–25°C in tropics) → **thermocline** (~500–1,000 m; rapid fall) → cold deep layer (0–4°C).
### 7. DENSITY AND "-CLINE" LAYERS
- **Density rises** as **temperature falls**, **salinity rises**, and **pressure** (depth) increases.
- Denser water sinks, lighter rises: drives deep currents; cold polar water sinks and creeps towards the equator.
| Layer | Rapid change with depth in | Prefix |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermocline** | Temperature | thermo = heat |
| **Halocline** | **Salinity** | halo = salt |
| **Pycnocline** | **Density**; drives deep circulation | pycno = density |
| **Chemocline** | Chemistry (oxygen / hydrogen sulphide); **Black Sea** (meromictic: layers rarely mix) | – |
- Every cline is a **barrier to vertical mixing**.
### 8. OCEAN HEAT AND CHEMISTRY IN NEWS
- Oceans absorb **over 90% of excess heat** and about a quarter of human CO2 → **acidification**; **pH ~8.1** and falling.
- **2025**: record ocean heat content (about **23 zettajoules** more); about **90%** of the ocean surface saw marine heatwaves (WMO).
- **AP**: **NIOT**'s satellite station at **Nellore** runs **OMNI buoys**: real-time temperature, salinity, currents for **cyclone and IOD** forecasts.
