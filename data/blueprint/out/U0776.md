## U0776 | LOSS OF BIODIVERSITY – CAUSES, INVASIVE ALIEN SPECIES, IUCN RED LIST CATEGORIES
### 1. CAUSES OF BIODIVERSITY LOSS
- The word **biodiversity** was coined by **W. G. Rosen**.
- **Most important cause:** **habitat loss / destruction** (forest cutting, filling wetlands, dams, destruction of aquatic habitats). IPBES also names habitat destruction the main driver.
- Main reason for decrease = natural habitat destruction: loss of plants and the animals that depend on them. **Maximum danger** = destruction of natural habitats and vegetation.
- Main cause of destruction is **human influence**: habitat alteration and loss, exotic species, GMOs.
- In India: habitat loss (urbanisation, infrastructure, mining, farming) comes first, then climate change, invasives, pollution, overexploitation.
#### 1.1 Evil Quartet vs HIPPO
| Evil Quartet (NCERT) | HIPPO (popularised by **E. O. Wilson**) |
|---|---|
| Habitat loss and fragmentation | **H**abitat destruction |
| Over-exploitation | **I**nvasive species |
| Invasive alien species | **P**ollution |
| **Co-extinctions** | human **P**opulation |
| — | **O**ver-harvesting |
- Newer lists add **climate change** and **disease**.
!TRAP Co-extinction belongs to the Evil Quartet, not HIPPO; pollution and population belong to HIPPO only.
#### 1.2 Which is NOT a Cause
- Accepted causes: **modern agriculture**, **loss of genetic variation**, habitat destruction, **global warming**, **habitat fragmentation**, **alien species invasion**.
- NOT causes/threats: "**loss of appetite**", "**promotion of vegetarianism**", "expansion of protected areas".
- Over-exploitation extinctions: **Steller's sea cow**, **passenger pigeon**.
!KEY APPSC 2025: Deforestation destroys natural habitats and drives species to extinction.
### 2. DEFORESTATION AND HABITAT FRAGMENTATION
- **Primary driver** of deforestation and habitat loss: **agricultural expansion and urban development** (PYQ 2022).
- Tropical rainforest cover fell from **14% to 6%** of land surface.
#### 2.1 Chain to Extinction
1. Agricultural expansion and urban development
2. Habitat destroyed and fragmented
3. Edge effects
4. Interior species decline
5. Extinction
#### 2.2 Fragmentation and Edge Effect
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fragmentation** | Large habitats broken into small patches; hits **migratory animals** and **wide-ranging mammals and birds** hardest (disrupts movement, separates populations) |
| **Edge effect** | Patch boundaries get more **light, wind, heat, predators, weeds**; as fragments shrink, interior species decline |
#### 2.3 Disturb vs Restore Ecological Balance
| Disturbs balance | Restores balance |
|---|---|
| **Clear-felling**, shifting cultivation, mining, quarrying | **Afforestation**, eco-restoration of degraded land |
| **Draining wetlands**, mangrove-to-shrimp-farm conversion | Protected areas, wildlife corridors, eco-bridges |
| Urban sprawl on coasts and wetlands | **Removal of invasives with native restocking** |
### 3. CLIMATE CHANGE AND CONSEQUENCES
#### 3.1 Climate Change as Driver
- Warming causes **coral bleaching**, ocean acidification, shifting ecosystem boundaries and **disrupted migration**.
- Rising temperatures **shift species ranges** and raise extinction risk in hotspots (5 Jun 2026).
- A warming Arctic is opening to **alien plants** (12 Feb 2026).
#### 3.2 Consequences of Loss
- Disrupts species interactions, **pollination**, **nutrient cycling**, water and soil services; once lost, biodiversity cannot be recreated.
- Reduces **food security** of resource-dependent communities; cultural and ethical loss.
- **Zoonotic spillover:** emerging diseases via habitat disruption and wildlife trade; answer = **One Health** approach (links health of humans, animals, ecosystems).
### 4. EXTINCTION
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Extinction** | Death of the last individual of a species, globally or locally |
| **Co-extinction** | Loss of one species drags down dependants: a plant and its **obligate pollinator**; parasites of an extinct host fish |
| **Background extinction** | Slow natural loss at a low steady rate |
| **Mass extinction** | High percentage of species lost in a short geological period |
| **Sixth extinction** | Present, **human-driven** wave; rates **tens to hundreds of times** background (IPBES) |
- **Five** mass extinctions in the past 500 million years; the **fifth** = end-Cretaceous, about **66 million years ago** (dinosaurs).
- Sixth extinction is linked to mankind's **over-exploitation** of natural resources, habitat fragmentation/loss, ecosystem destruction, pollution and global climate change; humans are the primary cause; it is happening faster than feared.
- **High-risk traits:** large body size, small population, low reproductive rate, narrow specialised niche, endemism.
#### 4.1 Recorded Extinctions
- IUCN Red List (2014): **784** extinctions in 500 years; 5 Australian marsupials confirmed extinct in Red List 2026-1.
- **Cheetah** declared extinct in India in **1952**: the only large mammal lost in India since Independence.
- Recorded extinct from India (ZSI): **cheetah, pink-headed duck, mountain quail** (NOT the passenger pigeon).
#### 4.2 Extinct vs Extinct in the Wild vs Living
| Status | Examples |
|---|---|
| Extinct (EX) | Passenger pigeon, Steller's sea cow; cheetah in India (regional) |
| Extinct in the Wild (EW) | Hawaiian crow, Père David's deer (survive in captivity) |
| Living threatened | Great Indian Bustard (CR), tiger (EN) |
### 5. INVASIVE ALIEN SPECIES
- **Definition:** non-native species that spreads without control and causes measurable harm ("arrive, survive and thrive").
- Three tests: **non-native origin, uncontrolled spread, measurable harm** (NOT commercial usefulness).
- Invasives contributed to about **40%** of known animal extinctions since the **17th century**; threaten about **1 in 10** Red List species.
- Ecological damage per invasive species is higher in the **Global South**.
#### 5.1 Invasives in India – Origin and Impact
| Species | Origin | How it came / impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Water hyacinth** (Eichhornia, now Pontederia crassipes) | South America (Amazon) | "**Worst aquatic weed in the world**" (APPSC 2024); "**Terror of Bengal**"; monotypic stands in lakes, rivers, rice paddies |
| **Parthenium** hysterophorus (**Congress grass**) | Tropical America | With imported **wheat in the 1950s**; cropland and pasture; allergies, dermatitis |
| **Lantana camara** | Central/South America | As an **ornamental**; family **Verbenaceae** (not Asteraceae); forest understorey; allelopathy; IUCN 100 worst; ~25% of Amrabad TR forest floor |
| **Prosopis juliflora** (Seema Chintakaya, Ganda babool, Vilayati Kikar, karuvelam) | Mexico, South America, Caribbean | Dry land; **lowers groundwater**, raises soil salinity (Rayalaseema); degraded **Banni** grasslands; reduces biodiversity |
| **African catfish** (Clarias gariepinus) | Africa | Predatory; **banned** in India |
| **Senna spectabilis** | South America (not North America) | Nilgiri-Bandipur-Wayanad |
| Red-eared slider | North America | Pet trade |
| Mosquitofish | United States | Mosquito control |
| Giant African Snail | Africa | Takes over native habitats |
- Invasive fish of concern: **African catfish, Nile tilapia, red-bellied piranha, alligator gar**.
- **Cashew** is native to **Brazil**, introduced by the Portuguese about five centuries ago, first in **Goa**; of cashew, papaya and red sanders only **red sanders** is native to India.
#### 5.2 Laws and Actions
| Law / framework | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Destructive Insects and Pests Act, 1914** | Stops entry of crop pests and diseases into India and their inter-state spread |
| **Plant Quarantine Order, 2003** | Pest risk analysis and phytosanitary checks |
| **DPPQS** | Under **Agriculture Ministry** (not MoEFCC); fumigates uncertified imports with **methyl bromide** |
| CBD Article 8(h) | Alien species |
| **WPA Amendment 2022** | Centre regulates/prohibits invasive alien species under **Section 62A** |
!TRAP New **Schedule IV** lists **CITES-appendix specimens**, NOT invasive species.
- **Ujani reservoir, Maharashtra:** **BNHS** started India's **first community-driven** removal and native restocking (suckermouth catfish, tilapia, African catfish) (Aug 2026).
- **Tamil Nadu** cleared Prosopis from **517 villages in 32 districts** (7 May 2026); recovery depends on the landscape's earlier condition. Removal alone does not always restore landscapes; blanket eradication is debated.
#### 5.3 Global Targets – Kunming-Montreal GBF
- **Target 6:** cut the introduction and establishment of invasive alien species by at least **50% by 2030**, and eradicate or control them in priority sites.
- **Target 3 (30x30):** conserve at least **30%** of land and sea areas by 2030.
### 6. IUCN, RED LIST AND REPORTS
- **IUCN:** founded **1948**; HQ **Gland, Switzerland** (APPSC 2025); a union of governments and civil-society organisations, **NOT a UN agency**.
- **Species Survival Commission** runs specialist groups, e.g. **Invasive Species Specialist Group (ISSG)**, which developed the **Global Invasive Species Database** (1998-2000).
- IUCN World Conservation Congress 2025: **Abu Dhabi**, "Our World, Our Future".
- **CITES** regulates wildlife trade; legally binding but does not replace national laws.
- **Phoenix Species Project** (Aug 2026): **$200 million**, **Re:wild and Bezos Earth Fund**, to recover 100 most threatened species.
#### 6.1 Red Data Book / Red List
- Published by **IUCN**; lists **threatened plants and animals** (not endemic species of hotspots, not protected sites).
- IUCN's endangered species list = **Red List** (APPSC 2025); Brown, White and Black lists are distractors.
- Started **1964**; nickname "**Barometer of Life**"; measures **extinction risk** to guide conservation.
- **IUCN Green Status (2021)** measures species **recovery**.
| Page colour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red / pink | Endangered / critically endangered |
| Green | Formerly threatened, now recovered |
- Red List **2026-1 (July 2026)**: **175,909** assessed; **49,505 (~28%)** threatened (44,000+ in 2024).
- **India's Red List Roadmap:** **70,000** species by **2027**, led by the **NBA**. 7,076 Indian animal species in Red List categories.
#### 6.2 Report → Publisher
| Report / database | Publisher |
|---|---|
| Global Assessment on Biodiversity (2019): ~**25%** of species threatened | **IPBES** ("Intergovernmental Panel on Nature"), not WWF/ZSL |
| Living Planet Report / **Living Planet Index** (vertebrate populations since **1970**) | **WWF** with Zoological Society of London |
| Red List / Red Data Book | IUCN |
| Global Invasive Species Database; 100 of the World's Worst IAS | IUCN SSC ISSG |
### 7. IUCN RED LIST CATEGORIES
- **Nine** categories, highest to lowest risk: **EX → EW → CR → EN → VU → NT → LC**, plus **DD** and **NE**.
| Code | Full form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| EX | Extinct | No reasonable doubt the last individual has died, after full surveys (not automatic after ten years unrecorded) |
| EW | Extinct in the Wild | Only in captivity or cultivation, outside natural range |
| CR | **Critically Endangered** | **Extremely high** risk in the wild; more severe than EN |
| EN | Endangered | Very high risk; "species in danger of extinction" |
| VU | Vulnerable | High risk |
| NT | **Near Threatened** (not "Not Threatened") | Close to threatened |
| LC | Least Concern | Widespread, abundant |
| DD | Data Deficient | Not enough data |
| NE | Not Evaluated | Not yet assessed |
- **Threatened = CR + EN + VU.**
!TRAP APPSC 2023 and 2025 (G2 Mains P2): CR = Critically Endangered; there is no "Critically Extinct" category.
#### 7.1 Criteria A-E
| Criterion | Basis |
|---|---|
| A | Declining population |
| B | Geographic range (size, fragmentation) |
| C | Small population size |
| D | Very small or restricted population |
| E | Quantitative extinction-risk analysis (PVA) |
- Economic value in trade is NOT a criterion.

### 9. FLAGSHIP, KEYSTONE, UMBRELLA, ENDEMIC
| Term | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Flagship** | Appealing species to win public support | Tiger, Asiatic lion |
| **Keystone** | Effect far greater than its numbers; determines persistence of many other species | Tiger (apex predator), vultures (scavengers) |
| **Umbrella** | Protecting it protects many others | Tiger; pygmy hog |
| **Endemic** | Found only in one region | Red sanders, golden gecko, Gooty tarantula |
!TRAP Tiger appears as flagship, keystone and umbrella species.
