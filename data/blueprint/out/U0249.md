## U0249 | WAVELL PLAN AND SIMLA CONFERENCE; CABINET MISSION PLAN 1946; INTERIM GOVERNMENT
### 1. PLANS AND PROPOSALS 1940–1947 – THE CHAIN
#### 1.1 Plans Compared
| Plan / event | Date | Core proposal |
|---|---|---|
| **August Offer** (Linlithgow) | **Aug 1940** | Accepted the demand for an **Indian-framed constitution** after the war |
| **Cripps Mission** | **March 1942** | **Dominion Status** + constitution-making body after the war; provinces may opt out |
| **C.R. Formula** / Gandhi-Jinnah talks | **1944** (talks 9–27 Sept, Bombay) | League backs Congress; **plebiscite** in Muslim-majority areas |
| **Wavell Plan** / Simla Conference | **1945** | All Executive Council members Indian except Viceroy and C-in-C; **caste Hindu–Muslim parity** |
| **Cabinet Mission** | Arrived **24 March 1946**; plan **16 May 1946** | Weak Union, grouping of provinces, Constituent Assembly, Interim Government |
| **Mountbatten (3 June) Plan** | **3 June 1947** | Partition into **two dominions**; date advanced to **15 Aug 1947** |
- **Dickie Bird Plan / Plan Balkan** – earlier Mountbatten idea of provinces as independent successor states; **rejected by Nehru** as Balkanisation.
- Chain: Cripps → Cabinet Mission → Mountbatten turned "freedom" into "freedom with partition".
!KEY Order: August Offer (1940) → Cripps (1942) → Wavell Plan (1945) → Cabinet Mission (1946).

### 2. CRIPPS MISSION, 1942
- **Sir Stafford Cripps** – a **Labour** politician, member of the War Cabinet.
- Offered **post-war Dominion Status** and a constitution-making body; provinces could opt out.
- **Gandhi:** "a **post-dated cheque** on a crashing bank".
- **Congress Working Committee** rejected it on **7 April 1942** – no immediate self-government, no control over defence; League rejected it as it gave no clear Pakistan.
- Failure led to **Quit India (August 1942)**.
!PAIR Cripps | 1942 | Dominion Status || Cabinet Mission | 1946 | Constituent Assembly planned
### 3. LEAGUE DEMANDS, C.R. FORMULA AND DESAI-LIAQUAT PACT (1943–45)
| Demand / formula | Author / year | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| **"Divide and Quit"** | Muslim League, **Karachi session, December 1943** | Slogan answering "Quit India" |
| **C.R. Formula** | **C. Rajagopalachari, 1944** (not Wavell) | See below |
| **Desai-Liaquat Pact** | **Bhulabhai Desai** (Congress leader, Central Legislative Assembly) + **Liaquat Ali Khan**, **January 1945** | Interim government with **equal Congress and League nominees + 20% for minorities**; never formally accepted |
#### 3.1 C.R. Formula – Provisions
- League to back the independence demand and join a provisional government.
- After the war, a **plebiscite in contiguous Muslim-majority areas** (NW and NE) to decide on separation.
- If separation: **joint agreements** on defence, commerce, communications.
- **Gandhi-Jinnah talks, Bombay (9–27 Sept 1944)** on its basis failed.
!TRAP Jinnah's demands – only Muslims to vote and no common centre – were NOT in the Formula; he rejected it for lacking them.
### 4. WAVELL PLAN AND SIMLA CONFERENCE, 1945
- **Lord Wavell** (Viceroy 1943–47); plan announced **14 June 1945**; Simla Conference **25 June – 14 July 1945**.
#### 4.1 Proposals
- All Executive Council members **Indians except the Viceroy and Commander-in-Chief**.
- **Parity between caste Hindus and Muslims**.
- Indians to frame their own constitution after the war.
- Aim: **break the constitutional deadlock**, not to avoid partition.
#### 4.2 Failure and Result
- Congress led by **Maulana Azad** (Congress President **1940–46**; negotiated with Cripps and Wavell); League by **Jinnah**.
- Cause: **Jinnah demanded that the League alone name all Muslim members** – no Muslim nominee for Congress or the **Punjab Unionists**.
- Wavell **closed the conference on 14 July 1945** instead of going ahead without the League.
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Simla closed without the League | League got a **de facto veto** |
| Unionist Party of Punjab weakened | Polarised 1945–46 elections |
### 5. ELECTIONS AND EVENTS 1945–46
#### 5.1 Provincial and Central Elections, Winter 1945–46
- **Congress** swept the general seats; **League won all 30 Muslim seats** in the Central Legislative Assembly and most Muslim seats in provinces.
- **League ministries:** **Bengal** (under **H.S. Suhrawardy**, PM of Bengal 1946 – 14 Aug 1947) and **Sindh**.
- **Congress ministries:** most other provinces, **including NWFP**.
- **Punjab:** **Khizr Hayat Khan** headed a Unionist-Congress-Akali coalition.
- **Madras (AP angle):** Congress won almost all Andhra seats; **Tanguturi Prakasam** became premier on **30 April 1946** (till March 1947).
- **Communists** had backed the British war effort → heavy defeat.

### 6. CABINET MISSION, 1946
#### 6.1 Origin and Members
- Sent by PM **Clement Attlee** (British PM at independence); dispatch announced **19 February 1946**; arrived **24 March 1946**; plan **16 May 1946**. Called the "**Ministerial Trio Mission**".
| Member | Office in Britain |
|---|---|
| **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** (leader) | **Secretary of State for India** |
| **Sir Stafford Cripps** | **President of the Board of Trade** |
| **A.V. Alexander** | **First Lord of the Admiralty** |
!TRAP Not members: **John Simon** (Simon Commission), William Wood.
- Purpose: set up machinery to frame the constitution + arrange an Interim Government; **last British attempt to keep India united**.
- **Rejected full Pakistan**: non-Muslims were **38% in the NW** and **48% in the NE** zone.
#### 6.2 Three-Tier Structure
- **Tier 1 – Union** with a weak Centre: only **Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications**.
- **Tier 2 – Groups** of provinces.
- **Tier 3 – Provinces**, holding all **residuary subjects**.
| Group | Provinces | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa | Hindu-majority |
| **B** | Punjab, NWFP, Sindh | Muslim-majority (NW) |
| **C** | Bengal, Assam | Muslim-majority (NE) |
- Interim Cabinet: all members Indians; minimum Viceroy interference. "Federal Union of British provinces" proposal of May 1946 = Cabinet Mission.
- **Gandhi** was totally in favour of the plan; its rejection was the last chance to avoid partition.
### 7. LEAGUE'S ACCEPTANCE, WITHDRAWAL AND DIRECT ACTION
| Party | Stand |
|---|---|
| **Congress** | Accepted the Constituent Assembly scheme; **grouping optional** (NWFP, Assam could opt out) |
| **League** | Accepted (June 1946) holding **grouping compulsory** → stepping stone to Pakistan |
- Trigger: **Nehru's statement of 10 July 1946** that Congress could change the scheme through its Assembly majority → **Jinnah withdrew**.
- League called for **"Direct Action" on 29 July 1946**.
- **Direct Action Day – 16 August 1946, Calcutta** – the **"Great Calcutta Killings"**; riots spread to **Noakhali**, Bihar and north India.
- **Gandhi at Noakhali** (Nov 1946 – Mar 1947): walked village to village for peace but **failed to stop the violence**.
!TRAP 29 July 1946 = call; 16 August 1946 = Direct Action Day. In 1946 the League also joined the Interim Government – true, but not the reason for withdrawal.
### 8. CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY
- Demand: **M.N. Roy 1934** → Congress 1935 → **August Offer accepted it 1940**.
- **Envisaged by the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)** – not Cripps, not Mountbatten.
- Strength **389 = 296 British India + 93 princely states**; about **1 seat per 10 lakh**.
- Provincial members elected **community-wise** (General, Muslim, Sikh) by provincial assemblies through **PR-STV**; princely members **nominated by rulers**.
- **July 1946 elections:** **Congress 208, League 73, others 15** (= 296).
- First met **9 December 1946**; League refused to attend.
!NUM 389 = 296 + 93; 296 = 208 + 73 + 15

### 10. MOUNTBATTEN, INDEPENDENCE ACT AND PARTITION
#### 10.1 Office-holders at Independence
| Office | Holder |
|---|---|
| Last Viceroy | **Lord Mountbatten** (replaced Wavell, March 1947) |
| First Governor-General of India | **Mountbatten** (15 Aug 1947 – 21 June 1948) |
| Governor-General of Pakistan | **Jinnah** |
| Congress President | **J.B. Kripalani** (1946–47) |
| British PM | **Clement Attlee** |
| British monarch | **George VI** |
#### 10.2 Indian Independence Act, 1947
- Based on the 3 June Plan; **royal assent 18 July 1947**.
- Created **two independent dominions**, India and Pakistan, from **15 August 1947**.
- Each dominion its own Governor-General; a **common Governor-General** allowed if both agreed.
- "Emperor of India" dropped from the British monarch's title.
- A princely state could **join India, join Pakistan or remain independent**.
#### 10.3 Partition Boundaries
| Province | Method of decision |
|---|---|
| **Punjab, Bengal** | Assemblies met in two parts; both voted to divide |
| **NWFP** | **Referendum** – joined Pakistan (boycotted by Khudai Khidmatgars) |
| **Sylhet** (Assam) | **Referendum** – joined East Pakistan |
| **Sindh** | Own assembly decided |
- **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** – British lawyer with no knowledge of India; chaired **both the Punjab and Bengal Boundary Commissions**; award published **17 August 1947**.
