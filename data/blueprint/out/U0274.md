## U0274 | CONSTITUTIONAL EVOLUTION – REGULATING ACT 1773 TO THE GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT 1935
### 1. BEFORE 1773 AND THE REGULATING ACT, 1773
#### 1.1 Background
- **Act of 1767**: Company to pay the British Government **£400,000 a year** (APPSC G2 2017).
- **1772**: famine, falling revenue and the Company's financial crisis (loan of about **£1 million** sought) → Parliament intervened in Bengal.
- **Lord North** committee report → Regulating Act.
#### 1.2 Regulating Act, 1773
- **First** step by Parliament to **control the EIC**.
- **Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal**: **Warren Hastings** (first) with a **4-member Executive Council**.
- **Bombay and Madras** made subordinate to Bengal.
- **Supreme Court at Calcutta** (Fort William), set up **1774**: **1 Chief Justice + 3 judges**; first CJ **Sir Elijah Impey**.
- Banned **private trade and bribes** by Company officials.
- Directors to send important letters on revenue, civil and military affairs to the British Government.
- Did **NOT abolish the Court of Directors** (it lasted till **1858**).
- **Amending Act 1781 (Act of Settlement)**: separated judicial and executive functions.
!TRAP "Regulating Act abolished the Court of Directors" = the incorrect statement (APPSC GS 2019).
### 2. PITT'S INDIA ACT 1784 AND THE ACT OF 1786
- **Dual Control / double government**: **Board of Control** (political) + **Court of Directors** (commercial).
- Board of Control: **6 members**, incl. a Secretary of State and the Chancellor of the Exchequer.
- Company territories = **"British possessions in India"**; Company a subordinate department of the State.
- GG's council **cut to 3**.
- **Act of 1786**: GG may **override his Council** and may also act as **Commander-in-Chief** (two separate powers); first used by **Lord Cornwallis**.
!KEY Board of Control was set up by Pitt's India Act 1784 (UPPSC 2015).
### 3. CHARTER ACTS 1793–1853
#### 3.1 Act-wise Provisions
| Act | Key provisions |
|---|---|
| **1793** | Monopoly extended **20 years**; override power extended to all future GGs and Presidency Governors; more control over Bombay and Madras; Board of Control salaries from **Indian revenue**; C-in-C not ordinarily a council member |
| **1813** | Trade monopoly **ended except tea and China trade**; **Christian missionaries** allowed (Charles Grant, Wilberforce); **Rs 1 lakh** a year for education – first such provision; asserted **Crown sovereignty** over Company territories |
| **1833 (Saint Helena Act)** | **Governor-General of India** created (**William Bentinck**, first; also last GG of Bengal); monopoly **completely ended** – Company purely administrative; **Law Member** added (council to 4) – **Macaulay**; **Law Commission** under Macaulay (origin of Law Ministry); legislation centralised in GG-in-Council; **Section 87**: no Indian debarred by religion, birth, descent or colour; open competition only **attempted** |
| **1853 (last Charter Act)** | **Legislative and executive** functions of the GG's council **separated**; **6 legislative members** added (Indian Legislative Council – mini-parliament); **open competition** put into operation (**Macaulay Committee 1854**); territories held **"in trust for Her Majesty"**, no fixed term (APPSC GS 2018) |
- Order: Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Act 1793 → Charter Act 1813.
- English education: 1813 grant → General Committee of Public Instruction (1823) → Orientalist–Anglicist controversy.
!TRAP 1813 kept tea and China trade; 1833 ended all trade. Calling the 1813 Act the "Magna Carta" is the wrong statement.
!TRAP 1833 Act did NOT provide that an Indian be Law Member; first Law Member = Macaulay, not Impey.
### 4. CROWN RULE – GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1858
- Rule transferred from the **Company to the Crown**.
- Governor-General also styled **Viceroy**: **Lord Canning** first; last Viceroy **Mountbatten**.
- **Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished**.
- **Secretary of State for India** created, with a **15-member Council of India**.
### 5. INDIAN COUNCILS ACTS 1861 AND 1892
#### 5.1 Indian Councils Act, 1861 (passed 1 August 1861)
- Restored legislative powers of **Bombay and Madras** (decentralisation).
- Viceroy nominated Indians as non-officials: **Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao** (by Canning) → beginning of representative institutions.
- **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** criticised lack of Indians (lesson of 1857) – he was NOT a nominee.
- **Portfolio system**: begun by **Canning (1859)**, statutory recognition in 1861.
- Viceroy's **ordinance** power – at most **six months**.
- New provincial councils: Bengal (1862), Punjab (1897).
- **Indian High Courts Act 1861** → High Courts of **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1862)**.
#### 5.2 Indian Councils Act, 1892
- **Indirect election** began: nomination on recommendation of **universities, municipalities, chambers of commerce** (APPSC GS 2019).
- **Budget discussion** (annual financial statement), **no vote**.
- Questions with **6 days' notice**; **no supplementary** questions.
#### 5.3 1861 vs 1892
| Feature | 1861 | 1892 |
|---|---|---|
| Indians | Nominated non-officials | More non-officials |
| Election | None | Indirect begins |
| Budget | No discussion | Discussion, no vote |
| Other | Portfolio, ordinance | Right to ask questions |
### 6. INDIAN COUNCILS ACT, 1909 – MORLEY-MINTO REFORMS
- **John Morley** = Secretary of State; **Lord Minto** = Viceroy, **"Father of Communal Electorate"**.
- **Separate electorate for Muslims**; formal elected representation (2018 key: Act allowing elected representation = 1909).
- Indians first in Viceroy's and Governors' Executive Councils: **S.P. Sinha**, first Indian, as **Law Member**.
- Central council: additional members to **60** (total **69**: 37 officials, 32 non-officials); **official majority** at Centre, non-official majority in provinces; still **unicameral**.
- **Supplementary questions**, budget discussion and resolutions allowed.
- No dyarchy, no provincial autonomy; most **short-lived** experiment (about a decade); sowed seeds of separatism.
### 7. GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1919 – MONTAGU-CHELMSFORD
#### 7.1 Basis
- **Montagu's declaration, 20 August 1917**: gradual responsible government; **Montagu-Chelmsford Report (1918)**.
- **Edwin Montagu** = Secretary of State; **Chelmsford** = Viceroy.
#### 7.2 Provisions
- **Provincial dyarchy** (Greek "di-arche", double rule) – first time.
- **Bicameral Centre** first time: **Council of State** (upper, **60**) + **Legislative Assembly** (lower); **direct elections**, limited franchise (property, tax, education).
- Term **"responsible government"** first used; central and provincial **budgets separated**; Central and Provincial lists defined.
- **Communal representation extended** to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans**.
- 3 of 6 Viceroy's Executive Council members Indian; **High Commissioner for India in London**; separate **ICS examination in India**.
- **Chamber of Princes**: **120** members; George V's Proclamation **23 Dec 1919**; first Chancellor **Ganga Singh of Bikaner**.
- **Public Accounts Committee** 1921; **PSC** provided, set up **1 October 1926** (Chairman + 4), became Federal PSC under 1935.
- **Statutory commission after 10 years** → **Simon Commission** appointed **1927** (two years early), 7 members, all British, chaired by **Sir John Simon**; boycotted ("Simon Go Back").
- Last Indian Legislature election under this Act: **1945**.
#### 7.3 Reserved vs Transferred Subjects
| Reserved (Governor + Executive Council) | Transferred (Governor + Indian Ministers) |
|---|---|
| Law and order, police, **administration of justice**, land revenue, irrigation, finance | **Local self-government**, education, public health, agriculture, public works |
### 8. GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1935
#### 8.1 Basis and Size
- Based on **Third Round Table Conference** and the **White Paper**; **321 sections, 14 parts, 10 schedules**.
#### 8.2 Federation and Centre
- **All-India Federation** ("Federation of India") of provinces + princely states – joining **optional**, no state joined → **never formed**.
- Princes included to counter-balance nationalist leaders.
- **Dyarchy at the Centre** proposed – **never worked**; **defence and foreign affairs** = reserved under the **GG**.
- Bicameral Centre: **Federal Assembly + Council of States**.
- **Three lists** (Federal, Provincial, Concurrent); **residuary powers with the Governor-General**.
- **Section 42 → Art. 123** (ordinance); **Section 63 → Art. 175** (Governor's address; not mandatory, 2026 row in Opposition States).
- **Council of India abolished**; Secretary of State given advisers.
#### 8.3 Provinces
- **Provincial dyarchy abolished** → **provincial autonomy**, responsible ministries from **April 1937** (APPSC GS 2025).
- **Bicameral in 6**: **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces** – NOT Punjab.
- Communal representation extended to **depressed classes, women, labour**.
- **Burma** separated; **Sindh** from Bombay; **Orissa** from Bihar.
- Franchise about **10%** of population.
- Congress did not form a ministry in **Punjab** (1937).
#### 8.4 Institutions
- **Federal Court**, Delhi, inaugurated **1 October 1937**; first CJ **Sir Maurice Gwyer**; replaced by the Supreme Court in **1950**.
- **RBI** formed **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act 1934**; Federal, Provincial and Joint **PSCs**.
- Carried into the Constitution: federal scheme, Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions, administrative details; main source of Centre–State distribution.
!TRAP UPSC 2024: "All-India Federation" correct; "Defence and Foreign Affairs under the federal legislature" WRONG.

#### FIRST / LAST / SPECIAL FACTS
- First parliamentary control: 1773; last Charter Act: 1853.
- First Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council: S.P. Sinha (1909).
- Indirect election 1892; separate electorate 1909; direct election 1919.
- Bicameral: Centre 1919; provinces (6) 1935.
- PSC provided 1919, set up 1926; RBI provided by 1935 Act, formed under 1934 Act.

### 12. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS

3. Provincial dyarchy = Montagu-Chelmsford (1919), not Morley-Minto.
4. Chamber of Princes = 1919, not 1909 or 1935.
5. 1935 bicameral provinces exclude Punjab.
6. Residuary powers in 1935 = Governor-General, not provinces.
7. 1935 did NOT bring dyarchy in both Centre and provinces.
8. Court of Directors abolished in 1858, not 1773.
9. Sir Syed Ahmad Khan was not a 1861 nominee.
10. Rs 1 lakh education grant = 1813.
