## U0163 | ANGLO-FRENCH RIVALRY – THE THREE CARNATIC WARS AND THE ENGLISH ASCENDANCY
### 1. THE THREE CARNATIC WARS – OVERVIEW
- The **Carnatic Wars** were fought between the **English and the French** companies in South India.
- Disciplined European infantry beat much larger Indian armies; the wars made the **English the kingmakers of South India**.
| War | Years | European context | Concluding treaty | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | **1746–48** | Extension of the **War of Austrian Succession** (Europe, 1740) | **Aix-la-Chapelle** (18 Oct 1748) | Madras returned to the English in exchange for **Louisburg** (North America); politically inconclusive |
| Second | **1749–54** | No European war; **Hyderabad and Carnatic successions** | **Pondicherry** (1754) | Inconclusive; Dupleix recalled |
| Third | **1756–63** (UPSC: 1758–63) | Extension of the **Seven Years' War** (1756–63) | **Paris** (1763) | Decisive English victory; French left with trading enclaves only |
- **Chronology:** capture of Madras (1746) → Aix-la-Chapelle (1748) → Pondicherry (1754) → Paris (1763).
!TRAP The Second war was NOT ended by the Treaty of **Yandabo** – that closed the First Anglo-Burmese War (1826).
### 2. CAUSES AND PARTICIPANTS
#### 2.1 First War (1746–48)
- Offshoot of Anglo-French rivalry in the Austrian War of Succession.
- **Immediate cause:** British **Commodore Barnett** captured some French ships.
- Fought between the **British and the French**; Nawab **Anwaruddin** of the Carnatic sided with the English.
- The First war did **not** bring lasting British supremacy.
#### 2.2 Second War (1749–54) – two succession disputes
| Dispute | English candidate | French candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Hyderabad (after **Nizam-ul-Mulk** died, 1748) | **Nasir Jang** (son) | **Muzaffar Jang** (grandson) |
| Carnatic | Anwaruddin; after 1749 his son **Muhammad Ali** | **Chanda Sahib** |
#### 2.3 Third War (1756–63)
- Part of the Seven Years' War; it began before the Treaty of Pondicherry could be ratified.
### 3. DUPLEIX AND THE POLICY OF INTERFERENCE
- **Dupleix** – Governor of **Pondicherry from 1742**; architect of **interference in Indian politics** through succession disputes.
- Backed Muzaffar Jang and Chanda Sahib; after **Ambur** Muzaffar Jang became **Subedar of the Deccan** and rewarded the French with grants.
- **Capture of Madras (Fort St. George), 1746:** taken by **La Bourdonnais** (Governor of **Mauritius**) with his fleet; he agreed to ransom Madras back to the English, but **Dupleix overruled him** and kept it.
- **Godeheu** replaced Dupleix and made peace.
### 4. TREATY OF PONDICHERRY (1754)
- Both companies **not to interfere** in the quarrels of Indian princes.
- Each kept its **existing possessions**; Dupleix recalled.
- Failed: the **Seven Years' War** broke out before England and France ratified it → Third war.

### 7. REASONS FOR ENGLISH SUCCESS
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Naval superiority** | Beat d'Aché's fleet off Pondicherry (1759); he withdrew to Mauritius; French naval strength badly reduced (Voltaire: hardly a battleship left) |
| **Bengal's resources after Plassey (1757)** | Paid for the southern wars; Clive also took **Chandannagar** |
| Better commanders | Clive, Lawrence, Coote |
| Company nature | English = **private body run for profit**; French = **state-controlled** |
| Strong army | French failure in the Deccan: the English had a strong army |
- **French failures:** recall of Dupleix; Lally quarrelled with officials and troops were unpaid; failures at Madras and Thanjavur; Bussy pulled out of Hyderabad.
### 8. FRENCH IN ANDHRA AND THE NORTHERN CIRCARS
| Place | Year | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Surat | 1668 | **First** French factory |
| **Masulipatnam** | 1669 | **Second** factory; patent from the Sultan of **Golconda** |
| **Yanam** | 1723 | French from 1723; enclave in AP's **Godavari delta**, part of **Puducherry UT** (not an AP district) |
| Tumnapalem | 1756 | Bussy won with **Vijayaramaraju** of Vizianagaram → **Treaty of Charmahal** |
| **Chendurthi** | 1758 | **"Plassey of Andhra"** (Malleson): drove the French out of the Northern Circars |
| Pondicherry | 1954 | Handed to India 1954; UT by **14th Amendment (1962)**; renamed **Puducherry 2006** (not 1987) |
- **Northern Circars:** won for the French by Bussy (1753); lost after Chendurthi and the fall of **Machilipatnam (1759)** to Forde.
- AP angle: the war in Andhra (Bobbili, Chendurthi, Machilipatnam) decided who held the Northern Circars.
- After 1763 the French kept **Pondicherry, Karaikal, Yanam, Mahe and Chandernagore**; Karaikal is in Tamil Nadu.

### 11. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS

2. **Yandabo** is Anglo-Burmese, not Carnatic.
3. **Pondicherry 1754** was inconclusive and never ratified before the Third war began.
4. **La Bourdonnais** took Madras; **Dupleix** refused the ransom.
5. **Lally** lost Wandiwash to **Coote**, not to Clive.
6. **Nasir Jang** (son) = English; **Muzaffar Jang** (grandson) = French.
7. **Yanam** is part of Puducherry UT, not an AP district.
8. Renamed Puducherry in **2006**, not 1987.
9. French company was **state-controlled**; English was **private**.
