## U0644 | WORLD POPULATION - DISTRIBUTION, DENSITY, FACTORS AND THEORIES (MALTHUS)
### 1. MALTHUSIAN THEORY
#### 1.1 Core Logic
- **Thomas Malthus** (18th century), **An Essay on the Principle of Population**, **1798**.
- Population grows **geometrically** (exponentially); food grows only **arithmetically**.
- Unchecked growth leads to **scarcity and famine**.
- Malthus = the scientist whose theory says population is too large with severe consequences: the **overpopulation** theory (APPSC).
#### 1.2 Two Checks
| Check | Acts on | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Preventive** | Lowers births | Moral restraint, late marriage, celibacy |
| **Positive** | Raises deaths | Famine, disease, war |
!TRAP Preventive = fewer births; Positive = more deaths. Do not swap them.
#### 1.3 Criticism
- **Technology, innovation and sustainable resource management** outpaced his predictions.
- "Population Bomb" fears drove policy for decades; now overtaken by **fertility decline**.
### 2. NEO-MALTHUSIAN WORKS AND OTHER THEORIES
#### 2.1 Book - Author
| Book / Report | Author | Year |
|---|---|---|
| An Essay on the Principle of Population | Thomas Malthus | 1798 |
| **The Population Bomb** | **Paul R. Ehrlich** | 1968 |
| **The Limits to Growth** | Donella Meadows and others, for the **Club of Rome** | 1972 |
- The Population Bomb opens with "**The battle to feed all of humanity is over**" (APPSC 2019).
- Limits to Growth used the **World3** computer model: growth limits reached within about 100 years if trends continue.
- "**New Population Bomb**" = rapid **urban growth in the Third World**.
#### 2.2 Other Theories
| Theory | Thinker | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Demographic transition** | Warren Thompson (1929); Frank **Notestein** (1945, named it) | Death rates fall first with industrialisation, birth rates later |
| **Optimum population** | **Edwin Cannan** (also Marshall, Sidgwick) | Size that gives the highest output per head; beyond it = surplus |
| Marxian / anti-Malthusian | Karl Marx | Hunger comes from exploitation, not overpopulation |
### 3. DISTRIBUTION OF WORLD POPULATION
- Most people live in the **Northern Hemisphere mid-latitudes** and **tropics of Asia**.
- Two major clusters: **East Asia** (eastern China, Japan) and **South Asia** (Ganga plains, Bangladesh delta).
- **Ganges-Brahmaputra delta**: among the most densely populated areas on Earth.
### 4. FACTORS AFFECTING DISTRIBUTION
#### 4.1 Physical vs Socio-economic
| Factor | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Water | River valleys densest | **Nile valley** |
| Landform | Plains favoured, mountains sparse | Ganga plains |
| Climate | Moderate climates attract; hot/cold deserts repel | Sahara, Arctic |
| Soil | Fertile alluvium supports dense farming | **Java, eastern China** |
| Minerals | Mining draws workers | **Katanga-Zambia** copper belt |
| Industry, urbanisation | Jobs concentrate people | **Kobe-Osaka** |
| Social-cultural | Religious centres attract; unrest repels | Pilgrim towns |
#### 4.2 The Exception
- **North-eastern USA** (New York, Philadelphia, Boston, Pittsburgh, Buffalo): dense but **not directly dependent on agriculture**; due to **industrial and urban** development (MPPSC 2021).
- Nile valley, Java, eastern China = agriculture-based dense areas.
!TRAP Odd-one-out: North-eastern USA is industrial; Nile, Java, eastern China are agricultural.
