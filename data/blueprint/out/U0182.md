## U0182 | REVOLT OF 1857 – POLITICAL CAUSES
### 1. SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE (WELLESLEY, 1798)
- Ruler keeps a **British subsidiary force at his cost**, accepts a **Resident**, gives up **independent foreign policy** (no other Europeans in service).
| State | Year |
|---|---|
| **Nizam of Hyderabad (FIRST)** | Sep **1798**, again 1800 |
| Mysore / Tanjore | 1799 |
| Awadh | Nov 1801 |
| Peshwa | Dec 1802 (**Treaty of Bassein**) |
| Bhonsle of Berar / Scindia | Dec 1803 / Feb 1804 |
| Holkar (**last Maratha**) | 1818 |
- **AP angle:** 1800 – Nizam ceded **Rayalaseema** to pay for the force = **"Ceded Districts"**.
### 2. DOCTRINE OF LAPSE (DALHOUSIE, 1848-56)
- No **natural male heir** → state lapses; adopted son inherits **private property only**, not the throne.
| State | Year |
|---|---|
| **Satara** (first) | 1848 |
| Sambalpur | 1849 |
| Baghat | 1850 |
| **Jhansi** (Damodar Rao not recognised) | 1853 |
| **Nagpur** (Raghuji III) | 1854 |
- **Jaipur** was NOT annexed under lapse.
- Withdrawn by **Queen Victoria's Proclamation**, read by **Canning** at the **Allahabad Durbar, 1 Nov 1858**; adoption accepted. Princes became **"breakwaters to the storm"** (Canning); after 1858 protected in return for **paramountcy**.
- **Rani Chennamma** (Kittur) – British refused to recognise her adopted son.
### 3. OTHER ANNEXATIONS
| State | Ground | Ruler |
|---|---|---|
| **Punjab** (1849) | Conquest – Second Anglo-Sikh War | Duleep Singh |
| **Awadh** (Feb 1856) | **"Misgovernment"**, not lapse | **Wajid Ali Shah** (exiled to Calcutta) |
#### 3.1 Awadh Effects
- **Summary Revenue Settlement 1856** – directly with village proprietors; **taluqdars** lost estates and arms, forts destroyed.
- **Bengal Army** sepoys hurt – families in Awadh bore the new revenue burden.
- Wajid Ali Shah's verse "Daro-deewar pe hasrat ki nazar karte hain..." was sung by revolutionaries in prison.
!TRAP Taluqdars "allowed to retain arms and forts" = FALSE.
### 4. TITLES, PENSIONS, MUGHAL SUCCESSION
| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|

- Fear of annexation turned a sepoy mutiny into a wider revolt.
