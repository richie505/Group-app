## U0346 | ATTORNEY GENERAL AND ADVOCATE GENERAL; SOLICITOR GENERAL
### 1. LAW OFFICERS – AN OVERVIEW
- **Attorney General (AG)** = **highest / first law officer of India** and **chief legal adviser** to GoI (APPSC 2018, 2019).
- Only the **AG** (Union) and **Advocate General** (State) are created by the Constitution; **SG and ASGs are statutory**.
- The **Chief Justice of India** is head of the judiciary, **NOT** the government's first law officer; the **Law Minister** handles legal matters at government level.
#### 1.1 AG vs SG vs Advocate General
| Basis | Attorney General | Solicitor General | Advocate General |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basis | **Art. 76, Part V** | Statutory (no Article) | **Art. 165, Part VI** |
| Serves | Union Government | Assists the AG | State Government |
| Appointed by | **President** | **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** | **Governor** |
| Qualification | Of **SC judge** (Art. 124(3)) | Distinguished senior advocate | Of **HC judge** (Art. 217) |
| Tenure | Pleasure of President | Fixed statutory term (**3 years**) | Pleasure of Governor |
| Pay fixed by | **President** | – | **Governor** |
| Floor rights | Parliament, **Art. 88** | **None** | State Legislature, **Art. 177** |
| Privileges | **Art. 105(4)** | – | **Art. 194(4)** |
| Precedence | **11** | **23** | **25** (with ASGs) |
!PAIR Article 76 | Attorney General | Union || Article 165 | Advocate General | State
!PAIR Article 88 | AG in Parliament | no vote || Article 177 | Advocate General in State Legislature | no vote
### 2. ATTORNEY GENERAL – ARTICLE 76
#### 2.1 Appointment and Qualification
- **Art. 76(1)**: appointed by the **President** (on the government's advice); must be **qualified to be a Supreme Court judge**.
- Qualifications (= SC judge): **citizen of India** and one of:
1. **High Court judge** for at least **5 years**.
2. **Advocate of a High Court** for at least **10 years**.
3. **Distinguished jurist** in the opinion of the **President**.
#### 2.2 Term and Removal
- **No fixed tenure** in the Constitution; holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (Art. 76(4)) – NOT the Prime Minister.
- **No grounds or procedure** for removal given; removable **at any time**.
- **Resigns** to the President; by custom quits when the **Council of Ministers changes**.
- **Remuneration** determined by the **President** – not by Parliament; **not charged** on the Consolidated Fund.
- **Not a member of the Union Cabinet**; part of the **Union Executive**.
!TRAP APPSC-G2 2025: "AG has a fixed (five-year) term" = FALSE. "Qualified to be an SC judge" and "advises GoI" = correct.
### 3. DUTIES OF THE ATTORNEY GENERAL
1. **Advise GoI** on legal matters referred by the **President**.
2. **Appear for GoI** in all **Supreme Court** cases concerning it.
3. Represent GoI in **Art. 143** (Presidential reference) cases.
4. Appear in **any High Court** when required.
5. Other legal duties assigned by the President.
- Matters go to the AG through the **Ministry of Law**; only legal matters of **real importance**.
- The AG does **NOT** chair **joint sittings** of Parliament.
### 4. RIGHTS AND LIMITATIONS OF THE AG
#### 4.1 Rights
- **Right of audience** in **all courts** in India.
- **Art. 88**: right to **speak and take part** in proceedings of **either House**, joint sittings and **committees** of which he is named – **without being a member**.
- **No right to vote**.
- **Art. 105(4)**: all **privileges and immunities** of an MP.
#### 4.2 Limitations
- Should **not advise or hold a brief against** the Government of India.
- Should **not defend an accused** in a criminal prosecution **without permission** of GoI.
- **Not a full-time government servant**, so **not barred from private legal practice**.
!KEY AG can attend and speak in Parliament but can never vote – same for the Advocate General in the State Legislature.
### 5. SOLICITOR GENERAL AND ADDITIONAL SOLICITORS GENERAL
- **Not mentioned in the Constitution**; statutory, governed by the **Law Officers (Terms and Conditions) Rules, 1987**.
- **SG = second law officer**; assists the AG; helped by several **ASGs**.
- Appointed by the Centre through the **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet**; normal term **3 years**.
- Appears in courts for the Union and also **tenders legal advice**.
- SG has **no Art. 88** floor rights in Parliament.
- **AG and SG** are **ex officio members** of the **Bar Council of India** (**Advocates Act, 1961**).
- Convention: law officers resign when a new government comes in.
!TRAP The Constitution does not govern the SG/ASGs – their rules are executive (1987).
### 6. ADVOCATE GENERAL – ARTICLE 165
#### 6.1 Office and Appointment
- **Highest law officer of the State** and official legal adviser to the State Government – the State counterpart of the AG (Art. 76).
- Appointed by the **Governor** (Art. 165(1)) – not the President.
- Qualification: qualified to be a **High Court judge** (Art. 217).
- **No fixed tenure**; holds office **during the pleasure of the Governor** (Art. 165(3)); changes with the State government by convention.
- **Remuneration fixed by the Governor**.
- **Duties (Art. 165(2))**: advises the State Government; other legal duties assigned by the Governor.
- Assisted by **Additional Advocates General**; order of precedence **No. 25**, below the ASG.
!TRAP "Holds office during the pleasure of the State Cabinet / Chief Minister" = FALSE; it is the Governor.
#### 6.2 Rights
- **Art. 177**: speak and take part in **either House** of the State Legislature and its committees – **no vote**.
- **Right of audience** in any court **in the State** (AG: all courts in India).
- **Art. 194(4)**: privileges of a member of the State Legislature.
- HC judges are appointed by the **President**; the Advocate General by the **Governor**.
