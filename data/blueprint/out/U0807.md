## U0807 | HEALTHCARE DELIVERY IN INDIA – THREE-TIER STRUCTURE, HUMAN RESOURCES AND INFRASTRUCTURE
### 1. THREE-TIER STRUCTURE
#### 1.1 Tier, Facility and Population Norm
| Tier | Facility | Norm (plain / hilly-tribal) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | **Sub Health Centre** – **first contact point** | **5,000 / 3,000** |
| Primary | **PHC** – referral for SHCs | **30,000 / 20,000** |
| Secondary | **CHC** – referral for PHCs; OT and beds | **1,20,000 / 80,000** |
| Secondary | District hospital, specialty hospitals | District |
| Tertiary | Medical colleges, AIIMS, super-specialty | Region / state |
- **Urban PHC**: 25,000–30,000 people within 2–3 km.
!TRAP The PHC belongs to the PRIMARY tier; the CHC is SECONDARY.
#### 1.2 Facility Counts
- **23,391 PHCs** in India (**December 2021**).
- Health Dynamics of India 2022-23 (Mar 2023): **1,69,615 SHCs**, **31,882 PHCs** (25,354 rural), **6,359 CHCs** (5,491 rural), **714 district hospitals**; urban PHC shortfall 36.7%.
!NUM 5,000 = SHC; 30,000 = PHC; 1,20,000 = CHC (plain areas).
### 2. HEALTH COMMITTEES
| Committee | Year | Key recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhore** (Health Survey and Development) | **1943–46** (earliest) | Appointed "**to survey the state of public health**"; integrated preventive and curative care; three-tier PHC system |
| **Mudaliar** (Health Survey and Planning) | **1959–62** | **Consolidate existing PHCs** before expanding; strengthen district hospitals |
| **Kartar Singh** | **1973** | **Multipurpose health workers**; one PHC per **50,000** |
| **Shrivastava** | **1975** | **Village health guides** (community volunteers); **Referral Services Complex** |
### 3. INDIAN PUBLIC HEALTH STANDARDS
- **IPHS 2022** = latest revision; norms for SHC/HWC, PHC, CHC, sub-district and district hospitals.
### 4. AYUSHMAN AAROGYA MANDIRS
- Launched **2018** as **Health and Wellness Centres**, by upgrading SHCs and PHCs; **1,50,000 by Dec 2022**; renamed Ayushman Arogya Mandirs.
- **2026**: more than **1.79 lakh** functioning.
- **CPHC package**: maternal and child health, communicable diseases, **NCD screening (hypertension, diabetes, oral, breast, cervical cancer)**, geriatric and palliative care, trauma care, Yoga and wellness.

### 6. HUMAN RESOURCES
- Doctor-population ratio: about **1:834** (NMC-registered allopathic doctors) vs **WHO norm 1:1000**.
- Real availability is lower – **doctors gather in cities**.
- **Nurse ratio** below global norms.
- **Specialists**: only **4,413 of 21,964** required at rural CHCs (Mar 2023) – shortfall **~80%**; Uttar Pradesh has the largest gap.
!TRAP 1:834 looks better than the WHO norm, but urban clustering makes rural availability far worse.
### 7. PUBLIC VS PRIVATE DELIVERY
| Indicator | Figure |
|---|---|
| **NFHS-3** | Private sector = main source of care for **70% of urban households** |
| Private inpatient share | Over **60%** |
| Private outpatient share | About **70%** |
| Average hospitalisation (Aug 2026) | Rs 50,508 private vs Rs 6,631 government |
| Urban skew | **70% of infrastructure** for **30% of population** |
- Public system: **mostly curative**, with limited **preventive, promotive and rehabilitative** care.
- Private sector leads **secondary, tertiary and quaternary** care in cities.
- Concerns: high **out-of-pocket expenditure**; "**missing middle**" of over **40 crore** people without cover; urban concentration of doctors.
- **Aug 2026**: Parliamentary Standing Committee's **176th Report** tabled (7 Aug 2026).
