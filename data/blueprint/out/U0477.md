## U0477 | UNEMPLOYMENT - TYPES (STRUCTURAL, FRICTIONAL, CYCLICAL, DISGUISED, SEASONAL, EDUCATED)
### 1. TYPES OF UNEMPLOYMENT
#### 1.1 Type → Cause → Where Predominant
| Type | Cause / feature | Example | Where predominant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frictional** | Transition between jobs; voluntary, short-term | Graduate seeking first job | - |
| **Structural** | Skill or geography mismatch; long-term | Workers displaced by automation | Whole economy, long run |
| **Cyclical** | Fall in **aggregate demand** (recession) | Layoffs in a recession | Industry and services |
| **Seasonal** | Predictable seasonal demand shifts | Farm labour in off-season | **Agriculture**, tourism, agro-industries |
| **Disguised** | Surplus labour, **MPL = 0** | Five family members on a plot needing two | **Agriculture**, family farms, rural |
| **Educated** | Qualified persons fail to find matching jobs | - | **Urban** |
| **Technological** | Change in technology | - | Manufacturing, routine services |
| Underemployment | Not using full skills or hours | Engineer working part-time as cashier | - |
| Institutional | Labour laws, high minimum wages, union rules | Hiring halted due to rigid layoff rules | - |
| Vulnerable | No formal contract or social security | Construction labour, street vending | Informal sector |
#### 1.2 Rural vs Urban
- NCERT rule: **rural = seasonal + disguised**; **urban = educated** unemployment.
- Under **PLFS**, open unemployment is higher in **urban** areas. AP: rural 3.4%, urban 5.9% (2024-25).
- Unemployment correlated with agriculture (off-season months) = **seasonal**.
!TRAP APPSC 2025 asked "predominant in agriculture" twice: **Disguised** (GS) and **Seasonal** (G2, where disguised was not an option). Read the options.
### 2. DISGUISED UNEMPLOYMENT
- More workers than needed; **marginal productivity of labour is zero** (or negative).
- **Identification:** withdrawing some workers leaves **total output unchanged**.
- Appear employed but add nothing; common among family members in agriculture.
- A **hidden** (not open) type; **not captured in PLFS headline rates**.
!KEY Extra labour with zero output = **disguised unemployment**, not underemployment.
### 3. CYCLICAL UNEMPLOYMENT
- Cause: fall in aggregate demand during **recession/depression**; firms lay off workers to cut costs.
- "Unemployment caused by depression in an economy" = **Cyclical** (APPSC 2019).
- Keynes: unemployment in developed economies = **deficiency in effective demand**.
### 4. FRICTIONAL VS STRUCTURAL
| Feature | Frictional (search) | Structural |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Short-term | Long-term |
| Cause | Time lag while searching/changing jobs | Skill-job mismatch; technology or demand change |
| Nature | Normal; exists even at full employment | Deep-rooted; needs reskilling/reform |
| Example | Worker quits to find a better job | Labour-intensive economy turning **capital-intensive** |
- At **full employment**, frictional and structural unemployment may still exist.
### 5. TECHNOLOGICAL UNEMPLOYMENT
- Job loss due to change in technology; a **subtype of structural** unemployment.
- **Automation** in manufacturing and **AI** in services may reduce demand for low-skilled, routine jobs.
### 6. OTHER CLASSIFICATIONS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Able and willing to work, no job at all |
| **Hidden/disguised** | Appears employed, contributes nothing |
| **Voluntary** | Unwilling at prevailing wage (high benefits, high marginal tax, waiting for better job) |
| **Involuntary** | Willing at going wage but no job |
| Visible underemployment | Fewer than normal hours |
| **Invisible underemployment** | Full-time at very low income or below ability (**MBA working as a driver**) |
| Long-term | Unemployed over 12 months |
| Unemployment trap | Benefits discourage work |
| **Natural rate** | **Frictional + structural** at full employment |
- **Labour force counts only involuntary unemployment.**
### 7. ECONOMISTS: OKUN, PHILLIPS, FRIEDMAN
| Economist | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Keynes** | Deficiency in effective demand (developed economies) |
| **Milton Friedman** | **Natural rate** of unemployment |
| **A.W. Phillips (1958)** | Inverse relation: unemployment vs **inflation** |
| **Friedman-Phelps** | Long-run Phillips curve **vertical** at natural rate |
| **Arthur Okun** | 1 percentage point unemployment above natural rate ≈ **2% fall in output** below potential GDP |
- **Stagflation** (high inflation + high unemployment) breaks the Phillips trade-off.
!PAIR Phillips | unemployment | inflation || Okun | unemployment | output
### 8. EDUCATED UNEMPLOYMENT
- Urban phenomenon; cause: curriculum mismatch with industry needs.
- Educated unemployment rises with high growth when professional education is lacking (A and R both true, R explains A).
- Maximum percentage of educated unemployed: **Kerala** (K.P. Kannan).
| PLFS 2025 group | Rate |
|---|---|
| Youth 18-29 | **14.8%** |
| Tertiary-educated youth 18-29 | **29.4%** |
| Secondary schooling and above | **6.5%** (over double national rate) |
| Gen Z (15-26) | **11.9%** (male graduates 29%, female graduates 36.9%) |
- **40.1%** of tertiary-educated youth are **NEET** (25 Aug 2026).
- AP (PLFS 2024-25): youth (15-29) **17.5%**; urban female youth 29.1%.
### 9. NATURE OF INDIA'S UNEMPLOYMENT
- India's basic problem: **structural** (labour demand < supply), with **seasonal, disguised and underemployment**; developed countries: mainly **cyclical** (demand-deficient).
- India = **labour-surplus** economy; projected skilled-labour surplus about **245.3 million by 2030**.
- Labour-intensive industry employs more hands.
#### 9.1 Causes
- Rapid **population growth**, **labour immobility**, shift to **capital-intensive** production.
- **Jobless growth**: growth driven by IT and finance, not labour-intensive manufacturing.
- Neglected secondary sector, less focus on **MSMEs**, stringent labour laws, slow infrastructure, import-oriented economy.
- About **90%** workforce informal; low female LFPR.
!TRAP Odd-one-out: "high growth of labour-intensive manufacturing" is NOT a cause; it would reduce unemployment.
#### 9.2 Latest
- **16 Jun 2026:** monthly PLFS (CWS) rate rose to **5.5% (May 2026)** from 5.2% (April).
- **7 Sep 2026:** 30-35 million seasonal labourers migrate annually.
- Debate: a falling rate may reflect **distress entry** into low-productivity work, not real job creation.
