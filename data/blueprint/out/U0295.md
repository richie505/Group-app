## U0295 | BASIC STRUCTURE DOCTRINE – KESAVANANDA BHARATI AND AFTER
### 1. MEANING AND NATURE OF THE DOCTRINE
- Parliament may amend **any part** of the Constitution under **Article 368**, including Fundamental Rights and the Preamble.
- But it **cannot alter, damage or destroy its core features** – the features that give the Constitution its identity.
- Basic structure = what **cannot be amended** in exercise of the power under Art. 368 (APPSC G1 2018 key).
- **Judge-made**: nowhere defined in the text; not added by any Act of Parliament or amendment.
- **Not exhaustive**: the Supreme Court has never listed it fully; the list grows **case by case**.
- Source of the theory = **judicial interpretation**.
- Some features are so essential that they cannot be abrogated (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973; approved in **Minerva Mills**, 1980).
- Fundamental Rights **can** still be amended, provided the basic structure is not damaged.
!TRAP "Basic Structure is explicitly defined in Part XX" = FALSE. "Incorporated by an Act of Parliament" = FALSE (APPSC G1 2024). It is not "the Preamble alone".
!TRAP "Fundamental Rights can never be amended after Kesavananda" = FALSE.
### 2. ORIGIN OF THE IDEA – PERSONS
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Dietrich Conrad** | German jurist; argued in India in **1965** that the amending power has **implied limitations**, based on Germany's **Basic Law** |
| **Justice Mudholkar** | Dissent in **Sajjan Singh (1965)** – first judicial reference to **"basic features"** |
| **CJI S.M. Sikri** | Headed the **13-judge** bench in Kesavananda Bharati |
- Country of the idea: **Germany** (Basic Law).
### 3. EVOLUTION – CHRONOLOGY OF CASES
#### 3.1 Before Kesavananda
| Year | Case / event | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1951 | **Shankari Prasad** | Art. 368 includes power to amend FRs; an amendment is not "law" under Art. 13 |
| 1965 | **Sajjan Singh** | Upheld the **17th Amendment**; reaffirmed Shankari Prasad; Mudholkar dissent |
| 1967 | **Golaknath** | Challenged the **17th Amendment** (not the 24th); overruled both earlier cases; FRs "**transcendental and immutable**"; amendment is "law" under Art. 13; Parliament cannot abridge FRs; **11 judges, 6:5**; first used **prospective overruling** |
| 1971 | **24th Amendment** | Parliament's reply – amended **Arts. 13 and 368** |
#### 3.2 Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
- Decided **24 April 1973**; **13 judges** (largest bench ever); majority **7:6**; CJI S.M. Sikri.
- **First propounded** the basic structure doctrine; **overruled Golaknath**; **upheld the 24th Amendment**.
- Held the **Preamble** is part of the Constitution and amendable, subject to the basic structure.
- Struck down the part of **Art. 31C** that stopped courts from asking whether a law carries out the DPSPs → **judicial review is basic**.
#### 3.3 After Kesavananda
| Year | Case | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1975 | **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain** | **First application**; struck down the **39th Amendment** clause (Art. 329A(4)) putting election disputes of PM (and President, VP, Speaker) beyond courts; free and fair elections basic |
| 1980 | **Minerva Mills** | Struck down **42nd Amendment's Art. 368(4), (5)** and the widened **Art. 31C**; judicial review, limited amending power, FR–DPSP harmony |
| 1981 | **Waman Rao** | Doctrine applies from **24 April 1973** – cut-off for Ninth Schedule / Art. 31A laws |
| 1994 | **S.R. Bommai** | Art. 356 misuse; **nine-judge** bench; **secularism and federalism** basic |
| 2007 | **I.R. Coelho** | **Nine judges**; Ninth Schedule laws after 24 April 1973 reviewable |
| 2015 | **NJAC case** (SC Advocates-on-Record Assn.) | **99th Amendment (2014)** and NJAC Act struck down, **4:1**; judicial independence basic |
!KEY First propounded = **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**, not Golaknath or Shankari Prasad (MPPSC 2025). First LIMITED FRs amendment power = **Golaknath (1967)**. First APPLIED = **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**.
!NUM Benches: Golaknath 11 judges, 6:5 | Kesavananda 13 judges, 7:6 | NJAC 4:1 | Bommai and Coelho 9 judges

### 6. NINTH SCHEDULE AND ARTICLES 31A, 31B, 31C
#### 6.1 Origin and purpose
- Created by the **1st Amendment (1951)** with **Art. 31B** (and 31A); assent by President **Rajendra Prasad**.
- 1st Amendment also added **Art. 15(4)** and the grounds of public order, friendly relations with foreign States and incitement to an offence to **Art. 19(2)**.
- Purpose: protect **land-reform / zamindari-abolition** laws from challenge for violating FRs.
- Covers **both Central and State** laws.
- Entries made **only by Parliament through a constitutional amendment**.
- Example: **76th Amendment, 1994** placed **Tamil Nadu's 69% reservation** Act.
- **13 Acts** at the start → **282** (more than 280) now.
#### 6.2 Judicial review of entries
| Case | Year | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Amendments, including inclusions, cannot destroy the basic structure |
| Waman Rao | 1981 | Cut-off **24 April 1973** (date of Kesavananda); later entries open to challenge |
| I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu | 2007 | **No blanket immunity**; "**rights test / direct impact test**" – post-1973 law invalid if it damages basic structure or abrogates the essence of **Arts. 14, 19, 21** |
- Sequence: **First Amendment (1951) → Kesavananda (1973) → Waman Rao (1981) → I.R. Coelho (2007)**.
- I.R. Coelho = "**9th Schedule case**".
#### 6.3 Saving articles
| Article | Saves | Inserted by |
|---|---|---|
| 31A | Estate acquisition, agrarian reform laws from Arts. 14, 19 | 1st Amendment, 1951 |
| 31B | Ninth Schedule laws | 1st Amendment, 1951 |
| 31C | Laws implementing **Art. 39(b), (c)** from Arts. 14, 19 | **25th Amendment, 1971** (not the First); widened by 42nd |
### 7. DOCTRINES – SUPREME COURT vs PARLIAMENT
| Doctrine / rule | Origin |
|---|---|
| Basic structure | Supreme Court – Kesavananda (1973) |
| Eclipse, severability, pith and substance, colourable legislation, harmonious construction, territorial nexus | Supreme Court (interpretive doctrines) |
| Prospective overruling | Supreme Court – Golaknath (1967) |
| DPSP primacy over Arts. 14, 19 (Art. 31C) | Parliament – 25th Amendment (1971) |
| Art. 13(4) | Parliament – 24th Amendment |
| Unlimited amending power (Art. 368(4), (5)) | Parliament – 42nd; struck in Minerva Mills |
### 8. SUPREMACY DEBATE AND CRITICISM
- **Judicial review** = inherent power of constitutional courts to check laws and executive action; accepts **supremacy of the Constitution and courts** (APPSC G2 2017 key).
- Basic structure rests on **supremacy of the Constitution**.
- Indian Parliament is **not sovereign in the British sense**: written supreme Constitution, FRs, judicial review, federal division, limited amending power.
- High Courts can void central laws: the **42nd Amendment's bar removed by the 43rd (1977)**.
| View | Argument |
|---|---|
| Criticism | No textual basis; "judicial rewriting" by unelected judges; open-ended, uncertain list; "judicial supremacy" |
| Defence | Shield against majorities; checks misuse of Art. 368; major contribution to constitutionalism |
#### 8.1 Latest developments
- **18 June 2026**: 75 years of the 1st Amendment (Ninth Schedule); I.R. Coelho limit restated.
- **2026**: free and fair elections as basic feature cited in debate on making **voting** more than a statutory right.
- **129th Amendment Bill, 2024** (simultaneous polls), JPC: ex-CJI **B.R. Gavai** – no violation of basic structure; ex-CJIs **U.U. Lalit** and **Sanjiv Khanna** raised doubts.
