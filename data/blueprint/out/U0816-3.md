### 1. DELIMITATION, SEAT FREEZE AND THE 131ST AMENDMENT BILL
#### 1.1 Seat Freeze History
| Amendment | Year | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **42nd** | 1976 | Froze inter-State seats on the **1971 Census**, until **2000** – to reward population control |
| **84th** | 2001 | Extended the freeze till the **first Census after 2026** |
| **87th** | 2003 | Constituency boundaries (delimitation) on the **2001 Census**; seat totals still frozen |
| **104th** | 2019 | Ended Anglo-Indian nomination (Art 331 in Lok Sabha – 2 members; Art 333 in Assemblies) |
| **106th** | 2023 | Women's reservation 33% – Arts **330A, 332A, 334A**; valid 15 years |
- At present: **inter-State allocation on 1971 Census**; **constituency boundaries on 2001 Census**.
- **Art 82** – readjustment after each Census (Lok Sabha); **Art 170** – Assemblies.
- Principle: **"one person, one vote, one value"**.
#### 1.2 Delimitation Commission
- Constituted **four times: 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002**; last exercise completed **2008** (on 2001 Census).
- Statutory body under the Delimitation Act; **appointed by the President**; chair – retired SC judge; members – CEC + State Election Commissioners.
- **Temporary body** – dissolves after its work; decides **SC/ST reserved constituencies**.
- Orders have the **force of law**; laid before Parliament **for information only** (no approval needed).
!TRAP Delimitation orders are NOT subject to courts – **Article 329(a)** bars judicial review.
#### 1.3 Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 – the Package
- Lok Sabha maximum **550 → 850** (**815 States + 35 UTs**) – amends **Art 81**; Art 82 (removes post-2026 freeze), Art 170 and **Art 334A** (early women's reservation).
- Delimitation on the **2011 Census**; one-third women's reservation.
- **"Schedule" mechanism**: a law lists State-wise seats – Lok Sabha up ~50% while each State's percentage share stays the same.
- Package of **three Bills**: 131st Amendment Bill; **Delimitation Bill, 2026** (new Commission replacing the Delimitation Act, 2002 framework; would fix SC, ST and women's seats); **Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026** (Delhi, J&K, Puducherry).
- Needed **special majority + ratification by half of the States**.
- **Art 75(1A)**: Council of Ministers ≤ **15%** of Lok Sabha → up to ~**122 ministers** at 850.
- **Art 108** joint sitting – a larger Lok Sabha dominates more; Rajya Sabha strength (Art 80) does **not** change automatically.
- Gainers: **UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, MP** (Hindi belt); relative losers: **Kerala, Tamil Nadu**.
#### 1.4 Defeat of the Bill – April 2026
- **528 voted – 298 in favour, 230 against**; failed to get the required special majority of **326** (**Art 368**).
- **Delimitation Bill 2026 withdrawn** after the defeat (it sought women's quota by **2029**).
- Revived the debate on **delinking women's reservation from delimitation**.
- **106th Amendment Act brought into force from 16 April 2026** (procedural step); Act passed **September 2023**; valid **15 years**.
!NUM 298 for | 230 against | 326 needed | 528 voted | 550 → 850 proposed cap
#### 1.5 Lok Sabha Expansion to ~816 Seats (Proposal)
- Aim: women's reservation **without waiting for post-Census delimitation**, from the **2029 Lok Sabha polls**; delimitation planned on the **2011 Census**.
- Seats **543 → ~816** (~50% rise); about **273** seats (one-third) for women; **States' proportions unchanged** (1971 basis in initial phase); possible lottery, every third seat.
- Southern seats **129 → 195**; share stays **23.8%**.
- ~273 extra women's seats to be allotted to **parties by vote share in the last general election** from **pre-declared lists** (limited proportional representation; hybrid with FPTP; temporary for one or two cycles).
- Amendments needed: **Art 81, Art 170, Delimitation Commission Act** – two-thirds majority.
| Parameter | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Total seats | 543 | ~816 |
| Women representation | ~14% | ~33% |
| Basis of allocation | 1971 Census | Same (initial phase) |
| Delimitation | Mandatory for change | Temporarily bypassed |
!TRAP Lok Sabha expansion does **not automatically trigger delimitation**.
- **Special majority** = two-thirds of members present and voting + majority of total membership.
#### 1.6 Parliament Static GK
- **Art 79**: Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha.
- **Art 80**: RS maximum **250 (238 elected + 12 nominated)**; current **245**; permanent – **one-third retire every 2 years**; member term **6 years** (Art 83); seats by population (not equal).
- RS members elected only by **elected MLAs (not MLCs)** – STV proportional representation.
- Lok Sabha: set up **1952**; constitutional maximum **550**; current **543**; Art 83 – 5-year term.
- **Art 170**: Assembly maximum **500**, minimum **60** (exceptions: Goa, Sikkim, Mizoram).
- **Art 243T**: SC/ST seats in every municipality in proportion to population; **Art 169**: Council abolition by ordinary law after Assembly special-majority resolution.
- Census every 10 years under **Census Act, 1948**.
### 2. WOMEN'S RESERVATION AND PANCHAYATS
#### 2.1 Women's Reservation Act (106th Amendment, 2023 – Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam)
| Article | Relates to |
|---|---|
| **330A** | Lok Sabha |
| **332A** | State Assemblies |
| **334A** | Commencement and duration |
- **33%** in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and **Delhi Assembly** – includes seats within SC/ST seats (sub-reservation for SC/ST women).
- Duration **15 years**; rotation after each delimitation; linked to delimitation after the first Census after 2026 – implementation unlikely before **2032–33**.
!TRAP Women's quota is NOT for **Rajya Sabha** or **Legislative Councils**; there is **no OBC sub-quota**.
- Representation: Lok Sabha ~**14–15%** (2019–24); Assemblies ~**9%**; **5%** in first Lok Sabha; Rwanda >60%, Sweden ~46%.
- Bills: **1996** (first), 1998, 1999, 2008; passed **Rajya Sabha in 2010** but lapsed; enacted 2023.
- Committees: CSWI (1971), **Margaret Alva (1987)**, **Geeta Mukherjee (1996)** – OBC women.
- WEF Gender Gap 2023: India **25.3%** parity in political empowerment; CEDAW 1979.
#### 2.2 Women in Panchayats – 49.7%
- Women = **49.7%** of elected PRI representatives – **12.14 lakh of 24.41 lakh** (2025 data); many win unreserved seats.
- **19 States/UTs** give **50%** reservation (e.g. Bihar, Rajasthan, MP, **AP**, HP, Odisha); Gujarat, Haryana, Tripura fell short of targets.
- Ministry of Panchayati Raj has **no national data** on reserved vs unreserved wins; **"Sarpanch Pati"** proxy problem.
#### 2.3 73rd Amendment (1992) Facts
- In force **24 April 1993**; **Part IX** (Arts 243–243O); **Eleventh Schedule – 29 subjects**.
- Three tiers; **intermediate tier optional** for States with population **below 20 lakh**.
- **Art 243D**: not less than **one-third** for women – seats and chairpersons (horizontal).
- **Art 243E**: 5-year term; fresh polls within 6 months of dissolution (not needed if remainder < 6 months).
- **Art 243K**: State Election Commission; **Art 243I**: State Finance Commission (every 5 years).
- 73rd/74th Amendments (1992): 33% in PRIs and ULBs.
!TRAP Constitution mandates **one-third** (Art 243D), not 50%. **District Planning Committee (Art 243ZD)** is from the **74th** Amendment, not the 73rd.

#### 3.4 ECI Basics
- Constitutional body under **Art 324**; set up **25 January 1950**; single-member till **1989**.
- **Art 324(2)**: President appoints CEC/ECs subject to Parliament's law.
- Tenure: **6 years or 65 years**, whichever is earlier.
- Conducts elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President, Vice-President.
- **Election Commission Act, 1991**: salaries, tenure – **not appointments**.
| Case | Held |
|---|---|
| **Mohinder Singh Gill (1978)** | Art 324 gives **plenary powers** where law is silent |
| **T.N. Seshan v UoI (1995)** | Multi-member EC valid; CEC **not superior** to ECs in decisions |
| **Anoop Baranwal v UoI (2023)** | Committee of PM, LoP, CJI till Parliament makes law |
#### 3.5 CEC Removal
- **Art 324(5)**: CEC removable only **like an SC judge** – **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**; special majority in both Houses; Presidential order.
- Notice: **100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha** members; **three-member inquiry committee**.
- Other ECs removed by the President **only on the CEC's recommendation**; similar process for SC judges (Art 124) and CAG.
!TRAP The "only on CEC's recommendation" rule is for Election Commissioners, not the CAG.
- INDIA bloc moved a removal motion; **notices signed by 63 RS and 130 LS MPs were rejected** by RS Chairman and LS Speaker – **no prima facie "proved misbehaviour"**.
- Issues cited: bias, data non-disclosure (consistent with Puttaswamy privacy), **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of rolls.
#### 3.6 SIR Upheld
- SC held the ECI **can conduct Special Intensive Revision** of electoral rolls (ADR v. ECI / Bihar SIR petitions); Bench headed by **CJI Surya Kant**.
- Power from **Art 324** (superintendence, direction, control) and **RP Act, 1950** (electoral rolls, incl. Section 21(3)).

### 6. LANDMARK SUPREME COURT JUDGMENTS
#### 6.1 Harish Rana – Right to Die with Dignity
- SC allowed **withdrawal of life support** for Harish Rana – right to die with dignity part of **Art 21**.
- **First time** the SC actually **implemented passive euthanasia** in a specific case; **active euthanasia remains illegal**.
- **CANH** (Clinically Assisted Nutrition and Hydration) = **medical treatment**; can be stopped in patient's best interest (family + medical boards).
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **P. Rathinam (1994)** | Right to die recognised; **overruled by Gian Kaur** |
| **Gian Kaur v. Punjab (1996)** | Right to live with dignity; **rejected right to die** |
| **Aruna Shanbaug (2011)** | Passive euthanasia in **exceptional cases** |
| **Common Cause (2018)** | Right to die with dignity; refuse treatment, **living wills** (simplified **2023**) |
- AP angle: **Chenna Jagadeeswar v. State of A.P.** – right to life does not include right to die.
#### 6.2 Conversion Ends Scheduled Caste Status
- SC: SC status only for **Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists**; conversion → immediate and complete loss of SC status.
- Case from the **Andhra Pradesh High Court** – complaint under SC/ST (PoA) Act by **Pastor Chintada Anand Paul**; HC quashed charges; SC upheld the AP HC judgment of **30 April 2025**; Bench **Justices Prashant Kumar Mishra and Manmohan** dismissed the SLP.
- **AP's 1977 GO** gives only **non-statutory concessions**, not SC status after conversion.
- Reconversion: prove **return, renunciation and acceptance by original caste** – burden on claimant.
- **Constitution (SC) Order, 1950** under **Art 341(1)**; **Clause 3** – only Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists; **Hindus 1950 → Sikhs 1956 → Buddhists 1990**; **Art 341(2)** – only Parliament amends; roots in GoI Act 1935 (1936 Order).
| Aspect | SC | ST |
|---|---|---|
| Article | 341 | 342 |
| Basis | **Untouchability** | **Tribal characteristics, isolation** |
- Related Articles: 341, 342, 15(4), 16(4).
#### 6.3 Sabarimala Review – Nine-judge Bench
- Case **Kantaru Rajeevaru v Indian Young Lawyers Association**; Bench chaired by **CJI Surya Kant**; hearing began **7 April 2026**; judgment **reserved after 16 days** of hearing (May 2026).
- Members include B.V. Nagarathna, M.M. Sundresh, Ahsanuddin Amanullah, Aravind Kumar, Augustine George Masih, Prasanna B. Varale, R. Mahadevan, Joymalya Bagchi.
- Centre: **rigid definitions** of "religious denomination" and "essential religious practices" **distort the plural nature of Hinduism**.
- Temple in **Periyar Tiger Reserve**, Kerala; deity Lord Ayyappa (celibate); **41-day penance**; **women aged 10–50** barred; upheld by Kerala HC 1991 (S. Mahendran); run by **Travancore Devaswom Board**; Rule 3(b), Kerala Hindu Places of Public Worship Rules, 1965.
| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 2017 | Five-judge Constitution Bench |
| **2018** | **4:1** – exclusion unconstitutional; Rule 3(b) struck down; dissent **Justice Indu Malhotra** |
| **2019** | Referred to larger Bench (7 questions framed in 2020) |
- Articles: 14, 15, 17, 25, **26** (denominational rights – subject to public order, morality, health; not absolute); **27** (no tax for a religion); **28(1)** (no religious instruction in wholly State-funded institutions).
- **Shirur Mutt (1954)** – essential religious practices doctrine; **Art 145(3)** – at least **five judges** for substantial constitutional questions.
- Judicial review basis: Arts 13, 32, 226; Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (2018): Section 144 CrPC cannot suppress legitimate protest – Art 19(1)(b).
#### 6.4 Meaning of "Industry" – Nine-judge Bench
- Led by **CJI Surya Kant**, hearings from **17 March**; re-examines **Bangalore Water Supply v. A. Rajappa (1978)** – seven-judge bench, majority by **Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer** (paras 140, 144).

#### 7.3 Sattankulam Custodial Murder

- **NHRC**: custodial deaths reported within 24 hours; **can only recommend, cannot punish**.

### 9. BILLS AND ACTS
#### 9.1 Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Bill, 2026 – Jan Vishwas 2.0
- Passed by Parliament – **decriminalises minor offences**; trust-based governance, ease of doing business; amends **784 provisions** in **79 Central Acts** (administered by **23 ministries**) – **717 provisions decriminalised** (ease of doing business) + **67 amended** (ease of living).
- Passed **Lok Sabha 1 April 2026**, **Rajya Sabha 2 April 2026**; reintroduced in March 2026 after the **2025 Bill** was withdrawn.
- Imprisonment replaced by **monetary/civil penalties**; graded compliance – warnings, improvement notices first.
- Enforcement moved to **administrative adjudication** (Adjudicating Officers + appellate authorities); serious offences keep criminal liability.
- **Fines revised 10% every three years** (not five).
- Linked to **World Bank B-READY**; Jan Vishwas Act, **2023** amended **183 provisions** in **42** Central laws.
!TRAP It does not abolish penalties – it converts criminal penalties into civil ones.
#### 9.2 Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Bill, 2026
