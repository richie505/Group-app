## U0409 | RESERVATION – CONSTITUTIONAL BASIS, INDRA SAWHNEY, CREAMY LAYER, EWS, PROMOTION
### 1. BACKWARD CLASSES IN THE CONSTITUTION
- **"Backward Classes" is NOT defined** in the Constitution (APPSC G2 2017: the "defined" statement is the wrong one).
- **Article 340**: the **President** appoints a commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes and recommend remedies. Kalelkar, Mandal and Rohini commissions were set up under it.
- Correct term: **Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBC)** – not "Socially and Economically".
- Under Art. 16(4), backward classes are identified mainly by **caste and social backwardness**, not economic criteria alone (Indra Sawhney).
- For the CEI Act, OBCs are classes "so determined by the **Central Government**".
!TRAP SEBC = Socially and **Educationally** Backward Classes. "Economically" is the distractor.

### 5. LANDMARK CASES
#### 5.1 Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992, Mandal case)
- **Caste** can be the main criterion of backwardness (overruled Balaji on this).
- Total reservation **not above 50%** except in **extraordinary cases**; applied **year by year**; carry-forward allowed within 50%.
- **Creamy layer** excluded from OBCs (the concept originates here).
- Reservation only at **initial appointment, not promotion** → answered by the **77th** Amendment.
- Directed a **permanent statutory body** for OBC lists → **NCBC**.
#### 5.2 Case Table
| Case | Year | Issue decided |
|---|---|---|
| State of Madras v. **Champakam Dorairajan** | 1951 | Caste-based quota struck down → **1st Amendment, Art. 15(4)** |
| **M.R. Balaji** v. State of Mysore | 1962 | Reservation should be within **50%**; caste not the sole test |
| Indra Sawhney | 1992 | 50% ceiling, creamy layer, no promotion quota |
| P.A. Inamdar | 2005 | No quota in private unaided institutions → undone by 93rd |
| **M. Nagaraj** | 2006 | Upheld 77th/81st/82nd/85th; promotion quota needs data on backwardness, inadequacy and efficiency; creamy layer and 50% ceiling not to be breached |
| **Ashoka Kumar Thakur** | 2008 | Upheld 93rd Amendment and 27% OBC quota (CEI Act); creamy layer applies to OBCs, not SCs/STs |
| **Jarnail Singh** | 2018 | Nagaraj wrong on needing quantifiable data on SC/ST backwardness; creamy layer can apply to SC/ST promotions; no 7-judge reference |
| **Jaishri Patil** (Maratha case) | 5 May 2021 | Maratha SEBC quota struck down (no extraordinary case beyond 50%); only Centre identifies SEBCs → reversed by **105th** |
| **Janhit Abhiyan** | 7 Nov 2022 | EWS upheld |
| Union of India v. **Rohith Nathan** | 11 March 2026 | **Parental salary alone** cannot place a candidate in the creamy layer; supernumerary posts ordered |
- Rohith Nathan follow-up (Aug 2026): Centre sought clarification on **958** CSE-2025 candidates; a special Bench likely.
### 6. CREAMY LAYER
- **Status-based exclusions**: children of constitutional post-holders, **Class-I officers**, parents **both Class-II** officers, senior armed-forces officers, large landowners/property owners, high earners. Not all OBC children are excluded.

### 7. EWS RESERVATION – 103RD AMENDMENT
- **10%** (up to 10%) for economically weaker sections **not covered** by SC/ST/OBC quotas; **over and above** existing reservation.
- In jobs and education, including **private** institutions; **enabling, not mandatory** for States.
- Criteria: family income **below ₹8 lakh**; agricultural land below **5 acres**; flat below **1,000 sq ft**.
- **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)**: upheld **3:2** by a **five-judge** bench; dissent: **CJI U.U. Lalit** and **Justice S. Ravindra Bhat**. The 50% cap applies to backward-class quotas; EWS stands outside it.
### 8. OBC QUOTA IN CENTRAL EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS
- Central jobs: **27%** OBC quota from **1993**.
- 93rd Amendment (Art. 15(5)) is the base of the **CEI (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006**: **27%** OBC seats; in force **January 2007** (GS 2007 key: 93rd Amendment).
- 93rd Amendment = 27% OBC reservation in admission to educational institutions.
- 40-50% of reserved central posts lie vacant.
!KEY 27% = Mandal quota (implemented 1990, posts 1993) = CEI seats (2006).
### 9. ANDHRA PRADESH – BC RESERVATION
#### 9.1 BC Groups
| Group | Who | Quota |
|---|---|---|
| BC-A | Aboriginal tribes, vimukta jatis, nomadic groups | 7% |
| BC-B | Vocational / occupational groups | 10% |
| BC-C | **SC converts to Christianity** | 1% |
| BC-D | Other classes | 7% |
| BC-E | Socially and educationally backward **Muslims** (2007 Act) | 4% |
- Combined BC-A to BC-E = **29%**. Earlier lists had only BC-A to BC-D. BC residential schools (2024-25) still admit by BC-A to BC-E groups.
#### 9.2 Commissions and Cases
- **Sukhdev case** (AP HC): caste alone cannot be the basis → **112 classes** listed on poverty, residence and occupation.
- **Anantharaman Commission**: **first AP BC Commission**, report **1970**; framed **A-D grouping** and **25%** quota.
- **Muralidhara Rao Commission**: took charge 27 Jan 1982; reviewed BC reservation and inclusion of minorities.
- **Muslim quota**: 5% (2004, 2005) struck down by AP HC; **2007 Act – 4%** (within 50% limit) struck down by AP HC in **2010**, but SC **interim order** lets it continue pending a Constitution Bench.
- **Kapus**: Justice **Manjunatha Commission (2016)** → 2017 Bill giving **5% as BC-F**; never approved by the Centre. 2019: 5% within the 10% EWS quota; withdrawn by the next government.
#### 9.3 Local Bodies
- **NTR** introduced **20%** BC quota in **1984**; raised to **34%** (polls 1995, 2001, 2006, 2013); cut to **24.15%** under GOs **176, 559, 560**.
- Current: **34% rural + 33.33% urban** (19 Aug 2026, G.O. Rt. No. 1065 for ULB polls); earlier 34% struck down for breaching the ceiling (27 Aug 2026).
- **Triple test** (Supreme Court): dedicated commission + local-body-wise proportion + total within **50%**.
- Tamil Nadu (8 Aug 2026): SC reserved verdict on TN's BC (Muslim) quota for converts.
