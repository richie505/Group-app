### 2. CHANDRAYAAN-1 (2008)
- **Orbiter + Moon Impact Probe (MIP)**; **1,380 kg**; **11 instruments** from India, USA, UK, Germany, Sweden, Bulgaria.
- NASA's **M3 (Moon Mineralogy Mapper)** → water/hydroxyl; **confirmed water molecules** on the surface.
- Other payloads: TMC, HySI, LLRI, HEX, **CIXS**, SIR-2, SARA, **Mini-SAR**, RADOM. (**Lyman Alpha Photometer** was a Mangalyaan payload.)
- **MIP** hit the south polar region on **14 Nov 2008** → **Jawahar Point**.
- India **4th** to place its flag on the Moon via impact probe, after **USSR, USA, Japan** (**Japan** landed an impact probe before India).
- 3,400+ orbits; contact lost **29 Aug 2009** after 312 days.

### 3. CHANDRAYAAN-2 (2019)
- **Orbiter + lander Vikram + rover Pragyan**; aimed at the **south polar region** (surface, minerals, water ice) – **not the North Pole**.
- Approved **2008** (Union Cabinet, PM Manmohan Singh).
- **Vikram crash-landed (Sep 2019)** → **Tiranga Point**; the **orbiter still works**, giving the highest-resolution lunar images.
### 4. CHANDRAYAAN-3 (2023)
#### 4.1 Mission
- **Propulsion Module + lander Vikram + rover Pragyan** (names kept from Ch-2; APPSC 2022).
- Follow-on to Ch-2: shows end-to-end **safe landing and roving**.
- Lunar orbit **5 Aug 2023**; Propulsion Module took the lander to **100 km** orbit.
- Mass **3,900 kg** (lander 1,752 kg incl. **26 kg** rover); life **one lunar day (~14 Earth days)**.
- Soft-landing limits: vertical ≤ 2 m/s, horizontal ≤ 0.5 m/s, slope ≤ 12°.
#### 4.2 Payloads → function
| Payload | On | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ChaSTE** | Lander | Surface thermal profile |
| **ILSA** | Lander | **Moonquakes** |
| **LRA** (NASA) | Lander | Passive laser retroreflector |
| **APXS** (Alpha Particle X-ray Spectrometer) | Rover | Elements (Mg, Al, Si, K, Ca, Ti, Fe) |
| **LIBS** | Rover | Elements |
| **SHAPE** | Propulsion Module | Studies **Earth** from lunar orbit |
#### 4.3 Landing site, rank, findings
- **23 Aug 2023**, **Shiv Shakti Point** (~600 km from the South Pole, between craters Manzinus C and Simpelius N) → **National Space Day, 23 August**.
- India **4th** to soft-land (after **US, Russia, China**) and **1st near the south pole**; Japan's **SLIM** later made Japan 5th.
- Pragyan **confirmed sulphur** (Aug 2023; APPSC 2023); LIBS also found **Al, Ca, Fe, Cr, Ti, Mn, Si, O**.
- APXS: soil rich in **iron, magnesium**, poor in aluminium; like meteorite ALHA 81005.

### 5. MANGALYAAN – MARS ORBITER MISSION
- India's **first interplanetary mission**; PSLV-C25, 5 Nov 2013; used a **Hohmann transfer**.
- Mars orbit **24 Sep 2014** (highly elliptical).
- **First country to reach Mars orbit on its maiden attempt**; **first Asian nation**; ISRO **4th agency** after **NASA, Roscosmos, ESA** (not CNSA); lowest-cost Mars mission.
- **5 payloads**: **Methane Sensor for Mars**, **Lyman Alpha Photometer**, **MENCA**, **Mars Colour Camera**, **TIS**.
- Designed for **6 months** → worked about **8 years**; contact lost **2022**.
- Methane in Mars' air was first measured by **ESA's Mars Express**; also by NASA's **Curiosity**.
- Mars: planet that may suit life. **Spirit (MER-A)**: first US rover on Mars, 4 Jan 2004 (twin Opportunity).
### 6. ADITYA-L1 (2023)
- India's **first space-based solar observatory / first solar mission**; launched **2 Sep 2023**, PSLV-C57.
- **Halo orbit** around **Sun-Earth L1**, ~**1.5 million km**; inserted **6 Jan 2024**; continuous view, no eclipse.
!TRAP APPSC 2022: "launched 25 January 2024" is WRONG.
#### 6.1 Seven payloads (4 remote sensing + 3 in-situ)
| Type | Payload | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| Remote | **VELC** (Visible Emission Line Coronagraph) | **Corona**; by **IIA** |
| Remote | **SUIT** (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope) | **Photosphere, chromosphere** (near-UV); by IUCAA |
| Remote | **SoLEXS** | Soft X-ray |
| Remote | **HEL1OS** | Hard X-ray |
| In-situ | **ASPEX** | Solar-wind protons, ions |
| In-situ | **PAPA** | Solar-wind electrons, ions |
| In-situ | **Magnetometer** | Magnetic field |
- 2025 solar maximum: VELC made the first visible-light spectroscopic observations of **CMEs**; **27 TB** data released.
- **Photosphere** = the Sun's bright "surface" emitting most visible light.
- Earth's **magnetic field** turns charged particles to the poles → **aurora**.
### 7. ASTROSAT AND XPOSAT
| Feature | AstroSat (2015) | XPoSat (2024) |
|---|---|---|
| FIRST | India's **first multi-wavelength space observatory** | India's **first polarimetry mission**; **world's second** after NASA's **IXPE** |
| Bands / target | **Visible, UV, X-ray** | X-ray polarisation of **black holes, neutron stars** |
| Payloads | **UVIT, LAXPC, SXT, CZTI, SSM** | **POLIX** (RRI) and **XSPECT** |
| Partner | TIFR, IUCAA, IIA, RRI | **Raman Research Institute, Bengaluru** |
!TRAP A PYQ dated XPoSat May 2023; it flew on 1 Jan 2024.
- **Pulsars** = rotating **neutron stars** (highly dense stars) sending regular pulses; strong magnetic fields.
- **Cepheids** = stars that brighten and dim periodically (cosmic yardsticks); **nebulae** = dust and gas clouds.

### 10. SPACE HISTORY
- **1975 Aryabhata**: first Indian satellite (Soviet rocket).
- **1980 SLV-3/Rohini**: India the **6th** nation to launch a satellite indigenously.
- Space Shuttle first flight: **Columbia, Apr 1981 (STS-1)**.
- **First satellite repaired in orbit**: NASA's **Solar Maximum Mission**, by **Challenger** crew, **Apr 1984 (STS-41C)**.
### 11. SPACE SCIENCE TERMS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lagrange points** | Five points (**L1–L5**) where a small object stays balanced; Sun-Earth L1 ~1.5 million km |
| **Escape velocity** | Minimum speed to escape gravity; Earth **11.2 km/s** – a body at 8 km/s returns |
| **Soft landing** | Controlled low-speed touchdown |
| **Hohmann transfer** | Fuel-saving elliptical transfer with **two burns** (used by MOM) |
- The **Moon has no atmosphere**: its escape velocity is below the RMS speed of gas molecules.
