## U0486 | URBAN DEVELOPMENT – URBANISATION TRENDS, CHALLENGES (HOUSING, SLUMS, TRANSPORT, WASTE, FINANCE)
### 1. URBAN SETTLEMENT CLASSIFICATION
#### 1.1 Statutory vs census town
| Type | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Statutory town** | Any place with a municipal corporation, municipality, nagar panchayat, cantonment board or notified town area; **notified by the State** by law; run by a ULB |
| **Census town** | Administratively a village but meets **all 3**: population **≥5,000**; **≥75% of male workers** non-agricultural; density **≥400/sq km** |
- Same three tests define an **urban area** (Census 2001).
- Census towns: **3,892** in 2011 (3,894 provisional); **1,362** in 2001.
- Towns (2011): 7,933-7,935 ≈ **4,041 statutory + 3,892 census towns**.
- **Census 2027** keeps the 1981 census-town criteria; RGI retained them despite **MoHUA's** objection before the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Housing and Urban Affairs (chair **Magunta Sreenivasulu Reddy**).
!TRAP "60% of males in non-agricultural work" is wrong – correct is **75%** (UPPSC 2010).
!NUM Census town = 5,000 – 75% – 400 (population – male non-farm workers – density)
#### 1.2 Size classes
- **Six** population-size classes, Class I to VI (CGPSC answer "06").
| Class | Population |
|---|---|
| Class I | 1 lakh+ |
| Class III | 20,000-49,999 |
| Class IV, V, VI | **Small towns** (under 20,000) |
| Class VI | Below 5,000 |
- Class I share of urban population: **62.32%** (2001); **70%** now; 468 Class I UAs/towns (2011 provisional).
- Highest growth: **Class I** towns (Census 1991); **Class III** (Census 2001).
- A village becomes a town when **traders and manufacturers** appear (not fully dependent on village resources).
### 2. URBANISATION TRENDS – INDIA
#### 2.1 Phases
| Phase | Period | Urban share |
|---|---|---|
| Slow | 1901-31 | 10.8-12% |
| **Medium / moderate** | 1931-61 | **12.2% → 18.3%**; urban population +45.46 million (135.86%) |
| Rapid | 1961 onward | – |
- Urban share: **11% (1901) → 31.1% (2011)**; cities grow fast through a steady village-city flow.
#### 2.2 Census 2011
- Urban population **37.71 crore (377.1 million) = 31.16%**; rural 68.84%; up from **27.81% (2001)**.
- India **2nd** in absolute urban population, **after China**.
- India's urban population (2001: 28.6 crore, 27.8%) exceeded the **USA's total population** (28.14 crore); USA has a higher level of urbanisation.
- India is **not over-urbanised** (31.1%), but large cities lack infrastructure.
- Cities: ~**3% of land**, ~**60% of GDP**; doubling city size lifts productivity ~12%.
- **Hidden urbanisation**: satellite **DEGURBA** estimate (EAC-PM) ~**63% urban**; under-counting leaves peri-urban areas without municipal funds.
- Urbanisation rose rapidly after 2001 (million cities 35 → 53) – unrelated to the mobile revolution.
- Most urban religious group: **Jains (~80%)**; Buddhists over 43%; Muslims, Christians ~40%; Hindus 29%.

### 4. CITIES – MILLION-PLUS AND MEGACITIES

### 6. CAUSES OF URBANISATION
- Main component: **migration** (natural increase similar in rural and urban).
- Push: **monsoon failure**, rural unemployment, non-growing farm land, rural-urban imbalance.
- Pull: **magnetic features of cities** – jobs, amenities; plus industrialisation, commercialisation, transport, social change.
- **Philip M. Hauser** – "**Population Implosion**" (rural-urban concentration); "**New Population Bomb**" = fast-growing Third World urban population.
- Crime rises with urbanisation and industrialisation – family and cultural disorganisation.
!KEY Statement sets mixing push and pull factors are usually **all correct**.
### 7. URBAN CHALLENGES
#### 7.1 Slums
- World Bank: ~**177 million** slum dwellers – largest in the world.
- **Mumbai**: slums **41.3% of urban households** – highest share (Census 2011).
- Among **million-plus cities**: **Greater Visakhapatnam ~44%** of population in slums; Greater Mumbai and Vijayawada ~41%.
- Slum population (2001): Greater Mumbai 6.5 m, Delhi 1.9 m, Kolkata 1.5 m, Chennai 0.8 m.
#### 7.2 Housing, waste, climate, governance
- Urban housing shortage ~**18.78 million units** (2012 Technical Group).
- Municipalities overwhelmed by **solid waste** and water demand.
- **Urban heat island** 3-5°C; two-thirds of urban dwellers face pluvial flooding by 2030.
- Governance: **fragmented authority**, little **fiscal autonomy** (ES 2025-26, ch.15 "Making India's Cities Work for its Citizens"); urbanising areas left under panchayats.
- **India Urban Observatory** – New Delhi (MoHUA); evidence-based planning.
- **MoHUA (2017)** = merger of Ministry of Urban Development + Ministry of Housing and Urban Poverty Alleviation.
- **e-lala** – e-commerce portal for small traders (CAIT); Ministry of Urban Development, Nov 2015.
!TRAP Social Forestry is NOT under the Minimum Needs Programme.
