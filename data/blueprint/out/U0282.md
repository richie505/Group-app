## U0282 | FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS – ARTICLE 12, ARTICLE 13 AND DOCTRINES
### 1. PART III – OVERVIEW AND NUMBER GAME
- Location: **Part III, Articles 12–35** (24 Articles); FRs are **granted by the Constitution**.
- Called the **"Magna Carta of India"**.
- Borrowed mainly from the **Bill of Rights of the USA** (not the Irish Constitution).
- Originally **seven**, now **six** (APPSC 2018).
- **Right to Property** removed by the **44th Amendment (1978)** – Art. 19(1)(f) and Art. 31 deleted; now a **constitutional/legal right under Art. 300A (Part XII)**.
- Map: **Art. 12** defines State; **Art. 13** voids conflicting laws; **Arts. 33–35** limits and legislation.
- Neighbours: **Part IV (Arts. 36–51)** DPSP; **Part IVA (Art. 51A)** Fundamental Duties.
#### 1.1 Six Groups
| Group | Articles |
|---|---|
| Right to Equality | **14–18** |
| Right to Freedom | **19–22** |
| Right against Exploitation | **23–24** |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | **25–28** |
| Cultural and Educational Rights | **29–30** |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | **Article 32 alone** (not 30–32) |
- **Art. 15** (no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth) and **Art. 17** (abolition of untouchability) fall under **Right to Equality**.
!TRAP Right to Protest and Right to Die are NOT among the Fundamental Rights.

### 2. FEATURES OF PART III
#### 2.1 Justiciable and Qualified
- **Justiciable**: aggrieved person can move the **SC directly (Art. 32)**; **HCs (Art. 226)** enforce FRs and other legal rights; the judiciary is the **guardian** of FRs.
- **Not absolute**: FRs are **qualified**, subject to **reasonable restrictions** imposed by law only on grounds the Constitution names.
- **Courts judge reasonableness** (judicial review, Arts. 13, 32, 226).
- **Art. 19** lists **11 restriction grounds** in all.
- FRs are **claims of citizens against the State**; they **limit the State – even Parliament** (Art. 13).
- **Both natural and legal**; protect individuals and minorities (APPSC 2023: both correct).
- **Britain** has **no written FRs**; protection rests on conventions and the rule of law.
!TRAP APPSC GS 2019: "FRs are absolute" and "all FRs are suspended in an emergency" – both FALSE (answer: Neither).
#### 2.2 Negative vs Positive Rights
| Nature | Articles |
|---|---|
| **Negative** (limits on the State) | 14, 15(1), 16(2), 18(1), 20, 22(1), 28(1) |
| **Positive** (confer privileges) | **21A, 29, 30** |
| **Absolute limits** on legislative power | 15, 17, 18, 20, 24 |
| Also **bind private persons** | **15(2), 17, 23, 24** |
### 3. CITIZENS ONLY vs ALL PERSONS
| Citizens only | All persons (citizens + foreigners) |
|---|---|
| **Art. 15** – no discrimination | **Art. 14** – equality before law |
| **Art. 16** – public employment | **Art. 20** – protection in conviction |
| **Art. 19** – six freedoms | **Art. 21 / 21A** – life, education |
| **Art. 29** – minorities' culture | **Art. 22** – arrest safeguards (not enemy aliens) |
| **Art. 30** – minorities' institutions | **Arts. 23–24** – exploitation |
| | **Arts. 25–28** – religion |
| | **Art. 32** – constitutional remedies |
- **Denied to aliens**: **Arts. 29–30, 19, 16** (APPSC G1 2022 key: I, II and IV) – **not 23–24**.
- **Enemy aliens** lose even **Art. 22** safeguards (**Art. 22(3)**).
- **Companies** can claim all-persons rights, but **not Art. 19**.
- A **British citizen** in India does not get **freedom of trade and profession**; foreigners do not get **freedom of expression, free movement, residence**.
- Citizens only: right against discrimination, **move freely [Art. 19(1)(d)]**, **contest elections**.
!KEY Five citizens-only Articles: 15, 16, 19, 29, 30.
### 4. ARTICLE 12 – MEANING OF "STATE"
#### 4.1 Definition
- **Government and Parliament of India**.
- **Government and legislature of each State**.
- **All local authorities**.
- **Other authorities** in India or under the control of the Government of India.
- **Private bodies**: writs do not ordinarily lie against them just because they do a public activity, unless the **State acts in collusion**.
#### 4.2 Instrumentality Cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal** | **1967** | **Statutory bodies** are "other authorities" |
| **R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority** | **1979** | Laid down **tests for an instrumentality or agency** of the State |
| **Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib** | **1981** | Society running a **Regional Engineering College** = State; test is **creation and control**, not legal form – govt share capital, financial aid, **deep and pervasive control**, monopoly, public function |
| **Pradeep Kumar Biswas** | **2002** | State if **financially, functionally and administratively dominated** by govt |
### 5. ARTICLE 13 – MEANING OF "LAW" AND SUPREMACY
#### 5.1 Clauses
| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| **13(1)** | **Pre-constitution laws** inconsistent with FRs are **void to the extent of inconsistency** |
| **13(2)** | State shall **not make laws** abridging FRs; such **post-constitution laws** void to that extent |
| **13(3)(a)** | "Law" includes **ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage** having force of law |
| **13(4)** | Art. 13 does **not apply to Art. 368 amendments** (**24th Amendment, 1971**) |
- Basis of **judicial review**; makes FRs **supreme over ordinary law** (Constitution ranks above laws).
!TRAP 13(1) refers to laws in force before the **commencement of the Constitution** – not "before Independence".
#### 5.2 Are Amendments "Law"?
| Case / Amendment | Year | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad** | **1951** | Amendment is **not "law"** under Art. 13; Parliament can amend FRs |
| **Golaknath** | **1967** | FRs have a **transcendental position**; Parliament **cannot** abridge them |
| **24th Amendment** | **1971** | Added **Art. 13(4)** in reply |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | **1973** | Amendable, but **basic structure** cannot be destroyed |
!TRAP 1967 twice: Rajasthan SEB (Art. 12) and Golaknath (Art. 13).
### 6. DOCTRINES – MEANING AND CASE
| Doctrine | Meaning | Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Severability** | **Only the inconsistent part is void**; the rest survives if separable | **A.K. Gopalan (1950)**; **R.M.D.C. (1957)**; Motor General Traders v. State of AP (1984) |
| **Eclipse** | Inconsistent law is **dormant, not dead**; **revives** when the conflict is removed; mainly pre-constitution laws | **Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of MP (1955)** – revived after the 1st Amendment; **State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills (1974)** – extended to post-constitution laws for **non-citizens** |
| **Waiver** | A person **cannot waive an FR**; only statutory/contractual rights can be given up | **Basheshar Nath v. CIT (1959)** |
| **Prospective overruling** | Ruling operates **only for the future** | **Golaknath (1967)** |
| **Progressive interpretation** | Constitution as a **living document** | **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** widened Art. 21; trauma care under Art. 21 – **SaveLIFE Foundation v. UoI (2026)** |
| **Absolute liability** | Hazardous industry liable **without strict-liability exceptions** | **M.C. Mehta (1987)** – Oleum gas leak |
| **Territorial nexus** | State law valid outside State if **sufficient connection** | **State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C. (1957)** |
| **Pith and substance** | Union–State List encroachment: looks at **true nature and character** of the law | Federal doctrine |
- Other federal doctrines: **aspect doctrine**, **colourable legislation**, **incidental and ancillary powers**, **repugnancy**.
!KEY APPSC GS 2018: "Void only to the extent of inconsistency, rest valid" = Doctrine of Severability.
!PAIR Severability | Part void | Rest survives || Eclipse | Law dormant | Revives later
### 7. WHO CAN LIMIT FRs – ARTICLES 33, 34, 35 AND 359
#### 7.1 Amendment, not Ordinary Law
- FRs can be changed **only by a constitutional amendment**, subject to the **basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973)**; an ordinary law cannot.
#### 7.2 Articles 33–35
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **33** | **Parliament alone** may restrict FRs of **armed forces, paramilitary, police, intelligence** and their telecom staff – even **non-combatants (cooks, barbers)** |
| **34** | Restrictions while **martial law** is in force; Parliament may pass an **indemnity Act**; "martial law" **not defined** |
| **35** | **Only Parliament** (not States) legislates on **16(3), 32(3), 33, 34** and punishments for Part III offences – for **uniformity** |
#### 7.3 Suspension – Article 359
- During a **National Emergency**, the **President** may by order suspend the right to move courts for **specified FRs**.
- **Never Arts. 20 and 21** (**44th Amendment, 1978**).
- Arts. 14, 19, 22 and Art. 32 for other rights **can be affected**.
### 8. FR OR NOT? – LOOK-ALIKES
| Right | Status |
|---|---|
| **Employment / work** | **Not FR** – DPSP **Art. 41** (APPSC 2024) |
| **Equal pay for equal work** | **Not FR** – DPSP **Art. 39(d)** |
| **Property** | **Art. 300A** – legal right |
| **Education (6–14)** | **Is an FR** – **Art. 21A** (**86th, 2002**); **RTE Act, 2009** |
| **Shelter** | **No Right of Equal Shelter** granted; courts read shelter into Art. 21 |
| **Vote** | **Not FR** – constitutional right under **Art. 326** (age **18**; **61st Amendment, 1988**) + statutory (**Sec. 19 RPA 1950**, **Sec. 62 RPA 1951**) |
- **Jul 2026**: opposition demand to make the right to vote a Fundamental Right.
!TRAP The right to vote comes from RPA **1951**, not "1957".
### 9. VIOLATION SCENARIOS
| Scenario | Article |
|---|---|
| Paying **below minimum wage** | **Art. 23** – forced labour (**PUDR / Asiad, 1982**) |
| **Child below 14** in a factory or mine | **Art. 24** |
| Admission denied in a State-aided institution on religion, race, caste, language | **Art. 29(2)** |
| Dalits kept from a **public well** | **Art. 15(2)** |
| Dalits kept from a **public shop** | **Art. 17** |
| **Banning an association** | **Art. 19(4)** – valid only on permitted grounds |
| Arts. 19/21 violated by a **private individual** | Ordinary legal remedy, not a writ |
### 10. RELATED FACTS AND DEBATES
- **1st Amendment (1951)** added **Arts. 31A, 31B** and the **Ninth Schedule** (75 years in 2026).
- **I.R. Coelho (2007)** limited Ninth Schedule protection.
- Criticism: many restrictions; **preventive detention**; no social-economic rights.

### 13. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Severability** = only inconsistent part void; **Eclipse** = dormant, revives; **Waiver** = an FR cannot be given up.
2. **13(1)** = before commencement, not before Independence.
3. Citizens only: **15, 16, 19, 29, 30**; Arts. 23–24 are NOT denied to aliens.
4. **Arts. 20 and 21** can never be suspended (44th).
5. **Art. 33** power is with **Parliament alone**; covers cooks and barbers.
6. Right to Constitutional Remedies = **Art. 32 alone**.
7. **Employment, equal pay, shelter, vote, property** are not FRs; **education (21A)** is.
8. Below minimum wage = **Art. 23**; public shop exclusion = **Art. 17**.
9. Based on the **US Bill of Rights**, not the Irish Constitution.
10. **Ajay Hasia (1981)** = deep and pervasive control; **Rajasthan SEB (1967)** = statutory bodies.
