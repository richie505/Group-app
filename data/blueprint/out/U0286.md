## U0286 | RIGHT AGAINST EXPLOITATION – ARTICLES 23-24; RIGHT TO FREEDOM OF RELIGION – ARTICLES 25-28
### 1. ARTICLES 23-28 AT A GLANCE
- **Right against Exploitation** = Arts. **23-24** (two Articles); **Right to Freedom of Religion** = Arts. **25-28** (four Articles).
- Right to religion is a **Fundamental Right**, not a legal right or DPSP (APPSC 2019).
| Article | Right | Available to |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | No **traffic in human beings, begar, forced labour** | Citizens + non-citizens; against **State and private persons** |
| 24 | No child **below 14** in **factory, mine, hazardous** work | Enforceable against private individuals too |
| 25 | **Freedom of conscience**; profess, practise, propagate religion | **All persons** |
| 26 | Freedom to **manage religious affairs** | **Religious denominations** or sections |
| 27 | Freedom from **taxes** to promote a particular religion | All persons, citizens and foreigners |
| 28 | Freedom from **religious instruction/worship** in certain institutions | All persons (individual right) |
- **Art. 29**: protection of interests of minorities; **Art. 30**: minorities' right to establish and administer educational institutions.
### 2. ARTICLES 23-24 – RIGHT AGAINST EXPLOITATION
#### 2.1 Article 23
- **23(1)**: prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar** and other **forced labour**.
- **Begar** = forced unpaid labour exacted by officials/zamindars.
- **23(2)**: State may impose **compulsory service for public purposes** (military/social service); payment not required in every case, but **no discrimination on religion, race, caste or class**.
#### 2.2 Article 24
- No child **below 14** in any factory, mine or other hazardous employment – an **absolute prohibition**; **harmless/innocent work** is not barred.
- Related DPSPs: **39(e), 39(f)** – tender age of children not abused. UN Charter: childhood deserves special assistance.
- **Arts. 21A and 24** together (education 6-14 + no hazardous work) make the right against exploitation meaningful for children (**APPSC G1 2022**).
#### 2.3 Laws → Year → Scope
| Law | Year | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act** | **1956** | Brothels, pimping, living off another's earnings |
| **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act** | **1976** | Debt bondage (brick kilns, agriculture, domestic work) |
| **Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act** | **1986** | 2016 amendment → **Child and Adolescent Labour Act**: below 14 banned except helping family after school; **adolescents 14-18** barred from hazardous work |
| Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act | 2005 | NCPCR, SCPCRs |
| **POCSO** | 2012 | Sexual offences against children |
| **BNS** | 2023 | Trafficking = **Sec. 143 BNS** (replaced IPC 370); **victim's consent immaterial** |
### 3. ARTICLE 25 – TEXT, POWERS, LIMITS
#### 3.1 Text and Terms
- **25(1)**: "all persons are equally entitled to **freedom of conscience** and the right freely to **profess, practise and propagate** religion" – subject to **public order, morality and health** and to the **other provisions of Part III**. Quoted text → identify Article (asked **2018, 2023**).
- Limits: **public order, morality, health**; **humanism is not** a ground.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conscience | Inner freedom to shape one's relation with God; includes freedom to **follow no religion** |
| Practise | Worship, rituals, ceremonies integral to the religion |
| Propagate | Spreading one's beliefs – **does NOT include a right to convert** another (it would impinge on the other's freedom of conscience) |
#### 3.2 State Powers – 25(2)
- **25(2)(a)**: regulate **economic, financial, political or other secular activity** associated with religion – not spiritual belief.
- **25(2)(b)**: social welfare and reform; **throw open Hindu religious institutions** of a public character to all classes of Hindus. State may step in to remove a social evil; no forcible conversion.
#### 3.3 Explanations
- **Explanation I**: wearing and carrying **kirpans** = part of the profession of the **Sikh** religion.
- **Explanation II**: "**Hindus**" includes **Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists** – **not Parsis**.
!TRAP Explanation I = kirpan (Sikh); Explanation II = 'Hindus' includes Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists – Parsis are not included.
### 4. ARTICLES 26-28
| Article | Right | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **26** | (a) establish and maintain religious/charitable institutions; (b) manage own affairs in matters of religion; (c) own and acquire property; (d) administer property in accordance with law | Public order, morality, health (not "other Part III" unlike Art. 25) |
| **27** | No one compelled to pay **taxes** whose proceeds promote a **particular** religion | Bars taxes, **not fees**; taxes may support all religions equally |
| **28(1)** | **No religious instruction** in institutions **wholly maintained out of State funds** | — |
| 28(2) | 28(1) does not apply to State-administered institutions set up under an **endowment/trust** requiring it | — |
| **28(3)** | In **State-recognised or aided** institutions, attendance is **voluntary**; minor needs **guardian's consent** | — |
- 12 May 2026: SC left it to the **Ministry of Education** whether schools imparting religious instruction fall under **Art. 26(a)** rather than 19(1)(g)/30(1).
- State-neutrality provisions: **Art. 27** (no tax for one religion), **Art. 28(1)** (no instruction in wholly State-funded institutions).
### 5. RELIGION CASES AND THE ERP TEST
- **Essential Religious Practice (ERP)**: court-made test, set in **1954** (**Shirur Mutt**); the Constitution does not define "essential".
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Shirur Mutt** (Commr. HRE v. Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar) | 1954 | Origin of **ERP** test; defined "religious denomination" |
| **Ramji Lal Modi v. UP** | — | **Sec. 295A IPC** upheld on public-order grounds (now **Sec. 299 BNS**) |
| **Rev. Stanislaus v. MP** | 1977 | **Propagate ≠ convert**; upheld **Orissa (1967)** and **MP (1968)** anti-conversion Acts |
| **Bijoe Emmanuel v. Kerala** | 1986 | Jehovah's Witness children **not compelled to sing the National Anthem**; expelling them broke the Constitution |
| St. Stephen's | 1992 | Minority-institution autonomy |
| **Javed** | 2003 | **Polygamy not an ERP** |
| **Shayara Bano** | 2017 | **Instant triple talaq** invalid; polygamy, nikah halala left open |
| **Sabarimala** (Indian Young Lawyers Assn.) | 28 Sep 2018 | **5-judge, 4:1** – women's age bar struck; Ayyappa devotees **not a separate denomination**; exclusion not an ERP |
#### 5.1 Sabarimala Aftermath and Hijab
- **Jan 2019**: **Bindu Ammini and Kanaka Durga** – first women of the barred age to enter (**PYQ G2 2025**).
- **2026 review reference**: **nine-judge Bench headed by CJI Surya Kant** on ERP across faiths (17 Apr 2026). Centre (SG Tushar Mehta, 14 May 2026): protection not limited to ERP; **burden on the challenger**.
- **Karnataka HC (2022)**: hijab not an ERP; **Allahabad HC (26 Aug 2026)** relied on it.
!TRAP Sabarimala 2018 = 5 judges, 4:1; 2026 review = 9 judges under CJI Surya Kant.
### 6. CONVERSION AND SACRILEGE LAWS
- Early State anti-conversion laws: **Orissa Freedom of Religion Act, 1967**; **MP Act, 1968** – upheld in **Stanislaus (1977)**.
- **Punjab: Jaagat Jot Sri Guru Granth Sahib Satkar (Amendment) Act** – assent **April 2026**; sacrilege up to **life imprisonment**; challenged in **Punjab and Haryana HC** as protecting one faith (Aug 2026).
### 7. SECULARISM – MEANING AND INDIAN MODEL
- Meaning: **equal respect for all religions**, no discrimination on religious grounds (APPSC 2019); **no state religion**. Applies to **all citizens, including the irreligious** – a positive concept.
- 'Secular' was added to the Preamble by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**; Bommai held it present even before.
- **Sarva Dharma Sambhava** = state neutrality + positive intervention for reform.
- Roots: **Ashoka's Dhamma** (RE 7, RE 12), **Nehru Report (1928)**, **Karachi Resolution (1931)**. Constituent Assembly **rejected 'God'** in the Preamble (**1949**).
- Related Arts.: 14-16, 25-30, **325** (no exclusion from rolls on religion).
| Indian (positive) | Western (negative) |
|---|---|
| **Principled distance**, equal respect | **Strict separation** |
| State may **reform** (Art. 25(2)), temple entry | Religion purely private |
| Aid allowed (Amarnath Yatra, Haj; minority institutions) | No state aid |
- **Theocratic state**: an official religion guides law and policy – **not** a feature of India.
- Indian secularism also protects **language** identity; **Art. 343**: Hindi = official language of the Union; **India has no national language**.
!TRAP APPSC G1 2018: "Not applicable to irreligious persons" is NOT a component; "religious state" is not a feature.
#### 7.1 Secularism Cases
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** | Basic structure doctrine; secularism a basic feature |
| **S.R. Bommai (1994)** | Secularism a **basic feature**; anti-secular State govts can be dismissed under **Art. 356** |
| **Abhiram Singh (2017)** | Widened **Sec. 123(3) RPA** – appeals to religion, caste etc. = **corrupt practice** (not Bommai) |

### 8. RELIGION IN INDIAN SOCIETY
#### 8.1 Religious Composition (Census 2011)
| Religion | Share |
|---|---|
| Hindus | **79.8%** (below 80% for the first time) |
| Muslims | **14.2%** – largest religious minority |
| Christians | 2.3% |
| Sikhs | 1.7% |
| Buddhists | 0.7% |
| Jains | 0.4% |
- Jains, Parsis, Buddhists counted separately (among **six notified minorities**). Jul 2026: **Ravidassias** again demanded a separate religion code in the Census.
#### 8.2 Pluralism, Fundamentalism, Revivalism
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Religious pluralism** | Freedom to practise any religion or none + mutual respect; blocked by **exclusivism** |
| **Fundamentalism** | Return to literal "fundamentals" of scripture; rejects plural readings. Forms in India (since 1970s): **communalism, separatism, fanaticism** |
| **Revivalism** | Restoring a golden past – **Arya Samaj's "Back to the Vedas"** |
- **Bhakti** (6th-18th c.) rejected ritualism and caste; Bhakti-Sufi interaction built harmony.
- **AP angle**: **Vijayanagara** used Muslim officers and protected Jains; **Bammera Potana** offered the *Andhra Maha Bhagavatamu* to **Lord Rama**, not Krishnadevaraya. **AP Endowments Act, 1987** regulates temples and mutts; **Sri Swamy Hathiramji Mutt**, Tirupati – hereditary Dharmakarta of TTD.
