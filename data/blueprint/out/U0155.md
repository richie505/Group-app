## U0155 | ENGLISH EAST INDIA COMPANY – CHARTER OF 1600, SURAT, MADRAS, BOMBAY, CALCUTTA; FARMAN OF 1717
### 1. FOUNDATION AND THE CHARTER OF 1600
#### 1.1 The Charter
- Established **1600** as an English **joint-stock trading company**.
- Royal charter granted by **Queen Elizabeth I** on **31 December 1600** (APPSC-G2 2025: EIC founded 1600).
- Official name: "**The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies**".
- Formed by the group "**Merchant Adventurers**" (association c.1599).
- **15-year monopoly** of East Indies trade, later made **perpetual** – between the **Cape of Good Hope** and the **Magellan Straits**.
- Gave **trading rights only**, not territorial power.
!KEY APPSC-GS 2024 key: the 1600 Charter gave **exclusive trading rights** – not territorial governance or legislative power.
#### 1.2 Mughal Emperor in 1600
- Emperor of India when the Company was formed = **Akbar** (reigned **1556-1605**), a contemporary of **Elizabeth I** (1558-1603).
!TRAP The Company was formed under Akbar, not Jahangir; but its first factories (1611, 1613) came under **Jahangir**.
#### 1.3 Organisation and Powers
| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Governor + 24-member committee** | Later the **Court of Directors** (elected annually) |
| Shareholders = **General Court** | Later the **Court of Proprietors** |
| Nature | **Private joint-stock** company vs **state-run** Portuguese and French ventures |
#### 1.4 Later Charters (1600-1708)
| Year / ruler | Change |
|---|---|
| **1657 – Cromwell** | Charter for a **permanent joint stock** |
| **Charles II, James II** | Delegated sovereignty: **coin money**, **jurisdiction**, **war and peace with non-Christians** |
| 1708 | Rival English companies merged (United Company) |
- **First voyage**: **1602** under **James Lancaster** (capital **£68,000**) → factory at **Bantam**.
- **Levant Company (1592)**: first British company chartered to trade with the **Ottoman Empire** and eastern areas incl. India (silk, spices).
- Early objective: **eastern trade**, especially **spices and textiles**.
#### 1.5 Significance
- A **trading company (1600)** turned into a **ruling power** after **Plassey (1757)** and **Buxar (1764)**.
- Territorial rule over **Bengal** came only after 1757; Madras and Bombay stayed trading settlements before that.
- Its rule ended with the **Government of India Act, 1858**, when the Crown took over.
- The **Andhra coast** (Machilipatnam 1611, Armagaon 1626, Madras 1639 on land from the Chandragiri ruler) was its first home in the south.

### 3. EARLY CONFLICTS – SWALLY TO CHILD'S WAR
| Event | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Battle of Swally** | **1612** | **Captain Thomas Best** defeated the **Portuguese** near Surat → Jahangir's farman (1613) |
| **Shivaji** | 1664, 1670 | **Sacked Surat twice**; threatened the English factory |
| **Child's War** | **1686-90** | **Josiah Child's** EIC attacked Mughal shipping → crushed by **Aurangzeb**; Company **pardoned on payment of a fine** |
!TRAP Child's War was against **Aurangzeb** and the English LOST; Swally (1612) was against the **Portuguese** and the English WON.

| Power | Surat factory |
|---|---|
| English | **1613** |
| Dutch | **1616** |

- The **English** were the first European company to set up a factory at Surat; the Dutch followed in 1616.
- Surat was the early **Western Presidency**; later moved to **Bombay**.
!TRAP First English factory: **Surat** (India, permanent) vs **Machilipatnam** (Andhra, temporary; APPSC G2 2025).
### 5. THE ENGLISH ON THE ANDHRA COAST
#### 5.1 Machilipatnam Arrival
- **1611**: ship "**Globe**" under **Captain Anthony Hippon** reached Machilipatnam → **first English factory in Andhra** (APPSC G2 2025 key).
- Permission from **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda (ruled **1580-1612**) – fits the date.
!TRAP APPSC-G2 2025 A-R: "Globe under Captain **Hawkins**" – A is false; it was **Hippon**. APPSC G2 2008 key named Ibrahim Qutb Shah, but Ibrahim died in **1580** – correct answer is Muhammad Quli.
#### 5.2 Moves Along the Coast
1. **Dutch rivalry** + harassment by Golconda officials.
2. **Pulicat (1621)**, Chandragiri king – abandoned due to **Dutch hostility**.
3. **Armagaon (1626)** – sole shelter on the east coast for a time.
4. Back to Machilipatnam after the **Golden Farman**.
5. Founded **Madras (1639)**.
| Year | Andhra settlement |
|---|---|
| **1611** | Machilipatnam (1st) |
| **1621** | Pulicat (2nd) |
| **1626** | **Armagaon** (3rd) – **Dugarajapatnam**, now **Tirupati district**; **35 miles north of Pulicat** |
| **1682** | **Visakhapatnam** factory |
- Other factories: **Nizampatnam, Narsapuram, Veeravasaram, Bheemunipatnam**.
- **British factory in Nellore district** = **1626** (Armagaon).
- Andhra coast = EIC's **first home in the south**.
#### 5.3 Golden Farman
- From the **Sultan of Golconda** (**Abdullah Qutb Shah**), **1632**.
- Privilege: **free trade in Golconda ports** for a fixed **500 pagodas a year**.
- Brought the English **back to Machilipatnam**.
### 6. MADRAS – FORT ST. GEORGE
- Founded **1639** by **Francis Day** (with **Andrew Cogan**) at **Madraspatnam**.
- Grant from the **Chandragiri** ruler **Venkatapati Raya III**, with efforts of the **Damerla brothers** (**Venkatadri** and **Ayyappa**).
- **Fort St. George** = **first fort built by the British in India** (1639-40); completed on **St George's Day, 23 April 1644**.
- Seat of the **Madras Presidency**; some books call Madras the Company's HQ in India.
- **Madras = India's first municipality (1687)**; **Bhimili = Andhra's first (1861)**.
!PAIR Madras | 1639 | Francis Day || Calcutta | 1690 | Job Charnock
### 7. BOMBAY – ACQUISITION FROM THE PORTUGUESE
1. **1661**: **dowry** of **Catherine of Braganza** (Portuguese princess) to **Charles II**.
2. **1668**: Charles II **leased** Bombay to the Company for **£10 a year**.
3. Bombay **replaced Surat** as the chief west-coast settlement.
!TRAP Bombay transfer to the Company = **1668** (not 1688).
### 8. CALCUTTA – JOB CHARNOCK AND THE THREE VILLAGES
- **Job Charnock**, chief of the **Qasim Bazar** factory, chose **Sutanuti** over Hooghly; arrived **1690** → **founder of Calcutta**.
- **1691** imperial order: trade in Bengal for **Rs 3,000 a year** in lieu of dues.
- **1698**: Company bought the zamindari of **Sutanuti, Gobindapur and Kalikata**.
- **1700**: **Fort William** – nucleus of Calcutta; **Presidency of Fort William in Bengal**.
- **Sutanuti** = English trading centre and Calcutta base; **Batavia** was the Dutch eastern centre.
| Presidency | Seat |
|---|---|
| Madras | Fort St. George |
| Bombay | Bombay (replaced Surat) |
| Bengal | Fort William, Calcutta |
### 9. FARMAN OF 1717 – FARRUKHSIYAR
- Issued by Emperor **Farrukhsiyar**; mission led by **John Surman**.
- Called the "**Magna Carta**" of the Company's trade; increased trading privileges.
| Region | Privilege |
|---|---|
| **Bengal** | **Duty-free trade** for **Rs 3,000 a year**; right to issue **dastaks** |
| **Surat** | All duties dropped for **Rs 10,000 a year** |
#### 9.1 From Dastak Misuse to Rule
1. **Dastaks** misused by Company servants for **private trade**.
2. Nawab of Bengal's **revenue drained** – a deeper cause of **Plassey**.
3. **Plassey (1757)** + **Diwani (1765)** after **Buxar (1764)** → **Bengal** = first major territorial base.
4. **Regulating Act 1773**; Company rule ended by the **GoI Act 1858**.
!TRAP Madras and Bombay were trading settlements; **Bengal** became the first major territorial base, only after 1757.
### 10. TRADE OF THE EARLY SETTLEMENTS
| Goods | Source |
|---|---|
| **Chintz**, **calicoes**, muslins, palampores | **Coromandel / Andhra** (Machilipatnam) |
| **Diamonds**, rubies | **Golconda** |
| **Indigo** | Gujarat, Coromandel |
| **Saltpetre (shora)** and **opium** (best) | **Bihar** / Bengal |
| **Silk and cotton piece-goods** | **Bengal** (not Gujarat) |
- Main business of English settlements in Andhra (Fort St. George) = **trade of locally woven piece-goods**.
- Indian goods were paid for mainly with **imported bullion**.
