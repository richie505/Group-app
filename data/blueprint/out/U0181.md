## U0181 | ADMINISTRATIVE CHANGES – CIVIL SERVICES, ARMY, POLICE, JUDICIARY, LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT
### 1. CIVIL SERVICES – COMPANY ERA
#### 1.1 Cornwallis and the Covenanted Service
- **Lord Cornwallis** (Governor-General 1786-1793) = **"Father of Civil Services in India"**.
- Created the **Covenanted Civil Service** (later the ICS), dominated by Europeans.
- Banned **private trade and gifts**; raised salaries; promotion by **seniority**; separated revenue from justice.
#### 1.2 Training and Recruitment
| Year | Step | By / Under |
|---|---|---|
| 1800 | **College of Fort William**, Calcutta – training of Company recruits | **Wellesley** |
| 1806 | Replaced by **East India College, Haileybury** (England) – law, political economy, Indian languages | Company |
| 1833 | Charter Act – **Law Commission** under **T.B. Macaulay**; codification | – |
| 1853 | **Charter Act** – ended Directors' **patronage**; open competitive examination (backed by **Trevelyan** and **Robert Lowe**) | – |
| 1854 | **Macaulay Committee** – exam in **London**; preference for Oxford/Cambridge graduates | – |
- Till 1853 appointments were by **patronage of the Company's Directors** (APPSC 2018).
- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (1832, evidence before Parliament) = first Indian to **demand** Indians in higher posts.
- Wellesley = the "Bengal Tiger"; Bentinck = modern Western education.
### 2. CIVIL SERVICES – CROWN ERA AND INDIANISATION
#### 2.1 Chronology
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1861 | **Indian Civil Services Act** – limited Indian entry, mainly uncovenanted posts (7 years' residence, local-language exam) |
| 1863-64 | **Satyendranath Tagore** – first Indian in the ICS (passed 1863, joined 1864; posted to Bombay Presidency) |
| 1869 | **Surendranath Banerjea** selected; disqualified over age, restored by court, later **dismissed** for a small judicial error |
| 1878 | **Lytton** cut maximum age to **19** |
| 1878-79 | **Statutory Civil Service** (Lytton) – **one-sixth** of covenanted posts for Indians of "high families"; later abolished |
| 1885 onwards | **INC** demands higher age limit and **simultaneous examinations** from its first session |
| 1886-87 | **Aitchison Commission** |
| 1912-15 | **Islington Commission** |
| 1919 | **GoI Act 1919** (Montford) – separate ICS exam in India |
| 1922 | Exam held **simultaneously in India and England** (Allahabad, later Delhi) |
| 1923-24 | **Lee Commission** |
| 1 Oct 1926 | **Public Service Commission** set up |
| 1935 | GoI Act 1935 – **Federal PSC** + Provincial PSCs |
| 1 Jan 1941 | Indians (**617**) outnumbered Europeans (**585**) in the ICS |
| 1947 / 1950 | ICS → IAS; UPSC replaces Federal PSC |
- Maximum age: **23** (1859) → **22** (1860) → **21** (1866) → **19** (1878).
- **Montagu-Chelmsford Report** recommended the simultaneous exam.
- Order: 1919 Act → 1922 exam → 1924 Lee → 1926 PSC.
#### 2.2 Service Commissions
| Commission | Year | Viceroy | Main point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aitchison** | 1886 | **Dufferin** | Covenanted/uncovenanted replaced by **Imperial, Provincial, Subordinate** services; age limit **23** |
| **Islington** | 1912-15 | **Hardinge II** | Indianisation of higher posts |
| **Lee** | 1923-24 | **Reading** | **50:50** Indian-European parity in ICS within 15 years; independent **Public Service Commission** |
- **Sardar Patel** called the ICS the **"Steel Frame"** of India.
!TRAP Aitchison = three-tier services; Islington = Indianisation; Lee = 50:50 + PSC. Do not swap.
### 3. JUDICIARY – GOVERNOR-GENERAL REFORMS
| Governor-General / Act | Reform |
|---|---|
| **Warren Hastings** (1772) | District **Diwani Adalat** (civil, under the **Collector**) and **Faujdari Adalat** (criminal); Sadar Diwani and Sadar Nizamat Adalats at Calcutta |
| **Regulating Act 1773** | **Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)**; first Chief Justice **Sir Elijah Impey** |
| **Cornwallis Code 1793** | Collector **lost judicial powers** (only revenue collector); post of **District Judge**; separated revenue, judicial and commercial branches; **4 provincial courts of appeal**: Calcutta, **Dacca, Murshidabad, Patna** |
| **Bentinck** | **Abolished** provincial courts of appeal and circuit (work to **Commissioners**); separate Sadar courts at **Allahabad**; vernacular/English replaced Persian |
- Cornwallis feared the Collector's power – kept judicial and revenue work apart. He gave limited judicial powers to revenue collectors earlier (already civil magistrates); his criminal and judicial rules still support the system.
!TRAP Hastings = Collector **gets** civil adalat; Cornwallis = Collector **loses** judicial powers; Bentinck = **abolishes** appeal courts.

### 5. POLICE REFORMS
#### 5.1 Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Thana** | Circle (every **20-30 sq miles**) under an Indian **daroga** |
| **Daroga** | Local face of the **"aura and authority of Company Bahadur"**; known for misuse of power; appointed and supervised by the **Magistrate** |
| **Kotwal** | Older (Mughal) chief of **town** police; kept in towns under Cornwallis |
#### 5.2 Reformers
| Introducer | Reform |
|---|---|
| **Cornwallis (1793)** | Took police duties from **zamindars/landlords**; thanas under darogas |
| **Munro** – Judicial Commission 1814-18 | Police under the **Collector**; minor crimes tried by **village officers** (village munsif) |
| **Canning** – Police Act **1861** | Modelled on **Royal Irish Constabulary**; meant to crush dissent |
| **Curzon** – Police Commission **1902-03** | Chairman **Sir Andrew Fraser**; criticised corruption and inefficiency |
!TRAP Police Act 1861 is Canning's model, not Cornwallis's.
### 6. MUNRO AND THE MADRAS SYSTEM (AP ANGLE)
- **Thomas Munro** returned in 1814 as President of the **Judicial Commission (1814-18)** for Bengal and Madras.
- **Governor of Madras 1820-27**; spread the **Ryotwari** system.
- Madras Presidency governed **Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema**.
- **Andhra petitions** of the 1850s asked for trained judges, police separate from revenue officials and Indians in higher posts.
- Jun 2026: AP approved **Rs 130.38 crore** for phase-1 of the **AP Judicial Academy**.
- 14 Jul 2026: Karnataka Governor suspended the KPSC Chairperson and sought a Supreme Court reference under **Art. 317(1)** – a first for KPSC.
### 7. ARMY REORGANISATION AFTER 1857
| Body | Year | Point |
|---|---|---|
| **Peel Commission** | **1859** | More European troops: Bengal army **1 European : 2 Indians**; Madras/Bombay **1 : 3**; mixed castes in regiments; artillery with Europeans |
| **Eden Commission** | **1879** (Lytton) | Recommended amalgamating the three **presidency armies** (Bengal, Madras, Bombay) |
| Presidency armies abolished | **1 April 1895** | Merged into one Indian Army under four commands |
!NUM Army ratio: Bengal 1:2; Madras and Bombay 1:3 (European : Indian).
### 8. ILBERT BILL, 1883
- Viceroy **Ripon**; drafted by **Law Member C.P. Ilbert**.
- **Aim:** remove disqualifications of Indian magistrates – let Indian **district magistrates and sessions judges** try **European British subjects** in criminal cases; put Indians and Europeans on par.
- **Opposition:** fierce European agitation = **"White Mutiny"**.
- **Outcome (1884):** diluted – a European tried by an Indian judge could demand a **jury of at least half Europeans**.
- **Indian Association** **backed** the Bill; it exposed British racism and taught the value of organised agitation.
!TRAP The Indian Association supported (not opposed) the Ilbert Bill.
### 9. LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT AND DECENTRALISATION
#### 9.1 Milestones
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| **29 Sep 1688** | **Madras** – first Municipal Corporation (now **Greater Chennai Corporation**, oldest) |
| 1726 | Corporations at Bombay and Calcutta |
| 1817 | **Lottery Committee**, Calcutta – town planning with lottery funds (Strand Road, Dalhousie Square) |
| **1870** | **Mayo's Resolution** – financial decentralisation |
| **1882** | **Ripon's Resolution** – "Magna Carta" of LSG |
| 1907-09 | **Royal Commission on Decentralisation** (chairman **C.E.H. Hobhouse**) – stressed village panchayats; only recommendations |
| 1919 | LSG a **transferred** subject under dyarchy |
| **1924** | **Cantonments Act** – passed by the **Central legislature** |
| 1935 | LSG a **provincial** subject |
#### 9.2 Mayo vs Ripon
| Mayo (1869-72) | Ripon (1880-84) |
|---|---|
| Resolution **1870** | Resolution **1882** |
| First split of **Central and Provincial finances**; provinces given police, jails, education, medical with fixed grants; told to raise local taxes | Local boards with **elected non-official majority**; local autonomy |
| Also statistical survey and **first census (1872)** | **"Father of Local Self-Government in India"** |
- After Ripon, bodies stayed **short of money and controlled by officials**.
!KEY APPSC 2025: Cantonments Act 1924, Madras corporation 1688, Ripon father of LSG – all three correct.
!PAIR Mayo | 1870 | financial decentralisation || Ripon | 1882 | Magna Carta of LSG
