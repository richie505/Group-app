## U0605 | ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE – PRESSURE BELTS; PLANETARY, PERIODIC AND LOCAL WINDS; JET STREAMS
### 1. PRESSURE – BASICS AND INSTRUMENTS
- Heated air **expands, becomes lighter → low pressure**; cooled air contracts → high pressure.
- Pressure **falls with altitude**.
- **Standard sea-level pressure: 1013.25 mb (hPa)**.
- **Isobars** join places of equal pressure (reduced to sea level).
- **Pressure gradient** = fall of pressure per unit distance; **sets wind speed** (APPSC-G2 2011). Air moves **high → low**.
- **Closely spaced isobars** = steep gradient = **strong winds**.
| Barometer | Weather |
|---|---|
| **Sudden / steady fall** | Low pressure → cloud, rain, **storm** |
| Rising | Fair, settled |
| Instrument | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Barometer** (mercury, aneroid) / barograph | Pressure (mb, hPa) |
| **Anemometer** | Wind **speed** (km/h, knots) |
| **Wind vane** | Wind **direction** |
### 2. WIND CONTROLS – CORIOLIS, FRICTION, GEOSTROPHIC WIND
- **Coriolis force**: due to Earth's rotation; deflects **right in NH, left in SH** (**Ferrel's law**, APPSC-G1 2019).
- Coriolis is **zero at the equator, maximum at poles**; **grows with wind speed**.
- **No cyclones within ~5°** of the equator (Coriolis negligible); tropical cyclones stay between 30°N and 30°S.
- **Friction** slows surface winds, stronger over land; negligible aloft.
- **Geostrophic wind**: upper air, **parallel to isobars**; **pressure gradient force = Coriolis force**, no friction.
- Coriolis decides direction; friction affects speed.
### 3. PRESSURE BELTS
| Belt | Latitude | Other name / nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Equatorial low** | **0–5° N and S** | **Doldrums, ITCZ**; calm rising air; trades converge |
| **Subtropical high** | **30–35° N and S** | **Horse latitudes**; calm, sinking dry air, hot deserts |
| **Subpolar low** | **60–65°** | Westerlies meet polar easterlies; storms |
| **Polar high** | Poles (90°) | Cold, dense, sinking air |
- Belts **shift 5–10°** with the overhead sun (**north in NH summer**, south in winter).
- **Horse latitudes** and ITCZ asked in APPSC 2019.
!TRAP APPSC-G1 2024: "Doldrums are strong winds" and "NH trades blow from NW" are both WRONG → **neither**. Doldrums = **calm**; NH trades = **north-east**.
### 4. ITCZ AND THE MONSOON
- **ITCZ**: low-pressure trough near the equator where **NE and SE trades meet**; shifts with the sun.
- NH summer: ITCZ moves **north over the Indian plains** (monsoon trough) – this **helps** the monsoon (a result, not cause).
- Winter: ITCZ moves **south** of India; highs over **Tibet and Siberia** → **north-east monsoon**; most of India dry under **NE trades**.
- Southward shift of doldrums → heavy rain in southern states.
- Winter **anticyclonic** conditions: high pressure, low temperature. **Western disturbances** give North India winter rain – they do not cause the anticyclone.
- Himalayas = **climatic divide**, giving India its distinct monsoon climate.
### 5. PLANETARY WINDS
| Wind | From → to | NH | SH |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade winds** (easterlies) | Subtropical high → equatorial low | **NE** | **SE** |
| **Westerlies** | Subtropical high → subpolar low (35–60°) | **SW** | **NW** |
| **Polar easterlies** | Polar high → subpolar low | NE | SE |
- Trade winds push tropical cyclones **west onto east coasts**; westerlies turn them **north-east** (Cyclone Montha, Kakinada, Nov 2025).
- **Hadley cell**: rises at equator, sinks near **30°**, returns as trades (meridional). **Walker circulation**: **east–west** Pacific cell.
- NW Europe ports open all year; SW winds blow there all year (true, but not the reason).
### 6. ROARING FORTIES, FURIOUS FIFTIES, SHRIEKING SIXTIES
| Name | Latitude |
|---|---|
| **Roaring Forties** | **40–50° S** |
| **Furious Fifties** | **50–60° S** |
| **Shrieking Sixties** | Around **60° S** |
- **SH westerlies** are stronger and steadier – open ocean, little land friction; NH westerlies less steady (landmasses).
- Roaring Forties: strong, constant, blow west to east; over the **Indian Ocean**.
### 7. JET STREAMS AND POLAR VORTEX
- **Jet streams**: narrow, fast upper winds at **10–12 km**; regulate monsoon onset and withdrawal.
- **First identified by Wasaburo Ooishi (Japan, 1920s)**; widely known in **World War II** (bomber pilots).
| Jet | Position / season | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet** | **South of Himalayas in winter**; north in summer | Carries **western disturbances** from the Mediterranean; leaves before monsoon "burst"; returns **October** |
| **Tropical Easterly Jet** | **Summer**, **5–20°N**, June–early Oct (South China Sea → East Africa) | Upper anticyclone; **strengthens SW monsoon** |
| **Somali (Findlater) Jet** | Low-level, off **East Africa** | With Somali Current **boosts monsoon** |
- STWJ-carried western disturbances worsened 2026 monsoon extremes; may have fed flash floods in Nepal and Tibet (Aug 2026).
- **El Niño** weakens the monsoon; **positive IOD** (warmer western Indian Ocean) can offset it. UK Met Office expects the **biggest El Niño in over a century** (Aug 2026).
- **Polar vortex**: year-round **cold low** around the poles; **anticlockwise**; strongest in **winter**; stratospheric part 15–50 km.
- **Strong vortex keeps cold air up north** (APPSC 2018). **Rossby waves / sudden stratospheric warming** weaken it → winds reverse, vortex splits → cold spells in mid-latitudes.
!PAIR STJ | westerly, winter | western disturbances || TEJ | easterly, summer | strengthens SW monsoon
### 8. PERIODIC WINDS – BREEZES AND MONSOON
| Breeze | Time | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Sea breeze** | **Day** | Sea → land (land heats faster, lower pressure) |
| **Land breeze** | **Night** | Land → sea |
| **Valley (anabatic)** | Day | **Uphill** |
| **Mountain (katabatic)** | Night | **Downhill**; cold air in valleys → **inversion, frost** |
- Land and sea breezes are **daily**; monsoon reverses **seasonally**. They moderate **AP coastal** temperatures.
- **Monsoon**: from Arabic **"mausim" = season**; **seasonal reversal of winds** from **differential heating of land and sea**; summer sea → land, winter land → sea; clear wet and dry seasons.
| Theory | Year | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Halley** | **1686** | Giant land-sea breeze |
| **Modern theory** | **1957** | ITCZ shift + jet streams + Tibet Plateau + ENSO |
- **AP**: SW monsoon 2026 reached AP via **Rayalaseema on 6 June 2026** (two days late; normal 4 June). IMD expected **below-normal** AP rain under El Niño (Jun 2026).
- AP coastline **1,053.07 km**, **third longest** in India (AP Socio-Economic Survey 2025-26).
