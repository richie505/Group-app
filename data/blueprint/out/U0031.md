## U0031 | SPREAD AND DECLINE OF BUDDHISM; BUDDHIST AND JAIN ARCHITECTURE – STUPA, CHAITYA, VIHARA
### 1. STUPA, CHAITYA AND VIHARA – BASICS
- **Stupa, Chaitya and Vihara** are all related to **Buddhism** (Jains also cut caves and viharas).
- **Stupa** = solid relic mound for worship by circumambulation.
- **Chaitya** = **place of worship** (prayer hall, usually with a stupa at the apsidal end).
- **Vihara** = **residence of monks** (monastery).
- **Arama** = a complete complex of **stupa + vihara + chaitya + school**, e.g. **Nandaluru**.
| Feature | Stupa | Chaitya | Vihara |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Relic mound | Prayer hall | Monastery |
| Use | Worship, circumambulation | Congregational worship | Living quarters of monks |
| Example | Sanchi, Amaravati | Karla, Guntupalli | Ajanta viharas, Nalanda |
#### 1.1 Parts of a Stupa
| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Anda** | Hemispherical mound / dome (the cosmos) |
| **Medhi** | Raised drum or terrace below the dome, used for circumambulation |
| **Harmika** | Square railing on top of the anda (abode of the gods) |
| **Yashti** | Central mast rising from the harmika |
| **Chhatra / Chhatri** | Tiered umbrellas on the yashti (honour, protection) |
| **Vedika** | Railing separating the sacred from the secular |
| **Torana** | Ornamental gateways, usually at the four directions |
| **Pradakshina patha** | Path for clockwise circumambulation |
| **Ayaka pillars / Ayakapata** | Special feature of **Andhra** stupas; **Purna Kumbha** among linked symbols |
!TRAP Ayaka pillars are an Andhra-only feature; they are not part of the Sanchi model.
#### 1.2 Types of Stupa (Nagarjunakonda classification)
| Type | Content |
|---|---|
| **Dhatugarbha** | Bodily relics of the Buddha |
| **Paribhogika** | Objects used by the Buddha, e.g. alms bowl |
| **Uddeshika** | Memorial only (no relics) |
### 2. ANICONIC SYMBOLS AND THE BUDDHA IMAGE
| Symbol | Event in the Buddha's life |
|---|---|
| **Lotus and bull** | Birth |
| **Horse (Kanthaka)** | Renunciation (Great Departure) |
| **Empty seat under the Bodhi tree** | Enlightenment (Bodh Gaya) |
| **Dharmachakra (wheel)** | First sermon (Sarnath) |
| **Stupa / footprints** | Parinirvana |
- **Hinayana** shows the Buddha through **symbols**; **Mahayana** adopted **images** of the Buddha.
- The Buddha was first shown in **human form** in the **1st century CE**, at **Gandhara** and **Mathura**.
!PAIR Dharmachakra | First sermon | Sarnath || Horse Kanthaka | Renunciation | Great Departure
### 3. MAJOR STUPAS – BUILDER, PERIOD, OLDEST AND LARGEST
#### 3.1 Early and Major Stupas (chronological order)
| Stupa | Location | Builder / period |
|---|---|---|
| **Piprahwa** | UP (ancient **Kapilavastu**) | Among the earliest, c. **5th c. BCE**; inscribed relic vessel linked to the Buddha's kin |
| **Sanchi** | Raisen, MP | **Ashoka**, 3rd c. BCE brick stupa; **stone casing under the Shungas**; **toranas by the Satavahanas** (south torana – **Siri Satakarni**); **UNESCO 1989** |
| **Bharhut** | MP | **Shunga**, 2nd c. BCE; called the most ancient stupa in India |
| **Amaravati** | AP | Satavahana Mahachaitya; built in Pulumavi II's time, enlarged by Ikshvakus |
| **Devnimori** | Gujarat | **Western Kshatrapas**, c. 4th c. CE |
| **Dhamek** | Sarnath, UP | **Gupta**, c. 500 CE |
| **Kesariya** | Bihar | Later large brick stupa; about **32 m** high |
- Relics of the Buddha were divided into **nine stupas** after his death: Rajagriha, Vaishali, Kapilavastu, Allakappa, Ramagrama, Vethapida, Pava, Kushinagar, Pippalivana.
#### 3.2 Largest / Oldest Ranking
| Rank | Stupa |
|---|---|
| Largest in the world | **Borobudur** (Java, Indonesia; UNESCO World Heritage Site) |
| Largest (tallest) brick stupa in India | **Kesariya** (Bihar) |
| Most ancient in India | **Bharhut** |
| Largest in AP and South India | **Amaravati** |
| Oldest in AP | **Bhattiprolu** |
!TRAP Kesariya = largest brick stupa in India (about 32 m), slightly taller than Borobudur; Borobudur is still the largest Buddhist monument in the world.
#### 3.3 Sarnath
- **B. R. Mani's excavations (2013–14)** showed activity at Sarnath **before Ashoka**.
- On the **UNESCO Tentative List since 1998**; **nominated in 2025–26**.
### 4. RECENT EXCAVATIONS OF BUDDHIST SITES
- **Vaishya Tekri (near Ujjain, MP)** – believed a **Mauryan 3rd c. BCE** stupa; estimated **350 ft diameter, 100 ft high**, possibly larger than Sanchi; linked to **Ashoka's wife Devi**; thought to be **Kanakagiri Vihara**.
  - **Aug 2026:** Madhya Pradesh plans to restart excavation at Vaishya Tekri.
- **Ratnagiri (Jajpur, Odisha)** – **ASI** resumed digging in **Dec 2024** after about six decades; **Jan 2025** finds include a **colossal Buddha head**, a giant palm, votive stupas and a monolithic elephant; a **Vajrayana** centre.
- **Kesariya (Bihar)** – ASI excavation suggests an additional terrace below ground level.

#### 5.3 Ajanta Paintings
- Paintings of the **Gupta and post-Gupta** periods, mainly the **5th–6th c. Vakataka** phase; earliest caves (**9 and 10**) are **Satavahana**.
- **Cave 1** – **Bodhisattva Padmapani**.
- **Cave 10** – **Svetagaja (Swetagaja) Jataka** (not Cave 11).
- **Cave 17** – important **Vakataka** painting cycle, incl. Jataka scenes.
- Gupta paintings survive at **Ajanta** and **Bagh** (MP).
| Site | Religion |

| Elephanta | Shaiva |
| Khajuraho | Shaiva, Vaishnava and Jain temples |
| Nalanda | Buddhist education centre |
!TRAP Svetagaja Jataka = Ajanta Cave 10, not 11. Padmapani = Cave 1.

### 9. SPREAD OF BUDDHISM – MISSIONS AND PATRONS
#### 9.1 Ashoka's Missions (after the Third Council, Pataliputra)
| Missionary | Region |
|---|---|
| **Mahinda + Sanghamitta** | **Sri Lanka** |
| **Sona + Uttara** | **Suvarnabhumi** |
| **Majjhantika** | Kashmir and Gandhara |
| **Mahadeva** | Mahishamandala (Mysore) |
| **Rakkhita** | Vanavasi (North Kanara) |
| **Maharakkhita** | Yona (Greek) country |
- **Mahavamsa:** **Mahadeva Bhikshu** took Buddhism **from Andhra to Sri Lanka** with 100,000 followers (not the reverse).
#### 9.2 Andhra Patrons
| Patron | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Satavahanas** | Vedic kings but lavish donors; Sanchi south torana (Siri Satakarni) |
| **Gautami Balashri** | Nasik cave |
| **Naganika** | Naneghat |
| **Veerapurushadatta** (Ikshvaku) | Rebuilt the **Nagarjunakonda Maha Chaitya** and **Sriparvata University**; Buddhism flourished with support of **royal women** of his family |
| **Upasika Bodhisri** | Great service in the reign of Ikshvaku king **Ehuvala Chantamula** (APPSC G2 2012, 2019) |
| **Vishnukundins** | **Last** dynasty to support Buddhism in Andhra; **Indrapalanagara vihara** |
- Ikshvakus were hostile to Saivism (sculpture of a Shivalinga trampled).
- **Lay patronage** dominated in Andhra: **queens, merchants, guilds and artisans**; e.g. a deposit of **2,000 karshapanas** with a **weavers' guild** for monks' robes.
!TRAP Bodhisri = Ehuvala Chantamula's reign; Vishnukundins = LAST Andhra patrons.
### 10. DECLINE OF BUDDHISM
- **Later Vajrayana phase:** Salihundam, Bojjannakonda; **Siddha Nagarjuna** at Sriparvata; Vikramashila in Bihar.
- **Causes:** loss of royal patronage; **Brahmanical revival (Bhakti)**; **Vajrayana ritualism**.
- **Hostile rulers:** **Pushyamitra Shunga**, **Shashanka**, **Mihirakula**; **Turkish raids** on Nalanda.
- **Hiuen Tsang (7th c. CE)** found **Sriparvata** working **without patronage**.
