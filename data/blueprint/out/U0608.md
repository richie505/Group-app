## U0608 | ATMOSPHERIC DISTURBANCES – TROPICAL CYCLONES, TEMPERATE CYCLONES, ANTICYCLONES, THUNDERSTORMS, TORNADOES
### 1. DISTURBANCES AT A GLANCE
| Disturbance | Nature | Scale | Clouds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical cyclone | Low-pressure system over warm tropical oceans | Large | Cumulonimbus, rainbands |
| Temperate (extra-tropical) cyclone | Mid-latitude low from air-mass interaction, with fronts | Large | Stratiform and cumuliform |
| Anticyclone | High-pressure centre, outward sinking air | Large | Generally clear |
| Thunderstorm | Violent convective storm with lightning, thunder | Local | Cumulonimbus |
| Tornado (twister) | Violently rotating funnel | Very local | Cumulonimbus supercell |
| Western Disturbance | Extratropical storm affecting NW India | Regional | – |
| Cloudburst, squall | Intense local rain; sudden gusty wind | Local | – |
!KEY **Fronts** belong to **temperate cyclones only**; tropical cyclones, anticyclones, thunderstorms and tornadoes have none.
### 2. TROPICAL CYCLONES – FORMATION AND LIMITS
#### 2.1 Conditions
| Condition | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sea-surface temperature above 26.5°C** over a large area | Heat and moisture – most essential |
| Large supply of warm, moist air | **Latent heat of condensation** = energy source |
| **Coriolis force** | Rotation; too weak within about **5°** of the Equator |
| Unstable troposphere | Vigorous convection |
| **Low vertical wind shear** | Keeps vertical structure organised |
| Pre-existing disturbance | Seed low / cyclonic circulation |
- Form **only over warm oceans**, not over rivers or lakes (**APPSC-GS 2025**); cold currents suppress them.
- Force decreases away from the sea; they **weaken after landfall** (cut off from moisture).
#### 2.2 Where They Form and Do Not
- Belt: **between 30°N and 30°S** (**APPSC 2022 key**); mainly 5°–30° N and S, western parts of tropical oceans.
- **None at the Equator** and none in the **South Atlantic and south-east Pacific** (low SST, strong shear, ITCZ seldom there).
- A tropical cyclone in May is linked to local temperature variation.
- Marine heatwaves raise cyclone winds by up to 20% and rain by up to 12% (2026 study).
### 3. STRUCTURE, ROTATION AND STEERING
| Part | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | Calm, clear, sinking air; **lowest pressure**; higher temperature; 20–50 km across |
| **Eyewall** | Ring of towering cumulonimbus; **strongest winds**, heaviest rain |
| Spiral rainbands | Curved bands of rain and wind |
- Air moves **inward and rises**; rotation **anticlockwise in NH, clockwise in SH** (Coriolis).
- Trade winds push cyclones **west**; mature storms may **recurve north-east** under westerlies.
- Threats: high winds, heavy rain, flooding, **storm surge** (abnormal rise of sea water – **biggest killer**).
### 4. CYCLONE VS ANTICYCLONE
| Basis | Cyclone | Anticyclone |
|---|---|---|
| Centre | Low pressure | High pressure |
| Air | Inward, rising | Outward, sinking (diverging) |
| Weather | Cloudy, stormy | **Clear, dry, calm, settled** |
| NH rotation | Anticlockwise | **Clockwise** |
| SH rotation | Clockwise | Anticlockwise |
- **Heat dome**: a stalled warm high traps hot air (27 May 2026).
### 5. TEMPERATE CYCLONES AND WESTERN DISTURBANCES
| Feature | Tropical | Temperate |
|---|---|---|
| Latitude | 5–30° | **35–65°** (mid and high) |
| Origin | Only over warm seas | **Land and sea** |
| Fronts | None | **Clear frontal system** (warm, cold; occluded may develop) |
| Size, winds | Smaller, very intense | **Larger area, gentler winds**; rain over wide area, longer period |
| Movement | East to west (trades) | **West to east** (westerlies) |
!TRAP **APPSC-G1 2024**: "Temperate cyclones do not have a clear frontal system" is the INCORRECT statement.
- **Western Disturbance**: weak cyclonic (extratropical) disturbance from the **Mediterranean**; reaches **NW India in winter** at the occluded stage; about 10–15 cm rain for **rabi** crops, snow on mountains; rain **decreases west to east** across the NW plain.
### 6. TORNADOES, THUNDERSTORMS AND LOCAL STORMS
#### 6.1 Tornadoes
- Rotating column from a **thunderstorm** to the ground; dark funnel; winds **105–322 km/h**; moves up to about 90–100 km/h; lasts minutes (rarely over 15–20).
- From rotating **supercells**; an ordinary thunderstorm is a short-lived single-cell cumulonimbus.
- Mostly **USA** (**Tornado Alley**): warm moist **Gulf of Mexico** air meets cold dry continental air with strong shear.
| Feature | Tornado | Hurricane |
|---|---|---|
| Size, life | Small, minutes | Hundreds of km, days |
| Where | **Land-based** | **Ocean-born** |
- **Waterspout** = tornado over water; seen over the **Brahmaputra, Barpeta (Assam)**, 26 Aug 2026.
#### 6.2 Thunderstorms and Local Storms
- Cumulonimbus up to 15 km tall in unstable, moist air. **Thunder** = sound of air expanding explosively after **lightning** heats it.
| Storm | Season / region |
|---|---|
| **Kaal Baisakhi / Nor'westers** | Pre-monsoon; West Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Assam ("tea showers"); approach from NW |
| **Aandhi** (dust storm) | Summer; dry plains of NW India |
| **Cloudburst** | Himalaya, June–August; about **100 mm/hour** over 20–30 sq km |
- **Hailstorms**: strong instability, moisture, updrafts; synoptic causes = Western Disturbances and wind discontinuity lines; plateaus and hills boost convection.
- **Damini App**: lightning alerts in **15 languages**; **104** detection locations.
### 7. REGIONAL NAMES
| Name | Region |
|---|---|
| **Hurricane** | North Atlantic, Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico, NE / eastern Pacific (USA) |
| **Typhoon** | NW Pacific, China Sea (100°–180°E most at risk), Japan, Philippines |
| **Cyclone** | North Indian Ocean (India), South Pacific |
| **Willy-willy** | **North-west Australia** |
| **Baguio** | **Philippines** |
| Tornado | Small funnel over land (USA) |
- Threshold for hurricane / typhoon / cyclone: **74 mph (119 km/h)**. **Super typhoon**: about 240 km/h (strong Cat 4–5); Super Typhoon **Bavi** = 3rd Cat-5 storm of 2026 (10 Jul 2026). Typhoon **Noul** hit Guangdong (Jul 2026).
- India gets tropical cyclones, **not hurricanes**. PYQ key: strong tropical cyclones (hurricanes) start in the Caribbean.
### 8. MEASUREMENT SCALES AND IMD CATEGORIES
#### 8.1 Scales
| Scale | Measures | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Saffir-Simpson** | **Hurricanes** (wind speed; estimates property damage, coastal flooding) | Cat 1–5 |
| **Fujita / Enhanced Fujita** (Ted Fujita) | **Tornadoes** | EF0–EF5 (6 categories) |
| **Beaufort** | Wind force | 0–12; **gale = force 8** (about 62–74 km/h) |
| Richter (Charles Richter, 1935) | Quake magnitude | Logarithmic |
| Moment magnitude (Mw) | Quake magnitude (energy at source) | Modern standard |
| **Modified Mercalli** | Quake **intensity** (effects, damage); mainly USA | I–XII |
| **MSK-64** | Quake intensity; India and Europe; UNESCO-approved 1964 | I–XII |
| INES | Nuclear events (IAEA, 1990) | 0–7 |
- Saffir-Simpson: Cat 1 **119–153**; Cat 2 154–177; Cat 3 178–208; Cat 4 209–251; Cat 5 **252+** km/h.
#### 8.2 IMD Categories (North Indian Ocean; 3-minute sustained wind)
| Category | km/h | Knots |
|---|---|---|
| Low Pressure Area | < 31 | < 17 |
| Depression | 31–49 | 17–27 |
| Deep Depression | 50–61 | 28–33 |
| **Cyclonic Storm** (named from here) | **62–88** | 34–47 |
| Severe Cyclonic Storm | 89–117 | 48–63 |
| Very Severe | 118–166 | 64–89 |
| **Extremely Severe** (added **2015**) | 167–221 | 90–119 |
| **Super Cyclonic Storm** (highest) | **222+** | 120+ |
- APPSC 2021 key: a tropical cyclone has winds **at or above 62 km/h**; APPSC 2018 key gave Cyclonic Storm as the 63–88 km/h option.

### 10. INDIA'S EXPOSURE AND MAJOR CYCLONES
- India faces about **10%** of world tropical cyclones (**APPSC-GS 2023**). Coastline: old **7,516.6 km**; remeasured 2023–24 = **11,098.81 km** (+47.7%); about 5,900 km directly exposed.
- **Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea ≈ 4:1**; Bay gives **75–80%** (high SST, Pacific typhoon remnants). East coast (Odisha, AP, WB, TN) more prone; **Odisha** faces most disasters.
- Arabian Sea: fewer but more intense; 2019 storms **Vayu, Hikka, Kyarr, Maha, Pavan**.
- Season **bimodal**: main **Oct–Dec**, secondary Apr–May; in monsoon at onset (June) and withdrawal (Sept).
- **INCOIS (Hyderabad)**, 1995–2019: El Nino may cut post-monsoon Bay cyclones (**15** in 9 El Nino years vs **33** in 11 La Nina years).

- Geo-informatics cannot predict all cyclone impacts, such as death count.
- Sep 2026: an Andaman Sea low could become the first cyclone of the SW monsoon season, heading for Odisha.
