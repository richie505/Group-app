## U0320 | FEDERAL STRUCTURE OF INDIA – FEDERALISM, UNITARY BIAS, SEVENTH SCHEDULE AND RESIDUARY POWERS
### 1. FEDERALISM – MEANING AND TYPES
#### 1.1 Definitions
- **Federalism:** Union and States **share power**, each **supreme in its own sphere**, under a **written Constitution**, with an **independent umpire** (judiciary).
- **Unitary government:** one supreme central government; local units are its **creations** with delegated power; **no constitutional division of powers**.
- **Confederation:** loose league of **sovereign States** that keep the **right to secede**.
- Federation = constitutional division of powers.
#### 1.2 Unitary Government – Merits and Demerits
| Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|
| Uniform laws and policy | Over-centralisation |
| Quick decisions | Local needs neglected |
| Strong crisis response | Poorly suited to **large, diverse** countries |
- Regional diversity through autonomous governments is a **federal** trait, not a unitary one.
!TRAP "Ideal for large countries" is NOT a characteristic of unitary government.
#### 1.3 Holding-together vs Coming-together
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Coming-together** | Independent States pool sovereignty (integration) | **USA, Switzerland, Australia** |
| **Holding-together** | Large country divides power to hold diversity; stronger Centre (disintegration) | **India, Spain, Belgium** |
- India moved from a **unitary colonial system** to a federal one, so it has a **centripetal bias**.
- Centralising design because of **Partition** and the joining of **14 provinces** and **500+ princely States**.
!PAIR USA | integration | coming-together || India | disintegration | holding-together
#### 1.4 Federal and Unitary Countries
| Federal | Unitary |
|---|---|
| **USA** (by agreement of States), **Canada, Australia, Argentina** | **UK** (Parliament holds all law-making power) |
| Switzerland, Germany, Russia, Brazil | **France, Italy**, Japan, China, **Sri Lanka** |
!TRAP Sri Lanka is unitary, not federal.
### 2. INDIA – A UNION OF STATES
#### 2.1 Article 1
- **Art. 1(1):** "**India, that is Bharat**, shall be a **Union of States**." Names used: **Bharat and India**.
- The word "**Federation**" is **nowhere** used in the Constitution.
- "A Union of States" is derived from the **British North America Act, 1867** (Canada).
- Territory of India = **States + UTs + acquired territories**; **Union of India** = States only. Both listed in the **First Schedule**.
#### 2.2 Ambedkar's Reasons for "Union"
1. The federation is **not the result of an agreement** among States (unlike USA).
2. **No State has the right to secede**; the Union is **indestructible**.
- States are divisions only for **administrative convenience**; the Union of India is an **indissoluble union** of States.
#### 2.3 Indestructible Union of Destructible States – Articles 2-4
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| 2 | **Admission or establishment** of new States not part of India (e.g. Sikkim) |
| 3 | **Formation** of new States; alter area, boundary, name – by **simple majority** |
| 4 | Laws under Arts 2-3 amend **First and Fourth Schedules**; **not** Art. 368 amendments |
- Art. 3 Bill needs the **President's prior recommendation** and reference to the State legislature, whose views are **not binding**; **no consent** needed.
- Power to make a new State lies with **Parliament**.
- No territorial guarantee, so India = "**indestructible Union of destructible States**"; USA = "indestructible Union of **indestructible** States".
!KEY Article 3: Parliament can alter State boundaries without the State's consent.
### 3. FEDERAL VS UNITARY FEATURES
#### 3.1 Classification Table
| Federal features | Unitary (non-federal) features |
|---|---|
| **Dual polity** (two governments) | **Strong Centre** (Canadian model); Union prevails in Concurrent List |
| **Written** Constitution; **supremacy** of the Constitution | **Residuary powers** with Parliament |
| **Division of powers** (Seventh Schedule, three Lists) | **Single Constitution** (States cannot frame their own) |
| **Rigid** Constitution (federal parts need State ratification) | **Flexibility**: most provisions amended by Parliament alone |
| **Independent judiciary** (umpire in Centre-State disputes) | **Single citizenship** |
| **Bicameralism** (Rajya Sabha for States) | **Integrated judiciary**; Centre appoints HC judges |
| Unequal Rajya Sabha seats listed by some as federal | **Governor appointed by Centre** (Arts 155-156) |
| | **All India Services** (Art. 312) – recruited by UPSC, central control |
| | **Emergency provisions** (Arts 352-360) |
| | Parliament can alter State boundaries (Art. 3) |
| | Integrated **audit (CAG)** and **elections (EC)** |
| | "Union of States", no right to secede; **unequal Rajya Sabha** representation |
| | President's approval on State Bills; Parliament's authority over State List |
- **Independence of the judiciary** = the **essential** feature showing India is federal (UPSC 2021); **judicial review** strengthens the federal principle – not a unitary element.
- Judicial review includes examining **executive orders**; it does not question the **policy merits** of laws.
- Federal features in another key: written Constitution, distribution of powers, supremacy, independent judiciary, unequal representation in Rajya Sabha, dual governments.
- India is federal **because of the distribution of powers** between Union and States.
- The Constitution is **neither wholly rigid nor wholly flexible**.
!TRAP "Not a federal feature" answers: Office of the Governor (APPSC-G1 2018), CAG (APPSC-G2 2017), "Allocation of financial resources" (APPSC-G2 2017), Single Citizenship.
#### 3.2 Centrally Controlled Institutions – Violating Federal Principles
- APPSC 2025: **Office of the Governor**, **Central (All India) Services**, **CAG**, **Emergency powers** (esp. **Art. 356**) – **all four** are criticised as violating federal principles.
- **All India Services** (Art. 312): recruited and controlled by the Centre but serving the States – a centrally controlled institution.
!TRAP Do not leave out **Central Services** – it is one of the four centrally controlled institutions said to violate federal principles.
- Critics call the Governor the **Centre's agent**.
- CAG audits **both Union and State** accounts; EC runs elections to Parliament and State legislatures.
#### 3.3 Form of Government
- **Parliamentary form** (British model): nominal head = **President**; real executive = **Council of Ministers**, collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha** (Arts **74(1), 75**).
- India has an **integrated judicial and administrative** framework.

### 5. SEVENTH SCHEDULE – THE THREE LISTS
#### 5.1 Lists and Entries (Article 246)
| List | Original entries | Now | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I – Union** | **97** | **98** | National importance, uniform law |
| **II – State** | **66** | **59** | Local/regional importance |
| **III – Concurrent** | **47** | **52** | Both legislate; Union law prevails |
- **Art. 245** territorial extent; **246** the three Lists; **248** residuary; **254** repugnancy.
- Precedence: **Union > Concurrent > State**; a valid Union law prevails in a Concurrent conflict.
- Numbering still ends at 97/66/47 because entries were inserted (e.g. **92A** by the **6th Amendment, 1956** – inter-State sales tax) or omitted.
- Parliament makes laws for the Union, the Territory of India, and Indian subjects and their property **anywhere in the world**.

### 6. RESIDUARY POWERS AND BORROWED FEATURES
#### 6.1 Article 248
- **Article 248** (Part **XI**, Chapter I) + **Entry 97, List I**: Parliament has **exclusive** power over matters in **no list**.
- Includes **residuary taxes** (gift tax, wealth tax, expenditure tax).
- Art. 248 made "**subject to Article 246A**" by the **101st Amendment, 2016** (GST).
| Model | Residue with |
|---|---|
| **India, Canada** | Centre |
| **USA, Australia** | States |
| **GoI Act 1935** | **Governor-General** |
!NUM Entry 97 = residuary entry of List I and also the original Union List count.
#### 6.2 Borrowed Features
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| Federal scheme, Governor, emergency provisions | **GoI Act, 1935** |
| Federation with a **strong Centre** | **Canada** |
| **Concurrent List** | **Australia** |
- The federal system is described as inspired by **Canada** (strong Centre).
### 7. 42ND AMENDMENT TRANSFERS (1976)
- **Five** subjects moved State → Concurrent: **education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, administration of justice**.
- Administration of justice = courts **other than** the SC and HCs. Higher education moved too.
- Inserted **Entry 20A**: **population control and family planning**.
!TRAP Public order was NOT transferred; it stays in the State List.
- Debate: Centre uses Concurrent entries for sweeping laws – **farm laws** (Entry 33), **NEP** and **NMC Act** (education).
### 8. SWITCHING TO UNITARY MODE – EMERGENCY AND OVERRIDING POWERS
#### 8.1 Overriding Powers
| Article | Power |
|---|---|
| **249** | **Rajya Sabha** resolution (**2/3 present and voting**) in national interest → Parliament legislates on State List; valid **1 year**, renewable; Lok Sabha takes no part |
| **250** | During National Emergency, Parliament legislates on State List; laws lapse **6 months** after revocation |
| 252 | Two or more States request Parliament to legislate |
| 253 | Laws to implement international agreements |
| **356** | President's Rule – Parliament/President exercise State powers |
- Emergency turns the system unitary **without a formal amendment** – a unique feature.
#### 8.2 Emergencies Compared (Part XVIII, Arts 352-360)
| Basis | 352 National | 356 President's Rule | 360 Financial |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | War, external aggression, **armed rebellion** | Failure of constitutional machinery (also Art. 365) | Threat to financial stability or credit |
| Approval | **1 month**, special majority | **2 months**, simple | **2 months**, simple |
| Duration | 6 months at a time, **no maximum** | 6 months at a time, **max 3 years** | **Until revoked** |
| Used | **1962, 1971, 1975** | Over 100 times | **Never** |
- Beyond **1 year**, President's Rule needs a **National Emergency** in operation + **EC certification** (Art. 356(5)).
- If the Lok Sabha is dissolved after the Rajya Sabha approves, the proclamation survives **30 days** from the new Lok Sabha's first sitting (all three).
#### 8.3 Effects of National Emergency on States
- Union can **direct any State** on its executive power.
- Parliament may extend the **Lok Sabha and Assembly terms** by **1 year at a time**, at most **6 months** after it ends.
- **Art. 354:** President may **modify revenue distribution** (Arts 268-281) – **reduce or cancel** transfers; lasts till the end of the **financial year** in which the proclamation ceases.
- **Art. 19** suspended automatically only for **war or external aggression** (Art. 358); others by order (Art. 359) except **Arts 20-21**.
- Only National Emergency affects Fundamental Rights; Arts 356 and 360 do not.
!TRAP "Increase the transfer" to States is a WRONG statement (APPSC-G2 2017).
#### 8.4 Financial Emergency (Art. 360)
- Directions on **financial propriety**; State **Money Bills** reserved for the President; **salary cuts** incl. **SC and HC judges**.
- No automatic takeover of State legislative powers. "Art. 311 – Financial Emergency" is a mismatch.
#### 8.5 44th Amendment, 1978 – Safeguards
- "Internal disturbance" → "**armed rebellion**" (APPSC-GS 2018); approval **2 months → 1 month**; **Arts 20-21** non-suspendable; Cabinet's **written advice**.
- **1/10th** of Lok Sabha members' notice → sitting within **14 days** to disapprove.
- 38th Amendment barred judicial review of Emergency; **Minerva Mills** reopened it.
- **1975 Emergency:** 25 June 1975 (internal disturbance); withdrawn **21 March 1977**. **Samvidhan Hatya Diwas: 25 June**.
- **AP** under President's Rule in **1973** (Jai Andhra agitation).
### 9. SUPREME COURT AND FEDERALISM
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **State of West Bengal v Union of India (1962)** | Not truly federal; States **not sovereign**; Parliament can acquire State property |
| **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** | **Federal character** is a basic feature |
| **S.R. Bommai (1994)** | **Federalism = basic structure**; Art. 356 open to **judicial review**; floor test |
| **MADA v SAIL (July 2024, 9 judges, 8:1)** | **Royalty is not a tax**; States can tax mineral rights |
!KEY Federalism as basic structure: S.R. Bommai (1994).
### 10. RAJYA SABHA, AMENDMENT AND ASYMMETRIC FEDERALISM
#### 10.1 Representation and Amendment
- Rajya Sabha seats: **Fourth Schedule**, broadly by **population** (UP 31, small States 1); US Senate **2 per State** (equal). Unequal representation counted as a unitary feature.
- Amendment (Art. 368, Part **XX**): most provisions by Parliament alone; **federal provisions** need ratification by **half the States**.
| Schedule | Subject |
|---|---|
| First | States and UTs |
| Fourth | Rajya Sabha seats |
| Fifth | Scheduled Areas |
| Seventh | Three Lists |
| Tenth | Anti-defection |
#### 10.2 Asymmetric Federalism
- **Art. 370 (former):** J&K's separate constitution and flag; made **inoperative Aug 2019**; upheld by SC **Dec 2023**.
- Indian States have **no separate constitutions** (former J&K the exception); each US State has its own.
| Article | State |
|---|---|
| 371 | Maharashtra and Gujarat |
| 371A | Nagaland |
| **371D** | **Andhra Pradesh** – equal opportunities in public employment and education |
| 371F | Sikkim |
| 371J | Karnataka |
- **Fifth Schedule:** Scheduled Areas; Sixth Schedule: tribal areas of the North-East.
#### 10.3 India vs USA
| Feature | India | USA |
|---|---|---|
| Formation | Holding together | Coming together |
| Citizenship | **Single** | Dual |
| Lists / residue | Three Lists; residue with Centre | Only federal powers listed; residue with States |
| Judiciary | Single integrated | Dual |
| Upper House | By population | Equal (2 per State) |
- Common: written, supreme Constitution; division of powers; **a Federal Supreme Court** to interpret it; bicameral Parliament.
