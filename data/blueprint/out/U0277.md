### 2. LENGTHIEST WRITTEN CONSTITUTION
#### 2.1 Written, Single Document
- **Written** like the US Constitution; the British Constitution is **unwritten**.
- Contained in a **single document**.
#### 2.2 Number Game – Original vs Now
| Item | Original (26 Nov 1949) | Now (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | **395** (with Preamble) | **about 470** |
| Parts | **22** | **25** |
| Schedules | **8** | **12** |
- New provisions are added as **lettered Articles** (e.g. **21A, 51A**); numbering still ends at **Article 395**.
!TRAP Old notes say 9 original Schedules and 397 Articles – wrong. Original = 8 Schedules, 395 Articles, 22 Parts.
#### 2.3 Reasons for Length
- India's **size, diversity and complexity**.
- Heavy influence of the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
- **One Constitution for both Centre and States**.
- Dominance of **legal luminaries** (lawyers) in the Assembly.
- Detailed **administrative provisions**.

### 4. PARLIAMENTARY FORM OF GOVERNMENT
#### 4.1 Features and Reasons
- Follows the **British model**, not the American presidential one.
- **Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 74(1), Art. 75).
- Ministers are **members of the legislature**.
- Chosen because: already **familiar under the GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935**; suits India's **size and diversity**; avoids executive–legislature conflict.
- India is described as **a Union of States with a parliamentary system**.
- Cardinal features: **democratic republic**, **parliamentary form**, **supreme power vests in the People**; power not in a single authority (separation of powers).
#### 4.2 India vs USA vs Britain
| Point | India | Other |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | Parliamentary (British model) | USA: presidential, separation of powers |
| Legislature–executive | Cooperation and coordination | USA: separation |
| Parliament | **Not sovereign** – a creature of the Constitution | Britain: **parliamentary sovereignty** |
| Head of State | **Elected** (indirectly) | Britain: **hereditary** |
| Citizenship | **Single** | USA: **dual**; states have own constitutions |
| Judicial review | **Narrower** (Art. 21: "procedure established by law") | USA: judicial supremacy |
!TRAP APPSC GS 2019: "Britain's head of State is elected" = WRONG. India's head is elected; Indian Parliament is not sovereign.
### 5. DEMOCRACY, REPUBLIC AND CONSTITUTIONALISM
#### 5.1 Terms and Meanings
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Representative (indirect) democracy** | People elect representatives; **no referendum, initiative or recall** nationally |
| **Republic** | **No hereditary ruler**; head of State **elected** |
| **Sovereign democratic republic** | Elected head; ultimate authority with the people |
| **Parliamentary Democracy** | What India is |
| **Ultimate source of authority** | **"We, the People of India"** (Preamble) |
| **Supreme authority** | The **Constitution**, not Parliament |
- IYB 2026 description: "a **union of states**, a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic** with a parliamentary system of government".
- President elected **indirectly**; parliamentary system combined with **republicanism**.
#### 5.2 Constitutionalism
- **Constitutionalism**: government power **limited by a written, supreme Constitution**; power used by rules, not the ruler's wish (**absolute rule**).
- **Constitutional government**: government limited by the terms of the Constitution.
- Limits in India: **Fundamental Rights (Art. 13** – inconsistent laws void), **judicial review**, federal division of powers, **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**).
- Parliament's power to amend is limited by the **basic structure doctrine**.
- Basic-structure elements: **supremacy of the Constitution**, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power.
#### 5.3 Supremacy of the Constitution
- Constitution is the **supreme law**; **Union and States derive their powers from it**.
### 6. FEDERAL SYSTEM WITH UNITARY BIAS
| Federal features | Unitary features |
|---|---|
| Two levels of government (later three) | **Strong Centre** (Canada model) |
| **Division of powers** (three Lists) | **Single citizenship** |
| Written, supreme Constitution | **Integrated judiciary** |
| Independent judiciary | **Emergency provisions** |
| Bicameral Parliament | States have **no separate constitutions** |
- **Quasi-federal**: federal in form, unitary in spirit (Wheare).
- In an emergency the **federal structure turns unitary without formal amendment**.
- Indian States have **no separate constitutions and no dual citizenship** (the USA has both).
### 7. BLEND OF RIGIDITY AND FLEXIBILITY
| Method | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Simple majority** of Parliament (outside Art. 368) | Admission of new States, change of State boundaries |
| **Special majority** of Parliament (Art. 368) | Most provisions (e.g. Fundamental Rights) |
| **Special majority + ratification by half the State legislatures** | Federal provisions |
- Neither as rigid as the US nor as flexible as the British Constitution.
### 8. SECULAR STATE
- India is a **secular state**: **no state religion**.
- **Indian model (positive)**: equal respect to all religions; the State may legislate on religious matters for social reform.
- **Western model (negative)**: strict separation of State and religion.
- Freedom of religion: **Articles 25–28**.
!TRAP A theocratic / religious state is NOT a feature (APPSC GS 2018).
### 9. OTHER SALIENT FEATURES
#### 9.1 Judiciary
- **Synthesis of parliamentary sovereignty (Britain) and judicial supremacy (USA)**: courts can strike down laws.
- **Integrated and independent judiciary**: a single court system enforces both **Central and State laws**.
#### 9.2 Rights, Duties and Franchise
| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| Fundamental Rights | **Six** categories; all suspendable in National Emergency **except Arts. 20 and 21** |
| Right to Property | Moved out of FRs; now **Article 300A** |
| Fundamental Duties | **11** (42nd Amendment + one by the **86th**) |
| **Universal adult franchise** | From the start; voting age **21 → 18** by **61st Amendment, 1988** |
#### 9.3 Bodies, Emergency and Tiers
- **Independent bodies**: ECI, CAG, UPSC, SPSCs.
- **Emergency provisions**: **Part XVIII, Arts. 352–360** – National, State (President's Rule), Financial.
- **Three-tier government**: **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** added the local tier to the original two-level polity.
- Features form the base of the basic structure list: supremacy, federalism, secularism, parliamentary system, judicial review, independence of judiciary.
### 10. FEATURES vs NON-FEATURES – ODD ONE OUT
| Feature | Non-feature (odd one out) |
|---|---|
| **Single citizenship** | **Dual citizenship** (APPSC GS 2025) |
| Parliamentary form | **Presidential form** |
| Secular state | **Religious state** (APPSC GS 2018) |
| Universal adult franchise | Property/education-based franchise |
| Written constitution | Unwritten constitution |
| Elected head (republic) | Hereditary head of State |
- Full list of features: single citizenship, secular state, republic, independent judiciary, supremacy of the Constitution, division of powers, federalism, parliamentary government, independent constitutional bodies.
