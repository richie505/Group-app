### 2. INDUSTRIAL POLICY RESOLUTION 1948
- Announced **6 April 1948** by Industry Minister **Dr Shyama Prasad Mukherjee**.
- Laid the foundation of a **mixed economy** – roles and spheres of both public and private sectors (APPSC 2019, 2023).
| Category | Industries | Arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusive Central monopoly | **Arms and ammunition, atomic energy, railways** | State only |
| Mixed sector (**6**) | **Coal, mineral oils, iron and steel, aircraft, shipbuilding, telephone/telegraph apparatus** | New units by State; existing private units 10 years, then review |
| Centrally regulated (18) | Salt, automobiles, heavy chemicals, fertilisers, cement, sugar, paper, textiles etc. | Private, under Central regulation |
| Residual | All others | Private and co-operative |
### 3. INDUSTRIAL POLICY RESOLUTION 1956
#### 3.1 Background
- Announced **30 April 1956** (placed before Parliament by **Nehru**); reviewed IPR 1948 after the First Plan.
- Goal: **"Socialist pattern of society"** – Congress **Avadi session, 1955**.
- Basis of the **Second Plan** (Mahalanobis model – heavy and basic industries).
#### 3.2 Schedules
| Schedule | Content | Role of State |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **17** basic/strategic industries (defence, heavy industry, minerals, transport, power); arms, atomic energy, railways, air transport = monopolies | Exclusive State |
| **B** | **12** industries (other minerals, road and sea transport, machine tools, ferro-alloys, basic chemicals, aluminium) | Progressively State-owned; private may supplement |
| **C** | All remaining | Private initiative, **under licensing** |
#### 3.3 Major Provisions
- **Licensing, expansion of public sector, small and cottage industries** (APPSC 2023: all correct); also **balanced regional development, labour welfare**.
- Licensing rested on the **IDR Act, 1951** → grew into the licence-permit raj; model lasted until 1991.
!NUM Schedule A = 17 | Schedule B = 12 | Schedule C = rest
### 4. LICENSING COMMITTEES AND MRTP
| Person / committee | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **R.K. Hazari report (1967)** | Industrial licensing; **big business houses cornered licences** |
| **Subimal Dutt** – Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee (1967, report **1969**) | Led to the **MRTP Act**; proposed the **joint sector** |
- **MRTP Act 1969:** prevent concentration of economic power and monopolistic practices; replaced by the **Competition Act, 2002** (Competition Commission of India).
- **Joint sector** (State + private partnership): set out by the **Dutt Committee (1969)**, adopted in the **Industrial Licensing Policy 1970** (non-core heavy-investment sector) and the 1973 joint-sector statement. IPR 1948 had created the "mixed sector".
!TRAP Joint sector is also linked in exam keys with **IPR 1956** (planned there); the formal joint-sector policy came from the Dutt report (1969-70).
- Licence raj criticised for **rent-seeking, entry barriers**, firms kept too small.
### 5. POLICY STATEMENTS 1973, 1977, 1980
#### 5.1 1973 Statement
- Identified a **core sector** list; tightened MRTP.
- **Reservation of items for SSI** began in **1967** and continued after 1991 (fully dereserved 2015).
#### 5.2 Industrial Policy Statement 1977
- **Janata Government**; drafted largely by **George Fernandes**.
- Emphasis: **small-scale, tiny and cottage industries**, khadi, handlooms, **employment** (defined "tiny" units).
- **District Industries Centres (DICs):** one-roof support (credit, raw material, machinery, marketing) in every district.
- Curbed large houses via **MRTP and FERA** → **IBM and Coca-Cola** left India.
- Small and cottage industries backed mainly because they generate **large employment**.
#### 5.3 Industrial Policy Statement 1980
- Congress government; built on **IPR 1956** with changes.
- Focus: **technological upgradation**, modernisation, **export orientation** (first strong emphasis).
- **Economic federalism:** tax holidays and cheap credit for **backward districts**.
- Removed the "artificial barrier" between large and small units; MRTP firms could expand capacity **25%** without approval; **SSI investment limits raised**; excess capacity regularised.
### 6. NEW INDUSTRIAL POLICY 1991 – BACKGROUND AND DELICENSING
#### 6.1 Background
- **24 July 1991**; **PM P.V. Narasimha Rao**, **FM Manmohan Singh**; after the **balance-of-payments crisis**.
- Start of **LPG**; ended the **licence-permit-quota raj** – licensing, expansion limits, price controls, import-export curbs removed.
- Liberalisation tools: **delicensing, import deregulation, lower import duty on capital goods** → cheaper technology and capital equipment, more competitive home manufacturing.
- Government role moved from **regulator → facilitator**.
#### 6.2 Compulsory Licensing
| Stage | Industries under licensing |
|---|---|
| July 1991 | **18** |
| 1993 | Automobiles, white goods, leather delicensed |
| 1996-97 | Electronic goods, then 5 more delicensed |
| Older list | 6 (incl. drugs and pharmaceuticals) |
| **Now** | **5** |
- **Present 5:** **alcohol, cigarettes/tobacco, electronic aerospace and defence equipment, industrial explosives, hazardous chemicals**.
- Drugs and pharmaceuticals later delicensed. Delicensed units file only an **Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM)**.
### 7. PUBLIC SECTOR RESERVATION AND PSU REFORM
| Stage | Reserved for public sector |
|---|---|
| IPR 1956 | **17** (Schedule A) |
| NIP 1991 | **8** – arms and ammunition, atomic energy, atomic minerals, coal and lignite, mineral oils, specified minerals, rail transport etc. |
| Later | **3** – arms and ammunition, atomic energy, rail transport |
| After **9 May 2001** (arms opened) / now | **2** – **atomic energy, railway operations** |
- NCERT public-sector-only list: defence equipment, atomic energy, railway transport (**never pharmaceuticals**).
- **1991 PSU measures:** up to **20% disinvestment** in selected PSUs (began **1991-92**); autonomy via **MoUs**; chronically sick PSUs to **BIFR** (under **SICA 1985**); National Renewal Fund (1992).
- **2016:** SICA repealed, BIFR dissolved → cases to the **IBC**.
!TRAP Today's two are NOT "atomic energy and defence". Reserved 17 → 8 → 3 → 2; licensing 18 → 6 → 5.
### 8. NIP 1991 – COMPETITION, FOREIGN INVESTMENT AND FINANCE
#### 8.1 MRTP and Competition
- **MRTP asset threshold scrapped** → no prior approval for expansion, mergers → **M&A rose**.
- MRTP replaced by **Competition Act, 2002**.
#### 8.2 Foreign Investment and Technology
- **Automatic approval up to 51% foreign equity** in a list of **high-priority, high-investment, high-technology** industries (capital goods, entertainment electronics, food processing) – NOT all industries (APPSC 2012).
- Automatic approval for **foreign technology agreements** in priority industries; foreign brand names allowed.
- **FIPB** set up to speed approvals; **abolished June 2017** → FIF portal → National Single Window System (Aug 2022).
- Equity in **SSI units** by other undertakings: up to **24%** (also foreign equity, 1995-96). SSI investment limit: **₹60 lakh**.
- Now: **100% automatic route** in most sectors (negative-list approach).
#### 8.3 Financial and Corporate Provisions
- **Mandatory convertibility clause** (FIs converting term loans into equity) **relaxed for term loans of less than 10 years** (APPSC 2025 key).
- Easier capital-market access; equity and technology norms eased.
!KEY 1991 = 51% automatic (priority list); SSI equity = 24%; now 100% automatic in most sectors.
### 9. IMPACT AND CRITICISM OF 1991 POLICY
#### 9.1 Impact
- **M&A** freed from MRTP pre-approval.
- Forex reserves record **USD 702.28 bn (Nov 2025)**.
- FDI: repatriation by foreign investors $178.9 bn (2022-23 to 2025-26) → net FDI falling.
- MSMEs hurt by **import competition** after protection was removed; SSI reservation continued after 1991.
#### 9.2 Criticism vs Non-Criticism
| Criticisms | NOT criticisms (achievements) |
|---|---|
| Unclear policy for weak industries | Higher growth |
| Threat of global competition | FDI and forex surge |
| Regional concentration (Maharashtra, Gujarat, TN, Karnataka) | **Increase in consumer choice** |
| Small units closed by cheap imports; jobless, capital-intensive growth | Global Indian MNCs |
- **"Neglect of agriculture"** is NOT a listed criticism.
- **SEZs:** discouraging foreign investment is NOT an objective.
