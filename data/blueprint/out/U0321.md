## U0321 | CENTRE-STATE LEGISLATIVE RELATIONS – ARTICLES 245-255, REPUGNANCY, PARLIAMENT ON THE STATE LIST
### 1. CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK – PART XI
- **Part XI** (Relations between the Union and the States) = **Articles 245-263** (APPSC-GS 2023).
- Distribution of powers regulated by **Articles 245 and 246**; borrowed from **Canada** (strong Centre) + a third **Concurrent List** (GoI Act 1935 tradition).
- Power to impose **taxes and fees** also flows from the Seventh Schedule via Art. 246.
| Articles | Subject |
|---|---|
| **245-255** (Chapter I) | **Legislative** relations |
| **256-263** | **Administrative** relations; **263** Inter-State Council |
| **268-281** | **Distribution of revenues**; **280** Finance Commission; **281** its recommendations |
| **301-307** (Part XIII) | Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse |
!TRAP Article 248 is in Part XI, not Part XII.

- **Art. 255:** an Act is **not invalid** for want of a required recommendation or previous sanction if the **Governor/President later assents**.
### 3. TERRITORIAL EXTENT – ARTICLE 245
| Legislature | Extent |
|---|---|
| **Parliament** | Whole or any part of India; **alone** can make **extra-territorial** laws |
| **State legislature** | **Own State** only; outside reach only with a real **territorial nexus** |
- **Art. 245(2):** no Parliament law is invalid for **extra-territorial operation** – this is **not a limit**.
- Parliament makes laws for the Union of India, the Territory of India, and **Indian subjects and their property anywhere** in the world.
- **Exceptions:**
  - **Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep:** **President's regulations** have the force of Acts of Parliament.
  - **Scheduled Areas:** the **Governor** may **bar or modify** Acts of Parliament.
  - Governor of **Assam** for Assam tribal areas.
!KEY Extra-territorial laws = Parliament only; a State needs territorial nexus.
### 4. SEVENTH SCHEDULE AND ARTICLE 246
#### 4.1 Article 246 Scheme
| Clause | Who | List |
|---|---|---|
| 246(1) | **Parliament** – exclusive | **List I (Union)** |
| 246(2) | **Both** – concurrent | **List III (Concurrent)** |
| 246(3) | **State** – exclusive, subject to (1) and (2) | **List II (State)** |
| 246(4) | **Parliament** for areas not in a State (UTs) | **All three Lists** |
- **Order of predominance:** **Union List > Concurrent List > State List**.
- **Art. 246A (GST)** breaks the strict list division: **simultaneous** power; **only Parliament** for **inter-State supply (IGST)**.

### 6. RESIDUARY POWERS – ARTICLE 248
- **Article 248** (**Part XI**) + **Entry 97, Union List**: subjects in **no list** – vested **exclusively in Parliament**.
- Examples: **space research**, **cyber law**, **residuary taxation** (APPSC-G2 2025: both statements correct).
| Country | Residuary power with |
|---|---|
| **India, Canada** | **Centre** |
| **USA, Australia** | **States** |
- **Sarkaria Commission (1988):** move all residuary powers **except taxation** to the **Concurrent List**.
!PAIR India | Canada | Centre || USA | Australia | States
### 7. PARLIAMENT ON THE STATE LIST – FIVE SITUATIONS
| Article | Trigger | Duration / effect |
|---|---|---|
| **249** | **Rajya Sabha** resolution, **2/3 of members present and voting**, **national interest** | Resolution up to **1 year**, renewable; law ceases **6 months** after it lapses |
| **250** | **National Emergency** (Art. 352) | Whole or any part of India; lapses **6 months** after Emergency ends |
| **252** | Resolutions of **two or more States** (not one) | Only consenting States; **only Parliament** amends or repeals |
| **253** | **International treaties, agreements, conventions** | Whole or part of India; **no State consent** |
| **356** | **President's Rule** | Law continues until the **State Legislature** alters, repeals or re-enacts it |
#### 7.1 Article 249 – Resolution
- Majority: **two-thirds of members present and voting** (APPSC-GS 2025); a **special power** of the **Rajya Sabha**.
- In force **not exceeding one year** (Art. 249(2)); renewable any number of times, **1 year at a time**.
- State **may still legislate**, but **Parliament's law prevails** in conflict (Art. 251).
#### 7.2 Article 250 – Emergency
- During a Proclamation of Emergency, **Parliament** (not a minister) legislates on State List matters for **any part of India** (APPSC-GS 2023).
- State power **not suspended**; Union law prevails on conflict.
!TRAP "Only consenting States" is Article 252, not 250.
#### 7.3 Article 252 – Consent of States
| Act under Article 252 | Year |
|---|---|
| Prize Competition Act | **1955** |
| Wild Life (Protection) Act | **1972** |
| Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act | **1974** |
| Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act | **1976** |
| Transplantation of Human Organs Act | **1994** |
- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** is **not** an Art. 252 law.
- A request by **one State** is invalid; requesting States lose the power to amend.
#### 7.4 Article 253 – International Agreements
- Parliament implements treaties for **whole or any part of India**, even on **State List** subjects, **without the consent of any State** (APPSC-GS 2018).
!PAIR Art. 252 | needs consent | two or more States || Art. 253 | no consent | treaties
### 8. LIMITS ON PARLIAMENT AND CENTRAL CONTROL OVER STATE LAWS
- **State List:** Parliament cannot legislate except under **Arts 249, 250, 252, 253, 356**.
- **Arts 301-303:** Parliament cannot **prefer or discriminate** between States in trade laws, except in **scarcity**.
- **State Bill** with **reasonable restrictions on inter-State trade** in public interest needs the **President's previous sanction** (Part XIII, Arts 301-305).
- **Art. 254(2):** Governor may **reserve** a State Bill for the President (an exception to aid and advice).
- **Nov 2025 SC advisory opinion:** **rejected deemed assent** and **fixed timelines** for Governors and the President.
### 9. REPUGNANCY – ARTICLE 254
1. Central vs State law on a **Concurrent** subject.
2. **254(1):** **Union law prevails**; State law **void to the extent of repugnancy**.
3. **254(2):** State law **reserved and assented by the President** prevails **only in that State**.
4. **Proviso:** Parliament can later **override** it by a fresh law – States become "practically powerless".
!TRAP Conflict on Concurrent subjects = Article 254 (not 250); Art. 251 covers laws under 249/250.
### 10. DOCTRINES OF INTERPRETATION
Most doctrines are **judicially evolved**, not written in the Constitution.
| Doctrine | Meaning | Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Pith and substance** | **True nature** of a law decides; **incidental encroachment** on another List is fine (Art. 246) | **Prafulla Kumar v Bank of Commerce (1947, Privy Council)**; **State of Bombay v F.N. Balsara (1951)** – prohibition law valid under State List; State of Rajasthan v G. Chawla (1959) |
| **Colourable legislation** | "**What cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly**" | **Kameshwar Singh v State of Bihar (1952)**; **K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v State of Orissa (1953)** |
| **Harmonious construction** | Overlapping entries **read together** so both operate | — |
| **Severability** | Only the **unconstitutional part** void, if separable (Art. 13) | A.K. Gopalan (1950); F.N. Balsara (1951) |
| **Eclipse** | Pre-Constitution law conflicting with an FR is **dormant**; revives if the right is amended | **Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v State of M.P. (1955)** |
| Basic Structure | Parliament cannot destroy basic features | Kesavananda Bharati (1973) |
| Judicial Review | Courts test **legislative acts and executive actions** (Arts 13, 32, 226) | Marbury v Madison (1803, USA) |
!KEY Pith and substance = doctrine for Union-State List encroachment.
