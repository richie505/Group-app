## U0659 | URBANISATION IN INDIA – TRENDS, CLASS-SIZE OF TOWNS, MILLION-PLUS CITIES, URBAN AGGLOMERATIONS
### 1. URBAN DEFINITIONS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Statutory town** | Any place with a **municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee**; notified by the State, run by a ULB |
| **Census town** | Place without urban status that meets the Census urban criteria (criteria set in **1981**) |
| **Urban agglomeration (UA)** | Continuous urban spread = **town + outgrowths**, or **two or more contiguous towns** |
| **Million-plus / metropolitan city** | **10 lakh+** people; **Art. 243P**: metropolitan area = 10 lakh or more |
| **Mega city** | **10 million+** |
| Rural | Any area that is not urban |
- 1981 Census-town criteria criticised for **male bias**, uniform thresholds, density on administrative boundaries (not satellite imagery); RGI keeps them for **Census 2027** (Sep 2026).
!TRAP The Census definition makes urbanisation look **slower**: peri-urban areas are missed.

#### 2.2 Three Phases
| Phase | Years | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow** | **1901–31** | ~0.35% a year; 33.46 million in 2,049 towns (1931) |
| **Medium** | **1931–61** | Towns 2,049 → 2,330; 78.94 million urban (1961) |
| **Rapid** | **1961 onwards** | **78.94 → 377.11 million** (+262%) |
- Indicators: number of towns, urban share, urban growth rate, average town size, urban density.
### 3. THE 2001–11 URBAN JUMP – COMPONENTS AND CAUSES
| Indicator | Figure |
|---|---|
| Urban population | **286 → 377.1 million** (**+31.8%** decadal growth) |
| Urban share | 27.81% → **31.16%** |
| Rural / urban 2011 | **68.84%** (833.1 million) / 31.16% |
| Absolute increase | **Urban 91.0 million > rural 90.4 million** |
| Census towns | **1,362 → 3,894** |
| Statutory towns | **3,799 → 4,041** |
!KEY FIRST time since Independence: absolute urban increase (~91 million) exceeded rural increase (2001–11).
- **Main reason** for the jump: **reclassification** – "categorisation of erstwhile rural areas as urban" (**APPSC G1 2017**).
- **Three components** of urban growth: **natural increase**, **net rural-urban migration**, **reclassification / new towns**.
- Migration is the main reason urban and rural growth differ.
!TRAP **31.16%** = 2011 urban share; **31.8%** = decadal urban growth (not 41.8%). Census towns = **3,894**, not 3,784.
#### 3.1 Causes of Urbanisation (APPSC 2008 – "all of the above")
- Growth of **industry and trade**; **pressure of population on rural land**; **low farm productivity**; services and transport.
#### 3.2 Hidden Urbanisation
| Source | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **World Bank (2010)** | **55.3%** in "urban-like" areas |
| **EAC-PM** | **~63% (2015)**, satellite data |
| Janaagraha ASICS 2023 | Census undercounts urbanisation |
- UN "**Degree of Urbanisation**" method would count peri-urban areas.
### 4. CLASS-SIZE CLASSIFICATION OF TOWNS
| Class | Population |
|---|---|
| **I** | **1,00,000 and above** |
| II | 50,000–99,999 |
| III | 20,000–49,999 |
| IV | 10,000–19,999 |
| V | 5,000–9,999 |
| **VI** | **below 5,000** |
- **"Top-heavy"**: Class I cities hold **~70%** of urban population (Economic Survey 2025-26).
- Of ~9,000 towns only ~500 are large cities; most have under 1 lakh people (Jan 2026).
### 5. MILLION-PLUS CITIES AND URBAN AGGLOMERATIONS
- **53** million-plus UAs/cities in **2011**, up from **35** in 2001.
| Rank (UA, 2011) | UA | Million |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Greater Mumbai** | **18.4** |
| 2 | **Delhi** | 16.3 |
| 3 | **Kolkata** | 14.1 |
| 4 | Chennai | 8.7 |
- Only the **top three are mega cities** (10 million+); Chennai is not.
- **Delhi overtook Kolkata** in 2011. **2001 order** (APPSC G1 2004): **Kolkata > Delhi > Ahmedabad > Bangalore > Kanpur** (decreasing population).
- Hyderabad city (GHMC) 2011: ~68 lakh.
- **AP million-plus UAs (2011)**: **Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada**.

### 9. 74TH AMENDMENT AND URBAN LOCAL BODIES
| Provision | Fact |
|---|---|
| **74th Amendment, 1992** | In force **1 June 1993**; **Part IXA** (Art. 243P–243ZG) + **Twelfth Schedule** (**18 functions** – urban planning, slum improvement, urban forestry, poverty alleviation) |
| **Art. 243ZE** | **Metropolitan Planning Committee** for every metropolitan area; **two-thirds** of members elected by elected municipal members and panchayat chairpersons |
| ULB types | **Nagar Panchayat** – area in transition; **Municipal Council** – smaller urban area; **Municipal Corporation** – larger urban area |
- **16th Finance Commission (2026-31)**: **₹3.6 lakh crore** for ULBs; urban share **36% → 45%**.
- ES 2025-26: cities "**administered, not governed**" – too many agencies (ULBs, parastatals), low own-source revenue; municipal bonds incentive ₹100 crore for issues above ₹1,000 crore.

### 11. PROBLEMS OF URBANISATION AND URBAN GOVERNANCE FORUMS
- **Slums: 6.54 crore** people (Census 2011); housing shortage, solid waste, water and sewerage, traffic, pollution, urban floods.
- **Peri-urban areas**: under neither rural nor urban water rules, yet **recharge zones** for cities.
- Cities: **~3% of land, ~60% of GDP**; ES 2025-26 (Ch. 15): "**critical economic infrastructure**", large but not liveable.
- Projection: urban population **~60 crore by 2031**; over 50% by 2050.
- **UN-Habitat World Cities Report 2026** – released at **World Urban Forum, Baku**: ~**40% (3.4 billion)** face housing shortage; **1.1 billion** in slums.
- **UN-Habitat** (**1978**, HQ **Nairobi**): MoU with AP for a **Centre of Excellence for Urban Affairs, Amaravati** (Jul 2026).
- **Right to the City**: concept of **Henri Lefebvre** (book **Le Droit à la Ville, 1968**) – city dwellers' right to shape urban space.
- **APMCHUD** (Asia-Pacific Ministerial Conference on Housing and Urban Development): set up **2006**, first meeting **New Delhi**; permanent secretariat hosted by **India**; 6th edition New Delhi, 2016.
