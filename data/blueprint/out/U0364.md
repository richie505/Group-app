## U0364 | RIGHT TO INFORMATION ACT 2005 – STRUCTURE, EXEMPTIONS, AMENDMENTS AND ISSUES
### 1. BACKGROUND AND CHRONOLOGY
| Order | Year | Landmark |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1766** | **Sweden** – Freedom of the Press Act: **world's first** RTI law |
| 2 | **1948** | **UN – UDHR Article 19**: right to seek and receive information (a human right) |
| – | **1990** | **MKSS** (Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan), Rajasthan – **Aruna Roy, Nikhil Dey, Shankar Singh** (not Medha Patkar); **jan sunwais** demanding muster rolls and bills started the RTI movement |
| – | **1997** | **Tamil Nadu**: **first Indian state** RTI law (APPSC 2023); Goa the same year |
| 3 | **2002** | **Freedom of Information Act 2002**: **never notified**; **repealed** by the RTI Act 2005 (APPSC 2023) |
| 4 | **2005** | **Right to Information Act, 2005** |
!TRAP Order (APPSC 2025): Sweden → UN → FOI Act → RTI Act. 1766 = world first; 1997 = Tamil Nadu (Indian state first).
### 2. ACT MILESTONES, PREAMBLE AND NATURE
#### 2.1 Commencement
| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| Lok Sabha passed | **11 May 2005** |
| Rajya Sabha passed | **12 May 2005** |
| Presidential assent | **15 June 2005** (A.P.J. Abdul Kalam) |
| Fully in force | **12 October 2005** (after **120 days**) |
- Some sections (**4(1), 5, 12, 13, 15, 16, 24**) came into force **at once**.
- **CIC** constituted **12 October 2005**; first CIC **Wajahat Habibullah**; covers all Central public authorities.
#### 2.2 Preamble and Objectives
- Preamble terms: **transparency of information, revelation of information, informed citizenry, paramountcy of the democratic ideal** (all four).
- Aim: informed citizenry and transparency to **contain corruption** and hold governments **accountable**, harmonised with confidentiality needs.
- Objectives: transparency, **answerability**, preventing corruption, participation; **NOT** "empowering women and weaker sections".
#### 2.3 Nature of the Right
- RTI is a **statutory (legal) right**; it mandates a **timely response** to citizens' requests.
- The **right to know** is read into **Article 19(1)(a)** (also Article 21) – **State of U.P. v. Raj Narain**, **S.P. Gupta v. Union of India**.
### 3. KEY SECTIONS – NUMBER GAME
| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| **2(f)** | **"Information"**: material in **any form** – records, documents, e-mails, opinions, advice, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, samples, models, electronic data; plus private-body information a public authority can access by law |
| **2(h)** | **"Public authority"**: bodies set up by the Constitution, a law or notification, and bodies **substantially financed or controlled** by government |
| **2(j)** | **"Right to information"**: **inspection** of work/records, **notes, extracts, certified copies**, **certified samples**, **electronic** information (not creating new opinions) |
| 3 | Every **citizen** has the right |
| **4** | **Proactive (suo motu) disclosure** (APPSC 2025); 4(1)(b) lists what must be published |
| 5 | **PIOs/APIOs** designated by each public authority (not President/Governor) |
| **6** | Request – post, in person or e-mail; **Hindi, English or local official language**; **6(2) no reason** need be given |
| **7** | Disposal: **30 days / 48 hours** |
| **8** | **Exemptions**; 8(2) public-interest override; 8(3) **20-year** rule |
| **9** | Rejection on **copyright** of a person other than the State |
| **11** | **Third-party** information |
| 12 / 15 | Constitute **CIC / SIC** |
| 18 | **Complaints** and inquiry |
| **19** | **Appeals** (two-tier); **19(8)(b)** compensation |
| **20** | **Penalties** on PIOs; burden of proof on PIO |
| 22 | **Overrides Official Secrets Act, 1923** |
| **24** | **Second Schedule** intelligence/security bodies excluded, except **corruption and human-rights** information |
| 25 | Annual report, laid before legislature |
- **BPL applicants**: information **free of fee**; only **citizens** can apply.
!TRAP 18 = complaints; 19 = appeals; 20 = penalties; 22 = overrides OSA; 25 = annual report.
### 4. TIME LIMITS
| Stage | Limit |
|---|---|
| Normal reply by PIO (Sec. 7) | **30 days** |
| **Life or liberty** | **48 hours** |
| Through **APIO** | **35 days** (30 + 5) |
| **Third-party** (Sec. 11) | **40 days** |
| Human-rights allegations vs **Section 24** bodies | **45 days** (with Commission approval) |
| **First appeal** | Within **30 days**; decided in 30, extendable to 45 |
| **Second appeal** to CIC/SIC | Within **90 days** |
!TRAP "Normal reply in 45 days" is the standard wrong pair (APPSC 2025 key).
### 5. EXEMPTIONS – SECTIONS 8, 9, 24
| Clause 8(1) | Exempt information |
|---|---|
| (c) | Breach of **privilege** of Parliament/Legislature |
| (d) | **Commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property** |
| (e) | **Fiduciary relationship** |
| (f) | Information from a **foreign government** in confidence |
| (i) | **Cabinet papers** |
| (j) | **Personal information** |
- **8(2)**: disclose if **public interest** outweighs harm, even if covered by the OSA.
- **8(3)**: exempt information disclosed after **20 years** (except cabinet deliberations, security, foreign relations, incitement).
- **Section 9**: copyright of a person other than the State.
- **Section 24 / Second Schedule**: **IB, R&AW, CRPF** etc. (CBI added 2011); **CIC is NOT exempt** (it is the appellate body).
- **Procedure followed in decision-making** by a public office is **not exempt**.
### 6. INFORMATION COMMISSIONS
| Item | CIC | SIC |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | **Chief IC + up to 10 ICs** | State Chief IC + up to 10 ICs |
| Appointing authority | **President** (Sec. 12) | **Governor** (Sec. 15) |
| Selection committee | **PM** (chair), **LoP in Lok Sabha**, Union Cabinet Minister nominated by PM | **CM** (chair), **LoP in Assembly**, Cabinet Minister nominated by CM |
| Removal | President, after **Supreme Court inquiry** | **Governor** (Sec. 17), after SC inquiry |
- **High Court Chief Justice is NOT** on the SIC committee (G2 2025 key).
- **Nature**: **statutory** bodies (not constitutional); **civil-court powers**; hear second appeals and complaints.
- **Eligibility**: eminence in public life – law, S&T, social service, management, journalism, mass media, administration; **cannot be MP/MLA** (a sitting MP or State Legislature member cannot be an IC), hold office of profit, be linked to a party, or carry on business.
- **Removal grounds**: proved misbehaviour/incapacity, **insolvency**, conviction involving **moral turpitude**, paid employment, infirmity – **not** disagreement with the State Government.
- **Term**: originally **5 years or 65**, no reappointment; since 2019 Centre notifies – **3 years** (RTI Rules 2019).
#### 6.1 CIC vs CVC
| Item | CIC | CVC |
|---|---|---|
| Members | Chief + up to 10 | CVC + up to **2** VCs |
| Term | 3 years (2019 Rules) | **4 years or 65** |
| Appointment | President | President |
### 7. PENALTIES AND COMPENSATION
- **Section 20**: penalty imposed by the **CIC/SIC** on the PIO – **₹250 per day**, **max ₹25,000**; plus possible **disciplinary action**.
- Grounds: refusing to receive an application, delay, knowingly giving incorrect or misleading information.
- PIO must be heard first; **burden of proof on the PIO**.
- **Compensation**: Commission can require the public authority to compensate the applicant (**Section 19(8)(b)**).
!NUM ₹250/day; cap ₹25,000 = Section 20; compensation = 19(8)(b)
### 8. AMENDMENTS – 2019 AND DPDP 2023
#### 8.1 RTI (Amendment) Act 2019
| Item | Original 2005 | After 2019 |
|---|---|---|
| Term of CIC/ICs | 5 years or 65 | Notified by Centre (**3 years**) |
| Salary (central) | CIC = **Chief Election Commissioner**; IC = Election Commissioner | Fixed by Centre |
| Salary (state) | State CIC = Election Commissioner; State IC = **Chief Secretary** | Fixed by Centre |
- Criticism: tenure and pay in **executive hands** weaken Commissions' **autonomy**.
#### 8.2 DPDP Act 2023, Section 44(3)
- Rewrote **8(1)(j)** into a **blanket exemption** for personal information; dropped the public-activity / privacy-invasion test and the **public-interest override**.
- Timeline: notified **Nov 2025** → Attorney-General said it does not dilute RTI (Jan 2026) → referred to a **Constitution Bench (Feb 2026)** → **7 Aug 2026** SC agreed to examine whether the "en bloc embargo" is repugnant to RTI.
!TRAP 2019 = tenure and salaries; DPDP 2023 Sec. 44(3) = 8(1)(j).
### 9. PUBLIC AUTHORITY – PARTIES, NGOs, COURTS
| Body | Status |
|---|---|
| **NGOs** substantially financed by government | Public authorities (DAV College Trust, 2019) |
| **Supreme Court / CJI's office** | Covered (2019) |
| **BCCI** | **Not** a public authority under 2(h)(d) – not substantially financed (CIC, May 2026) |
| **MPLADS** works | Covered; tracked on **eSAKSHI** portal |
#### 9.1 Political Parties
- The 2005 Act does **not name** political parties.
- **CIC, June 2013**: **six national parties** – **INC, BJP, CPI, CPI(M), NCP, BSP** (not AAP) – declared public authorities under 2(h), due to **indirect government funding** (land, bungalows, free airtime).
- Parties **did not comply**; matter pending before the Supreme Court; RTI (Amendment) Bill 2013 to exclude them **lapsed**.
- Why include them: check **corruption and crony capitalism**, illegal **foreign contributions**; they enjoy state privileges (free **Doordarshan** airtime).
!TRAP There is no 2018 SC verdict making parties disclose under RTI; follow the CIC 2013 order.
### 10. LANDMARK JUDGEMENTS
| Case | Held |
|---|---|
| **CBSE v Aditya Bandopadhyay (2011)** | Evaluated **answer sheets = information**; examinees can inspect; **not held in fiduciary capacity**; warned against indiscriminate demands |
| **Girish Ramchandra Deshpande v CIC (2012)** | Service record, disciplinary action, assets, **tax returns = personal information** under 8(1)(j) |
| **Namit Sharma v Union of India (2012)** | RTI Act case on Information Commissions |
| **CPIO, SC v Subhash Chandra Agarwal (2019)** | **CJI's office is a public authority** (Constitution Bench) |
| **Zee Telefilms v UoI (2005)** | BCCI does public functions but is **not "State"** under Art. 12 |
| **Madras HC (2014)** | Said applicant should state reasons; **withdrawn** – Sec. 6(2) needs no reason |
- **CIC (15 Jun 2026)**: CBSE must disclose answer-book tender/procurement data; fiduciary plea under 8(1)(e) rejected.
### 11. PROACTIVE DISCLOSURE AND ONLINE RTI
- **Section 4** mandates **suo motu** disclosure so citizens need not apply; 2nd ARC: maximum information in public domain, **narrow reading of Section 8**.
- **AP SIC** (Chief IC **Vajja Srinivasa Rao**): inspection of **258 offices in 18 districts** found **Section 4(1)(b)** violations (Jul 2026).
- **AP, 22 June 2026**: secretariat publishes Action Taken Reports on the **seven sector white papers**.
- **RTI Online portal** (rtionline.gov.in) of **DoPT**, launched **2013**: applications, first appeals, online fee for central public authorities.
- **AP**: **Nov 2023** – AP High Court directed the state to set up an **online RTI portal** (following the SC's Pravasi Legal Cell judgment); new **SIC website** (sic.ap.gov.in) launched **8 Sep 2025**.
### 12. ISSUES – ACHIEVEMENTS, MISUSE, PENDENCY, ACTIVISTS
| Misuse by applicants | Misuse by public authorities |
|---|---|
| Frivolous, vexatious or bulk applications; personal disputes, blackmail | Bad-faith use of Section 8; delays; incomplete or misleading replies; weak disclosure |
- **Achievements**: breaks the culture of secrecy; reduces corruption; raised **responsibility in bureaucracy** but "miles to go" (UPPSC 2010: both true, second does not explain first).
- APPSC 2019 A-R: Act enacted for transparency but **misused by both** – both A and R true.
- **Pendency**: over **4 lakh** appeals/complaints pending across commissions (Jul 2026); chronic **vacancies**. "Excessive speed" is NOT a criticism.
- **Satark Nagrik Sangathan 2025** report card: significant gaps as the Act turned 20.
- **Economic Survey 2025-26**: "re-examination" of RTI – exempt confidential service records and draft policy discussions; possible **ministerial veto**.
- **Activists**: **more than 100** killed (Jul 2026); **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014** not operationalised; 2nd ARC: link RTI with **whistle-blower protection**.
