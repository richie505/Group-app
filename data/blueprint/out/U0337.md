## U0337 | ELECTIONS – DELIMITATION, MODEL CODE OF CONDUCT, EVM-VVPAT, NOTA, ELECTION PETITIONS
### 1. ELECTORAL PROVISIONS IN THE CONSTITUTION
#### 1.1 Part XV – Articles 324-329
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| 324 | **Election Commission of India** – superintendence, direction and control of elections; 324(2): CEC plus other Commissioners as the President decides |
| 325 | **One general electoral roll** per territorial constituency; no exclusion on religion, race, caste or sex |
| 326 | **Adult suffrage** for Lok Sabha and State Assemblies |
| 327 / 328 | Parliament / State Legislature may make election laws |
| 329 | **Bar on courts**: delimitation laws cannot be questioned; an election can be challenged only by an **election petition** |
- Local polls: **State Election Commissions** (Arts. **243K**, **243ZA**) run panchayat and municipal polls, not the ECI.
- **Assembly elections** are conducted by the **ECI** (Art. 324), not the State Election Commission.
- **Article 171** = composition of Legislative Councils (supervising elections is Article 324).
- Qualifications/disqualifications: **Art. 84** and **102** (Parliament); **173** and **191** (State Legislatures).
- **Simultaneous elections** may need amending Articles **83, 85, 172, 174 and 356**.
- ECI constituted in **1950** – permanent, independent constitutional body; **IIIDEM** is its institute that trains election managers.
- **Mohinder Singh Gill v CEC (1978)**: Art. 324 is a "**reservoir of power**" where Parliament has not legislated.
- **State of Karnataka v Prathik Parasrampuria (2026)**: removing black money from elections is a core ECI duty.
### 2. RIGHT TO VOTE AND ELECTORAL SYSTEMS
#### 2.1 Universal Adult Franchise
- **Article 326**: every citizen aged **18+** may vote unless disqualified for **non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, corrupt or illegal practice**.
- Voting age **21 → 18**: **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988**.
- **70th Amendment (1992)**: elected MLAs of **Delhi and Puducherry** added to the Presidential electoral college.
- **Kuldip Nayar v Union of India (2006)**: right to vote and right to contest are **statutory rights**, not Fundamental Rights (RPA 1950 Sec. 19; RPA 1951).
- Lok Sabha candidate: any citizen whose name is on the electoral roll of **a** constituency, at least **25 years** old.
- **National Voters' Day: 25 January**.
#### 2.2 Who is Elected How
| Office / House | Article | Electorate | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| President | 54 | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi, Puducherry) | Indirect; PR-STV; secret ballot |
| Vice-President | 66 | **All MPs – elected and nominated** | Indirect; PR-STV |
| Lok Sabha | 81 | Citizens directly | **FPTP** |
| Rajya Sabha | 80 | Elected MLAs | PR-STV |
| Legislative Assembly | 170 | Citizens directly | FPTP |
| Legislative Council | 171 | Indirect categories + Governor's nominees | PR-STV |
| Panchayats/Municipalities | – | Local voters | Minimum age 21 |
- State Legislatures have **no role** in electing the Vice-President.
#### 2.3 First-Past-the-Post vs Proportional Representation
- **FPTP** = plurality: the candidate with the **most valid votes wins**; no 50% majority needed. Governments rest on a majority of **seats**, not votes.
- Example: **Karnataka 1994** – Janata Dal won **116 seats with 36% votes**.
- Reasons PR was rejected for Lok Sabha: difficulty for voters, fragmentation, **unstable governments**, expense, **no by-elections**, weaker voter-MP link.
- "Guarantees direct election in every single-member constituency" is **NOT** such a reason.
- PR is not needed where a two-party system has grown (e.g. UK); PR helps minorities in multi-party systems.
!NUM Security deposit is forfeited when a candidate gets **one-sixth or less** of valid votes.
### 3. TYPES OF ELECTIONS AND SPECIAL CONSTITUENCIES

### 5. DELIMITATION
#### 5.1 Meaning and Basis
- **Delimitation** = fixing the **number of seats and boundaries** of Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies (including SC/ST reserved seats) on recent census figures, for equal representation.
- Principle: "**one person, one vote, one value**" (APPSC-G1 2024); **Art. 81(2)** uniformity of representation.
- Basis: **Art. 82** (Lok Sabha), **Art. 170** (Assemblies) – readjustment after each census.
#### 5.2 Delimitation Commission
- Constituted by the **Central Government** under an **Act of Parliament** (Delimitation Act); independent body.
- Members (2002 Act): a retired/sitting **Supreme Court judge** (Chairperson), the **CEC** or an EC nominated by the CEC, and the **State Election Commissioner** of the State concerned – ex officio. **Associate members** have no vote.
- Fixes SC/ST reserved seats. Orders have the **force of law**, **cannot be questioned in any court** (Art. 329) and **cannot be modified** by the Lok Sabha or Assemblies – two separate safeguards.
| Act | Commission | Chair |
|---|---|---|
| 1952 | 1952 | Justice N. Chandrasekhara Aiyar |
| 1962 | 1963 | Justice J.L. Kapur |
| 1972 | 1973 | Justice J.L. Kapur |
| 2002 | 2002 (12 July 2002) | Justice Kuldip Singh |
| J&K | – | Justice Ranjana Desai (orders 2022) |
- **Four** Commissions constituted (till December 2023); J.L. Kapur chaired two.
#### 5.3 Freeze and Amendments
| Amendment | Effect |
|---|---|
| 42nd (1976) | Froze State seat shares on **1971 Census** till 2000 (population control) |
| 84th (2001) | Freeze till the **first census after 2026**; boundaries tidied on 1991 Census |
| 87th (2003) | Boundary redrawing on the **2001 Census**; total seats unchanged |
- **Lok Sabha: 543** elected seats; ceiling **550 (530 States + 20 UTs)**.
!TRAP "Maximum 552 including 2 Anglo-Indians" is outdated – Anglo-Indian seats ended in 2020.
#### 5.4 Latest Developments (2026)
- **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026**: Lok Sabha ceiling 550 → **850**, delimitation on 2011 Census; **defeated** in Lok Sabha (298 for, 230 against – short of special majority). Delimitation Bill 2026 withdrawn (April 2026).
- Southern States fear losing seats to **UP, Bihar, Rajasthan** under population-based delimitation (April 2026).
- **Tamil Nadu Assembly resolution (17 Aug 2026)**: permanent freeze at **543**, keep **2.2:1** Lok Sabha–Rajya Sabha ratio, women's quota from **2029** not tied to delimitation.
- **EAC-PM (28 Aug 2026)**: split 170 oversized seats to reach **824**.
#### 5.5 Andhra Pradesh Angle
- **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014, Section 26**: Assembly seats AP **175 → 225**, Telangana **119 → 153**, but **subject to Article 170** – so the rise waits for delimitation after the **first census after 2026**. Supreme Court refused to order it (**July 2025**).
- The AP Reorganisation Bill, 2014 made follow-up changes to **RPA 1950**.
### 6. RESERVATION OF SEATS
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| 330 / 332 | SC/ST seats in Lok Sabha / Assemblies in proportion to population |
| 331 | President nominated **2 Anglo-Indians** to Lok Sabha |
| 333 | Governor nominated **1 Anglo-Indian** per Assembly |
| 334 | Originally **10 years (till 1960)**; **95th Amendment** gave the last-but-one extension; **104th (2019, in force Jan 2020)** extended to **2030** and **ended Anglo-Indian nomination** |
| 334A | **Women one-third** – **106th Amendment (2023)**, for 15 years; rotated after each delimitation; starts after next census (2027) and delimitation |
- Reserved seats have a **common electorate** – all voters vote; **no separate electorate**.
- ST seats go where ST share is highest; SC seats are spread and rotated at each delimitation.

### 7. RPA 1950 VS RPA 1951 AND ELECTION PROCESS
| Basis | RPA 1950 | RPA 1951 |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Pre-election framework | Actual conduct of elections |
| Deals with | Allocation of seats, delimitation framework, voter qualifications, **electoral rolls**, Council composition (Art. 171(2), Sec. 10, Third Schedule) | Nomination, scrutiny, counting; candidate disqualifications; **party registration (Sec. 29A)**; **corrupt practices**; **election petitions** |
| Linked Articles | 81, 82, 170, 171, 325, 326 | 84, 102, 173, 191, 324, 329 |
| Key sections | 19 voter eligibility; 20A overseas electors; 21 roll revision; 20B observers | 8 disqualification; 29A; 77-78 expenses; 80-100 petitions; 126, 126A |
- **Sec. 8**: conviction with at least **2 years** → disqualified during imprisonment + **6 years** after release.
- **Commencement**: **Sec. 14** – **President** notifies Lok Sabha poll on ECI's recommendation; **Sec. 15** – **Governor** for Assembly. The **ECI fixes the schedule** and nomination dates but does **not** issue the notification.
- **Sec. 77-78**: expense account to the **DEO** within **30 days**.
### 8. ELECTORAL ROLLS AND ELECTION MACHINERY
- Four **qualifying dates**: **1 January, 1 April, 1 July, 1 October** (Election Laws (Amendment) Act 2021) – not 1 December.
- **Summary revision** = annual; **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** = rebuilds the whole roll. SC let the ECI's **Bihar SIR** proceed; it removed about **70 lakh** names, and turnout was the highest since Independence (CEC, 17 Aug 2026).
- **Overseas elector** (Sec. **20A**, RPA 1950, 2010): NRI enrols at the **Indian passport** address.
| Officer | Role |
|---|---|
| Chief Electoral Officer | Assists ECI in each State/UT |
| District Election Officer | Usually the **Collector**; receives expense accounts |
| Returning Officer | Conducts poll in a constituency, declares result |
| ERO / BLO | Electoral rolls; BLOs carry standard photo ID cards (2026) |
| Observers (Sec. 20B) | **General, police, expenditure** – not judicial |
- During elections the ECI controls officers on poll duty – can **transfer** them or act against biased conduct.
- **Polling measures 2026**: max **1,200 electors** per polling station; candidate booths just beyond **100 m**.
### 9. MODEL CODE OF CONDUCT
#### 9.1 Legal Status and Timeline
- **ECI guidelines** for parties, candidates and the ruling government – **not enacted by Parliament**, not statutory; authority: **Art. 324**.
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1960 | First drafted by **Kerala** government; used in the Kerala Assembly election |
| 1968 | Formalised by the ECI |
| 1974 | Revised |
| 1979 | **Part VII – "party in power"** added |
| 1991 | Strictly enforced by CEC **T.N. Seshan** |
- In force from the **announcement of the election schedule** (**Harbans Singh Jalal v UoI, 1997, P&H HC**) until results.
- Example: from **15 March 2026** for Assam, Kerala, TN, West Bengal, Puducherry; lifted **7 May 2026** except the Falta re-poll seat.
- Covers speeches, polling booths, processions, manifestos, general conduct.
#### 9.2 Part VII Curbs and Sanctions
- Ministers may **not combine official visits with campaigning**; no official machinery/transport; **no new schemes or grants**.
- Ordinary official visits without campaigning are **NOT** restricted.
- Sanction: censure up to **suspension/withdrawal of recognition** under **Para 16A, Election Symbols Order, 1968**.
- 2026: a petition alleged the PM's national broadcast during Assembly polls breached Part VII.
### 10. EVM-VVPAT, NOTA AND INDELIBLE INK
#### 10.1 EVM and VVPAT
- EVM = vote-recording and counting device – **not an electoral system**; used under ECI control for Lok Sabha and Assembly polls.
- **EVM first used: Parur (Kerala), 1982**; **VVPAT first: Noksen (Nagaland), 2013**.
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu v UoI (8 April 2019)**: VVPAT slips tallied in **5 polling stations per Assembly segment** (up from one).
- **ADR v ECI (26 April 2024)**: SC rejected **100% VVPAT** counting; second/third-placed candidates may seek checks of **5% of EVMs** per constituency by engineers, by written request within **7 days** of results, at their cost.
- EVM totalisers: SC hearing (2 Sep 2026).
#### 10.2 NOTA
- **PUCL v Union of India, 27 September 2013** – NOTA button on all EVMs; secret negative vote linked to **Art. 19(1)(a)**.
- First Lok Sabha use **2014** (1.1% of votes, over 6 million). Symbol (ballot with **black cross**) designed by **NID Ahmedabad**.
- **Symbolic only**: even if NOTA gets most votes, the top candidate wins – **no fresh poll**.
- Using NOTA = active voting, **not abstention**.
- **Not** for Rajya Sabha/Legislative Councils (struck down by SC, **2018**).
| Basis | NOTA | Old Rule 49-O |
|---|---|---|
| How | Button on EVM | Voter refused to vote before the Presiding Officer; noted in **Form 17-A** |
| Effect | Symbolic | Broke secrecy; replaced |
#### 10.3 Indelible Ink

#### 11.2 Postal Ballot and Home Voting
- Postal ballot: **service voters, special voters, electors on election duty, persons in preventive detention**.
- Absentee senior citizens: **85 years and above** (raised from 80).
#### 11.3 Exit Polls and Opinion Polls
- **Exit poll**: survey of voters just after leaving the polling station; **Sec. 126A** bars it from start of polling in the first phase till **half an hour after the last phase** closes.
- **Opinion poll**: survey before elections; **Sec. 126** bars it in the **48 hours** before close of poll.
#### 11.4 Party Recognition
- **Recognition**: **Election Symbols Order, 1968** (ECI order, not an Act). **Registration**: **Sec. 29A, RPA 1951** – separate.
| National party (any one) | State party (any one) |
|---|---|
| Recognised State party in **4 or more States** | 6% votes in Assembly election + 2 seats |
| **6%** votes in 4 States + **4 Lok Sabha seats** | 6% votes in Lok Sabha election + 1 LS seat |
| **2%** of Lok Sabha seats from at least **3 States** | **3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats**, whichever more |
| – | 1 LS seat for every 25 from the State |
- **Six** recognised national parties (2025): **BJP, INC, BSP, CPI(M), AAP, NPP**; TMC, NCP and CPI lost national status.
- ECI delisted **808** registered unrecognised parties for non-compliance.
- APPSC-G2 2025: all listed national-party conditions valid → "None of the above".
### 12. ELECTION PETITIONS
| Section (RPA 1951) | Content |
|---|---|
| 80 | **No election can be questioned except by an election petition** (base: Art. 329(b)) |
| 80A | Tried by the **High Court**; to try to decide within **6 months** |
| 81 | Filed within **45 days** of the returned candidate's election |
| 100 | **Grounds** for declaring an election void |
- Appeal: **Supreme Court**. President/VP disputes: **only the Supreme Court (Art. 71)**, not the ECI.
- **Grounds (Sec. 100)**: candidate not qualified/disqualified; **corrupt practice**; **improper rejection of nomination**; improper acceptance of votes or breach that affected the result.
- Offences (bribery, undue influence, personation): IPC Chapter IXA → from **1 July 2024 BNS Chapter IX (Sections 169-177)**.
!TRAP Bar on questioning = **Section 80**, NOT Section 81 (APPSC-G2 2025); Section 81 = 45-day limit.
