## U0307 | LEGISLATIVE PROCEDURE – ORDINARY, MONEY, FINANCIAL AND CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT BILLS; JOINT SITTING
### 1. BILLS – BASICS
- A **Bill** is proposed legislation; it becomes an **Act** only after the **President's assent** (**Article 111**).
- **Article 79**: Parliament = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**. The President is an integral part of Parliament but **not a member of either House**.
- Ordinary Bill: in **either House**. Money Bill: **only in the Lok Sabha**. Creation of a new **All-India Service** starts with a **Rajya Sabha** resolution (Art. 312).

### 2. GOVERNMENT BILL VS PRIVATE MEMBER'S BILL
| Feature | Government Bill | Private Member's Bill |
|---|---|---|
| Introduced by | A **minister** | Any MP who is **not a minister** (not necessarily nominated) |
| Notice | **7 days** | **1 month** |
| Drafted by | Concerned department with the **Law Ministry** | The member |
| Defeat | Signals **loss of confidence** | **No bearing** on the government |
- Lok Sabha: time is allotted by the **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions**; it classifies them and examines private members' constitutional amendment bills before introduction.
- Rajya Sabha has **no such committee**; its **Business Advisory Committee** allots time.
!NUM Only **14** Private Members' Bills have become law; the last in **1970**.
### 3. ORDINARY BILL – STAGES AND PRESIDENT'S ASSENT
#### 3.1 Three Readings in Each House
| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **First reading** | Leave to introduce; introduction; title and objectives read; published in the **Gazette** |
| **Second reading** | **General discussion** → committee stage (select/joint committee) → consideration stage: **clause-by-clause** voting on amendments |
| **Third reading** | Bill accepted or rejected **as a whole** |
!KEY APPSC 2018: **General discussion** on a Bill takes place at the **Second Reading** – not the First or Third.
#### 3.2 Second House
- Same three stages. It may **pass, amend, reject** or **keep it pending for 6 months** → deadlock → **joint sitting**.
- For an ordinary Bill the second House has **no time limit**; for a Money Bill the Rajya Sabha has **14 days**.
#### 3.3 President's Options – Article 111
| Bill | Assent | Withhold | Return for reconsideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary Bill | Yes | Yes | Yes (once) |
| Money Bill | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| Financial Bills I and II | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Constitutional Amendment Bill | **Must assent** (24th CAA, 1971) | No | No |
- **Suspensive veto**: if a returned Bill is re-passed, with or without amendment, the President **must assent**.
- **Pocket veto**: no time limit is fixed → possible. India has **no qualified veto**.
- A Money Bill is normally assented to, because it is introduced on the President's recommendation.
### 4. MONEY BILL – ARTICLES 109 AND 110
#### 4.1 Definition – Art. 110(1)
A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** these matters:
1. Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**.
2. Regulation of **borrowing** by the Government of India.
3. Custody of the **Consolidated Fund** or **Contingency Fund**, and money into or out of them.
4. **Appropriation** of money out of the Consolidated Fund of India (CFI).
5. Declaring any expenditure **charged** on the CFI.
6. Receipt, custody or audit of money of the CFI or **Public Account**.
7. Matters incidental to the above.
#### 4.2 NOT Money Bills – Art. 110(2)
- Bills only for **fines or penalties**.
- **Fees for licences** or fees for services.
- **Taxes by local bodies** for local purposes.
#### 4.3 Procedure
- Introduced **only in the Lok Sabha** (Art. 109(1)), **only by a minister**, **only on the President's recommendation** (APPSC 2019).
- **Speaker certifies** it; the decision is **final** (Art. 110(3)); no need to consult anyone. The certificate is endorsed when sent to the Rajya Sabha (Art. 109) and to the President (Art. 111).
- **Rajya Sabha**: only **recommends within 14 days**; **cannot amend or reject**. Lok Sabha is **not bound** to accept the recommendations (Art. 109(2)). Not returned in 14 days → **deemed passed** by both Houses.
- **No joint sitting**; President **cannot return** it.
- **Appropriation Bill** is a Money Bill: Rajya Sabha need not pass it; it is treated as passed if not returned in 14 days.
- **AP angle:** the **AP Legislative Council** also only recommends on a Money Bill within **14 days**; it cannot reject or amend it finally. **Article 200**: a Governor **cannot return a Money Bill** for reconsideration.
#### 4.4 Aadhaar Cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **K.S. Puttaswamy** | **2018** | Upheld the **Aadhaar Act** passed as a Money Bill |
| **Rojer Mathew** | **2019** | Money Bill question referred to a larger **7-judge bench** |

### 5. MONEY BILL VS FINANCIAL BILLS VS FINANCE BILL
#### 5.1 Article 117 – Three Categories of Financial Bills
- **Money Bills** (Art. 110), **Financial Bill (I)** (Art. 117(1)), **Financial Bill (II)** (Art. 117(3)).
| Feature | Money Bill (Art. 110) | Financial Bill I (Art. 117(1)) | Financial Bill II (Art. 117(3)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art. 110 matters | **Only** | Some, plus general matters | **None** – only spending from CFI |
| Speaker's certificate | Required | Not required | Not required |
| House of introduction | Lok Sabha only | Lok Sabha only | **Either House** |
| Who introduces | Minister only | Minister only | Minister or private member |
| President's recommendation | At introduction | At introduction | At **consideration** stage only |
| Rajya Sabha | Recommend only (14 days) | **Amend or reject** | **Amend or reject** |
| Joint sitting | **Not allowed** | Allowed | Allowed |
| President can return | No | Yes | Yes |
- In FB-I: an amendment **reducing or abolishing** a tax needs **no** recommendation; one **increasing** a tax needs it.
- **Finance Bill**: gives effect to the **Budget's tax proposals**; usually certified as a Money Bill.
- "No Bill imposing tax without the President's recommendation" – **Article 117**.
!KEY Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, but **not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill**.
!TRAP Not all financial bills start only in the Lok Sabha: **FB-II can start in either House**. A Bill only involving expenditure can start in either House.
!PAIR Money Bill | Art. 110 | RS cannot amend/reject || Financial Bill I | Art. 117(1) | RS can amend/reject
### 6. BILLS BY HOUSE OF INTRODUCTION AND PRIOR RECOMMENDATION
- **Cannot originate in the Rajya Sabha**: **Money Bills** and **Financial Bills (I)**.
| Bill | Article | President's prior recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| New States, alter boundaries/names | **Art. 3** | Required (either House) |
| Money Bills and FB-I | **Art. 117(1)** / 110 | Required |
| FB-II / expenditure from CFI | **Art. 117(3)** | Before consideration |
| Taxation in which **States are interested** | **Art. 274** | Required |
- State level: a Bill restricting **inter-state trade** needs the President's previous sanction before introduction in a State legislature.
### 7. BUDGET – ARTICLES 112–117
- **Annual Financial Statement** = **Article 112**; prepared by the **Budget Division** of the **Department of Economic Affairs**, Ministry of Finance.
- **Rajya Sabha** can **discuss** the Annual Financial Statement but **cannot vote** on the **Demands for Grants** (Art. 113); voting is only in the Lok Sabha.
- **Appropriation Bill** (Art. 114) – no withdrawal from CFI without it.
- **Vote on Account** (Art. 116) – grant in advance for part of the year until the budget is passed.
- **FIRST Union budget** of independent India: **26 November 1947**, by **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty**.
#### 7.1 Cut Motions (only in Lok Sabha)
| Motion | Reduction | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy cut** | Demand reduced to **Re 1** | Disapproval of the policy |
| **Economy cut** | By a **specified amount** | Economy in spending |
| **Token cut** | By **Rs 100** | A specific **grievance** |
- **Guillotine**: on the last day allotted, all remaining demands are put to vote **without discussion**.
### 8. JOINT SITTING – ARTICLE 108
#### 8.1 Grounds (deadlock after one House passes a Bill)
1. The other House **rejects** it.
2. Houses **finally disagree** on amendments.
3. More than **six months** pass without it being passed (prorogation and adjournments of over **4 consecutive days** are excluded).
#### 8.2 Procedure
- **Summoned by the President**, who **"notifies his intention"** to summon it; then neither House proceeds further with the Bill.
- If the President **does not summon** it → the **Bill comes to an end** (APPSC G2 2017).
- **Presides**: Lok Sabha **Speaker** (Art. 118(4)) → Deputy Speaker → **Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman** → member chosen by those present.
- **Quorum**: **one-tenth** of total membership; decided by **simple majority** of members of both Houses **present and voting** together.
- **Excluded**: **Money Bills** and **Constitution Amendment Bills** (Art. 368). Applies to **ordinary bills**; **Financial Bills I and II** can go.
!TRAP The Vice-President / Rajya Sabha **Chairman does NOT preside** over a joint sitting.
#### 8.3 Joint Sittings Held (only three)
| Year | Bill |
|---|---|
| **1961** | **Dowry Prohibition** Bill |
| **1978** | **Banking Service Commission (Repeal)** Bill |
| **2002** | **Prevention of Terrorism** Bill (POTA) |
!NUM 1961 – 1978 – 2002 = Dowry – Banking Service Commission – POTA
### 9. CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT BILL VS ORDINARY BILL
| Feature | Ordinary Bill | Constitutional Amendment Bill (Art. 368) |
|---|---|---|
| House | Either House | Either House |
| Introduced by | Minister or private member | Minister or private member |
| President's prior recommendation | Not needed (except listed cases) | **Not needed** |
| Majority | Simple majority | **Special majority** – majority of total membership + **two-thirds** of members present and voting, in **each House separately** |
| State ratification | No | Half the States for federal provisions |
| Deadlock | **Joint sitting** possible | **No joint sitting** |
| President | Assent / withhold / return | **Must give assent** |
- **Latest:** **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024** (One Nation One Election, new **Article 82A**) – still with the **JPC**, not passed; opposition argues the government lacks a two-thirds majority (Aug 2026).
### 10. LAPSING OF BILLS – DISSOLUTION, PROROGATION, ADJOURNMENT
#### 10.1 On Dissolution of Lok Sabha
| Lapses | Does not lapse |
|---|---|
| Bill **pending in Lok Sabha** (originated there or sent by RS) | Bill pending in **Rajya Sabha**, not yet passed by LS |
| Bill **passed by LS, pending in RS** | Bill for which a **joint sitting** was notified before dissolution |
| Bill passed by RS, pending in LS | Bill passed by both Houses, **pending assent** |
| — | Bill **returned** by the President for reconsideration |
- **Rajya Sabha is never dissolved**.
- **No joint sitting** can be summoned for a Bill that has **already lapsed**; a joint sitting **already summoned** is held as scheduled even after dissolution.
!TRAP APPSC G1 2024: "A Bill passed by LS but not taken up in RS does not lapse" = **FALSE**.
#### 10.2 Effect of Other Events
| Event | Bills | Motions / notices |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment / adjournment sine die** | Do **not lapse** – taken up next session (APPSC 2025) | Continue |
| **Prorogation** | Do **not lapse** | Pending notices lapse, except notices for introducing Bills |
| **Dissolution** | As in 10.1 | **All pending** Lok Sabha business lapses |
