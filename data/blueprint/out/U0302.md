## U0302 | CABINET SYSTEM, CABINET COMMITTEES, COLLECTIVE AND INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBILITY; ATTORNEY GENERAL OF INDIA
### 1. PARLIAMENTARY VS PRESIDENTIAL FORM
| Parliamentary (India) | Presidential (USA) |
|---|---|
| **Nominal** (President) and **real** (PM) executives | President = head of State and government |
| Majority party rule | Fixed-term executive |
| **Collective responsibility** to Lok Sabha | No responsibility to legislature |
| Ministers are MPs (**double membership**) | Secretaries not members of Congress |
| PM's leadership; **LS can be dissolved** | No dissolution |
| Political homogeneity; secrecy | **Separation of powers** |
- Also: **cabinet/responsible government**, **Westminster model** (Britain, Japan, Canada, India).
- **Defining feature**: **collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha** – not separation of powers or judicial review.
- **Necessary features**: majority party rule + collective responsibility + PM as centre of power (complete separation of powers = presidential).
- **Why chosen**: experience under the **GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935** and to avoid a **personality cult**.
- **Underlying principle** of Cabinet form: a mechanism of parliamentary democracy ensuring **collective responsibility of the Government to the people**.
### 2. COUNCIL OF MINISTERS VS CABINET
| Council of Ministers | Cabinet |
|---|---|
| Wider body, 60–70 ministers of all ranks | Nucleus – Cabinet Ministers only |
| Constitutional – **Arts 74–75** | Word only in **Art 352(3)**; rest by convention |
| Rarely meets as a body | Meets regularly; takes policy decisions |
| Collectively responsible to LS | Directs and supervises the Council |
- Word **"Cabinet"** inserted in **Article 352(3)** by the **44th Amendment, 1978** – Emergency only on the **written advice of the Union Cabinet**; the only place it appears.
### 3. COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY – ARTICLE 75(3)
- Council collectively responsible **only to the Lok Sabha** – not RS, Parliament as a whole or the President. States: **Article 164(2)** – to the Legislative Assembly.
- **"Sink or swim together"**: whole Council incl. PM resigns if a **no-confidence motion** passes (moved **only in LS**) or it loses majority.
- **Budget** (treated as a **Money Bill**) defeated in LS → PM submits the Council's resignation.
- RS ministers also answer to the LS; **defeat in the Rajya Sabha** does not affect survival.
- **PM's death or resignation** dissolves the whole Council.
- Cabinet decisions **bind all**; a dissenting minister must defend them or **resign**.
- **Oath of secrecy** (Third Schedule, given by the President) keeps Cabinet talks secret → public unity.
### 4. INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBILITY – ARTICLE 75(2)
- Ministers hold office during the **President's pleasure**; each answers to Parliament for lapses in his portfolio.
- In practice the **PM** can ask a minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him.
- **Personal statement**: a resigned minister may explain his resignation in the House with the **Speaker's consent**.
!PAIR Article 75(3) | Collective | To Lok Sabha || Article 75(2) | Individual | President's pleasure

### 6. CABINET COMMITTEES
- **Extra-constitutional**; set up by the PM under the **Transaction of Business Rules, 1961**.
- **Eight** committees (April 2025):
| Committee | Chair | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Appointments (ACC)** | PM | Senior appointments – Cabinet Secretary, Solicitor General |
| **Security (CCS)** | PM | National security, defence, nuclear doctrine |
| **Political Affairs (CCPA)** | PM | "**Super cabinet**"; decided **caste enumeration in the Census** (30 Apr 2025) – not CCEA |
| **Economic Affairs (CCEA)** | PM | Economic and infrastructure projects |
| **Parliamentary Affairs** | **Rajnath Singh** (2024) | Government business, sessions |
| **Accommodation** | **Amit Shah** (2024) | Government accommodation |
| **Investment and Growth** | – | Set up **2019** |
| **Employment and Skill Development** | – | Set up **2019** |
- **AP angle (Jul 2026)**: CCEA approved **3rd and 4th lines** on **Ballari–Guntakal**, **Rs 1,264 crore**.
!KEY Not PM-chaired: Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation.
### 7. CABINET SECRETARIAT AND CABINET SECRETARY
- **Cabinet Secretariat** works **directly under the PM**; serves Cabinet and committees; administers the Business Rules.
- **Cabinet Secretary**: **senior-most civil servant**; "first among equals" among Secretaries; **11th** in the Table of Precedence; chairs the Civil Services Board; heads the **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC)**.
- Appointed by the **ACC** for **2 years**, extendable up to **4**.
- **6 Aug 2026**: **T.V. Somanathan** given a one-year extension beyond 30 Aug 2026; Home Secretary **Govind Mohan** extended up to 22.8.2027.
- State equivalent: **Chief Secretary** – every file goes to the Cabinet through him; **G. Sai Prasad** took charge as **AP Chief Secretary** (Feb 2026).
### 8. ATTORNEY GENERAL – ARTICLE 76
- **Highest law officer**; appointed by the **President**; must be **qualified to be a Supreme Court judge**; remuneration as the **President determines** (Art 76(4)).
- Advises GoI on legal matters; does duties referred by the **President** (not a Governor).
- **No fixed term**; holds office at the **President's pleasure**; by convention resigns when the government changes.
- **Audience in all courts**.
- **Article 88**: speaks in either House, joint sittings and committees (can be named a member) – **cannot vote**. **Article 105(4)**: MPs' privileges.
- **Limits**: not in the Cabinet; not a full-time servant – **private practice allowed**, but cannot advise or brief **against GoI**, defend accused in criminal cases or become a company director without permission.
- First AG: **M.C. Setalvad**. **R. Venkataramani** – AG since 1 Oct 2022 (after K.K. Venugopal); re-appointed for 2 years from **1 Oct 2025**.
!TRAP AG's right in Parliament = **Article 88**, not 78.
### 9. AG VS ADVOCATE GENERAL VS SOLICITOR GENERAL
| Basis | Attorney General | Advocate General | Solicitor General |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basis | **Art 76** | **Art 165** | **Non-constitutional** (statutory) |
| Serves | Union | State | Union – assists AG |
| Appointed by | President | Governor | **ACC** |
| Tenure | President's pleasure | Governor's pleasure | **3 years** |
| House rights | **Art 88** | **Art 177** (both Houses, committees, no vote) | None |
| Rank | First law officer | – | Second law officer |
| First | M.C. Setalvad | – | **C.K. Daphtary** |
- **Additional SGs** also non-constitutional.
### 10. CABINET IN PRACTICE – KITCHEN CABINET, GoMs, DOMINANCE
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Kitchen cabinet** – PM's informal inner circle of trusted colleagues and friends (ministers and non-ministers); e.g. Indira Gandhi's advisers | Criticised as an **extra-constitutional power centre** bypassing the Cabinet |
| **GoMs/EGoMs** – ad hoc groups reporting to Cabinet | Delay; dilute collective responsibility |
| **Single-party majority** | "**Cabinet dictatorship**" over Parliament |
| **Coalition era (1989–2014)** | Coordination committees and allies' demands weakened cohesion |
- **Shadow Cabinet** – UK only; none in India.
