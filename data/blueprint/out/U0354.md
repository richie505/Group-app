### 2. COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME AND NES
#### 2.1 Grow More Food Inquiry Committee (1952)
- Chairman: **V.T. Krishnamachari** (APPSC G2 2008); distractors – Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao.
- Found the campaign too narrow → **integrated village development**; suggested a **nationwide extension organisation** → CDP and NES.
#### 2.2 CDP (1952)
- Launched **2 October 1952** (Gandhi Jayanti) by **Nehru**, in the **First Five Year Plan** (1951-56); began with **55 community projects** (pilot).
- **First major rural development programme** after Independence; called "**mother of social development programmes**".
- **Aim**: **development of villages through public participation** (APPSC 2018); a step towards **Article 40**.
- Components: agriculture, irrigation, communications, health, housing, **social education** (adult literacy, community centres, youth and women's clubs).
- AP: Andhra State Plan (1953, capital **Kurnool**) carried CDP under the First Plan.
!KEY APPSC 2019: **Social education** was a component of the **CDP** – NOT of NAEP, IRDP or the National Literacy Mission.
#### 2.3 National Extension Service (1953) and Organisation
- **NES (1953)**: lighter, extension-staff version that spread the CDP pattern nationwide; **NES followed CDP** (BPSC 2022).
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Block** | Unit of planning and development (**about 100 villages**) |
| **Block Development Officer** | Heads the block team of extension officers |
| **Village Level Worker (Gram Sevak)** | **Multipurpose extension worker** for a group of villages |
- Failure: seen as **government-imposed**, run top-down; **poor people's participation** → Balwant Rai Mehta review.

### 3. BALWANT RAI MEHTA COMMITTEE (1957)
#### 3.1 Appointment and Report
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Appointed | **16 January 1957**, Government of India (Team for the Study of Community Projects and NES) |
| Chairman | **Balwantrai G. Mehta** |
| Reviewed | **CDP (1952) and NES (1953)** – not IRDP |
| Report | **24 November 1957** |
| Accepted | **NDC, January 1958** – States free to vary the pattern; basic principles common |
- Finding: **public participation is indispensable**; needs full cooperation of the **local public**.
- Remedy: **democratic decentralisation** – term **first used** by this committee (APPSC 2019); "later came to be known as Panchayati Raj".
- **Balwant Rai Mehta = "Architect / Father of Panchayati Raj"**.
#### 3.2 Recommendations
| Tier | Level | Election | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gram Panchayat** | Village | **Direct** | Local body |
| **Panchayat Samiti** | Block | **Indirect** | **Executive body**; block = basic unit of planning |
| **Zila Parishad** | District | **Indirect** | **Advisory, coordinating, supervisory** |
- Tiers "**organically linked**" through indirect elections.
- **District Collector = chairman** of the Zila Parishad.
- **Genuine transfer of power** and **adequate resources**; all planning and development work entrusted to these bodies.
!TRAP APPSC 2019: BRM did NOT recommend a **two-tier** system, **direct election to the Zila Parishad**, or the ZP as **executive**. **Nyaya Panchayat is not a tier** (UPPSC 2024).
- Under the **73rd Amendment**, Panchayat Samiti members are **directly elected** (UP: **Kshetra Panchayat**) – unlike BRM's indirect model.

#### 5.3 Nyaya Panchayats

- **Ashok Mehta**: separate from development panchayats, presided by a **qualified judge**; **Singhvi**: for **clusters of villages**.
### 6. ASHOK MEHTA COMMITTEE (1977-78)
#### 6.1 Appointment
- Appointed **December 1977** by the **Janata government** of **PM Morarji Desai** (APPSC G2 2008); report **August 1978**, **132 recommendations**.
#### 6.2 Recommendations
- **Two-tier**: **Zila Parishad** (district, **executive**, district planning) + **Mandal Panchayat** (group of villages, **15,000-20,000** people).
- **Term: 4 years** (APPSC G2 2023, 2025: "two tier system with four years of term").
- **District = first point of decentralisation** below the State.
- **Official participation of political parties**; elections by the State's Chief Electoral Officer with the CEC; superseded body → **elections within 6 months**.
- **Compulsory taxation powers**; **social audit** by a district agency and a committee of legislators.
- **Constitutional recognition**; **Minister for Panchayati Raj** in the State cabinet; SC/ST reservation by population; separate **Nyaya Panchayats**.
#### 6.3 Fate and Legacy
- Janata government fell before finishing its term → **no national action**; two-tier model **never adopted nationally**.
- **Sixth Plan (Janata, 1978-83)** gave PRIs new life; dropped after 1980, never an official Plan (APPSC G2 2017).
- Post-1977 panchayats = "**Second Generation Panchayats**"; **West Bengal** (Panchayat Act 1973; polls June 1978), **Karnataka** and **AP** adopted its ideas.
#### 6.4 Comparison
| Point | Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Ashok Mehta (1978) |
|---|---|---|
| Tiers | 3 | 2 |
| Unit | **Block** (Samiti executive) | **District** (ZP executive) |
| Elections | Direct at village, indirect above | Party participation at all levels |
| Collector | ZP chairman | Reduced role |
| Constitutional status | Not proposed | Recommended |
!TRAP UPPSC 2023: "three-tier replaced by two-tier" is correct; the block statement is false – Ashok Mehta made the **district** the unit.

### 8. L.M. SINGHVI, THUNGON AND GADGIL COMMITTEES
#### 8.1 L.M. Singhvi (1986)
- Appointed by the **Rajiv Gandhi government**; report "**Revitalisation of Panchayati Raj Institutions for Democracy and Development**" (APPSC 2018, G2 2008).
- **Constitutional recognition** in a new chapter; regular free and fair elections; viable gram panchayats with more funds; **Gram Sabha = "embodiment of direct democracy"**; nyaya panchayats for clusters of villages.
- APPSC 2018 key: Singhvi **first suggested constitutional status** (Ashok Mehta had also urged it).
!TRAP Sarkaria, Rajamannar and G.V.K. Rao are distractors for the "Revitalisation" report.
#### 8.2 Thungon and Gadgil (1988)
| Committee | Body | Recommendations |
|---|---|---|
| **P.K. Thungon** | Sub-committee of Parliament's Consultative Committee | Constitutional recognition; 3 tiers; ZP pivot; **5-year term**; supersession max 6 months; **list of PR subjects** (→ **Eleventh Schedule**); SC/ST and women reservation; State Finance Commission; **Collector as ZP CEO** |
| **Gadgil** | **Congress Party** Committee on Policy and Programmes | Constitutional status; 3 tiers; **direct elections at all levels**; reservations; SFC and SEC; **model for the 64th Bill** |
#### 8.3 Rao vs Singhvi
| Point | G.V.K. Rao (1985) | L.M. Singhvi (1986) |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Administrative set-up for rural development | Revitalising PRIs for democracy |

| Big idea | DDC as ZP CEO | Constitutional recognition |

### 10. ROAD TO CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS (1989-1993)
| Step | Fact |
|---|---|
| **64th Amendment Bill** | **Rajiv Gandhi** government, Lok Sabha, **July 1989**; first attempt at constitutional status; based on Singhvi, Thungon, Gadgil |
| Fate | **Passed Lok Sabha**, **defeated in Rajya Sabha (October 1989)** for lack of special majority |
| **65th Amendment Bill** | **Nagarpalika** Bill for municipalities; also defeated in the Rajya Sabha (1989) |
| **National Front (V.P. Singh) bill, 1990** | **Lapsed** – Lok Sabha dissolved |
| **Narasimha Rao bills** | Modified bills, **September 1991** → **Joint Parliamentary Committee** → passed **December 1992** |
| **73rd Amendment** | In force **24 April 1993** (not 15 August 1993); **Part IX (Art. 243-243O)**, **Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)**; 74th = Municipalities |
- Opposition objection: **Local Government is a State List** subject – Centre would deal with local bodies over States' heads and **centralise power**.
- 64th Bill = legislative origin of the 73rd Amendment (BPSC 2020).
- Key Articles: **243B(2)** – middle tier may be skipped below **20 lakh** population; **243D(3)** – at least **one-third** seats for **women**; **243G** – powers of Panchayats; **243J** – audit; **243K** – **State Election Commission**. Regular remuneration to members is **not** a provision.
- PRIs made **justiciable**; constitutional obligation on States; women now **over 49.7%** of elected representatives, **19 States/UTs** reserve 50% (23 Apr 2026).
- **National Panchayati Raj Day: 24 April** – 2025 (32nd anniversary) main event at **Lohna Uttar GP, Madhubani**; 17th edition held 24 April 2026.
### 11. ANDHRA PRADESH PANCHAYATI RAJ
| Law / reform | Provision |
|---|---|
| **AP Panchayat Samithis and Zilla Parishads Act, 1959** | Began the **three-tier** structure (1 Nov 1959) |
| **AP Gram Panchayats Act, 1964** | Consolidated village-panchayat law |
| **Mandal system, 1985** (NTR) | Ended hereditary **Patel-Patwari** system |
| **Mandal Praja Parishads Act, 1986** | **Mandal Praja Parishads replaced block Panchayat Samithis** (Ashok Mehta idea) |
| **AP Panchayat Raj Act, 1994** | Act 13 of 1994 (21 April 1994); implements the 73rd Amendment; 278 sections; Gram Panchayat – Mandal Parishad – Zilla Parishad |
- Intermediate tier in AP = **Mandal Praja Parishad** (1999 list: Assam – ZP at sub-division; Meghalaya – Janajati Parishad; Mizoram – no Gram Panchayat).
- **2026**: the 13 Zilla Praja Parishads are reorganised into **28** (one per district) after terms ended 23-24 Sep 2026; MPTC/ZPTC polls on **5 November 2026**; SEC **Anil Chandra Punetha** ordered photo electoral rolls.
- **NIRDPR** (national rural development institute) is in **Hyderabad**.
