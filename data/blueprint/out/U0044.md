## U0044 | SHAKAS, PARTHIANS AND WESTERN KSHATRAPAS – RUDRADAMAN I, JUNAGADH INSCRIPTION, SAKA ERA
### 1. SEQUENCE OF FOREIGN INVADERS
| Order | Invaders | Period |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Indo-Greeks | 2nd c. BC |
| 2 | **Sakas** | 1st c. BC; first prominent king **Maues (Moga)**, c. 80 BC |
| 3 | **Parthians (Pahlavas)** | 1st c. CE |
| 4 | **Kushanas** | 1st c. CE (Kushana period 1st-3rd c. CE, after Mauryas, Shungas, Kanvas) |
- Another PYQ order: **Greeks (326 BC, Alexander) → Sakas (1st c. BC) → Kushans (1st c. AD)**.
!TRAP Alexander's Greeks = 326 BC; Indo-Greek rule in India = 2nd c. BC. In either order Greeks come first, Kushanas last.
### 2. SHAKA BRANCHES AND REGIONS
| Branch | Region | Rulers |
|---|---|---|
| North-west | **Taxila** | Liaka Kusulaka, **Patika** (Taxila copper plate) |
| Mathura | **Mathura** | **Rajuvula, Sodasa** |
| **Kardamaka** | Kathiawar, Malwa (**Ujjain**) | **Chashtana, Rudradaman I** |
| **Kshaharata** | Western / Upper Deccan (**Nasik**, Junnar, Karle) | **Bhumaka, Nahapana** |
- Saka centres: **Mathura, Ujjain, Girnar**; Sakas were often at war with the **Satavahanas**.
- Ruled as **Satrapas and Mahasatrapas** (satrap system); absorbed into society as **Vratya Kshatriyas**.
!KEY Kardamaka = Ujjain (Chashtana, Rudradaman); Kshaharata = Nasik (Bhumaka, Nahapana).
### 3. WESTERN KSHATRAPAS – RULERS AND CONFLICTS
| Ruler | Contribution / conflict |
|---|---|
| **Nahapana** (Kshaharata) | Defeated by **Gautamiputra Satakarni**, who **re-stamped** his silver coins |
| **Chashtana** (Kardamaka) | Clash with **Vasishthiputra Pulumavi** recorded by **Ptolemy** |
| **Rudradaman I** | Ruled c. **130-150 CE** from **Ujjain**; **twice defeated Satakarni**, lord of Dakshinapatha, but **spared** him as a close relative; also conquered the **Yaudheyas** |
| **Rudradamanika** | Rudradaman's daughter; married **Vasishthiputra Satakarni** |
- **Jogalthembi hoard**: thousands of Nahapana's silver coins re-stamped by Gautamiputra Satakarni.
- **Nasik Prashasti**: issued by **Gautami Balashri**, mother of Gautamiputra; calls him destroyer of **Shakas, Yavanas and Pahlavas**.
- **AP angle**: after Rudradaman I beat Pulumavi, the Satavahana centre moved from **Pratishthana (Paithan)** to **Amaravati**.
!PAIR Records: Naganika | Nanaghat || Ushavadatta | Nasik and Karle || Rudradamanika | Rudradaman's daughter
### 4. KSHAHARATA INSCRIPTIONS
| Inscription | Issuer | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Nasik** caves | **Ushavadatta (Rishabhadatta)**, Nahapana's **son-in-law** | Deposited **2,000 karshapanas** with the **weavers' guild at Govardhana** at **12%** a year |
| **Karle** | Ushavadatta | Gift of a village to monks of the Karle chaitya |
| **Junnar (Manmodi)** | **Ayama**, Nahapana's minister (**amatya**) | Gift of a cave/mandapa, dated **year 46** |
- **Kolika** = weavers; interest on guild deposits was generally 12%.
!TRAP Govardhana weavers' deposit = **Nasik** inscription whether the question names Rishabhadatta or Ushavadatta. Junnar = **Ayama**, not Ushavadatta.
### 5. JUNAGADH (GIRNAR) INSCRIPTION OF RUDRADAMAN I
- **First long inscription in Sanskrit**; earlier long records were in **Prakrit**.
- Dated **Saka 72 = 150 CE**.
- Records Rudradaman's victories over Satakarni and the Yaudheyas and the repair of **Sudarshana lake**.
#### 5.1 Sudarshana Lake – Who Did What
| Work | By |
|---|---|
| Built | **Pushyagupta** (Vaishya), governor of **Chandragupta Maurya** |
| Canals added (under **Ashoka**) | **Tushaspha** (Yavanaraja) |
| Repaired (Rudradaman I) | Pahlava minister **Suvishakha** |
| Repaired again (Gupta period, **Skandagupta**) | **Chakrapalita**, son of governor Parnadatta |
!KEY "2nd-century Sanskrit rock inscription on Sudarshana repair" = Rudradaman I.
### 6. PARTHIANS (PAHLAVAS)
- **Gondophares**: greatest Parthian ruler; **Takht-i-Bahi inscription** (near **Peshawar**).
- Christian tradition: **St Thomas** visited Gondophares' court.
### 7. CONTRIBUTIONS – COINAGE, DRESS, MILITARY
- **Coinage**: many **silver** coins in western India (not only gold); **Kardamaka silver coins carry Saka-era dates**; Chashtana issued silver and copper (copper relatively rare).
- **Gold-silver ratio 1:35**: 1 suvarna = 35 karshapanas (Nasik record).
- **Dress**: Shakas and Kushanas introduced **trousers, long coats, helmets, boots**.
- **Military**: Shakas, Parthians, Kushanas had **better cavalry** (not elephants).
- **Epigraphy**: Sanskrit enters long royal records with Junagadh.
### 8. ERAS
| Era | Began | Founder / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Kali** | **3102 BC** | Traditional |
| **Vikrama Samvat** | **57 BC** (often written 58 BC) | **Vikramaditya** of Ujjain; also **Krit Samvat, Malav Samvat**; linked with victory over Sakas |
| **Saka** | **78 AD** | Exam key: **Kanishka**; also equated with **Chashtana's** accession; "Shalivahana era" legend links it to Gautamiputra Satakarni |
| **Kalachuri / Chedi** | **248 AD** | Kalachuris |
| **Gupta** | **319-320 AD** | **Chandragupta I** (dynasty founded by **Srigupta**) |
| **Harsha** | **606 AD** | Harshavardhana's accession |
| Lakshmana Sena | 1119 AD | Bengal |
- Era of Muslim rule in India: **1192 AD**.
!TRAP "58 CE" for Vikram Samvat is a slip: it began in BC, not CE. Gupta era may appear as 319 or 320 AD.
#### 8.1 Converting Dates
- **AD = Saka + 78** (not 77); Saka = AD − 78. Saka 556 = 634 AD; **2009 AD = Saka 1931**.
- **Vikrama = Saka + 135**; CE = Vikrama − 57.
- **Badami rock inscription of Pulakesin I**: Saka 465 = Vikrama **601** (465 + 78 + 58).
### 9. SAKA ERA AS NATIONAL CALENDAR
- Adopted as India's **national calendar from 22 March 1957**.
- First month **Chaitra**; **1 Chaitra = 22 March** (**21 March** in a leap year).

### 12. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. Junagadh = first long **Sanskrit** inscription, not Prakrit.
2. Sudarshana built by **Pushyagupta**, canals by **Tushaspha**, repaired by **Suvishakha** and later **Chakrapalita**.
3. Junnar = **Ayama**; Nasik and Karle = **Ushavadatta**.
4. Nahapana is **Kshaharata**; Chashtana and Rudradaman are **Kardamaka**.
5. AD = Saka + **78**, not 77; Vikrama = Saka + **135**.
6. Vikram Samvat began **57 BC**, not 58 CE.
7. Nasik Prashasti was issued by **Gautami Balashri**, not Gautamiputra himself.
8. Takht-i-Bahi = **Gondophares** (Parthian), not a Saka ruler.
9. Foreign rulers had better **cavalry**, not elephants.
10. 1 Chaitra = **22 March**; 21 March only in a leap year.
