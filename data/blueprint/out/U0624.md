### 2. PHYSIOGRAPHIC DIVISIONS
- **Six divisions** (relief and structure): **Himalayas, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, Islands**.
| Division | Key features |
|---|---|
| Himalayas | Young fold mountains; separate the subcontinent from the rest of Asia; world's highest range |
| Northern Plains | Alluvial; Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar, Khadar; **youngest** division |
| Peninsular Plateau | Ancient stable crystalline landmass; **largest and oldest** (from Gondwana Land); rich in metallic minerals |
| Indian Desert | West of the Aravallis; Thar, dunes, Rann |
| Coastal Plains | Narrow in west, broad in east |
| Islands | A&N (tectonic/volcanic), Lakshadweep (coral) |
- Types of plains: **alluvial** (Indo-Gangetic), **flood**, **deltaic** (Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta – world's major delta), **coastal**, **loess** (wind-blown silt, China), **outwash** (glacial meltwater).
- **Chambal**: ravines and badlands (gully erosion). **Sahara** is not in the subcontinent.
- **Paleomagnetic** results show the Indian landmass moved **northward**.
### 3. NORTHERN PLAINS
#### 3.1 Belts from Hills to Plains
| Belt | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Bhabar** | Coarse gravel and pebbles at the foot of the Shiwaliks; **streams sink** underground |
| **Terai** | Marshy, waterlogged, forested belt where Bhabar streams **re-emerge** |
| **Bhangar** | **Older** alluvium above flood level; **kankar** nodules |
| **Khadar** | **Newer** alluvium of floodplains, renewed yearly |
!TRAP Order hills → plains: Bhabar → Terai → Bhangar/Khadar. Bhangar = older, Khadar = newer.
#### 3.2 Regions
- Area **6,52,000 sq km**; 2,400 km long (W–E); 70–500 km wide; northern edge = **Himalayan Frontal Fault**.
- Gradient: land drops only ~**200 m** over **1,600 km** from Delhi to the Bay.
- **Punjab Plain**: formed by **Indus tributaries**; has **doabs**.
- **Ganga Plain**: ~3,75,000 sq km; alluvium 2,000–3,000 m thick. Divisions: **Ganga–Yamuna Doab** (upper >200 m, middle 100–200 m, lower <100 m), **Rohilkhand, Avadh, Bihar, Bengal** plains – **not Malwa** (a plateau).
- **Brahmaputra Plain**: Assam valley; river enters Bangladesh.
- **Kosi Plain** (north Bihar): huge alluvial fan / **megafan** with shifting channels; Kosi = "**Sorrow of Bihar**".
- **Molassis Basin**: **Mizoram** (Purvanchal); soft loose deposits; common language Lushai.
### 4. INDIAN DESERT AND THE RANN OF KUTCH
- **Thar**: west of the **Aravallis**; sandy rolling plain; rainfall **below 150 mm**.
- **Marusthali** = true desert (west); **Bagar** = semi-arid strip (east) along the Aravallis.
- Features: **barchan and seif** dunes, salt lakes (**Sambhar** saline), desertification spreading north.
- Order of features (APPSC 2025): arid climate → **sand dunes** → **thorny bushes** → **seasonal streams** only with rain.
- Inland drainage: Luni, Machhu, Rupen, Saraswati, Banas, Ghaggar – end in salt lakes or are lost in sand.
- **Luni**: only significant river; rises in the **Aravalli** (south-west of Ajmer); **511 km**; seasonal; lost in the **Rann of Kutch** – does not reach the sea.
#### 4.1 Rann of Kutch
- **Seasonal salt marsh** flooded in the monsoon; a lowland; **Great Rann** (north) and **Little Rann** (south); Kachchh (Gujarat) and Sindh (Pakistan).
- Formation: once a **shallow arm of the Arabian Sea** / Kutch an island amid seas and lagoons; repeated falls in sea level and aridity made it a salt plain – best example of such marshland.
- **Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary**; rich in solar energy. **Banni grasslands** (edge of the salt marsh) – Gujarat.
- **Agaria / Agariya**: traditional salt-pan workers of the Little Rann.
### 5. PENINSULAR PLATEAU
#### 5.1 Structure
- **Gondwana shield** (Precambrian); triangular block of igneous and metamorphic rocks; about **3,600 million years** old.
- **Tilts east** → most rivers flow east.
- Coal formed in the **Carboniferous** (Damodar, Son, Mahanadi, Godavari basins).
- **S.P. Chatterjee**: Peninsula in **8** parts – North Central Highlands, South Central Highlands, Eastern Plateau, Meghalaya Plateau, North Deccan (has Mahanadi Basin), South Deccan, Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats.
- **Horst**: Satpura (between Narmada and Tapi); Aravalli = Archaean horst.
#### 5.2 Sub-divisions
| Sub-division | Location / rock | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Central Highlands** | **North of the Narmada**; Aravalli, Malwa, Vindhya | 800–1,200 m; slope SW → NE; Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand eastern extensions |
| **Malwa Plateau** | Western MP, SE Rajasthan; Deccan Trap basalt, black soils | Between **Aravalli (north)** and **Vindhya (south)**; **Bundelkhand** on its east; ~1,50,000 sq km |
| **Deccan Plateau** | **South of the Narmada**; ~7 lakh sq km | Mainly **basalt** (APPSC 2025); Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, AP, TN |
| **Chota Nagpur** | Mainly **Jharkhand**; also Odisha, WB, Bihar, Chhattisgarh | ~86,239 sq km; "a front sloping" plateau, series of levels; **storehouse of minerals**, "**Ruhr of India**"; Patland plateaus Ranchi, Hazaribagh, Palamu; Hundru Falls |
| **Meghalaya Plateau** | Garo, Khasi, Jaintia hills | Detached extension, separated by the **Malda Gap** (fault between Rajmahal hills and Meghalaya); Bhuban Hills (Barak Valley, Assam) are NOT part |
| **Dandakaranya** | Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, Maharashtra, AP | ~**89,078 sq km**; 480 km E–W, 320 km N–S |

- **Chota Nagpur**: drained by **Damodar** (from Khamarpat Hill), **Koel, Subarnarekha**; coal share 80% when asked, **68.39% (2024-25)**.
- **Damodar does not rise at Amarkantak**.
- Dandakaranya keys: Chhattisgarh and Odisha, and Andhra Pradesh.
#### 5.3 Deccan Traps
- **Fissure eruptions** of basaltic lava, end-**Cretaceous** to early Eocene; 'Trap' = **Swedish for step**.
- Area once ~**10 lakh sq km**, now ~**5 lakh sq km** (Kuchchh, Saurashtra, Maharashtra, Malwa, N. Karnataka).
- Thickness **3,000 m in the west → 150 m in the east**.
- Weathers into **regur** (black cotton soil).
#### 5.4 Vindhya, Satpura and Central Ranges
- **Vindhya**: north of the Narmada; **Mukandara Hills** = extension in SE Rajasthan.
- **Satpura** ("Seven Folds"): between **Narmada and Tapi**; southern boundary of the Narmada valley; eastern Gujarat through Maharashtra–MP border to Chhattisgarh; **Tapti lies south** of it. Highest **Dhupgarh (1,350 m)**, Pachmarhi, **Mahadeo** hills.
- **Narmada Valley**: between Vindhya and Satpura.
- Vindhya and Satpura converge at **Amarkantak** (Maikal range) – not Yercaud (Shevaroy) or Sahyadri.
- **Maikal Range**: eastern end of the Satpura; MP–Chhattisgarh; Ramgiri hills part of it.
- West → east: **Satpura, Mahadeo, Maikal, Chotanagpur**; Barwani, Mahadeo, Maikal; Mahadeo, Maikal, Chotanagpur, Khasi.
- North → south: **Mahadeo Hills, Satpura Range, Sahyadri**.
- **Kaimur**: MP, UP, Bihar. **Dalma Hills**: Jharkhand. **Ajanta Range**: only Maharashtra (Aravalli, Sahyadri, Satpura span several States).

#### 8.2 Emergent vs Submergent
| Emergent | Submergent |
|---|---|
| Land rose / sea fell | Land sank / sea rose |
| Wide plains, bars, **lagoons**, **deltas** | Narrow coast, **estuaries**, rias |
| **East coast**: broad, dry | **West coast**: narrow, wetter; estuaries suit ports (Malabar = compound) |
- Coasts broke from Gondwana in the **Cretaceous–early Tertiary**; coastline is straight due to **faulting**.

### 11. HIMALAYAN RANGES, VALLEYS AND TERMS
- **2,414 km**, Nanga Parbat to Namcha Barwa; ~5 lakh sq km; 200–500 km wide.
- Structural zones N → S: **Indus-Tsangpo Suture, Main Central Thrust, Main Boundary Thrust, Himalayan Frontal Fault**.
| Range | Features |
|---|---|
| Trans-Himalaya | Karakoram, Ladakh, Zaskar – north of Himadri |
| **Himachal (Lesser)** | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat; valleys **Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu**, Lahul-Spiti (APPSC 2025); Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Darjeeling |
| **Shiwalik (Outer)** | Youngest, narrowest; folded river deposits; **duns** |
- **Duns**: long valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks – **Dehra Dun (largest)**, Patli Dun (Uttarakhand), Kotli Dun, **Nalagarh Dun (HP)**.
- **Karewas (Vudr)**: lacustrine gravel-and-mud terraces of the Kashmir Valley; Tertiary; **saffron** (largest output in Kashmir; also Spain, Iran, Greece, Italy). Kashmir valley formed in a **synclinorium**.
- Regional divisions: Punjab **Indus–Sutlej**; Kumaon **Sutlej–Kali**; Nepal **Kali–Tista**; Assam **Tista–Dihang**. Namcha Barwa in the Arunachal Himalaya; Valley of Flowers in the Himachal.
- Valleys: **Tura** – Meghalaya (not HP); **Lahaul, Parvati, Kullu** – HP; **Ghepan Lake** (glacial) – Lahaul.
- Glaciers: **Siachen** – largest/longest in India (Karakoram, Ladakh); **Gangotri** – Uttarakhand; **Zemu** – Sikkim.
- Cho Oyu, Lhotse are Himalayan; Annamalai, Sirumali are not.
