## U0176 | LAND REVENUE SYSTEMS – PERMANENT SETTLEMENT, RYOTWARI AND MAHALWARI; AREAS AND EFFECTS
### 1. THE THREE SYSTEMS – ORIGINATOR, YEAR, AREAS
| System | Introduced / devised by | Year | Areas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Permanent Settlement** (Zamindari) | **Lord Cornwallis**, planned with **John Shore** | **1793** | **Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Varanasi**; Northern Circars (Andhra, **1802**) – about **19%** |
| **Ryotwari** | **Alexander Read** and **Thomas Munro** | **Baramahal 1792**; Madras Presidency **1820** | **Madras, Bombay** (extended by **Mountstuart Elphinstone**), Assam, Coorg, Berar, East Punjab – about **51%** |
| **Mahalwari** | **Holt Mackenzie** (Regulation VII of **1822**); revised **1833** under **Bentinck** by **R.M. Bird** | 1822 (revision 1833) | **North-Western Provinces** (Agra-Oudh), parts of **Punjab**, Gangetic valley, Central India – about **30%** |
- Madras and Coorg = **Ryotwari**, not Permanent Settlement; Bombay = mainly Ryotwari; Ryotwari also common in **West India**.
- Different land systems in different parts of India created **different classes** in society.
!TRAP Permanent Settlement "introduced in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in **1795**" = incorrect; year is **1793**. "Planned by John Shore" = correct (APPSC G2 2025).
!TRAP Mahalwari: **Mackenzie 1822 = proposal**; Bird/Bentinck 1833 = revision.
### 2. COMPARISON OF THE THREE SYSTEMS
| Basis | Permanent | Ryotwari | Mahalwari |
|---|---|---|---|
| Settled with | **Zamindar** | Individual **ryot** | **Village / mahal** |
| Owner | Zamindar (proprietor) | Ryot (proprietor) | Village community jointly |
| Revenue | **Fixed forever** | **Periodically revised** (about 30 years) | Periodically revised |
| Collector | Zamindar | Individual farmer | Headman (**lambardar**) |
| On default | Estate auctioned (**Sunset Law**) | Cultivator's land seized/auctioned | Village **collectively liable** |
| Share of British area | 19% | 51% | 30% |
!PAIR Permanent Settlement | Bengal | Cornwallis || Ryotwari | Madras | Sir Thomas Munro
### 3. PERMANENT SETTLEMENT – FEATURES AND RATIONALE
- Also called the **Zamindari system**; zamindars recognised as **proprietors**; rights **hereditary, transferable, saleable**.
- Began as a **decennial (10-year) settlement**, made **permanent in 1793**.
- **Rent share:** State **10/11**, zamindar **1/11** of rent collected.
- **Sunset Law:** revenue due by **sunset of the due date**, or the estate was auctioned.
- **Reasons for fixed, high demand:** sure income, low collection cost, a **loyal landlord class**, hope of land improvement. It did **not** let the Company gain from later price rises.
- Many zamindars **did not issue pattas**; no official check on them – they only paid 10/11 of collections.
- **Gains from agriculture went to the zamindars**, not the Company or the cultivators (APPSC 2022).
- **Litigation:** more litigation after 1793 was traced to Cornwallis's **judicial reforms**, especially **abolition of court fees**; zamindars sued tenants.
- **Haftam (1799)** and **Panjam (1812)** regulations let zamindars **seize tenants' property**.
- **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885:** rights of landlords and tenants in **Bengal and Bihar**.
### 4. RYOTWARI – FEATURES
- Ryot = **proprietor**; paid revenue **directly to Government**; pattas given; lands **surveyed and assessed** before taxing.
- Could **sell, mortgage, gift or lease** land while assessment was paid; not evicted while revenue paid.
- First demand: **50% of estimated produce** (not 30%).
- Assessment fixed on **land, not crop**; revised about every **30 years** (two separate features).
- **No automatic exemption** after a bad harvest; occasional remission or reduction possible.
- Munro: "The Ryotwari is a system which has **always prevailed in India**."
- Criticism: State became the **"ultimate landlord"**; over-assessment, poor records, unclear boundaries; revenue often heavy, not fixed low.
### 5. RYOTWARI – FIRST AREAS AND EARLY EXPERIMENTS
| First | Fact |
|---|---|
| First in **Madras Presidency** | **Baramahal (1792)**, by Captain **Alexander Read**, assisted by **Munro** |
| Baramahal area | **Salem, Krishnagiri, Dharmapuri, Tiruvannamalai**; acquired after the **Third Mysore War (1792)** |
| First in **Andhra** | **Rayalaseema (Ceded Districts)**, Munro, **1802** |
| General Madras adoption | **1820**, Munro as Governor |
| Bombay | Mountstuart Elphinstone |
- After Munro left (**1808**), **village-lease settlements** (headmen as lessees) replaced ryotwari in the Ceded Districts until ryotwari was restored about **1820**.

### 6. THOMAS MUNRO
| Years | Post |
|---|---|
| 1780 | Arrived in Madras as a military cadet (born 27 May 1761, Glasgow) |
| 1792–99 | Assistant to Read in **Baramahal** |
| **1800–07** | **First Principal Collector of the Ceded Districts**, HQ **Anantapur** |
| 1814–18 | President of the **Judicial Commission** |
| **1820–27** | **Governor of Madras**; Ryotwari across Madras except permanently settled areas – **"Father of Ryotwari"** |
- **Palegars:** crushed the Rayalaseema palegars with officer **Campbell**; abolished the auction system; Ryotwari **1802**; field survey 1802–07.
- **Why Rayalaseema:** no strong zamindars; he distrusted palegars and big intermediaries.
- **Philosophy:** **direct contact with cultivators**, no intermediaries; spoke **fluent Telugu**, worked without interpreters; fond of Rayalaseema.
- Died of **cholera at Pattikonda** (Kurnool), **6 July 1827**; buried at **Gooty**.
!KEY Munro = Ceded Districts (Rayalaseema); Read = Baramahal; Elphinstone = Bombay.
### 7. MAHALWARI AND TALUQDARI
- **Unit:** the **mahal** (village or estate) – the whole village as one unit; designed by Mackenzie for the **Bengal Presidency** (NWP).
- Revenue fixed for the village, collected through the **headman / village community**; each cultivator liable for his share; periodically revised.
- Common ownership: **"Bhai Chara"** or **"Mahali"**.
| Official | Role |
|---|---|
| **Holt Mackenzie** | Designed the system, 1822 |
| **R.M. Bird** | Revised it (Regulation IX of 1833); survey-based; **"father of land settlement in north India"** |
| **James Thomason** | Carried on Bird's settlement in the NWP (Lt.-Governor) |
!TRAP **Taluqdari = Awadh (Oudh)**; "Taluqdari – Bombay" is the wrong pair.

| Region | System |
|---|---|
| Rayalaseema (Ceded Districts) | **Ryotwari, 1802** |
| Northern Circars (Coastal Andhra) | Mainly **zamindari**; **Haveli lands** later Ryotwari |

#### 8.1 Andhra Revenue Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Haveli** | Land under direct Company control |
| **Peshkash** | Fixed payment by zamindars (Vizianagaram, Bobbili, Pithapuram) |
| **Mootahs** | Haveli lands grouped and auctioned to **mootahdars** after 1802; held as proprietary estates; many fell into arrears |
| **Moturpha** | Tax on **professions, trades and looms** (weavers, artisans, merchants) |
| **Inam** | Rent-free title |
### 9. EFFECTS AND ECONOMIC IMPACT
#### 9.1 Zamindari Areas
- Old estates **sold for arrears** → new landed class of **urban merchants and moneylenders**.
- **Absentee landlordism** (zamindars moved to Calcutta, left agents), **sub-infeudation, rack-renting**; peasants became **tenants-at-will**.
- No investment in land; cultivation rose after 1800 but fixed demand gave the Company nothing extra.
#### 9.2 Ryotwari and Mahalwari Areas
- **Rigid cash revenue** even when crops failed → ryots borrowed from **Mahajans**, mortgaged or lost land; **land alienation**.
- No famine remission, unlike Mughal **zabt** (one-third share, taccavi loans); Mahalwari's collective liability broke village solidarity.
- American Civil War cotton slump → defaults → land passed to moneylenders.
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Commercialisation | Cash revenue forced **indigo, cotton, opium, jute** |
| Land | Became a **saleable/transferable commodity** |
| New classes | **Merchant-landlords** (Zamindari areas); **moneylender-landlords** (Ryotwari and Mahalwari areas) |
| Result | Loyal landlord class + **rural indebtedness** → peasant revolts |
- **Alice Thorner:** Ryotwari **destroyed the village system** and separated farmers from the government.
- **15 Jul 2026:** AP Cabinet reclassified the residential part of **Pulladigunta (Guntur)**, long held as "Thopu Poramboke"; **201 families** got ownership.
