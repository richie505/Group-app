## U0170 | ESTABLISHMENT OF BRITISH RULE IN ANDHRA – ADMINISTRATIVE CONSOLIDATION AND ITS EFFECTS
### 1. DISTRICT REORGANISATION IN THE MADRAS PRESIDENCY
- Andhra was ruled as part of the **Madras Presidency**; the district was the unit of rule.
#### 1.1 Year-wise changes
| Year | District change |
|---|---|
| **1802** | **Rajahmundry + Kakinada** collectorates united as **Rajahmundry** district |
| **1819** | **Ganjam Collector** also made **Agent to the Governor** for the Agency tracts |
| **1859** (after Crown rule) | **Rajahmundry → Godavari**, **Machilipatnam → Krishna** districts, renamed on the suggestion of **Cotton** |
| **1904** | **Guntur** and **East Godavari** formed |
| **1925** | **West Godavari** formed |
!TRAP **1904** = Guntur + East Godavari; **1925** = West Godavari; **1859** = Godavari + Krishna.
### 2. COLLECTOR AND GOVERNANCE
- The **District Collector** held revenue, magisterial and police powers – the pivot of British rule.
- In the Agency tracts the **Ganjam Collector** acted as **Agent to the Governor** (from 1819).
- **Munro** – Ryotwari: settlement made directly with the ryot.
!KEY Collector = revenue + magistrate; Agent to the Governor = special charge of hill (Agency) areas.
### 3. ANICUTS AND IRRIGATION PROJECTS
#### 3.1 Why Dowleswaram was built
1. **Guntur famine (1832–33)** – the trigger.
2. **Cotton's 1844 report** to the Board of Revenue urging an anicut near Rajahmundry.
3. **Sanctioned late 1846**.
4. **Built 1847–52**.
#### 3.2 Anicuts → river, engineer, year
| Anicut | River | Engineer / assistant | Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dowleswaram** (Godavari anicut) | **Godavari** | **Sir Arthur Cotton**; Indian assistant **Veenam Veeranna** | Begun **1847**, main work completed **April 1852**; cost **Rs 1.5 lakh** |
| **Krishna anicut, Bezwada** (now **Prakasam Barrage**) | **Krishna** | Proposed by **Col. Henry Atlock (Lake)**; built by **Captain Charles Orr** on Cotton's plan | 1852/53–**1855** |
| **Sangam anicut** | **Penna** (Nellore) | Supplies Nellore's canals | **1882–86** |
- **APPSC G2 2012 key** dates Dowleswaram to **1855**; answer 1855 only if the options force it – the anicut itself was completed in **1852**.
- **Ramapada Sagaram** – proposed by **Sir Shonthi Venkataramamurthy** = forerunner of today's **Polavaram** project (PYQ 2019).
- **Godavari and Krishna deltas** = the **"Rice Bowl of Andhra"**.
- **Latest (Feb 2026):** **Prakasam Barrage** fitted with **automated crest-gate telemetry and SCADA**.
!PAIR Dowleswaram | Godavari | Cotton + Veeranna || Bezwada | Krishna | Charles Orr
### 4. BRITISH ADMINISTRATORS → CONTRIBUTION
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Sir Arthur Cotton** | Godavari and Krishna anicuts; pioneer of irrigation in South India; **knighted 1861**; called **"Apara Bhagiratha"** and **"Cotton Dora"** |
| **Veenam Veeranna** | Cotton's Indian officer at Dowleswaram; made **"Rao Bahadur"** and Assistant Engineer |
| **Charles Orr** | English officer who built the **Krishna anicut** and worked with Cotton |
| **C.P. Brown (1798–1884)** | **Kadapa Collector**; **Telugu revival**; editor of the **Madras Literary Journal**; Curator, **Madras Manuscript Library** |
| **Colin Mackenzie (1754–1821)** | **First Surveyor General (1815)**; preserved manuscripts and records |
| **G.N. Taylor** | **Rajahmundry Sub-Collector**; started **Anglo-Vernacular schools** across most of Andhra (PYQ 2017) |
| **Thomas Munro** | **Ryotwari** |
| **M.G. Ranade** | Called **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** the **"Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar of South India"** (APPSC G2 2019) |
!TRAP Mackenzie = first **Surveyor General**; Brown = **Madras Literary Journal**; Taylor = **Anglo-Vernacular schools**.
### 5. COMMUNICATIONS – RAILWAYS AND CANALS
| Item | Feature |
|---|---|
| **First railway in Andhra** | **Puttur–Renigunta**, Madras Railway, **1862** |
| Railways in general | Reached Andhra in the **late 19th century**; linked the interior with the **Madras port** |
| **Buckingham Canal** | **Salt-water** navigation canal along the coast through **Pulicat Lake**; extended as **famine relief in 1876–78**; now part of **NW-4 (Kakinada–Puducherry)** with the **Eluru and Kakinada canals** |
- **Fort St. George** – built by the English East India Company at **Madrasapatnam**.
!TRAP Buckingham Canal is part of **NW-4**, not NW-5.
### 6. ECONOMIC EFFECTS
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Deindustrialisation** | Artisans lost patronage; **British goods entered duty-free** → handicrafts declined |
| **Land revenue payable in cash** | Shift to **commercial crops** – **tobacco (Guntur)**, **indigo**, **cotton**, **oilseeds** |
| Less land under food | Region became **famine-prone** |
| Ryotwari burden | Heavy cash assessment pushed ryots to moneylenders |
- Most people depended on **agriculture**; **rice** was the main crop.
### 7. FAMINES UNDER BRITISH RULE
| Famine | Year | Region / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Guntur famine** (Nandana / Dokkala / Pedda Karuvu) | **1832–33** | Guntur; about **1.5 lakh** deaths, a third of the district; led to the Dowleswaram anicut |
| **Ganjam (Orissa) famine** | **1866** | Ganjam and Bellary districts of Madras also hit |
| **Great Famine** | **1876–78** | Worst in the Ceded Districts – **Bellary (incl. Anantapur), Cuddapah, Kurnool**; Buckingham Canal extended as relief work |
!NUM 1832–33 Guntur → 1866 Ganjam → 1876–78 Great Famine
### 8. TELUGU JOURNALS AND BRITISH RULE
- **Hitavadi (1862)** – first Telugu literary journal, started by **Rev. J.E. Sharkey** of the Church Missionary Society.
- Later Telugu papers – **Krishna Patrika** and **Andhra Patrika** – openly criticised British rule; British reports called their influence "pernicious".
- After **1906** (militant nationalism) Telugu writing turned openly hostile to foreign rule.
