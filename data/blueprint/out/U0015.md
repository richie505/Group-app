## U0015 | ARYAN QUESTION – ORIGINAL HOME THEORIES AND ARCHAEOLOGICAL CORRELATES
### 1. MEANING OF 'ARYA'
- 'Arya' originally meant **"noble"** / of high birth; later persons of noble lineage and social standing.
- **Classical Sanskrit** sense: Arya = **"a nobleman"**.
- It was the Indo-Iranian people's **own name**; Vedic people used it for themselves and for the noble class. The Indo-Iranian group that wrote the **Avesta** and the **Vedas** called themselves Aryans.
- 'Aryan' = a **speech group** (linguistic-cultural label): speakers of the **Indo-Aryan / Indo-Iranian** branches of the Indo-European family.
- It is **not an ethnic or racial group**; "superior race" is a rejected colonial misuse.
- **Max Muller** popularised the 'Aryan' idea.
!KEY Exam options "ethnic group / nomadic people / speech group / superior race" – answer: **speech group**.
### 2. LINGUISTIC AND EPIGRAPHIC EVIDENCE
#### 2.1 Indo-European language family
- **Vedic Sanskrit** belongs to the **Indo-European** family (Europe, Asia, Middle East).
- Indo-European (Aryan) family = about **73%** of Indian speakers.
| Branch | Where |
|---|---|
| Iranian | Outside India |
| **Dardic** | **Jammu and Kashmir** |
| Indo-Aryan | North India |
#### 2.2 Boghazkoi inscription and Avesta
| Evidence | Where / when | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Boghazkoi inscription** | Asia Minor (Anatolia, modern **Turkey**); c. **14th century BCE** (c. 1400 BCE) | **Peace treaty** between the **Hittites** and the rulers of **Mitanni**; names Vedic gods **Indra, Mitra, Varuna, Nasatyas** |
| **Avesta** | **Iran** – oldest Iranian (**Zoroastrian**) text | Language and ideas close to the **Rigveda**; common Indo-Iranian stock |
- Boghazkoi shows Vedic-speaking people and gods in **West Asia** → supports a **Central Asian homeland** and movement towards Iran and India.
!TRAP Indra, Varuna, Mitra, Nasatyas → **Boghazkoi**, not Behistun, Mansehra, Shahbazgarhi, Junagadh or Ecbatana.

### 4. ARCHAEOLOGICAL CORRELATES
#### 4.1 Horse and spoked wheel
- Harappans used **solid wheels**; **spoked-wheel chariots** and horse archaeology are part of the migration argument.
- Changes in pottery, burial practices and settlements after the Harappan decline also cited.
#### 4.2 Cultures and their link
| Culture | Region | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **Andronovo** | Central Asian steppes (Bronze Age, 2nd millennium BCE) | Steppe pastoralists with **horses and chariots**; linked to Indo-Iranian speakers |
| **BMAC** (Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex) | **Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, north Afghanistan** | **Contact zone** migrants passed through |
| **Gandhara Grave culture** | **Swat valley**, north-west Pakistan | **Horse remains**, new burial customs; linked to early Indo-Aryans |
- Sequence: Andronovo → BMAC → Gandhara Grave culture.
- Open question: link between **Painted Grey Ware** and Later Vedic textual geography – unsettled.
### 5. MIGRATION VS INDIGENOUS DEBATE
| Theory | Proponents | Key arguments | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Migration** | Max Muller, Mortimer Wheeler, Asko Parpola | Linguistics, Rigvedic geography, horse/chariot archaeology, genetics (**R1a haplogroup**) | Mainstream: came from Central Asia c. **1500 BCE** |
| **Indigenous / Out of India** | **S.R. Rao, B.B. Lal, Shrikant Talageri** | Vedic-Sarasvati continuity; script decipherment claims; astronomical dating of Rigveda | Marginal |
| **Synthesis** | **Romila Thapar, Upinder Singh** | Small groups migrated, **elite dominance**, cultural assimilation; no "invasion" | Nuanced consensus |
- **B.B. Lal**: PGW linked with the Vedic tradition; Sarasvati = Rigvedic river.
#### 5.1 Rakhigarhi DNA
- **Rakhigarhi** (**Hisar** district, Haryana): about **550 ha**, **largest known Harappan settlement**, on the seasonal **Drishadvati**.
- **Mound 7** burial plot: **56 skeletons**, incl. a **~4,600-year-old woman**.
- Her DNA had **no "Steppe Pastoralist" gene** → cited against invasion, for indigenous continuity.
- Genetics overall: migration + admixture + continuity; no final answer.
### 6. ARYAN–HARAPPAN RELATIONSHIP
- Chronology: Harappan decline first → Early Vedic (**1500–1000 BCE**) culture pastoral, mobile, warlike.
| Feature | Harappan | Rigvedic |
|---|---|---|
| Settlement | Urban, planned | Rural, pastoral; fortified mud settlements |
| **Horse** | No evidence (UPSC 2017 key) | Domesticated; central to warfare |
| Warfare | No horse-drawn chariots | Chariots; **coat of mail and helmet** |
| Wheel | Solid | Spoked |
| Transmission | **Seals and script** | **Oral hymns** |
### 7. DASAS, DASYUS AND PANIS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dasas** | A **branch of early Aryans** (per **Iranian literature**) |
| **Dasyus** | **Original inhabitants**; possibly phallus worshippers; did not raise cattle for dairy |
| **Panis** | Non-Aryan **cattle-holders and traders** |
- **Trasadasyu**: mild towards Dasas, hostile to Dasyus.
- **Divodasa** of the **Bharata** clan defeated **Sambara**.
