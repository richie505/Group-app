## U0615 | BIOLOGICAL CHARACTERISTICS OF OCEANS – MARINE ECOSYSTEMS, CORAL REEFS, BLEACHING, UPWELLING
### 1. MARINE ECOSYSTEMS
- Marine ecosystems cover about **71%** of Earth's surface (~97% of its water).
| Marine | Not marine |
|---|---|
| Coral reefs, **hydrothermal vents** (chemosynthetic life), intertidal zone, **estuaries**, salt marshes, mangroves, **seagrass beds**, **kelp forests** | **Rivers, freshwater lakes** (lotic/lentic freshwater ecosystems) |
### 2. OCEAN ZONES
| Zone | Extent / feature |
|---|---|
| **Littoral / intertidal** | Between high- and low-tide marks |
| **Neritic** | High-water line over the continental shelf to ~**200 m**; **richest fishing zone** |
| **Photic (euphotic)** | Sunlit upper layer (~200 m); photosynthesis |
| **Epipelagic** (sunlit) | **Highest share** of marine habitats and life |
| Pelagic | Open water column |
| **Benthic** | Sea floor and its organisms |
| **Aphotic** | Dark; no photosynthesis |
| Abyssal, hadal | Deep floor and trenches; far less life |
### 3. MARINE ORGANISMS AND PLANKTON
| Group | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Plankton** | Drift with water; phytoplankton (plants) + zooplankton (animals) |
| **Nekton** | Swim actively |
| **Benthos** | Live on the bottom |
- **Phytoplankton** (diatoms, dinoflagellates, cyanobacteria) = **main primary producers** of the ocean.
- Oceans (mainly phytoplankton and photosynthetic bacteria) produce about **50% or more** of Earth's oxygen – **more than all rainforests** (50–70% vs about 30%).
### 4. UPWELLING ZONES
- Winds push surface water **offshore** → cold, **nutrient-rich** water rises → plankton boom → rich fisheries.
| Upwelling zone | Current / season |
|---|---|
| **Peru–Chile** | **Humboldt** (anchoveta) |
| **Namibia** (SW Africa) | **Benguela** |
| **Somalia** / Horn of Africa | **Findlater**, SW monsoon |
| **Kerala** / SW India | SW monsoon – **oil sardine, mackerel** |
- **El Nino** suppresses Peru upwelling → less phytoplankton → fisheries fail.
### 5. CORAL REEFS – NATURE AND CONDITIONS
#### 5.1 Nature and Composition
- Built from skeletons of tiny colonial **coral polyps**; skeleton of **calcium carbonate** (aragonite). (Oyster shells too are calcium carbonate.)
- **Mutualism** with **zooxanthellae** (single-celled algae): algae photosynthesise and give **food and colour**; coral gives **shelter and nutrients**.
#### 5.2 Growth Conditions
| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Latitude | Tropics, about **30°N–30°S** (most within 25°N–25°S) |
| Temperature | Above ~**20°C**; optimum **23–25°C** |
| Water | Clear, shallow (< ~50 m), sunlit, normal salinity |
| Avoid | **Muddy river mouths** – sediment and freshwater inhibit growth |
- **Biorock (mineral accretion)** technology restores damaged reefs.
#### 5.3 Epithet and Roles
- **"Rainforests of the Sea"** – cover **< 1%** of the ocean floor yet support **> 25%** of marine species; host far more animal phyla than rainforests.
- Break **storm waves**, prevent coastal erosion; nurseries, fisheries, tourism.
!KEY APPSC 2024 (G1 Prelims P2): "rainforests of the sea" and "break wave power" are **both true**, but the second does **not explain** the first (the epithet is due to species richness).
### 6. REEF TYPES
| Type | Feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fringing** | Close to shore; shallow lagoon or none (commonest in India) | Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kutch |
| **Barrier** | Parallel to coast, separated by a **wide, deep lagoon** | **Great Barrier Reef** |
| **Atoll** | **Ring/horseshoe**-shaped reef enclosing a lagoon, no central island | **Lakshadweep** (India's only true atolls) |
### 7. CORAL REEF PLACES
#### 7.1 World
- **Great Barrier Reef**: largest reef system and largest living structure; off **Queensland**, **north-eastern Australia**, **Coral Sea (Pacific)**; ~**2,300 km**, 10°S–24°S; ~3,000 reefs + 900 islands; **UNESCO 1981**. Next largest: **Mesoamerican Barrier Reef**.
- More than one-third of reefs lie in **Australia, Indonesia, Philippines**.
#### 7.2 India
| Place | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Gulf of Mannar** (TN) | Reef; **India's first marine biosphere reserve** (21 islands, seagrass, **dugongs**) |
| **Gulf of Kutch** (Gujarat) | Reef |
| **Andaman & Nicobar** | **Highest diversity**; first **National Coral Reef Research Institute**, **Chidiyatapu** |
| **Lakshadweep** | Formed by coral; only atolls; giant *Pavona clavus* colony near **Kadmat** |
| Malvan (Maharashtra) | Patchy reefs |
- **No reefs**: **Gulf of Cambay**, **Sundarbans** (mangroves), most east coast – **West Bengal, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh** (river sediment + freshwater).
- **AP angle**: AP has **no true reefs**; its fisheries rely on Bay of Bengal plankton, threatened by marine heatwaves.
- **Feb 2026**: NGT cleared the **₹92,000-crore Great Nicobar project**, directing the Environment Ministry to protect and regenerate corals.
### 8. CORAL BLEACHING
- **Meaning**: polyps **expel zooxanthellae** and lose pigments, turning white (APPSC G2 2017); stressed, **not necessarily dead**.
- **Main cause**: **rising sea-surface temperature / marine heatwaves** (global warming).
- Others: pollution, **extreme low tides**, **ocean acidification**, crown-of-thorns starfish, coral mining, tourism – NOT more carbonate.
| Global event | Period | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **1998** | Strong El Nino |
| 2nd | **2010** | — |
| 3rd | **2014–2017** | 68.2% of reef areas |
| 4th | **2023 – ~mid-2025** | Fastest, widest: **84%** of reefs in **83 countries** |
- Every strong El Nino since 1998 came with a global bleaching event; recovery takes **> 15 years**.
- **GCRMN 2026**: major disturbances now every **4–6 years**; **87%** of reefs faced heat stress in 2024.
- **India, Feb 2025**: bleaching in Gulf of Mannar, Palk Bay, Lakshadweep, A&N, Gulf of Kachchh. **Andaman mass bleaching** linked to a ~**2°C** rise in Indian Ocean temperature.
### 9. OCEAN ACIDIFICATION AND ALGAL BLOOMS
#### 9.1 Ocean Acidification
- **"Evil twin of global warming"** / "the other CO2 problem".
- CO2 + water → **carbonic acid** → pH falls (seawater ~8.1, alkaline) → **carbonate ions fall** → corals, plankton, shellfish (**calcifiers**) struggle to build shells.
- Oceans absorb about **30%** of human-made CO2.
- Worst in **cold temperate seas** (higher CO2 solubility) and melting Arctic; it does not necessarily affect cloud formation.
#### 9.2 Algal Blooms and Red Tides
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Harmful algal bloom** | Excess N and P (farm runoff, sewage) → eutrophication → decay strips oxygen → **dead zones**, fish mortality |
| **Red tide** | Bloom of pigmented **dinoflagellates**; discolours water, kills fish, contaminates shellfish |
- Indian waters: blooms from estuary nutrient discharge, monsoon runoff, upwelling, mud banks.
- Dead zones: **Gulf of Mexico** (Mississippi runoff), Chesapeake Bay. **INCOIS** issues algal-bloom and bleaching alerts.
### 10. MARINE PROTECTED AREAS
| Target | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Aichi Target 11** (CBD, 2011–2020) | **10%** of coastal and marine areas by 2020 |
| **30x30** (Target 3, **Kunming-Montreal GBF, COP15, Dec 2022**) | **30% of land + 30% of oceans** by 2030 |
- Coverage: **8.6%** of ocean and coastal areas protected (2024); about **10%** (April 2026); only **2.8%** fully or highly protected.
