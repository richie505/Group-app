## U0048 | SATAVAHANA ADMINISTRATION, ECONOMY, COINAGE AND PRAKRIT LITERATURE (HALA'S GATHASAPTASATI)
### 1. NATURE OF KINGSHIP AND THE STATE
- King = **upholder of dharma** (Dharmashastra ideal); kingship **hereditary and patriarchal**.
- Power **more decentralised than the Mauryas**: **Mauryan-style officers (Amatya, Mahamatra)** beside **local feudatories**.
- King's council = **Mantri Parishad**.
- **Queen mothers**: **Naganika** – regent for her minor sons, issued **Naneghat**; **Gautami Balasri** – issued the **Nasik** inscription in her grandson **Pulumavi's** reign.
| Ruler / queen | Title / contribution |
|---|---|
| **Gautamiputra Satakarni** | Metronymics begin with him; **Ekabrahmana**, **Varnasankarya-nirodhaka** (stopper of mixing of varnas) |
| **Kuntala Satakarni** (13th king) | Made **Sanskrit the royal language**; court of **Gunadhya** and **Sarvavarma**; Katantra composed for him |
| **Hala** (17th king) | **Kavivatsala**; compiled the **Gathasaptasati**; honoured poets **Kumarila** and **Sripalita**; reign = golden age of Prakrit |
| **Yajna Sri** | Called **Trisamudradhipati** (lord of three seas) in Banabhatta's **Harshacharita** |
!PAIR Titles: Kavivatsala | Hala || Ekabrahmana | Gautamiputra || Trisamudradhipati | Yajna Sri
### 2. ADMINISTRATIVE UNITS AND OFFICERS
#### 2.1 Units
| Unit | Meaning | Head |
|---|---|---|
| **Rashtra** | Province | — |
| **Ahara** | District / largest administrative division (PYQ key) | **Amatya** (distant aharas: **Mahasenadhipathulu**) |
| **Vishaya** | Sub-division | — |
| **Gulma** | Cluster of villages / military outpost | **Gulmika (Gaulmika)** |
| **Grama** | Village (lowest unit) | Village head; **Gramani** over **5 or 10 villages** |
- Hierarchy: **Rashtra → Ahara → Vishaya → Grama**.
- Aharas in inscriptions: Satavahani, **Govardhana** (most prominent, Nasik inscription), **Sopara**, Mamala, Kodura, Vaijayanti.
#### 2.2 Officers and Bodies
| Officer / body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Amatya** | Minister / district officer who ran the Aharas; not hereditary, often transferred |
| **Mahamatra** | Special officer; also ran Aharas |
| Visvamaatyulu | Consulted for important decisions |
| Rajaamatyas | Advisors |
| Mahaamatyas | Special officers |
| **Mahasenapati / Senapati** | Day-to-day military administration of regions (king = supreme commander) |
| **Gulmika** | **Village headman** (G2 2008); head of a gulma; law and order (Myakadoni) |
| **Heranika** | **Treasurer** |
| **Bhandagarika** | Stores |
| **Rajjugahaka** | **Land surveyor** |
| Pradeshika | Police |
| **Pattikapala** | Drafted land-grant inscriptions |
| **Akshapatakas** | Kept records of the king's orders |
| Mahatalavara | Commander of border provinces |
- Village headships, often held by military officers, became **hereditary** → helped **feudalism** grow.
#### 2.3 Urban Administration
- **Nigama Sabha** = urban council that ran **cities/towns, not villages** (G2 2008); members **Gahapatis**; named in the **Bhattiprolu** inscription.
- **Gahapati (Gaaha Pati)** = representative of farmers; also part of urban administration.
- Towns: **Barukaccha, Sopara, Kalyan, Govardhana**, Pratishthana, Kondapur, Dhanyakataka, Vijayapuri, Nasik.
!TRAP Nigama Sabha = cities; Gulma = group of villages; Heranika = treasurer, not surveyor.
### 3. FEUDATORIES AND ARMY
#### 3.1 Feudatories
| Title | Feature / region |
|---|---|
| **Raja** | Highest feudatory; **could issue coins** |
| **Maharathis** | Hereditary; collected and remitted taxes; **western Deccan** |
| **Mahabhojas** | Powerful local chiefs; **Konkan** (not Eastern Bengal); inscriptions omit the emperor's name → near-independence |
| **Mahatalavaras / Mahasenadhipatis** | Generals of **border provinces**; later feudal lords |
- Grading: **Raja > Mahabhoja > Senapati**.
- Semi-feudal **Samanta rajyas** paid tribute and sent troops: **Chutus – Mysore; Kuras – Kolhapur; Ikshvakus – Vijayapuri**. Many became independent after the fall.
#### 3.2 Army and Camps
- **Pliny**: **1,00,000 infantry, 2,000 cavalry, 1,000 elephants**; chaturanga bala plus archers.
- **Amaravati sculptures**: infantry in front, cavalry and elephants on flanks, archers at the rear.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kataka** | **Permanent** military camp / fortified centre in cities |
| **Skandhavara** | **Temporary** campaign camp |
!KEY APPSC G1 2024: Katakas and Skandhavaras = military camps that served as **administrative centres** when the king was present (not tax-free monk villages or rock-cut areas).
### 4. TAXES AND TAX-FREE LAND GRANTS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bhaga** | Land revenue; king's share of harvest = **1/6** |
| **Kara** | General levy |
| **Deya-meya** (Bhodga, Deeva Meeya) | King's share of peasant produce, **in kind** |
| **Karukara** | Tax on **artisans** and professionals (sistu) |
| Moturpha | Tax on trades |
| **Vetti** | Forced labour |
- **Land revenue** = chief income; plus tribute from feudatories and trade taxes.
- **FIRST**: Satavahanas made the **first tax-free land grants (agraharas)** to **Brahmanas and Buddhist monks**, with immunities; a root of the early-medieval feudal order.
- **Naneghat**: Satakarni I gave land, cows (**godhana**) and gold to Brahmanas.
- **Gautamiputra**: village at **Tirasmri** to monks + 100 nivartanas of his own land.
- **Immunity**: royal officers and soldiers could not enter granted land.
!TRAP Bhaga = land revenue (1/6); Karukara = artisans' tax.
### 5. AGRICULTURE AND GUILDS
#### 5.1 Agriculture
- Cultivation spread in the **Krishna-Godavari** basin; **paddy transplantation**; main crops **rice and cotton**.
- Irrigation: **ghatiyantras**, made by the **Udayantrika** guild.
| AP centre | Product |
|---|---|
| Guduru | Fine cotton |
| Vinukonda | Metal |
| Palnadu | Diamonds |
| **Guntupalli** | **Copper and iron** (not gold) |
| Karimnagar-Warangal | Iron ore |
#### 5.2 Guilds (Srenis / Srenulu)
- **Shreni** = guild of traders/artisans of one craft; fixed work rules, quality, prices, wages; controlled members.
- Head: **Shreshthi (Sethi)**; **18 guilds** in Satavahana inscriptions.
- Guilds existed in **north and south India**, not only the north; not registered with a central authority.
- Acted as **banks**: took deposits, paid interest, including to monasteries; interest **12%** (APPSC 2019).
| Guild | Craft |
|---|---|
| **Kolika** | **Weavers** |
| **Padhika** | **Butchers** (a PYQ-book key: carpenters) |
| **Tailika / Tilapishaka** | Oil pressers |
| **Udayantrika** | Water-machines |
| **Gandhika** | Perfumers |
| **Kulara / Kularika** | Potters |
| Malakara | Florists |
| **Sarthavaha** | Long-distance / foreign traders |
- **Nasik inscription of Ushavadatta (Rishabhadatta)**, Nahapana's son-in-law: **2,000 karshapanas** with the **Govardhana weavers' guild** at **1% a month = 12% a year** (robes for monks); **1,000** at **3/4% a month** for other needs.
- Later guilds: **Nigamalu** = Ikshvaku trade guilds; **Chitrameli** = medieval agriculturists' guild.
### 6. TRADE ROUTES, CENTRES AND PORTS
#### 6.1 Inland Routes
- **Ujjain → Bharuch (Barygaza)** via **Paithan and Tagara**.
- **Kalyan → Naneghat pass → Junnar → Paithan**: main route from the west coast into the Deccan.
- **Tagara (Ter)**: emporium on the route from Pratishthana to the **east coast**; **Pratishthana**: capital and inland centre on the Godavari; **Vengi**: trade centre.
- Satavahanas = road builders, "**Mahapatha nirmatalu**".
- 2026 route maps: Ujjain–Paithan–Tagara–Bharuch.
#### 6.2 Ports
| Port / centre | Identification |
|---|---|
| **Maisolia** | **Machilipatnam**; textiles (Ptolemy) |
| **Kantakossyla** | **Ghantasala** |
| **Koddura (Koduru)** | Gudur area, Krishna; **only east-coast port in the Periplus** |
| **Dhanyakataka** | Amaravati / Dharanikota |
| Kalingapatnam | Srikakulam coast |
| **Bharukachha** | Bharuch, Gujarat; Satavahana after Nahapana's defeat |
| **Sopara, Kalyan** | West coast, **Maharashtra**; Kalyan destroyed by the Sakas (Periplus) |
| **Motupalli** | Rose later under the **Kakatiyas** (Eastern Chalukya context) – not Satavahana |
- Satavahanas were pioneers of **west-coast** trade (Bharukachha, Sopara, Kalyan, Vyjayantipura).
| Place | Other name |
|---|---|

### 7. INDO-ROMAN TRADE
| Source | Author / language | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural History** | **Pliny** (Latin) | Drain of Roman gold for fine cloth; about **12.5 million denarii a year** |
| **Periplus** | Anonymous Greek | **Arikamedu** = "market street of the Romans" (east coast) |
| Exports | Imports |
|---|---|
| Muslin (Mallaselai), silk, gems, **pearls, diamonds, ivory, spices**, pepper, Wootz steel | Wine (Italy, Arabia), copper, gold and silver coins, gold (Malaya, Sumatra), silk and **camphor (China)** |
- **Roman coins** at **Bhattiprolu**, many at **Nagarjunakonda**; gold coins at Attirala, **Salihundam**, Vijayawada, **Vinukonda**, Nellore.
- **Constantine** gold coin at **Kudali Sangameswaram** (Alampur) = later period.
- Coins of **Pulomavi and Yagnasri** show thriving sea trade.
- Roman trade **peaked** under the Satavahanas (1st c. BCE–3rd c. CE) and **declined after them**.
- Egypt (Feb 2026): ~**30 Indian inscriptions** (Tamil-Brahmi, Prakrit, Sanskrit) of the Indo-Roman phase found in **Theban tombs**.
!TRAP G2 2025 assertion-reason: "trade flourished after the Satavahanas" is false.
### 8. COINAGE
#### 8.1 Metals
- Metals: **lead (most common), copper, bronze, silver, potin**.
- **No gold coins**: gold used only as bullion. **Nickel** not used.
- Currency **karshapana** in copper and silver; **1 suvarna (gold) = 35 karshapanas** (Rishabhadatta inscription).
!TRAP **Iron coins with thin copper coating** = **Ikshvakus** (Krishna-Guntur; APPSC key), not Satavahanas.
#### 8.2 Coin Types
| Type | Issuer / fact |
|---|---|
| **Ship-motif** | **Vasishthiputra Pulumavi** and **Yajna Sri Satakarni** (ship with anchor – trade with Siam, Java, Borneo) |
| **Portrait coins** | Indigenous innovation; began with **Gautamiputra**; silver portraits common after him |
| **Restruck** | Gautamiputra restruck Nahapana's silver – **Jogalthambi hoard, 13,270 coins** |
| **Bilingual** | Later silver portraits: **Prakrit + a Dravidian language** (G2 2025) |
- Motifs: elephant, horse, lion, camel, chaitya, dharmachakra; reverse **Ujjain symbol** (cross with four circles).
- **Kondapur mint (Medak)**: legend "**Siri Chimuka Sata**".
- Legends in **Prakrit, Brahmi** (not Kharosthi).
#### 8.3 After the Satavahanas
- **No later ruler issued ship-motif coins** (G2 2025 key).
- **Ikshvakus**: lead coins – **Phanigiri** pot of **3,730** lead coins (elephant obverse, Ujjain symbol reverse; 2024).
- **Pallavas**: lead, copper and bronze coins with **bull and lion** symbols.
### 9. LANGUAGE AND SCRIPT
- **Official / royal / inscriptional language = Prakrit** (G2 2011); script **Brahmi**; earliest stone inscription = Prakrit.
- Satavahanas = **nurturers of Prakrit** (more than Sanskrit); Sanskrit grew alongside Prakrit and folk languages; later Mahayana texts in Sanskrit.
- **Desi Bhaasha** (common people) = **Dravidian** family.
| Dynasty | Official language |
|---|---|
| Satavahanas | Prakrit |
| Early Ikshvakus | Prakrit |
| **Salankayanas** | **Sanskrit** replaced Prakrit (of 8 inscriptions, 5 Sanskrit, 3 Prakrit) |
| Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi | Telugu |
| Andhra Pradesh | Telugu from **1966** (not 1956) |
| FIRST | Fact |
|---|---|
| First complete Sanskrit inscription | **Junagadh** of Rudradaman |
| First Telugu word | "**Nagabu**" (Amaravati) |
| Telugu word in Myakadoni (8th year of Pulumavi, Bellary) | "**Vepuru**" |
| Desi words in Gathasaptasati | **Atta, Addam, Potta, Pilla**, Padi, Pandi, Ammi, Tuppu |
- Telugu script from **Brahmi** via Bhattiprolu and Telugu-Kannada scripts; Pali = most early Buddhist literature.
- "**Italian of the East**" (Telugu, words end in vowels): phrase from **Niccolo de Conti**, used by the British.
- **Gundaram forest** (Jayashankar Bhupalpally, Telangana; Jun 2026): new Satavahana-era Prakrit-Brahmi inscriptions on divisions, chiefs, land grants.

### 10. SOCIETY AND WOMEN
- **Chaturvarna** worked (G2 2008); **Sakas and Yavanas absorbed**; Brahmanical ideology not abolished.
- Occupational groups: Gandhikas, weavers, oil pressers, potters, florists, sarthavahas.
- **Religion**: kings followed **Vedic** religion (Ashvamedha); **queens and women donors** supported **Buddhism** (Gautami Balasri gave a vihara).
- **Shipping**: seafaring merchants in Gathasaptasati + ship coins.
#### 10.1 Women
- Women **respected**: matronymics; queens' inscriptions; **independent donations at Bharhut, Sanchi, Amaravati** without naming husbands or fathers; women artisans, merchants and poets.
- Society still patriarchal; **Kamasutra** records **vetti** by women.
!TRAP "Women were looked down upon" = incorrect option (G2 2008).
#### 10.2 Social Picture in the Gathasaptasati
- Rural life, **sugar and jaggery**; **sati**; **no child marriage**; prostitution; **hanging of criminals**.
- **Brundagana** (group dances, choral singing); house-wall paintings; plight of **wives of seafaring merchants**.
- Arts had a **public aspect**.

#### 11.1 Gathasaptasati and Hala
- Anthology of **700 Prakrit gathas** on **love, romance, rural life, nature** (G2 2025); opens with a prayer to **Shiva**.
- **Most important literary source** for Satavahana **society** – not political history.
- **Women contributors**: **Anulakshmi, Anupalabdha, Reva (Revati), Madhavi** (G2 2017).
- **First** work covering all **Navarasas**; Desi words = earliest traces of Telugu.
#### 11.2 Brihatkatha, Katantra and Lilavati
- **Legend**: Gunadhya, court poet of **Kuntala Satakarni**, quarrelled with **Sarvavarma** over how fast the king could learn Sanskrit; he lost, gave up Sanskrit and wrote in **Paishachi**.
- Brihatkatha mentions **slavery**; Sanskrit versions: **Kathasaritsagara** (Somadeva), **Brihatkathamanjari** (Kshemendra).
- **Katantra**: written so **Kuntala Satakarni** could learn Sanskrit quickly.
- **Lilavati Parinayam**: Hala marries **Sinhala princess Lilavati** at the **Bhimeswara temple, Draksharamam** (Saptagodavari); also says a Satavahana king patronised Nagarjuna.
