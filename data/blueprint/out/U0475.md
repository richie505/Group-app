### 2. REGIONAL PATTERN OF POVERTY
#### 2.1 Highest and Lowest
| Measure | Highest | Lowest |
|---|---|---|
| Tendulkar ratio 2011-12 | **Chhattisgarh 39.93%** | Goa 5.09% |
| Largest number of poor, 2011-12 | **Uttar Pradesh** (about 5.98 crore) | - |
| National MPI 2023 (NFHS-5, 2019-21) | **Bihar** (0.16, 28th) | **Kerala** (0.002), then Goa, Tamil Nadu |
| Rangarajan line (RBI update, 2022-23) | Chhattisgarh (rural 25.1%, urban 13.3%) | HP rural 0.4%; TN urban 1.9% |
| NCAER survey, % below poverty line | **Bihar** | - |
!TRAP Highest ratio = **Chhattisgarh** (Tendulkar); highest MPI = **Bihar**; largest number of poor = **UP**, not Bihar.
#### 2.2 Concentration Belt (BIMARU + East)
- Concentrated-poverty belt: **Bihar, UP, MP, Rajasthan, Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh** (Kerala is NOT in it). UP MPI 0.103; Jharkhand 0.131.
- More than **50% of India's poor** (World Bank): **Bihar, UP, MP, Odisha**.
- **"Ethiopia of India"** (malnutrition): **Madhya Pradesh**.
- Developed States: Punjab, Maharashtra, Haryana, Gujarat, West Bengal, Kerala, Tamil Nadu.
#### 2.3 Convergence (Latest)
- High-poverty States fell fastest.
- MPI exits 2013-14 to 2022-23: **UP 5.94 crore**; Bihar 3.77; MP 2.30; Rajasthan 1.87.
- Oct 2025: 2011-12 to 2022-23, rural **Odisha 47.8% → 8.6%**; urban **Bihar 50.8% → 9.1%**.
### 3. SOCIAL GROUP POVERTY
- Highest: **Scheduled Tribes** (Tendulkar 2011-12: rural ST about **45%**); **SCs** next.
- Education gap: **35.1%** of those with no formal education are poor vs **14.9%** with post-secondary education.
- AP: districts with large tribal populations have higher poverty.
### 4. CAUSES OF REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Agricultural productivity and **irrigation** | Delta districts gain; rain-fed **Rayalaseema** lags |
| Infrastructure and investment | Regional gaps high and rising: investment only in select locales |
| Agro-climatic suitability | Some areas less suited to development |
| Human capital | Low education = higher poverty |
| Social transfers | **PMGKAY** food, **MGNREGA**, **PMAY-G** helped cut poverty |

### 7. AP POVERTY-REDUCTION INSTITUTIONS
| Body | Full form / role | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **SERP** | **Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty**: social mobilisation of rural poor into women's SHGs and livelihoods | **2000** |
| **Velugu** | **District Poverty Initiatives Project**, **World Bank** aided, run by SERP | 2000 |
| **IKP** | **Indira Kranthi Patham**: Velugu renamed; women's organisations, livelihoods for BPL households | 2004-05 |
| **MEPMA** | **Mission for Elimination of Poverty in Municipal Areas**: **urban** SHGs, under Municipal Administration and Urban Development | 2007 |
| **APMSS** | **Andhra Pradesh Mahila Sadhikara Samstha**: poverty reduction of rural and urban poor households through women's SHGs | - |
!TRAP **Velugu → renamed IKP**. SERP = rural; MEPMA = urban.
### 8. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS
- 2025-26 **"graduation approach"** models: **Satat Jeevikoparjan Yojana** (Bihar); Kerala poverty-eradication model; **Samaveshi Aajeevika Yojana** (Centre, under DAY-NRLM).
- **18 Dec 2025:** **VB-G RAM G Bill, 2025** passed by Parliament; rural job guarantee raised to **125 days**.
- Social protection coverage: **22% (2016) → 64.3% (2025)**.
