## U0529 | BANKING SECTOR REFORMS – NARASIMHAM COMMITTEES, BASEL NORMS, BANK MERGERS, PRIVATISATION DEBATE
### 1. FINANCIAL SECTOR REFORMS AFTER 1991
#### 1.1 What the Reforms Covered
- Covered banking, capital, government debt and forex markets.
- **Interest-rate deregulation** (APPSC-GS 2023 key).
- **CRR and SLR cuts**: SLR was **38.5%** in 1991-92.
- **Prudential norms**: income recognition, asset classification, provisioning; **CRAR introduced in 1992**.
- **Private bank entry**: **HDFC, ICICI** (by 1995); **FDI up to 74%** in private banks (**2004**).
- **RBI became the primary regulator**; **dual control** with the Finance Ministry's Banking Division ended.
#### 1.2 Reform → Year
| Year | Reform |
|---|---|
| 1992 | Prudential norms and CRAR; **SEBI made statutory** |
| 1993 | **Market-determined exchange rate** |
| 1995 | **Banking Ombudsman** |
| 1999 | **FEMA replaced FERA**; **IRDA Act** opened insurance |
| 2004 | FDI up to 74% in private banks |
#### 1.3 What Does NOT Belong
- **FRBM Act, 2003**: a **fiscal** reform, not a financial-sector reform.
- **Nationalisation of banks (1969, 1980)**: pre-reform.
- **ICICI Bank** is **not a nationalised bank**.
!TRAP FRBM Act 2003 is the usual odd-one-out in "which is NOT a post-1991 financial sector reform".
### 2. NARASIMHAM COMMITTEES I AND II
#### 2.1 Comparison
| Particular | Narasimham-I | Narasimham-II |
|---|---|---|
| Year | **1991** (reforms from 1992-93) | Set up **Dec 1997**, report **April 1998** |
| Official title | **Committee on the Financial System (CFS)** | **Committee on Banking Sector Reforms** |
| Chair | **M. Narasimham** (ex-RBI Governor) | M. Narasimham |
| Focus | Capital adequacy and prudential norms, **lower SLR/CRR**, less priority-sector lending, interest-rate deregulation, competition, ARCs, end dual control | **Consolidation**: merge strong banks and DFIs, close weak banks, **3-tier structure**, **CRAR 10%**, NPA management, universal banking |
- **Three tiers** (N-II): **2-3 international banks**, **8-10 national banks**, many **local banks**.
- N-II idea: **fewer and stronger banks** through mergers.
!TRAP Narasimham-I (1991) = Committee on the **Financial System**; Narasimham-II (1998) = Committee on **Banking Sector Reforms**. APPSC keys have used both names for the 1991 committee (GS 2019; G1 2022).

### 4. BASEL ACCORDS
#### 4.1 The Basel Committee and BIS
- Basel Accords are framed by the **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** under the **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)**, Basel, Switzerland.
- BCBS set up end-**1974** after the failure of **Bankhaus Herstatt**.
- **BIS est. 1930** → **world's oldest international financial organisation**; **not related to the UN** (MIGA, IFC, ICSID are World Bank Group).
#### 4.2 Basel I, II, III
| Accord | Year | Core content |
|---|---|---|
| **Basel I** | **1988** | **Credit risk only**; five risk buckets (0, 10, 20, 50, 100%); minimum capital **8% of risk-weighted assets** |
| **Basel II** | Proposed June 1999; framework **June 2004** | World standards for measuring capital adequacy; **three pillars: minimum capital requirements, supervisory review, market discipline** |
| **Basel III** | **2010** (reply to 2007-09 crisis); in India from **1 Jan 2013** | Better capital (**CET1**), **capital buffers**, **leverage ratio**, **LCR**, **NSFR**, counterparty credit risk |
- Basel III helps banks **absorb stress** and improves **risk management** and disclosure.
- **Leverage ratio** came only with **Basel III**.
#### 4.3 Basel Accords vs Basel Convention
- **Basel Convention** (**UNEP**, adopted 1989, in force 1992) = **transboundary movement of hazardous wastes**, not bank capital.
!PAIR Basel Accords | BCBS/BIS | Bank capital || Basel Convention | UNEP | Hazardous wastes
### 5. CAPITAL ADEQUACY – RBI VS BCBS NORMS
#### 5.1 Minimums
| Norm | BCBS minimum | RBI norm |
|---|---|---|
| **CET1** | **4.5%** | **5.5%** |
| **Tier 1** | **6%** | **7%** |
| **Total CRAR** | **8%** | **9%** |
| Capital conservation buffer (CCB) | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| Countercyclical buffer (CCyB) | 0-2.5% | Framework since **2015**, **not activated** (May 2026) |
| Leverage ratio | 3% | **4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for others** |
- India: CRAR introduced **1992** under BIS standards; set at **9%** under Basel I (1999). RBI's 9% is **stricter** than BCBS's 8%.
- 4.5% is the BCBS **CET1** floor, not "Tier I"; Basel III minimum **Tier 1 in India is 7%, not 8%**.
#### 5.2 Terms → Meaning
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CAR/CRAR** | Capital ÷ risk-weighted assets; measures **strength of banks' balance sheets** |
| **Tier 1** | Core equity: paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed (kept) profits |
| **Tier 2** | Mixed equity-debt, e.g. **subordinated debt** |
| **CCyB** | Checks excess credit growth; RBI 2015; main trigger = **credit-to-GDP gap** |
| **Leverage ratio** | Tier 1 capital ÷ total (non-risk-weighted) exposure; caps lending against own capital (APPSC-GS 2019) |
| **LCR ≥ 100%** | Stock of **HQLA** to survive a **30-day** stress |
| **NSFR** | Stable funding over a **1-year** horizon |
- **HQLA**: Level 1 = cash, G-secs, central-bank reserves; Level 2 = highly rated corporate bonds.
- **Macroprudential tools**: **LTV caps** (loan limited to a share of collateral value); **risk weights** raised on unsecured consumer credit (Nov 2023); liquidity ratios (LCR, NSFR).
- **Oct 2025**: RBI draft proposed **lower Basel III risk weights for MSMEs and real estate**.
!NUM LCR = 30 days | NSFR = 1 year | CCyB = credit boom | RBI CRAR 9% vs BCBS 8%

### 7. PSB REVIVAL – INDRADHANUSH, RECAPITALISATION, BBB
#### 7.1 Indradhanush (August 2015)
- Capital infusion + governance reform for PSBs; **7-point plan (A-G)**: **Appointments, Banks Board Bureau, Capitalisation, De-stressing, Empowerment, Framework of accountability, Governance**.
- **₹70,000 crore** budget capital over 4 years.
#### 7.2 Recapitalisation (October 2017)
- Package **₹2.11 lakh crore**: **₹1.35 lakh crore via recapitalisation bonds**; ~₹58,000 crore raised by banks.
- **Recap bonds**: Govt gives bonds to PSBs, money returns as equity → **no fresh cash** spent.
- **4R**: **Recognition, Resolution, Recapitalisation, Reforms**.
#### 7.3 P.J. Nayak Committee and Banks Board Bureau
- **P.J. Nayak (2014, RBI)**: weak PSB board governance; proposed a **Bank Investment Company** to hold Govt stake; stake **below 50%**; professional boards.
- **Banks Board Bureau (BBB)**: proposed by P.J. Nayak, an Indradhanush idea; working from **1 April 2016**; autonomous advisory body.
- Selects **PSB chairmen/whole-time directors** and boards of PSU insurers and FIs.
- **Replaced in 2022** by the **Financial Services Institutions Bureau (FSIB)**.
### 8. EASE REFORMS AND EASE INDEX
- **EASE = Enhanced Access and Service Excellence**: conceptualised 2015, launched **2017**; PSB Reforms Agenda from **Jan 2018**.
- Run by the **IBA's EASE Steering Committee**; PSBs report quarterly against benchmarks.
- Aims: better PSB governance and performance — **digital transformation, customer service**, risk management.
- **EASE Index**: **measures performance of PSBs**; quarterly benchmarking and ranking of mid-size and large PSBs; **not** MSME ease of doing business.
| Version | Theme |
|---|---|
| **EASE 8.0 "EASE ₹ise" (FY2026)** | **Risk and Resilience, Innovation, Socio-economic impact, Excellence** |
!TRAP APPSC G1 2024: "EASE is governed by a board of heads of premier FIs" = **FALSE**; the IBA steering committee runs it.

### 10. DEPOSIT INSURANCE AND BANKING LAWS (AMENDMENT) ACT 2025
#### 10.1 DICGC
- Set up **1978** by merging the Deposit Insurance Corporation (**1962**) and the Credit Guarantee Corporation; **DICGC Act, 1961**.
- **Wholly owned RBI subsidiary**.
- Cover **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** (raised from ₹1 lakh in **Budget 2020-21**, after the **PMC Bank** crisis).
- Covers all commercial banks (incl. **Indian branches of foreign banks**), RRBs, co-operative banks; excludes **government, inter-bank and NBFC** deposits.
- **Premium paid by the insured bank**, not the depositor.
#### 10.2 Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025
- In force **1 Aug 2025**.
| Provision | Change |
|---|---|
| "Substantial interest" threshold | **₹5 lakh → ₹2 crore** |
| Co-operative bank directors' tenure | **8 → 10 years** |
### 11. PRIVATISATION DEBATE
- **Budget 2021-22**: privatisation of **two PSBs and one general insurance company**.
- **IDBI Bank**: **LIC** bought majority stake in **2019** → RBI reclassified it as a **private sector lender**; **60.72%** stake on sale.
- **For**: efficiency. **Against**: **social mandate**; unions say it would weaken **PSL, rural credit, inclusion**.
