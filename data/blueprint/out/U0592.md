## U0592 | CONCEPT OF TIME – LOCAL AND STANDARD TIME, GMT/UTC, INDIAN STANDARD TIME, TIME ZONES, INTERNATIONAL DATE LINE
### 1. LONGITUDE AND LOCAL TIME
- Earth turns **360° in 24 h**, **west to east**, so places to the **east are ahead** (add) and places to the **west behind** (subtract). Moving west, local time falls behind.
| Longitude gap | Time gap |
|---|---|
| **1°** | **4 minutes** |
| **15°** | **1 hour** |
| 180° | 12 hours |
| 1' of arc | 4 seconds |
#### 1.1 Method
1. Longitude gap: **same side subtract**, **opposite sides add** (25°E and 35°W = 60° = 4 h).
2. Gap × 4 min = time gap.
3. **Add if east**, subtract if west.
- Flight/message: arrival local time = departure time + journey time ± longitude time (add when going east). Depart 10:00 a.m., 3-hour flight, 15° east → **2:00 p.m.**

### 2. TIME TERMS – GMT, UTC, DST
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **GMT** | Mean solar time at **Royal Observatory, Greenwich** (0°); Prime Meridian fixed at **International Meridian Conference, 1884** |
| **UTC** | World standard kept by **caesium atomic clocks**; corrected with **leap seconds** |
| **Daylight Saving Time** | Clocks +1 h in summer (mid/high latitudes) to use evening light; **India does not use it** |
| Natural units | Day (rotation), month (Moon's revolution), year (Earth's revolution) |
- Standard time is set from the Prime Meridian; **180°** = Date Line, **12 h** (720 min) from Greenwich.
- **NavIC** (7 satellites: 3 GEO + 4 GSO) uses **rubidium** clocks; IRNSS-1F clock failed.
!TRAP UTC = caesium; NavIC = rubidium.
### 3. TIME ZONES
- **24 theoretical zones**, 15° each; more in practice (half/quarter-hour offsets).
| Country / place | Fact |
|---|---|
| **France** (with overseas territories) | **12 zones – most** |
| Russia | 11 |
| UK 9, Australia 9, Denmark 5 (6 with Realm), New Zealand 5 | More than four |
| **Brazil** | Exactly 4 |
| **China** | **One zone** despite its width |
| Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai + **Colombo** | Same clock (UTC+5:30) |
| **Nepal** | UTC+5:45 |
| Accra, Dublin, Lisbon, London | GMT (not Madrid; Addis Ababa, 38°45'E, is GMT+3) |
- Ahead to behind GMT: **Japan (+9) > Iraq (+3) > Greece (+2) > Cuba (−5) > Costa Rica (−6)**.
!TRAP Most time zones = France, not Russia.
### 4. INDIAN STANDARD TIME
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Standard meridian | **82°30'E** (82.5°E) (**APPSC 2019**) |
| Reference town | **Mirzapur**, UP (old keys: Lucknow; passes Allahabad) |
| City closest | **Rewa** |
| IST vs GMT | **GMT + 5 h 30 min** (82.5 × 4 = 330 min) (**APPSC 2019**); 0000 UTC = 5:30 a.m. IST |
| States crossed (W→E) | **UP, MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh** – 5 (**not West Bengal**, APPSC 2021) |
| In AP | **Kakinada** district |
| Meets Tropic of Cancer | **Koriya district, Chhattisgarh**, Baghelkhand Plateau |
| Keeper | **CSIR-National Physical Laboratory**, New Delhi (1947) |
- History: separate **Bombay and Calcutta times** from 1884; single IST after **1947**.
#### 4.1 IST in 2026
- **Jul 2026**: **White Rabbit** IST network at Regional Reference Standards Laboratory, **Jakkur (Bengaluru)** – sub-nanosecond sync over fibre, independent of GPS; protocol by **CERN** (not ISRO); "One Nation, One Time" (NPL-ISRO). Uses: banking, payments, railways, airports, power grids, telecom; guards against GPS spoofing.
- **27 Aug 2026**: Consumer Affairs Ministry notified **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026** – IST the single legal reference; in force **180 days** after Gazette publication.
### 5. INDIA'S EXTENT AND THE TWO-TIME-ZONE DEBATE
| Extent | India | AP |

| Latitude | **8°4'N – 37°6'N** | 12°41'N – 19°07'N |

### 6. INTERNATIONAL DATE LINE
- Imaginary pole-to-pole line roughly along **180°**, in the **middle of the Pacific Ocean** (**APPSC 2021**); passes the **Bering Strait** (Chukchi Peninsula, Russia / Seward Peninsula, Alaska).
- **Zigzags** where land is in the way: Chukotka, **Aleutians**, Kiribati, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa.
| Crossing | Date change |
|---|---|
| **Eastward** (Asia → America) | **Subtract a day** (repeat) |
| **Westward** (America → Asia) | **Add a day** (skip) |
| Place | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Big Diomede** (Russia) / **Little Diomede** (USA) | Neighbours, different dates |
| **Samoa** | Moved to the Asian side in **2011** |
| **Kiribati's Line Islands** (UTC+14) | First to see a new day |
- Old key: crossing 180° east to west a day is lost, west to east gained; shortest ship path is along the meridian.

### 8. ROTATION, AXIAL TILT AND SEASONS
- Rotation (west to east) gives day and night and time differences; the **circle of illumination** divides day from night; Coriolis deflects winds right in NH.
- Axis **23½°** from the perpendicular = **66½°** to the orbital plane. **Parallelism** (angular momentum) → **opposite seasons** in two hemispheres.
- Tilt gives seasons, unequal day length, tropics and polar circles – **not** day and night.
- **Precession**: **Vega** was pole star ~14,000 years ago, again in ~12,000 years. **Milankovitch cycles** drive long-term climate change.
- Melting ice shifts mass away from the axis → slows rotation.
- To walk east, keep the **pole star on your left**. Stars rise perpendicular to the horizon at the Equator.
### 9. SOLSTICES, EQUINOXES AND DAY LENGTH
| Date | Event | Sun overhead | NH |
|---|---|---|---|
| **21 March** (20/21) | Vernal equinox | Equator | Spring; day = night |
| **21 June** (20/21) | Summer solstice | Tropic of Cancer | Longest day |
| **23 Sept** (22/23) | Autumnal equinox | Equator | Autumn; day = night |
| **22 Dec** (21/22) | Winter solstice | Tropic of Capricorn | Shortest day |
- **APPSC-G1 2019** match: winter 22 Dec, summer 21 June, vernal 21 March, autumnal 23 Sept.
- 21 June = **International Day of Yoga**. **Uttarayana**: Sun's northward movement, 23 Dec to 21 June.
- **SH longest day: 22 December** (second fortnight); NH longest: second half of June. Of the options, Delhi has the longest June day.
- Solstice: day and night unequal, biggest contrast. Winter solstice noon Sun at Tropic of Cancer: **43°**.
- 23 Sept to 21 Dec: NH days shorten. Southern summer: South Pole tilts to the Sun.
| Latitude | 21 June | Equinox | 22 Dec |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equator | 12 h | 12 h | 12 h |
| Tropic of Cancer | ~13½ h | 12 h | ~10½ h |
| Arctic Circle | 24 h day (Sun does not set) | 12 h | 24 h night |
| North Pole | Continuous day | Sunrise/sunset | Continuous night |
- North Pole lit ~21 March to ~23 September; poles get ~6 months day and 6 months night.
