## U0445 | STRUCTURAL CHANGE – SECTORAL COMPOSITION OF OUTPUT AND EMPLOYMENT
### 1. SECTOR CLASSIFICATION
#### 1.1 Three-Sector Scheme and Other Names
| Sector | Other name | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | **Agricultural** sector | Crops, livestock, forestry, **fisheries**, **mining & quarrying** |
| Secondary | **Industrial** sector | Manufacturing, electricity, gas, **water supply**, utilities, construction |
| Tertiary | **Service** sector | **Trade**, hotels, **transport**, **storage**, communication, banking, real estate, public administration and defence |
| Quaternary | **Knowledge** sector | R&D, information, software, education, consulting |
| Quinary | **Top decision-making** | Government policy, scientific research |
!PAIR Trade | Service sector | Tertiary || Water supply | Industrial sector | Secondary
- The service sector includes transport, communication and hotels – **not** mining, forestry or fishing.
#### 1.2 GVA Sector Groups (National Accounts)
| Group | Components |
|---|---|
| Agriculture and allied | Agriculture, **livestock**, forestry, fishing |
| Industry | **Mining and quarrying**, manufacturing, electricity-gas-water supply, construction |
| Services | Trade-hotels-transport-communication; finance-real estate-professional services; public administration, defence, other services |
!TRAP Mining = **primary** in the three-sector scheme, but it is counted under **Industry** in GVA groups and the AP Socio-Economic Survey.

### 3. THEORIES OF STRUCTURAL TRANSFORMATION
| Model | Author | Prediction |
|---|---|---|
| Three-sector | **Colin Clark and Allan Fisher** | As income rises, labour and output shift **primary → secondary → tertiary** |
| Dual-sector | **W. Arthur Lewis (1954)** | Surplus farm labour (near-zero marginal productivity) moves to modern industry until it runs out – the **Lewis turning point** |
- India deviates: output moved to services, but labour stayed on farms (~**46%** of workers still in agriculture).
- A **structural change** in the composition of national income has occurred – changing sector shares = structural change (APPSC-G1 2022 assertion, true).
### 4. SECTORAL SHARES OVER TIME
#### 4.1 At Independence and Across Plans
| Year | Agriculture / primary | Industry | Services |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1947 | about **50%** | small | – |
| 1950-51 | **largest**, over half | about **13%** | **30.3%** |
| 1980-81 → 1990-91 | 38.1% → 34.0% | 25.9% → 24.9% | **37.65% → 42.55%** |
| 1991 | about 29% | – | – |
| 2011-12 (old series) | 18.5% | **32.5%** (mfg 17.4%) | about 49% |
| 2017-18 (PYQ key) | about **17%** (revised 18.3%) | **29.2%** | **54.4%** |
| 2022-23 (old series) | 18.2% | 27.6% (mfg 14.3%) | **51.9%** |
| 2025-26 | **below 20%** | manufacturing stagnant | **54.3%** (Jun 2026) |
- As the economy develops, the **tertiary share rises**. Decreasing order of contribution now: **services > industry > agriculture**.
- At Independence, **agriculture** was the largest contributor to GDP (GS 2023).
#### 4.2 New GDP Series (Base 2022-23, Feb 2026)
- **2024-25, current prices (20 Mar 2026): primary 21.4%, secondary 25.8%, tertiary 52.9%** – figures to quote now.
- New series shows higher agriculture and industry shares, lower services.
- Manufacturing **14.7%** (not 14.3%): Rs 38.6 lakh crore (2023-24); built from **ASI** (factory) + **ASUSE** (unincorporated units).
!TRAP The 2024-25 primary share (21.4%) **includes mining**, so it is not comparable with the old agriculture-forestry-fishing share.
### 5. SUB-SECTOR COMPOSITION AND RANKING
| Sub-sector (old series) | 2011-12 | 2022-23 |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing | 17.4% | 14.3% |
| Fishing and aquaculture | 0.8% | 1.3% |
- Older-period ranking of GVA shares: **agriculture-forestry-fishing > trade-hotels-transport-communication > manufacturing > finance-insurance-real estate-business services**.
- Highest services share in GDP in 2006: **trade, hotels, transport and communication**; fastest-growing service, 2006-2010: **communication**.
### 6. OUTPUT VS EMPLOYMENT – THE MISMATCH
| Sector (2021-22) | Share of GDP | Share of workforce |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 21.82% | **43.96%** (largest employer) |
| Tertiary | **53.89%** | 33.80% |
- Primary = largest employer but least output; tertiary = most output.
- Manufacturing employs only **11.4%** of workers (2023-24): 1.9 crore organised + 3.3 crore unorganised.
- **MSMEs** = second-largest employer after agriculture.
### 7. SERVICES-LED GROWTH AND THE MISSING MANUFACTURING STAGE
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Missing middle** | Agriculture → services without a manufacturing-led stage |
| **Premature deindustrialisation** | Services need high skills, cannot absorb semi-skilled farm workers → **jobless growth** |
| **Premature leapfrogging** | Services take value; manufacturing fails to absorb surplus farm labour |
| Nominal vs real share | Fall in manufacturing's nominal share = **relative-price effect** (Economic Survey 2025-26); real share steady at **17-18%** |
| Share logic | A sector growing slower than GDP loses share even if output rises |
- Post-1991 growth: **service-led** – not import-, export- or agriculture-led (APPSC-GS 2018).
- **EAC-PM Chairman** (Nov 2025): services-led growth cannot last without a manufacturing base.
- India: **2.9%** of global manufacturing value added; **1.8%** of merchandise exports; **44th** on the Economic Complexity Index.
### 8. MANUFACTURING SHARE TARGETS
| Policy | Year | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **National Manufacturing Policy** | 2011 | Manufacturing **25% of GDP** by **2022**; **100 million** jobs |
| **Make in India** | **25 Sep 2014** | **25%** of GDP – deadline extended from 2022 to **2025** |
- Target not met: share fell to 14.3% (2022-23).
