## U0168 | THE NIZAM OF HYDERABAD, SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE, AND THE CESSION OF THE NORTHERN CIRCARS
### 1. ASAF JAHI DYNASTY – FOUNDER AND NIZAMS
#### 1.1 Founder
- **Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan**, title **Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I**; independent rule from **1724**, after defeating the Mughal governor **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakar Kheda (11 Oct 1724)**; kept nominal allegiance to the Mughals.
#### 1.2 Nizams and their reigns
| Nizam | Reign | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II)** | 1762–1803 | **Capital Aurangabad → Hyderabad (1763)**; Circars leased; **first Subsidiary Alliance (1798)** |
| **Sikandar Jah (Asaf Jah III)** | 1803–29 | **Secunderabad** cantonment named after him |
| **Nasir-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah IV)** | 1829–57 | **Wahabi conspiracy (1839)** – Mubariz-ud-Daula arrested; **Berar Treaty (1853)** under Dalhousie gave **Berar, Raichur, Osmanabad (Dharashiv)**; Salar Jung family rose; died 16 May 1857 |
| **Afzal-ud-Daula (Asaf Jah V)** | 1857–69 | Nizam during **1857**; title **"Star of India"** for loyalty |
| **Mir Mahbub Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VI)** | 1869–1911 | **Youngest Asaf Jahi** ruler – Nizam as an infant (APPSC 2022); Salar Jung I regent |
!TRAP Afzal-ud-Daula = 1857 + "Star of India"; **Mahbub Ali Khan = youngest**.
#### 1.3 Nizam Ali Khan – treaties with the British
| Year | Treaty / event |
|---|---|
| 1766 | **Treaty of Hyderabad** – Northern Circars leased |
| 1768 | **Treaty of Masulipatnam** – confirmed the Circars |
| 1788 | Guntur handed over |
| 1790–92 | Joined the British against Tipu; got lands from the Krishna to beyond the Pennar |
| 1798 | **Subsidiary Alliance** (Treaty of Hyderabad) |
| 12 Oct 1800 | Treaty ceding the **Ceded Districts** |
### 2. SALAR JUNG FAMILY AND 1857 AT HYDERABAD
- **Mir Turab Ali Khan** made Diwan on **31 May 1853** after the death of Siraj-ul-Mulk → **Salar Jung I**; the family rose under **Nasir-ud-Daula**.
- Served **three Nizams** (Nasir-ud-Daula, Afzal-ud-Daula, Mahbub Ali Khan); died **1883**; **abolished revenue farming**.
- **17 July 1857:** **Turrebaz Khan** attacked the Residency to free **Chida Khan**; repulsed by **Davidson**.
### 3. NORTHERN CIRCARS AND GUNTUR CIRCAR
#### 3.1 Grant and lease
| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| **1759** | **Salabat Jang** gave the British **Machilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Vakal Mannar** and part of Kondapalli |
| **1765** | **Mughal firman of Shah Alam II**, received by **Clive**, granted the Circars; **Nizam Ali refused** it at first |
| **1766** | **Treaty of Hyderabad (12 Nov 1766)**: Circars leased for **military aid or ₹9 lakh a year**; signed by envoy **Calliaud** |
| **1768** | **Treaty of Masulipatnam**: Nizam acknowledged Shah Alam's grant; Company kept the Circars for ₹9 lakh + military help |
| **18 Sep 1788** | **Guntur** handed over for **₹7 lakh**, after **Basalat Jung's** death (1782) and 6 years of talks |
- **Circars handed over (1766):** **Mustafanagar (Kondapalli), Eluru, Rajamahendravaram, Srikakulam** – **not Kurnool** (Ceded Districts, 1800).
- Modern coastal districts: Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, East and West Godavari, Krishna.
#### 3.2 The Andhra negotiator
- **Kandregula Jogipantulu** – go-between sent by the British to Nizam Ali after the 1765 grant; used the **Gollapudi pay mutiny** of the Nizam's army to propose a lease at **₹15 lakh**; the 1766 treaty fixed **₹9 lakh** (APPSC G2 2019).
- Nizam Ali made him **Majumdar of Rajamahendravaram**; British gave him **"Raja Bahadur"**.
- **Col. Ford** – negotiator for the Company's resident at Machilipatnam with Nizam Ali (APPSC Group-2 key).
#### 3.3 Guntur Circar – the distinction
- **Guntur = the fifth Circar** (**Kondaveedu / Murtazanagar**); held by Nizam Ali's brother **Basalat Jung**.
- **Main area of conflict between Nizam Ali and the British** = Guntur Circar, not the Circars as a whole.
!NUM ₹15 lakh = proposed lease; ₹9 lakh = 1766/1768 figure; ₹7 lakh = Guntur (1788)
### 4. ROAD TO 1798 AND THE HYDERABAD RESIDENCY
- Nizam beaten by the Marathas at **Kharda (1795)** → blamed **Sir John Shore's neutrality** → dismissed British battalions, relied on **French troops under Raymond** → **Wellesley** forced the alliance.
- **Resident James Kirkpatrick** reported the French battalions; built the Residency, the "Koti".
- **Resident's role:** controlled the Nizam's **foreign relations** and supervised the **subsidiary force**.
### 5. SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE – AIMS, TERMS, EFFECTS
#### 5.1 Author and aims
- **Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)**, who came in 1798 amid the struggle with France (fear of **Napoleon**); he did **not invent** the method but gave it its **final shape**.
- **Aims:** counter **French influence**; **British paramountcy without direct administration**; large army at the states' cost – military and political dominance, **not a fixed income**.
#### 5.2 Terms
1. Accept a **British Resident** at the court.
2. **Surrender foreign affairs**; no dealings with other states without Company consent.
3. Maintain a **permanent subsidiary force**, paid in cash or by **ceding territory**.
4. **Employ no other Europeans** without Company consent.
5. In return the Company **protects the borders**.
#### 5.3 Effects
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Sovereignty | Lost; foreign policy gone |
| Finances | Drained → **territorial cessions** (Nizam's Ceded Districts) |
| Government | Rulers dependent, neglected government → pretext for annexation |

| 5 | **Peshwa** (Treaty of **Bassein**) | **31 Dec 1802** |
| 6 | **Bhonsle** (Nagpur/Berar) | Dec 1803 |
| 7 | **Scindia** (Gwalior) | Feb 1804 |

- **First state = Hyderabad (1798)** (APPSC G2 2002, 2008; G1 2004).
- Some keys (IAS 1994) give **Nawab of Oudh** as first: Shuja-ud-Daulah accepted a subsidiary force after 1765; Awadh was a Company-protected buffer.
- **Refused:** **Tipu Sultan** → Fourth Mysore War; **Holkar of Indore** resisted longest – the **last Maratha** to sign (1818).
!TRAP First under **Wellesley's system** = Hyderabad, not Awadh.
### 7. CEDED DISTRICTS AND ANDHRA CONSEQUENCES
- By the treaty of **12 October 1800** the Nizam ceded lands gained from Mysore (1792, 1799): **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary**, in lieu of the subsidy = **Dattamandalalu (Ceded Districts)**.
- Name **Rayalaseema** proposed by **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao (1928)**; approved at the **Andhra Mahasabha, Nandyal**, presided by **Dr S. Radhakrishnan**.
- **10 July 1794 – Padmanabham:** Company defeated **Chinna Vijayaramaraju** of Vizianagaram; Chandurti (1758) – Conflans beaten.
- **1801:** **Nellore and Chittoor** from the **Carnatic Nawab** → whole Andhra under the Company.
!KEY Northern Circars + Ceded Districts ≈ **Coastal Andhra + Rayalaseema**; **Telangana stayed under the Nizam**.
### 8. POLICIES AND THEIR AUTHORS
| Policy | Author | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| **Ring-fence** | **Warren Hastings** | **Buffer states** to protect Company frontiers |
| **Subsidiary Alliance** | **Wellesley (1798)** | Political and military control of states |
| **Subordinate isolation** | **Lord Hastings (1813–23)** | Each state bound separately to the Company |
| **Doctrine of Lapse** | **Dalhousie (from 1848)** | Annex states without a natural male heir; adopted heirs barred |
| Subordinate union | Crown, after 1858 | Princes = **"breakwaters to the storm"** |
| Dhamma | Ashoka | Moral and social harmony |
| Blood and Iron | Balban | Strong monarchy |
| Market Control | Alauddin Khalji | Large army at lower cost |
| Blue Water | Francisco de Almeida | Portuguese naval supremacy |
| Non-Alignment | Nehru | Independent foreign policy |
- **Annexation policy = Doctrine of Lapse** (APPSC 2018).
- **Awadh = classic ring-fence buffer** after Buxar (1764); Hastings sold **Kora and Allahabad** to its Nawab; it shielded Bengal from the **Marathas and Afghans**.
- Chronology: Permanent Settlement (1793) → Subsidiary Alliance → Doctrine of Lapse → **Partition of Bengal (last)**.
!TRAP **Warren Hastings = ring-fence**; **Lord Hastings = subordinate isolation**.
### 9. PARAMOUNTCY AND THE RAJPUT ALLIANCES
- **Rajput treaties (1817–18)** – Jodhpur, Jaipur, Bundi – under **Lord Hastings**; negotiator **Charles Metcalfe**.
- **Aims:** protection from **Marathas and Pindaris**; accept British paramountcy / sovereignty; isolate them from the Maratha powers.
- **Grounds for intervention:** states had no external life; British gave effect to their **international commitments** (APPSC G2 2017); also **succession disputes** and **misgovernment**.
- **After 1858:** annexation stopped; **right of adoption** recognised.
### 10. DALHOUSIE'S ANNEXATIONS
| State | Year | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| Sindh | 1843 | Conquest (**Ellenborough**, 1842–44) |
| **Satara** | **1848** | **Lapse – the first** |
| Jaitpur | 1849 | Lapse |
| **Sambalpur** (Orissa) | 1849 | Lapse |
| **Punjab** | **1849** | Conquest, Second Anglo-Sikh War; **Duleep Singh** deposed |
| **Baghat** | 1850 | Lapse |
| Udaipur (Chhattisgarh, not Mewar) | 1852 | Lapse |
| Lower Burma (Pegu) | 1852 | Conquest |
| **Jhansi** | **1853** | Lapse – Raja Gangadhar Rao died; adopted Damodar Rao not recognised |
| Berar | 1853 | From the Nizam for **subsidy arrears** |
| **Nagpur** | **1854** | Lapse – Raghuji III died heirless |
| **Awadh** | **Feb 1856** | **Misgovernment** – **Wajid Ali Shah**; reports of **Sleeman and Outram** |
- **Lapse order:** Satara → Sambalpur → Baghat → Udaipur → Jhansi → Nagpur.
- Titles and pensions of the rulers of **Tanjore and the Carnatic** abolished.
- **Not lapse:** Karnataka (Carnatic), **Jaipur** (Jaitpur was); **Gwalior** never annexed.
- Lapse and Awadh angered rulers, taluqdars and sepoys → **Revolt of 1857**.
