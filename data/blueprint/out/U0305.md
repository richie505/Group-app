## U0305 | PRESIDING OFFICERS – SPEAKER, DEPUTY SPEAKER, CHAIRMAN; SECRETARIAT; SESSIONS, ADJOURNMENT, PROROGATION, DISSOLUTION
### 1. PRESIDING OFFICERS AT A GLANCE
| Office | Chosen | Resigns to | Removal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Speaker** | LS from its members (**Art 93**); date by the **President** | **Deputy Speaker** (Art 94(b)) | **Effective majority**, **14 days'** notice (**Art 94(c)**) |
| **Deputy Speaker** | LS from its members; date by the **Speaker** (Rule 8(1)) | **Speaker** | Same |
| **Chairman (RS)** | **VP** ex officio (Arts 64, 89(1)) | – | Only by removal as VP |
| **Deputy Chairman** | RS from its members (Art 89(2)) | **Chairman** | RS effective majority, 14 days' notice (**Art 90(c)**) |
- Resignations go **laterally**, not upward to the President or PM.
- **No separate oath** for Speaker, Deputy Speaker or Deputy Chairman – only the oath as an **MP**.
- **14 days' notice**: removal of the **President, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Deputy Chairman**. The **Governor** is removed by the President at will, without notice.
- Salaries of Speaker and Deputy Speaker: **charged on the Consolidated Fund** (**Art 97**).
### 2. SPEAKER – ELECTION, OFFICE, REMOVAL
#### 2.1 Election and status
- Speaker's provisions: **Arts 93–96** (election, vacation, removal), **100** (casting vote), **110** (Money Bill), **118** (joint sitting rules).
- **Art 93**: chosen by the House from **among its members** – must already be an **elected MP**; the six-month grace is only for **Ministers**.
- **LS Secretariat** works directly under the Speaker's control.
#### 2.2 Vacation and dissolution
- Vacates on ceasing to be a member (Art 94).
- On **dissolution**, the Speaker **does not vacate** – continues till **just before the first meeting** of the new LS; the **Deputy Speaker vacates at once**. States: **Art 179** same rule for the State Speaker.
- Removal only by an LS resolution passed by a **majority of all the then members** (not simple, not two-thirds).
#### 2.3 Own removal resolution (Article 96)
| Situation | Presides | Speaks | Votes first instance | Casting vote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal sitting | Yes | Yes | **No** | **Yes** (Art 100(1)) |
| Own removal resolution | **No**, even if present | Yes | **Yes** | **No** |
- **Chairman (RS)** during his own removal: cannot preside and **cannot vote**, but may speak.
!TRAP Removal pending: Speaker **can vote** (first instance); Chairman **cannot**.
#### 2.4 Pro-Tem Speaker (Article 95(1))
| Basis | Regular Speaker | Pro-Tem Speaker |
|---|---|---|
| Article | 93 | **95(1)** |
| Chosen by | Elected by LS | **Appointed and sworn in by the President** – usually the **senior-most** member (list sent to the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs) |
| Role | Presides; enforces rules | **Administers oath** to new MPs; holds the Speaker's election |
| Tenure | Through dissolution | Ends when the regular Speaker is elected |
### 3. SPEAKER'S POWERS
| Power | Article / basis |
|---|---|
| **Casting vote** only in a tie | **Art 100(1)** |
| **Decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill** – final | **Art 110(3)** |
| **Certificate** endorsed on every Money Bill sent to RS and the President | **Art 110(4)** |
| **Presides over joint sitting** (summoned by President, Art 108) | **Art 118(4)** |
| **Anti-defection** decisions | Tenth Schedule – subject to judicial review (**Kihoto Hollohan, 1992**) |
| Adjourns/suspends sitting without **quorum** (one-tenth) | Art 100(3) |
| **Recognises Leader of Opposition** – party needs **one-tenth** of House strength; LoP statutory since the **1977 Act** | Speaker's direction |
| Fixes Deputy Speaker's election date | Rule 8(1) |
| Rulings protected from courts | Art 122 |
- Money Bill decision cannot be questioned by any House or the President; the RS cannot overrule it.
- Resigned Minister makes a statement in LS with the **Speaker's permission**; cannot be forced to.
!KEY President appoints the Pro-Tem Speaker; **adjournment sine die**, Tenth Schedule decisions and the Deputy Speaker's election date are the **Speaker's**.
### 4. DEPUTY SPEAKER AND PANEL OF CHAIRPERSONS
#### 4.1 Deputy Speaker
- Elected by LS from its members (Art 93); date fixed by the Speaker, told to members by the **Secretary-General**.
- **From the Opposition** – only a **convention**, not constitutional.
- **Art 95**: does the Speaker's work when the office is vacant or he is absent.
- In the Chair: same powers as the Speaker – **no appeal to the Speaker** against his ruling; **not subordinate** to the Speaker.
- Out of the Chair: an ordinary member – debates, moves motions, **votes in the first instance**.
- Own removal: cannot preside; may speak and vote in first instance; no casting vote.
- Automatically **chairman of any committee** he is appointed to.
- Joint sitting order (**Art 118(4)**): **Speaker → Deputy Speaker → RS Deputy Chairman**.
- LS Deputy Speaker post **vacant since June 2019** – through the 17th and 18th Lok Sabhas; also vacant in **8 State Assemblies** (Art 178), Jharkhand for **21 years** (Jun 2026).
#### 4.2 Panels
- **Panel of Chairpersons**: nominated by the **Speaker**, up to **ten**; a member presides when both Speaker and Deputy Speaker are **absent**, **not when the Speaker's post is vacant**.
- RS: **Panel of Vice-Chairpersons** nominated by the Chairman.
### 5. CHAIRMAN AND DEPUTY CHAIRMAN OF THE RAJYA SABHA
- **VP** is ex-officio Chairman; has a **casting vote** only; is not a member of RS.
- When the VP **acts as President**, he draws the President's salary and the **Deputy Chairman** does the Chairman's work (**Art 91**).
- **Jan 2026 (Justice Varma case)**: SC upheld the Deputy Chairman's power under **Art 91** to decide a judge-removal motion when the Chairman's office was vacant.

### 7. SESSIONS – ARTICLE 85
- **Art 85(1)**: President summons each House at such time and place as he thinks fit; the Houses may be summoned **separately**.
- **Maximum gap six months** – from the **last sitting of one session** to the **first sitting of the next** → at least **two sessions a year** (APPSC G2 2017).
- **Not in the Constitution**: number of sessions (Budget, Monsoon, Winter are a **convention**; Budget, Feb–May, longest) or **minimum sitting days** (not 60, 90 or 100).
- **Session**: first sitting → prorogation or dissolution. **Recess**: prorogation → next session.
- **Quorum**: **one-tenth** of total members, **including the presiding officer** – **Art 100(3)** (casting vote is 100(1)).
- States: **Art 174** – same six-month rule; State Assemblies averaged only **24 sittings in 2025** (Odisha 43, Nagaland 7).
- **Monsoon Session 2026**: only **2 oral answers** in LS – low scrutiny.
### 8. ENDING A SITTING OR SESSION
| Device | By whom | Ends | Effect on business |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | Presiding Officer | A sitting, for hours/days/weeks | Session unaffected; business stays |
| **Adjournment sine die** | Presiding Officer | Sitting, no date fixed | Precedes prorogation; **bills continue** (APPSC 2025) |
| **Prorogation** – **Art 85(2)(a)** | **President** on CoM advice (Governor in States) | **The session**, not the House | **Bills do not lapse**; pending **notices lapse**, except notices to introduce bills |
| **Dissolution** – **Art 85(2)(b)** | **President** – LS only | **Life of the House** | Pending business **lapses** (subject to **Art 107**); RS is permanent |
- Sequence: adjournment sine die → prorogation → recess → new session.
!PAIR Adjournment / sine die | Presiding officer | Sitting || Prorogation / dissolution | President | Session / House
### 9. PRESIDENT'S ADDRESS – ARTICLES 86 AND 87
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **86** | President may address **either House or both** at any time, require attendance, and send **messages** on pending Bills |
| **87** | **Special address** at the start of the **first session after each general election** and the **first session of each year** – not after every prorogation |
- Outlines government policy; prepared by the **Union Cabinet**; debated on a **Motion of Thanks**.
### 10. PARLIAMENT SECRETARIAT – ARTICLE 98
- **Art 98(1)**: **separate secretarial staff** for each House; **common posts** may be created; Parliament may regulate recruitment by law.
- **Art 98(3)**: until such law, rules by the **President after consulting the Speaker/Chairman**; appointment orders given by the Speaker.
- Head: **Secretary-General** of each House – appointed by the **presiding officer**, not the Union Government; rank of **Cabinet Secretary**.
- **Independent of the executive**: LS Secretariat under the Speaker, RS Secretariat under the Chairman.

### 13. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. Speaker resigns to the **Deputy Speaker**, not the President.
2. Removal needs an **effective majority** and **14 days'** notice – not two-thirds.
3. Speaker continues after dissolution; Deputy Speaker does not.
4. During own removal: Speaker votes in first instance, **no casting vote**; Chairman cannot vote.
5. Pro-Tem Speaker is **appointed by the President** (Art 95(1)), not elected.
6. Speaker's Money Bill decision **final – 110(3)**; certificate **110(4)**.
7. Quorum **100(3)**; casting vote **100(1)**.
8. Prorogation: bills **do not lapse**; dissolution: bills lapse.
9. Deputy Speaker from Opposition is only a **convention**.
10. Panel member cannot preside when the Speaker's **office is vacant**.
