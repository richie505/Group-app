## U0556 | BIFURCATION AND AP'S STATE REVENUE – REVENUE DEFICIT, LOSS OF HYDERABAD, RESOURCE GAP
### 1. LOSS OF HYDERABAD REVENUES
- **Hyderabad region (with Rangareddy and Medak)** gave **nearly 50%** of undivided AP's **tax and non-tax revenue**.
- Revenue lost: **corporate taxes, stamp duties, commercial taxes** (not agricultural land revenue).
| Asset lost | Effect |
|---|---|
| HITEC City, Financial District | Loss of IT/ITeS exports |
| Genome Valley pharma cluster; corporate and bank head offices; commercial real estate; MSME supply chains | **Narrower tax base** (GST, stamp duty, motor-vehicle tax) |
| Loss of Hyderabad as a whole | **Structural revenue deficit** |
- Residual AP kept its **agricultural base** – that was not lost.
- Division followed the **population ratio 58.32 : 41.68** (AP : Telangana).
!KEY Hyderabad region ≈ 50% of undivided AP's revenue → structural revenue deficit for residual AP.
### 2. RESOURCE GAP OF THE FIRST YEAR
#### 2.1 Meaning
- **Appointed day = 2 June 2014**; the Act got presidential **assent on 1 March 2014**.
- **Resource gap** = **revenue deficit** (revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts) for the **first ten months** (2 June 2014 – 31 March 2015).
- Method difference: the **Centre** used standardised accounting and **excluded new State schemes** (e.g. farm-loan waiver); **AP** counted its **actual expenditure**.
!TRAP The State was divided on 2 June 2014, not "2 March 2014".
#### 2.2 Competing estimates for 2014-15
| Estimate by | Resource gap 2014-15 |
|---|---|
| **Union Finance Ministry** | **₹4,118 crore** |
| **CAG** (2 Jun 2014 – 31 Mar 2015) | **₹13,776 crore** (APPSC 2016) |
| **State (AP)** | **₹16,078 crore** |
- Bifurcation-year gap: **more than ₹15,000 crore** (APPSC 2022 key).
- CAG figure is not ₹20,745 / 16,211 / 21,481 crore (distractors).
### 3. DEMANDS TO THE CENTRE
#### 3.1 Special Category Status
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu** demanded **Special Category Status (SCS)** citing post-bifurcation revenue loss (APPSC 2018).
- SCS for **5 years** was promised on the floor of the **Rajya Sabha (2014)**.
- Under SCS the **Centre bears 90%** of CSS costs (ordinary States 60%) plus tax breaks.
- Centre's refusal: the **14th FC** ended the general/special category distinction and raised devolution **32% → 42%**.
- Chronology: **APRA (2014) → 14th FC award (2015) → TDP leaves NDA over SCS (2018)**.
#### 3.2 APRA sections behind the demands
| Provision | Demand / grant |
|---|---|
| **Sec. 94(1)** | **Tax holidays** |
| **Sec. 46** | **Backward-area packages** |
| **Sec. 94(3)** | **₹2,500 crore** for capital buildings |
- Other demands: revenue deficit grants; full Polavaram funding at revised cost; capital-city grants.
### 4. CENTRAL ASSISTANCE AND FINANCE COMMISSIONS
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| RD grant for 2014-15 | **₹3,979 crore**, released in instalments up to 2016-17 |
| **Special Assistance Measure (SAM)** | Centre funds **90% of externally aided projects** – alternative to SCS |
| **14th FC** (from 2015-16) | Normative, static post-devolution gap; RD grants **₹22,113 crore (2015-20)** |
| **16th FC** | **Ended RD grants for all States**; AP's divisible-pool share rose to **4.22%** |
| Amaravati | **₹15,000 crore** special assistance fast-tracked (January 2026) |
- **Central institutions**: **IIT Tirupati, IIM Visakhapatnam, NIT Tadepalligudem, AIIMS Mangalagiri** (11 in all).
- AP says actual deficits keep exceeding the 14th FC's static projections.
!PAIR SCS | 90% of CSS costs | Not given || SAM | 90% of externally aided projects | Given instead

### 6. FRBM BREACHES, DEBT AND CAG REMARKS
- **Accounts at a Glance 2024-25** (Principal AG; tabled 6 Mar 2026): **RD 3.75% vs FRBM target 2.70%**; **FD 5.05% vs 4% ceiling**; borrowings ₹81,071 crore.
- **Debt (2024-25 RE): ₹5,64,488 crore = 35.15% of GSDP**; 2025-26: ₹6,35,177 crore (36.04%); market loans 75% of debt.
- **Off-budget borrowings** (CAG): ₹1,18,394 crore (Mar 2022) → **₹1.28 lakh crore = 9.7% of GSDP (Mar 2023)**.
- **Guarantees** (2023-24): ₹1,54,797 crore (11% of GSDP).
!TRAP 2024-25: 3.75% / 5.05% (Accounts at a Glance) vs 3.79% / 5.11% (Finance Accounts).
### 7. WELFARE AND COMMITTED SPENDING
- 2024-25 RE: **revenue spending ₹2,24,343 crore** vs **capital outlay only ₹24,072 crore**.
- AP blames shrinking revenue base, interest, welfare and the loss of a capital city.
- Commercial tax collections +16% in June 2026 (Q1 FY27: ₹15,323 crore).
### 8. POWER SECTOR – REFORMS, DISCOMS AND DUES
- **Department of Expenditure** borrowing window: FD of **3.5% of GSDP + 0.5%** conditional on **power-sector reforms**.
- **February 2021**: AP became the **second State** to complete these reforms (APPSC 2023).
- **UDAY**: AP signed on **24 June 2016** – the **13th State**; State took over about **₹11,000 crore** of DISCOM debt.
- CAG on power subsidy: spending at **121% (2023)** and **120% (2024)** of budget estimates.
- **Telangana power dues**: on 29 Aug 2022 the Union Power Ministry (Sec. 92, APRA) ordered Telangana to pay about **₹6,756 crore** to APGENCO; the **Telangana High Court quashed** the order (Oct 2023).
