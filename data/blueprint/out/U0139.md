## U0139 | MUGHAL PAINTING, MUSIC, LANGUAGE AND LITERATURE; PERSIAN-SANSKRIT TRANSLATION MOVEMENT
- **Exams:** Group 1 Prelims / Group 2 Screening | **CORE**
- **Rows:** A-116 Mughal painting, music, language and literature; Persian-Sanskrit translation movement; S1-52 Mughal language, literature and painting; decline of the Mughal Empire
- **PYQ importance:** APPSC 8 questions | other exams 100
### 1. MUGHAL PAINTING – EMPEROR-WISE EVOLUTION
- Evolution: **Persian miniature → realistic, colourful Indian style**.
- **Humayun** laid the foundation: brought the Persian masters **Mir Sayyid Ali** (of Tabriz) and **Khwaja Abdus Samad** → founded the Mughal school (APPSC G2 2025, statement a).
- **Jahangir's reign = golden period / zenith** of Mughal painting; **Aurangzeb withdrew patronage** and painting declined.
#### 1.1 Emperor → Emphasis
| Emperor | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Humayun | Foundation through the two Persian masters Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad |
| Akbar | **Illustrated manuscripts**; **Tasvir Khana** atelier (painting karkhana) of **100+ painters** (Daswant, Basawan, Mukund); Hamzanama, Tutinama, Akbarnama, Razmnama; 3-D figures and realism |
| Jahangir | **Zenith**: portraits, **albums**, flora and fauna, naturalism and precision; European influence; introduced the **halo (nimbus)** behind the emperor's head |
| Shah Jahan | Formal **court scenes**, albums (**muraqqas**), floral borders, heavy use of **gold** |
| Aurangzeb | Withdrew patronage → painters moved to **Rajasthan, Punjab Hills and Bihar**, fuelling Rajput and Pahari schools |
- Akbar set up painting karkhanas at **Agra, Fatehpur Sikri and Lahore**.
- **Jahangir** moved the focus from large illustrated manuscripts to **albums and individual portraits**: events of his life, birds, flowers, animals; he had his own studio and supported European and Persian art.
#### 1.2 European Influence (NEW angle)
- European pictures reached **Akbar's court through the Portuguese** → **portraiture** and the depiction of **smaller distant objects for spatial depth**.
- Mughal style = muted, naturalistic colour, **perspective and shading** (European influence), cosmopolitan.
!KEY APPSC G2 2025: Humayun brought the Persian masters + Jahangir's reign = golden age – both statements correct.

### 3. REGIONAL PAINTING SCHOOLS
| School | Region / features |
|---|---|
| **Rajput** | Rajasthan kingdoms; Hindu epics, love legends, daily life; **bold, vivid colours; flat, 2-D**; indigenous, decorative |
| **Pahari** | North-western Himalayan hill states: Himachal, Jammu and Kashmir, Tehri/Paudi Garhwal, Almora; **17th-19th c.** |
| **Kangra** | Himalayan region; peaked under **Raja Sansar Chand** |
| **Kishangarh** | Rajasthan; patron **Raja Sawant Singh (Nagari Das)**, throne 1748; **Bani Thani**; Radha-Krishna theme |
| **Deccani** | Began in the **Bahmani** court, continued in successor states; at Golconda founder **Mir Hashim** (later invited to the Mughal court); heavy gold |
| **Kalighat** | Native, later Bengal style – **not shaped by Mughal painting** |
- Mughal painting **influenced** the Pahari, Rajasthani and Kangra schools.
- **Bani Thani**: by **Nihal Chand**; inspired by Sawant Singh's poem ***Bihari Jas Chandrika***. Other Kishangarh artists: Amirchand, Chhotu, Bhawanidas, Sitaram. Kishangarh rulers supported the **Nimbarka** sect; Sawant Singh wrote verses praising Krishna.
- Deccan's first coloured miniatures: historian **Sherwani** – the **14 miniatures** (including the Battle of Rakshasi-Tangadi).
- Qutb Shahi Deccani painting (Mir Hashim) = a school independent of the Mughal style.
!TRAP Which school was NOT affected by Mughal painting? **Kalighat**.
### 4. MUSIC AT THE MUGHAL COURT
#### 4.1 Musicians
| Musician | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Tansen** | Original name **Ramtanu Pande** (not Makaranda Pande); from Gwalior; disciple of **Swami Haridas**; first at the court of **Raja Ramchandra of Rewa (Bhata/Bandhavgarh)** – Akbar sent a firman for him; one of Akbar's **Navaratnas**; **dhrupad**; tomb at **Gwalior** |
| Tansen's titles | **'Tansen'** from **Raja Vikramjit of Gwalior** (not Akbar); **'Mian'** from **Akbar** |
| Tansen's ragas | **Mian ki Todi, Mian ki Malhar, Darbari Kanada** |
| **Baiju Bawra** | Dhrupad singer at the court of **Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior** |
| Baz Bahadur | **Last Sultan of Malwa** (1555-1562), musician and poet; romance with **Rupmati**; defeated by Akbar's general Adham Khan; later submitted and served Akbar as a court musician |
#### 4.2 Form → Court / Patron
| Form | Court / patron |
|---|---|
| **Dhrupad** | Origin in Vedic chanting; began with **Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior (1486-1516)** – before the Mughals; Tansen and Baiju were masters |
| **Khayal** | **Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela'**; composers **Sadarang and Adarang** |
| **Tappa** | Grew at Muhammad Shah's court (UPPSC 2023); refined by **Shori Miyan**, late 18th c. (Awadh/Lucknow) |
- Tarana, Dadra and Ghazal also grew at Muhammad Shah's court.
- **Mal Gurjari** = a **raga** invented by Raja Man Singh Tomar, not a person. Greats: Man Singh Tomar, Tansen, Sadarang-Adarang.
- **Sitar** = Persian long-necked lute (**setar**) + Indian **veena**.
- Non-Islamic vocal style (as per the answer key): **Taraka**.
- **Aurangzeb** ended court music, yet **played the veena**; many Persian works on classical music date from his reign.

### 6. TRANSLATION MOVEMENT – PERSIAN WORK → ORIGINAL → TRANSLATOR
#### 6.1 Akbar's Translation Department
- Called the **Maktab Khana**, at **Fatehpur Sikri**; headed by **Faizi**.
- Translated **Sanskrit, Arabic, Turkish, Greek** and other works into **Persian**.

### 7. DARA SHIKOH AND HINDI POETS
#### 7.1 Dara Shikoh – Sufism-Vedanta Synthesis
- **Shah Jahan's eldest son**; **Qadiri** Sufi, linked with **Miyan Mir** and **Muhibullah Shah**.
- **Majma-ul-Bahrain** ("Mingling of the Two Oceans") – common ground of **Sufism and Vedanta**.
- **Sirr-i-Akbar** ("The Great Secret", 1657) – Persian translation of about 52 Upanishads; its Latin version (**Anquetil-Duperron**) took the Upanishads to Europe.
#### 7.2 Hindi Poets → Works and Patrons
- **Akbar's reign = golden era of Hindi poetry** (his tolerance led to a surge).
| Poet | Works | Patron / period |
|---|---|---|
| **Tulsidas** | **Ramcharitmanas** (Awadhi devotional poem), **Vinay Patrika** | Contemporary of Akbar and Jahangir; not a court poet |
| Surdas | **Sur Sagar** | Akbar's age |
| **Keshavdas** | **Ramchandrika**, Kavipriya, Rasikpriya, Ratan Bavani, Chhandamala | **Bundela court of Orchha** |
| **Kavindracharya Saraswati** | ***Kavindra-kalpalata*** (Hindi) | Shah Jahan; got him to **cancel the pilgrim tax at Banaras and Prayag** |
| **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khana** | Hindi **dohas**; Persian Baburnama | Akbar (Navaratna); a Muslim scholar with a big role in Hindi literature |
| Padmakar | **Ram Rasayan**, Ganga Lahari, Prabodh Pachasa | Later poet |
- **Faizi** = Persian poet with Sufi themes.
!TRAP MPPSC 2025: **Ram Rasayan is NOT by Keshavdas** – it is by Padmakar.
### 8. LANGUAGES – PERSIAN, URDU/REKHTA AND DECCANI
- **Persian = Mughal court language**; it had replaced Sanskrit and Arabic (from the Sultanate) as the language of administration, diplomacy and high culture.
- **Urdu** (Hindavi → Rekhta, Lashkari, Urdu) grew out of **military camps and bazaars**; Perso-Arabic script; mix of Persian, Arabic, Turkic, local Prakrits and Braj. Later patronised by princely courts (**Delhi, Lucknow**).
- **Deccani/Dakhni Urdu**: lingua franca of Deccan camps and bazaars; grew under the **Qutb Shahis**.
| Person / era | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Mulla Khiali** | First Deccani poetry in Telangana |
| **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** | **First Urdu poet with a diwan**; built the Charminar |
| **Abdullah Qutb Shah's era** | **Golden Age of Urdu** |
| Ghavvasi | Tuti Nama (1639), from the Sanskrit Shuka Saptati |
#### 8.1 AP Angle – Qutb Shahi Culture
- Qutb Shahi official language **Persian**, people's language **Telugu**; Deccani painting and Deccani Urdu flourished.
- **Kuchipudi** grew under **Siddhendra Yogi**; **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** granted Kuchipudi village to its dancers.
- **Aurangzeb annexed Golconda on 3 October 1687** as the 21st Suba; Nizam-ul-Mulk took part in the siege.
- Translation + painting + Hindavi/Urdu = **composite Indo-Persian culture**.
### 9. LATER MUGHALS AND DECLINE
#### 9.1 Chronology of Later Mughals
| Emperor | Reign | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Bahadur Shah I** | 1707-1712 | First after Aurangzeb |
| **Jahandar Shah** | 1712-1713 | Killed by Farrukhsiyar with the Sayyid Brothers |
| **Farrukhsiyar** | 1713-1719 | Puppet of Sayyid Brothers; deposed 1719 |
| Rafi-ud-Darajat, Rafi-ud-Daulah | 1719 | Short puppet reigns |
| **Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela'** | 1719-1748 | Nadir Shah's invasion; khayal, tappa |
| **Shah Alam II** | 1759-1806 | **Treaty of Allahabad** – Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa to the British after Buxar; first Mughal on a Company pension |
| **Bahadur Shah Zafar (II)** | 1837-1857 | **Last Mughal**; Urdu poet; led the **1857 revolt**; exiled to **Rangoon**, died 1862 |
#### 9.2 Causes of Decline
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Aurangzeb's **Deccan wars** | Last **~27 years** in the Deccan against the Marathas → drained treasury |
| Religious policy | **Jizya revived 1679** → alienated Rajputs, Marathas, Sikhs |
| **Sayyid Brothers** (kingmakers) | **Abdullah Khan and Husain Ali Khan** controlled Jahandar Shah's fall and Farrukhsiyar; removed with **Nizam-ul-Mulk's** help |
| Jagirdari crisis | Noble factions (Turani, Irani, Hindustani) |
| Others | Weak successors, regional powers, Marathas, foreign invasions |
#### 9.3 Successor States → Founder and Mughal Post
| State | Founder | Mughal post / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyderabad** | **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I** | Subahdar of the Deccan; autonomous **1724** after defeating Mubariz Khan at **Shakarkheda**; never openly declared independence |
| **Awadh** | **Saadat Khan Burhan-ul-Mulk** | Governor **1722**; title Burhan-ul-Mulk from Muhammad Shah |
| **Bengal** | **Murshid Quli Khan** | Diwan; recognised by Farrukhsiyar as Nawab Nazim **1717**; merged diwan and subahdar offices |
| **Arcot (Carnatic)** | **Saadatullah Khan** | Nawab of the Carnatic; made the office hereditary, named nephew **Dost Ali** without the Nizam's approval |
#### 9.4 Invasions of Nadir Shah and Ahmad Shah Abdali
- **Nadir Shah** (Afsharid ruler of Iran) defeated Muhammad Shah at the **Battle of Karnal, 24 February 1739**; entered Delhi 20 March 1739 and sacked it; carried away the **Peacock Throne** (built by Shah Jahan) and the **Koh-i-Noor**.
- **Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)**, a commander of Nadir Shah, took the Afghan throne in 1747; invaded north India repeatedly **(1748-1767)**, plundering down to Delhi and Mathura; won the **Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761)** over the Marathas.
