## U0319 | TRIBUNALS – ARTICLES 323A AND 323B; ADMINISTRATIVE AND SPECIALISED TRIBUNALS; TRIBUNAL REFORMS
### 1. TRIBUNALS – NATURE AND CONSTITUTIONAL PLACE
- **Quasi-judicial** bodies: neither executive body nor court; set up to cut **pendency** and bring **domain expertise**.
- Placed in **Part XIV-A** (Articles **323-A** and **323-B**), inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s advice.
- **75th Amendment, 1994** added the **rent and tenancy** clause (h) to 323-B.
- Chairpersons get the status of a **High Court judge**.
| Part | Subject |
|---|---|
| IX-A | Municipalities (243-P–243-ZG) |
| IX-B | Co-operative Societies (243-ZH–243-ZT) |
| X | Scheduled and Tribal Areas |
| XI | Union–State relations |
| XIV | Services (308–323) |
| XIV-A | **Tribunals** |
!TRAP Tribunals are in Part XIV-A, not Part XI or Part X.
### 2. ARTICLE 323-A VS ARTICLE 323-B
| Basis | Article 323-A | Article 323-B |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects | Only **public service matters**: recruitment and service conditions of Union, State, local-authority and statutory-body employees (also PSUs) | **Taxation**, foreign exchange, industrial disputes and labour, **land reforms**, urban land ceiling, **foodstuffs**, **elections** to Parliament and State Legislatures, **rent and tenancy** |
| Who creates | **Parliament only** (Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985) | **Parliament and State Legislatures** (States within their competence) |
| Structure | One CAT + State tribunals; **no hierarchy** | **Hierarchy** allowed (original + appellate) |
!PAIR 323-A | service matters | Parliament only || 323-B | other subjects | Parliament + States
### 3. ADMINISTRATIVE ADJUDICATION – MERITS AND CRITICISM
- **Merits:** expertise, specialisation, speed, low cost; follow **natural justice**, not the full **Code of Civil Procedure**.
- **Criticism:** run by the **same ministries** whose decisions they review; executive control via the **Finance Act 2017** and the 2021 Ordinance.
- **R. Gandhi (2010):** members performing judicial functions must be **independent of the executive**.
### 4. CENTRAL ADMINISTRATIVE TRIBUNAL (CAT)
#### 4.1 Basis and Setting Up
- Legal basis: **Article 323A** + **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985**; statutory, quasi-judicial; set up by the **Central Government**.
- Set up in **1985** under PM **Rajiv Gandhi** (not Lal Bahadur Shastri, UPSC 2009). **First tribunal under Art. 323A.**
#### 4.2 Composition and Data
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Members | From both **judicial and administrative** streams (APPSC-G1 2022) |
| Strength | **69** = **35 Judicial** (incl. Chairperson) + **34 Administrative** |
| Judicial members | Qualified as **HC judges** |
| Administrative members | **Additional Secretary** or above |
| Benches | Main bench **New Delhi** + **18** benches |
| Disposal rate | **92.9%** |
#### 4.3 Jurisdiction and Procedure
- **Original jurisdiction** over service matters of All-India Services, Central civil services, civil posts under the Centre, **civilian defence employees**, notified PSUs.
- Not for **armed forces personnel** or **private-sector** employees.
- Not bound by CPC; natural justice; **nominal fee**; parties may appear **in person**.
- Appeal first to the **Division Bench of the High Court**, then the Supreme Court – not directly to SC.
!TRAP CAT covers civilian defence employees, not armed forces personnel.
### 5. STATE ADMINISTRATIVE TRIBUNALS – AP ANGLE
- SATs are set up by the **Centre at the request of the State**; **joint tribunals** possible for two or more States.
- **32nd Amendment, 1973** – **Article 371D** gave constitutional authority for the **AP Administrative Tribunal** (public-service grievances).
- Presidential order of **19 May 1975** under **Art. 371D(3)** constituted APAT; it worked from **6 July 1976**.
- Reconstituted under the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 from **1 Nov 1989**.
- **2016:** Telangana removed from its jurisdiction.
- **Abolished in 2020** (Centre's notification of **14 Jan 2020**); its staff moved to the **AP High Court**, which now hears these service cases.

### 7. NATIONAL GREEN TRIBUNAL
- **NGT Act, 2010**; statutory; established **18.10.2010**; hears **environmental disputes**.
- Gives effect to the **right to a healthy environment** read into **Article 21**.
- Applies **sustainable development**, **precautionary principle** and **polluter pays** (Section 20).
- Guided by **natural justice**; **not bound by the CPC, 1908**.
- Benches: Principal **New Delhi** + zonal **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai**.
- Appeal **directly to the Supreme Court within 90 days**.

### 9. TRIBUNAL REFORMS
#### 9.1 Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021
- Abolished: **Film Certification Appellate Tribunal**, **IPAB**, **Airport Appellate Tribunal**, **Authority for Advance Rulings (Customs)**, **Plant Varieties Protection Appellate Tribunal**; functions to **High Courts**.
- Tenure **4 years**; minimum age **50**; Chairperson up to **70**, members up to **67**.
- Search-cum-Selection Committee sent **two names** per vacancy.
#### 9.2 Madras Bar Association (Nov 2025)
- Struck down **minimum age 50**, **4-year tenure** (made 5), **two names** (now **one name per vacancy**), equating members with civil servants; ordered an **NTC within four months**.
#### 9.3 Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026
- Moved by Law Minister **Arjun Ram Meghwal**; **Lok Sabha 10 Aug 2026** (voice vote), **Rajya Sabha 11 Aug 2026**; repeals the 2021 Act.
| NTC feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| HQ | **New Delhi** |
| Members | **5**: Chairperson (former **SC judge** or former **HC Chief Justice**), 2 judicial (consulting the **CJI**), 2 technical (**25+ years**) |
| Tenure | **5 years or age 70**, whichever earlier |
| Role | Appointments, performance review, discipline |
