## U0423 | INDIA-PAKISTAN RELATIONS – KASHMIR, TERRORISM, INDUS WATERS TREATY, TRADE AND DIALOGUE
### 1. KASHMIR – ACCESSION AND UN REFERENCE
- **Instrument of Accession**: signed by **Maharaja Hari Singh**, **26 October 1947**, after the **tribal invasion**; Indian troops airlifted **27 October**.
- Ceded only **defence, external affairs and communications**; land revenue stayed with the State.
- Sequence: tribal invasion → accession → airlift → **First Indo-Pak war**.
- **Nehru** referred Kashmir to the **UNSC** on **1 January 1948** under **Article 35** of the UN Charter; **UNSC Resolution 47 (1948)** called for a plebiscite conditional on Pakistan's withdrawal — never held.
!TRAP Accession 26 Oct vs airlift 27 Oct; UN reference under Article 35 of the UN Charter.
### 2. ARTICLE 370 AND AFTER
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Article 370 operative | **17 November 1952** |
| J&K Constituent Assembly approved accession | **17 November 1956** |
| J&K Constitution in force | **26 January 1957** |
| Article 370 abrogated; J&K and Ladakh UTs | **5 August 2019** |
| Supreme Court upheld abrogation | **December 2023** |
- **Pakistan's response**: downgraded diplomatic ties, suspended bilateral trade and the **Samjhauta Express**, raised it at the UN.
- **Gilgit-Baltistan**: India claims it as part of J&K — a political status issue, not a border dispute.
### 3. WARS AND OPERATIONS
| War | Trigger / operation | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **1947–48** (first Kashmir war) | Pakistan-backed tribal invasion | UN ceasefire **1 January 1949**; Karachi Agreement 1949 |
| **1965** | Pakistan's **Operation Gibraltar**, **Operation Grand Slam**; India crossed towards Lahore | **Tashkent Declaration 1966** |
| **1971** | Bangladesh crisis | Pakistan surrendered **16 December 1971**; Bangladesh created; Simla 1972 |
| **1984 Siachen** | India's **Operation Meghdoot** (April 1984) | Dispute over unmarked line beyond **NJ9842** |
| **1999 Kargil** | Pakistani intrusion; India's **Operation Vijay** | Pakistan withdrew under US pressure; **first war between nuclear-armed states** |
- **2003 LoC ceasefire**, reaffirmed in **2021** (backchannel talks before it).
!TRAP 1965 was NOT the first Kashmir war — that was 1947–48 (APPSC 2019).
!PAIR Gibraltar | Pakistan | 1965 || Meghdoot | Siachen | 1984
### 4. AGREEMENTS → YEAR AND SIGNATORIES
| Agreement | Year | Signatories / key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Karachi Agreement** | July 1949 | Military representatives under UNCIP; fixed the **ceasefire line** |
| **Tashkent Declaration** | **January 1966** | **Shastri–Ayub Khan**; Soviet mediation; **Haji Pir Pass** returned to Pakistan; Shastri died at Tashkent |
| **Simla Agreement** | **2 July 1972** | **Indira Gandhi–Z.A. Bhutto**; bilateral settlement; ceasefire line → **LoC** |
| **Lahore Declaration** | **February 1999** | **Vajpayee–Nawaz Sharif**; nuclear CBMs; bus diplomacy |
| **Agra Summit** | **July 2001** | **Vajpayee–Pervez Musharraf**; **no joint declaration** |
- Chronology (UPPSC 2002): **Tashkent → Indo-Soviet Treaty (1971) → Simla → Farakka (1977)**.
!TRAP Tashkent did NOT create the LoC — Simla (1972) did. Simla is India–Pakistan (APPSC 2018), signed by Indira–Bhutto (APPSC 2022).
### 5. COMPOSITE DIALOGUE AND DISPUTED PLACES
- **Composite Dialogue**: agreed **1997** → restarted **2004** → stopped after **26/11 (2008)**.
- **Eight baskets**: peace and security/CBMs; J&K; Siachen; **Wullar Barrage/Tulbul**; Sir Creek; terrorism and drugs; economic cooperation; friendly exchanges — **not** nuclear disarmament.
| Place | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Sir Creek** | **96-km tidal estuary**, Rann of Kutch, between **Gujarat and Sindh**; Pakistan: **1914 map line**; India: **mid-channel thalweg rule**; unsettled |
| **Siachen** | Glacier secured in Operation Meghdoot; line beyond NJ9842 unmarked |
### 6. CROSS-BORDER TERRORISM → INDIA'S RESPONSE
| Attack | Date | Group | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parliament** | **13 Dec 2001** | JeM/LeT | **Operation Parakram** (troop mobilisation) |
| **Mumbai 26/11** | Nov 2008 | LeT | Composite Dialogue suspended |
| **Uri** | Sept 2016 | Pakistan-based militants | **Surgical strikes** across LoC |
| **Pulwama** (CRPF convoy) | **14 Feb 2019** | JeM | **Balakot airstrike**; MFN withdrawn |
| **Pahalgam** (26 civilians killed) | **22 Apr 2025** | — | **Operation Sindoor** (strikes inside Pakistan); IWT in abeyance |
- **Samjhauta Express** bombed 2007.
- India's line: "**zero tolerance** for terrorism"; terror "will have consequences" (Jaishankar, 33rd ARF, 24 Jul 2026). At the UNSC (Jan 2026) India said terrorism "can never be normalised".
!KEY Uri → surgical strikes; Pulwama → Balakot; Pahalgam → Sindoor; Parliament → Parakram.
### 7. TERROR LISTINGS
- **UNSC 1267 sanctions committee** (ISIL/Al-Qaida): lists terrorists and groups for **asset freeze and travel ban**.
- **Masood Azhar** (JeM chief): listed **May 2019** after **China lifted its "technical hold"**.
- **FATF grey list** (increased monitoring): Pakistan **June 2018 → October 2022**.
### 8. TRADE AND CONNECTIVITY
- **Kartarpur Corridor**: agreement **October 2019**; opened **November 2019** for **Guru Nanak's 550th birth anniversary**; visa-free from **Dera Baba Nanak (Gurdaspur)** to **Gurdwara Darbar Sahib, Kartarpur (Narowal)**.
- Agreement renewed for 5 years in **October 2024**; India closed the corridor in **May 2025** after Operation Sindoor; still closed (Apr 2026).
- **MFN**: granted to Pakistan **1996**; withdrawn **February 2019** after Pulwama with **200% customs duty**; Pakistan never reciprocated.
- **Samjhauta Express** (Attari–Lahore): started **1976** after Simla; suspended 2019.
- **Wagah–Attari**: main land trade route. After Pahalgam India closed the **Attari ICP**, revoked SAARC visa-exemption and cut High Commission strength **55 → 35**.
### 9. INDUS WATERS TREATY 1960
- Signed at **Karachi, 19 September 1960** by **Nehru and President Ayub Khan**; broker: **World Bank** (APPSC G2 2017).
| Rivers | Allotted to |
|---|---|
| Eastern: **Ravi, Beas, Sutlej** | India |
| Western: **Indus, Jhelum, Chenab** | Pakistan (India: domestic use, agriculture, **run-of-river hydropower**, limited storage) |
- Pakistan gets about **80%** of 168 MAF; India about **20%**; criticised for **no aquifer or drought clause**.
- **Permanent Indus Commission**: one Commissioner from each country.
| Article IX level | Handled by |
|---|---|
| "Questions" | PIC |
| "Differences" | **Neutral Expert** |
| "Disputes" | **Court of Arbitration** |
- No hierarchy → World Bank appointed both for **Kishanganga and Ratle**.
#### 9.1 Abeyance (2025) and after
- After Pahalgam, the **CCS** put the Treaty "**in abeyance**" from **23 April 2025** — until Pakistan "**credibly and irrevocably**" gives up support for cross-border terrorism. **First ever suspension** since 1960; the Treaty has no abeyance clause.
- Effects: India stops sharing flow data; can build storage on western rivers; Chenab projects **Kirthai-II (930 MW)** and **Sawalkote (1,856 MW)** (Jun 2026).
- **Jul 2026**: India sent a formal notice to renegotiate.
- **Aug–Sep 2026**: PCA Court of Arbitration (The Hague), **Ratle** case: Treaty "remains in force", interim measures; India rejected it — "**no jurisdiction**" (1 Sep 2026).
