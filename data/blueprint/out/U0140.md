## U0140 | DECLINE OF THE MUGHAL EMPIRE – CAUSES, HISTORIOGRAPHY, LATER MUGHALS AND SUCCESSOR STATES
- **Exams:** Group 1 Prelims | **HIGH**
- **Rows:** A-117 Decline of the Mughal Empire – causes and historiography; the Later Mughals; regional successor states
- **PYQ importance:** APPSC 5 questions | other exams 30
### 1. LATER MUGHALS IN ORDER
| Ruler | Reign | Key events | Fate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bahadur Shah I** (Muazzam) | 1707-1712 | Released **Shahu**; called **"Shah-i-Bekhabar"** | Died in office |
| **Jahandar Shah** | 1712-1713 | Wazir **Zulfiqar Khan**; under mistress Lal Kunwar; called **"Lampat"** (the profligate) | Defeated and killed by nephew **Farrukhsiyar** with the Sayyids |
| **Farrukhsiyar** | 1713-1719 | Puppet of the **Sayyid brothers**; executed **Banda Singh Bahadur (1716)**; **1717 farman** – duty-free trade for the English in Bengal | Blinded, deposed and killed by the Sayyids, 1719 |
| **Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela'** | 1719-1748 | Removed the Sayyids with **Nizam-ul-Mulk's** help; **Nadir Shah 1739**; Abdali's first raid 1748 | – |
| Ahmad Shah | 1748-1754 | Control to **Udham Bai**; Marathas took Malwa, Bundelkhand | Blinded and deposed by **Imad-ul-Mulk** (1754) |
| Alamgir II | 1754-1759 | **Plassey 1757**; repeated Abdali invasions; Punjab and Multan ceded to Abdali | Murdered by Imad-ul-Mulk |
| **Shah Alam II** (Ali Gauhar) | 1759-1806 | **Third Panipat 1761**, **Buxar 1764**; **first Mughal pensioner** of the Company; English captured Delhi in **1803** | Blinded by **Ghulam Qadir Rohilla, 1788** |
| Akbar II | 1806-1837 | Gave **Ram Mohan Roy** the title "Raja" | British pensioner |
| **Bahadur Shah Zafar (II)** | 1837-1857 | **Last Mughal emperor**; told his successors would lose the royal title and the Red Fort | **Exiled to Rangoon** after 1857; died 1862 |
- ★ Order (APPSC G1 2024): **Bahadur Shah I → Jahandar Shah → Farrukhsiyar → Muhammad Shah → Shah Alam II**.
- Order: Jahandar Shah → Muhammad Shah → Ahmad Shah → Alamgir II.
#### 1.1 Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela'
- Called "Rangeela" for wine, beauty and apathy to administration; watched animal fights; **eunuchs and ladies** controlled the government.
- **Last Mughal emperor to sit on the Peacock Throne (Mayur Singhasan / Takht-i-Taus)**; emperor at the time of Nadir Shah's attack.
#### 1.2 Shah Alam II's Exile
- Not allowed to enter Delhi by Wazir **Ghaziuddin (Imad-ul-Mulk)** – he spent his first years away from the capital out of fear of his strong wazir and unstable politics, **not** fear of invasion from the north-west.
- After Buxar he lived at **Allahabad** under Company protection; returned to Delhi in **1771-72** with Maratha help (**Mahadji Scindia**, in Madhav Rao I's time).
!TRAP Ahmad Shah ruled 1748-**1754**, not 1748-1757 (that overlaps Alamgir II). The Sayyids dominated **Farrukhsiyar**, not Jahandar Shah (whose wazir was Zulfiqar Khan).
!TRAP Exiled to Rangoon after 1857 = **Bahadur Shah II**, not Muhammad Shah.

### 3. SAYYID BROTHERS – KING-MAKERS
- **Abdullah Khan** = **Wazir**; **Husain Ali Khan** = **Mir Bakhshi**.
- King-makers **1713-1720**: made Farrukhsiyar emperor and later removed him; then puppet successors.
- Eliminated under **Muhammad Shah** with **Nizam-ul-Mulk's** help; Husain Ali killed in 1720.
- **Saadat Khan** joined the plot against the Sayyids, siding with Muhammad Shah.
- The Sayyids' control bred corruption and mismanagement.
### 4. INVASIONS – INVADER, YEAR AND CONSEQUENCE
| Invader | Year | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Nadir Shah** (Persia) | **1739** | Defeated **Muhammad Shah** at **Karnal**; sacked Delhi; took the **Koh-i-Noor** and Shah Jahan's **Peacock Throne** |
| **Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)** (Afghan) | Raids **1748-1767** | Punjab and Multan ceded; entered Delhi **January 1757**, looted Delhi, Mathura, Agra; **Third Panipat, 14 January 1761** |
- Nadir Shah's contemporary historian: **Anand Ram Mukhlis**. Plunderer who took the Koh-i-Noor = **Nadir Shah** (APPSC 2018).
- **Abdali** had been **Nadir Shah's general**. In 1757 he made **Alamgir II** emperor, **Imad-ul-Mulk** Prime Minister, and **Najib Khan (Najib-ud-Daulah)**, the Rohilla chief he trusted, chief agent and Mir Bakhshi.
#### 4.1 Third Battle of Panipat (1761)
- Abdali vs the **Maratha Confederacy**.
- **Immediate cause**: Marathas expelled Abdali's son/viceroy **Timur Shah** from **Lahore**.
- **Result**: Maratha growth in the north stopped; the gap later helped British expansion.
- Marathas = strongest native power after Mughal decline.
- Impact of invasions: exposed Mughal military weakness; drained the treasury.

### 5. FARMANS, GRANTS AND TREATIES
| Year | Grant | By | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1717** | Farman | **Farrukhsiyar** | English Company freed from customs duties in Bengal – the Company's **"Magna Carta"** |
| **1719** | Grant through the Sayyids | Mughal court | **Chauth and sardeshmukhi** of the **six Deccan subas** to **Balaji Vishwanath** as Shahu's agent |
| **1765** | **Treaty of Allahabad** | **Shah Alam II** | **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** to the Company; emperor got **Kora and Allahabad + Rs 26 lakh a year** |

- **Diwani (1765)** = the Company's legal foothold for land and money power.

### 6. REGIONAL SUCCESSOR STATES – FOUNDER AND MUGHAL POST
| State | Founder | Mughal post / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyderabad** (Asaf Jahi) | **Nizam-ul-Mulk** (Chin Qilich Khan, Mir Qamar-ud-din Siddiqi) | **Subedar of the Deccan**; later **Wazir** |
| **Bengal** | **Murshid Quli Khan** | **Diwan, then Nazim**; ruled Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; started **revenue farming** |

- **Turanis** established Hyderabad; **Iranis** established Oudh.
- Match (APPSC G1 2024): **Husain Ali Khan** – a Sayyid brother; **Murshid Quli Khan** – Bengal; **Chin Qilich Khan** – Nizam of Hyderabad; **Safdar Jung** – Awadh.

!TRAP **Saadat Khan = Awadh; Saadatullah Khan = Carnatic.** Safdar Jung = Awadh, not Hyderabad.
### 7. ASAF JAHI HYDERABAD
#### 7.1 Founding
- Founder: **Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan Siddiqi**, the **first Nizam** (APPSC 2018, 2022).
- Background: Hyderabad first envisaged by Deccan viceroy **Zulfiqar Khan**, governed through **Daud Khan** from 1708.
- Farrukhsiyar made him Governor of the Deccan; he helped overthrow the Sayyids.
- Defeated **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakar Kheda (1724)** → virtually independent; **1724 = founding year**.
- Tolerant: **Puran Chand** as Diwan; Mughal-type jagirdari administration.
- Kept the Marathas out in his lifetime; died **1748**, after which Hyderabad became vulnerable.
#### 7.2 Titles of the Founder
| Title | Conferred by |
|---|---|
| **Chin Qilich Khan** | **Aurangzeb** |
| **Nizam-ul-Mulk** | **Farrukhsiyar** |
| **Asaf Jah** | **Muhammad Shah, 1725** |
!TRAP Kilich Khan was not Mubariz Khan; the title Asaf Jah was not given by Zulfiqar Khan.
#### 7.3 Rulers in Sequence
| Ruler | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Asaf Jah I** (Nizam-ul-Mulk) | 1724-1748 |
| Nasir Jung | 1748-1750 |
| Muzaffar Jung | 1750-1751 |
| Salabat Jung | 1751-1762 |
| **Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II)** | 1762-1803; signed Wellesley's **Subsidiary Alliance, 1798** (again 1800) |
| Mir Mahbub Ali Khan | 1869-1911 |
| **Mir Osman Ali Khan** | **1911-1948**; **seventh and last Nizam**; founded **Osmania University** |
- Nasir Jung, Muzaffar Jung and Salabat Jung are **not counted** in the official seven Nizams: they did not receive the title Asaf Jah.
- **Operation Polo (September 1948)** joined Hyderabad to the Indian Union; the Asaf Jahi dynasty was ruling then.
### 8. CAUSES OF DECLINE
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Aurangzeb's policies** | Empire at greatest extent: **Kashmir to Jinji, Hindukush to Chittagong**; intolerant policies + long **Deccan wars** emptied the treasury |
| **Weak successors, noble factions** | Nobles acted independently; rivalries broke central authority |
| **Jagirdari and agrarian crises** | Shrinking fiscal base → peasant and zamindar revolts |
| Others | Maratha rise, invasions of Nadir Shah and Abdali, European companies |
- Power gap filled by **regional states → Marathas → East India Company**.
### 9. HISTORIOGRAPHY OF DECLINE
| Historian | Work | View |
|---|---|---|
| **Jadunath Sarkar** | History of Aurangzeb; Fall of the Mughal Empire | Aurangzeb's religious policy; weak successors |
| **Irfan Habib** | **The Agrarian System of Mughal India (1963)** | **Agrarian crisis** – over-exploitation of peasants → revolts |
| **Satish Chandra** | Parties and Politics at the Mughal Court, 1707-1740 | **Jagirdari crisis** – too few jagirs for too many nobles |
| **M. Athar Ali** | The Mughal Nobility under Aurangzeb | Enlarged nobility (**Deccanis, Marathas**) worsened jagir shortage |
| **W.H. Moreland** | India at the Death of Akbar; From Akbar to Aurangzeb | Economic exploitation and stagnation |
| **François Bernier** | Travels in the Mogul Empire | **No private property in land** → no incentive; predicted ruin |
- **Revisionist view**: the 18th century = **regional growth, not collapse**; successor states ran their own economy and law-and-order and controlled the peasant surplus.
!TRAP **Irfan Habib = agrarian crisis; Satish Chandra = jagirdari crisis.**
