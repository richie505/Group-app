## U0340 | PARTY SYSTEM IN INDIA – ONE-PARTY DOMINANCE, MULTI-PARTY AND COALITION ERAS
### 1. NATURE OF THE INDIAN PARTY SYSTEM
- India has a **multi-party system** because society divides along **region, language, caste, religion and class**; a two-party system (UK, USA) presumes one dominant axis.
- The **original Constitution** had no provision on political parties; they entered via the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)**.
- **ECI classification**: national, State, registered-unrecognised parties (**cadre vs mass** is an organisational classification).
- **RUPPs**: over **2,800**; only about **750** contested 2024.
- Assertion-reason keys: no fixed party system due to too many parties, casteism, regionalism and regional leaders forming parties; parties are the **life-blood of democracy** and people blame them for bad governance (**R explains A**).
- **Mahatma Gandhi** advised **disbanding the INC** as a political party after Independence.
- Rise of the **BJP** → polarisation of voters on communal basis (per key). **TDP** is a regional party, not national.
### 2. ONE-PARTY DOMINANCE – THE "CONGRESS SYSTEM"
| Term / work | Scholar |
|---|---|
| "**Congress system**" (1964) and "**one-party dominant system**" | **Rajni Kothari** |
| Described Congress dominance as "dominance co-existing with competition, without a trace of alternative" | **W.H. Morris-Jones** |
| *Party Building in a New Nation* | **Myron Weiner** |
- **Congress system (1952-77)**: Congress ruled the Centre and most States as an **umbrella** of many interests – one-party dominance within a competitive multi-party democracy.
- Kothari's works: **Politics in India**, **Caste in Indian Politics**.

### 3. TURNING-POINT ELECTIONS
| Election | Significance |
|---|---|
| **1967** (4th) | Congress lost several States → **Samyukta Vidhayak Dal** coalitions; coalition era in States began |
| **1971** (5th) | **APPSC 2007 key**: end of Congress dominance; after the **1969 split**, Indira Gandhi's personalised politics |
| **1977** | **First non-Congress government** at the Centre |
| 1980 | Congress returned |
| **1989** (9th, November) | **End of the "Congress system"**; minority and coalition era began (RPSC 2023) |
| **2014** | BJP **282 of 543** – first single-party majority since **1984**; ended the 1989-2014 era |
### 4. FIRST NON-CONGRESS GOVERNMENT – 1977
- Party: **Janata Party** (merger of Congress (O), Jana Sangh, BLD, Socialists); PM **Morarji Desai**; 1977-79.
- First time Congress lost a general election; also the first coalition-type government, but with its own Lok Sabha majority.
!TRAP First non-Congress government = **1977**, not 1989 (1989 = first non-Congress coalition of the multi-party era).
### 5. COALITION AND MINORITY GOVERNMENTS AT THE CENTRE
| Government | PM(s) | Period | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| Janata Party | Morarji Desai | 1977-79 | First non-Congress |
| **National Front** (Janata Dal + regional parties; formed Aug 1988) | **V.P. Singh** | 1989-90 | Minority; outside support of **BJP and Left** |
| Samajwadi Janata Party | Chandra Shekhar | 1990-91 | Congress outside |
| Congress | **P.V. Narasimha Rao** | 1991-96 | Minority, **full term** |
| BJP | A.B. Vajpayee | 1996 | **13 days** |
| **United Front** | **H.D. Deve Gowda, I.K. Gujral** | 1996-98 | Congress outside |
| **NDA** | A.B. Vajpayee | 1998-2004 | Parties new to working together; ~6 years |
| **UPA** | Manmohan Singh | 2004-14 | Congress-led; **DMK** second largest party (2004-09) |
- **Janata Dal**: 11 October 1988 from Jan Morcha, Congress(S), Janata, Lok Dal.
- **APPSC 2007 key**: "only minority government to continue under two PMs" = Congress government of **P.V. Narasimha Rao** (United Front, with Deve Gowda then Gujral, is the obvious two-PM case – know both).
- **Deve Gowda – 12th PM** (counting acting PM Gulzari Lal Nanda); Narendra Modi – 15th.

### 8. CONSEQUENCES OF COALITION POLITICS
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Main result (APPSC key)** | **Increased role of regional and small parties** |
| Governance | Consensus building but **slow decisions**, policy U-turns, diluted measures |
| Checks | No single party has absolute power; regional voice in national policy |
| Defections | Horse-trading → **anti-defection law** (Tenth Schedule, 1985) |
| Federalism | More **bargained** after 1989 |
- Coalition politics ended one-party dominance; India is **not** a two-party system.
### 9. PARTY SYSTEMS IN STATES
- After **1967**: almost every opposition-ruled State had a coalition – exception **Tamil Nadu (DMK alone)**.
- **Bihar**: record **nine coalition governments, 1967-1971**.
| State | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Kerala** | Bipolar: **UDF** (Congress) vs **LDF** (CPI-M) |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | Congress dominance until **1983**; then **Congress vs TDP**; since **2014 TDP vs YSRCP** |
- **1994**: Congress won its **lowest** AP Assembly tally; strong TDP under **N.T. Rama Rao**.
- AP now: coalition with a dominant regional party and **BJP as supporting partner** (also Meghalaya, Nagaland, Puducherry) (12 Aug 2026).
- AP roots: **Justice Party**-era rise of **non-Brahmin agrarian castes** → base for regional parties.
- 12 Aug 2026: BJP alone in **11** States, Congress **3**, regional parties alone **4**, coalitions **10**.
- **INDIA bloc** meeting (8 June 2026): Congress called the only party present in all States.
- Bangaru Laxman (died March 2014, Hyderabad) – BJP.
