## U0648 | DEMOGRAPHIC DIVIDEND, AGEING AND POPULATION POLICY
### 1. DEMOGRAPHIC DIVIDEND - DEFINITION AND AGE GROUP
#### 1.1 Definitions
| Source | Definition |
|---|---|
| **UNFPA** | Growth potential when the **working-age share (15-64)** exceeds the non-working share (0-14, 65+) |
| APPSC-G2 2017 key | "A **rise in economic growth** due to a **rise in the share of working people** in the population" |
- Age group asked by APPSC/ILO: **15-64** (APPSC-G1 2022, APPSC-GS 2019); Indian projections often use **15-59** (another APPSC key: 15-59).
#### 1.2 Channels
- **Labour supply**, **savings and investment**, lower **dependency ratio**, freed **human capital**; rising dependants hurt it.
#### 1.3 Terms
- "**Demographic nightmare**" (ES 2018-19): dividend lost without health, education, skills, jobs.
- "**Demographic winter**": fertility below 2.1, **inverted pyramid**.
- "**Shrinconomics**": AP Health Minister (1 Apr 2026) on the "alarming" fertility fall.
### 2. INDIA'S DIVIDEND WINDOW
| Estimate | Window / peak |
|---|---|
| **UNFPA** | **Longest in the world**, five decades: **2005-06 to 2055-56**; opens at different times in different states |
| **ES 2018-19** | Peak about **2041** (59% aged 20-59) |
| **ES 2025-26** (UN projections) | Peak about **2030** (~65% aged 15-59) |
| **MoHFW** (19 Mar 2026), 15-59 | 833.8 mn (2021) > **peak 1,009.0 mn (2041)** > 998.1 mn (2051); closes after 2041 |
!TRAP Peak year differs by source: 2041 (ES 2018-19, MoHFW) vs 2030 (ES 2025-26).
- SRS 2024: 15-59 share **66.4%** (64% in 2014); 0-14 down to **24%**; median age **28**.
- **RBI** state-finances study: **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** crossed the demographic turning point; **Bihar, UP** have a wider window.
- Highest working-age shares (2021): **UAE 85.7%**, Qatar 85.6%.

### 4. POPULATION THEORIES AND AUTHORS
| Theory / work | Author | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Essay on the Principle of Population (**1798**) | **Thomas Malthus** | Population **geometric (1, 2, 4, 8)**, food **arithmetic (1, 2, 3, 4)** (UPPSC 2024); overpopulation theory (APPSC-GS) |
| Demographic transition (**1929**) | **Warren Thompson** | Demographic history |
| Formal **3-stage** transition | **Frank Notestein** (1940s-50s) | Rates change with development |
| **Optimum population** | **Edwin Cannan** | Size at which **per-capita income is maximum** |
| **The Population Bomb** (1968) | **Paul Ehrlich** | Mass famine from overpopulation |
| Marxian view | Karl Marx | Overpopulation is caused by **capitalism** (relative surplus population, unemployment), not nature |
- **Positive checks** (raise deaths): famine, disease, war. **Preventive** (lower births): moral restraint, late marriage.
!TRAP Thompson (1929) came first; Notestein gave the formal three-stage version.
### 5. DEMOGRAPHIC TRANSITION STAGES
| Stage | Birth / death | Growth | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 **High stationary** | High / high | Low | Pre-industrial |
| 2 **Early expanding** | High / falling | Explosion | **India 1951-81** |
| 3 **Late expanding** | Falling / low | Slowing | **India now** |
| 4 **Low stationary** | Low / low | Stable | **AP** |
| 5 **Declining** | BR **below** DR | Declining | **Japan, Germany** |
- Sequence: high-high > high birth-low death > low-low.
### 6. DEMOGRAPHIC HISTORY
- **British period**: high birth and death rates, **famines and epidemics**; growth about **0.19%** a year (1891-1921).
- Decline in **1911-21**; **1921 = "great divide"**; continuous growth after.
- 1951: **literacy 18.33%** (male 27.2%, female 8.9%); **life expectancy about 32 years**.

### 8. TOTAL FERTILITY RATE
#### 8.1 Terms and National Values
- **TFR** = average children a woman is expected to have over ages **15-49**; **replacement level 2.1**.
- Births per 1,000 a year = **crude birth rate**, not TFR.
| Source | India TFR |
|---|---|
| NFHS-4 / NFHS-5 | 2.2 / **2.0** |
| **NFHS-6** (2023-24, released 29 May 2026) | **2.0**, urban 1.6; **31 of 36** states/UTs below 2.1; A&N lowest (0.9); Bihar highest (2.7) |
| **SRS 2024** | **1.9** (rural 2.1, urban 1.5); below 2.1 for the fifth straight year; only six states above 2.1 |
#### 8.2 States
| Group | States |
|---|---|
| Ultra-low (SRS) | **Delhi 1.2**; Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal 1.3 |
| Above replacement (SRS) | **Bihar 2.9**, UP 2.6, MP 2.4, Rajasthan 2.3 |
| SRS 2021 | Highest Bihar 3.0; lowest Delhi, West Bengal 1.4 |
- At/below replacement (SRS 2021): **TN, AP, Kerala, Maharashtra** and most other large states.
#### 8.3 State Population-Control Measures
- **UP** draft Population (Control, Stabilization and Welfare) Bill **2021**.
- **Assam**: two-child rule for government jobs.
### 9. FAMILY PLANNING
- **1952**: national family planning programme, the **first in the world**.
- **Emergency (1975-77)**: coercive mass sterilisation; **1976** policy; renamed **Family Welfare Programme in 1977**.
- **Female sterilisation** dominant: **37.9%** of married women (NFHS-5; 36% in NFHS-4).
- **Mission Parivar Vikas (2016)**: **146** districts with TFR 3+ in **7 states** (UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Assam).
- **ASHA spacing advice**: **2 years** after marriage before the first child; **3 years** between births.
### 10. AGEING AND DEPENDENCY
#### 10.1 National Data
- Elderly 60+: **8.6% (2011) to 16% by 2041**.
- Median age: **24 (2011)**, 29, **36 (2036)**, **40 by 2051**.
- Around 2020: India **28**; China/US 37; **Japan 45**; Western Europe up to 49.
- **NITI Aayog**: **70%** of elderly depend on others; **78% have no pension** cover.
#### 10.2 States
- **Kerala** ageing: 60+ 16.5% (2021) to 22.8% (2036).
- Youthful: **Bihar, UP, Jharkhand**; intermediate: **Telangana**, Uttarakhand.
#### 10.3 Surveys and Indices
| Survey / index | By | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **LASI** | **MoHFW with IIPS Mumbai**; wave 1 2017-18, report 2021 | People aged **45+** |
| **India Ageing Report 2023** | **UNFPA with IIPS** | ~150 mn 60+ now to **347 mn by 2050** (~one-fifth); feminisation, ruralisation, regional variation, old-age poverty |
| **Quality of Life for Elderly Index** (Aug 2021) | Released by **Bibek Debroy**, EAC-PM | **Rajasthan** first among aged states (APPSC-GS 2023); **Mizoram** North-East; **Chandigarh** UTs |
### 11. ANDHRA PRADESH
- AP **TFR 1.5** vs India 2.0 (SRS 2020); goal **1.8**; AP CPR **71.1%** vs India 66.7% (NFHS-5).
- **Median age 32.5** vs India 28; under-14 share 25% (2011) to 15% (2036); 60+ ~10% to **19% (2036)** to 23% (2047).
- AP is **ageing faster** than India because of low TFR and high median age.
- Growth only **9.21%** + large youth workforce = classic dividend; literacy (**67.35%**) lags, so focus on learning and skills.
- **AP Population Management Policy (Feb 2026)**: shift from population control to **managing ageing and the dividend**.
- Southern states fear **lower tax devolution and fewer seats** after delimitation.
