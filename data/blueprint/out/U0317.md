## U0317 | SUPREME COURT – COMPOSITION, APPOINTMENT, COLLEGIUM, NJAC AND JURISDICTIONS
### 1. ESTABLISHMENT AND CONSTITUTIONAL BASIS
- **Part V, Chapter IV – Articles 124-147**.
- **Article 124**: establishment and constitution of the Supreme Court (Court of Record is Art. 129, not 124).
- **Federal Court** set up **1937** (GoI Act, 1935); **Privy Council** appeals abolished **1949**.
- SC replaced the Federal Court and the Privy Council's Judicial Committee on **26 Jan 1950**; **first sitting 28 January 1950** (two days later), in part of the old Parliament House.
- Moved to its **Tilak Marg** building in **1958**.
- **Art. 130**: seat at **Delhi**, or elsewhere as the **CJI** decides with the **President's approval**. No regional benches yet.
- **Custodian / guardian of the Constitution** through judicial review; final interpreter and guardian of Fundamental Rights.
- Benches usually 2 or 3 judges; **Constitution Bench ≥ 5** (Art. 145(3)); Art. 145 – SC frames its own rules.
- **Art. 348(1)(a)**: all SC and HC proceedings in **English**.
!TRAP 1937 Federal Court; 1949 Privy Council appeals end; 26 Jan 1950 SC replaces it; 28 Jan 1950 first sitting; 1958 Tilak Marg. The Council of States is the Rajya Sabha, not a court.
### 2. NUMBER OF JUDGES – ARTICLE 124(1)
- **Parliament alone** increases the number, by **ordinary law** (simple majority) – APPSC-G2 2025 key. Law: **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956**.

|---|---|
| 1950 | **8** (CJI + 7) |
| 1977 | 18 |
| 1986 | 26 |
| 2009 | 31 |
| 2019 (Amendment Act 37 of 2019) | **34** (CJI + 33) |
| **16 May 2026** | **38** (CJI + 37) – Ordinance |

!TRAP Older figure 34 (2019) is out of date; present sanctioned strength 38 (May 2026).
### 3. APPOINTMENT, QUALIFICATIONS, TENURE
#### 3.1 Appointment – Art. 124(2)
- **President** appoints judges after consulting the CJI and such judges as he thinks needed; consulting the CJI is **compulsory** for other judges (an autonomy safeguard).
- **CJI** appointed by the **President** (APPSC-GS 2025 key); by convention the **senior-most** judge; the collegium only recommends.
- Convention broken: **A.N. Ray (1973)**, **M.H. Beg (1977)**; Second Judges Case later upheld the senior-most convention.
#### 3.2 Qualifications – Art. 124(3)
- **Citizen of India** and: **5 years** as HC judge, or **10 years** as HC advocate, or a **distinguished jurist** in the President's opinion – never used in 76 years (Justice Bhuyan, 31 Aug 2026).
- **L. Nageshwara Rao** – not earlier an HC judge; 7th elevated directly from the Bar (sworn 13 May 2016, retired 7 Jun 2022).
#### 3.3 Tenure, Oath, Resignation
- Retire at **65**; age determined as Parliament provides by law (**Art. 124(2A)**, 15th Amendment 1963).
- **Oath** before the **President** (form in **Third Schedule**) – to uphold the Constitution and the law. **Resignation** in writing to the **President**.
- CJI has **no fixed term in years** – serves till 65.
- **Salaries** fixed by Parliament by law (**Art. 125(1)**); until then, Second Schedule.
### 4. ACTING, AD HOC AND RETIRED JUDGES
| Article | Who | Appointed by |
|---|---|---|
| **126** | **Acting CJI** | **President** |
| **127** | **Ad hoc judge** (qualified HC judge) – when no **quorum** | **CJI** with **President's previous consent**, after consulting the HC Chief Justice |
| **128** | **Retired judges** sitting | CJI with **President's prior consent** + **judge's consent** |
- Art. 128 judge has full powers of an SC judge but is **not deemed a regular judge**.
- **No additional judges** in the SC – additional and acting judges only in **High Courts (Art. 224)**.
!TRAP APPSC-G2 2025 key "Both i and ii": Art. 128 needs President's consent AND the retired judge's consent. Ad hoc judge is appointed by the CJI, not by the President on collegium advice.
### 5. JUDGES CASES, COLLEGIUM AND NJAC
#### 5.1 Judges Cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **First – S.P. Gupta** | **1981** | Consultation ≠ concurrence; **executive primacy** |
| **Second – SCAORA** | **1993** | Consultation = concurrence; **judicial primacy**; **collegium born** (CJI + 2) |
| **Third – Presidential Reference (Art. 143(1))** | **1998** | Collegium **CJI + 4** senior-most; plural consultation; if two give adverse opinion, name not sent |
| **Fourth – SCAORA (NJAC case)** | **2015** | NJAC and 99th Amendment **struck down**; collegium restored |
!KEY Assertion-Reason: the Second Judges Case created the collegium BECAUSE it held consultation means concurrence (R explains A).
#### 5.2 Collegium
- **Not in the Constitution**, not created by an Act – **judicially evolved** (adopted **1993**; present form since 1998).
- Formal appointment by the **President** – SC judges **Art. 124(2)**, HC judges **Art. 217(1)**.
- **Zero executive** members; Government (IB checks) may **return a name once** – bound to appoint if reiterated.
| Collegium | Members | Handles |
|---|---|---|
| Supreme Court | **CJI + 4** senior-most (5 members) | SC appointments; **HC transfers** (with CJs of both HCs) |
| For HC appointments (SC side) | **CJI + 2** senior-most SC judges | HC judges and HC Chief Justices |
| High Court | **HC Chief Justice + 2** senior-most HC judges | Initiates HC appointments |
- **June 2026 collegium**: CJI **Surya Kant**, Vikram Nath, B.V. Nagarathna, M.M. Sundresh, P.S. Narasimha (joined after J.K. Maheshwari retired, 29 Jun 2026).
#### 5.3 NJAC – 99th Amendment, 2014
- **99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014** + **NJAC Act, 2014**; inserted **Art. 124A** (APPSC-GS 2022).
| Stage | Date |
|---|---|
| Passed Lok Sabha | 13 Aug 2014 |
| Ratified | **16 States** |
| Assent | 31 Dec 2014 |
| In force | 13 Apr 2015 |
| Struck down | **16 Oct 2015** |
- **Six members**: **CJI (Chairperson)**, **2 senior-most SC judges**, **Union Law Minister**, **2 eminent persons** (chosen by PM, CJI and Leader of Opposition; one from SC/ST/OBC/minority/women). **Attorney-General NOT a member**.
- **Fourth Judges Case**: **5-judge** bench led by **J.S. Khehar**, **4:1**; lone dissent **J. Chelameswar** (upheld NJAC). Ground: executive role hurt **judicial independence – Basic Structure**. India has no NJAC now.
- Earlier attempt: **Constitution (98th Amendment) Bill** – National Judicial Commission, brought by **Arun Jaitley** (Law Minister, NDA).
!PAIR Bench leader | J.S. Khehar | majority || Dissent | J. Chelameswar | upheld NJAC
#### 5.4 Transfers and Memorandum of Procedure
- **Art. 222**: **President** transfers HC judges (incl. Chief Justices) after consulting the CJI.
- **MoP**: CJI + 2 recommend HC Chief Justices (from outside parent HC) → **Law Minister** → State consulted → President appoints after consulting CJI and Governor.
- **Sep 2026**: Punjab Cabinet objected that the State was bypassed in appointing the Punjab and Haryana HC Chief Justice. Aug 2026: Centre notified 30 HC judges.
### 6. JURISDICTIONS → ARTICLE
| Jurisdiction | Article | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Original** | **131** | Centre-State, State-State legal disputes; **exclusive** |
| **Writ** | **32** | FRs only; original but **not exclusive** (HC Art. 226) |
| **Appellate** | **132 / 133 / 134** | Constitutional / civil / criminal |
| **Special leave** | **136** | Any court or tribunal **except military** (136(2)) |
| **Review** | **137** | Own judgments; subject to Parliament's law and Art. 145 rules |
| **Advisory** | **143** | Opinion **not binding** |
| **President / VP election** | **71** | Original, exclusive, final |
| Enlarged Union List jurisdiction | 138(1) | By Parliament |
#### 6.1 Original Jurisdiction – Art. 131
- Only disputes on **legal rights** between Centre and States or between States – SC as a **federal court**; main body for Centre-State fiscal disputes.
- **Excluded**: inter-State **water disputes (Art. 262)**; **Finance Commission** matters (Art. 280); **pre-Constitution treaties**; political questions; **Art. 290** expense adjustments; **private party** suits for damages against the Union; disputes between private companies; **election disputes** of Parliament and Legislatures (go to **High Courts** – APPSC-G1 2022 key "II and III").
#### 6.2 Appellate Jurisdiction
- **Art. 132** (with **134A** certificate): constitutional interpretation cases.
- **Art. 133**: civil – substantial question of law of general importance, HC certificate.
- **Art. 134**: HC reverses acquittal and sentences to **death** – appeal **as of right**, no certificate.
- **Art. 136 SLP**: discretionary, plenary, residual; needs no HC certificate.
#### 6.3 Advisory – Art. 143
- **143(1)**: President refers questions of **law or fact** of public importance; Court **may** give opinion. Only the **President** can seek it.
- **143(2)**: pre-Constitution treaty matters – Court **must** give opinion.
- Examples: **Gujarat Assembly elections** delay – reference by **President Abdul Kalam (Aug 2002)**; **16th Presidential Reference (Nov 2025)** on Bill timelines.
### 7. SPECIAL POWERS
| Article | Power |
|---|---|
| **129** | **Court of Record**, power to punish contempt |
| **141** | Law declared binding on all courts |
| **142** | **Complete justice** – not constrained by ordinary laws; **no HC equivalent** (Anil Kumar Jain v. Maya Jain) |
| 145 | Rules; ≥ 5 judges for constitutional questions |
- Art. 142 uses: **deemed assent** to 10 TN Bills (2025); **Right to Safe Travel** under Art. 21 with nationwide directives (27 May 2026).
- **Curative petition**: **Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)**; after a review (Art. 137) is dismissed; inherent power anchored in Art. 142.
- **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** (H.N. Sanyal Committee) defines civil and criminal contempt; the Constitution does not define it.
- **e-SCR** – electronic Supreme Court Reports; free digital database of reported judgments, launched **2023**.
- **V.V. Giri** appeared in person before the SC as sitting President in a petition challenging his election.
### 8. REMOVAL OF JUDGES
- **Art. 124(4)**: by **order of the President** after an **address by Parliament**; grounds **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**.
- **Special majority** in **each House** in the **same session** – majority of total membership + two-thirds present and voting.
- **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968** (Art. 124(5)): motion signed by **100 LS / 50 RS** members; **Speaker / Chairman may admit or reject**; **3-member committee** (SC judge/CJI, an HC Chief Justice, a distinguished jurist).
- Constitution says "**removal**"; "impeachment" only for the President (**Art. 61**). Neither the CJI nor the President alone can remove a judge.
| Office | Article |
|---|---|
| SC judge / HC judge | 124(4) / 218 |
| CAG / CEC – removed like an SC judge | 148 / 324(5) |
| Judge | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **V. Ramaswami (1993)** | Motion **failed** in Lok Sabha |
| **Soumitra Sen** (Calcutta HC, 2011) | Rajya Sabha passed it; **resigned (1 Sep 2011)** before LS vote |
| Yashwant Varma (2026) | Speaker to table inquiry report despite his resignation (11 Jul 2026) |
- **No judge removed so far**.
- **In-House Procedure (1999)**, after **C. Ravichandran Iyer (1995)**, for misconduct short of removal; **Restatement of Values of Judicial Life (1997)**, 16 points.
### 9. INDEPENDENCE SAFEGUARDS
| Safeguard | Provision |
|---|---|
| Security of tenure | Removal only per Art. 124(4) / 217 |
| Salaries | Fixed by Parliament (Arts 125, 221); not cut after appointment except **Financial Emergency (Art. 360)** |
| Expenses | SC salary and pension – **Consolidated Fund of India**; HC salary – State fund, HC pension – CFI |
| Conduct not discussed | Parliament (**Art. 121**), State Legislature (**Art. 211**), except on removal motion |
| Post-retirement practice | Retired SC judge – **no court or authority** (**Art. 124(7)**); retired HC judge – only SC and other HCs (**Art. 220**) |
| Contempt power | Art. 129 / 215 |
- Judicial independence is part of the **Basic Structure** – why NJAC fell.
### 10. HIGH COURT VS SUPREME COURT
| Basis | High Court | Supreme Court |

| Appointment | President by warrant (217) – not the Governor | President by warrant (124) |
| Oath | **Governor** (219) | **President** |
| Resignation | President | President |
| Retirement | **62** | **65** |
| Court of Record | **215** | **129** |
| Writs | FRs + legal rights (**226**); superintendence (**227**) | FRs only (32) |
| SC review of own judgments | – | **Art. 137** |
| Advisory | None | 143 |
- HC Articles: 214 HCs for States, 216 constitution, 217 judges, 233 District Judges. **AP High Court at Amaravati since 1 Jan 2019**.

### 12. REFORM PROPOSALS AND INDIA VS USA
| Body | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Law Commission, 14th Report (1958)** | Appoint Bar members directly; not strictly by seniority |
| **Law Commission, 214th Report (2008)** – not 1998 | Review appointment procedure |
| **NCRWC – Venkatachaliah** (set up 2000, report 2002) | **National Judicial Commission** |
| Parliamentary Standing Committee (2023) | MoP to ensure women and marginalised candidates |
- Reforms: Judicial Standards and Accountability law, permanent disciplinary authority, asset disclosure, RTI (administrative side) – **not** abolition by executive notification.
- Criticism: collegium **"imperium in imperio"**, judges selecting judges; transparency debate revived by **Justice Ujjal Bhuyan (26 Aug 2026)**.
- **P. Wilson's (DMK) private member's Bill (19 Feb 2026)**: social diversity, **90-day** limit to notify names, **regional benches**.
| Point | India | USA |
|---|---|---|
| Advisory | Yes (143) | No |
| Appellate scope | Very wide incl. SLP | Mainly constitutional/federal |
| Review standard | "Procedure established by law" – narrower | "Due process of law" – "third chamber" |
| FR writ | Art. 32 (itself an FR) | No equivalent |
- India blends **American judicial supremacy** with **British parliamentary sovereignty**.

### 15. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS
!BOX VERY IMPORTANT
1. **Parliament** (ordinary law) increases SC judges; the **President** fixes HC strength.
2. **126** acting CJI (President); **127** ad hoc (CJI + President's consent); **128** retired (both consents).
3. **No additional judges** in the SC.
4. Collegium is **not** in the Constitution; born **1993**, CJI + 4 from **1998**.
5. **99th** Amendment void on **16 Oct 2015**; Khehar led, **Chelameswar** dissented; AG not an NJAC member.
6. Election disputes of legislatures and inter-State water disputes are **outside Art. 131**.
7. **Art. 143** opinion does **not** bind the President.
8. Retired SC judge cannot plead **anywhere** in India (124(7)); retired HC judge can in SC and other HCs.
9. "Removal", not "impeachment"; **V. Ramaswami** failed, **Soumitra Sen** resigned.
10. **Art. 142** complete justice – no HC equivalent.
