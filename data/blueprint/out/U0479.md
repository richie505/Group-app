## U0479 | LABOUR FORCE PARTICIPATION, FEMALE LFPR, INFORMALITY AND THE QUALITY OF EMPLOYMENT
### 1. KEY TERMS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Employment elasticity** | % change in employment ÷ % change in output (GDP); high = more jobs per unit growth |
| **Jobless growth** | At the height of economic growth, **unemployment of labour increases** (APPSC 2019); near-zero elasticity |
| **Missing middle** | Services-led path skipping the manufacturing stage |
| **Gig economy** | **Independent contractors/freelancers** instead of permanent workers (APPSC 2019) |
| **U-shaped hypothesis** | Female LFPR falls with early income growth, rises later with education and services |
| **Feminisation of agriculture** | Basic reason: **rural-to-urban migration of men** (APPSC 2018) |
- Very low employment elasticity: **mixed farming**.
!TRAP "Jobless growth" was coined by **Nick Perna** (early 1990s), not Arthur Lewis or an Indian economist.
### 2. JOBLESS GROWTH AFTER 1991
- Growth led by **capital-intensive** IT and finance; job creation not concentrated in **labour-intensive manufacturing**.
- Manufacturing stage skipped ("missing middle").
- Employment growth lagged labour-force growth (creeping unemployment, mostly unorganised sector).
- GDP grew 6-7% but formal jobs lagged; about **12 million** enter the job market yearly (7-10 million young Indians a year, 15 May 2026).
- Direct factory employment fell **61% → 47%** (2011-2023); contract workers rose to **42%** (13 Mar 2026).
### 3. INFORMAL SECTOR VS INFORMAL EMPLOYMENT
| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Informal/unorganised **sector** (enterprise) | **Unincorporated private enterprises** owned by individuals/households, with **fewer than 10 workers** |
| Informal **employment** (worker) | Workers without social security, paid leave or dismissal notice, in any sector |
- **NCEUS** (National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector), set up **2004**, chaired by **Arjun Sengupta**.
- Nearly **82%** of the workforce is in the informal sector and nearly **90%** informally employed (India Employment Report 2024).
- AP informal share: **62.3% rural, 45.6% urban** (2024-25); overall about **55%**.

- By **Labour Bureau**; establishments with **10+ workers** in **9 sectors**: manufacturing, construction, trade, transport, accommodation and restaurants, IT/BPO, education, health, financial services.

### 6. FEMALE LFPR
| PLFS round | Female LFPR (15+) |
|---|---|
| 2017-18 | **23.3%** |
| 2020-21 | 32.5% |
| 2023-24 | **41.7%** |
| 2025 | about **40%** (rural **45.9%**, urban **27.7%**) |
- Rural 33.0% → 45.9% vs urban 23.3% → 27.7% (2019-20 to 2025): **widening rural-urban gap** (24 Aug 2026).
- **Rural women = 80%** of rise in female workers (2017-18 to 2023-24): subsistence farming, unpaid family work, distress.
- **Barriers:** social norms, unpaid care work, unsafe transport.
- PLFS 2025: LFPR about **59%**, WPR about **57%**, UR about **3%**.
!TRAP **23.3%** = all-India FLFPR 2017-18 AND urban FLFPR 2019-20.
### 7. GIG AND PLATFORM ECONOMY
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| NITI Aayog **"India's Booming Gig and Platform Economy"** (June 2022) | **77 lakh** gig workers (2020-21) |
| Projection | **2.35 crore by 2029-30** (about **6.7%** of workforce) |
| Other NITI projections | 1.27 crore (2024-25), 1.43 crore (2025-26) |
| **Code on Social Security, 2020** | Defines gig and platform workers; **aggregators pay 1-2% of annual turnover** to a fund (insurance, pensions) |
- FY25: grew 55% to about **120 lakh** (over 2% of workforce).
- PLFS does not yet capture gig workers separately; most are outside e-Shram.
### 8. LABOUR CODES AND E-SHRAM
- **4 Labour Codes** consolidate **29 central labour laws**; in force from **21 Nov 2025**: Code on Wages 2019, Code on Social Security 2020, Industrial Relations Code 2020, OSH and Working Conditions Code 2020.
- **e-Shram** portal (launched **26 Aug 2021**): national database of **unorganised workers**; each gets a **12-digit Universal Account Number**; about **31.89 crore** registrations (Aug 2026), including about 11.5 lakh platform workers.
