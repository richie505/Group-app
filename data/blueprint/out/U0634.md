## U0634 | INDIA – EARTHQUAKE ZONES, LANDSLIDE-PRONE AREAS, CYCLONE-PRONE COASTS; SEISMIC ZONING MAP
### 1. INDIA'S HAZARD AND VULNERABILITY PROFILE
- **Disaster = Hazard x Vulnerability.**
| Hazard | Exposure |
|---|---|
| Earthquake | ~**59%** of landmass prone to moderate-to-high earthquakes (NDMA, 2023) |
| Flood | ~**40 million ha**, about **12%** of land (not 40%) |
| Drought | ~**68%** of cultivable area vulnerable |
| Cyclone / tsunami | ~**5,700 km** of the ~**7,516 km** coastline |
| Landslide | **12.6–15%** of area, about **0.42 million sq km** (GSI) |
- Under the withdrawn 2025 map, more than **60%** of land would have been moderate-to-high earthquake risk.
- **Tsunami** is a **natural**, not man-made, disaster.
- **DM Act 2005**: shifted focus from post-disaster **relief → prevention, mitigation, preparedness**.
!TRAP Flood-prone area is about 12% of India, not 40%.
### 2. SEISMIC ZONING MAP
#### 2.1 The Official Map
- Prepared by **BIS** under **IS 1893**; **four zones: II, III, IV, V** (Zone V = highest risk).
- **2002 revision**: after the **1993 Killari** quake struck a **low-risk** area, old **Zone I was merged into Zone II**.
#### 2.2 The Withdrawn Revision (Zone VI)
- BIS revised map used **probabilistic seismic hazard assessment (PSHA)**; added a **Zone VI**; drew on **69,519 events** (2600 BC–2019) and **36 seismogenic source zones** (IIT Madras study, 22 Mar 2026).
- **Rolled back in March 2026** (8 and 12 Mar 2026) after criticism over method and cost → the **four-zone map still applies**.
!KEY Zones merged = I into II (2002). Zone VI = proposed, then withdrawn (Mar 2026).
#### 2.3 Zones → Places
| Zone | Places |
|---|---|
| **Zone V** | **Kutch (Rann of Kutch)**, **North-East**, Himalayan belt (J&K, Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand), North Bihar, **Andaman & Nicobar** |
| **Zone IV** | **Delhi** |
| **Zone III** | **Amaravati** (core capital region, Vijayawada, Guntur) |
| Zones II–III | Most of the stable southern peninsula |
- **Andhra Pradesh has no area in Zone V.**
- **Crustal strain**: GSI monitors through **34 GNSS stations**.

### 5. LANDSLIDE-PRONE AREAS
#### 5.1 Regions
| Region | Why prone |
|---|---|
| **Himalaya** (J&K, Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal) | **Young, fragile geology**, active tectonics |
| **North-East** | Steep slopes, heavy rain |
| **Western Ghats incl. Nilgiris** | **Steep escarpments, orographic rain** (not permafrost) |
| Eastern Ghats, Vindhyas | Parts of AP, Odisha |
- Landslides occur **mostly in hilly areas** (PYQ).
#### 5.2 Landslide Hazard Zonation
- **Landslide Hazard Zonation (landslide zoning)**: divides hilly areas into **homogeneous units** by degree of **actual or potential** landslide susceptibility, hazard or risk (PYQ).
- Zonation maps are **probabilistic** – they show likelihood, not exact forecasts.
!TRAP Zonation does not predict the exact time or place of a landslide.
#### 5.3 Mitigation
- **Structural**: retaining walls, rock bolting, wire mesh, drainage improvement, slope grading.
- **Non-structural**: Landslide Hazard Zonation mapping, early warning, land-use regulation, awareness.
- **Regulatory**: restrict construction in high-risk zones; enforce **mountain-specific (hill) building codes**; regulate settlements.
- **LANDSLIP** project: GSI building a **regional Landslide Early Warning System**.
### 6. CYCLONE-PRONE COASTS
- Cyclones come from both the **Bay of Bengal** and the **Arabian Sea**; the Bay of Bengal generates about **four times** as many → **east coast** hit far more.
- Exposure: **13 coastal states/UTs** and **84 coastal districts** cyclone-affected.
- **Most cyclone-prone state: Andhra Pradesh** (APPSC GS 2025), then **Odisha, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal**.
| IMD cyclone warning centres (7) | Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Thiruvananthapuram, **Visakhapatnam**, Bhubaneswar |
|---|---|
- **Hyderabad is NOT** an IMD cyclone warning centre.
- **ISRO DMS**: maps coastal vulnerability; runs the **NDEM** database. Remote sensing, **GIS** (spatial data) and satellites address coastal disaster vulnerability.
### 7. TSUNAMI-PRONE COASTS AND WARNING CENTRES
- Most exposed: **east coast and Andaman & Nicobar Islands** (as in the 2004 tsunami).
- **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)**: at **INCOIS, Hyderabad**, set up after the **26 Dec 2004** tsunami, operating since **2007**; seismic monitoring, ocean buoys, sirens.
- **INCOIS**: autonomous body since **1999** under the **Ministry of Earth Sciences**; unit of the Earth System Science Organisation.
- **Indian Ocean Tsunami Warning System**: coordinated by **IOC**, set up after 2004; regional centres in India, Indonesia, Australia and others.
!TRAP Tsunami warning = INCOIS, Hyderabad (not IMD).
