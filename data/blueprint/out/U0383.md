## U0383 | CASTE – FEATURES, THEORIES OF ORIGIN, VARNA-JATI DISTINCTION
### 1. VARNA AND JATI
- "Caste" from **Portuguese casta** (race, breed).
| Varna | Jati |
|---|---|
| theoretical **4-fold** scheme of the Vedas (Chaturvarna) | real local groups; **thousands** of sub-castes |
| excludes "untouchables" | regional variations |
| almost no mobility | mobility possible, esp. middle castes |
- **1931 Census**: **4,147** castes. Colonial Census enumerated castes from **1871**; 1901 Census (Risley) ranked them by precedence.
### 2. THEORIES AND STAGES OF ORIGIN
| Theory | Proponent | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional / Divine | **Purusha Sukta** (Rig Veda 10.90) | varnas from head, arms, thighs, feet |
| Racial | **Herbert Risley** | racial difference; Aryans kept apart |
| Occupational | **Nesfield** | hereditary occupations → guilds → castes |
| Brahminical / Political | **Abbé Dubois** | Brahmin device for purity |
| Cultural | **S.C. Roy** | commensality and marriage bars |
- Early Vedic: no rigid varna. **Later Vedic**: four hereditary varnas crystallise. **Post-Maurya/Gupta**: Manu Smriti, rigid caste. British: caste councils' powers go to courts.
### 3. FEATURES OF CASTE
- **Ghurye** (*Caste and Race in India*, 1932), **six features**: segmental division; hierarchy; restrictions on feeding and social intercourse; civil and religious disabilities and privileges; lack of free choice of occupation; restrictions on marriage (endogamy).
- Other features: ascribed by birth, hereditary occupation, caste panchayat as court.
- Two dimensions: **vertical** (ranking) and **horizontal** (grouping).
### 4. DEFINITIONS BY SCHOLARS
| Scholar | Definition / idea |
|---|---|
| **S.V. Ketkar** | *History of Caste in India* (1909): membership by birth + ban on marrying outside |
| **Risley** | families with common name, claimed mythical ancestor, same hereditary calling |
| **C. Bouglé** | hereditary specialisation, hierarchy, **repulsion** |
| Martindale and Monachesi | birth-fixed duties sanctioned by religion |
| **A.W. Green** | ideally no mobility up or down |
### 5. PURITY-POLLUTION AND CULTURAL TRAITS
- **Louis Dumont**, *Homo Hierarchicus* (1966): **pure vs impure**; religious status encompasses power.
- **McKim Marriott**: interactional approach; ranking by exchange of **"coded substances"** (food, water, services).
- Cultural traits: pollution by touch, untouchability, Sanskritic rituals, commensal taboos (not universal inter-caste commensality).
### 6. JAJMANI SYSTEM
- Socio-economic and ritual interdependence: landholding **jajman** gets hereditary services from **kamin** castes.
- **William Wiser**: *The Hindu Jajmani System* (1936).
| System | Region |
|---|---|
| Jajmani | North India |
| **Bara Balute** (Balutedars) | Maharashtra |
| **Mirasi** | Tamil Nadu |
| **Adade** | Karnataka |
| **Ayagar (Ayagadu)** | Andhra: **12** hereditary functionaries paid by Manyam land or harvest share |
### 7. CASTE AMONG NON-HINDUS
- **Muslims**: **Ashraf** (noble; foreign descent, upper-caste converts), **Ajlaf** (clean occupational groups), **Arzal** (lowest; converts from untouchable castes).
- **Christians**: from St Thomas (52 AD); about 74% identify with lower castes.
- **Sikhs**: Arora, Khatri, Jat, Ramgarhia; **Mazhabi Sikhs** = largest SC component.
| SC Order | Covers |
|---|---|
| 1950 | Hindus only |
| **1956** | Sikhs added |
| **1990** | Buddhists added |
### 8. TRIBE-CASTE CONTINUUM
- **F.G. Bailey**: tribe and caste = poles of a **continuum** (*Tribe, Caste and Nation*, 1960).
- **Surajit Sinha**: tribal little tradition vs Hindu great tradition; **secondary state formation** (chiefs take Raja titles).
- **N.K. Bose**: *The Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption* (1941).
- **Ghurye**: tribes as **"backward Hindus"** (contested by Xaxa, Elwin).
- **Susan Bayly, Nicholas Dirks**: caste as a colonial construct shaped by the Census.
