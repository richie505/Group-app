## U0607 | AIR MASSES AND FRONTS – CLASSIFICATION AND ASSOCIATED WEATHER
### 1. AIR MASS – NATURE AND CLASSIFICATION
- **Air mass**: a large body of air with relatively **uniform temperature and humidity**; controls temperature, humidity, stability, precipitation.
- Forms over a uniform **source region** (land or ocean); boundary between two air masses = **front**.
- **Bergeron** code: **first letter = moisture** (c = continental, dry; m = maritime, moist); second letter = thermal source (T, P, A, E).
| Code | Source region | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| mT | Tropical oceans | Warm, moist |
| cT | Sahara, Arabia (hot deserts) | Hot, dry |
| mP | High-latitude (polar) oceans | Cold, moist |
| cP | Siberia, Canada | Cold, dry |
| cA | Arctic basin, Greenland ice | Very cold, very dry |
| mE | Equatorial oceans | Hot, very humid |
- **Polar anticyclones** (Siberian, Canadian, Antarctic Highs): cold, dense cP/cA air; cold-core, strongest near the surface; clear skies, fog, frost.
!TRAP First letter = moisture, not temperature.
### 2. AIR MASS MODIFICATION
- Leaving its source, it exchanges heat and moisture with the surface and loses its nature.
| Situation | Result | Weather |
|---|---|---|
| Cold air over warmer surface | Heated from below, **unstable** | Convection, cumulus, showers |
| Warm air over colder surface | Cooled from below, **stable** | **Fog, stratus, drizzle** |
| Over ocean / land | Gains / loses moisture | – |
- **Hygroscopic nuclei**: dust and salt particles for condensation.
- A new air mass can cause a sudden rise or fall in temperature.
- **Atmospheric pressure** = force exerted by air; **wind** blows from high to low pressure, stronger with a steeper gradient.
### 3. FRONTS – TYPES AND WEATHER
- Lighter warm air rises over denser cold air. Mid-latitude cyclones form along fronts.
| Front | Formation | Slope | Weather |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm | Warm air rides over cold | **Gentle**, gradual uplift | Prolonged light-moderate rain |
| Cold | Cold air undercuts warm, lifts it sharply | **Steep** | Cumulonimbus, heavy short showers, squalls, sudden temperature fall |
| Occluded | Cold front overtakes warm front; warm air lifted off ground | – | Mixed; last stage of temperate cyclone |
| Stationary | Neither air mass advances | – | Cloud and rain for days |
- **Frontogenesis** = forming or sharpening of a front; **frontolysis** = break-up of a front.
- **Arcus (shelf/roll) clouds** form at the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts.
#### 3.1 Passage of a Front
1. Warm front approaches: **cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus**; pressure falls, steady rain.
2. Warm sector: warm, humid air.
3. Cold front: cumulonimbus, heavy showers, wind shift; then cold, clear air.
### 4. POLAR FRONT THEORY
- **Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes**, **Bergen School (Norway)**.
- Wave cyclones form on the polar front.
- Life cycle: wave → warm and cold fronts → occlusion → dissipation.
- **Western disturbances** reach NW India at the occluded stage.

### 5. AIR MASSES AFFECTING INDIA AND IN NEWS
| Season | Air | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | Cold continental Central Asian air | **Himalayas** block it: north India about **3–8°C warmer** than same latitude |
| Winter | Anticyclonic sinking air | Clear skies, light northerly winds, low humidity, large daily range |
| Monsoon | Moist maritime Indian Ocean air, fed by **Somali Jet** | SW monsoon rain |
- **AP**: dual influence; NE monsoon from winter air returning over the Bay of Bengal.
- Windward slope = orographic rain (Western Ghats); leeward = rain shadow.
- **Tornado Alley** (US Great Plains/Mississippi): polar cold air meets warm humid tropical air.
- **Strong polar vortex** keeps cold air north; **Arctic amplification**: meandering jet carries warm air into the Arctic.
- Jul 2025: an Antarctic high reaching far north brought snow to Chile's **Atacama** Desert, first in over a decade.
