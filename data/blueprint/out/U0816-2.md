## U0816-2 | JUN–AUG 2026: NATIONAL - POLITY AND GOVERNANCE: BILLS, ACTS, ORDINANCES, LANDMARK JUDGMENTS
### 1. SUPREME COURT STRENGTH RAISED TO 38
#### 1.1 Ordinance and Bill
- **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** (**May 2026**, under **Article 123**): **puisne judges 33 → 37**; total **34 → 38 including the CJI**.
- **Five judges sworn in**: 4 former HC Chief Justices + 1 from the Bar; 3 against existing vacancies, 2 on new posts.
- Concern: if the Ordinance lapses, strength **reverts to 34** - security of tenure, judicial independence.
- **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026** replaced the ordinance; passed by **Lok Sabha on 3 Aug 2026**; passed as a **Money Bill** - the Rajya Sabha only returned it (Aug 2026); amends the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956**.
- **First increase since 2019** (31 to 34).
- **No constitutional amendment needed** - **Article 124(1)** lets **Parliament fix the number by law**.
- **Puisne judge** = an SC judge other than the CJI.
!TRAP Puisne judges raised from 33 to **37** (not 36); total 38 includes the CJI.
#### 1.2 Evolution of strength (including CJI)
| Year | Strength |
|---|---|
| 1950 | **8** |
| 1956 | **11** |
| 2019 | **34** |
| 2026 | **38** |
#### 1.3 Why the increase
- SC pendency: about **92,101 cases on 1 January 2026**; 2025: **75,410** fresh cases vs **65,615** disposed.
- All courts backlog: **over 5.5 crore** cases; **5,600+ vacancies**.
- **India Justice Report 2025**: about **15.9 judges per million**; **Law Commission** recommended **50 per million**.
- Constitution Bench: at least **5 judges**.

- Ordinance **ceases six weeks after Parliament reassembles** unless approved.

### 2. LANDMARK JUDGMENTS
#### 2.1 Definition of 'industry' refined
- **Aug 2026**: **9-judge** Constitution Bench led by **CJI Surya Kant**, **6:3** majority.
- Reconsidered **Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa (1978)** - **7-judge** bench of **Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer**, **"Triple Test"** (systematic activity; employer-employee cooperation; goods/services for human wants).
- Held: the 1947 definition cannot be applied unchanged under the **Industrial Relations Code 2020**; ruling **prospective**.
- **IR Code 2020** merged the **Industrial Disputes Act 1947**, **Trade Unions Act 1926** and **Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946**.
#### 2.2 Police custody - BNSS Section 187(2)
- **State of Andhra Pradesh vs Suda Suresh Veera Venkata Naga Raju** - custodial death probe in AP.
- Police custody can be sought **in parts** during the initial **40 or 60 days**; **total still capped at 15 days**.
- Magistrate cannot impose a blanket "no extension" condition; SC allowed **7 more days** (within 15).
- **Section 38 BNSS**: accused may meet an advocate **during interrogation**, not throughout.
| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Sec 58 BNSS** | No detention beyond **24 hours** without a magistrate |
| **Sec 187(2) BNSS** | Police custody **15 days total**, in parts, within first 40/60 days |

| Sec 167 CrPC (old) | 15 days police custody, generally in the **first 15 days** |

### 3. RIGHT TO BE FORGOTTEN
- **Laksh Vir Singh Yadav v. Union of India** - **Delhi HC guidelines** on Right to be Forgotten (removal / de-indexing of personal data).
- Trigger: Delhi HC order of **29 May 2026** on limited online discoverability of judicial records - RTBF vs **open justice**.
- Part of **Right to Privacy under Article 21** (informational privacy) - **not absolute**; applies where old allegations or expired disputes cause harm without public interest, balanced against the public's right to information.
- **Justice Sachin Datta**: search engines may delist judgments linked to a name - mainly for **acquitted persons, private civil disputes, non-parties**.
- Called **right to erasure** in European law (**GDPR**); **DPDP Act, 2023** (run by **MeitY**) recognises erasure, but its use for court records is unclear.
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Rajagopal v. State of TN (1994)** | Right to be let alone; court records open to comment |
| **K.S. Puttaswamy v. UoI (2017)** | **Privacy a fundamental right**; informational privacy |
| Dharamraj Bhanushankar Dave v. State of Gujarat (2017) | Gujarat HC denied removal of acquittal details |
| Orissa HC (2020) | Online sexual abuse - only called for debate on RTBF |

### 4. ELECTIONS, ELECTORAL ROLLS AND CITIZENSHIP
#### 4.1 SC on SIR - ECI can examine but not decide citizenship
- **Association for Democratic Reforms v. Election Commission of India**: SC **upheld** the ECI order for **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of rolls - conducted before the **November 2025 Bihar Assembly elections**; **broadly proportionate**; ECI best placed to decide the method.
- ECI may examine citizenship **prima facie** and contextually (for the roll only); it **cannot finally declare** anyone a non-citizen - **deletion from roll ≠ loss of citizenship**.
- Doubtful cases go to the **competent authority (Central Government) under the Citizenship Act, 1955** - decide preferably before the next election in that constituency.
- Name on roll = only an **evidentiary presumption** of citizenship.
- **Aadhaar** accepted as **proof of identity** (not proof of citizenship).
- **Article 324** - superintendence, direction and control of elections; **RPA 1950** - rolls; **Section 16** - a **non-citizen cannot be registered** as a voter.
- **Assam D-voter**: 1997 revision marked "doubtful voters", referred to **Foreigners Tribunals**.
- **Passport ≠ proof of citizenship**: MEA clarification (no new policy, **24 June 2026**) - a travel document, may be issued to some non-citizens; **Passports Act, 1967** vs Citizenship Act, 1955.

### 5. VANDE MATARAM - PROTECTION AND GUIDELINES
#### 5.1 Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Act, 2026
- Amends **Section 3** of the **1971 Act**; the **National Song** gets the **same protection as the National Anthem** - punishes intentional prevention or disturbance of singing.
- Punishment: **up to 3 years**, fine or both; **Section 3A** (added **2003**) - repeat offence **minimum 1 year**.
- Does **NOT** make singing compulsory, prescribe stanzas or punish respectful silence.
- Passage: introduced **Rajya Sabha 24 July 2026**; passed RS **29 July**, LS **30 July**.
- 1971 Act enacted **23 December 1971**; **Section 2** - Flag and Constitution (burning, defacing etc.).
#### 5.2 MHA guidelines

#### 5.3 Vande Mataram story

### 7. TRIAL IN ABSENTIA - SECTION 356 BNSS
- **Special NIA Court, Jammu** issued a non-bailable warrant against **Hafiz Saeed** (Lashkar-e-Taiba chief) in the **Pahalgam attack** case; NIA may seek trial in absentia under **Section 356, BNSS 2023**.
- Applies only to a **proclaimed offender** under **Section 84 BNSS** - offence punishable with **10 years+, life or death**; **not for minor offences**.
- Court must **record reasons**; absence treated as **waiver of the right to be present**.
- Safeguards: **two consecutive arrest warrants at least 30 days apart**; newspaper notice with **30 days** to appear; notice at last residence; inform relative/friend; trial only after **90 days from framing of charges**; State-appointed defence lawyer; audiovisual recording.
- An accused appearing later may **cross-examine witnesses**.
| CrPC section | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 82 | Proclamation against absconder |
| 299 | Evidence in absence of absconder |
| 317 | Dispense with personal attendance |
!TRAP CrPC did NOT permit full trial in absentia; BNSS replaced CrPC 1973.
