## U0198 | WOMEN'S QUESTION IN THE 19TH CENTURY – WIDOW REMARRIAGE, AGE OF CONSENT, EDUCATION, WOMEN'S ORGANISATIONS
### 1. SOCIAL-REFORM ACTS – MASTER TABLE
| Year | Act | Governor-General | Provision (one line) | Reformer / mover |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1795, 1804 | **Bengal Regulations XXI (1795) and III (1804)** | – | Infanticide (incl. female) declared **murder** | – |
| 1802 | Anti-Infanticide regulation | **Wellesley** | Ban on child sacrifice at **Sagar Island** | – |
| **1829** | **Sati Regulation (Regulation XVII)** | **William Bentinck** | Sati illegal; participation = **culpable homicide** | **Raja Rammohan Roy** |
| 1843 | **Act V of 1843** | **Ellenborough** | Slavery illegal | – |
| **1850** | **Caste Disabilities Removal Act (Lex Loci, Act XXI of 1850)** | **Dalhousie** | A **convert** could still **inherit ancestral property** | – |
| **1856** | **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Act XV)** | Drafted under Dalhousie, **passed July 1856 under Canning** | Widow remarriage legal; **children legitimate** | **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** |
| **1870** | **Female Infanticide Prevention Act** | **Mayo** | Curb on killing female infants | – |
| **1872** | **Native (Civil / Brahmo) Marriage Act (Act III)** | – | Girls **14**, boys **18**; inter-caste allowed; polygamy barred | **Keshab Chandra Sen** |
| **1891** | **Age of Consent Act** | **Lansdowne** | Consent for girls **10 → 12** | **Behramji Malabari** |
| **1929** | **Child Marriage Restraint Act (Sarda Act)** | **Irwin** | Marriage: girls **14**, boys **18** | **Har Bilas Sarda** |
- Order: **Caste Disabilities Removal (1850) → Widows' Remarriage (1856) → Age of Consent (1891) → Child Marriage Restraint (1929)**.
- Reform was "not merely a British gift" – reformers used the colonial legislature against orthodoxy.
!KEY APPSC G2 2025: Lex Loci Act 1850 → a convert could still inherit ancestral property. It did **not** deal with sati, widows or child marriage.
!TRAP Female Infanticide Act = **1870, Mayo** – not "1856, Dalhousie".

### 3. ISHWAR CHANDRA VIDYASAGAR (1820-1891)
- Title **"Vidyasagar"** (Ocean of Learning) for Sanskrit learning; Principal of **Sanskrit College, Calcutta** – opened it to non-Brahmins.
- Widow remarriage: petitions and writings → **Act XV of 1856**; performed the **first lawful widow remarriage in Bengal, 7 Dec 1856**.
- Opposed **child marriage** and **polygamy** – book **Bahu Bibaho**.
- Education: Secretary of the **Hindu Female School**, later **Bethune School (1849)**, founded by **J.E.D. Bethune** (Dakshinaranjan Mukherjee's support); opened many girls' schools.
- Language: **Barnaparichay** – Bengali alphabet primer.
- Journal: **Somaprakash**.
- Andhra link: sent support for **Veeresalingam's** third remarriage.
- Maharashtra: **Vishnu Parashuram (Shastri) Pandit** – Widow Remarriage Association (1850).
!KEY APPSC-G1 2024: "upliftment of women" + "movement for the Widow Remarriage Act" → both correct for Vidyasagar.
### 4. AGE OF CONSENT CONTROVERSY
| Person / case | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Behramji M. Malabari** | Parsi, born **1853, Baroda**; greatest Parsi social reformer of the 19th century; **Notes on Infant Marriage and Enforced Widowhood (1884)**; edited **Indian Spectator** and **Voice of India** |
| **Rakhmabai case (1884-88)** | Refused to live with husband **Dadaji** (child marriage); he sued for **restitution of conjugal rights**; later among India's first practising women doctors (UPSC 2020) |
| **Phulmoni Dasi case (1889-90)** | Child bride (~10-11) died after forced cohabitation → **pushed the 1891 Act** |
| **B.G. Tilak** | **Opposed** the Bill in **Kesari** as foreign interference in Hindu custom; Moderates supported it |
- The 1891 Act regulated **consummation**, not marriage age.
### 5. MARRIAGE AND CONSENT AGES
#### 5.1 Native Marriage Act, 1872
- Passed at **Keshab Chandra Sen's** urging for **Brahmo marriages**; girls **14**, boys **18** (APPSC-GS 2025).
- **1878 Brahmo split**: Keshab married his minor daughter to the **Cooch Behar** prince, breaking the Act.
#### 5.2 Sarda Act, 1929
- Moved by **Har Bilas Sarda**, under **Irwin**; passed 1929, in force **1 April 1930**.
- Girls **14**, boys **18**; amended **1978** to **18 / 21**.
- Today: **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006** – 18 women, 21 men; a 2021 proposal to raise women's age to 21 is not law (Jan 2026).
#### 5.3 Ages Ladder
| Law | Age fixed |
|---|---|
| IPC 1860 | Consent 10 |
| Native Marriage Act, 1872 | Marriage 14 / 18 |
| Age of Consent Act, 1891 | Consent **12** |
| 1925 | Consent 14 |
| Sarda Act, 1929 | Marriage 14 / 18 |
| 1949 | Consent 16 |
| 1978 / PCMA 2006 | Marriage 18 / 21 |
| **POCSO 2012** / **BNS Section 63** | Consent **18** |
!TRAP **14/18** = both the 1872 Act and the 1929 Sarda Act; 1891 = consent **12**. Age of consent (18) ≠ minimum marriage age (18/21).
- Latest (Jan 2026): **State of UP vs Anurudh** – Supreme Court flagged POCSO misuse against consensual adolescent relationships.

### 10. ANDHRA WOMEN'S REFORM
- **Veeresalingam**: first girls' school, **Dowlaiswaram, 1874** (APPSC 2025 key: "at Rajahmundry"); second at **Innispeta, Rajahmundry, 1881**; first in Andhra to back **co-education**; night schools.
- **Widow Remarriage Association (1880)**; **first Andhra widow remarriage – 11 Dec 1881**, Rajahmundry (**Gogulapati Sriramulu** and **Seethamma/Gauramma**); about 53 remarriages in all.
- Widow homes: **Madras (1897)**, **Rajahmundry (1905)**; missionaries also ran widow homes.
- **M.G. Ranade** called him the **"Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar of South India"** (APHQ 2012).
| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|

| **Unnava Lakshmibayamma** | Widow asylum, **Guntur (1902)** → Sarada Niketan; monthly **Sri Sarada Niketanam** |
