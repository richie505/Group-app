## U0379 | INDIAN SOCIETY – SALIENT FEATURES, UNITY IN DIVERSITY, CONTINUITY AND CHANGE
### 1. SALIENT FEATURES
- **Multi-ethnic, multi-lingual, multi-class, patriarchal**.
- Matrilineal exceptions: **Khasi, Jaintia, Garo**.
- **Caste** = birth-based, closed, **cumulative inequality**; **class** = open, mobile, **dispersed inequality** (**Andre Beteille**, *Caste, Class and Power*).
- Nuclear households do not reject the joint-family ideal.
- Structural features: village population, religions, castes; **poverty** is not one.
- **Religion** = beliefs and practices about sacred things (**Durkheim**); builds **solidarity**.
- Census 2011: Hindu 79.8%, Muslim 14.2%; Hindus a minority in **8** States/UTs; 6 major religions.
### 2. UNITY IN DIVERSITY AND NATIONAL INTEGRATION
- Languages: about **122** (Census 2011) vs **22** in the Constitution; **4 families** (Indo-European, Dravidian, Austro-Asiatic, Tibeto-Burman).
- Bonds: **Shankaracharya** swapped priests between southern and northern temples; **Onam**; Bhakti-Sufi blending, Khanqahs.
- Threats: communalism, casteism, **regionalism**, linguism.
- Remedies: **National Integration Council** (1962, chaired by PM), SRA 1956, Zonal Councils, **Art. 350-B** (Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
- **Andhra (1953)** = first linguistic State; reorganisation a **"safety valve"** making India a **"State-Nation"**.
- Integration: political, economic, social, cultural, psychological dimensions.
- **Social stability**: order and continuity despite change; features: cohesion, political stability, **inclusive development**; promoted by family, education, welfare, tolerance; threatened by poverty, extremism.
### 3. INDOLOGY AND INDIAN SOCIOLOGISTS
- **Indological approach**: texts (Smritis, Puranas, epics), historical, continuity, cultural interpretation; surveys = field-view.

|---|---|

### 4. CONTINUITY AND CHANGE
| Force | Effect |
|---|---|
| Education | individualism, rights; questions joint-family customs |
| Urbanisation | nuclear households; family a consumption unit |
| Law | **Caste Disabilities Removal Act 1850** (no inheritance loss on conversion); Art. 17; Hindu Code; **RTE 2009** (in force 2010, Art. 21A) |
| Technology | digital citizenship; **DPDP Act 2023** |
- Harappan continuity limited: script undeciphered; cities fell c. 1900 BCE.
- Feb 2026: Mother Language Day (**21 Feb**) report proposed a National Mission for Mother-Tongue-Based Multilingual Education.
### 5. TERMS OF CHANGE
| Term | Originator | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Great / Little Tradition | **Redfield**; applied by Milton Singer (*When a Great Tradition Modernizes*, Madras), Marriott | elite vs folk |
| Universalisation | **McKim Marriott** (*Village India*, 1955) | little → great (local Jagannath → Vishnu) |
| Parochialisation | McKim Marriott | great → local (downward) |
| Cultural lag | **W.F. Ogburn** (*Social Change*, 1922) | non-material trails material |
### 6. FOUNDERS OF SOCIOLOGY
| Thinker | Key ideas |
|---|---|
| Auguste Comte (1798–1857) | Father of Sociology; coined the word; **positivism**; "Order and Progress"; **Three Stages**: theological (God's will), metaphysical (nature's force), positive |
| Emile Durkheim | Father of Modern Sociology; **social facts**, collective conscience, **anomie**, sacred-profane |
| Karl Marx | Scientific socialism; historical materialism; **surplus value**; alienation; *Communist Manifesto* 1848 (with Engels), *Das Kapital* 1867 |
| Max Weber | **Verstehen**; ideal type; bureaucracy (hierarchy, specialisation, impersonality, merit); traditional/charismatic/legal authority; 4 social actions; *Protestant Ethic*, *Religion of India* |
| C. Wright Mills | *Sociological Imagination* (1959): trouble ↔ issue; **power elite** (political, military, corporate) |
| Georg Simmel | formal sociology; dyad, triad |
| Manuel Castells | **network society** (1996) |
