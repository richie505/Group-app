## U0799 | ECO-FRIENDLY TECHNOLOGIES IN AGRICULTURE – IPM, BIOFERTILISERS, BIOPESTICIDES, ORGANIC AND NATURAL FARMING
### 1. SUSTAINABLE AGRICULTURE – MEANING AND PRACTICES
- **Sustainable agriculture**: produce food while **protecting the environment** and **conserving resources for future generations**.
- It **prioritises soil health and biodiversity**.
- Unlike organic farming, sustainable agriculture **may use synthetic inputs judiciously** (IPM, precision farming, conservation tillage).
- Shift from the Green Revolution to the **"evergreen revolution"**: ZBNF, organic farming, water conservation, IPM.
#### 1.1 Sustainable vs Harmful Practices
| Sustainable (exam answers) | Harmful |
|---|---|
| **Crop rotation**, **organic farming** | Excessive chemical fertilisers |
| **Mixed / inter-cropping**, **green manuring**, cover crops | Overuse of pesticides |
| Reduced tillage, drip irrigation, **IPM** | **Monoculture**, over-irrigation |
- Eco-friendly technology (PYQ answer): **organic farming** – avoids synthetic fertilisers and pesticides.
!TRAP IPM is a sustainable practice; monoculture and over-irrigation are harmful, even though they raise short-term output.
### 2. AGROCHEMICALS – ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT
#### 2.1 Fertiliser Impacts
- **Synthetic fertilisers and pesticides** cause **soil and water pollution / contamination**.
- **Excess nitrogen**: **soil acidification** (sours soil), **nitrate leaching** into groundwater, **eutrophication**.
- **Continuous chemical fertiliser use**: decline in **soil organic matter**, fewer microbes, **lower nutrient density** in food.
- **Broadcasting** granular fertiliser loses **50-70%** of applied nitrogen (as ammonia or nitrate leaching into aquifers).
- Careless fertiliser use causes nitrogen loss, soil and land degradation and water pollution (**all** of these).
- Careless synthetic fertilisers/pesticides cause **secondary pest outbreaks**, **pesticide resistance** and ecosystem contamination.
- Pesticide residues **bioaccumulate** in the food chain.
#### 2.2 Remedies
- Organic manure, vermicompost, biofertilisers.
- **Neem-coated urea**: slows **dissolution of urea and nitrification**, so nitrogen is released slowly → better **nitrogen-use efficiency**.
- Nitrogen-use efficiency is raised by **split application**, **nitrogen inhibitors** and **slow-release N fertilisers** (all).
- **PM-PRANAM**: States keep **50%** of the fertiliser subsidy saved, as **grants**.
#### 2.3 Restricted Pesticide in News
| Pesticide | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Carbosulfan** | Centre proposed a ban (29 Aug 2026): breaks down to highly toxic **Carbofuran**; harms birds and aquatic life; recommended by the **Registration Committee** under the **Insecticides Act, 1968** |
!TRAP Carbosulfan is the pesticide proposed for ban; Carbofuran is its toxic breakdown product.
### 3. FERTILISERS, MANURES AND SOIL AMENDMENTS
#### 3.1 Fertiliser → Effect
| Fertiliser | Effect / fact |
|---|---|
| **Urea** | **Vegetative growth** (wheat and most crops); about **46% N** → about **2.2 kg urea for 1 kg N** |
| **Phosphatic** | **Root development**, flowering, fruiting |
| **Potassic** | **Disease resistance**, drought tolerance, **quality** |
| **DAP** | Balanced **N and P** supply; **(NH4)2HPO4**; **18% N, 46% P2O5** |
| Neem-coated urea | Slow release, higher nitrogen-use efficiency |
#### 3.2 Organic Manures and Amendments
| Input | Composition | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Vermicompost** | Organic waste digested by **earthworms** | Restores soil microbes and organic carbon |
| **Farmyard manure** | Dung, urine and litter of farm animals | Slow-release nutrients, better soil structure |
| **Compost** | Decomposed crop residues and organic wastes | Recycles nutrients |
| **Green manure** | Legumes ploughed in green – **sunn hemp, dhaincha** | Adds nitrogen and organic matter |
| **Biochar** | Charcoal from **pyrolysis** of biomass | **Long-term carbon sequestration**; water and nutrient retention |
- **Earthworms** improve soil fertility – called **"farmer's friends"**.
- **Humus**: an **organic colloid**; dark material from decayed plant and animal matter.
- **Biochar** is also part of the growing medium in **vertical farming** and helps nitrogen-fixing microorganisms.
- AP plans **1,500 MT/day** of manure from **107 waste-processing centres** (Feb 2026).
#### 3.3 Green Manure
- A crop **grown and ploughed in while green** (or just after flowering); **legumes preferred** (fix nitrogen in **root nodules**) – clover, vetch, **sesbania**, sunn hemp, dhaincha.
- Green manure = green **undecomposed** material used as manure: grown green-manure crops, or **green leaf** from wastelands, bunds and forests.
- Adds organic matter (**humus**), fixes nitrogen → better soil structure, water retention, microbial activity.
- **Sunhemp** = most suitable green manuring crop.
- **Cowpea** and **green gram** = pulse, fodder and green manure (legumes).
### 4. BIOFERTILISERS
- Living microbes that add or mobilise nutrients for crops.
| Biofertiliser | Organism type | Crop / function |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhizobium** | Symbiotic bacterium | **Legumes**; root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen |
| **Azotobacter** | Free-living (non-symbiotic) bacterium | Fixes nitrogen for **non-legume** crops |
| **Azospirillum** | Associative bacterium | Cereal roots; fixes nitrogen in **rice** rhizosphere |
| **Azolla** | Water fern with cyanobacterium **Anabaena** | Nitrogen for **paddy** |
| **Blue-green algae** | Cyanobacteria | Fix nitrogen in **paddy** fields |
| **PSB (phosphobacteria)** | Bacteria | **Solubilise phosphate** for crops |
| **Mycorrhiza** | **Fungus** | The fungal biofertiliser (phosphorus uptake by roots) |
!TRAP Mycorrhiza is a fungus, not a bacterium; Azolla is a fern that works with Anabaena.
### 5. BIOPESTICIDES
- **Biodegradable, low toxicity, short persistence (short-lived)** – a key IPM component.
| Biopesticide | Source | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Bt** | Bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* | Caterpillars, **bollworms** |
| **Trichoderma** | **Fungus** (antagonist) | **Soil-borne fungal diseases** – wilt, root rot |
| **NPV** | **Baculovirus** | **Helicoverpa**, **Spodoptera** caterpillars |
| **Neem** | **Azadirachtin** | Wide range of insects (antifeedant) |
| **Beauveria bassiana** | **Entomopathogenic fungus** | **Borers, whitefly**, other insects |
!KEY Bt = bacterium; Trichoderma and Beauveria = fungi; NPV = virus.
### 6. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM)
- **IPM (FAO)**: use all suitable methods to keep pests **below economic injury levels**.
- Aim = **optimise, not maximise**, control.
#### 6.1 Components
| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| Cultural | Crop rotation, change of irrigation |
| Mechanical | Traps, mulches |
| Biological | Natural enemies – **green lacewings against aphids**; **pheromone traps** |
| Chemical | **Only when needed** |
!TRAP In IPM, chemical control is the last resort, not the first or usual line of defence.
### 7. OTHER AGROCHEMICALS AND REPELLENTS
#### 7.1 Pesticide Classes
| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Insecticides | **DDT**, **BHC** (benzene hexachloride), Sevin, Bt, **aluminium phosphide** |
| Fungicides | **Bordeaux mixture** (**copper sulphate + calcium hydroxide/lime**); **blue vitriol** (CuSO4) |
| Herbicides | **2,4-D** (broadleaf weeds); **sodium chlorate** |
| Rodenticides | **Zinc phosphide**, **potassium cyanide** (rat poison) |
| Broad-spectrum | **Chloropicrin** – fungicide, herbicide, insecticide and also **tear gas** |
- **Copper sulphate** (blue vitriol, **neela thotha**) destroys fungi in **water tanks**; also a crop fungicide.
#### 7.2 Mosquito Repellents
| Substance | Note |
|---|---|
| **Lemongrass** | Natural repellent; **citronella oil** |
| **Pyrethrum** | Natural botanical insecticide; acts on insect **nervous system** |
| **Allethrin** | **Synthetic pyrethroid**; active chemical against mosquitoes |
| **Prallethrin** | Pyrethroid; household **liquid vaporiser** |
| **Neem (Margo)** | Chief natural mosquito repellent |
### 8. ORGANIC FARMING – MILESTONES, SCHEMES, CERTIFICATION
#### 8.1 Sikkim
- **Sikkim**: India's **first fully organic State** and the world's **first 100% organic State** (**2016**; process began **2003**).
- Won the **Future Policy Gold Award, 2018 (FAO)**.
- PM cited Sikkim's model at its **50th Statehood** celebrations (May 2026).
- NE organic-farming states: **Sikkim, Meghalaya, Nagaland**; **Assam not among them**.
#### 8.2 Schemes and Certification
| Scheme / system | Launch | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **PKVY** (Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana) | 2015-16 | **First comprehensive CSS** for organic farming; clusters of **50 farmers / 50 acres**; 15.08 lakh ha, 25.38 lakh farmers, Rs 2,524.68 crore released (to 31 Jul 2025); **jaivikkheti.in** portal |
| **MOVCDNER** | 2015-16 | Organic value chains in the **North-Eastern Region**; PKVY covers the rest |
| **PGS-India** | – | **Participatory**, farmer-group certification for the **domestic** market |
| **NPOP** | **2001** | **Third-party** certification, mainly for **exports**; run by **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**; implemented by **APEDA** |
!PAIR Certification: PGS-India | Farmer groups | Domestic market || NPOP | Third-party | Exports (APEDA)
!TRAP NPOP is under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, not Rural Development or Agriculture.
### 9. NATURAL FARMING AND PERMACULTURE
- **Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF)**: developed by **Subhash Palekar**.
- **Permaculture**: coined by **Bill Mollison and David Holmgren** (Australia).
- Natural farming **cuts cultivation cost by 20-40%**; it is **knowledge-intensive** and yields may **dip during transition**.
#### 9.1 Inputs and Practices
| Input / practice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Jeevamrutha** | Microbial culture from **cow dung and urine** |
| **Beejamrutha** | **Seed treatment** |
| **Achhadana** | **Mulching** |
| **Whapasa** | Soil **aeration and moisture** |
#### 9.2 National Mission on Natural Farming (NMNF)
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Approved | **25 Nov 2024** |
| Outlay | **Rs 2,481 crore** (Centre 1,584 + **States 897**) till March 2026 |
| Coverage | **8.80 lakh ha**, 18.19 lakh farmers, 17,632 clusters (by March 2026) |
| Field workers | **70,021 Krishi Sakhis** |
- **BPKP** (Bharatiya Prakritik Krishi Paddhati) also promoted natural farming.
#### 9.3 Organic vs Natural Farming
| Feature | Organic farming | Natural farming |
|---|---|---|
| Inputs | **Approved organic inputs** (may be bought) | **On-farm, cow-based** inputs |
| Certification | **Certified** (PGS / NPOP) | No same certification |
| Example scheme | PKVY, MOVCDNER | NMNF, APCNF |
### 10. ANDHRA PRADESH – COMMUNITY-MANAGED NATURAL FARMING (APCNF)
- **APCNF**: formerly **Zero Budget Natural Farming**; bio-inputs **jeevamrutham, ghanajeevamrutham**; **over 7 lakh farmers** (Feb 2026).
- Implementing agency: **Rythu Sadhikara Samstha (RySS)**, a not-for-profit body under the AP Department of Agriculture (named implementing agency by a 2016 government order).
- **2018** = AP's **Year of Natural Farming**; promoted ZBNF.
- Pesticide reduction: a **Kurnool** study found APCNF farmers about **35% less likely to use pesticides** than conventional farmers, though about half still used some.
- Global recognition: **Gulbenkian Prize for Humanity, 2024**.
- **8 Jul 2026**: **Kenyan delegation** praised APCNF as a climate-resilient model after visiting **Guntur** farms (20+ crop plots; trap crops **marigold, castor**).
- **NTR district**: natural-farming model with **QR-based traceability** for organic produce.
- 23 May 2026: ZBNF called the core vision of the CM.
- Feb 2026: **200 new certified PKVY organic clusters** in tribal **Visakhapatnam**; PKVY is AP's initiative on sustainable and organic farming.
- **Kharif 2026 green manure**: Rs 62.20 crore subsidy; 91,918 quintals of seed, of which **sunn hemp (Jilledu) 50,311** quintals.

### 12. ADVANTAGES AND LIMITATIONS OF ORGANIC / NATURAL FARMING
| Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Reduced chemical input** | **Yield dip for 2-3 years** in transition |
| **Improved soil health**, biodiversity | **Certification** cost and effort (organic) |
| **Residue-free produce** | Weak market premium for natural produce |
| Lower cost (natural: 20-40%) | Knowledge-intensive; ZBNF's scalability questioned |
!TRAP Organic farming's main benefit is reduced chemicals and better soil health – NOT guaranteed higher yields.
