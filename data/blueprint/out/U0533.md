## U0533 | FINANCIAL MARKETS – MONEY MARKET INSTRUMENTS AND CAPITAL MARKET SEGMENTS
### 1. MONEY MARKET VS CAPITAL MARKET
| Basis | Money market | Capital market |
|---|---|---|
| Tenure | **Up to 1 year** (overnight to 364 days) | **Above 1 year** (long-term debt and equity) |
| Regulator | **RBI** | **SEBI** |
| Instruments | T-bills, CP, CD, call money, TREPS/CBLO, commercial bills | Equities, debentures, bonds |
| Sub-markets | **Call Money Market, Treasury Bill Market** | **Government Bond Market, Stock Market** |
- Individuals do not directly take part in the money market; it has no physical location.
- Per the PYQ key, **RBI is not a money-market participant** (it regulates it).
!TRAP **Equity shares** are a capital-market (pure) instrument; T-bills and CPs are money-market instruments.
### 2. ORGANISED VS UNORGANISED MONEY MARKET
| Organised | Unorganised |
|---|---|
| RBI, commercial banks, DFHI, regulated NBFCs, **money market mutual funds**, insurers | **Indigenous bankers**, **moneylenders**, chit funds, hundis, unregulated finance companies |
| Controlled and coordinated by RBI | Not controlled by RBI |
- **Co-operative credit institutions** sit between the two.
- **Call money market** is the **most unstable part** of the organised money market: repayable on demand.
### 3. MONEY MARKET INSTRUMENTS
| Instrument | Maturity | Issuer / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Commercial Paper (CP)** | **7 days to 1 year** | Started **1990** (Vaghul); **unsecured promissory note** of eligible corporates; credit source for corporate industry |
| **Certificate of Deposit (CD)** | Banks: up to 1 year; FIs: **1-3 years** | Started **1989** (Vaghul); banks and FIs |
| **Call money** | **Overnight** | Inter-bank |
| **Notice money** | **2-14 days** | Inter-bank |
| **CBLO** | Short term | **Collateralised Borrowing and Lending Obligation**; by **CCIL** from **20 January 2003**; needs collateral |
| **TREPS** | Short term | **Tri-party repo**; replaced CBLO; third party handles collateral and settlement |
| **Commercial bills** | Usually 90 days | Trade bills drawn by firms, discounted by banks |
!TRAP **Vaghul Committee**: CD **1989**, CP **1990**.
### 4. GOVERNMENT SECURITIES AND THE GILT-EDGED MARKET
#### 4.1 Types
| Security | Issued by | Maturity |
|---|---|---|
| **Treasury Bills** | **Centre**, sold by **RBI auction** | **91, 182, 364 days** |
| **Dated G-Secs** | Central Government | **One year or more**; pay coupon |
| **SDLs** | **State Governments** | Long-term dated securities |
| **Cash Management Bills** | Centre | Very short term; temporary cash mismatches |
- **G-Sec**: tradeable paper showing debt of the Central or a State Government; issued by **RBI** under the **Government Securities Act, 2006**.
#### 4.2 Treasury Bills
- **Zero-coupon**: issued at a **discount**, redeemed at **face value**; no periodic interest (Government bonds pay interest).
- **First issued in India in 1917** (First World War).
#### 4.3 Gilt-Edged Market
- Market of **Treasury Bills and Government bonds**; interest and repayment on due date are sure → **default risk almost zero**.
- **Not** corporate paper.
- **Primary dealers**: RBI-regulated underwriters and market makers in G-Secs; **SBI DFHI = largest**.
- **RBI Retail Direct**: individuals buy G-Secs directly via a **Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account**.
- Who trades: banks, PDs, insurers, MFs, provident funds, FPIs, individuals.
- **OMO**: RBI **sells** G-Secs → **absorbs** liquidity; **buys** → **injects** liquidity.
- **FAR** (June 2026) now covers new 15-, 30-, 40-year G-Secs and **sovereign green bonds**; record FPI bond inflow ₹55,518 crore (June 2026).
- **AP angle**: AP plans SDL borrowings of **₹73,383 crore** in 2026-27.
- US government debt is backed only by the faith of the Government; a default does not cancel bondholders' legal claims.
### 5. CAPITAL MARKET – STRUCTURE AND INSTRUMENTS
#### 5.1 Structure
- Gilt-edged market; **industrial securities market** (**primary + secondary**); DFIs (IFCI, ICICI, IDBI, SIDBI, UTI); intermediaries (merchant banks, MFs, leasing, venture capital companies).
#### 5.2 Instruments
| Type | Instruments |
|---|---|
| Pure | Equity, preference shares, debentures, bonds |
| **Hybrid** | **Convertible preference shares, partly/optionally convertible debentures** |
| **Venture capital** | Long-term **start-up capital** for new firms with growth potential; a form of **private equity**; promoting it = entrepreneur development |
| **Private equity** | Investment in unlisted firms |
| **ETFs** | Trade on exchanges close to NAV (CPSE ETF, Bharat-22 ETF) |
- **Sweat equity shares**: given to directors/employees at a discount or for non-cash value (know-how, IPR).
- **CCP regulators**: RBI – **CCIL** (money, G-Sec, forex); SEBI – **NSCCL, ICCL** (capital markets); IFSCA only IFSC entities.
- Corporate bonds: 16% of GDP (Korea 79%); ~98% private placement. CRISIL (Dec 2024): to reach **₹100-120 lakh crore by FY2030**; CAGR ~9%.
- **Budget 2026-27**: market-making for corporate bonds, derivatives on corporate bond indices, larger municipal bond markets.
### 6. ALTERNATIVE INVESTMENT FUNDS, REITs AND InvITs
- **AIFs**: privately pooled funds from expert investors; defined under **SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012**; include hedge funds and venture capital (not plain bonds, stocks).
| SEBI AIF category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Category I** | **Venture capital, angel**, SME, social venture, **infrastructure** funds |
| **Category II** | **Private equity, debt funds** (e.g. Self-Reliant India Fund) |
| **Category III** | **Hedge funds**, leveraged trading; may trade commodity derivatives |
- **September 2025**: SEBI added an AIF category only for **accredited investors**.
- **REITs** pool **real-estate** assets; **InvITs** pool **infrastructure** assets; used for asset monetisation. InvIT dividends and interest are **taxable at slab rate**.
### 7. BOND TYPES AND YIELDS
#### 7.1 Bond Terms
- **Bondholder** = creditor to the issuer; **maturity** = repayment date; **coupon** = interest; **face value** = nominal value; **yield** = investor's return at market price.
#### 7.2 Types
| Bond | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Green bond** | Only **environmental/climate** projects; long-term; tax incentives; first by **EIB (2007)**; World Bank labelled one 2008 |
| **Social bond** | Only social projects |
| **Sustainability bond** | **Both** environmental and social projects |
| **Masala bond** | **Rupee-denominated**, issued **overseas**; **investor bears currency risk** |
| **Zero-coupon** | Discount, no periodic interest (e.g. T-bills) |
| Inflation-indexed | Principal/coupon adjusted to inflation |
| **Sovereign Gold Bond** | **2015**; **2.5% interest** + gold price; **new issues stopped** |
#### 7.3 What Moves Yields
- **Bond price and yield move in opposite directions**.
- **Higher policy rates, inflation, government borrowing** → yields **rise**.
- **RBI OMO purchases** + foreign inflows → yields **fall**.
- BRIC coined by **Jim O'Neill (2001)**; first BRIC summit **2009, Yekaterinburg**; K.V. Kamath first NDB President (2015).
