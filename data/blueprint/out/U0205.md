## U0205 | EXTREMISTS – LAL-BAL-PAL AND AUROBINDO; IDEOLOGY; SURAT SPLIT 1907
### 1. MODERATES VS EXTREMISTS
- **Extremists** = **Assertive Nationalists**, the aggressive wing of the Congress; influence grew after **1906**.
- Goal: **Swaraj** (complete freedom), more immediate than the Moderates' gradual self-government.
- They rejected the **"politics of prayer, petition and protest"**; Tilak blamed the Congress for it, and the prayer-and-petition policy ended under him.
#### 1.1 Comparison
| Basis | Moderates | Extremists |
|---|---|---|
| Period | **1885–1905** | **1905–1916** (Assertive nationalism) |
| Goal | Self-government **within the Empire**, gradual reform | **Swaraj**: complete freedom |
| Method | **3 Ps**: prayers, petitions, protests; **constitutional agitation** | **3 Ss**: self-reliance, self-sacrifice, strong will; mass agitation |
| View of British | Faith in British justice and liberalism | British rule exploitative; no faith in British generosity |
| Swadeshi–Boycott | Unwilling to extend it | Wanted it wider, all-India |
| Social reform | Pro-reform | Comparatively **orthodox** (Tilak opposed the **Age of Consent Bill**) |
!TRAP 3 Ps = Moderates; 3 Ss = Extremists.
#### 1.2 Classification of leaders
- **Moderates:** Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gokhale, S.N. Banerjee, Ranade, W.C. Bonnerjee, R.C. Dutt, Anand Mohan Bose, G.V. Joshi.
- **Extremists:** Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh (the **four pillars**: Lal, Bal, Pal, Ghose).
| Regional group | Leader |
|---|---|
| **Maharashtra** | Bal Gangadhar Tilak |
| **Bengal** | Bipin Chandra Pal (and Aurobindo) |
| **Punjab** | Lala Lajpat Rai |
- **Swadeshi in Delhi:** Syed Haidar Raja.
!TRAP APPSC 2018: the militant leader is **Tilak**; Ranade, Gokhale and G.V. Joshi were Moderates. Lajpat Rai and Tilak were **not** Moderates.
### 2. EXTREMIST IDEOLOGY
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Swaraj** | Complete freedom from British control, not colonial self-government |
| **Passive resistance** | Boycott, Swadeshi, national education, mass agitation, self-sacrifice; doctrine set out by **Aurobindo** in *Bande Mataram* |
| **Spiritual nationalism** | Nation as **mother goddess** (Bande Mataram, *Bhavani Mandir*); Hindu festivals for mass contact; pride in India's past |
| **Atmashakti** | Self-reliance; constructive work such as **cooperatives** |
- Ideology: self-government by **aggressive means** instead of petitions and constitutional ways.
- **Contribution:** made nationalism a **mass movement** and Swaraj the goal.
- Word **Swaraj** was first used by **Dayanand Saraswati**; as a political demand it is linked with **Tilak** ("Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it").

### 4. BAL GANGADHAR TILAK
- Born **23 July 1856, Ratnagiri**; joined Congress **1890**; **never INC President**.
- With **Agarkar**: New English School, **Deccan Education Society** → **Fergusson College**.
- **Kesari** (Marathi, vernacular) and **Mahratta** (English), both **1881**; Agarkar an early editor.
- Revived festivals: public **Ganesh Utsav (1893)**, **Shivaji Jayanti (1895)**.
- **June 1897:** **Chapekar brothers** killed **Rand** (Plague Committee, Poona) and **Lt Ayerst**; Tilak accused of conspiracy.
#### 4.1 Sedition trials
| Year | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **1897** | 18 months; **Max Muller** pleaded for him ("my interest in Tilak is that of a Sanskrit scholar") |
| **1908** | **6 years in Mandalay (Burma)**; released **16 June 1914** |
| 1916 | Acquitted |
- Books: **Gita Rahasya** (Marathi, written in **Mandalay jail**); **The Arctic Home in the Vedas** (Arctic/North Pole as Aryan homeland).
- Quote: "If God were to tolerate untouchability, I would not recognise him as God."
- **Home Rule League:** **28 April 1916**; president Joseph Baptista; Besant's League **September 1916**.
- Backed Non-Cooperation but died **1 August 1920**; bier lifted by **Gandhi, Shaukat Ali, Saifuddin Kitchlew**; elegy by **Hasrat Mohani**.
- **23 July 2026:** PM paid tribute on his birth anniversary.
### 5. LAJPAT RAI AND BIPIN CHANDRA PAL
#### 5.1 Lala Lajpat Rai (1865–1928)
- **Arya Samaj** leader; political guru **Mazzini**; translated *The Duty of Man* into Urdu.
- **Deported to Mandalay, 1907**, with **Ajit Singh**.
- Went to the **USA** after 1908; **Young India** (USA, 1916); **Indian Home Rule League of America (1917)**.
- Works: biographies of **Mazzini, Garibaldi, Shivaji, Shri Krishna, Dayanand**; *The Story of My Deportation* (1908); *Arya Samaj* (1915); *The United States of America: A Hindu's Impression*; **Unhappy India** (India vs USA and Britain).
- Lahore 1920: Urdu daily **Bande Mataram**, English weekly **The People**; also **The Punjabee**.
- **Only one of Lal-Bal-Pal to be INC President** – special session, **Calcutta 1920**; elected to the Central Assembly.
- First used the **safety-valve theory** (in *Young India*) to attack Moderates: Congress was **Dufferin's** idea.
- Called Congress sessions the "**Annual National Festival of Educated Indians**"; said Congress "was neither inspired by the people, nor devised or planned by them".
- Slogan "**Go back Simon**"; lathi-charge at **Lahore**; died **17 November 1928**.
#### 5.2 Bipin Chandra Pal (1858–1932)
- **Brahmo Samaj** member; called Congress a "**Begging Institute**".
- Weekly **New India (1901)**; *The Soul of India*; co-editor of *Bande Mataram*.
- **April 1907: toured Andhra**, promoting the Vande Mataram movement.
- **Arrested 1907** for refusing to testify in the *Bande Mataram* case; **not deported to Mandalay**.
- Lived in **England 1908–1911**.
!TRAP Abroad / away: Lajpat Rai = **USA**; Pal = **England**; Aurobindo = **Pondicherry**; Tilak = **Mandalay**.
### 6. AUROBINDO GHOSH (15 AUG 1872 – 5 DEC 1950)
- **New Lamps for Old** (**1893–94**): nine articles in **Indu Prakash** (Bombay); attacked Moderates; Congress cut off from the proletariat.
- Editor of **Bande Mataram** (with Pal); criticised the partition there; first principal of **Bengal National College**.
- **1908:** arrested after the **Maniktalla Garden** raid; about a year as undertrial in the **Alipore Bomb Case**; **acquitted 1909**; retired to **Pondicherry 1910**.
- Political career **1901–1910**; "Political freedom is the life-breath of a nation."
- Works: *Bhavani Mandir*, *Savitri*, **The Life Divine**, *Essays on the Gita*, *The Synthesis of Yoga*, *The Ideal of Human Unity*, *The Foundation of Indian Culture*.

#### 7.2 Gokhale (the Moderate contrast)
- Born **9 May 1866, Ratnagiri**; Elphinstone College 1884; professor at Fergusson College; follower of **Ranade**.
- Associate editor of *Sudharak* at 20; **Congress President at 39 (Benaras 1905)**.
- Compared **Curzon** to **Aurangzeb**; visited South Africa **1912**; told Gandhi to spend a year in India "**ears open but mouth shut**".
### 8. CALCUTTA 1906 AND SURAT SPLIT 1907
#### 8.1 Calcutta 1906
- President **Dadabhai Naoroji**; split averted by compromise.
- **Four resolutions:** **Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education**.
!TRAP **Annulment of the partition** was NOT one of the four Calcutta resolutions.
#### 8.2 Surat 1907 (December) – first split of the INC
- President: **Rash Behari Ghosh** (Moderates' choice).
| Side | Leaders | Demand |
|---|---|---|
| Extremists (**Garam Dal**) | **Tilak** | Tilak or Lajpat Rai as president; keep the 1906 resolutions; boycott all-India |
| Moderates (**Naram Dal**) | **Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta** | Drop the 1906 resolutions; keep the movement to Bengal |
- **Immediate cause:** quarrel over the four Calcutta resolutions.
- **Basic cause (UPSC 2016):** Extremists' **lack of faith in Moderates' ability to bargain**.
- Outcome: Moderates (majority) pushed Extremists out; scuffle; session suspended. By **1910** Moderates controlled Congress (Tilak jailed, Aurobindo retired, Pal arrested, Lajpat Rai away).
- **Reunion: Lucknow 1916.**
- The **Swadeshi Movement** (reaction to Curzon's partition) helped cause the split; partition did not end the Moderates' role.
!KEY Calcutta 1906 = Naoroji; Surat 1907 = Rash Behari Ghosh; Benaras 1905 = Gokhale.

### 13. HIGH-PROBABILITY MCQ TRAPS

3. Tilak was released in **1914**, not 1916.
4. **Pal was arrested**, not deported; Lajpat Rai was deported to Mandalay (1907).
5. Annulment of partition was **not** a Calcutta resolution.
6. *New Lamps for Old* = **Aurobindo**, in *Indu Prakash*.
7. Satya Shodhak Samaj = **Phule**, not Tilak; Bombay Chronicle = **Mehta**, not Gokhale.
8. Gokhale = **Moderate**, Gandhi's guru; never an Extremist.
9. Basic cause of Surat = **lack of faith in Moderates' bargaining**.
10. Lucknow Pact (1916) is **outside** the Extremist phase.
