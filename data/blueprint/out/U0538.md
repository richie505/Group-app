## U0538 | INDIAN TAX SYSTEM – DIRECT AND INDIRECT TAXES, TAX BUOYANCY, TAX-GDP RATIO, PROGRESSIVITY
### 1. TAX, FEE, CESS AND SURCHARGE
| Levy | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Tax** | **Compulsory** money burden imposed **by legislative authority** to support government; **no quid pro quo** (APPSC 2023) |
| **Fee** | Paid for a **specific service** – involves **quid pro quo** |
| **Cess** | "Tax on tax" for a **specific purpose**; ends when purpose is met; **not shared** with States |
| **Surcharge** | **Extra charge on tax payable** (% of tax); usually permanent; **none if no tax is due**; **not shared** (**Article 271**) |
- Cesses and surcharges are **outside the divisible pool** – a federal grievance of States.
- Example cess: **Clean Energy Cess** on coal (Finance Act 2010) → renamed **Clean Environment Cess** (Budget 2016-17).
- Budget 2016-17: **15% surcharge** on income above **₹1 crore**.
- **Levied by States / local bodies**: profession tax, road tax, entertainment tax, property tax; **Union**: customs, corporation tax.
!TRAP "Pecuniary burden imposed by legislative authority to support government" = **Tax**, not fee or cess.
### 2. CONSTITUTIONAL BASIS OF TAXATION
| Article / entry | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 265** | **No tax levied or collected except by authority of law** |
| Taxing entries | **Union List 15, State List 20, Concurrent List 3** |
| Residuary taxing power | **Parliament** (gift, wealth, expenditure taxes) |
| **80th Amendment, 2000** | Net proceeds of **all Union taxes** (minus cess, surcharge, collection cost) shareable on FC advice; effective **1 April 1996** |
| **Article 276** | **Profession tax** – State levy; ceiling **₹2,500 a year** – not a Union source (APPSC 2018) |
| **State List Entry 46** | **Agricultural income tax** – **States only** |
- **Union taxes**: income tax (non-agricultural), corporation tax, customs, Union excise, CGST/IGST.
- **Union Government alone**: **customs duties, corporation tax** (Union List).
- **Corporation tax** – levied and collected by the **Union**; **not levied by States**; now **shared** with States (per FC).
- **Shared with States**: income tax, corporate tax, customs duty.
- **Agricultural income tax** – **not shared** by the Centre; a **State subject**; exempt under Income-tax Act **Sec 10(1)**; nursery saplings = agricultural income; poultry and dairy are not.
- **Entertainment tax** – **not levied by the Union**; **State List (List II)**.
- **Road tax** – State governments.
- **Sales tax on toothpaste** (pre-GST) – imposed and collected by the **State Government**.
### 3. DIRECT VS INDIRECT TAXES – CLASSIFICATION
#### 3.1 Comparison
| Basis | Direct tax | Indirect tax |
|---|---|---|
| Burden | **Cannot be shifted**; impact and incidence on the **same person** | **Shifted** to consumer through **prices** |
| Examples | **Income, corporation, wealth, capital gains, property, gift, estate, dividend tax, STT** | **Customs, excise, service tax, sales tax, VAT, GST**, entertainment, luxury, entry taxes |
| Administered by | **CBDT** (Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963) | **CBIC** |
| Price effect | Generally **non-inflationary** | **Inflationary** |
| Nature | Usually **progressive** | May be **regressive** |
- **Income tax** – direct; does **not directly increase the price** of a commodity.
- **Income, corporate and wealth taxes** – **all direct** (APPSC 2021).
- **Sales tax** – **not a direct tax**.
- **STT** – a **direct tax** though collected by the exchange (from **1 Oct 2004**).
- **"Paper taxes"**: wealth, gift, estate duty – never yielded much revenue.
- **Impact** = on whom the tax is **first imposed**; **incidence** = who **finally bears** it. **Shifting** moves burden **forward** (consumers) or **backward** (suppliers).
#### 3.2 Base of Each Tax
| Tax | Type | Base |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax | Direct | Income of individuals, HUFs, firms, trusts |
| **Corporation tax** | Direct | **Net profit of companies** (22% base, effective ~25.17%) |
| Capital gains tax | Direct | Gain on sale of capital asset |
| **Wealth tax** | Direct | Net wealth – **1957 to 2015 (abolished)** |
| Customs duty | Indirect | Import / export (**Customs Act, 1962**) |
| **Excise duty** | Indirect | **Production, sale or licence** of goods; paid by **manufacturer** |
| Service tax | Indirect | Services (subsumed in GST) |
| VAT | Indirect | Value added at each stage |
| GST | Indirect | Supply of goods and services |
### 4. TAXABLE EVENT FOR EACH TAX
| Tax | Taxable event |
|---|---|
| **Central excise** | **Manufacture** of goods |
| **Customs** | **Import or export** |
| **Sales tax / VAT** | **Sale** of goods |
| **Service tax** | **Provision** of service |
| **GST** | **Supply** of goods or services (from **1 July 2017**) |
| Corporation tax | Company income / profit |
!KEY GST = supply; excise = manufacture; customs = import/export; VAT = sale.
### 5. PROGRESSIVE, PROPORTIONAL, REGRESSIVE TAXES
| Type | Feature | Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| **Progressive** | **Average tax burden rises with income**; higher marginal rates on higher slabs ("ability to pay") | **Income tax** |
| **Regressive** | Poor pay a **higher share** of income | Excise, customs, GST on essentials |
| **Proportional** | **One rate for all** | Sales tax (flat rate) |
| **Degressive** | Rate **rises at first, then becomes flat** | Capped slabs |
- **Indirect taxes are regressive** – charged at the **same rates for all income groups**.
- **Income tax** – **progressive and direct** (not collected by States, not proportional).
- **India's tax system** – **progressive in nature but overall degressive**: progressive direct taxes + regressive indirect taxes; becoming more progressive as the direct share rises.
- Income is best spread through **progressive taxation plus progressive expenditure** (welfare, subsidies, social schemes).
### 6. COMPOSITION OF TAX REVENUE – THEN AND NOW
#### 6.1 Historical Composition
- **First four decades of planning**: main Central revenue from **indirect taxes** (excise, customs, sales tax).
- Two largest Central tax sources (1995 question): **customs duty and income tax**.
- Largest revenue source: **Union excise** in 2004-05 and 2005-06; **corporation tax** in 2005-06, 2007-08 and 2013-14 (as per those years' questions).
- **Excise share** of gross tax revenue fell sharply: **30.4% (2005-06) → 20.8% (2007-08)** (ES 2009-10).
- Indirect share fell from 1990 to 2011; direct share in GNP (2004 question): about **5%**.
#### 6.2 Now
- **Direct tax share** in gross tax revenue: **61.2% (2026-27 BE)** vs indirect **38.8%**; 58.8% in FY25; 55.5% average FY22-FY25.
- 2026-27 BE: direct taxes **6.9% of GDP**, indirect **4.3%** (2024-25 actuals 6.7% / 4.8%).
- **Income tax** = **single largest tax source** (2025-26 and 2026-27: 21% of the rupee).
!TRAP Subsidy is **not public revenue**; it is expenditure. Income tax, borrowings and VAT are revenue.

### 8. TAX-GDP RATIO
- **Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / nominal GDP** – yardstick of fiscal capacity.
- **Low ratio** → large **informal sector** or high **evasion** (~4.3% of tax revenue lost yearly).
- **Falling ratio** → revenue growing **slower than GDP** (buoyancy < 1) → less fiscal space, more borrowing.
| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Centre gross tax revenue | **9.9% of GDP in 2019-20** (ES 2021-22; APPSC 2021); ~**11.5%** post-pandemic (FY22-FY25, ES 2025-26) |
| States | Own tax ≈ 7% of GSDP |
| Combined | ~19.6% |
| 2008-09 | **16.39%** (Centre + States about 16.5%) |
| India average (2001-22) | **16.36%** – lowest among emerging economies; below OECD |
| **World Bank tipping point** | **15%** |
### 9. TAX BUOYANCY, ELASTICITY AND EFFECTS OF TAX CHANGES
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Buoyancy** | % change in revenue / % change in GDP, **including discretionary changes** (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP |
| **Elasticity** | Same response **excluding discretionary changes** |
| **Laffer curve** | **Inverted-U**; revenue **zero at 0% and 100%** rates; **Arthur Laffer (1974)**; term by Jude Wanniski |
| **Base widening** | More taxpayers, fewer exemptions rather than rate hikes – **Chelliah approach** |
- **Recent buoyancy (2026-27 BE)**: gross **0.8**; **direct 1.1** vs **indirect 0.3** (GST not keeping pace).
- Buoyant revenues helped the fiscal deficit fall to **4.4% of GDP in FY26** (ES 2025-26).
- **Cut in direct taxes** → higher **disposable income** and **consumption demand**; may also help savings and investment (APPSC 2018). E.g. income up to **₹12 lakh** tax-free under the new regime.
- Caution: personal income tax growth in 2025-26 only 0.037% after rate cuts; Centre's GTR grew only 3.7% in Q1 2026-27.
- **Simpler tax system** raises **productivity** by removing **deadweight costs** and resource-allocation distortions (APPSC G1 2024).
- Standing Committee on Finance (chair **Bhartruhari Mahtab**) criticised "punitive" income-tax enforcement.
- **Direct Tax Code** – to replace the **Income Tax Act, 1961** with a single law.
### 10. TAX REFORM COMMITTEES AND VAT/SERVICE TAX HISTORY
#### 10.1 Committees
| Committee | Year | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **L.K. Jha** (Indirect Taxation Enquiry) | **1976** | Proposed **VAT** at manufacturing stage → MODVAT (not Shome) |
| **Raja J. Chelliah** (Tax Reforms Committee) | 1991-93 | **Direct and indirect** taxes; lower rates, broader base; service tax |
| **Vijay Kelkar Task Forces** | 2002 | **Both direct and indirect taxes** (two reports) |
| **Parthasarathi Shome** | 2012 | GAAR; scrap retrospective tax |
| **Tax Administration Reform Commission** (Shome) | 2013 | Tax administration restructuring |
#### 10.2 Chronology
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| **MODVAT** (excise, to ease cascading) | **1 March 1986** |
| **Service tax** (Chelliah advice) | **1994-95** |
| **CENVAT** (replaced MODVAT; Union excise) | **1 April 2000** |
| **First State VAT – Haryana** | **1 April 2003** |
| **State VAT nationwide** | **1 April 2005** |
| **GST** | **1 July 2017** |
- **Service tax rates**: 10% → 12% (Budget 2006-07); 12% → 14% (Budget 2015-16); from 2012-13, 12% on all services except the **negative list**.
- **VAT**: uniform, **non-cascading**; taxes value added at **all stages** between production and final sale; improves revenue buoyancy; now merged into GST but still applies to goods outside GST. "Self-enforcing system" does **not** explain VAT's idea.
- **Sales tax was not levied in Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep** (question period); GST covers all India.
- **Project Saksham** – new indirect tax network of CBEC (now CBIC); approved by CCEA, September 2016.
