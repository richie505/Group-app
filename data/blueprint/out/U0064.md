## U0064 | GUPTA ADMINISTRATION – FEUDALISM DEBATE, LAND GRANTS, AGRAHARAS, REVENUE AND LOCAL BODIES
### 1. NATURE OF GUPTA KINGSHIP
- **Divine claims:** kings likened to **Vishnu**; **Samudragupta** equated with **Kubera (Dhanada), Varuna, Indra and Yama (Antaka)**.
- **Succession:** hereditary, but **primogeniture was not firm**.
- Imperial titles: Maharajadhiraja, Parameshvara, Paramabhattaraka.
#### 1.1 Centralised Mauryas vs Decentralised Guptas
| Point | Mauryas | Guptas |
|---|---|---|
| Control | **Centralised**, state-regulated | **Decentralised** – run through **samantas** (feudatories) and grantees |
| Offices | Salaried bureaucracy | Many offices **hereditary** (e.g. Harisena inherited his post) |
| Pay | Cash | Often in **land or kind** |
- Samudragupta **reinstated** some defeated rulers as feudatories.
- **Tax trend:** land taxes rose, trade taxes fell.
### 2. OFFICIALS AND THEIR FUNCTIONS
| Official | Function |
|---|---|
| **Kumaramatya** | Top officers ranked with princes; ministers and commanders chosen from them; own office (adhikarana); often paid in cash |
| **Sandhivigrahika** | Minister of **peace and war** |
| **Mahadandanayaka** | Chief **judicial or military** officer |
| **Dandapashika** | District **police** officer |
| **Akshapataladhikrita** | Keeper of **royal records** |
| **Pustapala** | Keeper of **land-transfer records** |
| **Dutaka** | Spy or messenger |
- **Amatya** (Satavahana period) – minister who advised the king.
!TRAP Dandapashika = police, not judge; Akshapataladhikrita = royal records, Pustapala = land records.
### 3. ADMINISTRATIVE UNITS
| Unit (largest to smallest) | Level | Head |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhukti** (desha / rashtra) | Province | **Uparika** (governor appointed by king; sometimes "Maharaja") |
| **Vishaya** (pradesha) | District | **Vishayapati** |
| **Vithi** (esp. eastern India) | Group of villages | **Ayuktaka** |
| **Grama** | Village | **Gramika / Gramadhyaksha**; with kutumbis and mahattaras |
- Village land transactions needed the **consent of the village headman**.
!KEY UPPSC 2023: **Bhukti → Vishaya → Vithi → Grama** (ascending order = Grama → Vithi → Vishaya → Bhukti).
!TRAP Uparika heads the **bhukti** (province); Vishayapati heads the **vishaya** (district).
### 4. LOCAL BODIES – TOWN AND DISTRICT COUNCILS
- **Damodarpur copper plates:** a **district council** helped the district officer.
#### 4.1 Town Board (Adhikarana)
| Member | Represented |
|---|---|
| **Nagarashreshthi** | Chief banker / merchant; **chaired** the board |
| **Sarthavaha** | Chief **caravan trader** |
| **Prathama kulika** | Chief **artisan** (craft guilds) |
| **Prathama kayastha** | Chief **scribe** |
!KEY APPSC G2 2012: "Kayastha" = scribes.
### 5. JUDICIAL SYSTEM
- More developed than before; **civil and criminal law clearly separated**: **property disputes = civil**; **theft, adultery = criminal**.
- Law books of the period: **Narada, Brihaspati, Katyayana** smritis – the leading authorities on legal procedure; **Narada** survives complete and is devoted to civil law (vyavahara); Brihaspati and Katyayana survive in fragments.
### 6. REVENUE TERMS
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bhaga** | King's **share of produce** – main land revenue; **one-sixth** per law books |
| **Bhoga** | Periodic **supplies in kind** (fruits, firewood, flowers) |
| **Kara** | Periodic tax |
| **Udranga** | **Land revenue** / land tax in Gupta inscriptions |
| **Uparikara** | **Additional** (new) farm tax, probably on temporary tenants |
| **Udakabhaga** | **Irrigation** tax |
| **Halivakara** | **Plough** tax |
| **Hiranya** | **Cash** tax (Mauryan) instead of produce |
| **Bali** | Changed from voluntary to compulsory (oppressive) |
| **Sita** | Revenue from **crown land** |
| **Sulka (Shulka)** | **Customs duty / tolls** |
| **Uparnika** | Tax on farmers living on border land |
| **Klipta / Upaklipta** | Taxes on land sale and purchase |
| **Valiam** | Commodity tax |
| **Irai, Vari** | Chola terms for tax |
- Revenue share: the king took **one-fourth to one-sixth** of produce; **one-sixth** is the prescribed rate, called **Udranga and Bhaga** in inscriptions.
- Per the **Arthashastra**, the king owned the land and took **Bhaga** as his share; Bhaga and Bali were sources of revenue.
!TRAP Bhaga = produce share; Bhoga = supplies in kind; Hiranya = cash; Halivakara = plough; Udakabhaga = irrigation.
### 7. VISHTI AND PEASANT CONDITION
- **Vishti** = **forced (unpaid) labour** for the army, officials, state or donee (Telugu **vetti**).
- Treated as a **source of state income**, a kind of tax; imposed by state, governor or local chief.
- Mentioned in the **Junagadh inscription** and Gupta copper inscriptions.
- Peasants sank to a **semi-servile** status – tenant farming, sharecropping, tenancy at will, many taxes.
### 8. LAND TYPES AND MEASURES
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kshetra** | Cultivated land |
| **Khila** | Untilled / uncultivable waste land |
| **Aprahata** | Uncultivated **forest (jungle)** land (BPSC 2016) |
| **Vastu (Vasti)** | Habitable / house-site land |
| **Gopata Sarah** | Pasture land |
| **Kulyavapa, Dronavapa** | **Land measures** – area sown with one kulya / one drona of seed (UPSC 2020) |
### 9. LAND GRANTS AND AGRARIAN STRUCTURE
#### 9.1 Grant → Grantee
| Grant | Grantee | Rights |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmadeya** | Brahmanas, singly or jointly | Tax exemption + administrative rights |
| **Agrahara** | Brahmanas (religious functionaries) | Tax-free, hereditary land/village |
| **Devagrahara / Devadana** | **Temples and monasteries** | Revenue for worship |
| Secular grants | Officials, feudatories | Revenue in lieu of salary |
- Tax-free farm land went to **Brahmins** and religious functionaries (MPPSC 2014).
#### 9.2 Effects
1. **Revenue + administrative, judicial and police rights** passed to grantees, who collected taxes from peasants.
2. **Royal officials barred** from entering – led to **sub-infeudation**; grants did not bring direct royal rule.
3. **Tribal frontiers:** grants in forest areas brought virgin land under farming and absorbed **tribes into the caste order as new sub-castes** (APPSC GS 2023).
4. **Peasantry** became semi-servile.
#### 9.3 FIRST and AP Angle
- **Earliest land grants:** the **Satavahanas** – **100 nivartanas** to Buddhist monks (1 nivartana = about 1.5 acres) and agrahara-type grants to Brahmanas.
- **Satavahana and Ikshvaku** brahmadeya grants stopped royal officials from interfering.
### 10. THE INDIAN FEUDALISM DEBATE
- **Definition:** grant of land by an overlord to a donee with financial, administrative and judicial rights.
- **Essential elements (UPSC 2015):** administration based on **control of land**, **lord–vassal** ties, **weak central authority**.
- **Evidence cited (7th c.):** decline in long-distance trade and **gold coinage**, shrinking towns, closed village economy.
| Historian | Position |
|---|---|
| **R.S. Sharma** | **Proponent** – feudalism via land grants, sub-infeudation, vishti, decline of trade, urban decay, fewer gold coins |
| **D.C. Sircar** | **Critic** – grants transferred revenue rights, not ownership; no feudalism in the European sense |
| **Harbans Mukhia** | **Critic** – "Was there feudalism in Indian history?"; **no serfdom**, a largely free and autonomous peasantry |
| **B.D. Chattopadhyaya** | **Integrative / processual** model – grants spread state society into new areas |
!TRAP A **strong centre** with weak local authority is **not** a feudal feature.
