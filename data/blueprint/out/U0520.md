## U0520 | EXCHANGE RATE SYSTEMS AND RUPEE MOVEMENT; CURRENCY INTERNATIONALISATION
### 1. EXCHANGE RATE AND FOREX MARKET TERMS
- **Exchange rate**: the price or value of one currency in terms of another.
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Direct quote** | Units of **domestic** currency per **one unit of foreign** currency (e.g. ₹95 = $1) |
| **Indirect quote** | Units of foreign currency per one unit of domestic currency |
| **Spot rate** | Immediate delivery (settlement within two days) |
| **Forward rate** | Agreed now, delivered at a future date (importer's forward contract = hedging) |
| **Arbitrage** | Buy currency where cheaper and **simultaneously** sell where dearer |
| **Hedging** | Covering exchange-rate risk (forwards); speculation = open position on expected moves |
| **Hot currency** | Foreign currency that moves quickly, chasing higher short-term interest rates; hard = highest global faith, soft = easily available |
| **Import cover** | Months of imports payable from reserves |
| **Tobin Tax** | Tax on foreign exchange transactions; named after **James Tobin** |
!TRAP PYQ "false" statement: the direct quote defined as foreign units per one domestic unit (definitions swapped).
- Market participants: commercial banks, authorised dealers/brokers, monetary authorities. **Exchange banks (AD Category-I)** sanction forex for imports under FEMA.
- Costliest of the common currencies: **Pound Sterling** (₹125.18 vs Euro ₹109.05, US$ ₹93.00, 17 April 2026).
### 2. DEVALUATION, DEPRECIATION, APPRECIATION, REVALUATION
| Term | System | Meaning | Done by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Devaluation** | Fixed | **Official** reduction of value; Rs per $ raised | Government/monetary authority |
| **Revaluation** | Fixed | Official rise in value | Government/monetary authority |
| **Appreciation** | Flexible | **Market** rise; fewer rupees per $ | Demand and supply |
| **Depreciation** | Flexible | Market fall; more rupees per $ | Demand and supply |
#### 2.1 Reading a rate change
- **$1 = ₹60 → ₹58**: rupee **appreciated**, dollar depreciated.
- **$1 = ₹60 → ₹65** by market forces: rupee **depreciated**. ₹94 → ₹96 = depreciated; ₹96 → ₹94 = appreciated.
!KEY Devaluation/revaluation = government (fixed); depreciation/appreciation = market (floating).
### 3. EFFECTS OF DEVALUATION / DEPRECIATION
- Makes **exports cheaper** abroad (lowers export prices in foreign currency) and **imports dearer** at home → **promotes exports**.
- **Expenditure-switching**: spending switches to home-produced goods.
- **Devaluation improves the balance of trade** (A true) - but the reason "it **raises** export prices" is **false** (APPSC G2 2025: A true, R false).
- It does not **automatically** improve the trade balance - import costs rise too.
- After **1991**, export growth rose due to devaluation (~18%).
- **Limits**: **imported inflation**; inelastic oil demand → falling rupee widens the CAD; costlier oil and **ECB repayment**; a falling rupee makes buyers delay orders.
- Net impact (ES 2025-26): a weaker currency helps the trade balance more than it hurts through capital outflows → net positive.
- **South-East Asian devaluations (1997-98)** were unfavourable for Indian exports.
- Recognised BoP correction methods: devaluation, exchange depreciation, exchange control, monetary/fiscal measures, import curbs.
### 4. NEER AND REER
- **NEER**: weighted average of bilateral **nominal** rates against a **trade-weighted basket** of partner currencies; **base 2015-16 = 100**.
- **REER** = NEER adjusted for relative **inflation** differentials (REER = NEER × domestic prices / foreign prices); CPI-based; the **better competitiveness measure**.
- RBI indices: **40-currency** and **6-currency** trade-weighted (6-currency has a moving base).
- **Rise in NEER/REER = rupee appreciation**: imports cheaper, exports less competitive. **High/rising REER = overvalued rupee**; low = undervalued.
- Calendar 2025 (provisional): NEER **86.75**, REER **99.72**.
!TRAP A rise in REER does NOT mean better competitiveness - it signals an overvalued rupee.
### 5. EXCHANGE RATE REGIMES
| Regime | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fixed / pegged** | Fixed to a currency, basket or gold; government fixes the rate, RBI buys/sells to hold it |
| **Floating / flexible** | Set by **demand and supply** of foreign currency |
| **Managed float** | Market-set with central-bank intervention when necessary (direct forex deals or interest rates) - **India's system** |
- Post-Bretton Woods classes: **fixed** - currency unions, currency boards (Argentina 1991-Jan 2002), pegs; **intermediate** - bands, crawling pegs; **flexible** - managed and independent float.
- India's current policy = **market-determined** exchange rate.
!TRAP The World Bank does NOT determine a currency's price.
### 6. BRETTON WOODS, IMF AND INDIA'S EXCHANGE RATE HISTORY
#### 6.1 Bretton Woods and IMF
- **Bretton Woods Conference** (UN Monetary and Financial Conference): **44 nations**, **1-22 July 1944**, New Hampshire; fixed rates based on the **dollar**.
- Led to **IMF and IBRD** (Bretton Woods Twins, both HQ **Washington, D.C.**) - **not** the UN or WTO.
- **IMF and World Bank formally set up 27 December 1945**; **India a founding member**.
- IMF: monetary stability; lends for BoP problems, **only to members**. Members: **190** (PYQ key); **Liechtenstein 191st (Oct 2024)**.
- India's IMF quota: **2.75%** of SDR quota; **2.63%** of votes (March 2026).
#### 6.2 India's timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1947-1971 | **Par value** system under IMF |
| 1971 | **Smithsonian Agreement** (±2.5% band) |
| **Sept 1975** | Rupee **delinked from pound sterling**; pegged to a **basket** of currencies |
| **1992-93** | **LERMS** (Union Budget 1992-93): dual rate - **40% official, 60% market**; partial convertibility |
| **1 March 1993** | Rates **unified**, market-determined (managed float) |
| **August 1994** | **Article VIII** - current account convertibility |
| 2010 | Rupee symbol ₹ |
### 7. RUPEE DEVALUATION EPISODES
| Year | Extent / steps |
|---|---|
| **Sept 1949** | **First**; about 30.5%, with sterling |
| **June 1966** | **57.5%** (₹4.76 → ₹7.50 per US$); wars, drought, 36% inflation |
| **July 1991** | About **18%** in **two steps (1 and 3 July)**; BoP crisis; **last formal devaluation** |
!NUM 1949 - 1966 - 1991 = three devaluations; 1991 = two steps.
### 8. CONVERTIBILITY AND FERA TO FEMA
- **Convertibility**: free conversion of the rupee into other currencies and vice versa. **Capital account convertibility** = rupee exchangeable to trade financial assets.
- India: **full current account**, only **partial capital account** convertibility.
| Committee | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Tarapore I** | Set up 28 Feb 1997; **report May 1997**; roadmap to capital account convertibility |
| **Tarapore II** (Fuller CAC) | Appointed **20 March 2006**; report **31 July 2006**; three phases over five years to 2010-11 |
- **FERA 1973** (restrictive) → **FEMA 1999**: proposed in the **1997-98 Budget**; enacted 1999; **in force 1 June 2000** with a **two-year sunset clause**; finally implemented 2002.
- Limit on forex for current-account heads lowered in 2000 (FEMA); FCA actually rose.
!TRAP FERA's replacement is FEMA, 1999. One APPSC key credited a "Foreign Exchange Control Act" - no such replacement Act exists.
### 9. DETERMINANTS AND IMPOSSIBLE TRINITY
#### 9.1 Determinants
- Demand and supply of forex; **inflation** (lowers the rupee's value), **fiscal deficit**, **CAD**, forex/import cover, short-term debt, interest rates, capital flows.
- **Raghuram Rajan**: high inflation, high fiscal deficit, high **crude oil prices** weaken the rupee.
- **PPP**: long-run rate linked to the inflation differential. Long-run anchors (ES 2025-26): PPP, **savings-investment balance**, current account (East Asian lesson: manufacturing exports).
- Higher imports need not cause depreciation if backed by **net investment inflows**.
- **Large capital inflows** → **appreciation** (linked with **Dutch disease**).
- **Hawkish stance / repo hike** supports the rupee by attracting **carry-trade** capital.
- **Deliberate undervaluation** (as alleged of China) = **currency manipulation**.
#### 9.2 Impossible trinity (Mundell-Fleming trilemma)
- A country cannot have at once: **fixed exchange rate + free capital flows + independent monetary policy**.
### 10. RUPEE MOVEMENT AND RBI TOOLS
#### 10.1 Rupee in 2025-26
- **FY 2025-26 (to Jan 2026)**: rupee depreciated about **5.4%** (US tariffs, outflows); among the most depreciated, with the yen.
- **18-19 May 2026**: crossed **₹96.2/$**, down 6.3% since 1 Jan 2026; **Brent near $110** + **FPI outflows over ₹2 lakh crore**.
- Rupee at 95-96/$ through 2026 on the West Asia crude shock; two-month high **₹94.60** on 3 Sep 2026.
#### 10.2 RBI tools
- Aim: curb **excessive volatility**, not defend a fixed level.
| Tool | Example |
|---|---|
| **Spot intervention** | Selling dollars from reserves |
| **Forex swaps** | $5 bn, 3-year dollar-rupee **buy/sell swap** (Dec 2025; May 2026): banks sell dollars to RBI and buy back later at a premium; adds rupee liquidity |
| **NDF curbs** | Limit domestic banks' offshore NDF (Singapore/London) role; **Net Open Position** limits; Apr 2026 action on NDD contracts |
| **FCNR(B) support** | RBI bore full **hedging cost** on fresh FCNR(B) deposits till 30 Sep 2026; special measures mobilised **$136.38 bn** by 31 Aug 2026 |
| **Interest-rate signal** | Repo **5.25%**, unchanged for the 4th meeting (Aug 2026) |
| ECB easing | Capital-inflow step |
- **NOT RBI tools**: **fiscal policy adjustments** (APPSC 2025); **import curbs and export promotion** = Government structural measures.
- Reserves fell **$23.6 bn in FY26** defending the rupee.
### 11. FOREX RESERVES AND SDR
#### 11.1 Reserves
- Components: **FCA > Gold > SDRs > Reserve Tranche Position** (order, March 2025); **RBI = custodian**. SDRs are included.
- FCA in dollar terms includes valuation changes of euro, pound and yen holdings; rising **gold share** = diversification.
| Date | Reserves |
|---|---|
| End-March 2019 | US$ 412,871 mn |
| 27 Dec 2024 | US$ 640.3 bn |
| **16 Jan 2026** | **$701.4 bn** (up from $668 bn); ~11 months import cover; ~94% of external debt |
| 27 Feb 2026 | All-time high $728.494 bn (before West Asia conflict) |
| 31 Jul 2026 | $692.866 bn; FCA $564.68 bn |
| **15 Sep 2026** | **$785.71 bn** - India **4th**, overtaking Russia, after China ($3.85 tn), Japan ($1.21 tn), Switzerland ($1.09 tn) |
| 16 Oct 2026 | $721.0 bn |
#### 11.2 SDR
- **SDR** = **artificial currency / international reserve asset** created by the IMF in **1969** (First Amendment, agreed 1967); "**paper gold**"; supplements members' official reserves; interest-bearing.
- **Form: book-keeping entries** in IMF records (PYQ 2025); where options are only paper currency/gold/silver, the key chose paper currency.
- First defined in gold (35 SDR = 1 ounce); redefined as a currency basket after Bretton Woods collapsed (1973).
- Basket since **1 Oct 2016**: **US dollar, euro, renminbi, yen, pound sterling** - **no rupee**. Renminbi added (decided 2015). Weights from 1 Aug 2022: dollar 43.38%, euro 29.31%, renminbi 12.28%, yen 7.59%, pound 7.44%.
- Largest general allocation: about **$650 bn (SDR 456 bn), August 2021**.
- **Reserve (gold) tranche**: part of IMF quota paid in reserve assets (historically gold); **unconditional** drawing right.
### 12. SWAPS AND RUPEE INTERNATIONALISATION
#### 12.1 Swap arrangements
- **SAARC Currency Swap Framework**: RBI, **2012**; short-term BoP liquidity; **3-month** tenor, rollable twice. 2024-27: **₹250 bn INR window** + **$2 bn** USD-euro facility.
- **Maldives** drew $400 mn (Oct 2024) and made the **first INR drawdown** (₹30 bn, **April 2026**).
- **India-Japan BSA**: renewed from **28 Feb 2026**; two-way; local currencies against US dollars.
#### 12.2 INR trade settlement
- **RBI framework, July 2022**; built into **FTP 2023**: invoicing and settlement in rupees; cuts dollar dependence and exchange risk.
- **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts**: foreign banks' rupee accounts in Indian banks (Russia, UAE, Tanzania); since 2025 surplus may go into **corporate bonds and CPs**.
- **Vostro ("yours")** = foreign bank's account with a domestic bank; **Nostro ("ours")** = our account abroad.
- UAE: rupee-dirham settlement; rupee accepted at Dubai airports from July 2023.
#### 12.3 Symbol and bonds
- **Rupee symbol ₹**: adopted **15 July 2010**; designed by **D. Udaya Kumar**.
- **Masala bond**: rupee-denominated, issued **abroad**; investor bears currency risk; **first by IFC, Nov 2014** (IFC named it); **Kerala first state (2019)**; first listed in **Luxembourg**.
| Bond | Currency / country |
|---|---|
| **Masala** | Rupee (issued abroad) |
| **Samurai** | Yen, Japan |
| **Panda** | Yuan, China |
- **Resurgent India Bonds**: SBI, **5 August 1998**, five years; in US dollar, pound sterling, Deutsche Mark.
- **Euro**: single currency of the European Monetary Union; used by **20 of 27** EU states. **Seychelles** currency = rupee; Bhutan ngultrum, Malaysia ringgit, Maldives rufiyaa.
