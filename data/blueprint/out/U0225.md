## U0225 | GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT 1919 – DYARCHY, MONTAGU-CHELMSFORD REFORMS; ROWLATT ACT
### 1. OVERVIEW AND ROAD TO THE 1919 ACT
- Exams: **Group 1 Prelims + Group 2 Screening** | Tier: **CORE** | Rows: A-185; S1-88
- PYQ importance: **APPSC 16 questions** | other exams 105 | last APPSC year 2023
#### 1.1 Montagu Declaration, 1917
- Date: **20 August 1917**, made in the British **Parliament**.
- Text: "Gradual development of self-governing institutions with a view to the **progressive realisation of responsible government** in India as an integral part of the British Empire."
- **First official recognition** of self-government as an aim; pressure mainly from the **Home Rule Leagues**.
- **Dec 1917**: Montagu and Chelmsford toured India; **July 1918**: Report announced.
#### 1.2 Names Behind the Reform
| Person | Post |
|---|---|
| **Edwin Montagu** | Secretary of State for India |
| **Lord Chelmsford** | Viceroy |
!TRAP GoI Act 1919 = **Montagu-Chelmsford** Reforms; **Morley-Minto** Reforms = Indian Councils Act **1909**.
#### 1.3 Moderates' Exit
- Congress special **Bombay session, August 1918** (President **Hasan Imam**) condemned the reforms.
- Moderates under **Surendranath Banerjee** accepted the reforms, left Congress and formed the **Indian National Liberal Federation (1918)** (also called Indian Liberal Federation).
- **Amritsar Congress (1919, Motilal Nehru)**: reforms "**inadequate, unsatisfactory and disappointing**".
| Person / body | Reaction |
|---|---|
| **C.R. Das + Tilak** | Rejection (Amritsar, 1919) |
| **Gandhi + Malaviya** | Give the reforms a fair trial |
| **Krishna Patrika** | "Giving with the right hand, taking with the left" |
- AP angle: **Nyapati Subbarao** led a **27-member Andhra delegation** that met Montagu at **Madras** to demand a separate **Andhra province**; **Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao** presided over the **Special Andhra Mahasabha, Guntur, 17 Aug 1918**.
### 2. PROVINCIAL DYARCHY – RESERVED AND TRANSFERRED SUBJECTS
#### 2.1 Meaning
- **Dyarchy** = **dual government**: subjects given to the provinces split into **reserved** and **transferred**.
- Subjects first divided into **central** and **provincial** lists; **provincial budgets separated** from the central budget.
- The Act delegated a large part of authority to the provinces; governance did not stay unitary; it **clearly defined central and provincial jurisdiction**.
#### 2.2 Reserved vs Transferred
| Subjects | Examples | Run by | Answerable to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reserved** | **Administration of Justice, Land revenue, Finance, Law and order, Police** | Governor + Executive Council | **Not** the legislature |
| **Transferred** | **Education, Health, Local self-government** | Governor + **Ministers** from the elected legislature | **Legislature** |
- Flaw: ministers had transferred subjects but **no finance** (finance stayed reserved).
!TRAP **Local self-government** was transferred, not reserved.
!TRAP APPSC 2019: "dyarchy = single government" is FALSE; dyarchy = dual government.
!KEY Dyarchy (1919) was in the **provinces**; **provincial autonomy** came only with the **1935 Act**.
### 3. CENTRE, ELECTORATE AND DIRECT ELECTIONS
#### 3.1 Central Legislature (bicameral)
| House | Members |
|---|---|
| **Council of State** (upper) | **60** |
| **Legislative Assembly** (lower) | **145** (**104 elected**) |
- **Viceroy's Executive Council**: **6** members besides the Commander-in-Chief, **3 Indians**.
- First **President** of the Assembly: **Frederick Whyte (1921)**; first **Deputy President**: **Sachchidananda Sinha**.
#### 3.2 Direct Elections and Franchise
- **GoI Act 1919** = **first Act to introduce direct elections**.
- Franchise was **limited**: based on **property, tax and education**.
- The **1909 Act** had elected non-officials, largely via **indirect** and **separate** electorates.
- Communal (separate) electorates were extended in 1919.
!TRAP The APPSC GS 2018 key gave 1909. Rule: mark **1919**; if 1919 is not among the options, mark 1909.
### 4. BODIES CREATED AND STATUTORY REVIEW
| Body / office | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Public Service Commission** | **Section 96(C)**, GoI Act 1919 (APPSC GS 2022); set up **1 October 1926** after the **Lee Commission** |
| **Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal)** | **1921**, by **royal proclamation**; consultative body of rulers |
| **High Commissioner for India** | Office created under the 1919 Act in London |
| Legislature officers | President + Deputy President |
#### 4.1 Statutory Review Clause
- The Act required a **statutory commission after 10 years** to review its working.
- The **Simon Commission** was appointed in **1927**, **two years early**.
- **Muddiman Committee (1924)**: British response to the Swarajist demand to review the 1919 Act.
!NUM 96(C) = PSC section; 1926 = PSC set up; 1921 = Chamber of Princes; 10 years = review clause.
### 5. WORKING OF THE ACT – ELECTIONS AND MINISTRIES
#### 5.1 Madras and Provinces
- **First elections: 1920**; **Congress boycotted** (Non-Cooperation).
- **Madras, 1920**: **Justice Party** won **63 seats**; **A. Subbarayalu Reddiar** = first Chief Minister (Premier), **Dec 1920**; then **Raja of Panagal (1921)**. Justice Party held office most of 1920-37.
#### 5.2 Central Legislative Assembly
- Elections under the 1919 Act: **1923, 1926, 1930, 1934, 1945** (last in **1945**).
- **Swaraj Party** (C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru) contested **1923** to obstruct from within; won **42 of 104** elected seats (Nov 1923); backed by independents like **Jinnah** and **Malaviya**.
- **Vithalbhai Patel**: **first Indian President (Speaker)** of the Assembly, **1925**; co-founder of the Swaraj Party.
- AP angle: **V. Ramadas** elected to the Central Legislative Assembly (1923).
### 6. ROWLATT ACT, 1919
| Particular | Fact |
|---|---|
| Official name | **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919** |
| Passed | **March 1919** |
| Origin | **Rowlatt Committee**; replaced the wartime Defence of India Act with a permanent law |
| Provisions | Arrest **without warrant**, detention **without trial**, trials **without jury**; tribunals could admit evidence invalid under the Evidence Act |
| Response | **Rowlatt Satyagraha** – Gandhi's nationwide **hartal on 6 April 1919** |
- **13 April 1919**: **Jallianwala Bagh** massacre during the Punjab unrest.
- Gandhi **suspended** the satyagraha on **18 April 1919** after violence.
!PAIR Rowlatt Act | March 1919 | "Black Act" || GoI Act 1919 | December 1919 | Dyarchy

### 8. SIMON COMMISSION
#### 8.1 Appointment and Composition
- Appointed **8 November 1927** by **Stanley Baldwin's Conservative** government, to study the working of the 1919 Act.
- Other names: **Indian Statutory Commission**, "White Commission".
- **7 members, all British**: chairman **Sir John Simon** (Liberal) + 6 others, including **Clement Attlee**.
- **No Indian member** → boycott.
#### 8.2 Visits and Boycott
- Arrived at **Bombay, 3 February 1928** (APPSC GS 2021: 1928) → all-India hartal, black flags.
- Two visits: **3 Feb–31 Mar 1928**; **11 Oct 1928–13 Apr 1929**.
- **Madras Congress (Dec 1927, M.A. Ansari)**: boycott "at every stage and in every form".
- Slogan **"Simon Go Back"** – coined by **Yusuf Meherally** (APPSC GS 2018).
| Joined boycott | Did not join |
|---|---|
| Congress, **Jinnah's** League faction, **Hindu Mahasabha** | **Shafi's** League faction, Punjab Unionists, **Justice Party** |
#### 8.3 Places of Protest
| Place | Leader |
|---|---|
| **Lahore** | **Lala Lajpat Rai** – lathi charge **30 Oct 1928**; died of injuries (Nov 1928) |
| **Madras** | **Tanguturi Prakasam** – led the procession, **bared his chest** before police guns → "**Andhra Kesari**" |
| **Lucknow** | **Jawaharlal Nehru**, **G.B. Pant** |
| **Tenali / Guntur** | **G.V. Unnayya Sastri**; complete bandh in Guntur |
- Andhra route: Tenali – Guntur – Vijayawada – Ongole – Madras; anti-Simon committee president **S. Satyamurti**; **Bulusu Sambamurthy** toured Andhra.
- **Saunders** shot by **Bhagat Singh** and **Rajguru** (Dec 1928) in revenge for Lajpat Rai.
#### 8.4 Report (1930)
- Abolish provincial dyarchy → **responsible government / provincial autonomy** in provinces.
- Keep **separate electorates**; first used the term "**Scheduled Castes**".
- Its report became the **base of the GoI Act 1935**.
### 9. NEHRU REPORT AND JINNAH'S FOURTEEN POINTS
| Item | Nehru Report (1928) | Fourteen Points (1929) |
|---|---|---|
| Head | **Motilal Nehru** (chairman); **Jawaharlal Nehru** secretary | **Jinnah** (Muslim League) |
| Body | Committee of the **All Parties Conference** (9 members) | Muslim League |
| Reason | Reply to **Lord Birkenhead's** challenge to frame a constitution | League's answer to the Nehru Report |
| Demands | Constitution for India; discussed at **Calcutta (Dec 1928)** → one-year ultimatum | **Federal** constitution, **residuary powers to provinces**, **one-third** Muslim seats at Centre, **separate electorates** |
### 10. GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT 1935
#### 10.1 Structure
- **321 sections and 10 schedules** – the **longest** British Act on India; Royal assent **2 August 1935**.
- Basis: **Simon Report** + three RTCs + White Paper.
#### 10.2 Key Provisions
- **All-India Federation** (provinces + princely states; optional for states) – **never formed**; princes were to counter-balance nationalists.
- **Dyarchy at the Centre** – never operated.
- **Provincial autonomy** – provincial dyarchy abolished; began **April 1937**.
- **Bicameral legislatures** in some provinces; **India Council** abolished.
- **Federal Court** at **Delhi (1937)**; **Federal PSC** and **Provincial PSCs**.
- Local self-government became a provincial subject.
- **RBI**: RBI Act **1934** (Hilton Young Commission, 1926); started **1 April 1935**; nationalised **1 Jan 1949**.
#### 10.3 Elections and Congress Ministries
- **1937 provincial elections** under the 1935 Act: Congress won; Madras premier **C. Rajagopalachari** (first Congress ministry).
- Centre continued under the 1919 structure.
- Congress ministries **resigned Oct–Nov 1939** – India dragged into **WWII** without consent.
- Muslim League "**Day of Deliverance**" – **22 Dec 1939**; Ambedkar joined.
!PAIR 1919 Act elections | Central Legislative Assembly 1923-1945 | provincial councils 1920 || 1935 Act elections | Provincial legislatures 1937 | Congress ministries
### 11. BORROWED FEATURES AND CHRONOLOGY
#### 11.1 1935 Act → Constitution
| 1935 Act feature | Constitution today |
|---|---|
| **Instrument of Instructions** | **Directive Principles** (APPSC 2023) |
| **Federal Court** | **Supreme Court** |
| Office of Governor; Governor's address (**Sec. 63**) | Governor; address under Art. 175/176 |
| Emergency powers | Emergency provisions |
| Federal scheme; PSCs | Union-State structure; UPSC/State PSCs |
- Sec. 63: Governor addressed the provincial legislature "in his discretion"; from 1937 the speech was prepared with ministers – a convention the Constituent Assembly kept.
- The 1935 Act = **largest single source** of the Constitution's structure.
