## U0397 | POVERTY, UNEMPLOYMENT AND SOCIAL EXCLUSION AS SOCIAL PROBLEMS
### 1. POVERTY – CONCEPTS AND SOCIAL INDICATORS
#### 1.1 Absolute vs Relative Poverty
| Concept | Meaning | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute poverty** | Income or spending below the minimum needed for basic needs | A fixed **poverty line** (MPCE cut-off) |
| **Relative poverty** | Being poor compared with others in the same society | Inequality / share of income |
| **Multidimensional poverty** | Deprivation in health, education and living standards together | **National MPI** (NITI Aayog) |
- **MPCE** (Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure) sets the poverty line in India.
- India's official poverty line is a **consumption-based** (not income-based) line.
#### 1.2 Social Indicators of Poverty
- **Illiteracy**, **malnutrition**, **poor health care**, lack of **safe drinking water** and **sanitation**.
!TRAP **Size of the house** is NOT a social indicator of poverty (UPPSC 2025 key: "Only 4" was the odd one).
#### 1.3 Culture of Poverty
- Concept given by **Oscar Lewis (1959)**.
- Meaning: **fatalism**, low aspiration and poverty are **passed down generations**; the poor adapt to poverty and it becomes a way of life.
!KEY "Culture of poverty" = Oscar Lewis (1959). "Social exclusion" = René Lenoir (France, 1974).

- Criticism: the **Tendulkar line is considered too low**.

### 3. MEASUREMENT – AGENCIES AND SURVEYS
| Body / survey | Role |

| **NSSO** (now **NSO**, MoSPI) | **HCES** (Household Consumption Expenditure Survey) about **every 5 years**; MPCE sets the line |
| **PLFS** (NSO) | The **standard for unemployment** data (APPSC-G2 2025 key) |

#### 3.1 PLFS Status Concepts
| Status | Reference period |
|---|---|
| **Usual status** | Activity over the reference year |
| **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** | **7 days** before the survey |
| **Current Daily Status (CDS)** | Activity recorded for **each day** of the reference week |
#### 3.2 Price Indices Used
- **Lakdawala:** CPI-AL for rural lines, CPI-IW for urban lines.
- **Rangarajan update (RBI, 2025):** poverty-line-basket price index.
!NUM PLFS CWS = 7 days; HCES about every 5 years; National MPI = 3 dimensions, 12 indicators.

- **3 equally weighted dimensions** (health, education, standard of living) and **12 indicators**.
- **Standard-of-living indicators:** cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, housing, electricity, assets, bank accounts.

### 5. UNEMPLOYMENT – TYPES
| Type | Meaning | Predominant sector |
|---|---|---|
| **Disguised** (hidden) | More workers than needed; marginal productivity of extra workers near **zero** | **Agriculture** (family farms), informal sector |
| **Seasonal** | Work only in some months of the year | **Agriculture**; also construction, tourism |
| **Structural** | Mismatch between **skills** and the jobs available; long-term | Industry / whole economy |
| **Cyclical** | Fall in labour demand during an **economic slowdown** | Industry and services (small in India) |
| **Frictional** | Temporary, while **changing or searching** for jobs | Urban labour market |
| **Educated** | Joblessness among the **educated**; weak skill-industry link | **Urban** areas |
!TRAP Disguised and seasonal unemployment = rural/agriculture. Educated and frictional = urban. Cyclical = trade cycle (slowdown).

### 6. POVERTY AND EMPLOYMENT PROGRAMMES – CHRONOLOGY
| Year | Programme | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **1972** | **Maharashtra EGS** | **First state-level** employment guarantee scheme |
| **1978-80** | **IRDP** | Self-employment through subsidy and credit |
| **1979** | **TRYSEM** | Training rural youth; component of IRDP |
| **1982-83** | **DWCRA** | Sub-scheme of IRDP so that benefits reach **rural women** directly |
| **1989** | **JRY** | Merged **NREP and RLEGP**; wage employment |
| **1999** | **SGSY** | Merged **IRDP, DWCRA, TRYSEM** (and others); SHG-based self-employment |
| **2005 / 2006** | **NREGA** | Legal guarantee of wage work |
| **2008** | **PMEGP** | Merged **PMRY and REGP**; credit-linked subsidy through **KVIC** |
| **2011** | **DAY-NRLM** | **Restructured SGSY**; SHG livelihoods |
| **18 Dec 2025** | **VB-G RAM G Bill, 2025** passed | Guarantee raised to **125 days**; changes funding pattern |
!TRAP DAY-NRLM restructured **SGSY** (SHG scheme), NOT JRY. SGSY merged IRDP; JRY merged NREP and RLEGP.
### 7. MGNREGA
#### 7.1 Timeline
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| NREGA passed | **August 2005** |
| In force | **2 February 2006**, started in **Anantapur district (AP)** |
| Renamed **MGNREGA** | **2 October 2009** |
| VB-G RAM G Bill, 2025 passed | **18 December 2025** |
#### 7.2 Provisions
- **Legal right** to at least **100 days** of unskilled wage work in a **financial year** per **rural household** (not per adult individual).
- Beneficiaries: **adult members** of any rural household willing to do unskilled manual work.
- Aim: guarantee the **"Right to Work"**.
- No work within **15 days** of demand → **unemployment allowance**.
- At least **one-third** of workers must be **women** (not half).
- **Gram Sabha** holds **social audits**.
- Follows the **Directive Principles**; the right to work is **not** in Part III (Fundamental Rights).
!PAIR MGNREGA | 100 days | per household || VB-G RAM G | 125 days | new funding pattern
#### 7.3 Debates
- **27 Jan 2026:** critics say budget caps and cost-sharing under VB-G RAM G hurt poorer states.
- MGNREGA gives way to the 125-day VB-G RAM G framework.
### 8. SOCIAL EXCLUSION
#### 8.1 Meaning
- A **process** that shuts people out of **facilities, benefits and opportunities** that others enjoy.
- Term coined by **René Lenoir** (France, **1974**).
#### 8.2 Bases of Exclusion
| Basis | Example |
|---|---|
| **Caste** | Untouchability against SCs |
| **Gender** | Denial of property, work, mobility |
| **Disability** | Lack of access to schools, jobs |
| **Religion** | Discrimination against minorities |
| **Region** | Backward and remote areas |
#### 8.3 Practices Leading to Exclusion
- **Untouchability**, **segregation**, denied access to **land, water and jobs**.
- **10 Aug 2026:** the Union **opposes a creamy layer for SCs/STs**, since income alone does not end caste-based exclusion.
### 9. POVERTY BY SOCIAL GROUP AND STATE
- **Scheduled Tribes** have the **highest poverty**, followed by **Scheduled Castes**.
- Cause: **discrimination and social exclusion** along with poor land and education access.
- Highest MPI poverty state: **Bihar**; lowest: **Kerala**.
!KEY Order of poverty by social group: ST > SC > others.
#### 9.1 Why Excluded Groups Stay Poor
| Group | Main link between exclusion and poverty |
|---|---|
| **Scheduled Tribes** | Remote forest and hill areas, land alienation, poor access to schools and health care |
| **Scheduled Castes** | Caste discrimination, landlessness, dependence on casual agricultural labour |
| **Women** | Lower wages, unpaid care work, weak property rights |
| **Persons with disability** | Barriers to education, transport and jobs |
- Poverty, unemployment and exclusion **reinforce each other**: excluded groups get fewer jobs, and joblessness deepens their exclusion.
- Policy response: wage employment (MGNREGA), self-employment through **SHGs** (SGSY, DAY-NRLM), and protective measures for SCs/STs.
### 10. INFORMAL LABOUR AND SOCIAL SECURITY
| Indicator | Figure (2025-26) |
|---|---|
| AP rural informal employment | **62.3%** |
| Gig workers | **2%+** of the workforce |
| Social protection coverage | **22% (2016) → 64.3% (2025)** |
- Migrant, casual and unorganised workers have **little social security**; seasonal migration follows seasonal unemployment.
- Link with **culture of poverty**: low income and insecurity are passed on to the next generation.
