## SEC 2:31:0 | Presiding officers
- Q: A resolution to remove the Speaker of the Lok Sabha requires advance notice of at least: => ANSWER: 14 days | WHY: Under Article 94 removal needs a majority of all the then members after 14 days' notice; the Speaker resigns to the Deputy Speaker.
- Q: Consider the following statements about the Panel of Chairpersons of the Lok Sabha: 1. A member of the panel may preside when the office of Speaker is vacant. 2. It is nominated by the Speaker and may have up to ten members. Which of the above statement(s)… => ANSWER: 2 only | WHY: The Speaker nominates up to ten members to the panel; a panel member may preside when both Speaker and Deputy Speaker are absent, but not when the Speaker's office is vacant.
- Q: The Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha addresses his resignation to the: => ANSWER: Chairman of the Rajya Sabha | WHY: The Deputy Chairman resigns to the Chairman and can be removed by an effective majority after 14 days' notice.
- Q: When the Vice-President acts as President, the duties of the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha are performed by the: => ANSWER: Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha | WHY: The Deputy Chairman performs the Chairman's duties when the Vice-President acts as President.
- Q: In which Lok Sabha did the office of Deputy Speaker remain vacant throughout? => ANSWER: 17th Lok Sabha | WHY: The Deputy Speaker's post remained vacant through the 17th Lok Sabha, and also through the 18th.
- Q: Under which Article is the Speaker removed by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the Lok Sabha? => ANSWER: Article 94(c) | WHY: Article 94(c) requires an effective majority, after 14 days' notice; a simple majority is not enough.
- Q: Which of the following statements regarding removal of the Deputy Speaker is incorrect? => ANSWER: Removal requires a two-thirds special majority. | WHY: Option B is incorrect: removing the Deputy Speaker needs an effective majority (a majority of all the then members), not two-thirds, after 14 days' notice.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is INCORRECTLY matched? => ANSWER: Speaker's resignation – addressed to the President | WHY: The Speaker resigns to the Deputy Speaker, not to the President.
- Q: Removal of which of the following requires NO advance notice? => ANSWER: The Governor of a State | WHY: The President removes a Governor at pleasure without notice; the other three removals need 14 days' notice.
- Q: On assuming office, the Speaker of the Lok Sabha takes: => ANSWER: only the oath as a Member of Parliament | WHY: The Speaker and Deputy Speaker take only the oath as MPs; there is no separate oath as presiding officer.
- Q: Consider the following statements about election as Speaker of the Lok Sabha: 1. A non-member may be chosen Speaker if elected to the House within six months. 2. The person must already be an elected member of the Lok Sabha. Which of the above statement(s)… => ANSWER: 2 only | WHY: The Speaker must already be an elected Lok Sabha member; the six-month relaxation applies only to Ministers.

- Q: Who is usually appointed Speaker Pro Tem of a newly elected Lok Sabha? => ANSWER: The senior-most member of the House | WHY: The Speaker Pro Tem is the senior-most member, sworn in by the President; the outgoing Speaker only stays in office until just before the new House first meets.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is INCORRECTLY matched? => ANSWER: Pro-Tem Speaker – elected by Lok Sabha members | WHY: The Pro-Tem Speaker is appointed by the President under Article 95(1); only the regular Speaker is elected by the House.

- Q: Who administers the oath to newly elected members of the Lok Sabha? => ANSWER: The Pro-Tem Speaker | WHY: The Pro-Tem Speaker swears in the new MPs and conducts the election of the regular Speaker.

- Q: The date of election of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha is fixed by the: => ANSWER: President | WHY: The President fixes the Speaker's election date and the Speaker fixes the Deputy Speaker's.
## SEC 2:31:2 | Normal sitting vs own removal (Art. 96)
- Q: While a resolution for his removal is under consideration, the Speaker of the Lok Sabha: 1. cannot preside over the House. 2. may vote in the first instance but not in the event of a tie. Which of the above statement(s) is/are correct? => ANSWER: Both 1 and 2 | WHY: Under Article 96 the Speaker cannot preside during consideration of his removal resolution, but may speak, take part and vote in the first instance, though not cast a vote in case of a tie.
- Q: In normal sittings of the Lok Sabha, the Speaker: => ANSWER: does not vote in the first instance but has a casting vote in a tie | WHY: Under Article 100 the Speaker does not vote in the first instance but gives a casting vote when votes are tied.
- Q: When not in the Chair, the Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha: => ANSWER: can debate, move motions and vote in the first instance | WHY: Out of the Chair the Deputy Speaker is an ordinary member who can debate, move motions and vote in the first instance, unlike the Speaker.
- Q: While a resolution for his own removal is under consideration, the Deputy Speaker can: => ANSWER: vote in the first instance | WHY: The Deputy Speaker cannot preside then but may speak, take part and vote in the first instance; no casting vote is allowed.

## SEC 2:31:5 | Ending a sitting or session
- Q: The period between prorogation of Parliament and the commencement of its next session is known as: => ANSWER: Recess | WHY: The period from prorogation to the next session is called a recess. It is different from adjournment, which interrupts a sitting within a session.
- Q: A quorum in either House of Parliament is ordinarily: => ANSWER: One-tenth of the total membership, including the presiding officer | WHY: Under Article 100(3), the quorum is one-tenth of the total number of members of the House, including the presiding officer.
- Q: Which of the following does NOT lapse on the prorogation of Parliament? => ANSWER: Bills pending before Parliament | WHY: Bills do not lapse on prorogation, although pending notices generally lapse except notices to introduce bills.
- Q: Consider the following statements about the Speaker on dissolution of the Lok Sabha: 1. The Speaker vacates office immediately on dissolution. 2. The Speaker continues until immediately before the first meeting of the newly elected Lok Sabha. Which of the… => ANSWER: 2 only | WHY: The Speaker does not vacate office on dissolution; he continues until just before the new Lok Sabha first meets.
- Q: Assertion (A): Prorogation ends the very life of the Lok Sabha. Reason (R): Adjournment suspends the work in a sitting for a specified time. => ANSWER: A is false but R is true | WHY: Prorogation only ends a session; it is dissolution that ends the Lok Sabha itself. R correctly describes adjournment.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is INCORRECTLY matched? => ANSWER: Prorogation – President under Article 85(2)(b) | WHY: The President prorogues under Article 85(2)(a); Article 85(2)(b) is dissolution.
- Q: The President's power to dissolve the Lok Sabha is provided in: => ANSWER: Article 85(2)(b) | WHY: Article 85(2)(b) covers dissolution and 85(2)(a) prorogation; Article 123 is about ordinances.
- Q: The lapse of pending bills on dissolution of the Lok Sabha is subject to the rules in: => ANSWER: Article 107 | WHY: Dissolution ends the Lok Sabha and its pending business lapses, subject to Article 107; the Rajya Sabha is permanent.
