## SEC 2:82:1 | Three municipalities (243Q)
- Q: Which one of the following is NOT one of the three types of municipalities specifically recognised under Article 243Q? => ANSWER: Cantonment Board | WHY: Article 243Q recognises Nagar Panchayats for transitional areas, Municipal Councils for smaller urban areas and Municipal Corporations for larger urban areas. Cantonment Boards are other forms of urban local bodies outside this classification.
- Q: A Municipal Corporation generally comprises which three principal authorities? => ANSWER: Council, Standing Committees and Commissioner | WHY: The three principal authorities of a Municipal Corporation are the Council headed by the Mayor, the Standing Committees and the Commissioner.
- Q: Under the Community Participation Law, an Area Sabha consists of: => ANSWER: All voters in a polling-booth area | WHY: An Area Sabha consists of all voters in a polling-booth area and sits below the Wards Committee.
- Q: Who is the chief executive of a Municipal Corporation and is ordinarily appointed by the State Government? => ANSWER: Municipal Commissioner | WHY: The Municipal Commissioner is the chief executive of a Municipal Corporation and is appointed by the State Government, commonly from the IAS.
- Q: The Municipal Commissioner is the: => ANSWER: State-appointed chief executive of a Municipal Corporation | WHY: The Municipal Commissioner is the State-appointed chief executive of a Municipal Corporation; the Mayor heads the Corporation and the Sarpanch the Gram Panchayat.
- Q: Which body governs an area in transition from rural to urban? => ANSWER: Nagar Panchayat | WHY: A Nagar Panchayat governs a transitional area; the Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti and Zila Parishad work at village, block and district level.
- Q: Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the accounts and audit of Panchayats? => ANSWER: Panchayat audit laws are enacted by the Union Parliament. | WHY: Option C is incorrect: under Article 243J, the State Legislature, not Parliament, provides for keeping and auditing Panchayat accounts.

## SEC 2:82:2 | Part IXA essentials
- Q: The Constitution (Seventy-Fourth Amendment) Act, 1992 inserted which Schedule? => ANSWER: Twelfth Schedule | WHY: The 74th Amendment inserted Part IXA (Articles 243P-243ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule; the Eleventh Schedule came with the 73rd.
- Q: Which one of the following is NOT a compulsory provision under the 74th Amendment? => ANSWER: Reservation for backward classes in every municipality | WHY: Reservation for backward classes is voluntary and is left to the State legislature. Ward Committees for municipalities with a population of three lakh or more, five-year terms, and SC/ST reservation are compulsory provisions.
- Q: Which Article of the Constitution provides for Ward Committees? => ANSWER: Article 243S | WHY: Article 243S provides for Ward Committees; 243Q covers types of municipalities, 243R their composition and 243T reservation.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about municipal tenure and elections? => ANSWER: An election must be held within one year of the dissolution of a municipality | WHY: An election must be held within six months of dissolution, not one year.
- Q: Which Article provides for the powers, authority and responsibilities of municipalities, including matters in the Twelfth Schedule? => ANSWER: Article 243W | WHY: Article 243W deals with the powers, authority and responsibilities of municipalities and links them with the Twelfth Schedule.
- Q: Under Article 243ZA, municipal elections are conducted by the: => ANSWER: State Election Commission | WHY: Under Article 243ZA, municipal elections are conducted by the State Election Commission, not the Election Commission of India.
- Q: Where an industrial establishment provides municipal services, the Governor may under Article 243Q specify the area as a(n): => ANSWER: Industrial township | WHY: Under the proviso to Article 243Q the Governor may specify such an area as an industrial township, and a municipality need not be constituted there.
- Q: The term of a municipality under Article 243U is calculated from the date of its: => ANSWER: First meeting | WHY: A municipality continues for five years from the date appointed for its first meeting, unless dissolved earlier according to law.
- Q: Under Article 243U, before a municipality is dissolved it must be given: => ANSWER: A reasonable opportunity of being heard | WHY: Article 243U requires that a municipality be given a reasonable opportunity of being heard before its dissolution.
- Q: Under Article 243R, which persons may be represented in a municipality but without the right to vote in its meetings? => ANSWER: Persons with special knowledge or experience in municipal administration | WHY: State law may provide for representation of persons with special knowledge or experience in municipal administration, but they cannot vote in municipal meetings.
- Q: Under Article 243S of the Constitution, a Wards Committee is mandatory in a municipality having a population of: => ANSWER: Three lakh or more | WHY: Article 243S makes Wards Committees mandatory in municipalities having a population of three lakh or more.
- Q: Consider the following statements regarding Wards Committees: 1. A Wards Committee may consist of one ward or more than one ward. 2. The State Legislature determines their composition and territorial area. 3. The councillor representing a ward is a member of… => ANSWER: 1, 2 and 3 | WHY: All three statements are correct. Wards Committees may cover one or more wards, while State law determines their composition and territorial area; the ward councillor sits on the relevant committee.
