# ROW 4:17 | Humidity, evaporation, condensation, clouds and forms of precipitation
## SEC 4:17:0 | Humidity terms
- Q: Water vapour per unit weight of air (g/kg), which is unaffected by changes in air volume, is called: => ANSWER: Specific humidity | WHY: Specific humidity (g/kg) is unaffected by volume changes; absolute humidity is vapour per unit volume (g/m³).
- Q: Air is said to be saturated when its relative humidity reaches: => ANSWER: 100 per cent | WHY: Saturation means relative humidity of 100 per cent; the dew point is the temperature at which this is reached and condensation begins.
- Q: If the actual amount of water vapour in the air remains unchanged and the air temperature rises, what happens to relative humidity? => ANSWER: It falls because the moisture-holding capacity of air increases | WHY: Warm air can hold more water vapour. Therefore, with the same actual vapour content, relative humidity falls as temperature rises and increases on cooling.
- Q: Latent heat that helps power thunderstorms and cyclones is released during: => ANSWER: Condensation | WHY: Evaporation absorbs latent heat and condensation releases it; this released energy powers thunderstorms and cyclones.
- Q: The amount of water vapour in the atmosphere ____ with altitude. => ANSWER: decreases | WHY: Water vapour falls with altitude; it is up to about 4 per cent in the humid tropics and under 1 per cent in deserts and polar areas.
- Q: Which one of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched? => ANSWER: Isobar — places of equal temperature | WHY: An isobar joins places of equal atmospheric pressure, not equal temperature. An isotherm joins places of equal temperature.
- Q: The temperature at which air becomes saturated is called the: => ANSWER: Dew point | WHY: The dew point is the temperature at which air becomes saturated; condensation then turns vapour into liquid or ice.
- Q: The latent heat of vaporisation of water is about: => ANSWER: 540 cal/g | WHY: The latent heat of vaporisation of water is 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg or 540 cal/g; 80 cal/g is the latent heat of fusion of ice.
- Q: What happens to the boiling point of water when pressure increases? => ANSWER: It increases | WHY: Raising the pressure raises the boiling point, which is why a pressure cooker cooks faster. Lower pressure at altitude lowers it.
- Q: Steam causes more severe burns than boiling water mainly because of: => ANSWER: The latent heat it releases on condensing | WHY: Steam releases extra heat (latent heat) when it condenses on the skin, so it burns more severely than boiling water.
- Q: During the operation of a refrigerator, the refrigerant: => ANSWER: Absorbs heat during evaporation and releases it during condensation | WHY: The refrigerant absorbs heat inside the fridge as it evaporates and gives it out at the back as it condenses. Option B reverses this.

## SEC 4:17:3 | Forms of condensation
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about the hydrological cycle? => ANSWER: Combustion is a normal stage of the hydrological cycle. | WHY: Combustion is not a stage of the hydrological cycle; evapotranspiration, sublimation and groundwater flow are components of it.
- Q: In India, the approximate annual precipitation and utilisable water resources are respectively: => ANSWER: 4,000 BCM and 1,139 BCM | WHY: India receives about 4,000 BCM of precipitation annually, of which approximately 1,139 BCM is utilisable, including 690 BCM of surface water and 449 BCM of groundwater.
- Q: Frost differs from dew because frost => ANSWER: forms as ice crystals when the dew point is at or below 0°C | WHY: Frost forms as ice crystals when the dew point is at or below 0°C. Dew is liquid moisture condensed on cool surfaces with a dew point above 0°C.
- Q: A cloud at ground level, with visibility under 1 km, formed when air cools below its dew point is: => ANSWER: Fog | WHY: Fog is a ground-level cloud with visibility under 1 km; mist is thinner and wetter, with visibility over 1 km.
- Q: Los Angeles smog, which requires sunlight and can produce PAN, is ____ in nature. => ANSWER: photochemical | WHY: Los Angeles smog is photochemical, needing sunlight and able to form PAN; London smog is sulphurous, cool and humid.
- Q: Layered ice balls formed in cumulonimbus clouds with strong updrafts are called: => ANSWER: Hail | WHY: Hail forms in cumulonimbus clouds with strong updrafts; sleet is rain frozen into pellets and snow forms by condensation below 0°C.
- Q: Dew and frost are favoured by which combination of conditions? => ANSWER: Clear skies, calm air, high relative humidity and long cold nights | WHY: Clear and calm nights allow surfaces to cool efficiently, while high humidity and long cold nights favour dew or frost formation. Clouds and wind tend to prevent them.
- Q: Which one of the following pairs is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Rain — drops exceeding 0.5 mm | WHY: Rain consists of drops exceeding 0.5 mm. Drizzle has fine drops under 0.5 mm and commonly comes from stratus clouds; snow consists of ice crystals formed below 0°C.
- Q: Above a wet-bulb temperature of about ____, the human body cannot cool itself by sweating. => ANSWER: 31.5°C | WHY: Above a wet-bulb temperature of about 31.5°C, sweating can no longer cool the body; wet-bulb equals dry-bulb only at 100% relative humidity.
- Q: About two-thirds of Andhra Pradesh's average annual rainfall of about 966 mm comes from the: => ANSWER: South-West monsoon | WHY: About two-thirds of AP's rainfall comes from the South-West monsoon and about one-third from the North-East monsoon.
- Q: The eastern side of the Western Ghats receives scanty rainfall because it lies on the: => ANSWER: Leeward side | WHY: The eastern side is leeward, in the rain shadow, where descending air is warm and dry; peninsular rainfall decreases from west to east.
## SEC 4:17:4 | Precipitation types
- Q: Pre-monsoon mango showers are an example of ____ precipitation. => ANSWER: convectional | WHY: Mango showers, like daily equatorial rain, are convectional precipitation; warm air lifted over cold air along a front gives frontal rain.
- Q: Which of the following is wrongly matched? => ANSWER: Rain-shadow area – Wet windward side of a mountain range | WHY: The rain-shadow area is the dry leeward side of a mountain range, not the wet windward side.
- Q: Which of the following pairs of precipitation type and its feature is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Relief-induced precipitation – Contrast between windward and leeward sides | WHY: Relief rain produces a windward–leeward contrast. Convectional rain gives afternoon showers in equatorial regions and frontal rain is typical of temperate regions.
- Q: Which of the following is wrongly matched with its role in Indian rainfall? => ANSWER: Aravallis – Heavy orographic rainfall in Rajasthan as they lie across the monsoon winds | WHY: The Aravallis give little orographic rainfall in Rajasthan because the winds run roughly parallel to them.
- Q: Which of the following is wrongly matched with its role in producing intense rainfall? => ANSWER: Sudden release – Accumulated water falls when updrafts strengthen | WHY: The accumulated water falls intensively when updrafts weaken, not when they strengthen.
## SEC 4:17:5 | Optical phenomena & extremes
- Q: The sun halo is generally observed as => ANSWER: a ring of about 22° formed by refraction through ice crystals in high cirrostratus clouds | WHY: A sun halo is typically a ring of about 22° produced when sunlight is refracted through millions of hexagonal ice crystals in thin, high cirrostratus clouds.
- Q: Silver iodide is useful as a cloud-seeding agent because: => ANSWER: Its crystal structure mimics that of ice | WHY: Silver iodide resembles ice crystallographically; seeding needs suitable clouds already present and cannot work in their absence.
- Q: Acid rain is precipitation with a pH below: => ANSWER: 5.6 | WHY: Acid rain has a pH below 5.6; the term was coined by Robert Angus Smith in 1872, and it comes mainly from SO2 and NOx.
- Q: A cloudburst is generally defined as rainfall of about: => ANSWER: 100 mm per hour or more over 20-30 sq km | WHY: A cloudburst is rainfall of about 100 mm per hour or more over a small area of 20-30 sq km, usually from cumulonimbus clouds. The other options give the wrong intensity, a daily rather than hourly rate, or far too large an area.
- Q: A rainbow is seen when: => ANSWER: The Sun is behind the observer and water drops are in front | WHY: The viewing condition for a rainbow is the Sun behind the observer and water drops in front; the rainbow itself comes from refraction, dispersion and internal reflection in the drops.
- Q: A secondary rainbow is formed by how many total internal reflections in each drop? => ANSWER: Two | WHY: A secondary rainbow uses two reflections in each drop (a primary uses one), so its colours are reversed and it is fainter.
- Q: In a primary rainbow, which colour appears on the outer side? => ANSWER: Red | WHY: Each wavelength refracts differently, so a primary rainbow has red on the outside and violet on the inside.

# ROW 4:18 | Air masses and fronts - classification and associated weather
## SEC 4:18:0 | Air mass basics
- Q: In the Bergeron classification of air masses, the first letter indicates the: => ANSWER: Moisture condition | WHY: The first letter denotes moisture: c means continental and dry, while m means maritime and moist.
- Q: The force exerted by air is called: => ANSWER: Atmospheric pressure | WHY: Atmospheric pressure is the force of air; temperature is the degree of heat, humidity the water vapour and cloudiness the condensed particles.
- Q: Which of the following best describes an air mass? => ANSWER: A large body of air with relatively uniform temperature and humidity | WHY: An air mass is a large body of air with fairly uniform temperature and humidity. A narrow boundary between air masses is a front.
- Q: Which of the following pairs of source region and air mass is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Polar ocean – mP | WHY: A polar ocean source gives maritime polar (mP) air. Tropical ocean gives mT, tropical land cT and polar land cP.
- Q: Which of the following statements is incorrect? => ANSWER: Passage over an ocean causes an air mass to lose moisture. | WHY: Option B is incorrect: passing over an ocean adds moisture to an air mass. Warm surfaces make it unstable, land drier and cold surfaces stable.
- Q: An air mass loses its original characteristics as it moves away from its source region because it: => ANSWER: Exchanges heat and moisture with the surfaces it passes over | WHY: A moving air mass is warmed or cooled and gains or loses moisture over new surfaces, so it loses its source-region character.
- Q: Air masses exert major control over which combination of atmospheric conditions? => ANSWER: Temperature, humidity, stability and precipitation | WHY: Air masses control temperature, humidity, stability and precipitation of the places they cross.
## SEC 4:18:1 | Air mass types
- Q: The very cold and very dry cA (continental Arctic) air mass originates over the: => ANSWER: Arctic basin and Greenland ice | WHY: cA originates over the Arctic basin and Greenland ice; cT comes from the Sahara and Arabia, mE from equatorial oceans and mP from high-latitude oceans.

## SEC 4:18:3 | Types of front
- Q: Which one of the following is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Stationary front — neither air mass advances — rain may persist for days | WHY: At a stationary front neither air mass advances, and cloud and rain may persist for days. Cold fronts are steep, warm fronts have gentle slopes, and occlusion lifts warm air off the ground.
- Q: Which of the following is wrongly matched with its description? => ANSWER: Warm front – Warm air advances over cold air; steep uplift | WHY: At a warm front, warm air rises gently over cold air, giving gradual uplift; steep uplift belongs to the cold front.
## SEC 4:18:4 | Passage of a front
- Q: Which sequence of clouds is normally associated with the approach of a warm front? => ANSWER: Cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus | WHY: The usual sequence ahead of an approaching warm front is cirrus, cirrostratus, altostratus and nimbostratus.
- Q: The passage of a cold front is followed by: => ANSWER: Cold, clear air | WHY: A warm front brings falling pressure and steady rain, the warm sector warm humid air, and the cold front is followed by cold, clear air.
- Q: Wind is the horizontal movement of air from: => ANSWER: High pressure to low pressure | WHY: Wind blows from high to low pressure, and a steeper pressure gradient makes it stronger.

- Q: Arcus or shelf/roll clouds commonly form: => ANSWER: At the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts | WHY: Arcus clouds form along the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts and mark the boundary between warm and cool air.

- Q: The Polar Front Theory of temperate cyclones was put forward by: => ANSWER: Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes | WHY: Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes of the Bergen School, Norway, proposed that wave cyclones form on the polar front where cold polar air meets warm westerly air.
