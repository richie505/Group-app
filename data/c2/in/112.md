# ROW 5:86 | Loss of biodiversity - causes, invasive alien species, IUCN Red List categories
## SEC 5:86:0 | Causes: two frameworks
- Q: Which of the following is one of the 'Evil Quartet' causes of biodiversity loss? => ANSWER: Co-extinctions | WHY: The Evil Quartet is habitat loss and fragmentation, over-exploitation, invasive alien species and co-extinctions.
- Q: The HIPPO framework of biodiversity loss was popularised by: => ANSWER: E. O. Wilson | WHY: E. O. Wilson popularised HIPPO (Habitat loss, Invasive species, Pollution, human Population, Over-harvesting); co-extinction belongs to the Evil Quartet.
- Q: Which one of the following combinations contains only recognised threats to biodiversity? => ANSWER: Global warming, habitat fragmentation and invasive alien species | WHY: Global warming, habitat fragmentation and invasion by alien species are recognised threats to biodiversity. Promotion of vegetarianism and protected-area expansion are not listed as biodiversity-loss threats in this context.
- Q: Which group is especially vulnerable to the effects of habitat fragmentation? => ANSWER: Migratory animals and wide-ranging mammals and birds | WHY: Habitat fragmentation particularly affects migratory animals and wide-ranging mammals and birds because it disrupts movement and separates populations.
- Q: The One Health approach is particularly relevant to addressing: => ANSWER: Zoonotic spillover arising from habitat disruption and wildlife trade | WHY: Zoonotic spillover may increase through habitat disruption and wildlife trade. The One Health approach recognises the interconnected health of humans, animals and ecosystems.
- Q: Which pair of species is correctly associated with extinction caused by over-exploitation? => ANSWER: Steller’s sea cow and passenger pigeon | WHY: Over-exploitation drove the Steller’s sea cow and the passenger pigeon to extinction.
- Q: The Living Planet Index is primarily a measure of the average change in: => ANSWER: Vertebrate populations since 1970 | WHY: The Living Planet Index, published through WWF's Living Planet assessment, measures the average change in vertebrate populations since 1970.
- Q: A charismatic species used to rally public support for conservation is known as a: => ANSWER: Flagship species | WHY: A flagship species is a charismatic species used to generate public support for conservation; the tiger and Asiatic lion are examples.
- Q: Which one of the following best describes a keystone species? => ANSWER: A species whose ecological effect is much greater than would be expected from its population size | WHY: A keystone species has an ecological effect disproportionately larger than its numerical abundance. The tiger as an apex predator and vultures as scavengers illustrate this concept.
- Q: Protection of which type of species is intended to protect many other species sharing its habitat? => ANSWER: Umbrella species | WHY: An umbrella species is one whose protection indirectly protects many other species. The tiger is an example.
- Q: The specialist groups functioning under the Species Survival Commission of the IUCN include the => ANSWER: Invasive Species Specialist Group | WHY: The Invasive Species Specialist Group (ISSG) functions under the IUCN Species Survival Commission.
- Q: A cited study found the ecological damage per invasive alien species to be higher in the: => ANSWER: Global South | WHY: Damage per invasive species was found higher in the Global South; removal alone does not necessarily restore landscapes and blanket eradication is debated.
## SEC 5:86:1 | Deforestation → extinction
- Q: The disappearance of a species that causes the decline or disappearance of species dependent on it is known as: => ANSWER: Co-extinction | WHY: Co-extinction occurs when the loss of one species causes the decline or loss of dependent species, such as an obligate pollinator or a parasite dependent on a host.
- Q: Which of the following is the primary driver of deforestation and habitat loss in the context of environmental degradation? => ANSWER: Agricultural expansion and urban development | WHY: Agricultural expansion and urban development are identified as primary drivers of deforestation and habitat loss.
- Q: Which of the following best explains the edge effect? => ANSWER: Patch boundaries experience increased light, wind, heat, predators and weeds, causing the decline of interior species. | WHY: The edge effect exposes habitat boundaries to greater light, wind, heat, predators and weeds. As fragments shrink, interior species consequently decline.
## SEC 5:86:2 | Extinction facts
- Q: Assertion (A): The sixth mass extinction is the present, human-driven wave of extinctions. Reason (R): Background extinction is the loss of a large share of species within a short geological time. => ANSWER: A is true but R is false | WHY: A is true. R is false: background extinction is slow natural loss at a low steady rate; rapid loss of a large share of species is a mass extinction.
- Q: Assertion (A): Climate change has no effect on species migration. Reason (R): Rising temperatures can shift species ranges. => ANSWER: A is false but R is true | WHY: A is false: warming can disrupt migration and alter ranges. R is true; warming can also raise extinction risk in hotspots and help alien plants reach the Arctic.
- Q: Invasive species have contributed to about what share of known animal extinctions since the 17th century? => ANSWER: 40% | WHY: Invasive species have contributed to about 40% of known animal extinctions since the 17th century and threaten about 1 in 10 Red List species.
- Q: In which year was the cheetah declared extinct in India? => ANSWER: 1952 | WHY: The cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952 and is regarded as the only large mammal lost in India since Independence.
- Q: Which of the following was recorded by the Zoological Survey of India as extinct from India? => ANSWER: Pink-headed duck | WHY: Cheetah, pink-headed duck and mountain quail were recorded as extinct from India; the passenger pigeon was not on this list.

## SEC 5:86:4 | IUCN categories (high → low risk)
- Q: Assertion (A): Under the IUCN Red List, a species not recorded for ten consecutive years is automatically classified as Extinct. Reason (R): Extinct in the Wild denotes a species surviving only in captivity or cultivation, outside its natural range. => ANSWER: A is false but R is true | WHY: A is false: Extinct is declared only when there is no reasonable doubt the last individual has died, after exhaustive surveys. - Q: Which one of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched? => ANSWER: NT — Not Threatened | WHY: NT stands for Near Threatened, not Not Threatened. DD means Data Deficient, NE means Not Evaluated and VU means Vulnerable.

- Q: The term ‘Threatened’ in the IUCN classification collectively refers to which of the following categories? => ANSWER: CR, EN and VU | WHY: The term Threatened includes Critically Endangered, Endangered and Vulnerable species.
