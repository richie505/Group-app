# ROW 4:11 | Weathering, mass wasting and soil formation; denudation and the cycle of erosion
## SEC 4:11:0 | Denudation
- Q: The first stage in the process of denudation is: => ANSWER: Weathering | WHY: Denudation proceeds through weathering, then mass wasting or erosion, transportation and finally deposition.
## SEC 4:11:1 | Weathering processes
- Q: Which one of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched? => ANSWER: Oxidation — Formation of clay from feldspar | WHY: Formation of clay from feldspar is associated with hydrolysis. Oxidation involves the rusting of iron-bearing minerals and may produce red or yellow soils.
- Q: Rainwater containing carbon dioxide forms carbonic acid. This process, which dissolves limestone and stores carbon as bicarbonate, is called => ANSWER: Carbonation | WHY: Carbonation occurs when carbon dioxide in rainwater forms carbonic acid, which dissolves limestone and converts carbon into bicarbonate.
- Q: Which one of the following correctly describes hydration in weathering? => ANSWER: Absorption of water by minerals causing them to swell | WHY: Hydration is the absorption of water by minerals, causing them to swell; conversion of anhydrite to gypsum is an example.

- Q: Weathering is fastest in ____ climates. => ANSWER: hot and wet | WHY: Weathering is fastest in hot, wet climates.
- Q: Exfoliation domes are commonly formed by => ANSWER: Onion-like peeling of granite | WHY: Exfoliation domes develop through onion-like peeling of granite, commonly associated with weathering and pressure or temperature effects.
## SEC 4:11:3 | Mass wasting
- Q: Which of the following pairs of mass-wasting type and its nature is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Slump – Rotational slip along a curved surface in weak, saturated soil | WHY: A slump is a rotational slip on a curved surface in weak, saturated soil. - Q: A very slow downslope movement of material, caused by repeated wetting-drying or heating-cooling, is called: => ANSWER: Creep | WHY: Creep is a very slow movement that may result from repeated wetting and drying or heating and cooling; rockfall is the free fall of rocks from cliffs.

- Q: Which one of the following is the correct sequence of dominant river work from the upper course to the lower course? => ANSWER: Vertical erosion — lateral erosion and transport — deposition | WHY: The upper course is dominated by vertical erosion, the middle course by lateral erosion and transport, and the lower course by deposition.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about the middle course of a river? => ANSWER: Vertical erosion is the sole dominant work. | WHY: Vertical erosion is not the sole dominant process in the middle course, which is marked by lateral erosion, transport, wider valleys and meanders.

- Q: A V-shaped valley develops through the sequence of: => ANSWER: Rills, gullies and valleys produced mainly by vertical erosion | WHY: Rills enlarge into gullies and then valleys through predominantly vertical erosion, producing a V-shaped valley.

- Q: Coastal landforms such as cliffs, arches and stacks are formed by: => ANSWER: Waves, currents and tides | WHY: Cliffs, caves, arches, stacks and beaches are coastal landforms made by waves, currents and tides.
- Q: Valleys are formed by which combination of agents or processes? => ANSWER: River, glacier, tectonic and karst processes | WHY: Valleys are carved by rivers, glaciers, tectonic faulting and karst solution.
- Q: Arêtes, horns, hanging valleys and moraines are formed by: => ANSWER: Glacial erosion and deposition | WHY: Arêtes, horns, U-shaped and hanging valleys and moraines all come from glacial erosion and deposition.

- Q: Which of the following is a feature of the old (lower) course of a river? => ANSWER: Oxbow lake | WHY: Oxbow lakes and natural levees belong to the old (lower) course; waterfalls, potholes and rapids are youth (upper) course features.

- Q: Karst topography develops most characteristically in regions underlain by: => ANSWER: Limestone or dolomite | WHY: Karst landscapes are carved mainly by groundwater dissolving limestone or dolomite.
