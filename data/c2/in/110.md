## SEC 5:74:4 | Succession phases (Clements, 1916)
- Q: In Frederic Clements' scheme of succession, which phase immediately follows nudation? => ANSWER: Invasion | WHY: Clements' sequence is nudation, invasion, competition and co-action, reaction and stabilisation.
- Q: The term ‘ecesis’ in ecological succession refers to => ANSWER: the successful establishment of invading organisms | WHY: Ecesis is the successful establishment of organisms that have migrated into an area during the invasion phase.
- Q: In Clements' phases of succession, organisms modify soil, water, light and temperature during: => ANSWER: Reaction | WHY: Reaction, the most important phase, is when organisms modify soil, water, light and temperature, making the site suitable for later species.
- Q: Seasonal succession differs from succession caused by a disturbance because seasonal succession is => ANSWER: a cyclic change driven by environmental rhythms | WHY: Seasonal succession is cyclical and is driven by environmental rhythms; it is not caused by a disaster or disturbance.

- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about primary and secondary succession? => ANSWER: Secondary succession is generally slower than primary succession | WHY: Secondary succession is generally faster, not slower, because soil, seeds or roots may remain.

- Q: In a hydrosere, which stage comes immediately after the reed-swamp stage? => ANSWER: Sedge-meadow | WHY: Hydrosere proceeds phytoplankton - submerged rooted plants - floating plants - reed-swamp - sedge-meadow - woodland - forest.
