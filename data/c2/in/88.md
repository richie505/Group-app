# ROW 4:38 | India - climate: mechanism of the monsoon, theories, seasons and their characteristics
## SEC 4:38:0 | Theories of the monsoon
- Q: According to the classical thermal theory of the monsoon, the summer monsoon is produced primarily because: => ANSWER: Land heats faster than the sea, creating a low-pressure area over land that draws moist winds from the sea. | WHY: Sir Edmund Halley's thermal theory, proposed in 1686, attributes the monsoon to differential heating of land and sea. Summer heating creates a low over land that attracts moist maritime winds.
- Q: The dynamic or ITCZ theory explains the southwest monsoon mainly through: => ANSWER: The northward shift of the ITCZ over the Ganga plain, drawing south-east trade winds across the equator as south-westerlies. | WHY: In summer, the ITCZ shifts northward over the Ganga plain as the monsoon trough. South-east trades cross the equator and are deflected to become south-westerly winds.
- Q: The tropical easterly jet of the Indian summer monsoon extends from the South China Sea towards: => ANSWER: East Africa | WHY: The tropical easterly jet extends from the South China Sea to East Africa during the monsoon.
- Q: The heating of the Tibetan Plateau contributes to the southwest monsoon by: => ANSWER: Creating an upper-air anticyclone and strengthening the tropical easterly jet | WHY: The Tibetan Plateau, at about 5,000 m, acts as a heat island in summer. Its intense heating produces an upper-air anticyclone and strengthens the tropical easterly jet.
- Q: Heat waves in north-western and central India generally peak in: => ANSWER: May–June | WHY: North-western and central India are hottest from April to June, and heat waves generally peak in May–June.
- Q: Dust aerosols over the Indo-Gangetic Plain assist the advance of the monsoon inland because they: => ANSWER: Heat the mid-troposphere and help pull the monsoon inland | WHY: Dust aerosols heat the mid-troposphere over the Indo-Gangetic Plain. This heating helps pull the monsoon inland.
- Q: ‘Nautapa’ refers to: => ANSWER: A nine-day period of peak summer heat in north India before the monsoon | WHY: Nautapa is the traditional nine-day peak of summer heat in north India and occurs just before the arrival of the monsoon.
- Q: The withdrawal of the southwest monsoon from India normally: => ANSWER: Begins in the north-west in October and ends by the end of November | WHY: Withdrawal begins from north-western India in October and is generally complete by the end of November.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about the retreating south-west monsoon? => ANSWER: The westerly jet shifts north of the Himalayas | WHY: During the retreat the westerly jet returns south of the Himalayas, not north. The other three conditions accompany the retreating monsoon.
- Q: Which phenomenon was being watched in 2026 as a possible offset to El Nino's adverse influence on the Indian monsoon? => ANSWER: Indian Ocean Dipole | WHY: The Indian Ocean Dipole was being monitored as a possible offset to the influence of El Nino.
## SEC 4:38:1 | Key terms
- Q: Monsoons are best described as: => ANSWER: Seasonal secondary winds that reverse direction with the season | WHY: Monsoons are seasonal or periodic secondary winds whose direction reverses with the season; they are not primary planetary winds.
- Q: The word 'monsoon' is derived from the Arabic word 'Mausim', which means => ANSWER: season | WHY: 'Mausim' is Arabic for season, reflecting the seasonal reversal of these winds. The word does not mean rain, wind or sea, though monsoons bring rain and are winds that blow from the sea in summer.
- Q: The principal role of the Mascarene High in the south-west monsoon mechanism is to: => ANSWER: Drive cross-equatorial flow towards the Indian subcontinent | WHY: The Mascarene High drives the cross-equatorial flow that contributes to the formation of the south-west monsoon.
- Q: The Somali jet intensifies the Indian monsoon in association with the: => ANSWER: Somalia Current | WHY: The Somali jet, together with the Somalia Current, intensifies the monsoon winds.
- Q: During a ‘break’ in the southwest monsoon, which one of the following conditions generally occurs? => ANSWER: The monsoon trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills, leaving the plains relatively dry. | WHY: During a monsoon break, the trough shifts towards the Himalayan foothills. The plains consequently experience dry conditions while the Himalayan region receives heavy rainfall.

- Q: The Bharat Forecast System developed under Mission Mausam provides: => ANSWER: 6 km resolution and 10-day rainfall forecasts | WHY: The Bharat Forecast System provides forecasts at 6 km resolution, including 10-day rainfall forecasts.
