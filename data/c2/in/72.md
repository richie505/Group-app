## SEC 3:86:0 | Asset classification
- Q: Which of the following asset classifications is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Sub-standard asset – NPA for 12 months or less | WHY: A sub-standard asset is an NPA for 12 months or less; an NPA for more than 12 months is doubtful.
- Q: A Special Mention Account classified as SMA-1 has overdue payments of: => ANSWER: 31–60 days | WHY: SMA-0, SMA-1 and SMA-2 cover 1–30, 31–60 and 61–90 overdue days; SMAs are early-warning accounts, not yet NPAs.
## SEC 3:86:1 | Definition
- Q: A loan or advance is generally classified as a Non-Performing Asset when the interest or principal instalment remains overdue for more than: => ANSWER: 90 days | WHY: An asset becomes an NPA when interest or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days.
- Q: A loan for a short-duration crop becomes an NPA when it remains overdue for: => ANSWER: Two crop seasons | WHY: Agricultural loans become NPAs after two crop seasons for short-duration crops and one crop season for long-duration crops.
- Q: Under income-recognition norms, interest on an NPA is booked as income: => ANSWER: Only when it is actually received | WHY: Interest on an NPA is not recognised as income until it is actually received. This forms part of the IRACP framework.
## SEC 3:86:2 | Key terms
- Q: The Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR) of a bank is: => ANSWER: Provisions held ÷ Gross NPAs | WHY: PCR is provisions held divided by gross NPAs; Gross NPA minus provisions gives Net NPA.
- Q: The Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR) of a borrower is calculated as: => ANSWER: EBIT ÷ interest due | WHY: ICR = operating earnings (EBIT) divided by interest due; it indicates a borrower's present and emerging risk.
- Q: Evergreening of loans means: => ANSWER: Giving fresh loans to repay old loans and conceal default | WHY: Evergreening involves extending fresh loans to repay old loans, thereby keeping the underlying default hidden.
## SEC 3:86:3 | Twin balance sheet problem
- Q: The twin balance sheet problem refers to the simultaneous existence of: => ANSWER: Over-leveraged corporates and capital-starved, NPA-laden banks | WHY: The twin balance sheet problem describes over-leveraged firms alongside capital-starved banks burdened with NPAs.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about the twin balance sheet problem? => ANSWER: Over-leveraged firms can undertake fresh investment without difficulty | WHY: Over-leveraged firms find it hard to invest, which is why private investment falls.
## SEC 3:86:4 | Causes & response
- Q: Aggressive bank lending during 2010-15 that later turned into NPAs went largely to: => ANSWER: Infrastructure, steel and power | WHY: Lending in 2010-15 went largely to infrastructure, steel and power without strict appraisal, leading to stalled projects.
- Q: In the NPA vicious cycle, the accumulation of NPAs leads directly to: => ANSWER: Provisioning and capital erosion | WHY: Aggressive lending and shocks create NPAs, which require provisioning and erode capital, resulting in weak credit growth.
- Q: The twin balance sheet problem was prominently framed in the Economic Survey: => ANSWER: 2016–17 | WHY: The Economic Survey 2016–17, under Chief Economic Adviser Arvind Subramanian, discussed the festering twin balance sheet problem.
- Q: The Asset Quality Review was carried out by the RBI in: => ANSWER: 2015 | WHY: The RBI conducted the Asset Quality Review in 2015.
- Q: The Government's 4R strategy in response to the banking-sector stress consisted of: => ANSWER: Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise and Reform | WHY: The four Rs were Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise and Reform. The response included the IBC, recapitalisation and NARCL.
