- Q: According to Manu Smriti, which type of marriage is considered the highest form and involves giving the daughter to a qualified, educated and virtuous groom? => ANSWER: Brahma Marriage | WHY: In Manu Smriti, Brahma marriage, where the father gives his daughter to a learned, virtuous groom without dowry or bride price, is the highest form.
- Q: Which of the following statements is incorrect? => ANSWER: Prajapatya Marriage involves the purchase of the bride by giving wealth to her family. | WHY: Option C is incorrect: buying the bride with wealth is Asura marriage. Prajapatya marriage stresses duty, family welfare and children.
- Q: Assertion (A): Paisacha Marriage is the most condemned form of marriage according to Manu Smriti. Reason (R): It involves deceit, intoxication, coercion or exploitation of a woman without her consent. => ANSWER: Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A | WHY: Both are true: Paisacha marriage, taking a woman by deceit, intoxication or force without consent, is the most condemned form because of that lack of consent. R explains A.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is wrongly matched? => ANSWER: Daiva marriage – Marriage through forceful abduction | WHY: In Daiva marriage the daughter is given to a priest officiating at a sacrifice; abduction is Rakshasa.
- Q: Consider the following statements about forms of marriage: 1. Prajapatya marriage emphasises duty, family welfare and procreation. 2. Rakshasa marriage involves forceful abduction of a woman. 3. Gandharva marriage requires parental approval. Which of the… => ANSWER: 1 and 2 only | WHY: Prajapatya stresses duty and children and Rakshasa is abduction; statement 3 is false because Gandharva marriage needs no parental approval.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is wrongly matched? => ANSWER: Rakshasa marriage – Marriage after receiving symbolic gifts | WHY: Rakshasa marriage is by forceful abduction; marriage after symbolic gifts is Arsha.
- Q: Consider the following statements: I. Rakshasa Marriage is associated with Kshatriyas. II. Abduction-type marriages are mentioned in epics as examples of Rakshasa Marriage. III. Gandharva Marriage is based on the purchase of the bride by the groom. Which of… => ANSWER: I and II only | WHY: I and II are correct: Rakshasa marriage is linked with Kshatriyas, and abduction marriages appear in the epics. III is false because buying the bride is Asura marriage, not Gandharva.
- Q: Manu Smriti recognises how many types of Hindu marriage? => ANSWER: Eight | WHY: Manu lists eight forms: four approved (Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya) and four disapproved (Gandharva, Asura, Rakshasa, Paisacha).
- Q: What is the status of Gandharva Marriage in Manusmriti? => ANSWER: Conditionally accepted | WHY: Gandharva marriage is conditionally accepted in Manu Smriti: listed as disapproved, but tolerated. Paisacha is the most condemned.

## SEC 2:107:3 | Marriage laws
- Q: The Special (Civil) Marriage Act, 1872 was also called the: => ANSWER: Native Marriage Act | WHY: The 1872 Act, also called the Native Marriage Act, provided monogamous civil marriage and prohibited child marriage.
- Q: Under the Special (Civil) Marriage Act, 1872, the prescribed marriageable ages were: => ANSWER: 14 years for the girl and 18 years for the boy | WHY: The Act prescribed a marriageable age of 14 years for a girl and 18 years for a boy.
- Q: Divorce under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 is provided in Section: => ANSWER: 13 | WHY: Section 13 of the HMA provides for divorce; the Act covers Hindus, Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs, and STs only if notified.
- Q: The Portuguese Civil Code, 1867 in force in Goa is regarded as: => ANSWER: A form of common civil code | WHY: The Portuguese Civil Code, 1867 operating in Goa is regarded as a form of common civil code.
- Q: Under Section 5 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, which of the following is NOT a stated condition for a valid Hindu marriage? => ANSWER: The parties must belong to the same sub-caste. | WHY: Belonging to the same sub-caste is not a condition under Section 5. The section includes conditions relating to a living spouse, age, prohibited degrees and sapinda relationship, subject to custom in the last case.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Anand Marriage Act — 1909; registration added in 2012 | WHY: The Anand Marriage Act was enacted in 1909 and registration was added in 2012. The Dissolution of Muslim Marriage Act is of 1939, the Foreign Marriage Act is of 1969 and the Parsi Act is of 1936.
- Q: Self-respect marriages were institutionalised by Periyar and validated in Tamil Nadu through which provision? => ANSWER: Section 7-A of the Hindu Marriage Act | WHY: Self-respect marriages were institutionalised by Periyar and validated through Section 7-A of the Hindu Marriage Act by a Tamil Nadu amendment in 1967.
- Q: Which of the following pairs (ceremony – solemnised by/at) is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Anand Karaj – Gurdwara | WHY: Anand Karaj takes place in a gurdwara; Nikah is conducted by a Kazi and a Special Marriage Act marriage by a Marriage Officer.
- Q: Section 13B of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 deals with: => ANSWER: Divorce by mutual consent | WHY: Section 13B provides for divorce by mutual consent.
- Q: Assertion (A): The Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019 declares instant triple talaq void and illegal. Reason (R): The Act provides imprisonment of up to seven years for pronouncing instant triple talaq. => ANSWER: A is true but R is false | WHY: A is true. R is false: the punishment is up to 3 years' jail plus fine; seven years is in the Assam polygamy Bill.
- Q: Under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, a child marriage is: => ANSWER: Voidable at the option of the child | WHY: A child marriage is voidable at the option of the child; the Act fixes minimum ages of 18 for women and 21 for men.
- Q: The Allahabad High Court held in July 2026 that the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act: => ANSWER: Overrides the personal-law puberty rule for all religions | WHY: On 1 July 2026, the Allahabad High Court held that the PCMA overrides the personal-law puberty rule for all religions.
- Q: Which one of the following is NOT identified as a contemporary trend in marriage? => ANSWER: Universal return to child marriage | WHY: Companionate marriage, rising age at marriage, serial monogamy and increasing divorce are identified trends. Universal return to child marriage is not one of them.
- Q: In Supriyo (October 2023), the Supreme Court: => ANSWER: refused to read same-sex marriage into the Special Marriage Act, with CJI Chandrachud in the minority | WHY: In Supriyo (Oct 2023) the Supreme Court refused to read same-sex marriage into the SMA; CJI Chandrachud was in the minority. It did not recognise same-sex marriage or strike down the SMA, and mandatory live-in registration is a feature of the Uttarakhand UCC, not this judgment.
- Q: Which legislation was the first Child Marriage Restraint Law in India? => ANSWER: Sharda Act, 1929 | WHY: The Sarda Act (Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929) was the first law restraining child marriage. The Age of Consent Act, 1891, was an earlier measure but not a restraint law.
- Q: Consider the following statements: 1. The first special marriage law was the Special Marriage Act, 1872. 2. The first codified Hindu marriage law after Independence was the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Which of the above statement(s) is/are correct? => ANSWER: Both 1 and 2 | WHY: Both are true: the Special Marriage Act, 1872 was the first special marriage law, and the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 the first codified Hindu marriage law after Independence.
