# ROW 3:143 | Special Category Status - criteria, the 14th Finance Commission position and the controversy
## SEC 3:143:0 | Origin & FIRSTs
- Q: Special Category Status (SCS) was first introduced in India in 1969 on the recommendation of which body? => ANSWER: The Fifth Finance Commission | WHY: Special Category Status was introduced in 1969 on the recommendation of the Fifth Finance Commission. The National Development Council endorsed the Gadgil Formula (1968-69) for distributing Plan funds, but it was not the body that recommended SCS.
- Q: Assertion (A): Special Category Status is not expressly provided for in the Constitution of India. Reason (R): It was an executive and Planning Commission-era classification. => ANSWER: Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A | WHY: SCS is not a constitutional category because it arose as an executive, Planning Commission-era classification linked to preferential Plan assistance.
- Q: Which formula was introduced in 1991 as the later version of the Gadgil Formula for Plan transfers to States? => ANSWER: Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula | WHY: The Gadgil Formula was later modified and became the Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula in 1991.
- Q: Which were the first three States to be given Special Category Status? => ANSWER: Jammu & Kashmir, Assam and Nagaland | WHY: The first three SCS States were Jammu & Kashmir, Assam and Nagaland (1969). Manipur, Tripura, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Mizoram, Meghalaya and Uttarakhand got the status later; Uttarakhand was the last of the 11.
## SEC 3:143:1 | Criteria
- Q: Which of the following was NOT a criterion associated with Special Category Status? => ANSWER: High population density and advanced infrastructure | WHY: High population density and advanced infrastructure were not criteria for Special Category Status. The criteria included difficult terrain, low population density or sizeable tribal population, border location, economic and infrastructural backwardness, and non-viable finances.
## SEC 3:143:2 | Benefits: SCS vs general
- Q: What was the rationale behind granting Special Category Status to States? => ANSWER: To give them a larger share of central taxes to cover the gap caused by geographic disadvantage and backwardness | WHY: SCS was meant to give such States a larger tax share to cover the gap arising from geographic disadvantage and economic and infrastructural backwardness.
- Q: Central loans to Special Category States carried ____ interest. => ANSWER: Concessional | WHY: SCS States got Central loans at concessional interest, while general States got normal terms.
- Q: What was the treatment of unspent funds in a Special Category State? => ANSWER: They did not lapse and could be carried forward | WHY: Unspent funds in Special Category States did not lapse and could be carried forward. In general States, such funds lapsed.
- Q: Under PMGSY, habitations eligible for road connectivity in Special Category States have a population of: => ANSWER: 250 or more, against 500 or more in the plains | WHY: The PMGSY threshold is 250+ in SCS States against 500+ in the plains. The other options give the wrong thresholds, and the last one reverses the concession. A similar concession applies to Consumer Welfare Fund seed money (90:10 for SCS against 75:25).
