## SEC 3:73:0 | Trade terms
- Q: The theory of absolute advantage in international trade is associated with: => ANSWER: Adam Smith | WHY: Absolute advantage is Adam Smith's idea; Ricardo gave comparative advantage based on lower opportunity cost.
- Q: Export promotion and import substitution are respectively described as: => ANSWER: Outward-looking and inward-looking strategies | WHY: Import substitution is an inward-looking strategy, whereas export promotion is an outward-looking strategy.
- Q: A countervailing duty is imposed to: => ANSWER: Offset the effect of foreign subsidies | WHY: Countervailing duty offsets foreign subsidies; anti-dumping duty addresses imports below home-market price, and rules of origin decide the national source.
- Q: When a country both imports and exports the same product, for example cars, the trade is called: => ANSWER: Intra-industry trade | WHY: Intra-industry trade is the import and export of the same product by a country. Entrepot trade is re-exporting imported goods. Inter-industry trade is exchanging different products, as comparative advantage predicts. Dumping is exporting below the home-market price or cost.
- Q: Which department frames and monitors India's Foreign Trade Policy? => ANSWER: Department of Commerce | WHY: The Department of Commerce frames and monitors India's Foreign Trade Policy.
- Q: For trade and tariff purposes, a duty-free enclave in an SEZ is treated as: => ANSWER: Foreign territory | WHY: A duty-free enclave is treated as foreign territory for trade and tariff purposes, though it lies within India.
- Q: A tax levied on imports is called a: => ANSWER: Tariff | WHY: A tax on imports is a tariff; an import quota is a quantity restriction and import licensing an administrative restriction.
- Q: Importing goods, storing or trading them, and then re-exporting them is called: => ANSWER: Entrepot trade | WHY: Entrepot trade means importing, storing or trading and then re-exporting goods.
