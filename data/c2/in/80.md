## SEC 4:4:0 | Why seasons occur
- Q: Which combination most accurately explains the seasonal variation in the angle of incidence of solar rays and the length of day? => ANSWER: Axial inclination, parallelism of the axis, revolution and the spherical shape of the Earth | WHY: Seasonal variation results from the Earth’s axial inclination, the parallelism of its axis, its revolution around the Sun and its spherical shape. These alter the Sun’s angle and the duration of daylight received by different regions.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT a factor affecting the distribution of insolation? => ANSWER: Direction of ocean tides | WHY: The major factors listed are the angle of incidence, duration of day, atmospheric transparency and land configuration. Direction of ocean tides is not one of them.
## SEC 4:4:1 | Summer vs winter
- Q: When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, it experiences: => ANSWER: Summer | WHY: The hemisphere tilted towards the Sun gets more direct rays and longer days, producing summer.
- Q: The summer hemisphere receives greater heating than the winter hemisphere because: => ANSWER: Its solar rays are more direct and its days are longer | WHY: Direct rays and longer days give the summer hemisphere more heating.
- Q: The summer hemisphere is the one that is: => ANSWER: Tilted towards the Sun | WHY: The summer hemisphere tilts towards the Sun, getting concentrated energy, a higher Sun and longer days.
- Q: More oblique solar rays produce lower heating because they: => ANSWER: Spread the same energy over a larger area | WHY: Oblique rays spread the same energy over a larger area, so heating per unit area is lower.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Vertically overhead Sun – No shadow at noon | WHY: An overhead Sun casts no shadow at noon; a higher Sun gives a shorter shadow, a lower Sun a longer one, and the midnight sun is the Sun visible above the horizon at midnight.
## SEC 4:4:2 | Distance is not the cause
- Q: The seasons on the Earth are caused mainly by: => ANSWER: Axial tilt | WHY: Seasons come mainly from axial tilt affecting solar angle and day length; Earth is in fact nearest the Sun during northern winter.

# ROW 4:5 | Interior of the earth - crust, mantle, core; discontinuities and seismic evidence
## SEC 4:5:0 | Sources of information
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about sources of information on the Earth's interior? => ANSWER: Meteorites provide direct evidence of the interior | WHY: Meteorites are an indirect source; only material such as magma and lava gives direct evidence.

- Q: Which one of the following is a direct source of information about the interior of the Earth? => ANSWER: Surface rocks from mining areas | WHY: Surface rocks obtained from mining areas are a direct source because the material itself is examined. Seismic waves, gravity anomalies and magnetic surveys are indirect sources that infer the interior from measurements.
- Q: Surface seismic waves are also called: => ANSWER: L-waves | WHY: Surface waves are L-waves; P-waves are primary and S-waves secondary waves.

- Q: Which of the following statements about seismic waves is incorrect? => ANSWER: S-waves can travel through solids and liquids. | WHY: Option A is incorrect: S-waves travel only through solids and stop at liquids. P-waves arrive first, S-waves are shear waves and surface waves do most damage.

## SEC 4:5:2 | Shadow zone
- Q: S-waves are absent everywhere beyond about ____ from the epicentre. => ANSWER: 105° | WHY: S-waves are absent beyond 105°, so the S-wave shadow zone is larger than the P-wave shadow zone.

- Q: The P-wave shadow zone lies approximately between: => ANSWER: 103° and 142° | WHY: The P-wave shadow zone is about 103°–142° from the epicentre.
## SEC 4:5:3 | Layers
- Q: Why do seismic waves help in identifying discontinuities inside the Earth? => ANSWER: Their speed changes with density, causing reflection and refraction at layer boundaries. | WHY: Changes in density alter seismic-wave velocity and produce reflection and refraction at boundaries, revealing internal discontinuities.
- Q: Which one of the following correctly represents the approximate thickness of oceanic and continental crust respectively? => ANSWER: 5 km and 30 km | WHY: Oceanic crust is approximately 5 km thick, whereas continental crust is approximately 30 km thick, though it may reach greater thickness beneath mountains.
