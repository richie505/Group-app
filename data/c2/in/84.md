## SEC 4:28:0 | Key terms
- Q: An earthquake occurring in an uninhabited desert, without causing damage to people or property, is best classified as a: => ANSWER: Hazard | WHY: A hazard is a potentially damaging event. It becomes a disaster when it interacts with a vulnerable population or exposed assets.
- Q: Under Section 2(d) of the DM Act, 2005, a disaster causes substantial loss beyond the coping capacity of the: => ANSWER: Affected community | WHY: Section 2(d) refers to the coping capacity of the affected community, not the State Government.
- Q: Which of the following was NOT an emphasis of the 2009 National Policy on Disaster Management? => ANSWER: Exclusive dependence on post-disaster relief | WHY: The 2009 policy emphasised prevention, mitigation, community participation and mainstreaming DRR; exclusive reliance on relief runs against it.
- Q: The National and State Disaster Mitigation Funds are primarily meant for: => ANSWER: Long-term reduction of disaster risk | WHY: The Mitigation Funds are intended for long-term risk reduction and mitigation rather than immediate relief alone.
- Q: Hazard-specific plans of the Union ministries are prepared under which Section of the DM Act, 2005? => ANSWER: Section 35 | WHY: Hazard-specific plans fall under Section 35 and Ministry DM Plans under Section 37; Section 25 concerns the DDMA.
- Q: Under the DM (Amendment) Act, 2025, an Urban Disaster Management Authority is headed by the: => ANSWER: Municipal Commissioner | WHY: A UDMA is headed by the Municipal Commissioner; UDMAs cover Municipal Corporation cities except Delhi and Chandigarh.
- Q: CAP-SACHET is associated with: => ANSWER: Early warning dissemination | WHY: CAP-SACHET is used for early warning dissemination, a preparedness measure like mock drills and stockpiling.
- Q: Compensation and psychosocial care are measures of which phase of the disaster management cycle? => ANSWER: Recovery and reconstruction | WHY: Compensation and psychosocial care are recovery measures; response includes restoring critical services.
- Q: Landslide Hazard Zonation divides hill areas into homogeneous zones on the basis of: => ANSWER: Degree of susceptibility, hazard or risk | WHY: Zoning is based on the degree of susceptibility, hazard or risk; such maps are probabilistic, not certain predictions.
- Q: The State Emergency Operations Centre of Andhra Pradesh is run by: => ANSWER: APSDMA | WHY: APSDMA runs the State Emergency Operations Centre, coordinates districts, operates early warnings and plans restoration.
- Q: Cyclone Michaung (December 2023) made landfall in Andhra Pradesh near: => ANSWER: Bapatla | WHY: Michaung made landfall near Bapatla; Hudhud hit Visakhapatnam in October 2014.
- Q: Regulating deforestation is a measure of which phase of disaster management? => ANSWER: Prevention | WHY: Prevention, which is proactive and long-term, includes controlling deforestation.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of the response phase of disaster management? => ANSWER: Being a long-term activity | WHY: Response is time-critical; being a long-term activity describes recovery.
- Q: Which of the following statements is incorrect? => ANSWER: Environmental vulnerability is based on weak governance, poor coordination and lack of preparedness. | WHY: Option C is incorrect: weak governance and poor coordination are systemic or institutional vulnerability. Environmental vulnerability is things like deforestation and mangrove loss.
- Q: Loss of mangroves that increases cyclone impact is an example of: => ANSWER: Environmental vulnerability | WHY: Mangrove loss is environmental vulnerability; weak houses are physical, caste and gender discrimination social, and poverty economic.
- Q: According to the Disaster Management Act, 2005, mitigation refers to measures aimed at: => ANSWER: Reducing the risk, impact or effects of a disaster or threatening disaster situation | WHY: Under the DM Act, mitigation means measures to reduce the risk, impact or effects of a disaster or threatening disaster. The other options narrow or distort it.
