# ROW 2:18 | Amendment of the Constitution - Article 368, types of majorities, procedure, limitations
## SEC 2:18:0 | Article 368 basics
- Q: The constitutional amendment procedure in India was borrowed from which country? => ANSWER: South Africa | WHY: India borrowed the amendment procedure from South Africa. The method of electing members of the Rajya Sabha was also borrowed from South Africa.
- Q: Which of the following expressions is used in Article 368 to describe Parliament’s constituent power to amend the Constitution? => ANSWER: May, in exercise of its constituent power, amend by way of addition, variation or repeal any provision | WHY: Article 368 states that Parliament may, in exercise of its constituent power, amend the Constitution by way of addition, variation or repeal of any provision, subject to the basic-structure limitation.
- Q: Who described the constitutional amendment process as appearing 'complicated' but being 'merely diverse'? => ANSWER: Granville Austin | WHY: Granville Austin said the process appears complicated but is merely diverse, calling it one of the most ably conceived aspects of the Constitution.
- Q: Which one of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched? => ANSWER: Article 368 — Citizenship | WHY: Article 368 contains the general constitutional amendment procedure, whereas citizenship is among the matters that can be changed by simple majority under Article 11. Articles 2–4, 169 and 327 relate to the subjects stated.
- Q: As of 2026, which is the latest constitutional amendment to have been enacted in India? => ANSWER: 106th Amendment (2023) | WHY: The 106th Amendment (2023) is the latest enacted amendment. The 101st (2016, GST) and 104th (2020) are earlier enactments, and the 131st was only a Bill that failed in the Lok Sabha in April 2026.
- Q: Which Article and Part of the Constitution govern the amendment powers and procedure? => ANSWER: Article 368 in Part XX | WHY: Article 368, in Part XX, gives Parliament the power to amend the Constitution and sets the procedure. Article 356 is President's Rule and 280 the Finance Commission.
## SEC 2:18:1 | Procedure
- Q: Which one of the following best characterises the nature of the Indian constitutional amendment procedure? => ANSWER: It is a synthesis of rigidity and flexibility. | WHY: The Indian Constitution combines different methods of amendment and is therefore neither purely rigid nor purely flexible; it is a synthesis of both.
- Q: A Bill for amendment of the Constitution may be introduced: => ANSWER: By a minister or a private member, without the President's prior permission | WHY: An amendment Bill needs no prior presidential permission and may be moved by a private member; Article 108 joint sittings do not apply to it.
- Q: The requirement that the President must give assent to a constitutional amendment bill was made obligatory by the: => ANSWER: 24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971 | WHY: The 24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971 made the President's assent to a constitutional amendment bill obligatory.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is correctly matched regarding Constitutional Amendment Bills and ordinary Bills? => ANSWER: Constitutional Amendment Bill — federal provisions may require ratification by half the States | WHY: Certain Constitutional Amendments affecting federal provisions require ratification by at least half the States. Ordinary Bills generally require a simple majority, may be subject to joint sitting, and are not covered by mandatory assent in the same manner.
- Q: Which one of the following statements is correct regarding the role of State Legislatures in constitutional amendment? => ANSWER: A State Legislature cannot initiate a constitutional amendment; it can only ratify certain Bills passed by Parliament. | WHY: States cannot initiate amendments under Article 368. Their role is limited to ratifying amendments concerning specified federal provisions; the creation or abolition of a Legislative Council follows Article 169 outside Article 368.
- Q: What time limit is prescribed for State ratification of an amendment affecting specified federal provisions? => ANSWER: No time limit is prescribed | WHY: State ratification is needed only for specified federal provisions, not every amendment, and no time limit is prescribed for it.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT a criticism commonly made of the constitutional amendment procedure in India? => ANSWER: A joint sitting is available to resolve a deadlock between the two Houses on an amendment Bill. | WHY: The Constitution provides no joint sitting mechanism for resolving a deadlock between the Houses on an amendment Bill. The other three are recognised criticisms of the procedure.
- Q: A Constitution Amendment Bill must be passed by a special majority in: => ANSWER: Each House separately | WHY: An amendment Bill can start in either House without the President's recommendation and needs a special majority in each House separately; there is no joint sitting.
- Q: In case of a deadlock between the two Houses over an Ordinary Bill, the Constitution provides for: => ANSWER: A joint sitting | WHY: An ordinary Bill needs a simple majority, both Houses have equal powers over it, and a deadlock can go to a joint sitting.
- Q: Amendments to specified federal provisions require ratification by: => ANSWER: At least one-half of the State Legislatures | WHY: Federal provisions need ratification by at least half the States; the President's qualifications and tenure need no ratification.
## SEC 2:18:2 | Three routes of amendment
- Q: A special majority for amending the Constitution means: => ANSWER: A majority of the total membership of each House and two-thirds of members present and voting | WHY: Special majority combines two requirements in each House: a majority of the total membership and a two-thirds majority of members present and voting.
- Q: Which of the following requires a special majority of Parliament along with ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures? => ANSWER: The Seventh Schedule | WHY: The Seventh Schedule, the President's election and representation of States need State ratification; Fundamental Rights need only a special majority.
- Q: Which one of the following subjects is amended by a simple majority of Parliament outside the procedure of Article 368? => ANSWER: Quorum in Parliament | WHY: Quorum is among the matters that can be altered by a simple majority outside Article 368. Fundamental Rights and DPSPs require the special majority procedure, while the election of the President also requires State ratification.
- Q: Creation or abolition of a State Legislative Council under Article 169 requires: => ANSWER: A simple majority of Parliament, outside Article 368 | WHY: Legislative Councils are dealt with by simple majority outside Article 368; the President's election needs special majority plus State ratification.
- Q: Which of the following can NOT be changed by Parliament by a simple majority outside Article 368? => ANSWER: The basic structure of the Constitution | WHY: The basic structure cannot be amended even by the special procedure; the other three are simple-majority matters outside Article 368.
- Q: Which one of the following is NOT a provision that may be amended by a simple majority outside Article 368? => ANSWER: The distribution of legislative powers between the Union and the States | WHY: The distribution of legislative powers, including the Seventh Schedule, is a federal provision requiring the prescribed State ratification. Citizenship, the Fifth and Sixth Schedules and the number of puisne judges of the Supreme Court are amendable by simple majority.
- Q: Which of the following can be changed by Parliament by a simple majority outside Article 368? => ANSWER: Quorum in Parliament | WHY: Quorum, MPs' salaries and parliamentary privileges are simple-majority matters outside Article 368; the others need a special majority.
- Q: Which one of the following statements regarding State ratification of a Constitution Amendment Bill is correct? => ANSWER: Ratification may be done by a simple majority of the members present and voting in each concerned State Legislature. | WHY: State ratification is by simple majority of each concerned State Legislature, and no time limit is prescribed. States cannot initiate constitutional amendments.
- Q: Which of the following pairs is correctly matched? => ANSWER: Election of the President – Articles 54 and 55 | WHY: Articles 54 and 55 concern the election of the President. The GST Council is Article 279A, State representation is the Fourth Schedule and citizenship is Article 11.
- Q: Which one of the following was added to the category of provisions requiring State ratification by the Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016? => ANSWER: The GST Council under Article 279A | WHY: The 101st Amendment Act, 2016 added Article 279A relating to the GST Council to the federal provisions requiring State ratification.
- Q: The majority required for the impeachment of the President of India is => ANSWER: Two-thirds of the total membership of the House concerned | WHY: Under the Article 61 type of majority, impeachment of the President requires two-thirds of the total membership of the House concerned.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about the constitutional amendment procedure? => ANSWER: Ratification by the States is required for every amendment | WHY: State ratification is required only for a limited group of federal provisions, not for every amendment.
- Q: Which of the following statements about State ratification is incorrect? => ANSWER: Ratification by every State is mandatory. | WHY: Option C is incorrect: ratification needs only half the States, by simple majority in each, not all of them. The first amendment that needed it was the 3rd (1954), on Entry 33 of the Concurrent List.
## SEC 2:18:3 | Bill types compared
- Q: Which one of the following correctly describes the President's role after a constitutional amendment bill has been duly passed? => ANSWER: The President must give assent and can neither withhold nor return the bill. | WHY: The President's assent to a duly passed constitutional amendment bill is obligatory. The President can neither withhold assent nor return the bill for reconsideration; this position was made mandatory by the 24th Amendment Act, 1971.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about presidential action on Bills? => ANSWER: The President may return a Money Bill once for reconsideration | WHY: A Money Bill can be assented to or withheld but never returned; only an ordinary Bill can be returned once.
- Q: Which of the following is NOT correct about the passage of Bills? => ANSWER: A Financial Bill (I) can only be recommended on, not rejected or amended, by the Rajya Sabha | WHY: A Financial Bill (I) originates only in the Lok Sabha, but the Rajya Sabha has full power to reject or amend it.
