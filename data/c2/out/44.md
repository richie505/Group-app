# ROW 2:45 | Tribunals - Articles 323A and 323B; administrative and specialised tribunals; tribunal reforms
## SEC 2:45:0 | Basics
### Constitutional place
- ★ Tribunals are in **Part XIV-A** (Articles **323-A** and **323-B**), not Part XI
- Inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, following the **Swaran Singh Committee**
- **75th Amendment** added the rent and tenancy clause
- **Article 323-A** — administrative tribunals; **Article 323-B** — tribunals for other matters
| Part | Subject |
|---|---|
| **IX-A** | Municipalities |
| **IX-B** | Co-operatives |
| **X** | Scheduled and Tribal Areas |
| **XI** | Union-State relations |
| **XIV** | Services |
| **XIV-A** | Tribunals |
- **Article 32** — Supreme Court judicial review; **Articles 226/227** — High Court review
### Nature and working
- Tribunals are **quasi-judicial** bodies, set up to reduce pendency in courts and bring **domain expertise**
- Chairpersons get the status of a **High Court judge**
- **CAT** procedure: not bound by the **Code of Civil Procedure**; follows **natural justice**; nominal fees; parties may appear **in person**
- **NGT** also not bound by the CPC, 1908, because it is guided by **natural justice**
- **Joint administrative tribunals** can be set up for **two or more States**
- **R. Gandhi (2010)** — members performing judicial functions must be **independent of the executive**
- **L. Chandra Kumar** struck down the exclusion clauses: tribunal decisions go first to a **High Court Division Bench**, then the Supreme Court

## SEC 2:45:1 | 323-A vs 323-B
- **Article 323A** — recruitment and service conditions of public servants; **Parliament alone** can legislate
- **Article 323B** — both **Parliament and State Legislatures** can act; subjects include **land reforms**
- **CAT** has original jurisdiction over service matters of Union civil servants; appeals go first to a **High Court Division Bench**, not directly to the Supreme Court
- **TDSAT** — telecom, broadcasting and airport tariff disputes; its cyber appellate orders lie before the **High Court**

## SEC 2:45:2 | Central Administrative Tribunal
### CAT
- Legal basis: **Article 323A** + **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985**; statutory, quasi-judicial body
- Set up in **1985** under PM **Rajiv Gandhi**
- **Judicial Members** — qualified as **High Court judges**; **Administrative Members** — served as **Additional Secretary** or above
- Main Bench at **New Delhi**, **18** benches, **69** members (**35 Judicial** incl. Chairperson, **34 Administrative**)
### State tribunals
- **State Administrative Tribunals** — set up by the Centre **at the request of the State Government**
- **32nd Amendment, 1973** — Administrative Tribunal for **Andhra Pradesh** under **Article 371D**

## SEC 2:45:4 | Case law
### Merits and limits of tribunals
- Merits: **expertise, speed, lower cost**; follow natural justice, not the full **Civil Procedure Code**
- **Article 323B** covers **elections** and **foodstuffs**; **323A** covers service matters (one tribunal for the Centre and each State)
- **CAT** covers Central civil servants, AIS officers, civilian defence employees, not **private-sector employees**
- Tribunals **supplement but do not replace** High Courts; they remain under High Court **judicial review**

# ROW 2:46 | Federal structure of India - features of federalism and unitary bias; Seventh Schedule (three lists), residuary powers
## SEC 2:46:0 | Federal vs unitary features
### Federal and unitary systems
- **Federalism** — Union and States share power, each **supreme in its own sphere** under a **written Constitution**, with an **independent umpire**
- **Independent judiciary** as umpire in Union-State disputes — an **essential feature of federalism**
- **Judicial review** reinforces the **federal** principle (not a unitary element)
- **Unitary** government: no constitutional division of powers; local units derive authority from the **Centre**
- Unitary merits: **uniform laws**, **quick decisions**, strong crisis response; regional diversity through autonomous governments is a **federal** trait
- **United Kingdom** — unitary; **Parliament** holds all law-making power
- **Sri Lanka** — **unitary** country (not federal)
### India's character
- ★ The Union of India is an **indissoluble union of States**
- India — **federal in structure with unitary features**; a **Union of States**
- **K.C. Wheare** — "a **unitary state with subsidiary federal features**"
- Supreme Court — "**fundamentally federal** with pronounced unitary elements"
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — federal character is a **basic feature**
- **Single citizenship** — unitary feature; **dual polity** and **bicameralism** — federal features
- India: **single citizenship** and a **single integrated judiciary**
- During an emergency the system can become unitary **without a formal amendment**
### Provisions
- **Article 249** — Parliament legislates on a State List subject after a **Rajya Sabha** resolution (two-thirds of members **present and voting**) in the national interest; Lok Sabha takes no part
- President's Rule beyond **one year** — needs a **National Emergency** in operation + **Election Commission** certification
- Emergency provisions — **Part XVIII** (Articles **352–360**); Part XX amendment; Part XVI reservations

## SEC 2:46:2 | Union of States
### Nature of the Union
- **Confederation** — sovereign States that retain the **right to secede**; federation — constitutional division of powers
- **USA** formed by **integration of States**; India moved from a **unitary colonial** system to a federal one (centripetal bias)
- Centralising design due to **Partition** and integration of **14 provinces** and **500+ princely States**
- **Coming-together** federation — **Switzerland**; **holding-together** — India, Spain, Belgium
- India — **indestructible Union of destructible States**; US — indestructible Union of indestructible States
- Constitution calls India a **Union of States** (not "Federation of States")
- **Ambedkar**: "Union" means India is **not the result of an agreement** among States, and States **cannot secede**
- **State of West Bengal v Union of India (1962)** — Constitution **not truly federal**, States **not sovereign**; also on Parliament acquiring State property
- Constitution is **neither wholly rigid** nor wholly flexible
### Articles 2–4
- **Article 2** — admission or establishment of **new States** not part of India
- Laws under **Articles 3 and 4** altering the First and Fourth Schedules are **not amendments under Article 368**
### Unitary features
- **All India Services** — recruited through the **UPSC**; serve Union and States under central control
- **CAG** audits Union and State accounts — unitary feature (also Governors' central appointment, AIS, emergency powers)
### Special provisions
- **Article 370** (former) — J&K's separate **constitution and flag**; made inoperative **August 2019**
- Indian States generally have **no separate constitutions** (former J&K the exception); each **US State** has its own

### Other
- **BNS, BNSS, BSA** came into force on **1 July 2024**, replacing IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act
- **Stock exchanges and futures markets** — **Union List** (with banking, foreign affairs, inter-State migration)
- Judicial review includes examining the constitutionality of **executive orders**
