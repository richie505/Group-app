# ROW 5:86 | Loss of biodiversity - causes, invasive alien species, IUCN Red List categories
## SEC 5:86:0 | Causes: two frameworks
### Frameworks
- **Evil Quartet** — habitat loss & fragmentation, over-exploitation, invasive alien species, **co-extinctions**
- **HIPPO** — popularised by **E. O. Wilson**: Habitat loss, Invasive species, Pollution, human Population, Over-harvesting
  - co-extinction belongs to the Evil Quartet, not HIPPO
- Recognised threats: **global warming**, **habitat fragmentation**, **invasive alien species** (not vegetarianism or protected-area expansion)
- **Habitat fragmentation** hits **migratory animals** and **wide-ranging mammals and birds** hardest (disrupts movement, separates populations)
- **Over-exploitation** extinctions — **Steller's sea cow**, **passenger pigeon**
- **One Health** approach — for **zoonotic spillover** from habitat disruption & wildlife trade; links health of humans, animals, ecosystems
### Indicators & species terms
- **Living Planet Index** (WWF) — average change in **vertebrate populations since 1970**
- **Flagship species** — charismatic species to rally public support (tiger, Asiatic lion)
- **Keystone species** — ecological effect far greater than its population size suggests (tiger as apex predator, vultures as scavengers)
- **Umbrella species** — protecting it protects many others sharing its habitat (tiger)
### Invasive species studies
- **Invasive Species Specialist Group (ISSG)** — under the IUCN **Species Survival Commission**
- Ecological damage per invasive species higher in the **Global South**; removal alone does not necessarily restore landscapes; blanket eradication debated

## SEC 5:86:1 | Deforestation → extinction
- **Co-extinction** — loss of one species causes decline/loss of dependent species (obligate pollinator, host-dependent parasite)
- Primary drivers of deforestation & habitat loss — **agricultural expansion** and **urban development**
- **Edge effect** — patch boundaries get more light, wind, heat, predators, weeds; as fragments shrink, **interior species** decline

## SEC 5:86:2 | Extinction facts
- **Sixth mass extinction** — the present, **human-driven** wave of extinctions
- **Background extinction** — slow natural loss at a low steady rate; rapid loss of a large share of species = **mass extinction**
- **Climate change** affects migration: rising temperatures **shift species ranges**, raise extinction risk in hotspots, help alien plants reach the Arctic
- Invasive species — about **40%** of known animal extinctions since the **17th century**; threaten about **1 in 10** Red List species
- **Cheetah** — declared extinct in India in **1952**; only large mammal lost in India since Independence
- Recorded extinct from India (ZSI) — **cheetah**, **pink-headed duck**, **mountain quail** (not the passenger pigeon)

## SEC 5:86:4 | IUCN categories (high → low risk)
### Categories
- **Extinct** — only when no reasonable doubt the last individual has died, after exhaustive surveys (not automatic after ten years unrecorded)
- **Extinct in the Wild** — survives only in **captivity or cultivation**, outside its natural range
- **CR** — **Critically Endangered**: extremely high risk of extinction in the wild (APPSC 2023, 2025); no "Critically Extinct" category
- **Threatened** = **CR, EN and VU**
- **NT** — **Near Threatened** (not "Not Threatened"); **DD** — Data Deficient; **NE** — Not Evaluated; **VU** — Vulnerable
- **Red Data Book** (Red List) — published by **IUCN**; **CITES** regulates wildlife trade

## SEC 5:86:5 | IUCN & Red List
### Causes & risk
- High-risk traits — **large body size**, **small population**, **low reproductive rate**, **narrow specialised niche**, endemism
- Primary cause of biodiversity loss — **habitat destruction** (deforestation, destruction of aquatic habitats)
- Ecological causes — modern agriculture, loss of genetic variation, habitat destruction ("loss of appetite" is not one)
- Biodiversity loss disrupts **pollination**, **nutrient cycling**, water & soil services → reduces **food security** of resource-dependent communities
### Invasive species in India
- Invasive alien species tests — **non-native origin**, **uncontrolled spread**, **measurable harm** (not commercial usefulness)
- Invasive fish of concern — African catfish, Nile tilapia, red-bellied piranha, **alligator gar**
- **Plant Quarantine (Regulation of Import into India) Order** — **2003**; pest risk analysis & phytosanitary checks
- **DPPQS** — under the **Agriculture Ministry** (not MoEFCC); fumigates uncertified imports with **methyl bromide**
- **Destructive Insects and Pests Act** — **1914**
- **Tamil Nadu** — cleared **Prosopis** from **517 villages** in **32 districts**; recovery depends partly on landscape's prior condition
### IUCN & assessments
- **IUCN** — union of governments and NGOs, not a UN agency; HQ **Gland**, Switzerland
- **IPBES** — about **25%** of assessed species threatened; habitat destruction main driver; publishes the **Global Assessment on Biodiversity** (not WWF/ZSL)
- **Red List** — started **1964**; "**Barometer of Life**"; measures extinction risk (APPSC 2025: IUCN's endangered list = Red List)
- **IUCN Green Status** — measures species **recovery**
- IUCN criteria **A–E** — declining population, geographic range, small population size, very small or restricted population, quantitative extinction-risk analysis (not economic value in trade)
- **India's Red List Roadmap** (National Biodiversity Authority) — assess **70,000** species by **2027**
- **Phoenix Species Project** (Aug 2026, **$200 million**, Re:wild & Bezos Earth Fund) — recover **100** most threatened species
- 2025 additions in India — **709** animal species (**483** new to science), **353** plant taxa
### Species
- **Gharial** — a **Project Crocodile** species
- **Irrawaddy dolphin** — **Chilika Lake** (Odisha) and Godavari estuary (AP)
- **Pygmy hog** — world's smallest wild suid, only surviving **Porcula** species; alluvial grasslands of **Assam**; captive breeding raised numbers **32-fold**
- **Tiger** — **Endangered** (not CR); **Asiatic lion** — Endangered, improved from CR

# ROW 5:87 | Conservation - in-situ (protected areas, biosphere reserves) and ex-situ (zoos, gene banks, seed banks)
## SEC 5:87:0 | In-situ vs ex-situ
### Concepts
- **In-situ** — most effective: keeps species **interacting with their environment**; protects the **whole ecosystem**
- **Ex-situ** — conserves the species or its **genes outside the natural habitat**
- In-situ examples — **national park**, sanctuary, reserved forest, biosphere reserve, **sacred groves**
- Ex-situ examples — **botanical garden**, zoo, gene bank, **cryobank** (frozen seeds, sperm, tissue)
- **Captive breeding** — when habitat permanently lost; e.g. **Gangetic gharials** bred and released into rivers
### Places & facilities
- **Sundarbans BR** — most animal species among Indian BRs (**2,626**)
- **Citrus Gene Sanctuary** — **Nokrek BR**, Garo Hills, Meghalaya; in-situ conservation of **crop wild relatives**
- AP's only biosphere reserve (APSES) — **Seshachalam**; only tiger reserve — Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam
- **Millennium Seed Bank** — **Wakehurst**, UK; **Chang La** — high-altitude seed vault
- **Field gene banks** — living collections of **vegetatively propagated** crops and **recalcitrant-seed** crops (mango, coconut, banana)
- **Acharya Jagadish Chandra Bose Indian Botanic Garden** — **Shibpur, Howrah** (WB); Great Banyan; Botanical Survey of India
- **Jatayu Conservation Breeding Centre**, **Pinjore** — **vultures**; GIB bred at **Sam** and **Ramdevra**; red panda at **Padmaja Naidu Himalayan Zoological Park**
- **Supreme Court 2024** — **underground power lines** in Great Indian Bustard habitat

## SEC 5:87:1 | PA categories (WPA 1972)
### Categories under WPA
- Four PA categories — **National Park**, **Wildlife Sanctuary**, **Conservation Reserve**, **Community Reserve** (Biosphere Reserve is not one)
- **National Park** — **no rights or grazing**; grazing fully banned
- **Wildlife Sanctuary** — grazing may be **regulated by the Chief Wildlife Warden**
- **Community Reserve** — declared by the **State Government**; conserves fauna, flora and **traditional and cultural values**; land already NP, sanctuary or conservation reserve cannot become one
- **Biosphere Reserve** — settlements allowed in the **transition zone**
### Around PAs
- **OECM** — area **outside the formal PA network** with long-term in-situ conservation outcomes; recognised by results
- **Eco-sensitive Zone** — **shock absorber** / transition between high protection and regulated development
- **Regulated in ESZ** — tree felling, hotels & resorts, road widening; **prohibited** — commercial mining, major hydro projects; **permitted** — ongoing agriculture, organic farming, rainwater harvesting
- Projects affecting wildlife — clearance from **Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL)**
### Places & bodies
- ★ **Suklaphanta Wildlife Reserve** — **Nepal** (Kanchanpur district; 305 sq km; wildlife reserve 1976, National Park 2017)
- **Manas BR** — linked with **Manas Wildlife Sanctuary** (not Sundarbans NP)
- **Silent Valley NP** — **Palakkad, Kerala** (tropical evergreen); **Valley of Flowers** — Chamoli, Uttarakhand; **Keibul Lamjao** — Manipur
- **Papikonda NP** — **Papikondalu ranges** along the **Godavari**
- **Lloyd Botanical Garden** — **Darjeeling**; Ooty — Government Botanical Garden; Lucknow — National Botanical Garden (CSIR-NBRI)
- **Anamalai Tiger Reserve** — India's **first Hornbill Conservation Centre**
- **National Medicinal Plants Board** — **2000**, under **Ministry of AYUSH**
- **Sacred groves** — preserved by communities on **religious beliefs**; trees and wildlife venerated
- **Meristem culture** — **virus-free propagation** of plants

## SEC 5:87:2 | Biosphere reserve zones
- **Biosphere Reserve** — zoned into **core, buffer, transition**; National Parks clearly demarcated; sanctuary boundaries not strictly specified
- **Buffer zone** — surrounds the core; only **regulated activities**
- **Transition zone** — outer interface: **settlements, farming, tourism**
- Ex-situ **limitations** — space & finance needs, cannot save the whole ecosystem; its **values** — education, research, reintroduction
- **WDPA** — run by **UNEP-WCMC** (Cambridge) with IUCN; tracks **30x30** progress

## SEC 5:87:5 | ESZ & global targets
### Protected-area numbers
- India's PA network — **981** (2020) → **1,134** (2026); **5.32%** of India's area
- 2014 global coverage — **15.2%** of land, **8.4%** of coastal-marine area
- Ocean — nearly **10%** protected, only about **3%** fully/highly protected; **1.66%** of high seas

- Wildlife Sanctuaries — notified by the **State** under WPA 1972 (not UNESCO)
- **CZA** — statutory, provided for by the **1991** WPA amendment; only recognises zoos
- **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024** — **180 days** for IPR matters, **90 days** generally
