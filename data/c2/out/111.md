# ROW 5:80 | Sustainable development - meaning, nature, scope and components
## SEC 5:80:0 | Three pillars
- ★ **Poverty alleviation** — not an environmental-protection issue (that list: climate change, pollution, environmental degradation, resource depletion)

## SEC 5:80:1 | Meaning & scope
### Natural capital
- ★ **Natural capital** — renewable and non-renewable resources created by nature: air, water, land, minerals, forests
- ★ **Roads** — not natural capital (man-made)
### Scope of sustainable development
- Meets **present needs** with **equity within and across generations**, plus inclusion
- Rejects depletion of natural capital and the opposition of environment to development
- Three classic pillars: **economic development**, **social equity**, **environmental protection** (**military security** is not one)
- **Triple bottom line** — **People, Planet, Profit** (social, environmental, economic)
- **Our Common Future** (Brundtland Report) — **1987**
  - Order: Stockholm Declaration **1972** → Our Common Future **1987** → Rio Declaration **1992** → Johannesburg Declaration **2002**
### National Environment Policy, 2006 and NGT
- **NEP 2006** — conservation and livelihood security are **linked objectives**
- **Wetlands (Conservation & Management) Rules, 2010** — from an Expert Panel under the **NEP 2006**
- **NGT** (HQ New Delhi) — benches at **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai**; no bench at **Hyderabad**

## SEC 5:80:2 | Principles
- **Precautionary principle** — lack of full scientific certainty is **no reason to postpone** cost-effective measures against environmental harm
- **CBDR** (Common but Differentiated Responsibilities) — shared responsibility, **developed nations carry greater duties** (capacity, historical contribution)
- **NGT** must apply: **sustainable development**, **precautionary** principle, **polluter-pays** principle

# ROW 5:83 | International treaties on chemicals and waste - Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm POPs, Minamata; Montreal Protocol and Kigali Amendment
## SEC 5:83:0 | Ozone layer & CFCs
### Ozone layer
- Ozone concentrated in the **stratosphere**, about **10–50 km**; absorbs harmful UV
- ★ Stratospheric ozone naturally regulated by **nitrogen dioxide**
- ★ **Dobson Unit (DU)** — measures **thickness / concentration of the ozone layer** (column from base to top of atmosphere)
- ★ **1 DU** = **0.01 mm** thickness at STP
### Ozone hole
- **Ozone hole** — total column ozone below **220 DU**
- Antarctic thinning greatest in **Southern Hemisphere spring** (about **August–November**)
- **Polar stratospheric clouds** — in extreme cold, convert stable chlorine into **active forms** on their surfaces; sunlight then lets them destroy ozone
- **CFCs** — main cause of depletion; release **chlorine** under UV, which catalytically breaks ozone (CO₂ and methane are GHGs, not main depleters)
- **ODS (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000** — under the **Environment (Protection) Act**

## SEC 5:83:1 | ODS & uses
- **HFCs** — **non-ODS** substitutes with high GWP; **CFCs** cause the ozone hole
- **CFCs** — cause both **global warming** and **ozone depletion** (from their chemistry); used in refrigerators, ACs, aerosol propellants

- **Soda-acid fire extinguisher** — CO₂ from **NaHCO₃** + **H₂SO₄**

# ROW 5:84 | Biodiversity - levels, values, measurement; India as a megadiverse country
## SEC 5:84:0 | Levels & measures
### Levels
- **Genetic diversity** — variation **within a species** (variety of ecosystems = ecosystem diversity)
- **Species diversity** — variety of species in an area; **richness** plus **abundance**
- **Species richness** — number of species; **evenness** — their relative abundance (low evenness: a few species dominate)
- **Ecosystem diversity** — one ecosystem **cannot** replace another's functions
- **Alpha** — within one area or community; **beta** — species turnover between ecosystems along a gradient; **gamma** — regional total
### Patterns and value
- Tropical richness — long evolutionary time **without glaciation**, stable climate, solar energy, productivity (not frequent glaciation)
- Most significant aspect of biodiversity — **maintenance of the ecosystem** (timber, ornamentals, ecotourism are only uses)
### Crops
- ★ **Oryza sativa** — **rice**
- **Hindustan (Indo-Burma) centre** of origin — **rice, sugarcane, mango, pigeon pea, brinjal, cucumber, black pepper**; many pulses and citrus

- Rivet popper — losing **keystone species** may collapse an ecosystem
- **Himalayas** — high richness as a **meeting point of different biogeographical zones**

## SEC 5:84:2 | Patterns & values
### Patterns
- Species-diversity indices — **Shannon-Wiener** and **Simpson**
- Ant survey — about **10** species near the North Pole, up to **2,000** at the equator
- Richest ecosystems — **tropical rainforests** (land), **coral reefs** (sea)
- Most **butterfly** species in the **tropics** — they cannot tolerate **low temperatures**
### Values
- **Productive use value** — timber, fibre, medicines, genetic material **sold commercially**
- **Consumptive use value** — fuelwood, fodder, wild food used locally, not sold
- **Option value** — possible future use (undiscovered medicines, crop genes)
- **Dasgupta Review** (2021) — by **Partha Dasgupta** for the **UK Treasury**; nature as an asset; GDP ignores nature's depreciation
- **Bioprospecting** — search in nature for valuable compounds and genes; **biopiracy** — patenting them or traditional knowledge without consent or compensation

## SEC 5:84:3 | India's share & ranks
### Concepts
- **CBD definition** — variability among living organisms from all ecosystems and the **ecological complexes** of which they are part
- Forest A (2 tigers, 6 deer, 7 rabbits) vs Forest B (1, 6, 8) — **same richness**, A has **greater evenness**
- Over **70%** of recorded species are animals; **insects** the richest group; plants about **22%**
- **TEEB** — from a **G8+5** initiative, **2007**; hosted by **UNEP**
### Megadiversity
- **17 megadiverse countries** (36 = number of hotspots)
- Criteria (Conservation International, **1998**) — at least **5,000 endemic plants** and a coastline bordering marine ecosystems
### India
- **2.4%** of land, **7–8%** of recorded species, **18%** of population
- **4** of the **36** hotspots; about **7.5%** of global fauna; over **30%** of its animals endemic

# ROW 5:85 | Biodiversity hotspots in India; endemism; Biological Diversity Act 2002 and access-benefit sharing
## SEC 5:85:0 | Hotspot basics
### Concept and criteria
- **Norman Myers** — introduced hotspots, **1988**; adopted by **Conservation International**, **1989**
- Criteria — at least **1,500 endemic vascular plant** species and loss of at least **70%** of original habitat
- **Threat perception** (habitat loss) is essential; hotspots have lost about **86%** of their former habitat
- Once **15.7%** of land, now under about **2–2.5%**, yet about **50%** of endemic plants
- Temperate hotspots — **Mediterranean Basin**, California Floristic Province, North American Coastal Plain
- **Coldspots** — relatively **low diversity**, losing habitat rapidly; still important for rare species, ecosystem functions, genetic diversity
- **Global 200** priority ecoregions — **WWF**; KBA standard — IUCN-led
### Western Ghats
- Also **Sahyadri**; one of the world's **eight hottest** hotspots
- **Podocarpus (Nageia) wallichianus** — only endemic **gymnosperm** tree of the Western Ghats
- **Lion-tailed macaque** — upper canopy of Western Ghats tropical evergreen forests
- Draft **Western Ghats ESA** — six States (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu); legal force on notification under the **EP Act, 1986**
### Related
- **Biodiversity Heritage Sites** — notified by **State Governments**; traditional uses continue
- **ABS** — CBD **1992**, operationalised by the **Nagoya Protocol, 2010**; covers plant, animal, microbial resources

## SEC 5:85:1 | Hotspot count over time
- Hotspots reached **36** in **2016** with the **North American Coastal Plain**
