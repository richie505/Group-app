# ROW 4:38 | India - climate: mechanism of the monsoon, theories, seasons and their characteristics
## SEC 4:38:0 | Theories of the monsoon
### Theories
- **Thermal theory** — **Sir Edmund Halley**, **1686**; **land heats faster than sea** → summer low over land draws moist sea winds
- **Dynamic / ITCZ theory** — ITCZ shifts **north over the Ganga plain** (monsoon trough); **south-east trades** cross the equator and turn **south-westerly**
### Upper air and heating
- **Tropical easterly jet** — from the **South China Sea** to **East Africa**
- **Tibetan Plateau** (~5,000 m) — summer heat island; creates an **upper-air anticyclone**, strengthens the tropical easterly jet
- **Dust aerosols** over the Indo-Gangetic Plain — **heat the mid-troposphere**, help pull the monsoon inland
### Before and after the monsoon
- **Heat waves** in NW and central India — peak in **May–June** (hottest April–June)
- **Nautapa** — traditional **nine-day** peak of summer heat in north India, just before the monsoon
- **Withdrawal** — begins in the **north-west in October**, complete by **end of November**
- Retreating monsoon: the **westerly jet returns south of the Himalayas** (not north)
- **2026**: **Indian Ocean Dipole** watched as a possible offset to **El Nino**
## SEC 4:38:1 | Key terms
### Meaning
- **Monsoons** — **seasonal secondary winds** that **reverse direction** with the season; not primary planetary winds
- **'Monsoon'** — from Arabic **'Mausim'** = **season**
### Drivers
- **Mascarene High** — drives the **cross-equatorial flow** towards the subcontinent
- **Somali jet** + **Somalia Current** — intensify the monsoon winds
- **Ocean Mean Temperature (OMT)** — upper Indian Ocean averaged down to the **26 °C isotherm**; predicts monsoon rain better than SST
### Rainfall and forecasting
- **Break** in the monsoon — trough shifts to the **Himalayan foothills**; plains dry, Himalayan region heavy rain
- **LPA** of SW monsoon rainfall (1971–2020) — **868.6 mm** (about 87 cm)
- **Tamil Nadu coast** dry in SW monsoon — **leeward / rain-shadow** side; winds blow **parallel** to the coast
- **Bharat Forecast System** (Mission Mausam) — **6 km** resolution, **10-day** rainfall forecasts
## SEC 4:38:2 | SW monsoon calendar
- **Andaman and Nicobar Islands** — onset about **25 May**
- **Kerala** — normal onset **1 June**
- **2026** — monsoon reached Kerala on **4 June** (three days late)
- **Monsoon burst** — sudden onset of the SW monsoon in the **first week of June**
- **Autumn** — **September–October**; **November–December** is **pre-winter**
- **'October heat'** — **hot and humid** weather as the monsoon retreats
- **Bimodal rainfall** in the south (October–November retreat) — from **Bay of Bengal cyclones**
## SEC 4:38:3 | Two branches
- **Arabian Sea branch** strikes the **Western Ghats** first — very heavy rain at **Agumbe** and **Hulikal**
- West-coast rain from Arabian Sea winds over the Western Ghats — **orographic**
- **Rajasthan** dry — Arabian Sea branch runs **parallel to the Aravallis**, so no uplift
- **BShw** — **semi-arid steppe**: NW Gujarat, **western Rajasthan**, parts of Punjab
- **Meghalaya** very heavy rain — **Bay of Bengal branch** forced up the **funnel-shaped** hills (orographic uplift)
## SEC 4:38:4 | Local storms
- ★ **Mango shower** — pre-monsoon showers of **Karnataka and Kerala**; help **early ripening of mangoes**
  - also called **April rains** / **summer showers**; caused by **thunderstorms over the Bay of Bengal**
  - mainly **Kerala and coastal Karnataka**; also Andhra Pradesh in April–May
- **Kaal Baisakhi** (Nor'westers) — violent evening thunderstorms of **West Bengal** (also Odisha, Assam); APPSC GS 2025 key
  - Assam name **'Bardoli Chheerha'**; also called **'tea showers'**
- **Loo** and **Andhi** dust storms — **Rajasthan and Punjab**
- **ET** (polar / tundra) — highlands of **J&K, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand**; not coastal Andhra Pradesh
## SEC 4:38:5 | IMD seasons
- **IMD SW monsoon season** — **June–September**; **October–December** is post-monsoon
- SW monsoon season gives about **75 per cent** of India's annual rainfall
- **Six traditional seasons** — **Vasanta, Grishma, Varsha, Sharad, Hemanta, Shishira** (**Pravaha** is not one)
- **Tornadoes** — peak **March–May**, with the Nor'westers, mainly eastern States
- **Hailstorms** — mainly **pre-monsoon (March–May)** and **winter**; **rare in the monsoon**; not mainly October–December

## SEC 4:38:7 | Extremes & missions
### Controls and offsets
- **Relief** — mainly controls the distribution of monsoon rainfall (unequal, variable)
- **Positive Indian Ocean Dipole** — can offset El Niño; an unfavourable **MJO** phase stalls the monsoon
### Winds and branches
- **Loo** — hot, dry, dusty afternoon wind of the northern and NW plains; also felt in **Rayalaseema**
- **Andhi** — pre-monsoon dust storm from the **Thar** into Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, western UP (March–June)
- **Bay of Bengal branch** — turned **westward** along the Ganga plain by the **Himalayas**
- Monsoon lasts **under 75 days** in **western Rajasthan**; over **120 days** in the SW peninsula
### Wet and dry places
- **Mawsynram** (Khasi Hills, Meghalaya) — **wettest**, ~**1,187 cm**; followed by **Cherrapunji**
- **Cherrapunji** — also called **Sohra**
- Below-60 cm zone — **Thar, Kutch, Ladakh-Karakoram** (not the Khasi Hills)
- **Konkan coast** — **windward** side of the Western Ghats, heavy rain
- Rain-shadow areas — **interior Maharashtra**, **Rayalaseema**, **Ladakh**
- **Hagari valley** — **driest** part of AP, **under 51 cm**; Sileru-Sabari valleys wettest (~140–152 cm)
### NE monsoon and Western Disturbances
- **NE monsoon** — **October–December** rain on the **Coromandel** coast, Rayalaseema, Nellore, Prakasam
- **Western disturbances** (from the **Mediterranean**) — **10–15 cm** rain in NW India, help **rabi** crops, **snowfall** in J&K, HP, Uttarakhand
- **Tamil Nadu coast** winter rain — from the **NE monsoon**, not Western Disturbances
### Missions
- **National Monsoon Mission** — **MoES**, **2012**; via IITM Pune and IMD; Phase 2 from **2017**
- **Mission Mausam** — launched **14 January 2025** with **IMD Vision 2047**; implemented by IMD, NCMRWF, IITM
- **2026** — **43%** June deficit → monsoon contingency plans; IMD forecast about **90%** of LPA

# ROW 4:39 | India - rainfall distribution, variability, El Nino linkage, drought and flood-prone areas
## SEC 4:39:0 | Rainfall zones
- **Sileru–Sabari valleys** — highest rainfall in AP, about **140–152 cm**
- Rainfall zones: **very heavy** — west coast, north-east; **heavy** — Western Ghats, Meghalaya; **moderate** — most of the **Northern Plains** and **central India**
## SEC 4:39:1 | Gradient & variability
- Rainfall falls **east to west**: **Kolkata** > Patna > Prayagraj > Delhi > Bikaner
- Monsoon lasts **< 75 days** in **western Rajasthan** (late arrival, early exit); **> 120 days** in the **south-west peninsula**
- **Coefficient of variation** = **(standard deviation / mean) × 100**; higher CV = more variable rainfall
- **CV above 50%** — **western Rajasthan, Kutch, Ladakh**
- **CV below 25%** — west coast, Western Ghats, north-east, eastern peninsula
## SEC 4:39:2 | ENSO & drought types
- **El Niño** weakens the Indian monsoon; **La Niña** generally **strengthens** it
- **Hydrological drought** — falling **river flow** and **groundwater**
- **Agricultural drought** — **soil moisture**; **meteorological drought** — **rain deficit**
## SEC 4:39:3 | All-India LPA bands
| Category | % of LPA |
|---|---|
| **Deficient** | **below 90** |
| Below normal | 90–95 |
| Normal | 96–104 |
| Above normal | 105–110 |

## SEC 4:39:4 | Drought extent
- About **68 percent** of India's agricultural / cultivable land is **drought-prone**
- **Chronically drought-prone** — **under 750 mm** rain, about **33%** of India
- **Drought-prone** — **750–1125 mm**, about **35%**
## SEC 4:39:5 | Drought management
- **Manual for Drought Management, 2016** (revised **2020**) — indicators: **rainfall deficit, vegetation, soil moisture, water**
- **Disaster Management Act** — **2005** (not 2006); national-level legislation
## SEC 4:39:6 | Drought & flood regions
- About **12 percent** of India's land is prone to **floods and river erosion**
## SEC 4:39:7 | Exam corner
- IMD state/district **'normal'** — **−19% to +19%**; excess **+20% to +59%**; large excess **+60%** or more
- Chronic drought in **Rayalaseema** and southern **Prakasam** — **NE monsoon failure** + groundwater depletion
- **Brahmaputra** — **'sorrow of Assam'** (not Bihar)
- **AP** average annual rainfall about **966 mm** — **two-thirds** from the SW monsoon, one-third from the NE monsoon
## SEC 4:39:9 | Rainfall zones, gradient, variability, drought and floods
- ★ Monsoon **duration decreases** from **southern to northern India** (south gets rain earlier and longer)
- ★ Annual rainfall in the **northern plains** **decreases east to west** (humidity of winds falls)
## SEC 4:39:10 | Drought extent; data — IMD categories, LPA bands, deficient years, extents

# ROW 4:40 | India - natural vegetation types and their distribution; forest cover (India State of Forest Report)
## SEC 4:40:0 | Tropical forest types
### Biodiversity and biomass
- ★ **Tropical moist / rain forests** — **greatest diversity** of plant and animal species; **maximum biodiversity**
- ★ **Tropical evergreen forests** — **maximum plant diversity**; need **> 200 cm** rain, 15–30 °C; about **7%** of the earth's land
- ★ **Tropical rain forest** — **maximum plant biomass**
- ★ **Tropical forests** grow **near the equator** (10°N–10°S); no dry season, so leaves are not shed all together
- ★ Tropical rain forest — leaf litter **decomposes fastest**; **epiphytes** and climbers reach the canopy
- ★ **Tropical deciduous forests** — most extensive in **Asia**
### Rainfall and share of forest area
| Forest type | Rainfall | Share of India's forest area |
|---|---|---|
| Tropical evergreen | **> 200 cm** | wet evergreen ~**8%** |
| Tropical moist deciduous | **100–200 cm** | ~**37%** |
| Tropical dry deciduous | **70–100 cm** | ~**28.6%** |
| Tropical thorn | **< 70 cm** | |
- ★ **Tropical moist deciduous** — **largest area** in India; central India, eastern slopes of Western Ghats, Himalayan foothills, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand
- Descending share: **moist deciduous > dry deciduous > wet evergreen**
- ★ **Teak** and **Sal** — products of the **tropical deciduous** forests
- **Moist deciduous** — sub-Himalayan **Bhabar and Terai**
### Species

|---|---|

- ★ **Sita Nadi** area — mainly **Sal** (also teak, bamboo); sanctuary 553.36 sq km
- **Khair** — dry/arid areas; yields **katha**

### Thorn forests
- **Thorn forests** — **open, stunted**, long roots, **thorns/spines**; **western** Andhra Pradesh (not eastern), Rajasthan, Kutch
- Deodar, pine, fir — **coniferous**, not thorn forests
### Fire, law and management
- **Dry deciduous** — **most vulnerable to forest fires** (seasonal dryness, dry biomass)
- **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** — diversion of forest land needs **Central Government** approval
- **CAMPA** — manages **compensatory afforestation** funds
- **High forest + selection system** — seed-raised, long-rotation timber; **uneven-aged**, mature trees removed singly or in groups
### Places
- **Banni** grassland — **Kutch, Gujarat**
- **Nallamala** — biggest forest tract of AP; AP's forests mainly **dry deciduous**

- **Black-necked crane** — breeds in **Ladakh**; **Siberian crane** wintered at Keoladeo

## SEC 4:41:0 | WPA 1972 categories
### Protected-area categories
- **National Parks** give the **highest protection**; sanctuaries allow some regulated activities and rights
- **Conservation Reserve** (**Section 36A**) — declared by the **State Government** on **government-owned land** adjoining / linking PAs, after consulting local people
  - local rights **unaffected**; **panchayats** sit on management committees
- **Community Reserve** — declared by the State on **private or community land** voluntarily offered; rights unaffected
  - land cannot already be inside a NP, WLS or Conservation Reserve
