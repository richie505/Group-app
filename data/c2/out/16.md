# ROW 1:124 | Folk culture of Andhra - Burrakatha, Harikatha, Veedhi Natakam, Oggu Katha, Tolu Bommalata
## SEC 1:124:0 | Who's who
### Pitamahas & founders
| Title / role | Person |
|---|---|
| Harikatha Pitamaha | **Adibhatla Narayana Dasu** (1864-1945), born at **Ajjada**, Vizianagaram district |
| Pitamaha of Burrakatha | **Sheikh Nazar** |
| Founder of Kuchipudi | **Siddhendra Yogi** |
| First published Telugu Yakshaganam (Sugreeva Vijayam) | **Kandukuri Rudra Kavi** |
- **Na Yeruka** — autobiography of **Adibhatla Narayana Dasu**; his kavyas include **Jagajyothi** and **Navarasa Tarangini**
### Yakshaganam
- **Koravanji** — precursor of the Telugu Yakshagana
- Andhra Yakshaganam — performed in **Telugu**, elaborate costumes, **epic / Puranic** stories
### Patrons & theatre
- **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (last Qutb Shahi ruler) — gifted the village of **Kuchipudi** to its performers
- Surabhi theatre: **Vanarasa Govinda Rao** came from a **leather-puppet** family (not a Bhagavata Mela family)
- **Mabhoomi** (Praja Natya Mandali hit play) — **Sunkara Satyanarayana** and **Vasireddy Bhaskara Rao**; Mandali used folk plays to spread socialist ideals
### Folk forms & society
- Burrakatha narrators carried news, reform and nationalist messages to **unlettered villagers**
- Andhra Movement: folk performance carried a distinct **Telugu cultural consciousness** to the villages

## SEC 1:124:1 | Folk forms through the ages
- Vijayanagara period: **Tholu Bommalata** popular in **rural Andhra**; Yakshaganam entertained royal courts and elites
- Annual **Telugu Mahotsavam** announced at the **Undavalli Caves** (27 Jul 2026) by Minister **Kandula Durgesh**

## SEC 1:124:2 | Harikatha vs Burrakatha
- **Burrakatha** — trio: main narrator in the middle + **two Vantalu** who respond and comment
- **Harikatha** — **solo** performance with **Chidatalu**
- Burrakatha instruments — **Gummata, Burra, Tambura, Ukki**; chorus phrase **'Thandana Taana'**
- **Guntur** — birthplace of Burrakatha

## SEC 1:124:3 | Tolu Bommalata
- Tolu Bommalata — **leather shadow-puppetry**; figures with movable heads, sung verses and dialogue, themes from epics and Puranas
- Figures: thin, **translucent leather**, painted on **both sides**; moved behind a white screen lit by an **oil lamp**

| Palnati Veeracharitra | **Srinatha** |

## SEC 1:124:5 | Kuchipudi origin
- Named after **Kuchipudi village**, **Krishna district**
- Founded by **Siddhendra Yogi**; developed from the **Bhagavata Mela** dance-drama tradition

## SEC 1:124:6 | Works & other names
- **Sheikh Nazar** — born at **Pannekallu**, Guntur; works **Palnati Yuddham**, **Bengal Karuvu**
- **Sunkara Satyanarayana** — Raithu Vijayam, Alluri Sitaramaraju, Veeresalingam

## SEC 1:124:7 | Exam corner

## SEC 1:124:8 | Folk forms through the ages — periods, other names and FIRSTs

## SEC 1:125:1 | Kuchipudi vs Bharatanatyam

# ROW 1:126 | Battle of Talikota (1565), the Aravidu dynasty and the legacy of Vijayanagara
## SEC 1:126:0 | Battle of Talikota
- **Sadasiva Raya** — nominal **Tuluva** king; **Aliya Rama Raya** his regent
- Vijayanagara fought a **confederacy of the Deccan Sultanates** — **Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar**
  - not the Bahmani kingdom (already broken up), not the Mughals or Gajapatis
- **Berar** (**Imad Shahi** dynasty) — stayed out of the alliance
- **Jagadeva Rao** — rebel from **Golconda** sheltered by Rama Raya; a cause of the coalition
- **Sewell** called the sacked city a **'graveyard'** — a view that may be exaggerated

## SEC 1:126:1 | The confederacy
- 1546-47: Rama Raya backed **Ahmadnagar** against Bijapur and Bidar and took the **Raichur doab**
- **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** of Golconda — key organiser of the coalition
### Sultanate → ruler at Talikota
| Sultanate | Ruler |
|---|---|
| Bijapur | **Ali Adil Shah** |
| Ahmadnagar | **Hussain Nizam Shah** |
| Golconda | **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** |
| Bidar | **Ali Barid Shah** |

## SEC 1:126:2 | Causes → results
- Marriage alliances: **Chand Bibi** — **Ali Adil Shah**; **Bibi Jamal** — Ibrahim Qutb Shah
- Hampi looted for about **six months**; damage severe but not total
- Sadasiva and Tirumala fled to **Penukonda**; **Chandragiri** became capital later, under **Venkata II**

## SEC 1:126:3 | Aravidu rulers (1570-1680)
- Aravidu — **fourth** dynasty of Vijayanagara; ruled **1570-1680** with **seven** rulers
- **Tirumala Raya** — founder; crowned at **Penukonda**, **1570**
- **Battle of Toppur** (**1616**) — the 'second Talikota'; **Jagga Raya** killed there
- **Sriranga III** — last Aravidu ruler; defeated at **Vandavasi** (**1642**) by **Mir Jumla** of Golconda

## SEC 1:126:4 | Venkatapati Raya II
- **Vilapakam inscription** (1601) — genealogy of the **Tuluva and Aravidu** dynasties
- Venkatapati Raya II permitted **Dutch** trade at **Pulicat**; allowed **Jesuit churches** at **Nellore** and **Chandragiri**
- Called the **'Second Andhra Bhoja'** (and 'Second Krishnadevaraya')
- **Shaivarka Mani Dipika** — **Appayya Dikshita**; Vasucharitra — **Ramaraja Bhushanudu**

## SEC 1:126:6 | Grant of Madras & Nayakas
- **1639**: **Damarla Venkatadri Nayaka** granted **Madras** to **Francis Day** (Machilipatnam council) — reign of **Venkatapati Raya III**
- **Toppur** (1616) won by **Velugoti Yachama Nayaka** and **Raghunatha of Tanjore**
### Nayaka kingdoms
| Kingdom | Founder / ruler |
|---|---|
| Tanjore | **Sevappa Nayaka** |
| Madurai | **Vishwanatha Nayaka** (granted it after capturing rebel father **Nagama Nayaka**) |
| Gingee | **Krishnappa Nayaka** |
| Mysore | **Raja Wodeyar** |
| Ikkeri (Keladi), c. 1500 | **Chowdappa Nayaka** |
- Madurai made independent c. **1565** by Vishwanatha's son **Krishnappa Nayaka**
- **Tirumala Nayaka** (Madurai 1623-1659) — built the **Tirumala Rayala Mahal**

## SEC 1:126:7 | Legacy & rediscovery
- **Amara-nayakas** — became hereditary **Palegars**, forerunners of the zamindars
- **Mir Jumla** of Golconda — melted temple idols into cannon at **Gandikota**, **1657**
- **Colin Mackenzie** — surveyed and mapped **Hampi** ruins in **1800**; later first Surveyor General of India
- **Caesar Frederick** — **Venetian** merchant; eyewitness account of the ruined capital, **1567** (not founder of the Aravidu line; Tirumala was)

## SEC 1:126:8 | Exam corner
- Aravidu dynasty named after **Araviti** (Aravidu) village, **Kurnool** district
- After Talikota the empire's centre shifted to the Telugu country (**Penukonda, Chandragiri**)

## SEC 1:126:9 | Talikota 1565 → date, names, place and sides
- ★ Talikota = **Rakshasa Tangadi** (**January 1565**) — **Vijayanagara** vs **Deccan Sultanates** (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar)
  - led to destruction of **Hampi** and decline of Vijayanagara
- ★ King at Talikota — **Sadashiva Raya** (nominal); **Aliya Rama Raya** regent
- ★ Capital after Talikota — **Penugonda**; Tirumala Raya founded the Aravidu dynasty there

## SEC 1:126:11 | Talikota → course and results
- ★ **Penukonda** — second capital of Vijayanagara after **1565** (Aravidu rulers)

## SEC 1:126:13 | Aravidu rulers → period and contribution — Rulers
- ★ **Battle of Talikota** — **1565**; Vijayanagara vs Bijapur, Bidar, Ahmadnagar, Golconda (**Berar** not included); Vijayanagara defeated

## SEC 1:126:14 | Aravidu rulers → period and contribution — Venkatapati Raya II (1585-1614)
- ★ **Venkatapati Raya II** — permitted **Jesuits** to build churches at **Nellore** and **Chandragiri**

## SEC 1:126:16 | Nayaka kingdoms → founder and contemporary ruler
- ★ **Raja Wodeyar** founded Mysore during the reign of **Venkata II**
- ★ Aravidu sequence: **Tirumala** (brother of Rama Raya) dethroned Sadasiva Raya, **1570** → his eldest son **Sriranga I** → **Venkata II**, who made **Chandragiri** his capital

# ROW 1:127 | Mughal Empire - Babur: Panipat, Khanwa, Chanderi, Ghaghra; Tuzuk-i-Baburi
## SEC 1:127:0 | Babur's career
- ★ Babur's given name — **Zahir-ud-din Muhammad**; 'Babur' = tiger (title / nickname)
- Descent: **Timur** on father's side, **Chingiz Khan** on mother's side
- ★ Samarkand captured **1501** (held 8 months); after taking **Kabul** (**1504**) dropped 'Mirza' and took the title **'Padshah'**
- **Mansabdari** system — introduced under **Akbar**, not during Babur's four-year rule
- Mughals did **not interfere in the Deccan** in Babur's time; Babur contemporary of **Krishnadevaraya** and Golconda's **Sultan Quli**

## SEC 1:127:1 | Invited by
| Inviter | Who |
|---|---|
| **Daulat Khan Lodi** | Governor of Punjab |
| **Alam Khan** | Ibrahim Lodi's uncle |
| **Rana Sanga** | Mewar |

## SEC 1:127:2 | Panipat I vs Khanwa
- After Panipat: lavish sharing of the Lodi treasure — nickname **'Qalandar'**
- Khanwa: Rana Sanga's Rajput confederacy joined by **Mahmud Lodi** and **Hasan Khan Mewati**
- Before Khanwa: declared **jihad**, renounced wine, remitted **tamgha** for Muslims
- After Khanwa: title **'Ghazi'**
- Khanwa: trenches and barricades to counter Rana Sanga's **elephants**; artillery decisive

## SEC 1:127:3 | Key terms
- ★ **Shahrukh / Shahrukhi** — silver coin popularised by **Babur** (Timurid style); he also issued copper coins
- **Rumi method (araba)** — **carts** chained as a barrier for matchlockmen and guns (not elephants)
- Babur introduced the **charbagh** garden

## SEC 1:127:4 | Tuzuk-i-Baburi
- ★ **Tuzuk-i-Baburi** (Baburnama) — written in **Turkish** (**Chagatai Turki**); **Persian** was the Mughal court language
- ★ Hindu states named in the Baburnama — **Mewar** and **Vijayanagara**
- Babur praised **Krishnadevaraya** as the most powerful ruler in India
- Babur set up **no administrative system** (shallow roots, empty treasury)

## SEC 1:127:5 | Buildings & limits
- At death, dominions: **Kabul, Badakhshan** → Punjab, Delhi, Agra → borders of **Bihar**
- First Mughal garden — **Ram Bagh (Aram Bagh), Agra**, on the **charbagh** plan

## SEC 1:127:6 | Exam corner
- **First Battle of Panipat** — first large-scale use of **field artillery** in North Indian warfare
  - Babur defeated **Ibrahim Lodi**, the last Delhi Sultan
  - firearms were not wholly unknown in India before Babur

## SEC 1:127:8 | Panipat I (1526) → tactics, terms and FIRSTs
- ★ Babur's empire included **Kabul**, **Punjab** and modern **Uttar Pradesh** (not modern Rajasthan)
  - governed through jagirs and paraganas; **Badakhshan** given to **Humayun**
- ★ Babur's arrival led to the **establishment of the Timurid dynasty** in the region
  - **gunpowder** entered India before Babur (used to demolish fort walls); Babur credited with its use in **cannons and guns**
  - not the introduction of the **arch and dome**

## SEC 1:127:11 | Books, buildings and coins → Tuzuk-i-Baburi, gardens, mosques
- ★ Mughal rulers — **Chagatai Turks** (name from Genghis Khan's second son, ruler of Central Asia and Turkistan)
- ★ Babur wrote his memoirs in **Turki**, but Turki was **not** the official court language

# ROW 1:128 | Humayun and Sher Shah Suri - Sur administration, land revenue, sarais, currency, Grand Trunk Road
## SEC 1:128:0 | Humayun vs the Afghans
### Battles
| Battle | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Dourah (Devara)**, on the Gomati | **1532** | Humayun drove Mahmud Lodi from **Jaunpur** |
| **Chausa** | **1539** | Sher Khan beat Humayun; took the title **'Sher Shah'** |
| **Kannauj** = Battle of **Bilgram** | **1540** | Sher Shah beat Humayun and took the throne |
| Sirhind | | Humayun defeated **Sikandar Sur** |
- **Gujarat, 1535**: pressed by Humayun, **Bahadur Shah** let the Portuguese fortify **Diu**
- **Rao Jaitsi** of **Bikaner** — defeated Babur's son **Kamran Mirza**, **1534**
### Exile, return, death
- Retook **Kandahar** and **Kabul** with help of Safavid **Shah Tahmasp** of Persia
- Died **January 1556** — fell from the stairs of the **Sher Mandal** library, **Purana Qila**, Delhi
- **Humayun-nama** — **Gulbadan Begum** (his sister); Hamida Banu Begum — his wife, Akbar's mother

## SEC 1:128:1 | Sher Shah: rise & Surs
### Sher Shah
- Farid got the title **'Sher Khan'** from **Bahar Khan** of Bihar for slaying a tiger
- **Jaita and Kumpa** — generals of **Maldeo of Marwar** at the battle of **Samel** (1544)
- Died **1545** in a gunpowder explosion at the siege of **Kalinjar**
### Successors
- ★ **Islam Shah** (Jalal Khan, Sher Shah's second son) — successor, ruled **1545-53**
- ★ Islam Shah — divided troops into units of **200, 250 and 500**
- ★ **Hemu** = **Hem Chandra Vikramaditya** — army chief and Prime Minister to **Adil Shah Suri**
- **Panipat II** — **5 November 1556**; Hemu struck in the eye by an arrow and captured
- **Sur dynasty** — last **Afghan** dynasty to rule Delhi; its revenue, coinage and roads set the Mughal pattern
### Related
- After Panipat I, Babur occupied **Delhi and Agra**
- Akbar enthroned at **Kalanaur** after Humayun's death (fall from the stairs of the **Dinpanah** library)

## SEC 1:128:2 | Sur administration
- Baburnama — written in **Chaghatai Turkish**; Persian the Mughal court language
- Officers **frequently transferred** so they did not build local interests
### Officials
| Official | Role |
|---|---|
| Chief Shiqdar (shiqdar-i-shiqdaran), sarkar | **law and order** |
| Chief Munsif, sarkar | revenue / civil officer |
| **Fotedar**, pargana | **treasurer** |
| Karkuns | Hindi and Persian writers |
| **Amin** | **assessment of revenue** |
- Shiqdar, qanungo, patwari — also revenue / local officials
- **Dagh** — **branding of horses**; chehra / huliya — descriptive roll of soldiers
- Policing: **local headmen** responsible for detecting crime; also censors of public morals and an espionage network

## SEC 1:128:3 | Land revenue
- **Rumi method** — **Ottoman** origin (carts linked as a barrier); **Tulughma** — Uzbek
- Fort of **Raisen** taken from **Puran Mal**
- Land measured with the **Gaz-i-Sikandari** (sikandari gaz) and the **jarib**; classified **good, middling, bad**
- State share — **one-third of average produce**; model for **Todar Mal's Bandobast**
### Revenue terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Qabuliyat** | deed signed by the peasant **accepting the revenue demand** |
| Patta | title deed given to the cultivator |
| Jaribana | surveyor's fee |
| Muhasilana | collector's fee |

## SEC 1:128:4 | Coins, roads, sarais
| Coin | Metal |
|---|---|
| Rupiya | silver |
| **Dam** | **copper** |
| Mohur | gold |
- **Sadak-e-Azam** (Grand Trunk Road) — **Sonargaon in Bengal** to the **Indus**
- Sarais also served as **dak-chowkis** (news and mail relay); market towns grew around them

## SEC 1:128:5 | Monuments
- ★ Sher Shah's tomb — **Sasaram**, Rohtas, Bihar; brick, partly stone-veneered, **octagonal**, set in a **tank**; noble specimen of Afghan architecture
- ★ Sher Shah (born Farid Khan, son of an Afghan jagirdar of Sasaram) — died **1545**, buried at **Sasaram**
- **Qila-e-Kuhna Mosque** — built by Sher Shah inside **Purana Qila**
- Babur's **Kabuli Bagh Mosque** — **Panipat** (commemorates 1526); Ram Bagh at Agra
- **Balban's Tomb** (first true arch in India) — **Mehrauli**, Delhi
- Krishnadeva Raya let the Portuguese build at **Bhatkal**
### Sequence
- Chunar siege, Sher Khan submits **1532** → Chausa **1539** → Qila-e-Kuhna Mosque **1541** → death at Kalinjar **1545**

## SEC 1:128:6 | Exam corner
### Battles
| Battle | Year | Sides |
|---|---|---|
| Khanwa | **1527** | Babur vs **Rana Sanga** |
| **Ghaghra** | **1529** | Babur's **last battle**, vs Afghans under **Mahmud Lodi** |
| Chausa | **1539** | **Humayun** vs Sher Khan (not Jahangir; APPSC G2 2025) |
| Sirhind | **1555** | Humayun beat **Sikandar Sur** |

- Sher Shah opposed Humayun at **Kannauj**
- Sher Shah's share — **one-third**, not one-fourth
- **Sultan Quli Qutb Shah** — ruled Golconda **1518-1543**; contemporary of Babur, Humayun, Sher Shah

## SEC 1:128:8 | Humayun → accession, battles, exile, return, death
- ★ **Humayun Nama** — **Gulbadan Begum**
- ★ Humayun's battles in order: **Devara (Daurah)** 1532 → **Chausa** (26 June 1539) → **Kannauj** → **Sirhind**
- ★ **Sher Shah** died **22 May 1545** at the siege of **Kalinjar** (Rajput fort) — his **last campaign**; wounded by a gunpowder explosion
- ★ Rulers in order: **Raziyya** (1236-40) → **Alauddin Khalji** (1296-1316) → **Sher Shah** (1540-45) → **Akbar** (1556-1605)
- ★ Mughal emperor with no mausoleum in India — **Humayun** (per the key)
- ★ **Rao Jaitsi** of Bikaner defeated **Kamran** (Rao-Jaitsi-Ro-Chhanda); Humayun had sent his brother Kamran against him

## SEC 1:128:9 | Sher Shah and the Sur successors → rise, titles, conquests
- ★ **Jaita and Kumpa** — of **Marwar**; Maldeo Rathore's generals at **Sammel**, impressed Sher Shah
- ★ **"I would have lost the empire for a handful of millet"** — **Sher Shah**, on Rajput valour
- ★ Sher Khan's titles: **'Hazrat-e-Ala'** after defeating **Nusrat Shah** of Bengal → **'Sher Shah'** (Sultan) after **Chausa**, 26 June 1539 (khutba and coins in his name)
- ★ Farid educated at **Jaunpur** ('Siraj-i-Hind'), left home **1494**

## SEC 1:128:11 | Sher Shah's land revenue → measurement, terms, legacy
- ★ Land **classified into three categories** by produce; measured by a **uniform system**
