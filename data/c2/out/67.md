# ROW 3:58 | Public distribution, procurement and buffer-stocking; food subsidy and FCI reform
## SEC 3:58:0 | FCI & food management
- Food management = **procurement, storage, movement, public distribution, buffer stocks**; **export of foodgrains** is NOT an objective
- **FCI** set up under the **Food Corporations Act, 1964**, on the recommendation of the **Food Grains Prices Committee** (**L. K. Jha**, 1964)
- **Regulation of agricultural markets** is NOT an FCI mission; it falls under **State APMC laws**
- **Indian Grain Storage Management and Research Institute** — HQ **Hapur** (UP); field stations at Hyderabad and Ludhiana
- Stocks far above buffer norms → **higher carrying costs**

## SEC 3:58:1 | Procurement systems
- **FCI** — nodal agency for procuring **wheat and paddy** under price support, working with State agencies; does NOT regulate markets (State APMC laws)
- Wheat and paddy procurement — **open-ended** if produce meets specifications
- **PSS** procurement of pulses and oilseeds — **target-based**, capped at **25%** of production
- **OMSS (Domestic)** — FCI sells **wheat and rice from the Central Pool** in the open market at **predetermined prices** to moderate market prices
- Recommendation: procurement in surplus States (**Punjab, Haryana, Andhra Pradesh**) handed to those States so FCI can focus on the **eastern States**

## SEC 3:58:2 | Price terms
- **Economic cost of FCI** = MSP + bonus + **procurement incidentals** + **distribution costs** (not MSP and bonus alone)
- **Ceiling price** — **maximum** price that may be charged; protects **consumers** (a pre-sowing floor price for farmers is a different concept)
- **Central Issue Price (CIP)** — price at which the **Centre issues grain to States** (and beneficiaries) for **PDS**
  - Floor price announced before sowing = **MSP**; FPS retail price fixed by **States**; OMSS(D) sales at separate predetermined prices
- Food subsidy — two components: **FCI subsidy** (NFSA, other welfare schemes, strategic reserve) and **subsidy to DCP States**
- **Hidden subsidy** — cost not shown as subsidy, e.g. **FCI's carrying cost financed by borrowing** (NSSF loans) instead of budget releases
  - Amount shown in the budget (incl. budgeted reimbursement to DCP States) = **explicit** subsidy

## SEC 3:58:3 | PDS evolution
### Stages
- PDS rationing began in the **1940s** against the **Bengal famine**; revived in the food shortages of the **1960s**
- Sequence: **PDS → RPDS → TPDS → AAY**

| Scheme | Launch | Note |
|---|---|---|
| RPDS | **June 1992** | backward, hilly and drought-prone areas |
| TPDS | **June 1997** | separate BPL and APL entitlements |
| AAY | **25 December 2000** | poorest households, **35 kg** per family per month |

- **NFSA, 2013** — **legal** food entitlement to about **800 million** people (not 400 million)
- Extended **PMGKAY** — free foodgrains to **80 crore+** NFSA beneficiaries from **1 January 2023**, extended five years to **31 December 2028**
### Procurement and storage
- **Decentralised Procurement (DCP)** — designated States **procure, store and distribute** grain for TPDS themselves; Centre pays the difference between State's economic cost and **CIP**
- **CAP storage** — **Cover and Plinth**: grain on raised plinths under polythene covers
### Digital reforms
- **SARTHAK-PDS** — end-to-end tracking from procurement to **Fair Price Shop** with real-time stock monitoring (checks leakage)
  - Approved by the **CCEA** for **2026-2031**; subsumes **SMART-PDS** (April 2023 - March 2026)
  - End-to-End Computerisation of TPDS ran **October 2012 - March 2020**
- **ONORC** — migrant lifts full or partial entitlement at an **ePoS**-enabled FPS after **biometric/Aadhaar authentication**; family draws the balance at the original location

## SEC 3:58:4 | Food subsidy (₹ cr)
- **PDS** — guaranteed minimum grain at affordable prices to **low-income groups**; equitable distribution, control of price swings
- Procurement aims — assure MSP, supply grain to weaker sections under **PMGKAY**, check prices
- **FPS retail price** — fixed by **State Governments** (operational role in PDS)
- Market regulation is NOT an FCI function or challenge — **State APMC laws**
- Food subsidy, earlier partly off-budget via **NSSF loans** to FCI, brought back on budget in **Budget 2021-22**
- Anti-leakage measures: **e-PoS, ONORC, biometric authentication** (ending procurement is not one)
- **SMART-PDS** — part of **SARTHAK-PDS** (which also covers intra-state movement and FPS dealer margins)

## SEC 3:58:5 | Shanta Kumar HLC (2014-15)
- **Shanta Kumar HLC (2014-15)** — cut NFSA coverage from 67% to about **40%** (not 50%)
- Estimated PDS leakage at about **46%**
- **Grain Storage Plan** (cooperative sector) — PACS selected by **District Cooperative Development Committees**; implemented by **NCDC**

## SEC 3:58:6 | Digital PDS reforms
- **Online Storage Management (OSM)** of FCI — from **1 April 2022**; storage, movement and distribution under TPDS
- **Anna Darpan** — modernises **FCI's** supply chain (food/PDS side, not Agriculture Ministry)
- **DBT** of NFSA food subsidy — **Chandigarh, Puducherry**, urban **Dadra and Nagar Haveli**
- **IM-PDS** — from **April 2018**; national portability under **ONORC**
- ONORC mobile app — **Mera Ration**

## SEC 3:58:7 | Andhra Pradesh
- **ONORC** — draw ration **away from where the card was issued** (portability via ePoS, Aadhaar-seeded cards, biometric authentication)
- **AP e-PDS** — **100% Aadhaar seeding** of rice cards

- ★ Procurement at support prices ensures **stability in farm prices**, **remunerative prices** to farmers and **food stocks** for public distribution

## SEC 3:58:10 | Institutions → year, Act, headquarters and role

- First batch of SAARC officials trained in foodgrain storage at IGMRI Hapur — **17-26 November**

## SEC 3:58:12 | Price terms → meaning
- ★ **Economic cost** of foodgrains to FCI = **MSP + bonus** + **procurement incidentals** + **distribution cost**
  - Acquisition cost (MSP + bonus + procurement incidentals) + distribution cost
- Influences the food subsidy bill and India's competitiveness in international foodgrain markets

## SEC 3:58:13 | Data — procurement and food subsidy

## SEC 3:58:16 | PDS reform schemes → period and content

- ★ PDS foodgrain prices raised to **lessen the burden of subsidy**

# ROW 3:61 | Indian industry - structure, growth and contribution; IIP; public sector role and performance
## SEC 3:61:0 | Industrial firsts
### Early industry
- First Bombay cotton mill **1854**; Rishra jute mill **1855**; **TISCO 1907**; sugar tariff protection **1932**
- **Jute mills** — foreign-dominated; **cotton mills** — predominantly Indian
- ★ **Ranchi** — industrial town on the **Chota Nagpur plateau** ("Ruhr of India")
### Planning era
- **IPR 1956** reserved **17** industries for the public sector; **1991** policy cut it to **8**
- **Second Plan** (**Mahalanobis** model) — **basic and heavy industries**
- Early public-sector base — **Sindri Fertilizers, Chittaranjan Locomotives, Integral Coach Factory, HMT**, ITI, Hindustan Cables
- **Commanding heights** — public sector in **capital-intensive, long-gestation** industries private capital avoided
- **1965–80 deceleration** — wars, droughts, infrastructure bottlenecks, licensing (not import liberalisation)
### Structure today
- Cotton textiles — bulk of cloth from the **powerloom** segment
- Industry concentrated in **Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka**; eastern and central States lag
- **Jobless growth** (2011-2023) — directly employed workers **61% → 47%**; contract workers about **42%**
- **RINL** — turned **Visakhapatnam** into a heavy-industry and port hub

## SEC 3:61:2 | Structure since 1991
- **Premature deindustrialisation** (**Dani Rodrik**) — manufacturing share peaks at a **lower income level** than in early industrialisers
- After 1991 **services** gained the largest GDP share (industry did not gain more than services)
- Post-LPG industrial growth favoured **capital- and skill-intensive** sectors
- Industry — about **25.3%** of GVA; public sector about 13–14% of GDP; manufacturing about **17–18%** in real terms
- Manufacturing's nominal GVA share fell due to **relative price changes** (real share steady)
- Crude steel 2024 — India **2nd**, **149.4 MT** (China 1,005.1 MT)
- Capacity under-utilisation — mainly **deficient basic infrastructure** (weak demand also)
- Highest capital intensity in manufacturing (2017-18) — **Madhya Pradesh**
- IIP FY 2023 fastest growth — **capital goods** and **infrastructure/construction goods**

- ★ **NHPC** — electricity generation; **Navratna**; largest hydropower developer

| Category | Started | Investment without Govt approval |
|---|---|---|
| Maharatna | **2010** | **₹5,000 crore** in one project |

# ROW 3:63 | Industrial policy - resolutions of 1948, 1956, 1977, 1980 and the New Industrial Policy 1991
## SEC 3:63:0 | Policy milestones
### IPR 1948
- Announced by Industry Minister **Dr Shyama Prasad Mukherjee**
- Foundation of the **mixed economy**
- **Mixed sector** (six) — coal, mineral oils, iron and steel, aircraft, **shipbuilding**, telephone/telegraph apparatus
### IPR 1956
- **Socialist pattern of society** — Congress **Avadi** session, **1955**
- **Schedule A** — **17** industries (State exclusive); **Schedule B** — **12** (progressively State-owned)
- Features — licensing, public-sector expansion, small and cottage industries, **balanced regional development**, labour welfare
- ★ Licensing rested on the **Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951**
- Beginning of **licence-permit-quota raj**; basis of the **Second Plan** (Mahalanobis)
- Public-sector-led, licence-based model that lasted until **1991**
### 1967–1980
- **R. K. Hazari report (1967)** — **industrial licensing**; big business houses cornered licences
- **1973** Statement — **core sector** list; tightened MRTP
- **1977** Statement (Janata) — drafted by **George Fernandes**; **tiny** units, khadi, handlooms, employment
  - **District Industries Centres** — one-roof support to small and cottage industries in every district
- **1980** Statement — raised SSI investment limits; **technological upgradation**; export orientation stressed for the first time
  - **Economic federalism** — tax holidays and cheap credit for **backward districts**
### NIP 1991
- PM **P. V. Narasimha Rao**, Finance Minister **Manmohan Singh**; **24 July 1991**; context: **balance-of-payments crisis**
- ★ Licensing kept for **18** industries; later cut to **5**: alcohol, cigarettes/tobacco, electronic aerospace and defence equipment, industrial explosives, hazardous chemicals
  - **Drugs and pharmaceuticals** later delicensed
- Delicensed units file an **Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM)**
- Removed licensing, expansion limits, price controls, import-export restrictions; starting point of **liberalisation**
- Public-sector-only list — defence equipment, atomic energy, railway transport (never pharmaceuticals)
- Equity in SSI units by other undertakings — up to **24%**
- ★ Criticisms — not "neglect of agriculture"; incl. ambiguous policy for weak industries, threat of global competition; investment concentrated in **Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka**
- **Increase in consumer choice** — an achievement, not a criticism
### Later
- Enactments: **IDR Act 1951 → MRTP 1969 → FERA 1973 → SICA 1985**
- **SEZ Policy** — **1 April 2000**; attract investment, promote exports
- **DPIIT** — FDI policy, Startups, industrial corridors, IPR
- AP **MSME & Entrepreneur Development Policy 4.0** — **175** MSME parks; **One Family One Entrepreneur** by 2030; capital subsidy **25%** (45% special categories)

- IPR 1948 exclusive **Central monopoly** — **arms and ammunition, atomic energy, railways**
