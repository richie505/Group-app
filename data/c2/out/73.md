# ROW 3:93 | Inflation - types, causes, measurement (CPI, WPI, core inflation), effects and control measures
## SEC 3:93:0 | Types of inflation
### Basic terms
- ★ **Deflation** — a persistent (sustained) fall in the general price level of goods and services; negative inflation
  - associated with reduced consumer spending, falling demand and economic slowdown
- **Disinflation** — fall in the inflation rate that stays **positive** (not negative inflation)
- **Reflation** — inflation returning after a period of deflation; a sign of recovery
- **Skewflation** — prices rise sharply in a **few items** (e.g. food) while overall inflation stays low
- **Stagflation** — inflation with **slow growth and unemployment**
- ★ Economic growth is usually coupled with (moderate) **inflation** — rising demand pushes prices up
- ★ Inflation benefits **debtors** — they repay creditors with money worth less; creditors lose
### Rate of inflation (ascending)
| Type | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Creeping** | below **3%** |
| **Walking** | about **3–10%** |
| **Galloping** | above walking; precedes hyperinflation |
| **Hyperinflation** | most severe |

## SEC 3:93:1 | Headline vs core
- **May 2026**: food **4.85%** > headline **3.93%** > core **3.73%** (order: Food > Headline > Core)
- **CPI** — retail prices; **WPI** — wholesale goods
- **Headline** inflation includes food and energy; **core** excludes them
- Headline is **more volatile** than core because it includes food and energy prices

## SEC 3:93:2 | Price indices
### Who publishes what
| Index | Compiled by | Base | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPI (Rural/Urban/Combined) | **NSO**, MoSPI | **2024** (replaced 2012 series) | headline inflation; DA revision |
| CPI-IW | **Labour Bureau** (not NSO) | 2016 | **DA of industrial workers** |
| WPI | **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT**, Ministry of Commerce and Industry | **2011-12** (revised from 2004-05 in 2017) | contract escalation, industrial analysis |
| CFPI | — | — | **food inflation**; 10 sub-groups (cereals, pulses, vegetables, milk) |
- WPI (2011-12 series): **697 items**, no services
- National income and GDP estimates — **NSO** under MoSPI (earlier CSO)
- **WPI** was India's headline measure before the RBI adopted **CPI (Combined)** in **April 2014** (Urjit Patel Committee framework)
- IIP measures industrial output, not prices
### Other measures
- **GDP deflator** = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100; a rise reduces purchasing power of money
- GDP deflator is **not** used for industrial workers' DA (that is CPI-IW)
- **Inflation-indexed bonds** — principal and/or interest linked to an **inflation index**; protects real return

## SEC 3:93:3 | CPI vs WPI
- New WPI series: **957 items** (earlier **697**); weights based on **Gross Value of Output**
- **CPI** — retail prices of goods **and services**; **WPI** — first-point bulk prices of **goods only**
- **Manufactured products** — largest WPI weight, about **64%**
- **GDP deflator** — broadest measure; covers all goods and services produced, incl. services
- Sale of securities through **open market operations** — a **monetary** measure
  - buffer-stock release — supply-side; taxes and government spending — fiscal

## SEC 3:93:4 | Inflation targeting
- **A.W. Phillips** (1958) — inverse relation between unemployment and wage inflation (**Phillips curve**)
- **Friedman** — long-run Phillips curve is **vertical**; no lasting trade-off
- Target for **1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031**: **4% CPI-Combined**, band **±2%** (2–6%), retained by Gazette notification
  - 6% is the upper limit of the band, not the target; WPI and core CPI are not the target index
- First breach of the 4% target in the new series: **June 2026**, at **4.38%**
- Average CPI inflation fell from **6.8%** (2012–16) to about **4.9%** after flexible inflation targeting

## SEC 3:93:5 | Effects
- Food inflation is **regressive** — poorer households spend a larger share on food
  - food and beverages ≈ **37%** of the CPI basket
- **Deflationary spiral**: postponed purchases → lower profits → higher unemployment → heavier real debt burden
- **Fixed-income groups** (pensioners) are vulnerable unless incomes are adjusted — not automatically protected
  - debtors and real-asset holders may gain; creditors may lose
- WPI inflation **9.87%** (June 2026) and **9.78%** (July 2026), far above CPI — due to fuel costs from the **West Asia conflict**

## SEC 3:93:6 | Control measures
- **OMSS (Domestic)** — FCI sells **wheat and rice**; pulses and onions belong to the **PSF** buffer
- **Repo rate** works on aggregate demand and liquidity — cannot directly resolve **supply shocks** (onion, crude oil)
- RBI issued **Inflation-Indexed Bonds** in **2013**

## SEC 3:93:7 | Exam corner
### Concepts
- **Core inflation** excludes **food and fuel**; shows the underlying, demand-side trend
- Supply shocks: monsoon failure, El Nino, costlier crude, rupee depreciation; **better storage and distribution efficiency** eases them
- **Stagflation** — inflation with **falling industrial output** and slow growth; higher government spending can add pressure
- **Cost-push** pressure falls with a permanent fall in **input costs**; hoarding, MSP increases and inflation expectations add to inflation
- Rise in **GDP deflator** → fall in **purchasing power of money**
- India's Phillips curve (2012–2026) relatively **flat** — about **92%** of workers informal, weak wage-bargaining power
### Law and data
| RBI Act, 1934 (amended 2016) | Subject |
|---|---|
| **Section 45ZA** | target-setting |
| **Section 45ZB** | MPC |
| **Section 45ZN** | report when band breached for **three consecutive quarters** |
- CPI inflation **2.2%** in **2025** — a **12-year low**
### Andhra Pradesh
- State-level Price Monitoring Committee headed by **Chief Secretary**; **DES** monitors **8 essential commodities** daily
- May 2026: AP retail inflation **4.9%** vs India **3.9%**; AP above national rate for **five consecutive months**

## SEC 3:93:8 | Inflation — definitions; headline vs core
- ★ When all prices rise, purchasing power of money **decreases**
- ★ **Headline inflation** — total inflation incl. food and energy prices
- ★ **Core inflation** — headline inflation excluding food (and fuel/energy); long-run trend, excludes transitory volatility
- ★ Headline is **more volatile** than core; core does **not** include food and energy, headline does **not** exclude food
- ★ Headline inflation = rate of change in the **CPI** Number (standard basket of a typical family)
- ★ Most common measure in India: **Consumer Price Index** — RBI uses **CPI (Combined)**, base **2024=100**
- ★ **CPI** and **WPI** are the two major indices; **WPI** was the main index till **April 2014**
- ★ Dearness allowance basis: **Consumer Price Index** (CPI-IW for public and private sector employees)

## SEC 3:93:9 | Inflation — types
- ★ **Demand-pull** inflation caused by **expansionary policies**, **fiscal stimulus**, **higher purchasing power** (not inflation-indexing wages or rising interest rates)
  - aggregate demand exceeds aggregate supply / full-employment output; too much money chasing too few goods
- ★ **Bottleneck inflation** — supply falls drastically while demand stays the same; arises from supply-side deficiencies; tough for government to tackle
- ★ **Stagflation** — high/rising inflation, slowing growth, steadily high unemployment; contributors: higher government spending, falling industrial production, sluggish growth and other listed factors (all four)
- ★ Fall in **inflation rate** definitely implies only that prices are **rising more slowly** than before (not that prices fell)

## SEC 3:93:10 | Causes of inflation in India — factor → effect
- ★ **West Texas Intermediate (WTI)** — a grade of **crude oil**; one of three major benchmarks with **Brent** and **Dubai**

## SEC 3:93:11 | Effects of inflation and deflation — gainers and losers
- ★ Inflation hurts **creditors** most — repaid in money of lower purchasing power
- ★ Inflation benefits **debtors**, not **bond-holders**

## SEC 3:93:13 | Price indices → publisher, base year, weights
### Publishers
- ★ **CPI-IW** — brought out by the **Labour Bureau**
- ★ **Labour Bureau** (est. **1920**; Ministry of Labour and Employment) — also compiles data on industrial disputes, closures, retrenchments, lay-offs
- ★ **WPI** — Office of Economic Adviser, **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**
- ★ WPI historically described as India's **primary inflation measure**
### Base years, weights, series
- ★ CPI-IW base shifted from **1982** to **2001**; later **2001 → 2016** (w.e.f. **September 2020**)
- ★ Manufactured products weight in WPI: **64.23%**
- ★ New WPI series introduced on **14 September 2010** (as per question period)
- ★ Among CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL and CPI-UNME, the odd one out: **CPI-UNME** (Urban Non-Manual Employees)
### Uses
- ★ **CPI-IW** — basis for estimating **urban poverty**; **CPI-AL** — **rural poverty**
- ★ **CPI-IW** — used to compensate wages (DA) of **Central Government employees**

## SEC 3:93:14 | WPI vs CPI vs GDP deflator; statistic → publishing body
- ★ Traditionally India's headline inflation measured by **WPI** (wholesale-level goods prices), before CPI became primary
- ★ Present: headline inflation based on **CPI-Combined (CPI-C)**
- ★ WPI: maximum weight to **manufactured products**
- ★ **Producer Price Index** — average change in prices of **produced goods** (output PPI: leaving production; input PPI: entering it)

## SEC 3:93:15 | Acts and committees — flexible inflation targeting, RBI Act sections
- ★ **Abhijit Sen** — chaired working group on WPI revision (2007); report **May 2008**; recommended base **2004-05**

## SEC 3:93:16 | Data — inflation trends and the food-headline-core order
- ★ RBI forecast for headline inflation in **FY2025**: **5.0%**

## SEC 3:93:17 | Control measures — monetary, fiscal, supply-side, schemes, indexed bonds
- ★ **Brent Index** — related to **crude oil prices** (North Sea market)
- ★ Inflation benefits the **debtors**

## SEC 3:93:18 | Universal angles
- ★ Price-hike: **businessmen profit**, consumers curtail needs — both true, but the second does not explain the first
  - price-hike lowers purchasing power of money
- ★ Inflation in India measured on **CPI**, **WPI** and **Labour Cost of Living Index** (all); RBI now uses **CPI (Combined)**

# ROW 3:94 | Indian tax system - direct and indirect taxes, tax buoyancy, tax-GDP ratio, progressivity
## SEC 3:94:0 | Tax, fee, cess, surcharge
- **Fee** — paid for a specific service; involves **quid pro quo** (a tax has none)
- **Article 271** — surcharge proceeds retained by **Centre**, not shared with States; cesses also outside the divisible pool

## SEC 3:94:1 | Direct vs indirect
- **Direct taxes** — generally non-inflationary; **indirect taxes** raise prices (burden built into prices)
- **GST** (indirect) — burden shifted through prices; **property tax** (direct) — cannot be shifted
- **Customs duty** — an **indirect** tax on imports and exports

## SEC 3:94:2 | Progressivity
- **Degressive tax** — rate rises with income at first, then becomes flat
- **Proportional tax** — one rate for all
- **Progressive tax** — average tax burden **increases** with income (higher marginal rates on higher slabs)

## SEC 3:94:3 | Constitutional basis
| Provision | Content |
|---|---|
| **Article 265** | no tax levied or collected except by **authority of law** |
| **Article 276** | **profession tax** — State levy, ceiling **₹2,500** per year |
| **State List Entry 46** | **agricultural income tax** — States only |
- **80th Amendment** (2000) on sharing Union taxes — effective **1 April 1996**

## SEC 3:94:4 | Taxable events
- **GST** — from **1 July 2017**; indirect tax on supply of goods and services
| Tax | Taxable event / base |
|---|---|
| Sales tax / **VAT** | **sale of goods** (VAT: value added) |
| Central excise | manufacture |
| Customs | import / export |
| Service tax | provision of service |
| **Corporation tax** | company income / profit |
| Income tax | income |

## SEC 3:94:5 | Key numbers
### Classification
- ★ **Income tax** — a **direct** tax (not indirect); incidence and impact on the same person
  - other direct taxes: corporation tax, wealth tax, capital gains tax, dividend tax
- **VAT** — an **indirect** tax passed to buyers, like excise, service tax, sales tax
- ★ Redistribution of income best through **progressive taxation** combined with **progressive** expenditure
- **Direct taxes** — generally **progressive**; indirect may be regressive
### Terms
- **Surcharge** — additional charge on tax payable (none if no tax due); **cess** — for a specific purpose
- **Corporation tax** base — **net profit** of companies
- **Wealth tax** — introduced **1957**, abolished **2015**
- **Tax shifting** — moving burden forward to consumers or backward to suppliers
  - **impact** — on whom first imposed; **incidence** — on whom it finally falls
- **Tax elasticity** — response of revenue to GDP **excluding** discretionary changes; **tax buoyancy** includes them
### Revenue data
- Early planning decades: **indirect taxes** (excise, customs, sales) dominated; now **direct taxes** dominate
- Budget 2026–27: **income tax** — single largest tax source (**21%** of the rupee)
- Centre's rupee 2026–27: largest overall source — **borrowings and other liabilities** (**24%**)
- AP own-tax revenue 2024–25: **SGST 38%**, **State Excise 22%**
### Tax-GDP ratio
- Tax-GDP ratio = total tax revenue / **nominal GDP**; low ratio may reflect large informal sector or evasion
- **World Bank** tipping point: **15%**; India's 2001–2022 average **16.36%**
- Centre's gross tax revenue ≈ **9.9%** of GDP in **2019–20**
- Falling ratio → revenue growing **slower than GDP**; weaker fiscal space, more borrowing

## SEC 3:94:6 | Indirect tax reform
- **Chelliah** approach — **lower rates** with a **broader base** (more taxpayers, fewer exemptions)
- **L.K. Jha** — Indirect Taxation Enquiry Committee (**1976**); suggested **VAT**, at manufacturing stage (led to **MODVAT**) — not Shome
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| **MODVAT** | 1 March **1986** |
| **CENVAT** | 1 April **2000** |
| First State VAT — **Haryana** | 1 April **2003** |
| State VAT nationwide | 1 April **2005** |

## SEC 3:94:7 | Exam corner
- ★ Per the source, "**self-enforcing system**" does not explain VAT's conceptualisation
  - VAT: harmonised, **non-cascading**; taxes value addition at each stage; improves revenue buoyancy
- ★ **Wealth tax** — a **direct** tax (cannot be shifted)
- **Laffer curve** — **inverted-U**; revenue **zero** at 0% and 100% tax rates
- Cut in direct taxes → higher **disposable income** and consumption demand (may also support savings, investment)
- Simpler tax system raises productivity by removing **deadweight costs** and resource-allocation distortions (APPSC-G1 2024)

## SEC 3:94:8 | Terms → meaning: tax, fee, cess, surcharge
- ★ **Tax** — pecuniary burden imposed by the legislative authority on individuals/property owners to support government
- ★ **Agricultural income tax** — not shared by Centre with States; it is a **State** subject
  - after **80th Amendment** (2000): net proceeds of all Union taxes (minus cess, surcharge, collection cost) shareable

## SEC 3:94:9 | Articles and Amendments — constitutional basis of taxation
- ★ Redistribution of income: **progressive taxation** + **progressive expenditure** (welfare, subsidies, social schemes)
- ★ Sales tax was not levied in **Andaman & Nicobar** and **Lakshadweep** (question period); GST covers all India since 1 July 2017
- ★ **CENVAT** — related to **Union Excise Duties**
- ★ **Corporation tax** — levied and collected by the **Union**; at present shared with States (per FC); earlier exclusive to Union
- ★ **Corporation tax** — not levied by State Governments

## SEC 3:94:10 | Direct vs indirect taxes — classification
### Classification
- ★ **Sales tax** — not a direct tax
- ★ **Excise duty** — an **indirect** tax on production, sale or licence of goods
- ★ **Income tax** — does not directly increase the price of a commodity to buyers
- ★ Sales tax on toothpaste — **imposed and collected by the State Government** (pre-GST)
- ★ **MODVAT** — related to **excise duty** (mid-1980s, to relieve tax cascading)
### Committees and sharing
- ★ **Kelkar Committee** — **both direct and indirect taxes** (two task force reports)
- ★ Shared with States: **income tax, corporate tax, custom duty** (per Finance Commission)
### Revenue data
- ★ **Excise duty** share of gross tax revenue declined significantly (Economic Survey 2009-10; 30.4% in 2005-06 → 20.8% in 2007-08)
- ★ Maximum Centre revenue 2007-08: **corporation tax**; Budget 2025-26: income tax **21%**
- ★ Two largest sources of Central tax revenue (1995 question): **custom duty and income tax**

## SEC 3:94:11 | Taxes → base and taxable event
- ★ **Excise duty** — on **production/manufacture** of goods; paid by manufacturer
- ★ **Customs duty** — tax on **import and export**; levied by Union; not a State tax revenue source
- ★ **VAT** — abolished/subsumed by **GST**
- ★ **VAT** — imposed **on all stages** between production and final sale; still applies to goods outside GST
- ★ **Corporation tax** — on **income of company**
- ★ **Entertainment tax** — not levied by the Union; **State List** (List-II), Seventh Schedule

## SEC 3:94:12 | Types — progressive, proportional, regressive taxes
- ★ **Sales tax** — not a direct tax; income tax, wealth tax, estate duty are direct
- ★ Indirect taxes are **regressive** — charged at the **same rates** for all income groups
- ★ **Direct Tax Code** — related to **income tax**; to replace the **Income Tax Act, 1961** with a single law
- ★ **Income tax** — **progressive** and **direct** (not collected by States, not proportional)
- ★ India's tax system — progressive in nature but overall **degressive**

## SEC 3:94:13 | Data — direct vs indirect share
- ★ First four decades of planning: principal Central revenue from **indirect taxes**
- ★ **Subsidy** — not public revenue; part of public expenditure (income tax, borrowings, VAT are revenue)
- ★ Share of direct taxes in GNP (2004 question): about **5%**
  - Budget 2026-27: direct taxes **6.9%** of GDP (BE), indirect **4.3%**; 2024-25 actuals 6.7% / 4.8%
- ★ Largest source of revenue at present: **income tax** (Union excise in 2004-05 and 2005-06)

## SEC 3:94:14 | Data — Centre's and States' revenue
- ★ Budget 2016-17: **15%** surcharge if income **above ₹1 crore**
- ★ Largest revenue source: **corporation tax** in **2005-06** and **2013-14**
  - Budget 2026-27 estimates: income tax ₹14,66,000 cr > corporation tax ₹12,31,000 cr > GST ₹10,19,020 cr
- ★ Union Government alone: **customs duties, corporation tax** (Union List)

## SEC 3:94:15 | Data — tax-GDP ratio: Centre, States, combined, peers
- ★ Centre's gross tax revenue in **2019-20**: **9.9%** of GDP (Economic Survey 2021-22)
- ★ Tax-GDP ratio **2008-09**: **16.39%** (Centre + States about 16.5%)
- ★ **Road tax** — levied by **State** governments, not the Government of India
- ★ **Service tax** — **indirect** tax; introduced **1994-95** on **Chelliah Committee** recommendation; replaced by GST from 1 July 2017

## SEC 3:94:16 | Terms and persons → tax buoyancy, elasticity, Laffer curve, base widening
- ★ **Project Saksham** — new **indirect tax network** (system integration) of CBEC (now **CBIC**); approved by CCEA, **September 2016**
- ★ Service tax: **10% → 12%** in Budget **2006-07**; 12% → **14%** in Budget 2015-16
  - from 2012-13: 12% on all services except negative-list services

# ROW 3:96 | Goods and Services Tax - structure, slabs, GST Council, IGST settlement, compensation cess
## SEC 3:96:0 | Road to GST
- **Arvind Subramanian** committee (**2015**) — **Revenue Neutral Rate** (rate keeping revenue at pre-GST level)
- **Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers** — chaired by **Asim Dasgupta**

## SEC 3:96:2 | GST Council
### Constitution
- **Article 279A** inserted by 101st Amendment (which **omitted Article 268A**)

- **Constitutional body**, never statutory — Mohit Minerals (2022) did not hold it statutory
- Chaired by **Union Finance Minister**; MoS Finance only a member
### Working
- Quorum: **one-half** of total members
- Voting: Centre **one-third**, all States **two-thirds**; decisions by **three-fourths** weighted majority of present and voting
- Anti-profiteering: **NAA → CCI** (December 2022) → **GSTAT Principal Bench** (from **1 October 2024**)
- Outside GST: **alcoholic liquor for human consumption**, electricity, stamp-duty real estate transactions, ATF

## SEC 3:96:3 | Components
- GST — **multi-stage, destination-based** tax on value addition; taxable event **supply** (not manufacture)
- **IGST** revenue settled to the **destination (consuming) State**
- Centre collects **CGST and IGST**; States collect **SGST**; GST Council recommends
- AP's largest own tax source (2024-25 RE): **SGST** — about ₹36,296 crore (**38%**); State excise (22%) second

## SEC 3:96:4 | Subsumed vs outside
- **Stamp duty** — not subsumed; purchase, luxury and entry taxes subsumed
- Subsumed: **8 Central** and **9 State** taxes (conventional count)
- GST replaced **indirect** taxes (excise, service tax, State VAT), not direct taxes
- **Alcoholic liquor for human consumption** — **constitutionally excluded**
- **Five petroleum products** — within GST constitutionally but not yet levied: crude oil, petrol, high-speed diesel, natural gas, ATF (not **lubricating oil**)
- Biggest indirect-tax reform since Independence — **unified framework** for many indirect taxes
- Ends "tax on tax" — **cascading of taxes**; creates a unified national market

## SEC 3:96:5 | Rates & mechanisms
### Rates
- RNR Committee (Arvind Subramanian): RNR **15–15.5%**, standard rate **17–18%**
- Ceilings: **CGST 20%**, **IGST 40%**
- Rates changed by **government notification** on GST Council recommendation, within statutory ceilings
- **56th GST Council meeting**: **5%** merit, **18%** standard, **40%** de-merit/sin
### Mechanisms
- **Zero-rated** (exports, SEZ supplies) — full ITC refund; **nil-rated** — no ITC
- **Reverse Charge** — liability shifts from **supplier to recipient**
- **Composition scheme**: goods **₹1.5 crore** (1%), services **₹50 lakh** (6%); no ITC
- **E-way bill** — goods above **₹50,000**; valid one day per **200 km**
- **Destination-based** — revenue accrues where goods/services are **consumed**

## SEC 3:96:12 | GST — basics, objectives and nature
- ★ GST — **non-inflationary**; eliminates **cascading** (tax-on-tax), reduces production costs
- ★ GST replaced a number of **indirect taxes** — excise duty, service tax, VAT, entry tax, several cesses

- ★ **Ghee** — under GST; **alcohol** for human consumption, **electricity**, **petroleum** products — outside
- ★ **101st Amendment Act** — provisions for GST

## SEC 3:96:15 | Structure and slabs — CGST, SGST, IGST, GST 2.0

# ROW 3:97 | Impact of GST on commerce, industry and state finances; unresolved GST issues
## SEC 3:97:0 | GST collections trend
- Monthly gross GST first above **₹2 lakh crore**: **April 2024**
- FY **2024-25** gross GST: **₹22.08 lakh crore**; average **₹1.84 lakh crore** per month
- **April 2026**: record gross **₹2.43 lakh crore**; growth **8.7%**; net **₹2.11 lakh crore**

## SEC 3:97:1 | GST 2.0 rate rationalisation
- Original 2017 slabs: **5%, 12%, 18%, 28%** (plus exempt goods and compensation cess over 28%)
- Four-slab structure intended as a **transitional arrangement** before simplification
- From **22 September 2025**: **0%**, **5%** merit, **18%** standard, **40%** demerit
- About **99%** of old 12% items → **5%**; about **90%** of old 28% items → **18%** (rest to 40%)
- Special rates: **rough diamonds 0.25%**; **gold, silver, jewellery 3%**

## SEC 3:97:2 | Impact on commerce & MSMEs
- GST's main gift to inter-State commerce — **common national market** through tax harmonisation; ITC reduces cascading
- **E-way bill** — mandatory above **₹50,000**; valid one day per **200 km**
- Removal of inter-State **check-posts** → reduced truck waiting, **transit time and logistics costs**
- Formalisation: taxpayer base **66.5 lakh** (2017) → **1.51 crore** (2025)
- **Composition scheme**: **1%** on goods, **6%** on services; no ITC

## SEC 3:97:3 | Key terms
### Credit and duty structure
- **ITC fraud** by bogus entities — **invoices without actual supply**
- **Inverted Duty Structure** — tax on inputs > tax on output → ITC accumulates → **working capital blocked**
### Destination principle
- Favours **consuming States** — taxation follows place of consumption
- **IGST** — levied by the **Centre** and apportioned; State share goes to the **destination (consuming)** State
- **AP** (consuming State) benefits through **IGST settlement**
- AP's **Net GST** = **SGST + State's share of IGST settlement**
- Producing States fearing loss: **Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka** — led to five-year compensation guarantee
### Court
- **AP High Court** (May 2026): overlapping Central and State GST probes — authorities must **decide inter se** who proceeds; prevents dual action

## SEC 3:97:4 | Impact on States
- Compensation: **five years** (July 2017 – June 2022) at **14%** annual growth over **2015–16** base
- Cess continued after the period to **repay loans** taken for compensation during **COVID-19**
- Cess scrapped on **22 September 2025** except on **tobacco products** (until loan obligations discharged)
- Unused cess balance: **50% Centre**, **50% States** in proportion to their **SGST** revenue (not population, not FC formula)

## SEC 3:97:5 | Unresolved issues
### Rates and compliance
- Multiple-rate structure's biggest drawback — **misclassification** and **classification disputes**
- Near-identical products in different slabs → **classification disputes and litigation**; standardising classification pending
- Small-business reform agenda: **pre-filled returns**, time-bound digital registration, automated exporter refunds (not manual filing)
### Outside GST
- **Petroleum and electricity** remain outside
- **Crude oil, petrol, high-speed diesel, natural gas, ATF** — within GST law but taxed outside until Council recommends a date under **Article 279A(5)**
- **Cesses** outside the divisible pool → concerns over State **fiscal autonomy** and vertical fiscal imbalance
### Institutions
- **GSTAT** Principal Bench — **New Delhi** (not Mumbai)
- Mid-2025: GSTAT notified but **functional benches absent** in many States; appeals piled up in High Courts
- **Mohit Minerals (2022)**: India a **flexible federation**; Council recommendations **persuasive**; simultaneous power from **Article 246A**; Council constitutional
- Centre's **effective veto**: its **one-third** vote can block the **three-fourths** majority

## SEC 3:97:6 | AP angle
- **Economic Survey 2025–26**: GST rate cut — **volume effects** may offset revenue loss
- GST 2.0 — **third pillar of tax reform**, after **2019 corporate tax cut** and **2025 personal income-tax reform**
- Surrender of indirect-tax powers + weak own revenues → States' greater **dependence on transfers**
- AP own tax revenue (2024-25 RE): **GST 38%** (₹36,296 crore), **State Excise 22%** (₹21,300 crore)

## SEC 3:97:7 | Exam corner
- GST 2.0 approved at **56th GST Council meeting** (3–4 September 2025); effective **22 September 2025**
- GST from **1 July 2017** under **101st Amendment Act, 2016**; key Articles **246A, 269A, 279A**

## SEC 3:97:10 | Impact on commerce, logistics and MSMEs
- ★ Gross GST in **October 2020**: **more than ₹1 lakh crore** (₹1,05,155 crore)
- 2025-26: highest-ever gross collection **₹22.27 lakh crore**, growth **8.3%**

## SEC 3:97:11 | Rate structure — multiple rates and GST 2.0 — Drawbacks of multiple rates
- Multiple-rate structure's biggest drawback — **misclassification** of goods and services and **classification disputes**
