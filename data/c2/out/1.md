# ROW 1:6 | IVC town planning - grid layout, citadel and lower town, drainage, Great Bath, granaries
## SEC 1:6:0 | Grid & houses
### Streets
- **Banawali** — only Harappan city with **radial streets**
- **Mohenjodaro**, **Harappa** — typical **grid** layout, streets crossing at **right angles**
- **Standardised bricks**, grid planning, drainage → **civic authority** and early engineering skill
### Houses & wells
- Rooms built round a **central courtyard**
- Doors and windows opened onto **side lanes**, not main streets
- **Staircases** → houses could be **multi-storeyed**
- **Mohenjodaro** — **greatest number of wells** (inside houses or neighbourhoods)
### Harappa finds
- **Cemetery H** — at **Harappa**; also a **coffin burial**, wheat and barley in a **wooden mortar**, **dice**
- **Cemetery R-37** — at **Harappa**
- Not Harappa: dockyard — **Lothal**; horse bones — **Surkotada**; bronze chariot — **Daimabad**
- **Dholavira** — three parts; **Chanhudaro** — no citadel

## SEC 1:6:1 | Site → plan
| Site | Plan feature |
|---|---|
| **Chanhudaro** | **no citadel** |
| **Lothal** | citadel and lower town in **one enclosure** |
| **Kalibangan** | **fortified lower town** |
| **Dholavira** | only city in **three parts**: citadel, middle town, lower town |
- Citadel — raised, fortified, on the **western side**, with public buildings; lower town to the **east**
- **Lothal** — **warehouse on a raised platform** beside the dock; **seal impressions** associated with it

## SEC 1:6:2 | Structure → site
### Granaries
- **Great Granary** — **Mohenjodaro**; largest building of the city
- **Harappa granary** — **12 rooms** (two rows of six)
- Granaries — stores of **farm surplus** (interpretation debated)
### Other structures
- **Fire altars** — **Kalibangan** (seven altars) and **Lothal**
- **Largest inscription** — **ten-sign signboard**, **North Gate of Dholavira**
- **Dholavira** — occupied in **seven stages**, c. **3000–1500 BCE**
- **Dholavira** — **stadium-like open ground**, special cemetery structures, reservoirs

## SEC 1:6:3 | Drainage
- **Planned drainage system** — the **most advanced feature** of IVC towns
- Brick sizes **differed** by construction (not one size for all)
  - House bricks ~**7 × 14 × 28 cm**; city-wall bricks ~**10 × 20 × 40 cm**
  - Both followed the **same ratio**

## SEC 1:6:4 | Great Bath
- ★ **Great Bath** — **Mohenjodaro** (Sindh, Pakistan)
- Located on the **citadel mound**
- Built of **burnt bricks** set in **gypsum mortar**; waterproofed with **bitumen**
- **Flights of steps** at its **north and south ends**
- Interpreted as for **ritual bathing and purification** (also read as a public water tank)

## SEC 1:6:5 | Bricks
### Streets
- Streets ran **north-south** and **east-west**, crossed at right angles, divided cities into **blocks**
- **Main streets** ~**10 metres** (30 feet) wide
### Bricks
- Standard brick ratio **1 : 2 : 4** (thickness : width : length)
- **Burnt bricks** common; **mud bricks** at **Kalibangan**
- **Dholavira** — exception, **sandstone and limestone blocks**
### Drains
- Covered drains had **manholes** for cleaning; **street slopes** gave natural flow
- **Banawali** — **lacked a systematic drainage system**
- **Kalibangan** — **wooden drains**
### Civic authority
- Absence of palaces/temples does **not disprove civic authority**; **standardised planning** suggests it

## SEC 1:6:6 | Dholavira water system
- **Water reservoirs, dams and embankments** — **Dholavira** (not Harappa)
- **Giant reservoirs** fed by **bunds, check-dams and channels**
- Major construction in **sandstone and limestone blocks** (vs baked brick at Mohenjodaro, Harappa)
- Some reservoirs partly **cut into rock**

## SEC 1:6:8 | Town planning features — streets, houses and wells
- ★ **Wells in houses** (developed stage of IVC) — **Mohenjodaro**
- ★ IVC — a **Bronze Age** civilization; **iron** known much later (post-Vedic, c. **1000 B.C.**)
- **Well irrigation** — **Allahdino** in **Sindh**

## SEC 1:6:9 | Sites → layout — citadel, lower town, three-part plan
- ★ **Dholavira** — divided into **three parts** (three townships): **Citadel, Middle town, Lower town**
  - In **Kachchh** district, **Gujarat**; built in a **rectangular** shape
- ★ **Lothal** — **port city** of IVC; only IVC place with evidence of ports (located in the **east** of the city)

## SEC 1:6:10 | Harappa — granary, burials and finds
- **Kalibangan**, **Ropar**, **Lothal** — IVC sites; **Pataliputra** (near Patna) — **not** an IVC site

## SEC 1:6:12 | Dholavira → water harvesting and other features
- ★ **Dholavira** — **highly advanced water management system**
- ★ Huge **rock-cut reservoir** ~**79 m × 12 m**, **7 m deep**

## SEC 1:6:13 | Building materials — bricks, ratio and statement traps
### Statement traps
- ★ IVC — **Bronze Age**; people **did not know iron**
- ★ **Drainage** — advanced, **covered with slabs**
- ★ **Trade and commerce** advanced; evidence of **internal and external** trade
- ★ **Not** the same brick size for all constructions
- ★ Houses **not** mostly single storey
- ★ **Main roads** ~**ten metres** wide on average
### Bricks
- ★ Both **baked** and **raw** bricks used
- ★ Burnt-brick ratio **4 : 2 : 1**
- ★ **Ornamental bricks** — **Kalibangan**; **curved bricks** — **Chanhudaro**

## SEC 1:6:-1 | (whole topic)
- ★ **Potteries** — the important reason to place IVC **before** the Aryan civilization
- ★ IVC **script** and **town planning** more developed than the pre-Aryans'
- ★ Pottery evidence in the early settlements of **Mehrgarh**

# ROW 1:8 | IVC economy - agriculture, crops, domesticated animals, internal and external trade, Meluhha

# ROW 1:10 | IVC society and polity - social stratification debate, burial practices, absence of temples/palaces
## SEC 1:10:0 | Stratification evidence
- **Variation in burial goods and ornament quality** — strongest indicator of **social differentiation**
- **Uniform bricks** — evidence of **organisation and authority**, not status

## SEC 1:10:1 | Social order
- **Caste** crystallised in the **Later Vedic period**; no textual evidence of a rigid Harappan caste system
- Weapons (maces, axes, spears) — **copper and bronze**; few and simple; **no horse-drawn chariots**
- **Huddled skeletons** at **Mohenjodaro** — once "massacre", now **multiple burials**

## SEC 1:10:2 | Views on the polity
### Layout
- **Citadel** (west) — **public buildings and elite areas**; **lower town** (east) — commoners' houses
### Theories of polity
| View | Based on |
|---|---|
| **Priestly rule** | **citadels, Great Bath, steatite Priest-King** (not conclusive) |
| **Merchant elites / civic administrators** | **trade-driven uniformity**, **no clear kings or palaces** |
| **Centralised state** | **uniformity over a huge area** (no single capital identified) |
- Standardised bricks, binary weights, uniform seals, grid towns → **significant central authority**
### Dress & toilet
- **Priest-King** — wears a **shawl**; no evidence of stitching in Harappan clothing
- **Lipstick** — **Chanhudaro**; also bronze mirrors, ivory combs, collyrium sticks
### Burials
- Grave goods (pottery, ornaments, mirrors) → **belief in an afterlife** and status differences, not cremation
- **Kalibangan** — **three** burial types
  - extended burials in rectangular pits; **pot burials**; circular pits with grave goods but **no skeleton**

## SEC 1:10:3 | No palaces, no temples
- Harappan political structure — not clear-cut; appears **corporate**
- No monumental temples/palaces → worship **domestic or open-air**; power **not personalised in a king**
- Public structures — chiefly **civic**

## SEC 1:10:4 | Burial type → site
| Burial practice | Site |
|---|---|
| **Extended burials with goods** and **coffin burial** | **R-37 cemetery**, Harappa |
| **Post-cremation / fractional burials**, Late Harappan | **Cemetery H**, Harappa |
| **Dog buried with a human** in an oval pit | **Ropar** |
| **Joint burials** | **Lothal** |
| **Pot burials** | **Surkotada** |
| **North–south burials** | **Rakhigarhi** |
- **Fractional burial** (Mohenjodaro, Harappa) — bones buried **after exposure** of the body

## SEC 1:10:5 | Daily life
- **Toy factory** — **Chanhudaro**; toys of **terracotta**

## SEC 1:10:6 | Exam corner
- **No royal palaces or temples** found; civic structures like the **Great Bath** known
- **Joint/double burial** — **Lothal**; dog with human — **Ropar**; pot burials — **Surkotada**
- Stratification and polity reconstructed from **material remains** — script gives no rulers' names
- Harappan polity — **corporate**; Later Vedic — **hereditary monarchy**, declining **Sabha and Samiti**

# ROW 1:12 | IVC religion - Pashupati seal, Mother Goddess, tree and animal worship, fire altars
## SEC 1:12:0 | Nature of religion
- IVC — **predominantly secular**; religious elements present but not dominant
- **No temple** found; worship possibly **domestic** or **open-air**
- **Great Bath** (Mohenjodaro) — **ritual purification**
- Differences in house size, burial goods, ornaments → **social stratification** (four-fold division speculative)

## SEC 1:12:1 | Pashupati seal
- Figure — **three-faced**, **horned**, seated in a **yogic posture**
- **John Marshall** — identified it as **Proto-Shiva** (Pashupati); debated
- Proto-Shiva reading treated cautiously — script **undeciphered**

## SEC 1:12:2 | Objects of worship
### Animals & trees
- Sacred animals — **humped bull, unicorn, elephant, tiger**
- **Pipal** — most depicted tree on seals; in ritual scenes with human figures
- Pipal motif goes back to the **Early Harappan** phase; continued in later Indian religion
### Fertility & fire
- **Terracotta female figurines** → **fertility cult**
- **Lingam and yoni** symbols, round and pierced stones (Harappa) — possible fertility objects; possible early Shaivite elements
- Row of **seven fire altars** — **Kalibangan**; fire altars also at **Lothal**
- **Swastika** — auspicious symbol of later times; **fire altars** (not swastika) linked, debatably, to **Vedic yajna**
### Later coins
- **Lakshmi** image — on the coins of **Muhammad Ghori**

## SEC 1:12:4 | Harappan → later Hinduism
### Harappan religion
- Mainly **nature- and fertility-oriented** (Later Vedic — sacrificial, ritualistic)
- **Fire altars** — not conclusive evidence of **Later Vedic yajna** (link debated)
- **Mirrors and combs** — known to the Harappans
- **Planned cities and drainage** → some form of **municipal governance**; citadels may indicate a ruling group (debated)
### Continuities
