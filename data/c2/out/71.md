# ROW 3:81 | Money supply - M0 to M4, high-powered money, money multiplier, velocity
## SEC 3:81:0 | Monetary aggregates (Working Group 1977)
### Framework and measures
- M1–M4 framework — **Second Working Group on Money Supply** (**1977**)
- **M0** (reserve money, monetary base) = currency in circulation + **bankers' deposits with RBI** + **other deposits with RBI**
- **M3** = broad money = **aggregate monetary resources**; M1 + time deposits with banks
- **M3** — most commonly used measure of **broad money** in India (M1 = narrow money, M0 = reserve money)
- Liquidity in descending order: **M1 > M2 > M3 > M4**
- **Demand deposits** — withdrawable instantly; **time deposits** are locked for a fixed period, least liquid
### Velocity and SLR
- **Velocity of money** — average number of times a unit of currency **changes hands** in a period (₹10 note used twice = ₹20 of transactions)
- Higher **SLR** — more bank funds locked in liquid assets → **reduces investible loan funds**
- SLR held by the **bank itself**; CRR held **with the RBI**; an SLR cut frees funds
### Euro
- ★ In **1999** the Euro was **not yet in circulation**

## SEC 3:81:1 | High-powered money (M0)
- ★ **High-powered money** = the **monetary base**
- Contingent functions of money — basis of credit, distribution of national income, **giving liquidity**
- **NM0–NM3** — **residency-based** aggregates (distinguish short-term from long-term deposits)
- **L1–L3** — add post-office, financial-institution and **NBFC** liabilities (broader liquidity)
- High **preference for cash** → higher currency-deposit ratio → **limits the credit multiplier**
- **Time deposits** are not part of M1; they are in **M3**
- **M3 − M1** = **time deposits with banks**

## SEC 3:81:2 | Credit creation
- Reserve requirement **10%**, no leakages → multiplier **1/0.10 = 10**; ₹100 reserve money supports about **₹1,000** of deposits
- **RBI** creates reserve money (issues notes); **commercial banks** create **deposit money** by lending beyond CRR/SLR reserves
- Money multiplier **m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr)**; higher cdr or rdr (CRR, SLR) lowers it
- **1978 demonetisation** — **₹1,000, ₹5,000 and ₹10,000** notes; High Denomination Bank Notes (Demonetisation) Act, 1978, under **Morarji Desai**, to curb black money

## SEC 3:81:3 | What moves the multiplier
- ★ Cash withdrawal of ₹1 lakh from a demand deposit → aggregate money supply **unchanged**
  - demand deposits fall by ₹1 lakh, **currency with the public** rises by ₹1 lakh
- ★ M1 = currency + **demand deposits** + other deposits with RBI; M3 = M1 + **time deposits** (aggregate / broad money, most used)
- RBI **purchase of government securities** (open market) → directly **injects reserve money**
- Fixed money supply + rising money demand → interest rate **rises** (falling demand lowers it)
- **Money multiplier** = **M3 ÷ M0** (total money supply / base money)

## SEC 3:81:5 | Money: functions & terms
- **Seigniorage** — face value minus production cost of currency (₹500 note costs about ₹4); issuer's profit, a source of RBI surplus
- **Near money** — **time deposits**, Treasury bills, bills of exchange, savings certificates (liquid but cannot directly pay for goods)
- **Token coin** — face value **higher than** the value of its metal (rupee coin)
- Not every highly liquid asset is legal tender; RBI notes **unlimited** legal tender, coins **limited** legal tender
- **Plastic money** (debit/credit cards) is **not legal tender**
- **Digital Rupee** — legal tender under the RBI Act as amended by **Finance Act 2022**

- **Hard money** — backed by **gold or credible assets**; soft money — paper money backed by government bonds
- **Currency in circulation** — **notes, rupee coins and small coins** (held by public and as banks' cash in hand)

# ROW 3:82 | Monetary policy - objectives, instruments (CRR, SLR, repo, reverse repo, MSF, OMO, LAF), transmission
## SEC 3:82:0 | Policy rates (Aug 2026)
### Objective
- **Price stability** — paramount objective (RBI Act, 1934); growth, financial stability, external balance also pursued
- **Fiscal policy** is **not** free from legislative and implementation lags
### LAF corridor
- Corridor, floor to ceiling — **SDF < Repo < MSF = Bank rate**
- **MSF rate** — **upper end** of the corridor; **repo + 25 bps**; SLR dip up to **2% of NDTL** (older design: 100-bps spread, 1% limit)
- Repo **5.25%** → MSF **5.50%**, SDF **5.00%**
- **Fixed-rate reverse repo** — absorbs funds **against G-secs**, used at RBI's discretion; **SDF** absorbs **without collateral**
| Tool | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Repo** | RBI's **principal policy lending rate**; banks borrow from RBI against securities; regular liquidity |
| **Reverse repo** | **RBI borrows** from banks to absorb surplus liquidity |
| **MSF** | overnight **emergency** borrowing above repo, dipping into SLR |
| **SDF** | absorbs surplus liquidity **without collateral** |
| **OMO** | RBI **buys or sells government securities**; a **monetary-policy** tool |
| Bank rate | RBI's lending rate |
### CRR and SLR
- **CRR** — cash balance **with the RBI**, earns **no interest**
- **SLR** — held by banks themselves in cash, gold, **unencumbered G-secs and T-Bills** (incl. SDLs) and approved securities
- SLR statutory range **0%–40% of NDTL**; 25% floor removed by **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2007**
- **Contractionary** policy — raise CRR, SLR, repo; **sell** G-secs
- **CRR/SLR cut** → more **lendable funds**, higher **money multiplier**
### Transmission and markets
- **MCLR** — aimed at **faster transmission**; an internal benchmark computed by each bank
- **Call money rate** — **market-determined**; RBI fixes CRR and SLR (true, but not the explanation)
- Repo cut works via **five channels** — interest-rate, credit, exchange-rate, asset-price, expectations
- **Informal credit markets** — outside the full reach of RBI policy (an obstacle to transmission)
- **G-sec yields** — moved by **US Fed** actions, **RBI** actions, domestic **inflation and short-term rates**
- Policy rates directly regulate **capacity for credit creation**

## SEC 3:82:1 | CRR vs SLR
- ★ **Repurchase option (Repo)** — used by RBI to regulate **money supply and inflation**
- SLR reduced from its 1991–92 level on advice of the **Narasimham Committee**
- **CRR** — **Section 42, RBI Act, 1934**; **SLR** — **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**
- SLR — **38.5%** (1991–92) → **18%** (September 2023)

## SEC 3:82:2 | Quantitative vs qualitative
- **Quantitative** tools — repo, reverse repo, OMO, CRR, SLR
- **Qualitative / selective** tools — **margin requirements**, **moral suasion**
- **Margin** = value of security − loan; raising the margin **reduces the loan** available against the same security

## SEC 3:82:3 | Frameworks & benchmarks

### Lending-rate benchmarks
- **Base Rate** — **1 July 2010**; based on **average cost of funds** (slow transmission)
- **MCLR** — **1 April 2016**; **marginal cost of funds** (marginal deposit cost, CRR/SLR carry, operating cost, tenor premium)
- RBI Internal Study Group on MCLR (**2017**) — chaired by **Janak Raj**; found weak transmission, recommended an external benchmark
- **EBLR** mandatory from **1 October 2019** — new floating-rate personal, retail and MSME loans linked to an **external benchmark** (repo, 3- or 6-month T-bill yield, or FBIL-published rate)
- **EBLR**-linked loans — **fastest** transmission; MCLR loans reset only **periodically**
- Who sets the rate — **MSF rate** by RBI/MPC; MCLR by each bank; EBLR by external benchmark; call rate by the market
- **MSF** rate is **higher** than repo (repo + 0.25%); overnight emergency window

## SEC 3:82:4 | Repo transmission
- MPC sets repo to **achieve the inflation target** and guide money-market rates
- Repo hike sequence — **higher money-market rates** → costlier credit → lower consumption and investment → lower aggregate demand
- **Surplus liquidity** pulls market rates **below repo**, speeding transmission; deficit liquidity weakens it
- **Expectations channel** — a credible target **anchors** expectations; does not remove all uncertainty

## SEC 3:82:5 | Liquidity tools
### Day-to-day liquidity
- **LAF** — RBI's primary window: injects via repo, absorbs via reverse repo and SDF
- **VRRR** — auction-based, absorbs liquidity for **longer than overnight** SDF; **VRR** injects
- **Fine-tuning operations** (short-term VRR/VRRR) — keep liquidity in line with the **policy stance**
- **Hawkish** — worried about **inflation**, tightens; **dovish** — worried about growth, easing bias
- Higher **CRR** → **reduces money in circulation**
### Durable liquidity and sterilisation
- **OMO** — outright purchase or sale of **government securities**; durable liquidity
- **I-CRR** — **10%**, announced **10 August 2023**; impounded about **₹1.1 lakh crore**
- **USD/INR buy-sell swap** (e.g. 3-year, $5 billion, December 2025) — injects rupee liquidity **without changing RBI balance-sheet size**
- **MSS** (**April 2004**) — sterilises liquidity from **forex purchases**; T-Bills issued, proceeds in a separate account Government cannot spend
- **Sterilisation** — offsetting excess rupee liquidity from **forex inflows**; tools **MSS, OMO, VRRR**

## SEC 3:82:6 | Obstacles & limits
- **Supply-driven** food/fuel inflation — **buffer stocks, duty cuts, farm reforms** (CRR, SLR, rates act on demand)
- **Ad hoc Treasury Bills** (automatic monetisation) ended **1997** → **Ways and Means Advances**
- Main post-1991 challenge — **exchange-rate volatility** from capital flows
- **Fiscal dominance** — large **government borrowing** pressuring money supply and inflation
- Narrower spread between Indian and US bond yields → **cuts India's borrowing costs**

## SEC 3:82:10 | Terms → meaning: policy rates, LAF corridor, stance — Policy rates
### Rates
- ★ **Repo Rate** — rate at which **RBI lends** short-term funds to commercial banks (against G-secs); under **monetary policy**

- ★ **OMO** — **purchase and sale of government securities** by RBI (trading in securities)
- ★ Inflation control — **raise Bank Rate, CRR, SLR**; **not** purchase of government securities

# ROW 3:83 | Monetary Policy Committee and the flexible inflation targeting framework
## SEC 3:83:0 | Road to FIT
### Committees and phases
- **Sukhamoy Chakravarty Committee** (**1985**) — **monetary targeting with feedback**; organised money market
- **N. Vaghul Committee** (**1987**) — money-market blueprint → **Commercial Paper** (1990)
- Phases — **monetary targeting** (1985–98) → **multiple-indicator** (1998–2014) → **glide path** (2014–16) → **FIT with MPC** (2016–)
- **Urjit Patel Committee** — **dropped** the multiple-indicator approach; **CPI** as nominal anchor; MPC-based rate setting
### Statute
- **RBI (Amendment) Act, 2016** — inserted **Sections 45Z to 45ZO**; statutory backing for FIT and MPC
- 2015 **Monetary Policy Framework Agreement** — not a statute; **Central Government sets the target**, RBI conducts policy to achieve it
- **MPC decisions** — **binding on the RBI**

## SEC 3:83:1 | Key sections
| Section (RBI Act) | Provision |
|---|---|
| **45ZA** | **Inflation target** — set by Central Government with RBI, every **five years**, notified in Gazette |
| **45ZB** | Constitution of the **MPC** |
| **45ZN** | **Failure** to meet the target |
- Failure — average CPI inflation **above 6%** or **below 2%** for **three consecutive quarters**
- On failure RBI reports **reasons**, **remedial actions** and **estimated time** to return to target

## SEC 3:83:2 | MPC composition (6 members)
- **Three external members** — appointed by the **Central Government** (not RBI Board)
- Term **four years**; **not eligible for reappointment**

## SEC 3:83:3 | MPC procedure
- Primary objective — **price stability** with growth in mind; financial stability also recognised
- MPC must meet **at least four times** a year by law; six (bi-monthly) is practice

## SEC 3:85:2 | Basel accords
### Basel framework
- Basel Accords — framed by **BCBS** under the **Bank for International Settlements**
- **Basel I** (**1988**) — **credit risk**; minimum capital **8%** of risk-weighted assets; **India prescribed 9%** (CRAR introduced **1992**)
- **Basel II** (**2004**) — three pillars: **minimum capital requirements, supervisory review, market discipline**
- **CRAR / CAR** = **capital ÷ risk-weighted assets**
- **Tier 2** capital — mixed equity-debt; includes **subordinated debt**; paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed profits are **Tier 1** core equity
### Macroprudential tools
- October 2025 — RBI proposed lower Basel III risk weights for **MSMEs and real estate**
- **Countercyclical capital buffer** — introduced by RBI in **2015**; main trigger **credit-to-GDP gap**
- **Leverage ratio** — caps total exposure against **Tier 1** capital; loan limited to a share of collateral = **LTV cap**
### Other
- **EASE 8.0** ("EASE ₹ise", FY2026) — **Risk and Resilience, Innovation, Socio-economic impact, Excellence**
- **Vaghul** — **call money market**; Goswami — industrial sickness; **Janakiraman** — stock-market scam; Abid Hussain — SSI de-reservation
