# ROW 5:69 | Energy storage - battery technologies, PLI for advanced chemistry cells, grid storage
## SEC 5:69:0 | Cell parts
- ★ **Oxidation** = **loss of electrons**
- Oxidation occurs at the **anode**; reduction (gain of electrons) at the **cathode**
- **Electrolyte** lets **ions** flow between electrodes and keeps them apart (no direct electronic path)
- Li-ion battery: **graphite** usually the **anode**; **lithium, nickel, cobalt** usually in the **cathode** (cathode makeup does not explain graphite anode)
- Supply-chain risk: **more than 70%** of **lithium processing** and most **cobalt refining** concentrated in a few countries

## SEC 5:69:1 | Battery chemistries
### Types and uses
| Battery | Feature | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ★ **Nickel-Cadmium (Ni-Cd)** | rechargeable; nickel oxide hydroxide + metallic cadmium electrodes; **KOH** alkaline electrolyte | calculators, cordless appliances, transistors, portable power tools |
| **Zinc-carbon** dry cell | generally **non-rechargeable** | **torches and clocks** |
| **Lead-acid** | — | **automotive starting and UPS** |
| **Flow batteries** | — | **grid-scale** storage |
| **NMC** | **nickel-manganese-cobalt** cathode (not LFP) | — |
- Ni-Cd use declined because **cadmium** is **toxic**
### Sodium-ion
- Promoted as a **safer, mineral-light alternative** (not a universal replacement for pumped storage)
- Volumetric energy density still **below LFP**
### Grid storage bids
- Remaining **10 GWh** put to global bids in **July 2026** for **grid-scale storage**

## SEC 5:69:2 | Non-battery storage
| Storage | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Compressed air** | air compressed into **caverns**, later expanded through **turbines** |
| **Flywheel** | **rotational kinetic energy** |
| **Pumped storage** | **pumping water uphill** |
| **Molten salt** | **thermal storage** in **concentrated solar power** plants; generation **after sunset** |
- **Pinnapuram** project (**Greenko**): integrates **solar, wind and pumped storage**
- Storage a bottleneck because it **balances variable renewable generation** and supports **24×7** renewable power
- ACC programme hurdles: **visa delays**, **local-content rules**, **missing technology** (not shortage of pumped-storage reservoirs)

# ROW 5:73 | Environment - components; ecology, ecosystem, food chain and food web, energy flow, ecological pyramids
## SEC 5:73:0 | Levels of organisation
### Levels
- Order: **organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere**
- **Community** — all populations of different species in an area
- **Biome** — large region defined by **climate** and **dominant vegetation**
- **Ecosystem** — **basic functional unit of ecology** (biotic community + non-living surroundings linked by energy flow and nutrient cycling)
- **Ecology** studies relationships between organisms and environment; an ecosystem is a functional unit of organisms and environment
- Ecosystems **open for energy and matter**; **Earth** open for energy, **nearly closed for matter**
### Population
- Population attributes: **density, mortality, age structure, sex ratio** (not dominant vegetation)
- **Density** — individuals per unit area; **mortality** — deaths per unit time; **natality** — births
| Age pyramid | Population |
|---|---|
| **Triangular** | **expanding** |
| **Bell-shaped** | **stable** |
| **Urn-shaped** | **declining** |
### Niche
- **Niche** — functional role ("profession") of a species; **unique** to each species
- **Grinnell** — niche concept (**Tansley** coined "ecosystem")
- **Fundamental niche** — full range of conditions and resources usable **in absence of other species**
- **Koala** (eucalyptus) — **specialist** with **narrow niche breadth**
- **Resource partitioning** — competing species coexist using **different parts or forms of a shared resource**
### Species roles
- **Keystone**: sea star **Pisaster ochraceus** controls **mussels** in intertidal zones (**Robert Paine**)
- **Foundation** species create or shape habitat — **coral**, **kelp**
- **Flagship** — public support through **charisma**; **umbrella** — wide-ranging, protection benefits many species
### Ecotones and ecosystem types
- **Ecotone** — transition zone between two ecosystems (e.g. forest and grassland); **lentic** = still water; **sere** = succession stage
- **Edge effect** — ecotone has **more species** and **higher density** of some species; such organisms = **edge species**
- **Crop field** — artificial / man-modified ecosystem; city an extreme artificial example
- **Rivers** — freshwater; **coral reefs, hydrothermal vents, tidal zones** — marine
- **Anthropogenic biome (anthrome)** — human-altered terrestrial biosphere; nutrient movement = **biogeochemical cycle**
- **Habitat fragmentation** — continuous habitat broken into small isolated patches
- India's first guide to **Open Natural Ecosystems** — launched at **UNCCD COP17, Ulaanbaatar**
### Other
- Fastest **decomposition**: detritus rich in **nitrogen and sugars**, **warm and moist** climate (lignin, chitin, cold, anaerobic slow it)
- **Gloger's rule** — climate and **colouration** (warm humid → darker)
- **Hydrophyte** — water; **xerophyte** — desert; **halophyte** — saline; **epiphyte** — on other plants
- **Biodiversity hotspot** (Myers; Conservation International): at least **1,500 endemic vascular plants** and lost at least **70%** of original habitat — hence rich and threatened

## SEC 5:73:2 | Ecosystem: components
### Environment
- **Physical** component: **nutrients, water, air**; chemical interactions = carbon and nitrogen cycles
- **Cultural** environment — man-made: social, economic, **political institutions**
- Primary functions of environment: **providing resources**, **assimilating wastes**, **habitat for biodiversity** (not generating synthetic chemicals)
### Structure and function
- **Structure**: species composition, trophic levels, abiotic materials
- **Function**: **nutrient cycling**, **energy flow** — underlie productivity and services
- Ecosystem is an **open system** (abiotic parts, producers, consumers, decomposers); a **functional unit** because biotic and abiotic parts interact
- Ecosystem **cycles nutrients**, has **its own productivity**, no fixed universal boundary
- **Biogeochemical cycle** — nutrients move through **biotic** (living) and **abiotic** (non-living) components
- **Abiotic factor** — any **non-living** component affecting organisms (light, temperature, water, soil)
- **Edaphic** = soil (texture, pH, minerals); **climatic** = light, temperature, rainfall; **topographic** = altitude, slope, aspect
### Producers, consumers, decomposers
- ★ **Producers** (autotrophs, green plants) — **intermediaries between biotic and abiotic** components
- Aquatic producers: **phytoplankton**, algae, submerged/floating plants; **zooplankton** are primary consumers
- **Chemoautotrophic bacteria** in deep ocean trenches oxidise **hydrogen sulphide**
- **Decomposers** link biotic and abiotic: convert dead matter into **inorganic nutrients** reused by producers
- Main decomposers: **saprophytic fungi and bacteria**; **Lactobacillus** — curd; **Rhizobium**, **Anabaena** — nitrogen fixation
- **Saprotrophs** — feed on **dead matter**
- **Trophic levels**: producers 1st, **herbivores 2nd**, **secondary consumers 3rd**, top carnivores 4th
- Ecological pyramids: **base = producers**, **apex = top consumers**; types: number, biomass, energy
### Ecosystems
- ★ **Ocean** — largest ecosystem; marine ecosystem covers about **71%** of Earth's surface
- **Lentic** (still): ponds, lakes, swamps; **lotic** (flowing): springs, rivers, streams
- Open oceans and shallow seas — **marine**, not freshwater
- **Taiga**: **slow decay, thick litter, acidic soil**; tropical rainforest: fast decay, thin poor soil
- Desert flora: deep roots, small/spiny leaves, waxy cuticles (not broad thin leaves)
- Lake zones: **littoral** — shallow, light reaches bottom, rooted plants; **profundal** — deep, dark, below limnetic, decomposers
- **Kolleru** — freshwater lake between Godavari and Krishna deltas; brackish lagoon on AP–TN border = **Pulicat**
### Associations and plant types
- **Protists** — unicellular **eukaryotes** of water and moist places
- **Mycorrhiza** — fungi + plant roots; increases **mineral and water absorption**
- **Lichen** — **fungus + algae or cyanobacteria**; indicator of **air pollution** (sensitive to **SO₂**)
- **Phreatophytes** — deep roots to **groundwater**; **hydrophytes** — buoyant **aerenchyma**
- **Halophytes** — **salt-tolerant**; xerophytes reduce water loss
- **Pitcher plants** — **nitrogen-poor soil**; trap and digest insects

## SEC 5:73:3 | Food chains
- **Photoautotrophs** — **green plants, algae, cyanobacteria**; **chemoautotrophs** — chemical oxidation (many archaea)
- **Detritus** food chain begins with **dead organic matter** (e.g. **leaf litter → earthworms → birds**); grazing chain begins with **living green plants**
- Aquatic chain: **phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → large fish**
- First trophic level: phytoplankton, seaweeds, green plants (not **zooplankton**, which are 2nd)
- Aquatic **biomass pyramid inverted**: phytoplankton small standing crop but reproduce and are consumed rapidly
- **DDT** — **thinning of bird eggshells**, crashes in **seabird** populations
- **Fig and fig wasp** — **coevolved mutualism**; each fig species pollinated by its own specific **wasp**
- **Jordan's rule** — temperature and **number of vertebrae in fish** (colder → more)
- **Lithophytes** — grow on **rocks**
- **Azotobacter** (free-living) — **nitrogen fixation**
- Carbon cycle: CO₂ taken up by **photosynthesis**; released by respiration, decay, combustion

## SEC 5:73:4 | Energy flow (one-way)
### Energy and carbon
- **Energy flow** is **unidirectional**, obeys laws of thermodynamics; nutrients, not energy, cycle
- About **10%** of energy passes to next level, rest lost as **heat** → energy decreases upward
- Food chains rarely exceed **4–5 trophic levels**: about **90%** energy lost per transfer (respiration, excretion, locomotion, heat)
- ★ CO₂ added to carbon cycle by **respiration**, **decay of organic matter**, **volcanic action** (and burning fossil fuel); **photosynthesis** removes it
- Ecological pyramids give no place to **saprophytes and decomposers**; cannot place one species at two levels
### Organisms
- ★ Herbivores: some **turtles** (green turtle), some **fish** (parrot fish), some **marine mammals** (dugong); some **snakes** are **viviparous** (green anaconda)
- ★ **Detritivores**: **earthworms, millipedes, woodlice** (feed on dead organic matter)
- **Decomposition** sequence: **fragmentation → leaching → catabolism → humification → mineralisation**
- Principal decomposers: **bacteria and fungi** (enzymes do catabolism); earthworms, nematodes, arthropods are helpers
### Environment and population
- **Physical** environment: lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere; **microbes** are biotic
- **Environment** may exist without life; a **habitat** always has life; **all habitats are environments**
- **J-curve** — exponential growth (dN/dt = rN); levelling off at **K** = **S-curve**
- **Weeds**, insects — **r-selected**
- **Parasites and symbionts** — **biotic** components
- **Liebig's Law of the Minimum** — growth limited by nutrient in **shortest supply**; **Shelford's law of tolerance** — too little or too much limits
### Interactions
- **Tiger–deer** — **predation**
- Large tree shading small plant — **amensalism (-/0)**; lichen — mutualism; cattle egret near cattle — **commensalism**
### Ecosystems
- Terrestrial: tundra, forest, grassland, desert, mountain; **estuary** is aquatic
| Water | Salinity |
|---|---|
| **Freshwater** | **< 5 ppt** |
| **Brackish** | **5–35 ppt** |
| **Marine** | **≥ 35 ppt** |
- **Mature forests**: little net oxygen — **respiration balances photosynthesis**; still carbon sinks

## SEC 5:73:5 | Productivity & food web
### Productivity
- ★ **Chlorella** — maximum efficiency converting solar to chemical energy
- Primary production efficiency about **1–2%** of incoming solar energy (10% = share passed between levels)
- **NPP = GPP − producer respiration**
- **Secondary productivity** — new organic matter by **consumers** (heterotroph level)
- Highest NPP per unit area: **tropical rainforest** (estuaries, coral reefs, swamps also high); **open oceans** low per unit area
- **Tropical rainforest** — highest terrestrial **species richness**
### Food web and stability
- Complex food web more stable — **more alternative feeding pathways**
- **Lion** — tertiary consumer when eating other carnivores (e.g. wolves), not primary
- **Resistance** — withstanding disturbance; **resilience** — recovering after it
- Most stable: **rainforest** (diverse, complex) vs tundra, desert, monoculture
### Other
- **Lichens** — indicators of **sulphur dioxide** pollution
- **Mangroves**: buffer cyclones and storm surges, reduce coastal erosion, store carbon
- **Phosphorus cycle** begins with **rock weathering**; water cycle driven by solar energy

## SEC 5:73:7 | Exam corner
### Trophic levels
- **Trophic level** — **functional position** of an organism in a food chain; one species may occupy more than one
- Energy transfer between levels about **10%**; flows one way
- "Tiger, grass, snake, frog" — missing **second trophic level** (herbivores)
- Grassland chain: **grass → grasshopper or rabbit → snake or hawk**
### Pollutants
- ★ **DDT** biomagnifies — highest in top level (**snake** among given organisms)
- **Bioaccumulation** — build-up in **one organism** over its life; **biomagnification** — rise at **successive trophic levels**
- **Minamata disease** (Japan) — **methylmercury**
- **Mercury** biomagnifies along food chains

## SEC 5:73:9 | Ecology — levels of organisation and the biosphere
- ★ **Food chain** — order in which organisms **feed upon each other** (e.g. Grasses → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle)
- ★ **Ecosystem** — **community of organisms together with their environment**
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ecological community** | organisms interacting with one another |
| **Biosphere** | Earth inhabited by living organisms |
| **Ecosystem** | community + environment |
| **Biodiversity** | flora and fauna of an area; **range of different species** in an environment |
- ★ Biodiversity measured as **alpha, beta, gamma**
- ★ **Carrying capacity** — maximum population an environment can sustain indefinitely; **waste generation within absorption capacity**

## SEC 5:73:10 | Habitat vs niche
- ★ **Niche** — **functional characteristics** of a species in its habitat; **unique** to a species, while many species share a habitat
- ★ **Niche** — full range of biotic and abiotic resources an organism uses **free from interference of other species**
- ★ **Ecological niche** — not only physical space but also **functional role** in the community
  - habitat niche (where it lives), food niche (what it eats)
- ★ Niche = range of conditions tolerated, resources used, functional role; **each species has a distinct niche**

## SEC 5:73:11 | Ecologists → contributions
- ★ **Ecological niche** concept — **Joseph Grinnell** ("ultimate distributional unit" of species and subspecies)

## SEC 5:73:13 | Population attributes — terms → meaning
- ★ **Natality** — **birth** of individuals in a population; rate expressed **per unit population** per unit time (not per female)
- ★ **Carrying capacity** — resource extraction **not above regeneration rate**; wastes **within assimilating capacity**

## SEC 5:73:14 | Ecosystem — concept, structure vs function, open vs closed
- ★ Producer system — **green plants** (autotrophs, photosynthesis); primary producers

## SEC 5:73:15 | Biogeochemical cycles — carbon, water, phosphorus
- ★ Carbon and energy needs of autotrophs met by **photosynthesis**; light energy → **chemical energy**
- ★ Driving force of an ecosystem — **solar energy**; energy flow **unidirectional**
- ★ Marine food chain: **Diatom → Crustaceans → Herrings**
- **Biogeochemical cycle** moves **chemical elements** (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus); energy does not cycle

## SEC 5:73:17 | Abiotic factors — types and limiting-factor laws
- ★ **Edaphic** — concerns **soil** (drainage, texture, pH)
- ★ **Xeric** condition — **low humidity**, inadequate water (arid, extremely cold habitats, salt marshes)
- ★ Land plants and animals — **terrestrial** organisms/habitats

## SEC 5:73:18 | Biotic components — producers, consumers, autotroph types
- ★ Energy flow: **Sunlight → Producers → Herbivores → Carnivores**
- ★ Photosynthesis with invisible (infrared) light — some **bacteria** (e.g. **green sulphur bacteria**, deep sea)
- ★ **Frogs** are **cold-blooded** (also snakes, lizards, fish, crocodile)
- ★ Most of Earth's **oxygen** produced by **algae**; about **90%** of photosynthesis by aquatic plants and algae (**85%** in sea)
- **Macroconsumers** = **phagotrophs** (ingest food); **microconsumers** = **saprotrophs** (decompose organic matter)

## SEC 5:73:19 | Decomposers and decomposition — steps and factors
- ★ **Decomposition** — breakdown of organic matter by **microorganisms** (bacteria, fungi) into simpler inorganic substances

## SEC 5:73:21 | Food chains and trophic levels — Trophic levels
- ★ **First trophic level**: ocean **phytoplankton**, **bacteria in active volcanoes**, **seaweeds**
- ★ Trophic level-I — **green plants** (autotrophs/producers)
- ★ Levels: I producers, II primary consumers (herbivores), III secondary (carnivores), IV tertiary (top carnivores); highest — **carnivores**
- ★ **Biomagnification** — increasing concentration of a substance (e.g. **pesticides**) at successively higher levels
- ★ **Marine phytoplankton and photosynthetic bacteria** produce about **50%** of world's oxygen; oceans produce more than rainforests

## SEC 5:73:22 | Food webs and ecosystem stability — terms

## SEC 5:73:23 | Energy flow — productivity and ten percent law — Nature of energy flow
- ★ **Secondary productivity** — rate of new organic matter by **consumers**
- ★ **Primary productivity** — rate solar energy is stored in green plants as food
- ★ Green plants capture about **1%** of sunlight on leaves; about **10%** passes to next level (**Lindeman's 10% law**)
- ★ Energy during transfer between levels **decreases**
- ★ Plants make food by **photosynthesis**; sunlight energy → **chemical energy**

## SEC 5:73:24 | Energy flow — productivity and ten percent law — Ten percent law
- ★ CO₂ added to carbon cycle by **volcanic action, respiration, decay of organic matter** (not photosynthesis)
- ★ Most stable ecosystem — **marine ecosystem** (unchanged over long periods)

## SEC 5:73:25 | Ecological pyramids — types, inverted cases, limitations
- ★ Trophic levels expressed in **ecological pyramids**
- ★ **Biomass** — total weight of all organisms in a unit area or volume
- ★ Biomass pyramid **inverted** in **pond** and **marine** ecosystems (fish biomass exceeds phytoplankton); gradual decrease in grassland and forest
- ★ **Marine** ecosystem is **not** a sub-category of **terrestrial** (aquatic, marine, wetlands are non-terrestrial)
- ★ **Fewer trophic levels** do not increase biodiversity

## SEC 5:73:26 | Biomagnification vs bioaccumulation — terms and examples
- ★ **DDT** — **non-biodegradable** pollutant (insecticide, organic halogen; disrupts nervous system)
- Biomagnifying pollutants: **persistent, fat-soluble, non-biodegradable**
- **Secondary pollutants** (formed in air): **PAN** and **smog**; **CO, SO₂, NOx** are primary
