# ROW 3:8 | Objectives of Indian planning; types of planning (imperative, indicative, perspective, rolling)
## SEC 3:8:0 | Planning Commission vs NDC
### Planning Commission
- **Planning Commission** set up **15 March 1950**; modelled on the **Soviet Gosplan**
- First Deputy Chairman — **Gulzarilal Nanda**; last — **M.S. Ahluwalia**
- **Deputy Chairman** — de facto head; **Cabinet Minister** rank; attended Cabinet without a vote
- Composition: **PM**, Finance and Planning Ministers ex officio, expert members, a Member-Secretary
  - **States / Governors** were **not** represented
### NDC
- **National Development Council** set up in **1952** (not 1950)
- NDC — **extra-constitutional** apex body that finally **approved** the Five Year Plans
### Planning after the Commission
- **NITI Aayog** — **bottom-up** planning, **cooperative federalism**; a **think tank**, not a fund allocator
- NITI Aayog has **no financial powers**; shift to **cooperative and competitive federalism**
- First Plan (from **1 April 1951**) — highest priority to **agriculture** (foodgrain imports, inflation)
- Multi-level planning: NITI Aayog (Centre) → Planning Boards (States) → **District Planning Committee** (district) → GPDPs (village)
- **73rd and 74th Amendments** — restored the role of rural and urban **local bodies** in development planning

## SEC 3:8:1 | Objectives
- **Economic growth** — the **principal** objective; modernisation, self-reliance, social justice are subsidiary / complementary
- **Socialistic pattern of society** — adopted at **Avadi Congress (1955)** → reflected in **Second Plan** and **IPR 1956**
- **Harrod-Domar** logic (First Plan): growth depends on the **savings rate** and the **capital-output ratio**

## SEC 3:8:2 | Plan → thrust
### Plans and their thrust

| Plan | Thrust |
|---|---|
| First | agriculture, irrigation, power; achieved **3.6%** vs target **2.1%** |
| Second | **rapid industrialisation**, socialistic pattern |
| Third | self-reliance, food self-sufficiency |
| Fourth (1969) | **target-group approach** begins (SFDA, MFAL, DPAP); weaker sections |

| Sixth | **poverty alleviation**, **Minimum Needs Programme**; IRDP |
| Ninth | Seven Basic Minimum Services |
| Twelfth | faster, sustainable, more inclusive growth |

### Planning terms
- **Deficit financing** — borrowing from the **RBI** (money creation); other sources: foreign aid, domestic savings (market borrowing, PSU surpluses)
- **Physical planning** — allocation in real terms (materials, manpower, output targets); money terms = **financial planning**
- **Rolling plan** concept — **Gunnar Myrdal**; Janata government ran it **April 1978 – March 1980**

## SEC 3:8:3 | Imperative vs indicative
- **Imperative planning** — binding **quotas** and allocation; **indicative** planning only guides the private sector
- Imperative planning examples — **USSR under Stalin** (from **1928**) and **China**; indicative — mixed economies, market guidance
- India shifted to **indicative** planning from the **Eighth Plan (1992–97)**, after the **1991 reforms**

## SEC 3:8:4 | Other types
- **Trickle-down approach** — First to Third Plans; growth benefits assumed to reach the poor
- **Decentralised planning** — decisions distributed to **lower governments / private units** within a broad policy; centralised = one authority
- **Perspective planning** — long-term, **15–20 years** or more; five-year and annual plans carved out of it
- Perspective plan **1985–2000** accompanied the **Seventh Plan**

## SEC 3:8:5 | Rolling plan (1978-80)
- ★ **Rolling plan** — suggested by **Gunnar Myrdal** for backward countries, in **"Indian Economic Planning in its Broader Setting"**
- ★ Consists of a plan for the **current year** (incl. annual budget), a plan for a **fixed number of years** (3, 4 or 5), revised **every year**, and a **perspective plan**
- Revised **every year** and **extended by one year** — flexibility in targets
- Criticism: yearly revision **weakened commitment** to targets

# ROW 3:9 | Plan models - Harrod-Domar, Mahalanobis, wage-goods (Vakil-Brahmananda), Gandhian and Bombay plans
## SEC 3:9:0 | Models compared

| Model | Plan | Bottleneck / priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Harrod-Domar** (g = s/k) | First | limited aggregate **savings** |
| **Feldman-Mahalanobis** | Second | scarcity of **capital goods**; heavy industry |
| **Wage-goods** | — | supply of **food** and essential consumer goods |
| Dhar | Fifth | — |

- **Mahalanobis** strategy — **unbalanced growth** centred on heavy industry; import substitution, self-reliance, dominant public sector
  - neglected agriculture, consumer goods, **exports** (no export promotion)
- **Wage-goods model** — **C.N. Vakil** and **P.R. Brahmananda** (1956); prioritised agriculture and wage goods
- **Janata** alternative strategy — agriculture, rural development, employment, **small and cottage industries**, minimum needs

## SEC 3:9:1 | Harrod-Domar logic
- Growth rises with the **savings rate**, falls with the **capital-output ratio**
- **COR** — capital needed per unit of output
  - **low COR** → **less** capital per unit, capital more productive, higher growth potential
  - **high COR** → investment yields **relatively less output**; high savings may still give slow growth
- ★ Low growth in India despite high savings and capital formation — **high capital-output ratio**

## SEC 3:9:2 | Mahalanobis model
- Aimed to raise investment from **7% to 11%** of national income
- Assumed a **closed economy**; neglected agriculture
- Second Plan growth about **4.2%** vs target **4.5%**
- Second Plan steel plants — **Durgapur, Bhilai, Rourkela**; Chittaranjan — locomotives; Perambur — coaches
- Wage-goods model book — **"Planning for an Expanding Economy"** (Vakil and Brahmananda, 1956)

## SEC 3:9:3 | Plans before First Plan

- ★ Bombay Plan — **not** Rajaji
- Bombay Plan — significant **State role**, public sector, protection of infant industries; **not** a non-interventionist State
- Gandhian Plan — decentralisation, Swadeshi, Sarvodaya, **Trusteeship**
- Dalal's Planning and Development Department — 1944
- ★ JP drafted the Sarvodaya Plan on Gandhian ideals; from **1954** devoted to the Sarvodaya Movement
- **Ram Rajya** — rule on **moral authority** and justice for the poorest; not a Hindu state
- **Solow-Swan** — neoclassical model on a **Cobb-Douglas** production function; no Indian Plan based on it

# ROW 3:11 | Overall achievements and failures of planning in India; end of the plan era (2017)
## SEC 3:11:0 | Achievements vs failures
- Achievements: savings, investment, food security, scientific manpower
- **Regional disparities** persisted — a failure, not an achievement
- **Land reforms** — poorly implemented; unequal land ownership persisted; income inequality **widened**

## SEC 3:11:1 | Growth record
- Plans I–XI averaged about **4.5%**, vs roughly **1%** before Independence

## SEC 3:11:2 | Third Plan → Plan Holiday
- Third Plan growth **2.2%** vs target **5.6%**; hit by the **1962 and 1965 wars** and **1965-66 droughts**

## SEC 3:11:3 | Fourth Plan: Gadgil strategy

- Targets: agriculture **5%** a year; exports about **7%** a year

- After **1991** — **indicative** planning: signals priority sectors, investment largely private; planning not a constitutional requirement
- **NDC** — established **1952** (Planning Commission 1950); now inactive; functions passed to **NITI Aayog's Governing Council**; formal abolition pending
- **Abolition of the Planning Commission** ended discretionary **plan grants**; NITI Aayog has no comparable financial powers

# ROW 3:12 | NITI Aayog - structure, mandate, verticals, differences from the Planning Commission
## SEC 3:12:0 | Basics
### Status and dates
- **NITI** — **National Institution for Transforming India**; non-constitutional, non-statutory
- Set up **1 January 2015** by a **Cabinet resolution**; **replaced** the Planning Commission (how it was made does not explain why)
- **Planning Commission** — Cabinet resolution, **15 March 1950**; first chairman PM **Jawaharlal Nehru**; never statutory or constitutional (not Patel / Rajendra Prasad)
  - Finance Commission — set up every five years
- First Vice-Chairman — **Arvind Panagariya**; first CEO — **Sindhushree Khullar**
### Mandate and approach
- Four mandate areas: knowledge and innovation, NITI for States, driving transformational change, **monitoring and evaluation**
- **No fund-allocation** power (that was the Planning Commission)
- Priorities: **Antyodaya**, small enterprises, inclusive development — not exclusive preference for large enterprises
- **Cooperative federalism** + **bottom-up** approach; State rankings → **competitive federalism**
### DMEO and SETU
- **DMEO** — **Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office**; set up **18 September 2015** by merging **PEO** and **IEO**
- NILERD (formerly IAMR) — separate autonomous institute
- **SETU** Steering Committee — chaired by NITI Aayog's **CEO** (not Vice-Chairperson)
- **Governing Council** — chaired by the **PM**; UTs with legislatures (**Delhi, Puducherry**) by **Chief Ministers**, other UTs by **Lieutenant Governors**
