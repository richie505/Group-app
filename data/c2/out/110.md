# ROW 5:74 | Biogeochemical cycles, ecological succession, ecosystem services
## SEC 5:74:0 | Types of cycles
- **Gaseous** cycles — **carbon**, **nitrogen**
- **Sedimentary** cycles — **phosphorus**, **sulphur**, **potassium**
- **Denitrification** — converts **nitrate** into **nitrogen gas**, returning N to the atmosphere
  - Bacteria: **Pseudomonas**, **Thiobacillus**
- **Wetlands** — **microclimate regulators**: stored water evaporates and holds heat, moderating local temperature and humidity

## SEC 5:74:1 | Cycle basics
### Carbon cycle
- **Photosynthesis** — does not add CO2 (it removes CO2)
- Processes that **add CO2**: **respiration**, **decomposition**, **fossil-fuel combustion**, **volcanic activity**
- Processes that **remove CO2**: photosynthesis, dissolution in oceans, carbonate sedimentation, silicate weathering
### Reservoirs
- **Nitrogen cycle** — principal reservoir is the **atmosphere** (N2 about **78%** of air), not soil
- **Phosphorus cycle** — **no atmospheric phase**; weathering releases P from rocks, much ends in **ocean sediments**
### Ecosystem links
- Removal of **decomposers** obstructs mineral movement — nutrients in dead matter not returned to soil
- **Wetlands** act as a **sponge** — store floodwater, recharge aquifers, sustain lean-season flow
- **Chemical pesticides** do not invariably increase ecosystem resilience — may alter soils and microbial networks and weaken it
- **Prosopis juliflora** (invasive) — "**ecological first responder**": spreads when **hydrology** and **soil chemistry** have changed

## SEC 5:74:2 | Nitrogen cycle
- **Ammonification** — organic N in dead matter to **ammonia** (decomposer bacteria and fungi)
- **Nitrification** stages: **Nitrosomonas** (ammonia to nitrite), **Nitrobacter** (nitrite to nitrate)
- **Denitrification** — removes N from soil, returns it to the atmosphere as **N₂**
  - Fixation, ammonification and nitrification add usable N to soil

## SEC 5:74:4 | Succession phases (Clements, 1916)
- Clements' sequence: **nudation** → **invasion** → **competition and co-action** → **reaction** → **stabilisation**
- **Invasion** — immediately follows nudation
- **Ecesis** — successful **establishment** of migrated organisms (invasion phase)
- **Reaction** — most important phase; organisms modify **soil, water, light, temperature**, making site suitable for later species
- **Seasonal succession** — **cyclic**, driven by **environmental rhythms**; not caused by disturbance

## SEC 5:74:5 | Primary vs secondary succession
- **Secondary succession** — generally **faster** than primary, because soil, seeds or roots may remain

## SEC 5:74:6 | Seres & theories
- **Monoclimax** — **Frederic Clements**; **polyclimax** — **Tansley**; **climax-pattern** — **Whittaker**
- **Xerosere** on bare rock: **crustose lichens** → **foliose lichens** → **mosses** → herbs → shrubs → forest
- **Hydrosere**: phytoplankton → submerged rooted → floating → **reed-swamp** → **sedge-meadow** → woodland → forest
- **Psammosere** — begins on **sand**; **lithosere** — bare rock (both sub-types of xerosere)
- **Halosere** — begins on **saline** soil or water

## SEC 5:74:7 | Ecosystem services (MEA 2005)
### MEA categories
| Service | Category |
|---|---|
| Food, water, timber, medicinal resources | **Provisioning** |
| **Soil formation** | **Supporting** |
| Climate regulation, **pollination** | **Regulating** |

- **Cultural services** — hardest to price in money, so their value is underestimated
### Forest services
- **Ecological services** — **flood moderation**, **groundwater recharge**; timber and bamboo are economic products
- AP forests — intangible services: **carbon sink**, water-flow regulation, soil and moisture conservation
### Functions and processes
- **Biodegradation** — **microorganisms** convert organic waste into nutrients for reuse
- **Waste decomposition** — breakdown of organic waste into simpler compounds; supply of drugs/herbal remedies = **medicinal resources**
- **Homeostasis** — maintenance of **ecological balance**
- **Nutrients** are **recycled**; **energy** flows through and is not recycled
- **Climax community** — stable terminal community in equilibrium with **climate**; pioneers colonise first, seral communities are intermediate
- **Phosphate-solubilising microbes** — release **bound phosphorus**
- **Wetlands** — control erosion, **retain sediment**, support nutrient cycling and biodiversity

## SEC 5:74:12 | Biogeochemical cycles — basics and types
- ★ **Nitrogen cycle** — main reservoir not soil but **atmosphere** (nearly **78%** N2)
  - Sulphur, phosphorus, calcium — **sedimentary** cycles (rocks and soil reservoirs)
- ★ **Phosphorus cycle** — **weathering of rocks** is main source; natural reservoir is **rock** (phosphates)
  - P in membranes, nucleic acids, energy transfer; shells, bones, teeth
- ★ **Macronutrients** (major quantities) — **potassium, sodium, magnesium, calcium**, sulphur, phosphorus, chlorine
  - Organisms need at least **27 elements**, **15 metals**

## SEC 5:74:14 | Carbon and nitrogen cycles — processes → agents
- ★ Plants take nitrogen as **nitrate** (atmospheric N2 not directly usable)
- ★ **Gaseous cycles** — nitrogen, oxygen, carbon, water; **H2** is not a gaseous cycle
  - **Sedimentary** cycles (reservoir Earth's crust) — iron, calcium

## SEC 5:74:15 | Nitrogen fixers and biofertilisers → organism → crop
### Blue-green algae (cyanobacteria)
- ★ **Blue-green algae** — biofertiliser for **rice / paddy**; associated with biological N fixation
- ★ Anabaena, Nostoc — fix atmospheric N via **heterocysts** into ammonia
### Azolla
- ★ **Azolla** — aquatic fern biofertiliser in **water-logged rice** fields (Azolla pinnata, high in N)
- ★ Azolla associated with / mixed with **blue-green algae**
### Legumes and nodules
- ★ **Rhizobium japonicum** — N fixation in **soybean**
- ★ Bacteria in legume root nodules — **symbiotic**; roots of **pulses** (legumes)
- ★ **Leghaemoglobin** — in **legume root nodules** (alfalfa, soybean)
- ★ **Boron** — necessary for **nodule formation** in legumes
- ★ Pea family (**Fabaceae / Leguminosae**) — **groundnut**, **horse-gram**, **soybean** all members
- Rhizobium — legumes; Azotobacter — non-leguminous upland crops
### Wetlands
- ★ Wetlands as "**kidneys**" of the Earth — aquatic plants absorb **heavy metals** and excess substances (**water purification**)

## SEC 5:74:16 | Other biofertilisers — mycorrhiza and phosphate solubilisers
- ★ **VAM** (Vascular Arbuscular Mycorrhiza) — **phosphatic** biofertiliser (P uptake)
  - Rhizobium, blue-green algae, Azolla — nitrogen fixation
- **Bacillus megaterium** — **phosphate solubiliser**; **Pseudomonas striata** also a phosphate solubiliser
- VAM aids phosphorus and water uptake, not denitrification; **Nitrobacter** converts nitrite to nitrate

## SEC 5:74:17 | Ecological succession — terms, persons and types
- ★ Grasslands: trees do not replace grasses due to **water limits and fire** (scanty rainfall; dry-season fires)
- ★ Surface without soil — **lichens** and **mosses** survive; ferns need soil
- ★ **Monoclimax theory** — **F.E. Clements**; one climax per region, **climate** the determining factor

## SEC 5:74:18 | Succession — Clements' phases and sere sequences
- ★ **Lichens** — symbiotic association of **algae and fungi**; can start succession on bare rock
- ★ Nitrogen-fixing plants — **alfalfa**, **chickpea**, **clover**
- **Hydroseres** better studied than xeroseres — **lake sediments** preserve a permanent record

# ROW 5:75 | Environmental issues and concerns - land degradation, desertification, deforestation, soil erosion
## SEC 5:75:0 | Key terms
- **Deforestation** — adds to greenhouse effect by reducing forests' **carbon sink** capacity
- **Desertification** — land degradation in **arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid** areas from climatic variation and human activity; not just spread of deserts
- Semi-arid desertification causes — **overgrazing** and **deforestation**
- **Tragedy of the Commons** — over-use of a shared pasture; **Garrett Hardin**, **1968**
- Modern agriculture effects — **soil degradation, pollution, ecological imbalance**
- **UNCCD COP14** (New Delhi, **2019**) — India raised restoration target to **26 Mha by 2030** (from **21 Mha**, Paris 2015)
- **PMKSY** Watershed Development Component — **ridge to valley** approach

## SEC 5:75:1 | How land degrades
- **Overgrazing** — strips **vegetation cover**
- **Water erosion** — removes topsoil, causes sedimentation; **wind erosion** — blows topsoil away in dry areas
- **Salinisation** and **waterlogging** — **poor drainage** and **over-irrigation** (incl. delta regions)

### CPCB
- **Central Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution** (1974) — renamed **CPCB** by the **1988** amendment to the Water Act

### Common provisions and APPCB
- Water Act and Air Act both require **prior consent** of the Board to establish and operate a polluting unit
- **APPCB** — set up **1976** under the Water Act

## SEC 5:76:1 | Water Act vs Air Act
- **Water Act, 1974** — India's first **comprehensive pollution-control law**; standards for **discharge of effluents**
- **Air Act** amended **1987** (not 1991) — added **noise** to definition of "air pollutant"
- **State Government** declares **air pollution control areas** — Chapter IV, **Sections 19–31** of Air Act; a special power of the Air Act, not the Water Act
- **CPCB** function — **coordinating SPCBs**; SPCB functions — inspection, sample analysis, granting consents
- Environmental compensation — **Section 31A** (Air Act), **Section 33A** (Water Act); upheld, but only when harm is **clear or imminent**

## SEC 5:76:2 | EPA 1986: umbrella law
### Water Act and Cess Act
- **Water Cess Act** — **1977**; cess on water consumed by specified industries and local authorities; subsumed into **GST** in **2017**
- Water Act — under **Article 252**; applies in States that **adopt it** by resolution
- Air Act used the **same board structure** — existing CPCB given air functions; SPCBs enforce in States
- CPCB works at **national** level; SPCBs at State level
- **CPCB chairperson** — nominated by **Central Government**; **SPCB chairperson** — by **State Government**
- **CTE** (before setting up) and **CTO** (before production) — granted by **State Pollution Control Board**
### EPA 1986
- Purpose — **protect and improve the environment** and control pollution
- Basis — **Article 253** (laws to implement international agreements); gives effect to **Stockholm Conference, 1972**
- Background — **Bhopal Gas Tragedy** (1984); called **umbrella legislation**
- Centre's powers include regulating **location of industries**, standards, hazardous waste, public health
- **Section 3(3)** — authorities, e.g. **National Ganga River Basin Authority** (2009)
- **Section 5** — directions; **Section 15** — **penalties**
- **Section 16** — **Environment Protection Fund** (Public Account); fines under Air, Water, EP Acts; **75%** States/UTs, **25%** Centre
- Notified under EPA — **Green Credit Rules**, Eco-Sensitive Zone notifications, Plastic Waste Management Rules
- Original penalty — up to **5 years** or fine **Rs 1 lakh**, plus **Rs 5,000/day** for continuing offence
- **Jan Vishwas Act, 2023** — decriminalised EPA penalty regime; for PLI Act 1991, moved penalty rates to the **rules**
### Institutions, cases, Constitution
- **Department of Environment** 1980 → **MoEF** **1985** → **MoEFCC** **2014**
- **Vanashakti v. Union of India** — ex post facto clearance not available in every case on paying a penalty; **Article 142** only in fit cases
- **Article 48A** — State to protect environment, forests, wildlife; added by **42nd Amendment, 1976**
- **Article 51A(g)** — Fundamental Duty to protect environment and have compassion for living creatures

## SEC 5:77:4 | Public hearing & 2026 changes

### Criticisms and orders

## SEC 5:77:5 | Cases on clearance

- **Rio+5** (**1997**) — UNGA special session, **New York**, reviewed **Agenda 21**
- **WSSD 2002** — Johannesburg Plan of Implementation
- **Rio+20** (**2012**) — "**The Future We Want**"; launched **SDG** process; High-Level Political Forum

### Rio Conventions and bodies
- **UNCCD** (1994) — one of three **Rio Conventions** with UNFCCC and CBD; Secretariat **Bonn**

- **CMS** — **Bonn Convention**; binding treaty under UNEP

## SEC 5:79:1 | CITES appendices
- **Appendix I** — generally **prohibits commercial trade**; e.g. tigers, rhinoceroses, **Indian and Chinese pangolins**
- **Appendix II** — corals, most sharks and rays; **striped hyena**, **rosewood (Dalbergia)**, **golden gecko** (AP-endemic)
- **Appendix III** — **walrus** (protected by **Canada**)
- Sharks and rays — **54** added to App. II at **CoP19**; **whale shark**, **oceanic whitetip**, **manta rays** to App. I at **CoP20**
- **Red sanders** (Pterocarpus santalinus) — endemic to **Seshachalam** and southern Eastern Ghats; IUCN **Endangered**
- **CMS Appendix I** — endangered migratory species, strict protection; App. II — need cooperation agreements
- **KMGBF 2050 goals** — **Goal C** fair sharing of benefits from genetic resources; **Goal D** finance and technology
- **IUCN** — union of governments and NGOs; maintains **Red List**; not a UN organ
