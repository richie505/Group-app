# ROW 3:77 | India and the WTO - agreements, ministerial conferences, public stockholding, fisheries subsidies, appellate body crisis
## SEC 3:77:0 | GATT vs WTO
### Origin
- ★ Earlier name of the WTO — **GATT**
- **GATT** signed at Geneva on **30 October 1947** by **23** founding contracting parties; in force **1948**
- **WTO** came into being on **1 January 1995** (not 1994), under the **Marrakesh Agreement** signed in **1994**
- WTO **replaced** the GATT; it is an international trade organisation (being an organisation does not explain why it replaced GATT)
- WTO wider than GATT: covers **goods, services, intellectual property and investment**; GATT dealt mainly with **goods**
### Organisation
- HQ **Geneva**; Director-General **Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala**
- India — founding member of **both** GATT and WTO
- Members as of April 2026: **166**
  - **123** = Uruguay Round contracting parties (and India was 123rd to accept the Fisheries Subsidies Agreement)
- **MFN** principle — advantage given to one partner must go to **all other WTO partners** (non-discrimination; permitted exceptions apply)
### Agreements and disputes
- Fisheries Subsidies Agreement — adopted at **MC12, Geneva** (June 2022), along with a COVID-related **TRIPS waiver**
- Fisheries Subsidies Agreement — WTO's first agreement with an **environmental-sustainability** objective
- Phase 2 of fisheries talks (overcapacity, overfishing) — not concluded at MC14; **deferred to the General Council** in Geneva
- Trade Facilitation Agreement — **Bali package, MC9 (2013)**; India ratified **2016**
- **DS456** — **solar cells**; India lost to the US (**2016**)
- **DS541** (MEIS, SEZ, EOU) — against India **2019**; ICT panel against India **2023**
- India on plurilaterals (IFD, E-Commerce) — may **fragment the WTO** by weakening consensus

## SEC 3:77:1 | Uruguay Round
- ★ **Arthur Dunkel** — Director-General of **GATT** (1980-1993); at the helm when the Uruguay Round was launched (September 1986)
- **Dunkel Draft** — Dunkel's proposals (as GATT DG and Chair of the Trade Negotiations Committee) for the **Uruguay Round** agreements
- ★ **Uruguay Round** — **8th** GATT round; launched at **Punta del Este** (September 1986), concluded **1993**; **123** contracting parties
- WTO grew out of the Uruguay Round; GATT from **1948**, WTO from **1995**
- Uruguay Round took rules beyond goods through **GATS, TRIPS and TRIMS**

| Agreement | Covers |
|---|---|
| **TRIMS** | trade-related investment measures |
| **TRIPS** | intellectual property rights |
| **GATS** | services |
| **SPS / TBT** | health and technical standards |

- GI protection — India relies on **TRIPS**; not a member of the **Lisbon Agreement** on appellations

## SEC 3:77:3 | AoA boxes
- AoA three pillars — **domestic support, market access, export competition**
- **Blue Box** payments — **exempt** from reduction commitments

## SEC 3:77:4 | Key terms
- **National treatment** — imported product, once inside the market, treated like a similar **domestic** product (internal tax, regulation)
  - equal treatment among foreign partners = **MFN**
- **NAMA-11** — **developing countries** (incl. India) on non-agricultural market access
- **G-90** — African Group, ACP countries and LDCs
- **De minimis** limit — **5%** of production value (developed), **10%** (developing)
- **Tariffication** — converting non-tariff barriers into tariffs; developed countries then cut tariffs **36% over 6 years**
- First to invoke the Peace Clause (rice, **2020**) — **India**

## SEC 3:77:6 | Exam corner

- AoA boxes — **Green, Blue, Amber** only; **Red Box** is not an AoA box (red-light category belongs to SCM)
- India's grievance: MSP procurement for the NFSA counted as **Amber Box** using an **outdated historical reference price**
- **Patents Act amendment, 2005** — introduced **product patents** to comply with TRIPS

## SEC 3:77:7 | WTO and GATT — origin and GATT vs WTO
### GATT
- ★ **GATT** — **General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade**; signed **30 October 1947** by **23** countries at Geneva
- ★ Tariff concessions in effect by **30 June 1948** through a "Protocol of Provisional Application"
- ★ GATT office established — **Geneva, 1948**
- ★ **MFN** clause — concessions granted to one nation must be given to **all** other members ("most favour to all countries")
### WTO basics
- ★ WTO established — **1995** (1 January 1995)
- ★ Created by the **Uruguay Round** (1986-93), 8th round of multilateral trade negotiations under GATT
- ★ HQ — **Geneva** (also ILO – Geneva; UNO – New York; FAO – Rome)
- ★ WTO — the main **"security guard" of international trade**; largest intergovernmental economic organisation regulating trade
- ★ WTO aims — promotion of **free trade** and resource flows, protection of **intellectual property rights**, managing **balanced trade** (all listed aims correct)
### Membership and leadership
- ★ **Nepal** — **147th** member, **23 April 2004**
- ★ **Serbia** — not a member; has **observer** status
- ★ **Roberto Azevedo** (Brazil) — replaced **Pascal Lamy** on **1 September 2013**; **6th** Director-General; second term 2017
### Terms in the news
- ★ **Agreement on Agriculture**, **SPS Agreement**, **Peace Clause** — all WTO terms
- ★ **Special Safeguard Mechanism** — WTO tool letting **developing countries** raise tariffs temporarily against import surges or price falls
- ★ AoA (negotiated in the Uruguay Round, in force 1 January 1995) — three pillars do **not** include **anti-dumping and compensatory duties**
- ★ **TRIMs** — quantitative restrictions on imports by foreign investors **prohibited**; apply to investment measures related to trade in **goods** (not services)
- ★ US threatened to seek WTO sanctions against developing countries for non-observance of **ILO conventions** (true); US itself unconcerned with labour standards

## SEC 3:77:8 | Uruguay Round, WTO basics and FIRSTs
- ★ **TRIPS** — Trade-Related Aspects of **Intellectual Property** Rights; minimum standards for IPR protection
- ★ **TRIMs** — **Trade-Related Investment Measures**; negotiated in the Uruguay Round
- ★ **Dunkel draft** (December **1991**) — Uruguay Round; linked to **intellectual property** rights (GATT ambit widened to services, IPR)
- ★ **Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala** — **Nigeria** (former Finance Minister); **7th** DG; **first woman** DG (appointed February 2021, took over March 2021)
- ★ WTO **does have** a way to settle trade disputes — members use the multilateral dispute-settlement system
- ★ **EXIM Policy 2002-07** — liberal, market-oriented; GATT/WTO played a role in liberalisation (both true, not cause and effect)
- ★ **BTIA** — India and the **European Union**; talks began **June 2007**, Brussels

## SEC 3:77:9 | Ministerial Conferences → venue, year, outcome; Doha Round and country groups — Ministerial Conferences
- ★ **Doha Round** — central issue **agriculture**; launched at the **4th** Ministerial Conference
- ★ **MC13** — **Abu Dhabi**, UAE, **26 February – 2 March 2024**
- ★ Ministerial Conference — WTO's **highest decision-making** body (trade ministers of members)

## SEC 3:77:10 | Ministerial Conferences → venue, year, outcome; Doha Round and country groups — MC14 (Yaounde, 2026) - other outcomes
- ★ **NAMA-11** — group of **developing countries** in NAMA (Non-Agricultural Market Access) talks; seeks flexibilities to limit opening in industrial goods
- ★ **Dunkel draft** (Uruguay Round) — seen as the last chance for a breakthrough in the stalled round
  - per key: acceptance mandatory for India in all sectors; agriculture — cut in agricultural subsidies
  - textiles: did **not** reiterate the **Multi-Fibre Arrangement**

## SEC 3:77:11 | Terms → meaning: MFN, AoA boxes, de minimis, tariffication, Peace Clause — Trade principles
- ★ **Amber, Blue, Green Box** — WTO **Agreement on Agriculture** terms; classify domestic farm subsidies by trade distortion
- ★ Traffic-light colours: **green** (permitted), **amber** (to be reduced), **red** (forbidden); AoA has **no red box**
- ★ **Red box** — not an AoA subsidy box
- ★ **Blue box** — **production-limiting** subsidies, allowed despite being trade-distorting (unlike amber)
- ★ Difference between MSP and market price paid directly to farmers — **Blue box** subsidy (per key)
- ★ AoA: developed countries to cut export subsidy by **36% (value)** or **21% (volume)** over **six years** (from 1986-90 levels)
  - India is a **developing country**, but the claim that it need not reduce subsidies is **false**
- ★ India **does import apples** from the USA (after six WTO disputes settled, PM's US visit **June 2023**)
- ★ Indian law prohibits import of **GM food** without approval of the competent authority

## SEC 3:77:13 | WTO agreements → subject, year and India's compliance
- ★ Food safety standards for dispute settlement — WTO works with the **Codex Alimentarius Commission** (under the SPS Agreement)
  - CAC created **1963** by **FAO and WHO**

- ★ **Nizamabad black clay pottery** (Azamgarh, UP) — GI tag **2015**

- ★ **164** members from **29 July 2016**; **Afghanistan** 164th
- ★ Indian **Patents Act** came into force **1972**; based on the **Justice N. Rajagopala Ayyangar Committee** (1957)

# ROW 3:78 | FDI and FPI policy in India - routes, sectoral caps, trends; FTAs and CEPAs signed by India
## SEC 3:78:0 | FDI vs FPI
- **FDI** — generally **long-term**; may bring management control and technology
- **FPI** — liquid, potentially short-term **"hot money"**
- **FII** — a form of **portfolio investment**; FDI is direct investment in enterprises
- **SEBI FPI Regulations, 2019** — brought **FIIs, sub-accounts and QFIs** together under the FPI framework

## SEC 3:78:1 | Forms of foreign capital
### What counts as what
- Listed company: holding of **10% or more** = FDI; below 10% = portfolio (Arvind Mayaram Committee threshold)
- **FDI** — largely **non-debt**, longer-term investment in Indian enterprises; **more stable** than FPI
- **FPI** — the **more volatile** flow; opened to India in **1992**; regulated by **SEBI** (FPI Regulations, 2019) with the RBI
- Foreign capital includes **FDI, trade credit, NRI deposits and ECBs**
- **ECB** — **debt** capital; FDI and FPI are non-debt
- **ADRs/GDRs** — equity raised abroad via depository receipts; counted as **portfolio investment** in BoP data

| Instrument / case | Classification |
|---|---|
| Participatory Note | **offshore derivative instrument** |
| Wholly owned subsidiary | FDI |
| 5% listed stake | FPI |
| Masala bond | **rupee bond** issued abroad |
### Greenfield vs brownfield
- **Greenfield** — new facility (e.g. **Hyundai, Sriperumbudur**)
- **Brownfield** — acquiring an existing business (e.g. **Vodafone-Hutchison Essar**)
### Routes and P-Notes
- **Automatic route** — **no prior** government approval
- **P-Note** — issued by a **registered FII/FPI** to an overseas investor, giving exposure to Indian shares **without SEBI registration**

## SEC 3:78:2 | Routes & sector caps
- Automatic route — no prior RBI/Government approval; only **post-facto reporting to the RBI**
- Not all sectors on automatic route; **over 90%** are, some still need the **Government route**

| Sector | FDI limit / route |
|---|---|
| Telecom | **100%**, automatic |
| Single-brand retail | 100%, automatic |
| Multi-brand retail | **51%**, Government route only (in practice treated as barred) |
| Defence | **74%** automatic; up to 100% Government route |
| Brownfield pharma | **74%** automatic (approval beyond) |
| Greenfield pharma, medical devices | 100%, automatic |
| Private-sector banks | **74%** (49% automatic) |
| Public-sector banks | 20%, Government route |
| E-commerce marketplace | **100%**, automatic |
### Insurance
- Insurance FDI cap: **26% → 49% (2015) → 74% (2021) → 100% (2025)**
### E-commerce
- FDI only in the **marketplace model**; inventory model generally barred
- **July 2026** relaxation — inventory-based FDI allowed where inventory is used to **export domestically manufactured goods**

## SEC 3:78:3 | Prohibited sectors
- **Prohibited**: lottery business, chit funds, atomic energy; railway **operations** (except permitted activities)
- **Railway infrastructure** — not prohibited; 100% automatic FDI

## SEC 3:78:4 | Policy milestones
- FPIs register with **SEBI**; overseen by SEBI and the RBI
- FDI policy — **negative-list** approach
- **FIPB** abolished **June 2017**; **DPIIT** is the nodal department
- **DPIIT** — under the **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**; its FIF Portal part of the **NSWS** since **August 2022**
- **FEMA, 1999** replaced **FERA, 1973**; **ECBs** regulated by the **RBI under FEMA**
- **Press Note 3** (April 2020) — land-border countries: China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan, Afghanistan; **Sri Lanka not covered**
- March 2026 amendment — from **1 May 2026**, automatic route if Chinese/Hong Kong beneficial ownership is up to **10%** (firm not registered in China, HK or another land-border country)
- **Gross FDI** is not entirely fresh capital — may include **accounting changes**

## SEC 3:78:5 | FDI data
### National
- FY 2024-25: gross FDI **$81 billion**, net FDI only **$353 million**
- FY 2024-25 top sources: **Singapore (30%) > Mauritius (17%) > United States (11%)**

### States
- H1 FY 2025-26 top states: **Maharashtra > Karnataka > Tamil Nadu**
- Google — **$15 billion, 1 GW** AI data centre at **Visakhapatnam**
- AP **Industrial Development Policy 4.0** — targets at least **$10 billion** FDI

## SEC 3:78:6 | FPI & trade deals
- Economic Survey 2025-26: services — **80.2%** of FDI (FY 2023-FY 2025); information and communication **25.8%**
- General Route FPI limit — **6%** of Central and **2%** of State Government securities
- FPI flows driven by **US Fed interest rates** and **global risk appetite**; flight to safety causes outflows

## SEC 3:78:7 | Exam corner
- **IRDAI** — headquarters **Hyderabad**; insurance is in the **Union List**; Budget 2025-26 raised insurance FDI to **100%**

## SEC 3:78:8 | FDI — nature and what counts as FDI
- ★ FDI includes **subsidiaries** of foreign companies in India, **majority foreign equity** holding in Indian companies, companies **exclusively financed** by foreign companies — **not portfolio investment**
- ★ FDI — investment for **lasting interest** and **control** over management
- ★ FDI's major characteristic — **largely non-debt creating** capital flow
- ★ FDI definition: capital instruments by a person resident outside India in an **unlisted** Indian company, or **10% or more** of post-issue paid-up equity (fully diluted) of a **listed** company
- ★ Most volatile flows — **both** Foreign Portfolio Investment and **NRI deposits** (driven by interest-rate variations)

## SEC 3:78:9 | FDI vs FPI and FDI vs FII
- ★ Sector attracting one of the highest FDI shares in the last decade (APPSC) — **Telecom**
- ★ **FII** — increases capital availability **in general**; **FDI** targets **specific sectors**
- ★ FDI more **stable** than FII; FDI brings **management skills and technology**, FII only capital
- ★ FII works mainly in the **secondary market**

## SEC 3:78:10 | P-Notes and classification of foreign capital
- ★ Capital via **ADRs/GDRs** (shares of Indian companies traded abroad) — **Foreign Equity Investment**
- ★ **Participatory Notes** — associated with **Foreign Institutional Investors** (now FPIs)
- ★ P-Notes — **offshore derivative instruments** with Indian stocks as underlying; issued by registered FPIs to overseas investors who invest **without SEBI registration**
- ★ **FDI** — stable; excess **portfolio** investment caused the **East Asian and Latin American** crises
- ★ **FCNR (B)** replaced FCNR (A) on **15 May 1993** — Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) account, a fixed deposit held in foreign currency
- ★ Not FDI — purchase of **bonds or stocks** of a company overseas

## SEC 3:78:11 | Acts, committees, policy bodies and Press Note 3
- ★ Largest forex inflow in **2005-06** — **portfolio investment**
- ★ FII investment in calendar **2005** — about **US $10.7 bn**
- ★ The term FII has since been superseded by FPI

## SEC 3:78:13 | Routes and sectoral caps → limit and route — Sector caps
- ★ Budget **2004-05** raised FDI caps in **Telecom, Insurance, Civil Aviation**; **Banking** cap not revised (odd one)
- ★ Insurance FDI limit raised to **74%** (Union Budget **2021-22**, in effect 19 May 2021)
- ★ **4 October 2012** — Government proposed raising insurance FDI from **26% to 49%**
- ★ FDI policy 2001-02: tea sector — foreign firm to **disinvest 26%** to an Indian partner within **5 years** (not 33%)
- ★ (2003 PYQ) **FDI up to 49% from all sources** permitted in private-sector banks
  - RBI January 2001 new private bank guidelines: initial paid-up capital **₹200 crore**, to rise to **₹300 crore** within three years

## SEC 3:78:14 | E-commerce FDI and prohibited sectors
- ★ **Retail trade** opened to FDI (2010 PYQ)
  - **2006** — up to **51%** FDI in **single-brand** retail (first easing)
  - **2012** — up to **51%** in **multi-brand** retail; **100%** approved in single-brand

## SEC 3:78:15 | Data — FDI flows, sources and sectors
- ★ Highest share in cumulative FDI inflows (2006 PYQ) — **Mauritius**
- ★ **2016-17** FDI equity inflows: **Mauritius > Singapore > Japan**
- ★ **2024-25**: **Singapore > Mauritius > USA**; Singapore now the largest source of equity inflows (Mauritius as per earlier period)
- ★ **Singapore** — largest FDI source in **2023-24**, because the **India-Mauritius tax treaty amendment** cut the appeal of routing via Mauritius (A and R true, R explains A)
- ★ Highest FDI inflows over the last two decades — **services sector**

- ★ FPI limit in outstanding **corporate bonds** — raised to **15%** (from 9%) in Union Budget **2020-21**

# ROW 3:79 | Structure of the Indian financial system - banks, NBFCs, development finance institutions, cooperatives
## SEC 3:79:0 | Money market: organised vs unorganised
### Organised vs unorganised
- Organised: **call money, Treasury bills, commercial bills, certificates of deposit** (also CPs, repos)
- Unorganised: **indigenous bankers and moneylenders**
- Indigenous bankers — **Shroffs, Seths, Multanis, Chettiars**; accept deposits, deal in **hundis**
- Moneylenders — governed by **state money-lending laws**; **Chit Funds Act, 1982** relates to chit funds
### Shadow banking
- **Shadow banking** — bank-like credit intermediation outside full bank regulation (e.g. **NBFCs**, money-market/liquid funds)
### Urban cooperative banks
- UCBs — traditional **dual control**: **State Registrar of Cooperative Societies and RBI**
- **NUCFDC** — umbrella body for UCBs

## SEC 3:79:3 | NBFC vs bank
- NBFCs **cannot issue cheques** drawn on themselves
- NBFCs — no **demand deposits**, no deposit insurance cover, no **LAF** access
- NBFCs do **not** maintain **CRR**; banks keep CRR and SLR; deposit-taking NBFCs keep **liquid assets**

## SEC 3:79:4 | NBFC rules
### Basics
- ★ **Muhammad Yunus** (Grameen Bank, Bangladesh) — pioneer of **microfinance**; Nobel Peace Prize
- Principal business test — financial assets > **50%** of total assets and financial income > **50%** of gross income
- Minimum NOF: **HFC ₹20 crore**; NBFC-MFI and NBFC-ICC **₹10 crore**
### Scale-Based Regulation
- Four layers, rising intensity: **Base → Middle → Upper → Top**
- **Top Layer** — kept empty; for a **sharp rise in systemic risk**
- **Upper Layer** — identified by RBI; bank-like norms for at least **five years**; must **list within three years**
- NBFC-UL list 2026–27 — **17** companies, including **Tata Sons**
### Microfinance
- NBFC-MFI (March 2022 framework) — at least **75%** of assets in microfinance loans; loans are **collateral-free**
- **AP microfinance crisis 2010** — multiple lending, over-indebtedness, coercive recovery, borrower suicides; MFI loan book in AP **collapsed**
- **Regulatory arbitrage** — using differences in regulatory treatment to operate outside the stricter banking framework

## SEC 3:79:5 | Rural cooperative credit (short-term)
- Village short-term credit — **PACS**; district — **DCCBs**; state — **StCBs**
- **PCARDBs** — **long-term** credit at village level
- StCBs and DCCBs — licensed by **RBI**, inspected by **NABARD**; PACS under **State Registrars**

## SEC 3:79:6 | Rural credit & cooperatives
### NABARD
- NABARD took over RBI's **agricultural-credit** functions and **ARDC**'s refinance portfolio (housing refinance is **NHB**'s role)
- NABARD manages **RIDF** — funded by banks' **priority-sector lending shortfalls**; also refinances StCBs, RRBs and land development banks

# ROW 3:80 | Reserve Bank of India - establishment, organisation and functions
## SEC 3:80:0 | Establishment
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1926** | **Hilton Young Commission** |
| **1934** | **RBI Act** passed |
| **1 April 1935** | RBI begins functioning |
| **1937** | Central Office moved **Calcutta → Bombay** |
| **1 January 1949** | **Nationalised** (not 1937) |
- Nationalised under the **RBI (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948**
- NBFCs regulated under **Chapter IIIB** of the RBI Act

## SEC 3:80:1 | Organisation
- RBI seal — **tiger and palm tree** (tiger replaced the lion of the East India Company double-mohur)
- Central Board — **21** members; directors hold office **4 years**; Governor and Deputy Governors up to **5 years**
- First Governor — **Sir Osborne Smith** (1935-37); signed no Indian note (King **Edward VIII** abdicated)
- First Indian Governor — **C. D. Deshmukh** (1943-1949)
- Governor since **December 2024** — **Sanjay Malhotra** (chairs MPC and Payments Regulatory Board); **Shaktikanta Das** 2018-2024
- **Section 26A, BR Act 1949** — deposits unclaimed **ten years or more** go to the **DEA Fund**; depositor's right to claim survives

## SEC 3:80:2 | Functions & legal basis
### Monetary policy
- Primary objective — **price stability**, keeping growth in mind
- **MPC** — **six** members; statutory body under the RBI Act; sets the policy repo rate
- Inflation target — set by the **Finance Ministry in consultation with the RBI**
- **2016** MPC and inflation-targeting law — RBI's **operational independence** on policy rates
### Currency and legal provisions
- **Section 22, RBI Act** — RBI's **sole right to issue banknotes** (except one-rupee note)
- RBI does **not mint coins**; coins and the **₹1 note** issued by the **Government of India** (Ministry of Finance)
- **Section 7, RBI Act** — Centre may give directions in public interest after consulting the **RBI Governor**
- Payment systems — **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**
- Payments banks licensed under **Section 22(1), BR Act 1949**
### Roles
- **Ways and Means Advances** and overdrafts — part of the RBI's **government-banking** function
- **Lender of last resort** — gives banks **emergency liquidity**
- **Banker to banks** — commercial banks keep **deposits and reserves** with the RBI
- **Controller of credit** — controls money and credit through **monetary policy**
- Banker to Government — transactions and **public debt**; also manages **forex reserves**, oversees payment systems

## SEC 3:80:3 | Currency management
### Notes and coins
- **₹1 note** — signed by the **Finance Secretary**; issued by the **Government of India (Ministry of Finance)**; other notes signed by the **RBI Governor**
- **Minimum Reserve System** — **₹200 crore**, of which **₹115 crore** in gold; replaced proportional reserve system after **1956**
- Highest denomination under **Section 24** — **₹10,000**
- Demonetisation power — **Section 26(2)**
- **8 November 2016** — **₹500 and ₹1,000** demonetised
- **₹2,000** note — withdrawn **May 2023**; still **legal tender**
- Notes carry **17** languages: Hindi and English in front, **15** in the language panel
### Organisation and banking regulation
- Four Deputy Governors — **two** from within RBI, one commercial banker, one economist (monetary policy)
- Autonomous institutes — **NIBM, IGIDR, IDRBT**; **Reserve Bank Staff College** is not one
- Branches without prior approval — at least **25%** of new branches in **unbanked rural** areas (also covers SFBs, payments banks, LABs, RRBs)
- **Bank amalgamations** — approved by the **RBI**; RBI can seek winding up of a bank
- RBI income — interest on securities and loans, **forex gains**, fees; **not** income tax collection

## SEC 3:80:4 | Presses & mints
- **Bank Note Press** — **Dewas**; **Currency Note Press** — **Nashik**
- **BRBNMPL** (RBI-owned) presses — **Mysuru** and **Salboni**
- Four Government mints (**Mumbai, Noida, Kolkata, Hyderabad**) — run by **SPMCIL**, not RBI-owned

## SEC 3:80:11 | Functions of RBI — core
- ★ RBI — **banker to the Central Government**; formulates and administers **monetary policy**; **agent** of Government for India's **IMF** membership (all four statements correct)
- ★ Main functions: banker to banks, monetary policy, **debt and cash management** for Central and State Governments
- ★ Not an RBI function — **credit creation** (done by commercial banks; RBI **controls** it)
- ★ **Money and credit** controlled by the **RBI** (qualitative and quantitative measures)
- ★ Credit creation by commercial banks controlled by the **RBI**
- ★ **ECBs** regulated by the **RBI** (eligible borrowers/lenders, end use, minimum average maturity)
- ★ RBI nationalised — **1949**
- ★ **Fiscal agent and adviser** to Government in monetary and financial matters — **RBI**

## SEC 3:80:12 | Functions of RBI — legal basis, and what RBI does not do
- ★ **Bank Rate** — rate at which the RBI **lends to / rediscounts bills** of commercial banks; a monetary policy instrument
- ★ **Lender of last resort** — **RBI** (emergency liquidity to banks)
- ★ **Repo rate** regulated by the **RBI**
- ★ Savings account interest in nationalised banks — fixed by **none** of RBI/Government; RBI **deregulated** it on **25 October 2011**; banks set it
- ★ Monetary policy objective — **price stability** while ensuring **economic growth**

## SEC 3:80:13 | Currency management — issuer, reserve systems, denominations, note features — Issuing authority
### Issuer
- ★ **One rupee note** — signature of the **Finance Secretary**, Ministry of Finance
- ★ **Coins** — issued by the **Ministry of Finance** (Government of India); RBI puts them into circulation

- ★ **Dewas** (Madhya Pradesh) — **currency note printing** press

- ★ Note issue based on **Minimum Reserve System**: **₹400 crore** foreign securities + **₹115 crore** gold = ₹515 crore (replaced proportional reserve)
- ★ Notes backed by gold coin, bullion, rupee coin, GoI rupee securities, foreign securities — **all of the above** (**Section 33**, RBI Act)
- ★ **Forex reserves** maintained by the **RBI**
### Income and functions
- ★ RBI income sources — **buying/selling Government bonds** and **buying/selling foreign currency** only
- ★ Not an RBI function — **regulation of foreign trade**
- ★ RBI functions: issuer of currency notes, banker to banks, banker to Government, controller of credit, exchange management and control
