# ROW 2:35 | Parliamentary committees - PAC, Estimates, Public Undertakings, Departmentally Related Standing Committees, ad hoc
## SEC 2:35:0 | Types of committees
### Standing committees
- **Business Advisory Committee** — allots time for government and other business; chaired by the **Speaker** (Lok Sabha)
- Other roles: privilege questions — **Committee of Privileges**; members' conduct — **Ethics Committee**; delegated rule-making — **Committee on Subordinate Legislation**
- **Watchdog group**: Subordinate Legislation, Government Assurances, Estimates, PAC, COPU, DRSCs — **not** the Business Advisory Committee
- **24 DRSCs**: **16** under Lok Sabha, **8** under Rajya Sabha (22 = strength of the PAC)
### Ad hoc committees
- Two kinds: **inquiry committees** on a particular subject; **Select / Joint Committees** on Bills (e.g. **Joint Committee on a Bill**)
- **Inquiry committees** are ad hoc, **not** standing committees
- Bill referred to a Select / Joint Committee by a **motion in the House**

## SEC 2:35:1 | Financial committees
- **Estimates Committee** — largest financial committee; **30 members, all Lok Sabha**; chairman from the ruling party
- **PAC** — **22** members (**15 LS + 7 RS**)
- Estimates Committee = **'continuous economy committee'** (suggests economies and alternative policies)
- Nicknames: PAC — **'watchdog'** on expenditure; Petitions Committee — **'Ombudsman'**
- **Committee of Privileges**: Lok Sabha **15** members nominated by the Speaker; Rajya Sabha **10**

## SEC 2:35:3 | PAC at work
### Financial committees
- Members elected annually by **PR-STV**; term **one year**; **Ministers ineligible**
- **Budget** = control **before** expenditure; financial committees = control **after** expenditure; they complement, not replace, the budget
### PAC
- Examines **Appropriation Accounts**, **Finance Accounts** and **CAG audit reports**
- Checks spending stayed within the **demand for grant** approved by Parliament
- Examines only **execution of policy**, not policy itself; recommendations **not binding**
- Chairman 2026-27: **K.C. Venugopal** (Opposition, by convention)
- **Estimates Committee** (not PAC) suggests **economies and alternative policies**; PAC = watchdog on departmental expenditure and irregularities
### Other committees
- **DRSC quorum** = **11** (not 15)
- DRSCs scrutinise **demands for grants**, **referred Bills**, **annual reports** and long-term policy of their ministries
- **Committee on Welfare of SCs and STs** — examines welfare programmes and **National Commissions' reports**; Ministers cannot serve
- **Lok Sabha-only**: **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions**, **Committee on Absence of Members**
- In **both Houses**: Petitions, Privileges, Rules, Papers Laid, Subordinate Legislation, Government Assurances
- **Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhisthan Bill, 2025** — before a **JPC** (both Houses), not a single-House Select Committee

- **Committee on Welfare of SCs and STs** — **30** members: **20 LS + 10 RS**
- **Committee on Petitions** — **'Ombudsman' of Parliament**; considers petitions on bills and matters of public interest

# ROW 2:36 | Parliamentary privileges; anti-defection law (Tenth Schedule) and the role of the Speaker
## SEC 2:36:0 | Parliamentary privileges
### Articles
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **105** | Privileges / freedom of speech in **Parliament** |
| **194** | Privileges / freedom of speech in **State Legislatures** |
| **122** | Parliamentary proceedings |
| **212** | State Legislature proceedings |
- Privileges **not codified** in a single statute; rest on conventions, rules, statutes, judicial rulings
### Procedure
- Notice of breach raised under **Lok Sabha Rule 222** / **Rajya Sabha Rule 187**, usually after Question Hour
- Committee of Privileges recommends; **House** takes final decision
- RS Privileges Committee — **10** members, usually chaired by the **Deputy Chairman**
- **Contempt** is wider: any act obstructing the House or members in their functions; **breach** = infringing a specific privilege
- **Raja Ram Pal (2007)** — privilege decisions reviewable only for **gross illegality or unconstitutionality**
### Anti-defection: current
- July 2026, Bench of **P.S. Narasimha** and **Alok Aradhe** on Paragraph 4: shortcomings are **matters for Parliament**
- Criticism: law **stifles intra-party dissent** and free expression without stopping mass defections

## SEC 2:36:1 | Making of the law
### Background
- Aim: **curb defections for office**, promote party discipline and **stable governments**
- **'Aaya Ram Gaya Ram'** — **Haryana**, **1967**
- **Y.B. Chavan Committee** studied defections, **1967-69**
- **1984** AP: **Nadendla Bhaskara Rao**'s defection toppled **N.T. Rama Rao**; NTR restored after a month
- **Jammu & Kashmir** — own anti-defection law in **1979**
### Amendments
- **52nd Amendment, 1985** — added **Tenth Schedule**; political parties entered the Constitution
  - assent **15 February 1985**; in force **1 March 1985**
- **91st Amendment, 2003** — strengthened the law, **deleted one-third split**, capped ministry at **15%**
### Articles
- **Art. 105(1)-(2)** — freedom of speech / privileges; 15% cap on State Council of Ministers = **Art. 164(1A)**
- **Art. 191** — State disqualifications; **190** — State vacation of seats; **101, 102** — Parliament counterparts

## SEC 2:36:2 | Grounds of disqualification
- Covers both Houses of Parliament and State Legislatures, incl. **MLCs**; **independents** and **nominated** members also covered
- **Independent** member — disqualified on joining **any party** after election; may become a **Minister** without joining a party
- **Six-month** window to join a party applies only to **nominated** members
- **Voluntarily giving up membership** possible without resigning — e.g. **campaigning for a rival party**
- **Expulsion** gives no protection; expelled member joining another party may be disqualified
- **One-third split** exception deleted by **91st Amendment, 2003**; only **Para 4 merger** remains
- Protected: **prior permission** of party; party **condones** vote within **15 days**; **merger** backed by **two-thirds**
- **Presiding Officer** may give up party membership on election to the Chair and **rejoin** after leaving office; joining another party disqualifies

## SEC 2:36:3 | Merger vs split
- **Para 4** merger needs: original **party merges** with another party **and** **two-thirds** of legislature party agree
- **One-third split** exception abolished in **2003**

## SEC 2:36:4 | Who decides disqualification?
- **Defection** disqualification: **Art. 102(2)** (MPs), **Art. 191(2)** (State legislators), read with the Tenth Schedule; clauses (1) = general grounds
- **General** (non-defection) disqualification of an MP — decided by the **President**, not the presiding officer
- **ECI's opinion** — compulsory and **binding** for both MPs and State legislators (general disqualification)
- **Karnataka case (2019)** — SC upheld disqualification of **17** rebel MLAs; Speaker could not bar them from contesting

## SEC 2:36:5 | Key cases
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)** | Law does **not** violate freedom of speech or basic structure; **Para 7** invalid — barred judicial review without **State ratification** under **Art. 368(2)** |
| **Sita Soren (2024)** | **No immunity for bribery** to speak or vote; 7 judges; overruled **P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998)** |
| **Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker, Manipur** | Decide petitions within **three months**; suggested considering an **independent tribunal** (did not transfer to ECI) |
| **Subhash Desai** | **Political party** (not legislature party) appoints the **whip** and leader |
| **G. Viswanathan** | **Expelled member** continues to belong to the party |
- Speaker / Chairman acts as a **tribunal** in Tenth Schedule cases; ECI gives no opinion here (that is under Arts **103** and **192**)
- Judicial review (Kihoto) — only **after the decision**, on grounds like **mala fides**, **perversity**, natural justice, constitutional mandate
- May 2026: **Palaniswami** camp (AIADMK) cited Subhash Desai to claim the **whip under Para 2(1)(b)**
- Newer proposals: **fixed timelines** for Speakers and an **independent tribunal** (SC suggestion, **2020**)

## SEC 2:36:6 | Reform bodies
| Proposal | Year |
|---|---|
| **Dinesh Goswami Committee** — disqualify only for voluntarily giving up membership or defying whip on confidence / no-confidence | **1990** |
| **Law Commission 170th Report** | **1999** |
| **Manish Tewari**'s private member's Bill | **2010** |
| SC suggestion of an **independent tribunal** | **2020** |
- Limiting whip to **confidence and money votes** — lets legislators **dissent freely** on matters not threatening the government's survival

## SEC 2:36:7 | Working & AP angle
### Delay
- Tenth Schedule prescribes **no time limit** for deciding a petition
- Concern: no time limit and Speakers **remain members** of their parties
- **Telangana**: SC allowed **three weeks** for the last two of **ten BRS** petitions, warning of contempt
- **23 YSRCP MLAs** who moved to TDP (2014-19) — petitions **never decided**
### Tactics and mergers
- **Resort politics** — sequestering legislators before a floor test to prevent poaching
- **Resignation route** — MLAs resign, lowering the majority mark, later re-contest on the new party's ticket
- **June 2019** — **4 of 6 TDP** RS MPs merged with BJP under **Para 4**; accepted by RS Chairman
- **April 2026** — **seven AAP** RS MPs joined BJP; also **twenty TMC** MPs joined the NPCI (June 2026)

## SEC 2:36:8 | Exam corner
- Original **Tenth Schedule** (Sikkim) — **35th Amendment, 1974**; omitted by the **36th**
- **Art. 164(1A)** — State ministry ≤ **15%** of Assembly, minimum **12** ministers
- Disqualified defector barred from Minister / remunerative political post until re-elected — **Arts 75(1B), 164(1B), 361B**
- **Para 4 two-thirds rule** criticised — can defend or facilitate **mass defection**

## SEC 2:36:10 | Anti-defection law — background, Amendments and FIRSTs — Background
- ★ **Jammu and Kashmir** — anti-defection law in **1979**; MLA leaving his party ceases to be a member

## SEC 2:36:11 | Anti-defection law — background, Amendments and FIRSTs — Amendment → provision
- ★ **Tenth Schedule** — **anti-defection law**
- ★ **52nd Amendment Act, 1985** — added the Tenth Schedule; banned **floor crossing**; related to **defection**
- ★ Further amended by the **91st Amendment, 2003**
- ★ Political parties got constitutional recognition first in **1985**

## SEC 2:36:13 | Tenth Schedule → coverage, grounds and exceptions (Paras) — Coverage
- ★ **Presiding officer** exception: may **give up** party membership on election, or **rejoin** after ceasing to hold office — both protected (dignity and impartiality of the office)

- ★ **Merger** recognised when not less than **two-thirds** of the members of the legislature party agree

# ROW 2:38 | Parliamentary form of government - features, merits, demerits; comparison with the presidential system
## SEC 2:38:0 | Parliamentary features
### Parliamentary (India)
- **Art. 74(1)** — President acts on aid and advice of Council of Ministers; advice **binding**
- Non-member minister must enter Parliament within **six months**
- **Prime Minister** — leads Council of Ministers; **Leader of the Lok Sabha** and chief spokesperson; President = nominal head
- **Westminster model** — originated in **Britain**
### Presidential
- ★ All executive powers vested in the **President** (head of State and Government)
- **Fixed term** for a single, directly elected executive
### UK comparison
- Britain only: **institutionalised shadow cabinet**, legal ministerial responsibility, ministers must be MPs
- UK: **ruling party** can replace the PM; India: removal linked to loss of **Lok Sabha confidence**

## SEC 2:38:1 | Parliamentary vs presidential
- Parliamentary — **fusion** of executive and legislative powers; presidential — **separation** of powers

## SEC 2:38:2 | Countries by system
- Parliamentary: **UK, Japan, Canada, India**; presidential: **USA, Brazil**
- **Semi-presidential** — President and PM coexist; President may have real powers; PM responsible to legislature
- Semi-presidential examples: **France, Russia, Sri Lanka**
- France's **Fifth Republic** — **1958**

## SEC 2:38:3 | India vs Britain
- India: PM from **either House**; Britain: PM from **House of Commons**
- **Basic structure doctrine** — Indian, does not govern the British Parliament

## SEC 2:38:4 | India vs USA
- **Residuary powers**: India — **Parliament**; USA — States
- Judicial review: India — **procedure established by law**; USA — **due process of law**
- USA **22nd Amendment** (presidential tenure) — **1951**; India has no PM term limit

## SEC 2:38:5 | Merits & demerits
- India is parliamentary **because** the executive is **responsible to the legislature** (defining test)
- Merit (parliamentary): **continuous accountability** of the executive to the legislature
- Demerits (presidential): **executive-legislature deadlock**, personality cult
- **Ambedkar**: India chose **responsibility** (continuous accountability) over guaranteed tenure / stability

## SEC 2:38:6 | Why the Constituent Assembly chose it & the debate
- **Ambedkar**: a democracy cannot maximise both **stability** and **responsibility**
- **Vasant Sathe** — proposals for a **presidential form**, 1970s-80s
- **NCRWC** advised **retaining** the parliamentary system
- **Shamsher Singh (1974)** — Governor's public criticism of Cabinet policy runs **counter** to the parliamentary system

## SEC 2:38:8 | Parliamentary government — meaning and features
- ★ Non-member minister ceases to be minister after **six months**
- ★ India parliamentary **because** the executive is **responsible to the legislature** (Council of Ministers led by PM, to Lok Sabha)
- ★ India is **democratic** and has a **written constitution**, but the latter does not explain the former (UK: democratic without one)

## SEC 2:38:9 | Countries → system of government
- ★ Presidential form first formed in the **USA** — oldest and purest form
- ★ Constitution of India provides **parliamentary** governance, influenced by the UK

## SEC 2:38:10 | Presidential and semi-presidential systems → features
### Presidential
- ★ President is **head of State** and **directly elected**; not accountable to the legislature
- ★ President is head of the State and the executive; appoints his Council of Ministers; **cannot dissolve** the Legislature
- ★ Basic feature: **single executive**; power centralised
### Parliamentary (India)
- ★ Institutional principles: **Cabinet members are MPs**; ministers hold office while they enjoy **confidence** of Parliament
- ★ Government **responsible to Parliament** and **can be removed** by it

## SEC 2:38:11 | India vs Britain and USA → feature comparison
- ★ Central and State governments get authority from the **Indian Constitution**; parliamentary system at Centre and in States
- ★ India is **not** a presidential system; President = **nominal head**; real executive = Council of Ministers headed by PM
- ★ Executive powers of the Union vested in the **President**
- ★ Council of Ministers responsible to **Parliament**
- ★ Parliamentary system also called **cabinet** or **responsible government**; stays in power while it has a majority in the popular House

## SEC 2:38:12 | Merits, demerits and the Constituent Assembly's choice
- ★ Main advantage: **executive remains responsible to the legislature** (collective responsibility, **Art. 75**)

# ROW 2:39 | Governor - appointment, powers, discretionary powers, position and controversies (Article 200/201, Article 356 reports)
## SEC 2:39:0 | Office & appointment

### Tenure, pay, immunity
- Term **five years**; continues until the **successor** enters office; may be transferred earlier
- Qualifications: citizen, at least **35**, no office of profit; cannot be an **MP or MLA** (must give up the seat)
- Emoluments charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State**; shared Governor's salary split in proportion fixed by the **President**
- Convention: Governor an outsider; **State CM consulted** (not mandated)
- **Art. 361**: **no criminal proceedings** during the term, even with a court's permission
### Chief Minister and Council of Ministers
- **CM** = real executive head; Governor = nominal / constitutional head
- State Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the **Governor** (not the CM)
- CM who **loses majority** must **resign** or may be **dismissed**
- **91st Amendment, 2003** — State ministry incl. CM ≤ **15%** of Assembly, minimum **12** (Art. 164(1A))
### Other
- **Art. 174** — gap between sessions ≤ **six months**
- Art. 356 proper use: **flagrant defiance** of constitutional directions; hung House with no alternative; improper: intra-party disputes, maladministration alone
- **Rajamannar Committee** — set up by **Tamil Nadu**, **1969**; Centre-State relations; recommendations **never implemented**
- **First ARC** study team — central armed forces deployable in States **on request or otherwise**
- **130th Amendment Bill** (2026) — automatic removal of PM / CM / Minister after **30 consecutive days** detention on charges of **5+ years**
- **Justice M. Fathima Beevi** — first woman SC Judge (**1989**); first **Muslim woman** in higher judiciary

## SEC 2:39:3 | Discretion (Art. 163)
- **Express discretion**: reserving a Bill for the President (**Art. 200**)
- Governor seeks information from the CM under **Art. 167** (Art. 239 = UT administration)
- **Art. 163(2)** — Governor's decision final on whether a matter is discretionary, but **not** complete immunity from judicial review

## SEC 2:39:4 | Bills: Art. 200 → 201
- Governor may return a Bill for reconsideration only if it is **not a Money Bill**; same for the President under **Art. 201**
- Re-passed returned Bill — Governor **must assent**; cannot withhold again
- A Bill may be reserved **only once**; again only if **materially altered**
- **Mandatory reservation**: Bill **derogating from High Court powers**
- **Art. 233** — Governor appoints **District Judges** in consultation with the **High Court**

# ROW 2:41 | State legislature - Assembly and Legislative Council; creation and abolition of the Council (Article 169)
