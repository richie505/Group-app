# ROW 3:0 | Basic characteristics of the Indian economy; features of a developing economy
## SEC 3:0:0 | Types of economies
### Mixed economy
- ★ Indian model of development ensures interest of **both State and Person** — India is a **mixed** economy
  - public sector administered by the **State**; private sector by individuals or groups
- **Mixed economy** — public and private **sectors coexist**; **joint sector** — one enterprise **jointly owned** by government and private investors
- India's system — pragmatic middle path between **liberal-capitalist, Soviet and Gandhian** models; growth by democratic means with social justice
- State regulates private sector through **taxes, subsidies and credit**
### Other types and terms
- **Command economy** — government's **central plan** decides production
- **Traditional economy** — **custom and tradition** decide what, how and for whom
- **Gandhian Plan (1944)** — **S. N. Agarwal**
- **Public sector** — **government** ownership or control; private sector — private ownership or control
- Bank lending — **financial sector**; farmer harvesting — **real sector**
- GDP estimation — **Central Statistical Organisation**, now the **NSO**
- GVA four institutional sectors — **general government, public corporations, private corporations, households** (cooperative societies not one)

## SEC 3:0:1 | India at Independence
- ★ **'The Accumulation of Capital'** (1956) — **Joan Robinson**
- Capital formation needs an adequate **savings rate**
- **Disguised unemployment** — more workers than needed, **marginal productivity zero**; mainly agriculture and unorganised sector
- **Underdeveloped economy** — low per capita income, low capital formation, agricultural predominance, disguised unemployment
  - public-private coexistence describes a **mixed** economy, not this
- **1951** — agriculture employed about **72 per cent** of the workforce
- **First National Income Committee (1951)** — 1948-49 national income **Rs 8,710 crore**; per capita income **Rs 225**
- **State per capita income** — **NSDP ÷ population** (net figure); per capita GSDP is gross

## SEC 3:0:2 | Nurkse: vicious circle of poverty
- **Ragnar Nurkse** — vicious circle: **low income → low saving → low investment → low productivity → low income**

## SEC 3:0:3 | Economists
- ★ **Adam Smith** — Scottish; **Father of (Modern) Economics** / Father of Capitalism
  - **Keynes** — modern macroeconomic thought, not this title
- **Angus Maddison** — India's share of world GDP fell from **22.6% (1700)** to **3.8% (1952)**
- **W. Arthur Lewis** — dual-sector model: surplus labour from **subsistence** sector to **modern** sector

## SEC 3:0:4 | Sectors by activity
| Activity | Sector |
|---|---|
| Fisheries, mining and quarrying | **Primary** |
| Mineral exploration | **Primary** |

- **Tertiary sector** — provides **services**, not physical goods; banking is tertiary for this reason
- **Industrial activity** — secondary, not primary
- **Disguised unemployment** — mainly **agriculture / primary** sector; removing surplus workers would not cut output
- **Financial sector** — money and financial assets (banking, bonds); **real sector** — flow of goods and services (agriculture, manufacturing)

## SEC 3:0:5 | GDP share vs employment
- **Agriculture employment** — **72 per cent** in **1951**; about **46.1 per cent** now (PLFS 2023–24, Economic Survey 2025–26) with about **18 per cent** of GVA
- **2024-25** (new series, current prices) GVA shares — **tertiary 52.9%**, secondary 25.8%, primary 21.4%
- **Private corporate investment** — **12.3%** of GDP (FY23) to **11.2%** (FY24); share of GFCF at decadal low of **33%**
- **Structural change** in national income — sectoral output shares changed while employment stayed concentrated in **agriculture**

## SEC 3:0:6 | Dual economy & demography
### Dual economy
- **Unorganised sector** — over **80%** of workers, about **45%** of national output
### Census and demography
- **Census 2011** population — **121.09 crore**
- Census 2011 — **68.8%** rural, **31.2%** urban; **72.4%** is the rural share of the **workforce**
- **Work participation rate** (Census 2011) — **39.8 per cent**
  - higher in less developed States (AP, Chhattisgarh) — **subsistence** activities need more manual work
- **Sex ratio** — **933** (2001), **940** (2011); females **48.46%** (2011)
- **TFR 1.9** — below the **replacement level of 2.1**
- **Demographic dividend** — peaks around **2030**; median age about **36 by 2036**

## SEC 3:0:7 | World standing
- **World Bank income group** (July 2026) — India **lower-middle income**; per capita income **USD 2,760**
- Classification based on **GNI per capita, Atlas method** (three-year average exchange rate); revised every **1 July**
- **PPP GDP** — India **3rd**, after **USA and China**
  - nominal rank differs: **4th** in 2025 (APPCA), **6th** (IMF WEO April 2026)

## SEC 3:0:8 | AP vs India (2025-26)
- **Commanding heights** under State control — **heavy industry, infrastructure, finance**; regulated private sector in consumer goods
- **AP GVA 2025-26** (current prices) — **services 43%**, agriculture and allied 33%, industry 24%
- **Visakhapatnam** — 1st in per capita income among AP districts, 2025-26 (DES, May 2026)
- **India GDP growth** — **7.2%** (2023-24), **7.1%** (2024-25), **7.7%** (2025-26)
- Large GDP alongside an **HDI rank of about 130**

## SEC 3:0:9 | Exam corner
- ★ **Disguised unemployment** — main characteristic of the **primary sector** (agriculture and allied); high population pressure on land
- ★ Largest source of national income — **service sector**
- Largest share of national income in the mixed economy — **private sector**
- Most important cause of underdevelopment — **co-existence of under-utilised manpower and under-utilised natural resources**
- Recorded causes of underdevelopment — rapid population growth with high **dependency ratio**, low human development, high poverty
  - **working efficiency** of the population is **not** a main cause (APPSC-G2 2017)
- **Tertiary sector** — also called the **service sector** (trade, transport, storage, communication)

## SEC 3:0:10 | Types of economies → defining feature
### Mixed and developing economy
- ★ Indian economy — **mixed economy**: public and private sectors coexist; combines **capitalism and socialism**
- ★ Indian economy — a **developing economy**; still shows features of an **underdeveloped** economy
- ★ **J.M. Keynes** is **not** the Father of Economics
- ★ Underdeveloped features — **inequitable income distribution**, **high dependency ratio**, **slower increase in national income**, change in banking and financial sector
- ★ Developing-economy features — **low per capita income**, excessive dependence on **agriculture**, high **population growth**
- ★ Disguised unemployment — **agricultural sector**
### Size and savings
- ★ **World Development Report 2008** (nominal GDP) — **USA > China > UK > India**
- ★ Capital formation — depends on **savings** and effective **financial institutions**; **investment** is essential
- ★ Gross domestic savings, descending — **household > private corporate > public corporations > government**

## SEC 3:0:14 | Sectors by activity → classification
- ★ **Storage** of agricultural produce — **tertiary** (a service)
- ★ **Dairy farm** — **primary**; **weaving cloth** — **secondary**
- ★ Primary sector — **agriculture**

# ROW 3:1 | Structural change - sectoral composition of output and employment; the missing manufacturing transition
## SEC 3:1:0 | Three-sector scheme
- ★ **Water supply** — **secondary** sector
- **Quaternary** — **knowledge** sector (research, information, knowledge-based services)
- **Quinary** — **top decision-making** (government policy, scientific research)
- **Tertiary (services)** — trade, hotels, transport, communication, banking, **public administration and defence**
- **Livestock** — **agriculture and allied**; mining and quarrying count under **industry**

## SEC 3:1:1 | Clark-Fisher prediction vs India
- **Clark-Fisher** sequence — **primary → secondary → tertiary**
- Sector growing slower than GDP — its **share declines** even if output rises
- **MSMEs** — second-largest employer after **agriculture**

## SEC 3:1:2 | Share of GVA over time
### Classification and theory
- **Trade** — tertiary (like transport, storage); water supply secondary; mining primary
- **W. Arthur Lewis** — dual-sector model (**1954**); **Lewis turning point** when surplus farm labour is exhausted
### Shares over time
| Year | Share |
|---|---|
| 1947 | agriculture about **50%** |
| 1950-51 | industry about **13%** (agriculture over half) |
| 1991 | agriculture about **29%** |
| 2011-12 (old series) | industry **32.5%** |
| 2017-18 (PYQ key) | agriculture about **17%** (revised 18.3%), services **54.4%**, industry 29.2% |
| 2021-22 | primary **21.82%** of GDP but **43.96%** of workforce; tertiary **53.89%** |
| 2024-25 (new series) | primary **21.4%**, tertiary **52.9%** |
| 2022-23 → 2025-26 | services **51.9% → 54.3%** |
- New series (base **2022-23**) — **manufacturing 14.7%** (not 14.3%); higher agriculture and industry, lower services
- 2024-25 primary share **21.4%** not comparable with old agriculture-forestry-fishing share — it **includes mining**
### Growth
- **Q1 2026-27** — manufacturing **9.2%**, services GVA **10.0%**
- **AP 2025-26** (constant prices) — **fishing and aquaculture** fastest in agriculture (**17.69%**); services **10.67%**, agriculture 10.14%, industry 9.15%

## SEC 3:1:4 | Key terms
- **Missing middle** — moving from agriculture to services **without a manufacturing-led stage**
- **Premature leapfrogging** — services absorb value while manufacturing fails to absorb **surplus farm labour**
- **Premature deindustrialisation** → **jobless growth**: services need **high skills**, may not absorb semi-skilled farm workers
- **Chairman of the EAC-PM** — services-led growth cannot be sustained **without a manufacturing base**
- Fall in manufacturing's **nominal** GVA share — **relative-price effect** (Economic Survey 2025-26); real share stable at **17-18%**
- **AP** — farm share **well above** the national share (India below 20%; India's services about 54.3%)
- **Telangana's** services lead over AP (2013-14 to 2024-25) — **real estate, IT, trade-hotels**

## SEC 3:1:5 | Manufacturing facts
- Manufacturing employment 2023-24 — **11.4%** of workers (1.9 crore organised, 3.3 crore unorganised)
- Manufacturing real growth 2025-26 — **12.5%**
- India — **2.9%** of global manufacturing value added; **1.8%** of merchandise exports; **44th** on the Economic Complexity Index

## SEC 3:1:6 | AP GSVA vs India
- **AP GSVA 2024-25** (AP Budget 2026-27) — agriculture and allied **37%**, industry **21%**, services **42%**

## SEC 3:1:7 | AP growth 2025-26
- Agricultural growth — **2.5%** (9th Plan), **2.4%** (10th Plan), **3.6%** (**11th Plan**, highest)
- **Industrial Development Policy 4.0** (**2024-29**) — AP's response to the missing manufacturing stage

## SEC 3:1:8 | Exam corner
- **ASI and ASUSE** manufacturing components — compiled by the **NSO** (not State DES)
  - GDP — **NSO**; State GSDP/GVA — **State DES**
- At Independence, **agriculture** contributed about **half** of GDP
- Changing shares of the three sectors — **structural change**
- Post-**1991** growth — mainly **service-led**

## SEC 3:1:9 | Sector classification → other name, activities and compiling bodies
- ★ Question-period order of GVA shares — **agriculture, forestry, fishing > trade, hotels, transport, communication > manufacturing > financing, insurance, real estate, business services**
- ★ Service sector includes **transport and communication** and **hotels** (not mining, forestry, fishing)
- ★ Fastest-growing service sector, **2006–2010** — **communication**
- ★ Highest services share in GDP in **2006** — **trade, hotels, transport and communication**

## SEC 3:1:11 | Data → sectoral share of GVA over time (old series)
- ★ Services share of GDP at current prices, **2017-18** — **54.4%**
- ★ Sector contributing most to GDP at Independence — **agriculture**

## SEC 3:1:12 | Data → GVA by sub-sector and the new GDP series (base 2022-23)
- ★ Decreasing order of contribution to GDP — **services > industry > agriculture**
- ★ As the economy develops, the tertiary share **increases** — **30.3%** (1950-51), **37.65%** (1980-81), **42.55%** (1990-91)

## SEC 3:1:15 | Terms and debates → the missing manufacturing stage
- ★ Post-**1991** growth — **service-led** growth

# ROW 3:5 | Occupational structure of India - trends and inter-state variation
## SEC 3:5:0 | Workers in agriculture
### Census origins
- ★ First Census — **1872** per the Census website (or **1881**); UPPSC key gave **1871**
### Agriculture's share
- Agricultural share of workers — **72% (1951)** to **54.6% (Census 2011)**
- **2022–23** — agriculture about **18%** of GVA but **42.4%** of workforce; occupational structure changed slower than output
- Slow shift out of agriculture:
  - **colonial deindustrialisation** pushed **artisans** onto the land
  - **capital-intensive** growth failed to absorb surplus farm labour
  - agriculture's share fell only marginally in the Plan era
  - only **4.2%** had formal vocational training
### Other facts
- Direct factory employment — **61% to 47%** (2011–2023); contract workers **42%**
- High share of '**other workers**' (services) — urbanised **Delhi, Chandigarh, Puducherry**
- Census 2011 — AP cultivators **14.5%** vs India **24.7%**; AP all agricultural workers **62.4%** vs India **54.6%**

## SEC 3:5:1 | Cultivators vs labourers
- **2001–2011** — cultivators fell **127.3 → 118.8 million**; agricultural labourers rose **106.8 → 144.3 million**
- Census 2011 — labourers **30.0%**, cultivators **24.7%** of workers
- **2011** — first Census where **labourers outnumbered cultivators**

## SEC 3:5:2 | Census 2011 workforce
- ★ First Census of the modern system — **1872** (**Lord Mayo**); first regular Census **1881** (**Lord Ripon**)

- Male workers (Census 2011) — about **332 million**

## SEC 3:5:3 | Key terms
### Structural retardation
- **Premature deindustrialisation** — manufacturing fails to absorb enough farm labour
- India vs **Clark-Fisher** — moved directly from agriculture to services, no manufacturing-led stage → **structural retardation**
- **Jobless growth** — output grows without matching rise in **quality employment**; evident in the **late 1990s**
### Women in agriculture
- Female workers — **55%** agricultural labourers, **24%** cultivators
- Women held only **12.8%** of operational holdings — **feminisation of agriculture**
- Drivers — subsistence farming, **unpaid family work**, **distress** employment (not formal manufacturing jobs)
### Organised and unorganised
- **Organised manufacturing** — registered factories with **10+ workers with power** (**20+ without power**)
- **NCEUS** — chaired by **Arjun Sengupta**; unorganised cut-off **fewer than 10 workers** (not 20)

## SEC 3:5:4 | Unemployment types
- **Formal informality** — EPFO 'formal' jobs that are **short-term, low-wage or contractual**
- **Structural** — **skills-jobs mismatch** (e.g. new technology); associated with industry, urban areas, educated workers
- **Frictional** — short gap **between jobs**; urban/organised sector
- **Cyclical** — follows a **business-cycle** slump
- **Seasonal** — work only in **sowing and harvesting** seasons (agriculture)

## SEC 3:5:5 | Women & informality

- **Social-protection coverage** — **19% (2015)** to **64.3% (2025)**; India **second after China**

# ROW 3:6 | Economic development since independence - phases and growth rates; the Hindu rate of growth
## SEC 3:6:0 | Hindu rate of growth
- **'Hindu rate of growth'** — coined **1978** by **Prof. Raj Krishna**
  - about **3.5%** a year real GDP growth (not 5.5%), **1950s to early 1980s**
- **IRDP** — experimental **1978–79**; nationwide **1980** (year of the second bank nationalisation)

## SEC 3:6:1 | Phases & average growth
- **1980–81 to 1989–90** — average growth about **5.13–5.18%**
- Post-reform peak — **8.5%** in **2003–08**; post-reform average about **6.5%**
- **AP 2025–26** — **9.88%** vs India **7.4%**; AP outgrew India in most years since **2014–15**

| Event | Year |
|---|---|
| First Agricultural University, **Pantnagar** | **1960** |
| First Green Revolution phase | **1966–67** |
| First bank nationalisation | **1969** |
| First KVK, **Puducherry** | **1974** |

## SEC 3:6:2 | Why growth stayed low
- **ICOR** — change in capital ÷ change in output; **lower ICOR** = more efficient investment
- **Harrod-Domar** — **g = s/k**; higher capital-output ratio, same savings → **lower growth**
- **Second Plan** — **heavy, capital-intensive** industries (not light consumer industries)
- Plan-era industrial growth about **6–8%**; **0.3%** was **agricultural** growth before Independence

## SEC 3:6:3 | Licence regime
- **Industrial Policy Resolution, 1956** — **Schedule A**: **17** industries reserved for State (cut to **8** in 1991); **Schedule B**: **12**
- **Bombay Plan (1944)** — industrialists themselves sought **state protection**; Licence Raj partly a capitalist demand

# ROW 3:7 | Economic planning in India - origins, Planning Commission, National Development Council
## SEC 3:7:0 | Pre-independence plans
### Early plans
| Plan / idea | Year | Associated with |
|---|---|---|
| **Karachi Resolution** | **1931** | economic freedom, **state ownership** of key industries |
| **Planned Economy for India** | **1934** | **M. Visvesvaraya** — ten-year plan to **double income**; industrialisation |
| Congress **National Planning Committee** | final report **1949** | initiated by **Subhas Chandra Bose**; chaired by **Jawaharlal Nehru** |
| **Bombay Plan** | **1944** | **eight industrialists** (J.R.D. Tata, G.D. Birla, Kasturbhai Lalbhai, John Mathai); state-led industrialisation with **protection** |
| **Gandhian Plan** | **1944** | **Shriman Narayan Agarwal**; decentralised, self-contained **villages** |

### Planning Commission and NDC
- ★ **Prime Minister** — ex-officio chairman of the **Planning Commission** and of the **NDC**
- Planning Commission — first Deputy Chairman **Gulzarilal Nanda**; last **M.S. Ahluwalia**; last Plan the **Twelfth (2012-17)**
- ★ President of the **Sugar De-control Committee** — **M.S. Ahluwalia** (as keyed)
- **NDC** — set up **August 1952** by an **executive resolution**

## SEC 3:7:1 | NDC at a glance
- NDC — approved **draft Five-Year Plans**; meant to meet at least **twice a year**
- Now **inactive**; role passed to **NITI Aayog's Governing Council**; formal **abolition pending**
- **Non-constitutional, non-statutory**, **advisory**; recommendations not binding
- **Sarkaria Commission** — constitutional status under **Article 263**; rename **National Economic and Development Council**
- Composition — **PM** chairs; **Secretary of the Planning Commission** as Secretary; **Chief Ministers** of all states members
  - **President** not a member; Deputy Chairman of Planning Commission not its chairman
- **NITI Aayog** — **think tank**; **bottom-up** planning and **cooperative federalism** (not mainly a fund allocator)

## SEC 3:7:3 | Planning vs Finance Commission
- ★ **Finance Commission** (**Article 280**) — constitutional authority recommending **tax share** of the States
- Finance Commission — **tax devolution and grants**; Planning Commission — planning and **resource allocation**
- **Planning Commission (1950)** and **NITI Aayog (2015)** — set up by **Cabinet resolutions**
- Planning Commission — in line with **Article 39** (Directive Principles)

## SEC 3:7:4 | Constitution & committees
- ★ **Economic and social planning** — **Concurrent List** (List III, Seventh Schedule), **Entry 20**
- **Metropolitan Planning Committees** — **Article 243ZE**; **District Planning Committees** — **Article 243ZD**
- **Thungon sub-committee (1988)** — **Zila Parishad** the pivot of planning (not Gram Sabha)

## SEC 3:7:5 | State & AP bodies
### Approval chain
- Draft Five Year Plan approved by the **NDC**; Planning Commission assessed resources and prepared it; **Finance Ministry** gave budgetary allocation
- **State Planning Board** — chaired by the **Chief Minister**; State draft plan sent to Planning Commission
- Draft district plan (from Panchayat and Municipal plans) forwarded to the State by the **DPC Chairperson**
- **DPC** — **four-fifths** elected by and from elected members of district panchayat and municipalities; **MPC** — **two-thirds** elected
### Status and criticism
- Planning Commission — "**super cabinet**", "economic cabinet", "parallel cabinet", "**fifth wheel of the coach**": **centralised** decisions, eroded **State autonomy**
- **Zonal Councils** — **statutory**; NDC and Planning Commission neither constitutional nor statutory; NITI Aayog Governing Council not statutory
- **AP** — district-level decentralised planning first in the **Sixth Plan (1980–85)**; **Rs 1 crore** each to **23 districts**; District Planning Councils
