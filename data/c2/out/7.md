# ROW 1:50 | Ikshvakus - political history, Vasishthiputra Chamtamula and Virapurushadatta
## SEC 1:50:0 | Origin theories
| Historian | Origin theory |
|---|---|
| **Vogel** | **Kannada** origin |
| Rapson, Buhler | North Indian origin |
| D.R. Bhandarkar | Kshatriya or mixed origin |
| Gopalachari | Telugu/Andhra origin |
- **B.S.L. Hanumantha Rao** — derives the name Ikshvaku from **ikshu** (sugarcane)

## SEC 1:50:1 | Ikshvaku rulers
### Founder: Vasishthiputra Chamtamula
- **Vasishthiputra Sri Chamtamula** (also **Santamula**) — founder of the Ikshvaku kingdom at **Vijayapuri**
- Performed **Ashvamedha**, Vajapeya and Rajasuya; evidence: Ashvamedha platform at **Nagarjunakonda**
- Titles of Chamtamula: **Gosatasahasra**, **Halasatasahasra**
### Virapurushadatta
- **Virapurushadatta** — **golden age of Buddhism** under the Ikshvakus; his **queens** were important donors
- Title of Virapurushadatta: **Sri Parvatadhipati**
### Ehuvala Chamtamula
- **Ehuvala Chamtamula** (son of Virapurushadatta) — **first Sanskrit inscriptions** in the Telugu lands
- **Gummadidurru** inscription is also his
- **Andhrabhrityas** — Puranic name for the Ikshvakus in general, not a title of Ehuvala Chamtamula
### Last ruler
- ★ **Rudrapurushadatta** — **last** Ikshvaku ruler; issued the **Gurajala** inscription

## SEC 1:50:2 | Dynasty facts
- **Lion** — Ikshvaku symbol; **Prakrit** — royal language
- Andhra origin evidence: **southern names** and the custom of marrying the **maternal uncle's daughter** (cross-cousin marriage)

## SEC 1:50:3 | Titles & names
- Puranas call the Ikshvakus **Sri Parvatiyas** (Sri Parvatiya Andhras) and **Andhrabhrityas**
- **Chamtamula** — gave **land, ploughs, bullocks and cows** to make the intelligentsia partners in agricultural development
- Ikshvakus — a **bridge dynasty**; brought the **Amaravati school** to its peak
  - built the first temples; moved Andhra towards an agrarian, Puranic order
  - did **not** replace Buddhism with Jainism

## SEC 1:50:4 | Ikshvaku firsts
- **Ikshvakus** — first **dated inscriptions** naming **sculptors**
- Early Hindu temples at Nagarjunakonda: **Pushpabhadraswami**, **Ashtabhuja Narayana**

## SEC 1:50:5 | Capital & sites
- ★ **Vijayapuri** — Ikshvaku capital, in the **Nagarjunakonda** valley (Nagarjuna Sagar valley); not at Amaravati
- Vijayapuri excavated by **R. Subrahmanyam**

## SEC 1:50:6 | Exam corner
- ★ Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda) — successors of the **Satavahanas** in the **3rd century CE**
- ★ APPSC Group-2 key: Puranic literature calls them **Andhra Bhrityas** (the statement "Sri Parvathiyas" was marked incorrect)
- ★ UPPCS 2020 key: Ikshvaku rulers called **Shriparvatiya** in the Puranas
  - were lords under the Satavahanas; declared independence after Satavahana decline
  - founder: **Shrisantmula**
- Virapurushadatta married **Rudrabhattarika**, princess of **Ujjain** — linked the Ikshvakus with the **Sakas / Western Kshatrapas**

## SEC 1:50:8 | Ikshvaku rulers → period and contributions
- ★ **Veera Purushadatta** — title **Sri Parvatadhipati**; Buddhism flourished in Andhra in his time
- ★ **Period of the Ikshvakus** — golden age of Buddhism in Andhra; Nagarjunakonda (Vijayapuri) a great Buddhist centre
- ★ **Sri Chantamula** (Vasishthiputra, founder) — made the intelligentsia partners in agriculture
  - performed Vedic sacrifices; donated lands, ploughs and cattle to Brahmins
- ★ **Mathariputra Veerapurushadatta** — married **Rudradhara Bhattarika**, daughter of the **Saka** ruler of **Ujjain**

# ROW 1:51 | Ikshvaku socio-economic conditions; role of royal women as donors
## SEC 1:51:0 | Royal women donors
- **Chamtisri** — regarded as the **first woman donor**
  - sister of **Sri Santamula**, paternal aunt of Virapurushadatta
  - built or restored the **Maha Chaitya** at Nagarjunakonda in Virapurushadatta's **sixth** regnal year
- **Upasika Bodhisri** — non-royal donor; daughter of **Revanta** of **Amaravati**
  - associated with the **Sinhala Vihara** and viharas for foreign monks (not a vihara for the Aparamahavinaseliya sect)

## SEC 1:51:1 | Donative inscriptions
- Royal women's donations to Buddhism made **without male intervention** (kings were Vedic)
- Most Nagarjunakonda records — **Prakrit Ayaka-pillar** inscriptions dated to Virapurushadatta's **sixth regnal year**

## SEC 1:51:2 | Economic terms & guilds
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bhaga** | land tax, **one-sixth** of produce |
| **Halikas** | farmers |
| **Nigamalu** (also Negimalu) | guilds; continued the Satavahana sreni tradition |
| **Sale** | weavers |
| Telikas / Telika | oil pressers |
| Kammarus | blacksmiths |
| Parnika Sreni | betel-leaf traders |
| Poosika Sreni | sweetmeat traders |

## SEC 1:51:3 | Agrarian economy
- **Vasishthiputra Sri Santamula** — brought Brahmins from the **north**; granted them **agraharas**
- **Santamula** — performed **Ashvamedha, Vajapeya and Agnistoma**; Ashvamedha altar at **Nagarjunakonda**
- Four-way division of state revenue: administration and army; temples and festivals; poets and scholars; gifts to religious bodies
  - **not** expansion of overseas colonies; some revenue also went to **tanks and canals**
- Ikshvaku period — transition from the **Satavahana trade economy** to an **agrarian, agrahara-based** early medieval order

## SEC 1:51:4 | Trade & coins
- Roman trade under the Ikshvakus **lower** than under the Satavahanas; one account: maritime trade virtually collapsed after the fall of Rome
- Roman contact: Roman **gold and silver coins** at Nagarjunakonda; Roman-style **amphitheatre** at Vijayapuri
  - Romans said to have built a **stadium** under Sri Santamula
- Exports: **spices**, iron, coconuts, cotton textiles; imports: gold, silver, wine, glassware
- Coins of **lead and copper**; symbols: Ujjain symbol, elephant, lion; found in Guntur, Krishna and Godavari districts

## SEC 1:51:5 | Society
- **Shudras** — majority; Brahmins benefited from **Brahmadeya** grants; new landlord class emerged
- Marriage alliances with the **Kshatrapas of Ujjain** and the **Chutus**; cross-cousin marriage began with them
- Ikshvaku inscriptions record: naming after **grandfather** (**Ehuvala Santamula**), matronymics (**Vasishthiputra**), **sati**

# ROW 1:52 | Ikshvaku religious conditions - Buddhism at Nagarjunakonda, Brahmanical temples
## SEC 1:52:0 | Kings & religious policy
- ★ **Shantisri** and **Harmyasri** — sisters of **Vasishthiputra Santamula (I)**
- **Sri Santamula** — worshipped **Virupakshapati** and **Mahasena**; granted **agraharas** to northern Brahmins
- **Virapurushadatta** — titles **Ashoka of the South** and **Sri Parvatadhipati**
  - adopted and greatly patronised Buddhism; did **not** completely abandon Brahmanical association
- **Ehuvala Santamula** — revived Brahmanism; built a vihara for the **Bahushrutiyas** in his **mother's** name
- **Rudrapurushadatta** — custom of **chhaya-stambhas** (shadow pillars) near graves began
- Dual patronage: kings performed **Vedic sacrifices**; queens, princesses and wealthy lay women funded **stupas and viharas**

## SEC 1:52:1 | Persons & Sriparvata
- **Sriparvata** — identified with **Nagarjunakonda / Vijayapuri**; traditionally **Dakshina Gaya**
- **Hiuen Tsang** (7th c.) — found the Sriparvata vihara **neglected**
- **Itsing** (c. 700 CE) — saw Indian students memorising Nagarjuna's **Suhrllekha** (letter to Yajnasri Satakarni)
- **Ceylonese monks** — made Nagarjunakonda their base for **Hinayana**
- **Bodh Gaya** — Hinayana; **Sriparvata** — **Mahayana**

## SEC 1:52:2 | Sects & centres
| Structure | Sect |
|---|---|
| Bodhisri's **Sinhala Vihara** | **Mahaviharavasin** (Theravada) |
| **Maha Chaitya** | Aparamahavinaseliya |
| Kodabalisri's vihara | Mahishasaka |
- Mahasanghika branches / related sects named: **Purvasailiya**, **Rajagirika**, **Siddharthika** (Mahaviharavasin is not one)
- **Kantakasala** = **Ghantasala**; Bodhisri built a stone mandapa at the eastern gateway of the Maha Chaitya there
  - and another opposite the Paravata Vihara

## SEC 1:52:3 | Mahachaitya & Bodhisri
- Maha Chaitya — a **Dhatugarbha** (relic) stupa over the Buddha's bodily relics
- Earlier Paravata Mahachaitya — built by **Yajnasri Satakarni** for **Acharya Nagarjuna**
  - rebuilt by **Chamtisri** in Virapurushadatta's sixth year with **nine Ayaka pillars**
- **Bodhisri** — **seven viharas** at **Papili**; viharas for monks from Sinhala, Kashmir, China and Gandhara

## SEC 1:52:4 | Brahmanical temples
- **Pushpabhadraswami** (Shaiva) — **Gajaprishtha** (elephant-back) plan; begun by Virapurushadatta, completed by Ehuvala
- **Ashtabhuja Narayanaswami** — **first Vaishnava temple** in Andhra; built by **Shivasena**, commander of Abhira king **Vasusena**
- **Ikshvakus** — the **last royal Buddhist zenith** in Andhra
- Nagarjunakonda temples: **Harati, Mahajana, Kumara Swami, Pushpabhadra Swami**, Ashtabhuja Narayana Swami
- **Hariti** — tutelary deity of the Ikshvakus; **Kumara Swami** — deity of commander **Elisiri's** temple

## SEC 1:52:5 | Cults at Nagarjunakonda
- ★ **Hariti** — protector of children; family deity of the **Ikshvakus** of Vijayapuri
  - Kakatiyas' deity: **Kakati**
- Hariti image — beginning of **Shakti worship**
- Under the Ikshvakus: **Puranic Shaivism, Vaishnavism and Shakti worship** all flourished
- Nagarjunakonda: **nine** Shaiva temples + one Vaishnava (Ashtabhuja Narayanaswami)
- **Sriparvataswami** (Vishnukundin family deity) = **Srisaila Mallikarjuna**
- **Pedavegi** inscription — land grant to Vishnu; Salankayana king **Nandivarman II**

## SEC 1:52:6 | Dynastic deities
- Salankayanas — devotees of **Bappa Bhattaraka** = regarding the **father as God**
- **Chitraratha Swamy** — Salankayana family deity = the **Sun** god
- **Vasavi** — worshipped by the **Arya Vysya** community
- **Virupaksha** — Vijayanagara rulers

## SEC 1:52:14 | Dynastic deities → dynasty
- ★ **Virupaksha** (**Lord Shiva**) — tutelary deity of the **Vijayanagara** kings
- ★ **N. Venkataramanaiah** — identified **Sriparvata Swamy** with the **Buddha**
  - Sriparvata (Srisailam / Nagarjunakonda area) — an important religious centre

# ROW 1:53 | Ikshvaku art, architecture and literature
## SEC 1:53:0 | Sculpture firsts
- **Mandhata** (Chakravarti Mandhata) sculpture — at **Jaggayyapeta** (Krishna valley Buddhist stupa site)
  - **first depiction of a Chakravarti**; most important Ikshvaku sculpture
- Carving sculptors' names — beginning of the **Sculptor's Style**; e.g. **Badantacharya** at the Mahavihara
- Ikshvakus laid the base of **Dravida** temple architecture

## SEC 1:53:1 | Amaravati School at Nagarjunakonda
- Nagarjunakonda — main centre of the **Amaravati School**; final, mature phase with **Mahayana** themes
- Early Ikshvaku carving — **'last flicker'** of the **Satavahana Amaravati** tradition (less stylisation, low relief)
- Major stupas — **wheel-shaped (spoked)** plan, Ayaka platforms in four directions
- **Ayaka platforms** — belong to **Buddhist stupas**
  - Agamic temple plan instead: garbhagudi, antarala, mantapa, dhvajastambha, prakara

## SEC 1:53:2 | Stupa types
| Stupa type | Content |
|---|---|
| **Uddeshika** | nothing; **purely commemorative** |
| Dhatugarbha | bodily relics |
| Paribhogika | objects used by monks |

## SEC 1:53:3 | Vijayapuri structures
- **Kreeda Vedi** (amphitheatre) — **sports arena** thought built with **Roman** help; no parallel elsewhere in India
- **Acoustic building** — designed on principles of sound; the site's **most unique** find
- **Kummakundam** — **water tanks / bathing pools**

## SEC 1:53:4 | Inscriptions & literature
- ★ Ikshvaku inscriptions — in **Prakrit** (initial court language)
- **Ehuvala Santamula** — introduced **Sanskrit** into inscriptions
- Earliest Sanskrit inscription of Andhra and South India — **Swarna Devalayam** (Golden Temple), Nagarjunakonda
- **Elisri** — **Sarvadeva** inscription in **Sanskrit**; Ehuvala Santamula's **Gummadidurru** record in **Prakrit**
- Telugu words in Ikshvaku records: **Bapi, Remmanaka, Adavi, Ehuvala, Anikki** (not Dhanyakataka)
- **Suhrllekha** (letter to Yajnasri) and Madhyamika works — **Acharya Nagarjuna**
- Udyotana's **Kuvalayamala** (8th c.) — foreign students at **Dhanyakataka and Nagarjunakonda**
- Ikshvakus — first **sculptor signatures**, first **Sanskrit inscription** in South India, first structural Hindu temples

## SEC 1:53:5 | Excavation & submergence
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1926** | site discovered by **A. Ranganatha Saraswati** |
| 1927–31 | work of **A. H. Longhurst** |
| 1938 | work of **T. N. Ramachandran** |
| 1954–60 | large **rescue excavation** before submergence |
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** — laid foundation stone of the Nagarjuna Sagar Project, **10 December 1955**
- **Salvage archaeology** — ASI dismantled and rebuilt monuments at safer locations
- **Transplanted monuments** — relocated to **Anupu** and **Nagarjunakonda island**

## SEC 1:53:6 | Exam corner
- **Amaravati** — **largest** stupa in Andhra Pradesh (G2 2019)
- **Bhattiprolu** — oldest stupa in Andhra; Jaggayyapeta — Mandhata sculpture
- Nagarjunakonda — India's only **island museum**

## SEC 1:53:12 | Excavations → year and excavator
- ★ **A.R. Saraswati** — discovered Nagarjunakonda in **1926**; later excavated by **A.H. Longhurst**

# ROW 1:54 | Vishnukundins - political history, Madhava Varma I and II
## SEC 1:54:0 | Post-Ikshvaku order
- ★ Order: **Ikshvakus → Brihatphalayanas → Salankayanas → Vishnukundins** → Eastern Chalukyas
- **Jayavarma** — only known Brihatpalayana king; Brahmanical, **Maheshwara-pada-parigrahita**
- **624 CE** — **Pulakeshin II** defeated **Manchana Bhattaraka** at **Kunala** (Kolleru), ending Vishnukundin rule
  - his brother **Kubja Vishnuvardhana** founded the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi
- Vishnukundins — link between the **Ikshvaku age** and the **Eastern Chalukyas** (who took over their agrahara system and administration)

## SEC 1:54:1 | Origin
- Most historians: **Brahmins** of the **Vishnukundina gotra**
| Historian | Origin theory |
|---|---|
| **B.V. Krishna Rao** | **Brahmins** |
| K. Gopalachari | Agnikula Kshatriya |
| N. Venkataramanayya | Brahmins who adopted Kshatriya ways |
- Original home: **Telangana** — Amrabad, Keesaragutta, Indrapalanagara; later expanded into coastal Andhra

## SEC 1:54:2 | Rulers
| Ruler | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Indravarma** (358–370 CE) | **founder**; **Vakataka vassal**; built Indrapalanagara |
| **Madhava Varma I** | ruled **22 years**; married a Vakataka princess; Undavalli, Bhairavakona, Mogalrajapuram caves |
| **Madhava Varma II** | **greatest** Vishnukundin; golden age, Vedic revival; **Velpuru pillar** |
| Govinda Varma | title **Shadabhigna** |
| Vikramendra Varma I | title **Mahakavi** |
| **Indrabhattaraka Varma** | title **Satyasraya**; **Ghatikas**; defeated by Maukhari **Ishanavarma**, killed in the Kalinga war |
| **Vikramendra Varma II** | **Chikkulla**, **Tundi** and second Tummalagudem plates |
| **Manchana Bhattaraka** | **last** Vishnukundin ruler |
- **Indrapalanagara** (**Sukrapuram**) = **Tummalagudem** (Telangana); capital of Madhava Varma I and Govinda Varma

## SEC 1:54:3 | Madhava Varma II
- Rise of Vishnukundins — after **Samudragupta's** southern campaign (**358 CE**) weakened the **Pallavas**
- **Madhava Varma II** conquered the Vaishnava **Salankayanas** of **Vengi** (Pedavegi, Eluru district)
- Sacrifices: **11 Ashvamedhas**, **16 Rajasuyas**, **1 Purushamedha**
- **Bezawada** inscription (12th c.) — sentenced his own **son to death** after the prince's chariot killed a poor woman's son
- **Khanapur plates** (Satara, Maharashtra) — married a daughter of Vakataka **Prithvisena II**

## SEC 1:54:4 | Capitals
- **Amarapura** — first capital; rulers called themselves **Amarapurishulu**
- **Madhava Varma II** — moved capital from Amarapuri to **Denduluru** (constant Pallava wars); fortified Amarapuri
- **Bezawada** (Vijayawada) — capital for a time after acquiring the Krishna region

## SEC 1:54:5 | Titles & inscriptions
| Title | Ruler |
|---|---|
| **Janasraya** | **Madhava Varma II** |
| Shadabhigna | Govinda Varma |
| Mahakavi | Vikramendra Varma I |
| Sakala Bhuvana Rakshabharanaikashraya | Vikramendra Varma II |
- **Trikuta-Malayadhipati** — Madhava Varma II, after defeating the **Anandagotrikas**; Trikuta = **Kotappakonda** (Guntur)
- **Chikkulla plates** (Tuni, East Godavari) — **Vikramendra Varma II**; Telugu phrase **'Vijaya rajya samvatsarambul'**
- **Tummalagudem plates** (Govinda Varma I) — **first Sanskrit inscription in Telangana**

## SEC 1:54:6 | Exam corner
- Govinda Varma's **Chaitanyapuri** inscription — in **Prakrit**; of **21** Vishnukundin inscriptions, not all were Sanskrit

## SEC 1:54:8 | Dynasty succeeded — post-Ikshvaku order
- ★ **Vijayapuri** — the **Ikshvaku** capital (not the Salankayana capital)

## SEC 1:54:9 | Vishnukundin rulers → period and contribution
- ★ APPSC Group-2 key: **Vikramendravarma** shifted the capital from Amarapuri (Amaravati region) to **Denduluru** (West Godavari)

## SEC 1:54:11 | Vishnukundin capitals → ruler and location
- ★ **Atti Varma** (Attivarman, Ananda gotra) — performed **Hiranyagarbha** danam; recorded in the **Gorantla plates**

# ROW 1:55 | Vishnukundin society, religion, administration and coinage
## SEC 1:55:0 | Administrative units
- Feudalisation view: tax-free **Brahmadeyas** with administrative rights created a **landed intermediary class**

## SEC 1:55:1 | Officers & terms
| Officer / term | Function |
|---|---|
| **Gulmika** | military officer over **border areas** |
| Akshapataladhikrita | kept records |
| Rajjuka | measured land with ropes |
| Phaladara | measured grain, fixed the king's share |
| **Bhaga** | tax of **one-sixth** of produce |
| Vishti | forced labour (Telugu **vetti**) |
- Social conditions: **varna rules more rigid**, **women's status declined**, **child marriage and sati** increased

## SEC 1:55:2 | Coinage & trade
- Coins of **iron with a thin copper coating** (APPSC GS 2018 key); brought to light by **Rayaprolu Subrahmanyam**
- **Ship-marked** coins + title **Trisamudradhipati** — importance of **sea trade**

## SEC 1:55:3 | Religion
- **Sriparvataswami** — family deity; kings called themselves **Sriparvataswami-padanudhyata**
- **Dantamukha Swami** — installed at **Velpuru** in the **33rd** year of Madhava Varma II
- Jainism declined with the rise of **Kapalika Shaivism**; Jain shrines such as Srisailam converted; **Kumarilabhatta** attacked Jainism

## SEC 1:55:4 | Buddhist viharas
- Queen of **Govinda Varma I** — **Paramabhattarika Mahadevi Vihara**; Govinda Varma I himself — **Govinda Vihara**
- **Madhava Varma II** — called a **'destroyer of Buddhism'**; Undavalli and Mogalrajapuram became Hindu shrines
- Vikramendra Varma II — granted a village to a vihara

## SEC 1:55:5 | Education & literature
- **Indrabhattaraka Varma** — **first mention of Ghatikas** in Andhra inscriptions
- Ghatikas of Amaravati and Vengi — **14 vidyas**: 4 Vedas, 6 Vedangas, Mimamsa, Nyaya, Dharmashastra, Puranas
- **Janasraya Chhandovichiti** — Sanskrit work on **prosody**; Madhava Varma II (or poet **Gunaswami** in his name)
- **Vikramendra Varma I** — called **Mahakavi** in the **Indrapalanagara plates** (Shadabhigna was Govinda Varma)

## SEC 1:55:6 | Exam corner
- ★ **Sanskrit** — medium of instruction in temple colleges (**ghatikas**) of South India
- **Ghatikas** — higher colleges of **Sanskrit and Vedic** learning
- **Sanskrit** — official language of the Vishnukundin period; Telugu began to enter records
| Plates | Donee |
|---|---|
| **Tundi** plates | **Shivavarma** (Tundi village) |
| Ipur plates | Agnisarma, Easwarasarma |
| Mutukali grant | Indravarma, Agnivarma |

## SEC 1:55:9 | Coinage and trade → metal, symbols, find-spots
- **Fa-hien** — reported **cowrie shells** (**gavvalu**) used as money

## SEC 1:55:12 | Grants → donees (agraharas)
- Vishnukundin features: **Vedic-Puranic revival**, temple and **agrahara** economy, **first Hindu cave temples**
- Brahmanical learning did **not** disappear; Ghatikas were key centres

## SEC 1:55:14 | Books → authors and language
- ★ **Janasraya** ('refuge of the people') — title of Vishnukundin **Madhavavarma**; linked to the prosody treatise **Janashraya Chandovichiti**
- Vishnukundins succeeded the Ikshvakus in the **Krishna–Godavari** region

# ROW 1:56 | Vishnukundin art and architecture - Undavalli, Mogalrajapuram and Bhairavakonda caves
## SEC 1:56:0 | Caves at a glance
- ★ **Undavalli** caves — **Vishnukundins**; rock-cut, multi-storied cave with a large reclining **Vishnu**
- Undavalli — on the **Krishna** (not Godavari) near **Vijayawada** (APPSC GS 2019 key), Guntur district
  - monolithic reclining **Ananta Padmanabha Swamy**
| Cave | Association |
|---|---|
| **Bhairavakonda** (Nellore) | **Shiva** and the **Kapalika** sect |
| Undavalli | Ananta Padmanabha Swamy |
| Mogalrajapuram | Trimurti, Durga, Nataraja |
| Akkanna-Madanna (Indrakeeladri) | Shaiva |
| Bojjannakonda | Visakhapatnam |
| Keesaragutta, Chaitanyapuri | Hyderabad |
- **Mogalrajapuram** — **five** caves; fifth is largest, with **Shiva Tandava Nataraja**

## SEC 1:56:1 | Remember by number
- **Undavalli** — **four** storeys, three main caves; carved **top-down** (fine upper levels, crude ground floor)
- **Bhairavakonda** — **eight** rock-cut caves (Kapalika Shaiva centre)

## SEC 1:56:2 | Undavalli: changing use
- Undavalli — first a **Jain abode** → used by Buddhist monks → converted into a Hindu temple

## SEC 1:56:3 | Architecture terms
- Mogalrajapuram **Caves 1–3** — sanctums behind a pillared mandapa; **Trimurti** (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva)
  - **Cave 4** — Durga; **Cave 5** — largest, Shiva Tandava Nataraja
- Typical Vishnukundin cave plan — **pillared mandapa** (ardhamandapa) in front of rock-cut **garbhagriha** cells

## SEC 1:56:4 | Vishnukundin vs Pallava caves
- Pallava **Mahendravarman I** caves — **7th century CE**; Vishnukundin caves — 4th–6th centuries
- Mahendravarman I's style (**lion-based pillars**) later taken up by the **Telugu Chodas** and **Eastern Chalukyas**

## SEC 1:56:5 | Attribution & style
- **Guptas** — earliest Hindu cave temples in India (**Udayagiri**, Madhya Pradesh)
- **Vishnukundins** — first Hindu cave temples in the **Telugu states**
- **Madhava Varma I** — credited with **Undavalli, Bhairavakonda and Mogalrajapuram** (APP and CDX)
  - APHQ dates Bhairavakonda to the **Pallava** period (7th–8th c.)
- Style — blend of **Ikshvaku and Vakataka** traditions, with Pallava borrowings
- **Telugu Mahotsavam** (two-day, AP govt) at Undavalli caves — **25–26 July 2026**
