# ROW 3:64 | Industrial sickness - meaning, causes, consequences and remedial measures
## SEC 3:64:0 | Who is sick?
### SICA definition
- **Original SICA (1985)**: registered for at least **7 years**; accumulated losses ≥ **net worth**; **cash losses**
- **1993 amendment**: registration cut to **5 years**; **cash-loss test dropped**
### RBI norm (micro / small units)
- Sick if any borrowal account is **NPA for more than 3 months**, or **50% net-worth erosion**
### Consequences and the shift to IBC
- Real consequences: NPAs, locked capital, unemployment, **revenue loss to the exchequer** (not an increase in revenue)
- **SICA → IBC** shift strengthened **creditors**; SICA criticised for protecting defaulting promoters and BIFR cases pending for years

## SEC 3:64:1 | Causes
| Cause | Type |
|---|---|
| **Obsolete technology** | **Internal production** cause |
| Poor working-capital management | Internal finance |
| Competition | External demand |
| EXIM changes | External policy |

## SEC 3:64:2 | Consequences
- State takeover of sick private **textile** and **engineering** units — mainly to **protect employment**
- Sickness concentrated in **textiles** (cotton mills), **jute**, **sugar** and **engineering**
- One-time restructuring of stressed **MSME loans** without asset-classification downgrade — **2019**; extended in 2020-21

## SEC 3:64:3 | Institutions over time
- **Industrial Reconstruction** Corporation / Bank of India — set up **1971** to rehabilitate sick units
- **SICA** — enacted **1985**, after the **Tiwari Committee** (1984-85); **BIFR** set up **January 1987**
- ★ **National Renewal Fund** — **retraining of labour**; financed **voluntary retirement**, retraining and redeployment of workers displaced by restructuring or closure
- ★ **"Golden Handshake"** — **Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS)**; used to reduce surplus staff; offered by both public and private sector companies

# ROW 3:65 | Industrial licensing, MRTP to Competition Act; disinvestment and PSE policy
## SEC 3:65:0 | Acts in order
### Licensing
- **IDRA, 1951** — **licensing** of new or expanded industrial units; **Development Councils**
- **Hazari Report (1967)** and **Dutt Committee (1969)** — licensing favoured **large business houses** → MRTP framework
- **Leather** delicensed in **1993**
- Five categories still licensed: **alcohol**, **cigarettes**, **hazardous chemicals**, **electronic aerospace and defence equipment**, **industrial explosives**
### Replacements
| Old | New |
|---|---|
| ★ **FERA** | **FEMA** |

| MRTP Commission | **Competition Commission of India** |

- MRTP Act recommended by the **Dutt Committee** (Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee)
- **Competition Act** — enacted **2002**, commenced **31 March 2003** (not 2016)
### AP
- After 1991, **APIIC** became a **facilitator** of industrial development

## SEC 3:65:1 | MRTP thresholds
- **MRTP Act** — enacted **1969**, in force **1970**
- **New Industrial Policy, 1991** — **scrapped** the MRTP asset threshold of **Rs 100 crore**; large firms no longer needed prior approval for expansion, mergers or takeovers due to asset size

## SEC 3:65:2 | Competition Act & CCI
### Competition Act and CCI
- **Competition Act, 2002** — recommended by **S.V.S. Raghavan Committee** (2000)
- **CCI** — set up **14 October 2003**; under **Ministry of Corporate Affairs**; **Chairperson + 2-6 members**
| Section | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Section 3** | Anti-competitive agreements: **cartels**, **bid-rigging**, price-fixing |
| **Section 4** | Abuse of dominance: unfair pricing, denial of market access |
| **Sections 5-6** | Combinations: M&A above thresholds |
### Related terms and bodies
- **Disinvestment** — sale of government equity in PSEs; **liberalisation** — removal of licences, quotas, controls
- **BIFR** (created under SICA 1985) — dissolved **1 December 2016**; cases went to NCLT / NCLAT
- **IBC** — enacted **28 May 2016** after the **T.K. Viswanathan**-led BLRC (2015)
- **NCLT** — set up **1 June 2016** under **Section 408**, Companies Act 2013; **NCLAT** — **Section 410**

## SEC 3:65:3 | Key terms
- **Disinvestment** — reduces government **equity** (government may keep control); **privatisation** — transfers **ownership and management control** to the private sector
- **Minority disinvestment** — government retains **more than 51%** and control; **majority disinvestment** — transfers control, keeps a minority stake
- **Strategic sale** — block of shares, usually **50% or more**, with **transfer of management control** to a strategic partner
- **DIPAM** instruments: **CPSE ETF**, Offer for Sale, IPO/FPO, strategic sale (SLR, CRR, repo are monetary/banking tools)
- Concerns about disinvestment: **undervaluation**, loss of **dividend income**, **national-security** risks (oil, defence, transport)
- **Asset monetisation** — **leases** public assets for a fixed period, ownership stays with government; **disinvestment** — **permanent sale** of equity

## SEC 3:65:4 | Disinvestment bodies

- **Rangarajan Committee (1993)** — disinvestment up to **49%** in PSUs reserved for the public sector; up to **74%** in others
- ★ **Disinvestment Commission (1996)** — first Chairman **G.V. Ramakrishna**

- **NLMC** — monetises **surplus land and buildings** of CPSEs, including loss-making ones
- **National Investment Fund** — **professionally managed**; in the **Public Account**; set up **2005** (1995-96 is RIDF)
### AP
- PPP ports in Andhra Pradesh: **Krishnapatnam** and **Gangavaram**

# ROW 3:68 | SEZs - policy, performance and criticism; industrial corridors (DMIC, CBIC, AKIC) and NICDC
## SEC 3:68:0 | EPZ to SEZ
### EPZ era
- **Kandla** — India's and **Asia's first EPZ**, **1965**
- **Surat** — first **private-sector EPZ**; converted into an SEZ on **1 November 2000**
### SEZ policy and Act
- **SEZ Policy** — announced **April 2000** through the **EXIM Policy 2000-01**
- **SEZ Act** — passed **May 2005**; Presidential assent **23 June 2005**; in force **10 February 2006**
- SEZ may be set up by the **Central Government, a State Government or any person** (public, private, joint sector), jointly or severally
- **Section 18** — allows an **IFSC** in an SEZ; only **one IFSC per SEZ**
- **DESH** proposal — removes the **positive Net Foreign Exchange** condition, allows DTA sales; still pending, has not replaced the SEZ Act

# ROW 3:71 | Economic reforms of 1991 - background, stabilisation and structural adjustment measures
## SEC 3:71:0 | Persons & committees
### Persons
- ★ First phase of liberalisation (**1991**) — under PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**
- **P.V. Narasimha Rao** — "**father of Indian economic reforms**"; **Dr. Manmohan Singh** — architect of liberalisation
- **C. Rajagopalachari** — coined "**Licence-Permit-Quota Raj**"
- Some liberalisation under **Rajiv Gandhi** (1980s); 1991 reforms far more comprehensive
### Committees
| Committee | Area |
|---|---|
| **Narasimham (1991)** | **Financial-sector reform** — interest deregulation, private banks (HDFC, ICICI) |
| **C. Rangarajan** | **Disinvestment of PSUs** |
| Raja Chelliah | Tax reforms (1991) |
| Malhotra | Insurance |
| Tarapore | Capital account convertibility |
### Terms
- **Dereservation** — cut in industries reserved for the public sector (1991)
- **Liberalisation** — removes domestic controls via de-licensing, import deregulation, private-sector expansion
- **Import liberalisation** — cutting **tariffs** and **QRs**
- **Globalisation** — outcome of liberalisation and privatisation; **outsourcing** a major feature
- LPG reforms opened the door to **multinational corporations**
- After financial reforms, **RBI** to shift from regulator to **facilitator**

## SEC 3:71:1 | Road to the crisis
- Long-term cause: persistent **fiscal profligacy** and rising fiscal deficit (others were immediate triggers)
- **NEP 1991** — response to a severe **Balance of Payments crisis**; reserves covered only about **two weeks** of imports

## SEC 3:71:2 | Crisis indicators
### Crisis
- **Gulf remittances** dried up (did not rise)
- Forex reserves, **June 1991**: about **$975 million** — about **two weeks** of imports
- Centre's gross fiscal deficit **1990-91**: **8.4% of GDP** (6.1% in 1980-81; 9.4% = Centre + States)
### Measures
- Rupee market-determined **1993**; **current-account convertible 1994**; capital account stayed partial
- Fiscal measures: **tax net widened** (not contracted), expenditure control, subsidy cuts, better tax collection
- **Tax rates cut**, **tax base widened**; MRTP asset limits scrapped; interest rates deregulated; private banks allowed
- Delicensed firms: **pricing freedom**, **no expansion curbs**; licensing only for **5-6** industries
- 1991 policy abolished MRTP asset limits, pre-entry restrictions, merger/expansion controls (SEBI's status was a separate reform)
- **Navratna** scheme — initially **9** PSUs (distinct from 97 Miniratnas)
- Remaining **QRs** removed **April 2001**; average tariffs about 125% → 30%; customs duty later 10%
- PSU **disinvestment** first used to **bridge the fiscal deficit**
### Outcomes and context
- After globalisation, **imports grew faster than exports**
- **Private airlines** entered civil aviation; power-distribution privatisation remained debated
- **Washington Consensus** (ten rules): privatisation, deregulation, property rights (not expansion of import quotas)

## SEC 3:71:3 | July 1991 response
- About **67 tonnes** of gold pledged (Bank of England and UBS); IMF loan
- Rupee **devalued** in two steps — **1 and 3 July 1991**
- Devaluation aim: make **exports cheaper**, correct the **balance of payments**

## SEC 3:71:4 | Stabilisation vs structural adjustment
| | Stabilisation | Structural adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Short-term**, **demand management** | **Long-term**, **supply-side** |
| Lead institution | **IMF** | **World Bank (IBRD)** |
- **SAPs** — IMF and World Bank loan packages to reduce **fiscal imbalances** and adjust for **long-term growth**
- **Eighth Plan (1992-97)** — began right after stabilisation and structural adjustment
- **Free trade** — reduction or removal of trade barriers (tariffs, quotas)

## SEC 3:71:5 | Trade & exchange sequence
- Sequence: **devaluation** (July 1991) → industrial **delicensing** → removal of EXIM scrips and import quotas → **market-determined exchange rate** (1993)
- **LERMS** — **March 1992** (60% market rate, 40% official); unified market rate **March 1993**
- **SEBI** statutory status — **1992**; **FEMA** replaced FERA — **1999**
- India — **founding member** of the **WTO (1995)**; WTO succeeded **GATT** (1948, 23 countries)
- **IT and outsourcing** exports helped **cushion the current account deficit**

## SEC 3:71:6 | In vs not in 1991 NEP
- **1980s** groundwork: **broad-banding of licences**, easier capital-goods imports, higher MRTP limit, FERA relaxation
- Structural measures: **delicensing**, **tariff reduction**, **public-sector disinvestment**
- **Not** in NEP 1991: **nationalisation**; **demonetisation** (India's demonetisations: **1978** and **2016**)
- Post-1991 direction: lower tariffs, invite **FDI and FPI**, IT and services exports as a cushion
- Persistent **CAD** — structural cause of India's **high cost of capital**
- **Globalisation** — integration with the world economy; more MNCs, trade, FDI, technology flows

## SEC 3:71:7 | Outcomes & criticism
### Growth data
- GDP growth: below 1% (1991-92), about 5% (1992-94), **6.3%** (**1994-95**)
- Forex reserves: about **$975 million** (1991) → about **$701.4 billion** (about 11 months of import cover)
### Criticism and trends
- Growth pattern **neglected agriculture**; **capital-intensive** growth created too few jobs
- **Jobless growth** — **output rose faster than employment**
- **Crony capitalism** — gains concentrated among large, **politically connected** firms
- **Railways** — remained largely a **state monopoly**
- After 1991, **agriculture's share of GDP fell**; forex reserves, FDI and India's share of world exports rose

## SEC 3:71:8 | Exam corner
- 1991 crisis: **high inflation**, very **low forex reserves**, large **fiscal deficit**
- **Export-led growth** — came with the **LPG reforms of 1991** (import substitution → export promotion); Mahalanobis model (Second Plan) — heavy industry, import substitution
- Post-reform economy: **greater global integration**, larger **private sector**
