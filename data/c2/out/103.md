# ROW 5:29 | Cyber security concerns - types of cyber crime, malware, ransomware, phishing, social engineering
## SEC 5:29:0 | Malware types
### Malware
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Virus** | Attaches to files; usually needs user to **run** the infected file |
| **Worm** | **Self-replicates** across networks **without user interaction** |
| **Trojan** | Disguised as legitimate software; **no self-replication**; opens a **backdoor** |
| **Adware** | Unwanted **ads**; bundled with free/pirated software |
| **Ransomware** | Encrypts files for ransom |
| **Rootkit** | **Administrator-level** access; **hides other malware**; hard to detect |
| **Botnet** | Hijacked devices, e.g. **Mirai** (IoT devices, large DDoS) |
### Named cases
- **WannaCry (2017)** — **ransomware that spread as a worm**; used **EternalBlue** (developed by US **NSA**)
- **Stuxnet** — worm with rootkit components; sabotaged **Siemens PLC**-controlled uranium centrifuges at Iran's **Natanz**
- **Pegasus** — zero-click spyware; **NSO Group, Israel**; exploits zero-days
### Cryptography
- **AES** — **symmetric** (same key encrypts and decrypts); **RSA** — asymmetric, underlies **PKI**

## SEC 5:29:1 | Named malware
- **Brain (1986)** — first IBM-PC boot-sector virus; from **Lahore, Pakistan**
- **Creeper (1971)** — first self-replicating program; **Elk Cloner** — infected **Apple II**
- **Petya** and **WannaCry** — **ransomware** (APPSC-G2 2025)
  - Petya encrypts the **master boot record**; WannaCry spread as a worm via **EternalBlue**

## SEC 5:29:2 | Attack techniques
- ★ **Phishing** — deceptive emails/texts asking to install software or divulge personal info
- **SolarWinds (2020)** — **supply-chain attack**; **Pegasus** — example of a **zero-day exploit**
- **Mirai** — **botnet** used in **DDoS** (many machines deny genuine users a service)
- **Denial-of-service** — flooding a server with traffic
- **Cyber espionage** — often state-sponsored; uses spear-phishing and zero-days
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Man-in-the-middle** | Secretly intercepting/altering communication between two parties |
| **Vishing** | Phishing through **voice calls** |
| **SQL injection** | Malicious database code via input fields |
| **Spam** | Unsolicited bulk mail |
| **E-mail bombing** | Flooding an inbox |

## SEC 5:29:3 | Security tools
- **CIA triad** — exposure = **confidentiality**, alteration = **integrity**, denied access = **availability**
- **Firewall** — controls network traffic; prevents **unauthorised network access**
- **Multi-factor authentication** — password plus **OTP or biometric**
- **Honeypot** — decoy system to lure attackers and study their methods
- **CERT-In** — **CVE Numbering Authority** for products made in India; **M-Kavach** — **C-DAC Hyderabad**
- **Social engineering** — manipulating **trust, urgency or curiosity**; can be AI-enabled
- **Dark web** — hidden internet, reached via **Tor**; illicit marketplaces, hacking tools, data dumps
- Cyber terrorism attribution hard — **VPNs, proxies and botnets** hide the source; aims to disrupt critical infrastructure

## SEC 5:29:4 | Frauds & crimes
- **Digital arrest** — impostors posing as police/agency officials on **video call** frighten victim and extort money
- SC: SOPs by **Centre, States, RBI and telecom authorities**; **zero FIRs**; restoration of victims' money
- **Identity theft** — IT Act **Section 66C**; sextortion — **66E/67A**
- **QR code** — scanned only to **make** a payment, never to receive
- **Crypto-jacking** — secretly using a victim's device to **mine cryptocurrency**

# ROW 5:32 | Data protection and privacy - Puttaswamy judgment, Digital Personal Data Protection Act, data localisation
## SEC 5:32:0 | Puttaswamy (2017)
### Privacy cases
- **K.S. Puttaswamy** — unanimous **nine-judge** bench; privacy under **Article 21** and **Part III**
  - Overruled **M.P. Sharma (1954)** and **Kharak Singh (1962)** insofar as they denied privacy as constitutional protection
- **Legality** — privacy intrusion must be backed by a **law**; public objective alone not enough
- **Gobind v State of MP** — privacy part of **personal liberty**
- **PUCL v Union of India (1997)** — **interception** safeguards
- **Right to Be Forgotten** (India) — limits **name-based searches**; does not erase judicial records
### Aadhaar judgment (26 September 2018)
- Five-judge bench, **4:1**; dissent — **Justice D.Y. Chandrachud**
- Upheld: Aadhaar-**PAN/ITR** linking (Section **139AA**, Income-tax Act)
- Struck down: mandatory linking with **bank accounts** and **mobile SIMs**
### DPDP scope
- DPDP Act **excludes non-personal data**; covers some **entities outside India** offering goods/services in India

## SEC 5:32:1 | Road to DPDP Act
- **2018** draft Bill — **Justice B.N. Srikrishna Committee** (2017-18)
- 2019 PDP Bill → **2021 JPC** report: **93** amendments, covered personal and non-personal data → withdrawn **2022** → **DPDP Act 2023**
| Committee | Subject |
|---|---|
| **Kris Gopalakrishnan** | **Non-personal data** |
| A.P. Shah | Privacy |
| Srikrishna | PDP Bill |
| A.K. Bhargava | Net neutrality |
- Exemptions: notified **State agencies**, **research, archiving, statistics**, possibly **startups**; **journalism — no exemption**
- Draft DPDP Rules — **3 January 2025**; final Rules notified **14 November 2025**

## SEC 5:32:2 | DPDP terms
- Child (under **18**) — **parent or guardian** is the Data Principal
- **Significant Data Fiduciary** — **Data Protection Officer in India**, **independent auditor**, **impact assessment** (no consent exemption)
- **Consent Manager** — registered entity for a Data Principal to give, manage, withdraw consent
- Rules require: clear, specific, informed **consent**; **plain-language notices**; **purpose limitation**; data minimisation; retention controls

## SEC 5:32:3 | Penalties (maximum)
| Breach | Max penalty |
|---|---|
| Reasonable **security safeguards** failure | **₹250 crore** |
| **Children's data** obligations | **₹200 crore** |
| Additional SDF duties | **₹150 crore** |
| Residual breaches | **₹50 crore** |

## SEC 5:32:4 | Board, rights & exemptions
### DPDP provisions
- **Section 7** — **legitimate uses** (State processing without consent)
- **Section 17** — Centre may exempt notified State agencies (sovereignty, security, public order)
- **Section 12** — Right to Erasure
- **Section 44(3)** — amended **RTI Section 8(1)(j)**; all personal info may be refused; public-interest override removed
- Verifiable **parental consent** below **18 years**; tracking, behavioural monitoring, targeted ads at children banned
- **Data Protection Board** — HQ **New Delhi**; members appointed by Centre; appeals to **TDSAT**
- DPDP Rules — full compliance **18 months** after notification → **May 2027**; Consent Manager provisions **November 2026**
### Other
- **Pegasus** technical committee — supervised by **Justice R.V. Raveendran**; malware on **5 of 29** phones, not conclusively Pegasus
- First national standards for cloud, data centres, ethical AI — **bias**, **data sovereignty**, **privacy**; linked to DPDP Act
- **GDPR Article 17** — Right to Erasure; RTBF originated in **Google Spain v. AEPD (2014)**

## SEC 5:32:5 | DPDP vs GDPR
- Data Principal rights: **information and access**, **correction**, **erasure**, **grievance redress**, **withdrawal of consent**, **nomination**
- Cross-border transfer — **blacklist (negative-list)**: allowed unless Centre restricts a country
- **Data Fiduciary** — DPDP term; GDPR says **Data Controller**

- **GDPR** — adopted **April 2016**; implemented **25 May 2018**
