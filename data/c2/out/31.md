## SEC 1:240:1 | August Offer, 1940
- First official acceptance that **Indians would frame their own constitution**
  - Demand raised by **M.N. Roy (1934)**; adopted by Congress **1935**
- Response to the **Ramgarh** position — war support if a **provisional national government** formed at the Centre
- **Congress rejected** it (Nehru: dominion status "**dead as a doornail**")
- **Muslim League welcomed** its minority veto; held only partition would satisfy it

## SEC 1:240:2 | Individual satyagrahis
- ★ **Vinoba Bhave** — started the Individual Civil Disobedience
- Aim — assert **freedom of speech**; oppose helping the British war effort with men or money; anti-war speeches, successive arrests
- First three satyagrahis — **Vinoba Bhave**, **Jawaharlal Nehru**, **Brahma Dutt**

## SEC 1:240:4 | Cripps proposals (1942)
### August Offer (contrast)
- Proposed a **post-war constitution-making body** (first accepted here), more Indians in the Executive Council, a **War Advisory Council**
- **Not** included — immediate transfer of **defence**
### Cripps proposals
- **Dominion status** after the war, with power to **secede**
- '**Fatal flaw**' — **provincial option** to stay outside the Union; first conceded by Cripps; germ of Partition / Pakistan
- Defence and army in the interim — **Britain**; Governor-General's powers retained
- Congress negotiators — **Nehru and Maulana Azad**; objected to the right to secede and no immediate transfer of power
- Outcome — **rejected**, negotiations failed

## SEC 1:240:5 | Cripps: why it failed
### Background
- Sent by **Churchill**'s government under pressure from **Franklin Roosevelt** and **Chiang Kai-shek**
- **Cripps** — **Labour** member of Churchill's **War Cabinet**
- **Muslim League** rejected — single Indian Union; Pakistan not explicitly conceded
### Quit India causes
- ★ Disappointment with the **Cripps Mission**; ★ threat of **Japanese attack**
  - ★ Not caused by Gandhi's articles provoking violent means
### Chronology
- ★ CDM (1930) → Individual Satyagraha (1940) → Cripps (March 1942) → Quit India (August 1942)
- ★ August Offer (8 August 1940) → Quit India (1942) → **INA trial (1945)** → **RIN Ratings Revolt (1946)**
- ★ August Offer → Cripps → Quit India → **Desai-Liaquat Pact (1945)**
- ★ Ministries resign (Oct 1939) → **Ramgarh** (53rd session, March 1940) → August Offer → Cripps

## SEC 1:240:10 | Cripps Mission, 1942 — context and proposals
- ★ Sent in **March 1942** by PM **Winston Churchill** (PM 1940-45, 1951-55); led by **Sir Stafford Cripps**
- ★ Official Congress negotiators — **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Maulana Azad**
- ★ Key proposals
  - ★ Indian Union with **Dominion status** immediately after the war; right to **secede** from the Commonwealth
  - ★ Indians to form their own **constitution**
  - ★ Any **province or State** could stay **outside** the Indian Union
- ★ **Cripps** — "**Here is a revolution taking place and we should move quickly**"; also member of the **Cabinet Mission (1946)**

## SEC 1:240:11 | Individual Satyagraha — satyagrahis, place, aim, Andhra
- ★ Launched by Gandhi, **October 1940** (17 Oct) after rejection of the August Offer — free speech; opposition to being dragged into WWII
- ★ First satyagrahi — **Vinoba Bhave**; ★ second — **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- ★ Vinoba began from **Pavnar (Paunar), Maharashtra**

## SEC 1:240:12 | Responses, failure and results
- ★ Congress **rejected** the Cripps proposals — only dominion status, provincial opt-out, princely-state nominees, no immediate transfer of power
  - ★ "Mission solely of whites" — true but **not** the reason
- ★ **Quit India Movement** — launched **8 August 1942**, Bombay AICC; response to the **Cripps Proposals**

# ROW 1:241 | Quit India Movement 1942 - causes, course, underground activity, repression, significance
## SEC 1:241:0 | Causes → launch
### Background
- **Cripps Mission** offered **no immediate self-government**; its failure (March–April 1942) a major cause of Quit India
- **Rangoon** fell to Japan — **March 1942**
- ★ Britain freed India in 1947 — Britain had become **weak** in WWII (A and R true, R explains A)
### Resolution and launch
- **Wardha** CWC resolution (**14 July 1942**) — proposed by **Nehru**, seconded by **Patel**; ratified by AICC, **Bombay**, **8 August 1942**
- ★ AICC session — **Gowalia Tank Maidan** (now August Kranti Ground), **Bombay**, 7–8 August 1942; movement began **9 August 1942**
- Resolution — immediate end of British rule, **provisional government** after withdrawal, mass civil disobedience under Gandhi
  - **Not** in it — continuing British rule until the war ended
- '**Quit India**' slogan — coined by **Yusuf Meherally**; Gandhi suggested the phrase in **Harijan** (April 1942)
- Gandhi's paper in 1942 — **Harijan** (Young India ceased 1932)
- **Operation Zero Hour** — pre-dawn arrest of Congress leaders, **9 August 1942**
### Course
- ★ **Parallel governments** — **Ballia** (August 1942, **Chittu Pandey**), **Satara**, Tamluk (Midnapore)
- ★ **Yogendra Shukla** — escaped **Hazaribagh** jail with JP (9 Nov 1942); arrested at Muzaffarpur, sent to **Patna**
### Other
- **Surendranath Banerjee** — "**Partition fell on us like a bomb**"
