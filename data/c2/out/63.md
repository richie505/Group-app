# ROW 3:27 | Demographic transition theory and India's position; demographic dividend and its window
## SEC 3:27:0 | Stages of demographic transition
### The stages
| Stage | Birth rate | Death rate |
|---|---|---|
| I | high | high |
| II | **high** | **falling** — produces a **population explosion** |
| III | falling | low |
| IV | low | low |
- India — late **Stage 3**, moving into **Stage 4**
- ★ **Stability of population** — birth rate = death rate and no internal/external migration → no change in population
- ★ **Urbanization** in India has reduced both **birth rate and death rate** (education, work, better medical care)
### Demographic dividend
- ★ Full benefit of the dividend needs **skill development** (bridging demand-supply gap of skills)
- Without health, education, skills and jobs for the working-age population, the dividend may turn into a **demographic nightmare** (Economic Survey)

## SEC 3:27:2 | Terms → meaning
- ★ **Total fertility rate (TFR)** — total live births a hypothetical woman would have through the reproductive age group, at the age-specific fertility rates
- ★ Highest maternal mortality in Asia (among options) — **Nepal** (UNFPA State of World Population 2025)
| Measure | Base |
|---|---|
| **MMR** (maternal mortality ratio) | per **1,00,000 live births** |
| CBR / CDR | per 1,000 mid-year population |
| IMR | per 1,000 live births |
| Maternal mortality rate | women aged 15-49 |
- **Dependency ratio** = (**0–14** + **65 and above**) ÷ **15–64**
- **Working-age population** — **15-64** years; young dependents below 15, old dependents 65+
- **Youth** (National Youth Policy 2014) — **15–29** years

## SEC 3:27:3 | SRS 2024 vital rates
### India and State extremes
- **IMR** of India (SRS 2024) — **24** (25 in 2023, 47 in 2010)
  - **Chhattisgarh** highest State IMR (36); under-five mortality rate 28
- Lowest **CDR** — **Delhi** (4.5), not Kerala
- Highest **CBR** — **Bihar**; lowest CBR — **Goa**; highest CDR — **Chhattisgarh**
- ★ Lowest urban IMR (among options) — **Kerala** (SRS 2023 urban IMR: Kerala 5, Maharashtra 10, Tamil Nadu 11, Gujarat 15)
### Dividend and AP
- Reducing **female labour-force participation** would undermine the demographic dividend; health, education, skills and jobs are needed to realise it
- **Andhra Pradesh** — **Stage 4**, a low-fertility setting; not in the same wide dividend-window phase as Bihar and Uttar Pradesh

## SEC 3:27:5 | State typology (RBI)
| Phase | States |
|---|---|
| **Ageing** (falling working-age share) | **Kerala**, **Tamil Nadu** |
| Wide dividend window | Bihar, Uttar Pradesh |
| Narrowing window | Telangana, Uttarakhand |
- Kerala's 60+ population projected at **22.8%** by **2036** — end of its demographic-dividend phase
- Ageing + falling working-age share → rising **pension-related** social expenditure pressure in Kerala and Tamil Nadu

## SEC 3:27:6 | Birth-rate determinants
- ★ Rising **Couple Protection Rate** — birth rate falls (most effectively) only if couples are of **younger age**
- ★ **Infant mortality rate** — proportion of children that die before the age of **1**
- ★ In India **more male** children are born each year than female (Census); infant mortality among **boys** is higher than among girls
- ★ Low **sex ratio** causes — high maternity death rate, high female child death rate, **female foeticide**, and birth of more male children (usually **105 boys per 100 girls**)
- India's **dependency ratio** is falling primarily due to a **fall in fertility** (fewer children)
- **Access to contraception** is a determinant of the birth rate

## SEC 3:27:7 | Exam corner
- Life expectancy at **Independence** — about **32 years**
- **Lower dependency ratio** — fewer dependants per worker → potential **demographic dividend**

## SEC 3:27:8 | Demographic transition — stages and India's position
- ★ Order of stages with development — **high birth & high death** → **high birth & low death** → **low birth & low death**
- Gradual change in the manner of population growth over a long period — **demographic transition**
- ★ **Crude death rate** of India — **below 9** per thousand (7.6 in 2005; **6.4** in 2023, SRS)
- ★ Lowest death rate in **2015** (among options) — **Maharashtra**
| State | CDR 2015 | CDR 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| **Maharashtra** | 5.8 | 6.1 |
| West Bengal | 5.9 | 5.7 |
| Gujarat | 6.1 | 6.4 |
| Karnataka | 6.6 | 6.8 |

## SEC 3:27:9 | Age groups and terms → meaning (CBR, CDR, MMR, dependency ratio, youth)
### Demographic dividend
- ★ **Demographic dividend** — due to India's high population in the **15-64** age group
- ★ **Demographic bonus** (around 2016) — a surge in the population of the **productive (working) age group**
- ★ Dependent population in the population pyramid — **0-14** years (65+ also dependent; 15-64 working)
- ★ **Dependency ratio** declining because the **15-59** population is relatively high; ratio fell from 79% (1975) to 58% (2008) to about 48% (2021)

# ROW 3:29 | Poverty - concepts (absolute, relative, chronic), poverty line debates
## SEC 3:29:0 | Types of poverty
- ★ **Culture of poverty** — **Oscar Lewis** (American anthropologist); poverty passed on as a way of life
  - Amartya Sen — capability approach
- ★ Poverty in India defined in terms of **calorie intake**
- **Absolute poverty** — defined by the UN at the **World Summit for Social Development, Copenhagen, 1995**; minimum survival resources; fixed calorie norm belongs here
- **Relative poverty** — income well below the **median** income of society (not a fixed calorie norm)
- **Subjective poverty** — personal perception
- **Chronic poverty** — persists over long periods or across **generations**; transient/cyclical poverty is temporary (shocks, seasons, cycles)

## SEC 3:29:1 | Poverty measures (FGT family)
| Measure | Captures |
|---|---|
| **Head Count Ratio** | incidence |
| **Poverty Gap Index** | depth |
| **Squared Poverty Gap** | **severity** (more weight to the poorest) |
- All three belong to the **Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT)** family
- Poor become poorer, number unchanged → **HCR** unchanged; PGI changes

## SEC 3:29:2 | Poverty line in India
- ★ Survey for poverty line — **NSSO** (household consumption expenditure survey)
- ★ Basis of poverty estimates — **household consumption expenditure**, not income (incomes of self-employed and daily wage workers hard to assess, seasonal fluctuations)
  - **MPCE** an important basis; poor when consumption falls below the line
- **Poverty Line Basket** — socially accepted minimum basket of basic needs **bought in the market**
- **Engel's Law** — as income rises, share spent on **food falls** (absolute amount may rise)
- **MMRP** — **7-day** recall for some food items; records about 10-12% higher consumption → **lower** poverty
  - First used by **Rangarajan**; Tendulkar moved from URP to MRP

## SEC 3:29:10 | Poverty line in India — basis, calorie norm, Tendulkar vs Rangarajan

# ROW 3:32 | Inequality - Lorenz curve, Gini coefficient, Kuznets hypothesis; income vs wealth inequality in India
## SEC 3:32:0 | Persons → curves
- **Gini coefficient** — **Corrado Gini**, **1912**, "Variability and Mutability"
- **Laffer curve** — tax rate vs tax revenue
- **Phillips curve** — inflation vs unemployment

## SEC 3:32:1 | Lorenz curve & Gini
- ★ **Lorenz curve** — measures **inequality of income**; **Max O. Lorenz** (American), **1905**
- 45-degree line = perfect equality; the farther the curve bows away, the **greater** the inequality
- **Gini** = area between Lorenz curve and equality line ÷ whole area under the equality line
- AP district-level **MPI gaps** — a measure of **intra-State inequality**

## SEC 3:32:2 | Kuznets curves
- **Kuznets hypothesis** — inequality first rises, then falls with growth → **inverted U**
- **Environmental Kuznets Curve** (per-capita income vs degradation) — **inverted-U**
- NSSO/NSO household inequality indicator — **MPCE** (monthly per capita consumption expenditure)

## SEC 3:32:3 | Inequality in India: dimensions
| Inequality | Data source |
|---|---|
| Consumption | **NSS/HCES** (MoSPI) |
| Income | PLFS wages, tax data, WID |
| Wealth | **AIDIS** |

# ROW 3:33 | Unemployment - types (structural, frictional, cyclical, disguised, seasonal, educated)
## SEC 3:33:0 | Types → cause & example
| Type | Cause / feature | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Disguised** | withdrawing some workers leaves **output unchanged** (zero marginal productivity) | agriculture, family farms |
| **Cyclical** | fall in **aggregate demand** (recession) | industry and services |
| **Educated** | qualified persons fail to find matching jobs | **urban** |
| Technological | change in technology | manufacturing |
| **Frictional** | transition between jobs (e.g. graduate seeking first job) | |
- India — mainly **structural and disguised**; developed countries — mainly **cyclical** (demand-deficient)
- **Underemployment** — not using full skills or hours (surplus labour with zero output is disguised unemployment)

## SEC 3:33:1 | Frictional vs structural
- **Structural** — long-term **skill-job mismatch**; frictional — short-term job search
- **Technological** unemployment — a subtype of **structural**

## SEC 3:33:2 | Paired terms
- Disguised unemployment not captured in PLFS headline rates — persons **appear employed**
- **Invisible underemployment** — full-time work at very low income / below ability (MBA working as a driver)
- Labour force counts only **involuntary** unemployment
- **Natural rate** of unemployment = **frictional + structural**
- Falling unemployment rate without real job creation — **distress entry** into low-productivity work

## SEC 3:33:3 | Economists
- **Keynes** — unemployment in developed economies from **deficiency in effective demand**
- **Natural rate** of unemployment — **Milton Friedman**
- **Friedman and Phelps** — long-run Phillips curve **vertical** at the natural rate
- **Stagflation** (high inflation + high unemployment) — breaks the Phillips-curve trade-off
- **Okun's law** — 1 percentage point unemployment above natural rate ≈ **2% fall** in output below potential GDP

## SEC 3:33:4 | Educated unemployment data
- **Seasonal** and **disguised** unemployment — mainly **rural**; educated unemployment — mainly urban
- India's basic problem — **structural** (with seasonal, disguised and underemployment)
- At full employment, **frictional and structural** unemployment may still exist
| PLFS 2025 group | Rate |
|---|---|
| Youth 18-29 | 14.8% |
| **Tertiary-educated** youth 18-29 | **29.4%** |
| Secondary schooling and above | 6.5% |
| Gen Z (15-26) | 11.9% |
- 40.1% of tertiary-educated youth are **NEET**

## SEC 3:33:5 | Why India's unemployment is structural
- Structural example — labour-intensive economy shifting to **capital-intensive** production
- Causes — **rapid population growth**, **labour immobility**, shift to capital-intensive production
- Expansion of labour-intensive manufacturing would **reduce**, not cause, structural unemployment
- **Jobless growth** — growth driven by capital-intensive sectors (**IT, finance**) rather than labour-intensive manufacturing

## SEC 3:33:7 | Types of unemployment → cause, nature, example
- ★ **Disguised unemployment** — **marginal productivity of labour is zero**; more persons employed than needed
- ★ Disguised unemployment in India — **agricultural sector** and **rural areas**
- ★ **Labour-intensive** industry — employs **more hands**
- ★ **Educated unemployment** rises with high growth when there is a lack of **professional education** (both true, R explains A)
- ★ India — a **labour-surplus economy**; projected skilled-labour surplus about 245.3 million by 2030
- ★ Maximum percentage of educated unemployed — **Kerala** (K.P. Kannan)

## SEC 3:33:11 | Economists → laws and statements on unemployment
- ★ **Phillips Curve** — **inflation and unemployment** (inverse); A.W. Phillips, **1958**

# ROW 3:34 | Measurement of unemployment - UPS, UPSS, CWS, CDS; PLFS methodology and recent findings
## SEC 3:34:0 | Four approaches
| Approach | Reference / unit | Counted employed if |
|---|---|---|
| **UPS** | **365 days** | worked major part, generally **183 days** or more |
| **UPSS** | 365 days | also subsidiary work for at least **30 days** |
| **CWS** | **7 days** | worked at least **one hour** on any day — captures short-term/seasonal unemployment |
| **CDS** | **half a day** | 4 or more hours = employed for that day |
- **CDS** gives the **highest** unemployment rate — counts days of unemployment even for persons employed under CWS

## SEC 3:34:1 | Labour indicators
- **Unemployment Rate** — unemployed ÷ **labour force**; Proportion Unemployed — unemployed ÷ population
- **Worker Population Ratio** — **employed ÷ total population**; WPR = LFPR × (1 − UR)
- **LFPR** — employed + unemployed per 100 persons (labour force over working-age population)
- **PLFS** — conducted by the **National Statistical Office** under **MoSPI**

## SEC 3:34:2 | Committees & agencies
### Agencies
- ★ **Labour Bureau** (1920) — compiles labour statistics incl. industrial disputes, closures, retrenchments and lay-offs
- Labour Bureau (**Ministry of Labour and Employment**) — Employment–Unemployment Survey and **Quarterly Employment Survey**
- **Standing Committee on Labour Force Statistics** — set up on advice of the **National Statistical Commission**; designed PLFS
- **PLFS** launched **April 2017**; first annual report (2017–18) released May 2019
- **CMIE** data — **monthly**, larger sample, different methodology
### Committees and terms
- **Bhagwati Committee** (**1973**) — reviewed unemployment estimates and employment-oriented policies
- **Dantwala Committee** — rejected a single number; **separate estimates** by status (usual, weekly, daily) and by category
- Census **main worker** — **183 days** or more in the year; less = marginal worker

## SEC 3:34:3 | PLFS milestones
- PLFS revamp (January 2025) — sample **raised** (not reduced) to about **2,72,304** households

## SEC 3:36:1 | MGNREGA milestones
- NREGA launched **2 February 2006** in **200 districts**; all districts from **1 April 2008**

- Significance — **safety net** during agrarian distress and crises such as **COVID-19**

## SEC 3:36:2 | MGNREGA features
### Entitlement
- Objective — livelihood security through at least **100 days** of guaranteed wage employment per financial year
- Unit of guarantee — the **rural household** (not each adult)
- For adult members volunteering for **unskilled manual work**
- Constitutional basis — **Article 41** (right to work)
- **Olga Tellis** (1985) — linked livelihood with **Article 21**; MGNREGA the only law built on the right to work
### Wages and allowance
- Wages within **15 days**; delay attracts compensation; DBT to Aadhaar-linked accounts
- No work within 15 days → unemployment allowance at least **one-fourth** of wage for first 30 days, **one-half** thereafter
- Women — at least **one-third** of beneficiaries (actual share over 50% of person-days)
### Administration
- Nodal Ministry — **Ministry of Rural Development**
- ★ **CAPART** — autonomous body under the **Ministry of Rural Development**
- **Social audit** — mandatory, by the **Gram Sabha** (which also plans and monitors works)
- **Bhuvan** (ISRO) — GeoMGNREGA geo-tagging; **NREGASoft** — MIS

## SEC 3:36:3 | MGNREGA vs VB-G RAM G Act 2025
