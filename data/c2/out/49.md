# ROW 2:82 | 74th Amendment - Part IXA, Twelfth Schedule, types of urban local bodies, ward committees, DPC and MPC
## SEC 2:82:0 | Road to the 74th
### Colonial to post-independence milestones
- **Madras** Corporation: first municipal corporation (**1688**; created by the 1687 charter)
- Bombay and Calcutta Municipal Corporations — **1726**
- Lord **Mayo**'s resolution (**1870**) — financial decentralisation
- Lord **Ripon**'s resolution (**1882**) — "**Magna Carta**" of local self-government
- **GoI Act 1919** — local self-government a **transferred** subject under dyarchy (elected Ministers), not reserved
- Chronology: Madras Corporation → Ripon (1882) → 1919 Act → Rajasthan Panchayati Raj (**1959**)
- **Hanumantha Rao Committee (1984)** — made the **Collector** central to district planning (Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao and ARC-I reduced the Collector's role)
### Making of the 74th Amendment
- 1989 Municipalities Bill lapsed; Bill of **September 1991** by the **P.V. Narasimha Rao** government became the 74th Amendment
- Passed by both Houses in **December 1992**; Presidential assent **April 1993**
- In force from **1 June 1993** (73rd: **24 April 1993**)
- Inserted **Part IXA** and the **Twelfth Schedule** (18 subjects; Eleventh has 29)
- **DPCs and MPCs** were inserted by the **74th** Amendment (Part IXA); 73rd = Panchayats, Part IX
- Local Government stays in the **State List**
### Key provisions
- **Voluntary**: manner of electing the municipal **Chairperson/Mayor** (left to State legislature)
- Articles: **243R** composition, **243U** duration, **243X** taxes and funds, **243Z** audit
- Women: at least **one-third** of seats and chairpersons' posts (Arts. **243D**, **243T**)
### Urban governance today
- **Cantonment Boards** — Ministry of **Defence**; MoHUA main ministry for ULBs; Home Affairs for ULBs in UTs
- ULBs called the "**stepchild**" of federalism: spend < 1% of GDP, employ ~10% of public workforce
- **Property tax** — key own-source revenue of ULBs, chronically under-collected
## SEC 2:82:1 | Three municipalities (243Q)
- Article **243Q** types: **Nagar Panchayat** (transitional area, rural to urban), **Municipal Council** (smaller urban area), **Municipal Corporation** (larger urban area)
- **Cantonment Board** — outside the 243Q classification
- Both 73rd and 74th systems linked with **district planning** bodies
- Municipal Corporation's three authorities: **Council** (headed by Mayor), **Standing Committees**, **Commissioner**
- **Municipal Commissioner** — chief executive of a Corporation, appointed by the **State Government** (commonly IAS); Mayor heads the Corporation, Sarpanch the Gram Panchayat
- **Area Sabha** (Community Participation Law) — all voters in a **polling-booth area**; sits below the Wards Committee
- Panchayat accounts and audit (Art. **243J**) — provided by the **State Legislature**, not Parliament
## SEC 2:82:2 | Part IXA essentials
### Structure
- 73rd: **Part IX** (Arts. **243–243-O**); 74th: **Part IXA** (Arts. **243P–243ZG**) + **Twelfth Schedule**
- **Technical and vocational education** — Eleventh Schedule, not Twelfth
### Articles
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| 243Q proviso | Governor may specify an **industrial township** where an industrial establishment provides municipal services (no municipality needed) |
| 243R | State law may give representation to persons with **special knowledge/experience** of municipal administration — **no vote** |
| 243S | **Wards Committees** — mandatory where population is **three lakh or more** |
| 243U | Term: **five years** from date appointed for **first meeting**; **reasonable opportunity of being heard** before dissolution |
| 243W | **Powers**, authority and responsibilities; linked to the Twelfth Schedule |
| 243ZA | Municipal elections by the **State Election Commission** |
| 243F / 243V | Minimum age **21** for Panchayat/Municipality membership |
### Wards Committees
- May consist of **one or more** wards; **State Legislature** decides composition and territorial area
- Ward **councillor** is a member of the committee
### Compulsory vs voluntary
- **Compulsory**: Ward Committees (3 lakh+), five-year terms, **SC/ST** reservation
- **Voluntary**: reservation for **backward classes** (State legislature)
### Elections after dissolution
- Election within **six months** of dissolution (not one year)
- Not required if less than **six months** of the term remain
### DPCs today
- DPCs **weak** in most States; operationalising them remains a mandate of the **Ministry of Panchayati Raj**
## SEC 2:82:3 | DPC vs MPC
### Planning committees
- **Article 243ZE** — MPC for every metropolitan area of **ten lakh or more** (may span more than one district)
- MPC elected representation in proportion to urban–rural **population** (not area)
- **DPC**: not less than **four-fifths** of members elected; rest by State law
- DPC **consolidates** panchayat and municipal plans into a draft district plan; MPC prepares a plan for the **whole metropolitan area**
- **Chairperson** of DPC/MPC forwards the draft plan to the **State Government**; manner of choosing the chairperson set by **State law**, not Parliament
### Urban local bodies
- Eight types of ULBs: Municipal Corporation, Municipality/Municipal Council, Notified Area Committee, Town Area Committee, Cantonment Board, Township, Port Trust, Special Purpose Agency (**not** Zonal Development Authority)
- **243V** disqualifications — law for elections to the **State Legislature** or a **State law**
- **243ZA** — electoral rolls and municipal elections by the **State Election Commission** (same authority as Art. 243K)

| 243D | Reservations |
| 243E | **Tenure** and dissolution of Panchayats |
| 243G | Powers of Panchayats |
| 243-I | State Finance Commission |
| 243J | **Accounts and audit** (Panchayats) |
| 243W | Municipal powers |
| 243Y | Finance Commission (municipalities) |
| 243Z | Audit (municipalities) |

| 243ZE | **Metropolitan Planning Committee** |

# ROW 2:83 | PESA Act 1996; Fifth and Sixth Schedule areas and tribal self-governance
## SEC 2:83:0 | Fifth vs Sixth Schedule
### Scheduled Tribes
- **Article 342** — ST list notified by the **President** after consultation with the **Governor** (Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950)
- Later inclusion/exclusion only by **Parliament by law**
- No STs notified in **Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, Puducherry, Chandigarh**
- STs ≈ **8.6%** of India's population (Census 2011)
- **NCST** (Art. **338A**) — inserted by the **89th Amendment Act, 2003**; NCST set up **2004**
### Fifth Schedule
- Governs Scheduled Areas and STs: **Part X, Article 244(1)** + Fifth Schedule (NE tribal areas: Art. 244(2) + Sixth)
- Scheduled Area criteria: tribal preponderance, compactness and reasonable size, viable administrative unit, economic backwardness (**not** industrial development)
- **President** declares Scheduled Areas; **Governor** reports to the President **annually or whenever required** (not the Chief Minister); Union may give directions to the State
- **Para 5** regulations for peace and good government — **Governor**, after consulting the TAC, with **President's assent**
- **Tribes Advisory Council**: not more than **20** members, about **three-fourths** ST MLAs; mandatory in every State with Scheduled Areas (**para 4**); advises on ST welfare
- TAC is a **Fifth Schedule** advisory body, not a Sixth Schedule body with legislative/judicial powers
- President may direct a TAC in a State with **STs but no Scheduled Areas**
- Parliament amends the Fifth and Sixth Schedules by **ordinary law** (not Art. 368)
### Sixth Schedule
- Basis: **Article 244(2)**; **Article 275(1)** — grants-in-aid for tribal welfare / administration of Scheduled and Tribal Areas
- States: **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram** (autonomous district councils); **Nagaland** has **Art. 371A**, not Sixth Schedule
- Areas in Parts I, II, IIA, III of the table to **para 20** — "**Tribal Areas**"
- Autonomous region within a district — **Regional Council**
- **Governor** specifies extent of **High Court** jurisdiction over tribal areas' judicial arrangements
- Application of laws: direction by the **Governor** in **Assam**; by the **President** in Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram
### Part IX and PESA
- **Article 243M** — excludes Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas from Part IX
- **243M(4)(b)** — Parliament may extend Part IX with modifications by **ordinary law** (not an Art. 368 amendment); PESA, 1996 did this
- **PESA** applies to **Fifth Schedule** areas, not Sixth Schedule areas
- Ten PESA States: AP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan (**not Kerala**)
- **AP** — Fifth Schedule State; **Assam** — Sixth Schedule
### AP angle
- **Alluri Sitharama Raju** district — carved from **Visakhapatnam**; tribal-majority, has **PVTGs**
## SEC 2:83:1 | Fifth Schedule: Governor & TAC
- ★ Bringing an area under the Fifth Schedule — prevents **transfer of tribal land**; President, consulting the Governor, can alter Scheduled Area boundaries; **10** States have Scheduled Areas
- Governor may direct that an Act of Parliament/State Legislature **not apply** or apply **with modifications** in a Scheduled Area
- **Para 5** regulations: prohibit/restrict **tribal land transfer** to non-tribals; regulate **money-lending** to STs
- PESA Gram Sabha may be at **habitation** level (**para, majra, tola**)
- Inconsistent State law ceases to operate **one year** from PESA's assent (**24 December 1996**)
## SEC 2:83:2 | Sixth Schedule councils
- Autonomous District Council: **30** members — **26** elected (adult franchise), **4** nominated by the **Governor**
- Council legislates on inheritance, village administration, social customs, land, forests (**not citizenship**)
- Judicial power: **village courts**; executive: revenue and taxes
- Financial assistance: **Article 275** grants-in-aid from the **Consolidated Fund of India**
- Union directions to a State on tribal administration — **Fifth** Schedule (Sixth: stays with the State executive)
- Sixth Schedule does **not** cover **Manipur** (hill-area committees, Art. **371-C**) or Nagaland
- **FRA 2006** — Other Traditional Forest Dwellers: **three generations (75 years)** of residence before **2005**
## SEC 2:83:3 | Tribal committees & PESA
### Committees
| Committee | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Dhebar** Commission (U.N. Dhebar) | **1960** | tribal welfare |
| **Lokur** Committee | **1965** | five ST criteria: primitive traits, distinct culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact, backwardness |
| **Bhuria** Committee (Dilip Singh Bhuria) | **1995** | Ministry of **Rural Development**; recognise traditional Gram Sabhas in Schedule V areas; basis of **PESA** |
| **Xaxa** Committee (**Virginius Xaxa**) | **2013** | set up by the **PMO**; review of tribal development; stop land alienation, strictly enforce FRA 2006 |
### Others
- **NCST** special focus: minor forest produce, mineral and water rights, rehabilitation of displaced tribals
- PESA prevents **higher-level Panchayats** from taking over Gram Sabha powers
- **Samatha v. State of AP (1997)** — transfer/lease of tribal land in Scheduled Areas to private parties for mining **null and void**
## SEC 2:83:4 | PESA: Gram Sabha powers
### Gram Sabha
- **Gram Sabha** — nucleus of self-rule under PESA (autonomous district councils = Sixth Schedule)
- ★ Gram Sabha meeting presided by an **ST member** elected by the Gram Sabha (not automatically the Sarpanch); chosen for **one year** by consensus, else oldest ST woman present
- Authority over **natural resources**, **minor forest produce**, **dispute resolution**, **project approvals**
### Powers
| Subject | Power |
|---|---|
| Minor forest produce | **Ownership** |
| Land acquisition / resettlement | **Consultation** |
| Minor-mineral prospecting licences / leases | **Recommendation** |
| Tribal land | **Prevent alienation**, restore unlawfully alienated land |
- **FRA 2006**: forest land diversion for public utilities needs **Gram Sabha** recommendation
### Implementation
- Minor minerals and minor forest produce provisions — among the **least implemented**; Gram Sabha consent reportedly ignored in some land acquisitions
- MoPR assessment: more than **40%** of elected representatives in PESA panchayats could not explain a Gram Sabha
- **Tana Bhagats** (Jharkhand): no PESA Gram Sabha consent for the **Bharatmala** highway; also called a breach of the **CNT Act, 1908**
- **Niyamgiri** case (2013): Gram Sabhas decided on **religious and forest rights**; rejected the **Vedanta** bauxite project
### AP
- **Tribal Medical College**, **Paderu** — opened **2024–25**, **50** seats

# ROW 2:85 | Public policy - meaning, types, policy cycle (formulation, implementation, evaluation), policy instruments

## SEC 2:85:2 | Policy cycle
- Sequence: agenda-setting → formulation → **adoption** → implementation → evaluation
- **Formulation** — research, stakeholder consultation, cost-benefit analysis, impact assessment of options
- **Communication and transparency** run through **every stage** (not only evaluation)
- **Policy termination or revision** — ends/revises ineffective policy; lessons fed back into agenda setting
## SEC 2:85:3 | Implementation approaches
- **Top-down**: central direction, faithful execution — **Pressman and Wildavsky**, "**Implementation**" (**1973**), chains of clearances derail programmes
- **Bottom-up**: **front-line officials** shape outcomes
  - **Michael Lipsky** — "**street-level bureaucracy**" (**1980**)
  - **Richard Elmore** — "**backward mapping**"
## SEC 2:85:4 | Instruments & delivery
### Bases and instruments
- Judicial interpretation as a policy base — **Right to Food** read into **Article 21**
- **RTI s. 4** proactive disclosure — **information** instrument (regulation = licensing/standards; direct provision = schools, PHCs)
- Policy failure causes: weak coordination, resource shortfalls, poor targeting; **real-time outcome tracking** is a remedy
### Monitoring and budgets
- **DMEO** — apex M&E office under **NITI Aayog**; evaluates Central schemes suo motu or on request; advises States
- Outcome-based governance — **ATRs** and **DMEO** evaluations
- **Gender budgeting** and **Outcome Budget** — both **2005-06**
- **DBT** — from **1 January 2013**; cuts ghost beneficiaries, removes middlemen; drawback: may **exclude** people without digital access
### Social audit
- **MGNREGA s. 17** — social audit by the **Gram Sabha**
- AP statewide NREGS social audits — **2006**; independent **SSAAT** — **2009**

# ROW 2:87 | Governance and good governance - meaning, dimensions, World Bank/UNDP indicators
## SEC 2:87:0 | Definitions by body
- ★ **Religiosity and emotionality** — not features of good governance
- **World Bank**: governance = manner in which **power is exercised** in managing a country's **economic and social resources** for development
- **World Bank** first brought "good governance" into discourse — **1989** report on **Sub-Saharan Africa**; **UNDP** definition later (**1997**)
- UN/UNDP eight attributes: consensus orientation, participation, rule of law, effectiveness and efficiency, accountability, transparency, responsiveness, equity and inclusiveness (**not** administrative centralisation)
- **DARPG** — India's nodal department for the World Bank **WGI**
- **NCGG** — created by upgrading the **National Institute of Administrative Research** (LBSNAA)
## SEC 2:87:1 | UN: 8 attributes
- **World Bank** stresses accountability, transparency, rule of law; **UNESCAP** stresses efficiency, effectiveness, equity, sustainability
- UNDP's three most critical characteristics: **transparency, accountability, responsiveness**
## SEC 2:87:2 | Four pillars
| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ethos** | **service** |
| Ethics | honesty and integrity |
| Equity | empathy for weaker sections |
| Efficiency | speedy ICT-based delivery |
## SEC 2:87:3 | WGI vs GGI
- **WGI** — since **1996**, 200+ countries; includes Government Effectiveness, Rule of Law
- DARPG **Good Governance Index** — launched on Good Governance Day **2019**; ranks States, UTs, districts; revised every **two years**
- WGI and V-Dem criticised — rely on **expert opinion and perceptions**
## SEC 2:87:4 | Indices → publisher
- **WGI** dimensions: Voice and Accountability, Political Stability, Government Effectiveness, Regulatory Quality, Rule of Law, Control of Corruption (**not HDI**)
- **GGI** — ten sectors incl. citizen-centric governance, environment, public health, agriculture and allied (**not foreign affairs**)
