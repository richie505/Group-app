### Ideas and achievements
- **Doctrine of Lapse** — Lord Dalhousie; **Two-Nation Theory** — Jinnah / Muslim League; **Hindutva** — V.D. Savarkar
- Greatest Moderate contribution — the **economic critique of colonialism**; destroyed myth of British benevolence
- Achievements: national identity, economic critique, democratic training; **not** complete independence
- **Swaraj** as objective — **political self-government**
## SEC 1:200:1 | Drain of wealth
- **Drain Theory** — India's wealth drained to **Britain**; an economic critique of colonialism
- Channels: **Home Charges**, salaries and pensions, unequal trade, British monopoly
## SEC 1:200:2 | Methods & limits
- Moderates — strictly **constitutional agitation**; distrusted mass action, **no mass civil disobedience**
- Annual INC session lasted only about **three days**; no effective machinery between sessions
- **Partition of Bengal, 1905** — exposed Moderates' "naive faith" in British justice
- **Lord Curzon, 1900** — Congress "tottering to its fall", to be assisted to a "peaceful demise"
## SEC 1:200:3 | Demands
- Economic: **cut** in military expenditure, lower land revenue, abolition of **salt tax**, tariff protection for Indian industry
- Constitutional: larger councils, **elected members**, control over the **budget**
- Administrative: **separation of judiciary from executive**; simultaneous ICS exams; higher ICS age limit
- Protested Tilak's arrest and deportation without trial of the **Natu brothers**
## SEC 1:200:4 | Bodies & gains
- **Servants of India Society** — Gokhale, **Poona, 12 June 1905**
- **British Committee of the INC** — London, **1889**; Wedderburn and Naoroji; journal **India**
- **Welby Commission (1895)** — examined **Indian expenditure**; a gain of Moderate pressure
- **Indian Councils Act 1892** inadequate: **no direct vote**, official majority kept, budget discussed but **not amended**

# ROW 1:202 | Partition of Bengal 1905, Swadeshi and Boycott Movement; techniques and impact
## SEC 1:202:0 | Partition to annulment
### Partition
- **Viceroy Lord Curzon** — announced the partition (1905); called Bengal "the **nerve centre of Indian nationalism**"
- ★ Partition of Bengal — **1905**
- **Japan's victory over Russia (1904–05)** — fed anger against the partition (Sumit Sarkar)
- Nationalists saw it as **Divide and Rule**; major response — **Swadeshi Movement**
### Key dates
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **7 August 1905** | boycott resolution at **Calcutta Town Hall**; Swadeshi formally proclaimed |
| **16 October 1905** | partition took effect (not 15 October) |
| **15 August 1906** | National Council of Education |
| **December 1906** | **Muslim League** formed at **Dacca**; Calcutta Congress (Naoroji) adopted **Swaraj** |
| **12 December 1911** | annulment, **Delhi Durbar** of **King George V** |
- 16 October 1905 — day of **mourning**, **hartal**, **Ganga bath**, **Rakhi-bandhan**
- Swadeshi leadership passed from Moderates to **Extremists** in **1906**
- Limitation — limited participation of **peasants and Muslims**; faded by **1908** (repression, Surat Split)
### Annulment
- ★ Partition revoked / cancelled — **1911**
- ★ Capital shifted **Calcutta → Delhi**, **1911**, announced at the same Durbar
- **King George V** — monarch at the annulment Durbar, not the Viceroy who announced partition; **Lord Hardinge II** — Viceroy at annulment
### Andhra beginnings
- **Madras Beach students' meeting (Sept 1905)** — presided by **G. Subramania Iyer**; began Swadeshi in Andhra
- **Mutnuri Krishna Rao** — arranged Pal's 1907 tour
- Pal at **Kakinada (April 1907)** — presided by **Kattiventi Perraju**; host at Machilipatnam — **Kompalle Hanumantha Rao**
- **1938** — Osmania students (incl. **P.V. Narasimha Rao**) expelled for singing **Vande Mataram**
## SEC 1:202:1 | The division
- Official reason — **administrative convenience**; real aim — weaken Bengali nationalism, divide Hindus and Muslims
- **Eastern Bengal and Assam** — capital **Dacca**; Western Bengal kept **Calcutta**
## SEC 1:202:2 | Techniques
- **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti, Barisal** — **Ashwini Kumar Dutt**
- **K.K. Mitra** — first advocated boycott, in **Sanjibani**
- Jatras, folk songs, melas, volunteer corps, akharas, lathi clubs — **mass mobilisation**
- Bengal — **Aurobindo Ghosh**, **Bipin Chandra Pal**, **Ashwini Kumar Dutt**
- Extremist Swaraj — **complete freedom**; via boycott, Swadeshi, national education, passive resistance
- Swadeshi — first **mass** movement; birth of mass nationalism

# ROW 1:204 | Extremists - Lal-Bal-Pal and Aurobindo; ideology; Surat Split 1907
## SEC 1:204:0 | Moderates vs Extremists
### Aims and methods
- **Moderates** — **gradual self-government** through petitions and constitutional agitation
- **Extremists (Assertive Nationalists)** — **Swaraj**, complete independence; more **immediate** Swaraj than Moderates
- Extremists **rejected Prayer, Petition and Protest**; favoured self-sacrifice, mass movement, aggressive resistance
- **Atmashakti** — **self-reliance**; constructive work such as **cooperatives**
- Extremist contribution — made nationalism a **mass movement**, Swaraj the goal
### Leaders
- ★ "**Swaraj is our birthright**" — **B.G. Tilak**
- **Tilak** — militant (Extremist) leader; **Ranade, Gokhale, G.V. Joshi** — Moderates
- Moderate pair — **Dadabhai Naoroji** and **Gokhale**; Extremists — Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Pal, Aurobindo
- ★ **Max Muller** — pleaded for Tilak: "My interest in Tilak is that of a **Sanskrit scholar**"
- ★ Tilak founded **The Mahratta** (English) and **Kesari** (Marathi)
### Dates
- After Surat — repression, **Newspapers Act 1908**, Tilak jailed; reunion at **Lucknow, 1916**
- **Tilak's Home Rule League** — **28 April 1916**; Besant's — **September 1916**
- **Arya Samaj** — **1875**; Deoband — 1866; Ramakrishna Mission — 1897
