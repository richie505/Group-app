# ROW 2:8 | Fundamental Rights - Article 12 (State), Article 13 and doctrines of eclipse, severability, waiver
## SEC 2:8:0 | Basics of Part III
### Where and what
- ★ Fundamental Rights are **granted by the Constitution**
- Called the **'Magna Carta of India'**; in **Part III, Articles 12 to 35**
  - **Art. 12** defines State; **Art. 13** voids inconsistent laws; **Arts. 33-35** limits and legislation
  - **Part IV** (Arts. 36-51) = Directive Principles; **Part IVA** (Art. 51A) = Fundamental Duties
- FRs are available **against the State**; **Article 12** defines which bodies count as 'State'
- **Britain** has no codified FRs; protection rests on **conventions and the rule of law**
### Number of groups and property
- Originally **seven** groups; presently **six**
- **44th Amendment (1978)** removed property from Part III
- Property now protected under **Article 300A** (Part XII), a constitutional/legal right
### Who gets them
- **Citizens only**: Articles **15, 16, 19, 29 and 30**
- **Citizens and foreigners**: Articles **14, 20-28**
### Interpretation
- **Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)** widened **Article 21** (progressive interpretation)

## SEC 2:8:1 | Six groups

| Group | Articles |
|---|---|
| Right to Equality | **14-18** |
| Right against Exploitation | **23-24** |
| Freedom of Religion | **25-28** |
| Cultural and Educational Rights | **29-30** |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | **Article 32** alone (not 30-32) |

- **Positive rights** (educational/cultural privileges): Articles **21A, 29 and 30**; other sets mainly restrict State action (negative)
- **Article 21** available to all persons; **19, 16, 29** only to citizens
- FRs can be amended **only by a constitutional amendment**, subject to the **basic structure** (**Kesavananda Bharati, 1973**); an ordinary law cannot

## SEC 2:8:2 | Art. 12 & Art. 13
### Nature and model
- Modelled principally on the **US Bill of Rights** (not the Irish Constitution)
- FRs are **qualified**, subject to **reasonable restrictions**; courts review reasonableness
### Article 12: State
- 'State' **includes all local authorities**
- **Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib (1981)**: society running a **Regional Engineering College** = 'State'; test is **creation and control**, not the formal legal form
### Article 13
- ★ **Article 13**: pre- and post-constitutional laws inconsistent with Part III are **void to the extent of inconsistency** (gives Constitution precedence over laws)
- **13(1)**: laws in force **before the commencement of the Constitution** (not "before Independence")
- **13(2)**: bars the State from making laws abridging FRs; such post-constitution laws void to that extent
- **13(3)** 'law' includes **ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage** having force of law
- **13(4)**, inserted by the **24th Amendment**: amendments under **Article 368** are **not 'law'** under Art. 13
### Other provisions tested here
- **Art. 22(3)** excludes **enemy aliens** from its safeguards; Arts. 23 and 24 are not denied to aliens
- **Art. 33**: power to restrict FRs of armed forces lies with **Parliament** alone; may extend to non-combatants (cooks, barbers)
- **Art. 39(d)**: **equal pay for equal work**
- Excluding Dalits from a public **well** → **Art. 15(2)**; from a public **shop** → **Art. 17**; both can operate against **private persons**
- **Art. 19(4)**: restrictions on association only on the **constitutionally permitted grounds**
- **I.R. Coelho (2007)** limited **Ninth Schedule** protection; Schedule came with the **1st Amendment (1951)**

## SEC 2:8:3 | Art. 12 cases
- **R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority (1979)**: tests for an **instrumentality or agency** of the State
- **Rajasthan SEB (1967)**: statutory bodies are **'other authorities'**

## SEC 2:8:4 | Doctrines → case
- **Golaknath (1967)**: FRs have a **transcendental position**; Parliament cannot abridge them
  - **24th Amendment (1971)** responded by adding **Art. 13(4)**
  - **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** came after: **basic structure**

| Doctrine | Meaning / case |
|---|---|
| **Eclipse** | inconsistent law stays **dormant** and may **revive**; **State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills** extended it to post-Constitution laws affecting **non-citizens** |
| **Severability** | only the **inconsistent portion** is void; **A.K. Gopalan, R.M.D.C., Motor General Traders** |
| **Absolute liability** | hazardous industries, no strict-liability exceptions; **M.C. Mehta, Oleum gas leak (1987)** |
| **Pith and substance** | Union-State **List encroachment**: looks at true nature and character of the law |

## SEC 2:8:5 | Limiting FRs
- **Article 33**: restriction of FRs of specified **forces**
- **Article 34**: restrictions during **martial law** and **indemnity** by Parliament
- **44th Amendment (1978)**: enforcement of **Arts. 20 and 21** can never be suspended under **Art. 359**
  - Arts. 14, 19, 22 and the right to move courts under Art. 32 for other rights can be affected

## SEC 2:8:6 | FR or not?
- **Right to employment**: not an FR; Directive Principle under **Article 41**
- **Article 21A** (education, ages **six to fourteen**): **86th Amendment, 2002**; given effect by **RTE Act, 2009**
- **Right to vote**: **statutory** right under **Section 62, RP Act 1951**; constitutional basis **Article 326**; not an FR

## SEC 2:8:7 | Exam corner
- Paying less than **minimum wage** = forced labour under **Art. 23** (**PUDR/Asiad, 1982**)
- Child labour in mines violates **Art. 24**

## SEC 2:8:8 | Fundamental Rights — basics, six groups and number game
### Where and what
- ★ FRs in **Part III, Articles 12 to 35**; six FRs
- ★ FRs are **justiciable**: enforceable by courts, subject to restrictions
- ★ **Rights** are **claims of citizens against the State**; they restrict the powers of the State
### Groups
- ★ **Article 17** (abolition of **untouchability**) is part of the **Right to Equality**
- ★ **Article 15** (no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth) falls under **Right to Equality (Arts. 14-18)**
- ★ **Right to Property** is not an FR; removed by the **44th Amendment**
- ★ **Right to protest** is not among the six FRs
### Citizens vs non-citizens
- ★ **Right to Constitutional Remedies**: available to **non-citizens** too
- ★ Citizens only: Articles **15, 16, 19, 29 and 30**
  - ★ A **British citizen** in India does not get **freedom of trade and profession**
  - ★ Foreigners do not get **freedom of expression**, free travel in India, acquiring property
- ★ Available to all: **equality before law (Art. 14)**, Art. 20, Art. 21
- ★ Citizens only: **right against discrimination** (Arts. 15, 16, 17), **move freely** [Art. 19(1)(d)], **contest elections**

## SEC 2:8:10 | Articles → rights for citizens only vs all persons
- ★ Citizens only: Articles **15, 16, 19, 29 and 30**

## SEC 2:8:11 | Articles 12 and 13 → meaning of State and law
- ★ **Article 13(3)(a)**: 'law' is comprehensive: **ordinances, orders, bye-laws, rules, regulations, notifications, customs and usages** having force of law
- Writs do **not ordinarily lie** against a **private body** merely because it performs a public activity; not absolute: **State collusion** can change it

## SEC 2:8:13 | Doctrines → meaning and case
- ★ Part III FRs similar (conceptually) to the **Bill of Rights of the USA**
- ★ **Right to Die** is **not** a Fundamental Right

## SEC 2:8:14 | Articles 33-35 and 359 → limiting and suspending FRs
- ★ Concept of FRs borrowed from the **USA** Constitution

## SEC 2:8:15 | Claimed rights → FR or not
- ★ **Right to vote** = **constitutional right** under **Article 326**; age **18**
  - ★ **61st Amendment (1988)** changed the voting age for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections
- ★ Constitution does not grant a **Right of Equal Shelter**
- ★ **Equal Pay for Equal Work** is not a Fundamental Right

## SEC 2:8:16 | Scenarios → Article violated
- No confirmed fact: this subsection's only MCQ has no official key

## SEC 2:8:17 | Universal angles
- ★ The **President** has the power to suspend **Fundamental Rights**

# ROW 2:9 | Right to Equality - Articles 14-18; reasonable classification, reservation jurisprudence
## SEC 2:9:0 | Articles 14-18
### Articles 14-16

| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **14** | equality before law; applies to **all persons** |
| **15** | prohibition of discrimination; **citizens only** |
| **16** | equality of opportunity in **public employment** |
| **18** | abolition of **titles** |

- **15(1)** grounds: religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth only; **descent** is not among them (it is in **16(2)**)
- **15(2)**: access to places like **hotels**; binds **private persons** as well as the State
- **16(1)**: general equality of opportunity in public jobs; **16(4)** reservation; **15(5)** educational advancement
- **16(2)** lists **descent and residence** among prohibited grounds; **16(3)**: **Parliament** may prescribe residence requirements
- **16(5)**: offices of a **religious or denominational institution** may be kept for persons of that religion/denomination
### Articles 17-18
- ★ **Article 17** abolishes **untouchability**; enforcing any disability from it is an **offence** punishable by law
- **18(1)**: no titles except **military and academic distinctions**
### Equality ideas
- **Social equality**: Articles **15, 17, 18**; **economic equality**: DPSP **39(b), 39(c), 39(d)**
- **Ambedkar**: liberty, equality, fraternity = an **inseparable trinity**
- **E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)**: equality is **antithetic to arbitrariness**

## SEC 2:9:1 | Two faces of Art. 14
- **Equality before law**: **British** origin, negative
- **Equal protection of the laws**: **American** origin, positive
- **Dicey** opposed **special administrative tribunals** for officials: everyone under ordinary law and ordinary courts

## SEC 2:9:2 | Rule of Law & classification
### Origin and index
- **Rule of Law** borrowed from **Britain** (A.V. **Dicey**, **1885**), like Cabinet System and Parliamentary Government
- ★ Indian Constitution similar to America in the **Rule of Law** (per key)
- ★ **Rule of Law Index**: released by the **World Justice Project** (measuring since **2008**; eight areas)
- Rule of Law is part of the **basic structure**: **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** and **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**
### Dicey's elements
- Three elements: **absence of arbitrary power**, **equality before law**, **primacy of individual rights**
  - Not an element: **people's responsibility to the Government**
- First element: regular law supreme; no punishment except for **breach of law**; rejects unlimited state power
- India took the first two but **reversed the third**: rights flow from the **Constitution**
- Rule of Law: **government subordinate to law**; a ruler above law = **'rule by law'**
### Article 14 tests
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**: any **arbitrary State action** violates Art. 14; unguided discretion may be struck down
- Reasonable classification: **intelligible differentia** + **rational nexus** with the object of the law
- **Mritunjay Tiwari v. Union of India**: SC questioned the caste-discrimination clause of the **UGC equity regulations**

## SEC 2:9:3 | Reservation cases
### Chronology

| **Indra Sawhney** | **1992** | upheld **27% OBC** quota; excluded **creamy layer**; **50% ceiling**; ruled out reservation in promotions |
| **M. Nagaraj** | **2006** | **quantifiable data** for promotion reservation; upheld **77th and 85th** Amendments |
| **Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta** | **2018** | |
| **Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil** | **2021** | **Maratha** quota struck down for crossing **50%** |
| **Janhit Abhiyan** | **2022** | upheld **103rd Amendment**, **10% EWS**, by **3:2**; economic criteria alone valid |
| **State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh** | **2024** | **7-judge** bench overruled **E.V. Chinnaiah (2004)**; sub-classification of SCs/STs on empirical data |

- Order: Champakam, Balaji, Indra Sawhney, Nagaraj
- **First Amendment (1951)** inserted **Art. 15(4)** to overcome **Champakam Dorairajan**

### Other points
- No unrestricted power for every State legislature to exceed the **50% ceiling**
- **Art. 16(4A)**: promotion reservation for SCs/STs with **consequential seniority**; backlog vacancies **16(4B)**
- **Samatha**: tribal land cannot be leased to private parties; caste-not-sole-determinant holding is **Janki Prasad Parimoo**
- Centre **opposes** an income-based **creamy layer for SCs and STs**
- **April 2026**: **Andhra Pradesh** aligned its OBC creamy layer with the **central income limits**

| Clause | Amendment | Subject |

- **Art. 29**: cultural and educational rights; **Art. 30**: minority educational institutions

## SEC 2:9:5 | Article 18 & awards
### Titles
- **Art. 18** advances **social equality** by ending artificial distinctions from State-conferred titles
- **18(2)**: a citizen shall **not accept a foreign title** at all; no consent route
- **Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (1996)**: **Bharat Ratna and Padma** awards are **not titles**, but not to be used as **prefixes or suffixes**
### Other facts filed here
- Civil case against **President/Governor**: **two months'** prior notice; criminal proceedings barred during term (**Art. 361**)
- **April 2026** SC: pensioners cannot be treated differently from serving employees on **dearness allowance** without valid Art. 14 classification
- **2017** notification: OBC creamy layer **₹8 lakh** a year; AP aligned with it
- **Art. 342A**: **President** specifies the Central List of SEBCs; States keep their own lists
- Rule of Law in India anchored in **Article 14**
- **15(3)**: women and children; **15(4)**: SCs, STs, OBCs; **15(5)**: educational advancement
- **Article 23**: prohibits **human trafficking** and forced labour; **24** child labour

## SEC 2:9:6 | Exam corner
- Article 15 special provisions: women, children, SEBCs, SCs, STs, **EWS [15(6)]**; **aged persons** not covered
- **M. Nagaraj (2006)** upheld the **77th and 85th Amendments** (promotion reservation), subject to conditions

## SEC 2:9:7 | Right to Equality → Articles 14-18 content
- ★ Right to Equality: five Articles, **14 to 18** (Art. 13 not among them)

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| ★ **14** | **equality before law** and **equal protection of the laws**; all persons |
| ★ **15** | prohibition of **discrimination** |
| ★ **16** | equality of opportunity in **public employment** |
| ★ **17** | abolition of **untouchability**; practice in any form forbidden |

- ★ Right to equal protection of the law (Art. 14) available to **all persons**; Arts. 15, 16, 19(1)(a), cultural rights not
- ★ **16(1) and 16(2)**: equal opportunity for all citizens in public employment
- ★ **16(2)**: no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, **descent**, place of birth, **residence**

## SEC 2:9:8 | Fundamental Rights → social concerns covered
- ★ OBC reservation: **Articles 15(4) and 16(4)**
- ★ **Champakam Dorairajan** led to amending the **right against discrimination**: **Art. 15(4)** by the **1st Amendment (1951)**

## SEC 2:9:9 | Types of equality → Article
- ★ **Economic equality** is not part of the FR to equality
- ★ Included: equality before law (**Art. 14**), social equality (**Art. 15**), equal opportunity (**Art. 16**)

## SEC 2:9:10 | Rule of Law → origin and Dicey's three elements
### Dicey
- ★ Rule of Law propounded by **A.V. Dicey**, British jurist
- ★ Main features: **limitation of powers**, **equality before law**, **liberty and civil rights**; not "people's responsibility to the Government"
- ★ Equality in India: Articles **14-18**; allows **reasonable classification**; equality not absolute
### Article 14
- ★ **Article 14**: equality before law and **equal protection of the laws**; all persons, citizens or foreigners
- ★ Law giving executive **unguided, uncontrolled discretionary power** violates **Article 14**
- ★ **Article 14** puts a limitation on legislative power
- ★ **Authoritarian government** is not a feature of Indian polity; India has Rule of Law and democracy

## SEC 2:9:11 | Rule of Law vs Rule by Law; equality before law vs equal protection; classification
- ★ State can **treat unequals differently** for a level playing field; **among equals** the law should be equal; equal protection allows **reasonable classification**

## SEC 2:9:14 | Reservation cases → SC/ST promotions, EWS, sub-classification, tribal rights
- ★ Up to **10% EWS** reservation in education and public employment: **Constitution (103rd Amendment) Act**

## SEC 2:9:15 | Clauses of Articles 15-16 → beneficiaries and amendments
- ★ **Article 15(5)**: reservation in admission, including **private and unaided** institutions, for SCs/STs/OBCs

## SEC 2:9:18 | Article 18 → titles, foreign titles, consent, national awards
- ★ Highest civilian award: **Bharat Ratna**; instituted with Padma Vibhushan in **1954**

# ROW 2:10 | Right to Freedom - Article 19 (six freedoms) and reasonable restrictions
## SEC 2:10:0 | Six freedoms → restriction grounds
### The freedoms

| Clause | Freedom |
|---|---|
| **19(1)(a)** | speech and expression; **right to information** read into it |
| **19(1)(b)** | peaceful assembly; **armed gatherings** excluded |
| **19(1)(c)** | associations, unions, **co-operative societies** (added by **97th Amendment, 2011**) |
| **19(1)(d)** | movement |
| **19(1)(e)** | **reside and settle** in any part of India |
| **19(1)(f)** | property: **deleted** by the **44th Amendment (1978)** |
| **19(1)(g)** | profession, trade or business |

- Article 19 rights: **citizens only**; not foreigners, not a company as a legal person
- **Travel abroad**: **Article 21**, not 19(1)(d)
- **Cricket Association of Bengal**: telecast rights, no government monopoly over **airwaves**; **PUCL**: phone-tapping safeguards
- **Right to strike**: only a **legal right**; a **gherao** is not peaceful assembly
- Peaceful protest is everybody's right but **must not disturb other citizens**
- No right to carry on a **dangerous or immoral trade** (e.g. trafficking)
### Restriction clauses

| Clause | Restricts | Grounds noted |
|---|---|---|
| **19(2)** | speech | **eight** grounds: sovereignty and integrity, security of State, friendly relations, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement; no sedition or economic grounds |
| **19(3)** | assembly | **sovereignty and integrity**, **public order** (not morality, security of State) |
| **19(4)** | association | Centre invoked it to defend **FCRA** curbs |
| **19(5)** | movement, residence | **general public interest**, **protection of Scheduled Tribes** |
| **19(6)** | profession, trade | general public interest, **professional/technical qualifications**, **State trade/monopoly** |

- **Unbecoming criticism** is not a ground to restrict expression
- **Sixteenth Amendment (1963)** added **sovereignty and integrity of India** to 19(2); other added grounds came with the **First Amendment (1951)**
### Other facts filed here
- Preventive detention without an **Advisory Board**: max **three months** (Article 22)
- Statutory **Zero FIR**: **BNSS**, not BNS; BNS covers organised crime and mob lynching

## SEC 2:10:1 | Scope
- A **detainee** keeps **freedom of expression** (may write or publish books) subject to lawful restrictions
- Reasonableness test: **State of Madras v. V.G. Row (1952)**: nature of right, purpose, urgency, proportionality
- **Courts** decide reasonableness; a restriction must not **eclipse** the right
- Expression protected **irrespective of the medium**: press, television, Internet
- **Kaushal Kishor v. State of UP (2023)**: Art. 19(2) grounds are **exhaustive** (no extra curbs on ministers' speech); Arts. 19 and 21 enforceable against **non-State actors**
- **Article 20**: protection in respect of conviction; **21** life and liberty; **22** arrest and detention; **19** six freedoms

## SEC 2:10:2 | Amendments adding grounds
- **First Amendment (1951)** rewrote **Art. 19(6)**: professional/technical qualifications and **State monopoly** (shielding nationalisation)
  - 7th, 44th, 97th did not touch 19(6)

## SEC 2:10:6 | Exam corner
- **Freedom of the press**: not expressly listed; read into **Art. 19(1)(a)**
- No FR to **strike**; travel abroad under **Art. 21**; **19(1)(f)** deleted

## SEC 2:10:7 | Article 19(1) → six freedoms (number game) — clauses → freedom
- ★ **Article 19** prescribes the **six freedoms**

# ROW 2:11 | Articles 20, 21, 21A and 22 - protection in conviction, life and liberty, education, preventive detention
## SEC 2:11:0 | Article 20: three protections
- **Article 20**: protection in respect of **conviction for offences**; 21 life and liberty; 21A education; 22 arrest and detention
- **20(1)**: applies to **criminal laws**; bars conviction and greater penalty, not necessarily the trial
- **20(2)** (double jeopardy): only prosecution and punishment by a **court or judicial tribunal**, not **departmental proceedings**
- **20(3)**: criminal proceedings; **oral and documentary** testimony; **fingerprints/physical specimens** not testimonial (**Kathi Kalu Oghad, 1961**)
- **'Procedure established by law'**: **Japan**; **'due process'**: **United States**
- **Article 21**: **citizens and non-citizens**; includes dignity, health, protection against solitary confinement

## SEC 2:11:1 | Article 20 notes
- **Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010)**: **narco-analysis, polygraph, brain-mapping** without consent violate **Art. 20(3)**
- Article 20 protections: **citizens, foreigners** and **legal persons such as companies**
- **BNSS Sections 47 and 58** ≈ **CrPC Sections 50 and 57** (grounds of arrest, production before magistrate)

## SEC 2:11:2 | Gopalan vs Maneka
- Article 21 uses **'procedure established by law'**
- **A.K. Gopalan (1950)**: narrow; law valid if enacted by correct **legislative procedure**
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**: law must also be **just, fair and reasonable**; protects against **arbitrary legislative action**
- **Unguided executive discretion** violates **Article 14**: it is arbitrary and undermines the **Rule of Law**

## SEC 2:11:3 | Rights read into Art. 21

| Case | Right read into Art. 21 |
|---|---|

- **Articles 23-24** = **Right against Exploitation**; **Articles 25-28** = **Right to Freedom of Religion** (**four** Articles)
- Freedom of religion (Arts. 25-28) is a **Fundamental Right**, not a mere legal right or DPSP

- SC left it to the **Ministry of Education** whether schools imparting religious instruction fall under **Article 26(a)**
- **Theocratic state**: an **official religion** guides law and policy; not a feature of the Indian Constitution

- **Emile Durkheim**: religion as **collective worship** of society; **sacred vs profane**
- **July 2026**: **Ravidassias** renewed demand for a separate **religion code** in the Census; Jains, Parsis, Buddhists already counted separately (among six notified minorities)

## SEC 2:12:3 | Art. 25: text, powers, limits
### Article 25
- **Conscience**: inner freedom to mould one's relation with God; **propagate**: spreading one's beliefs to others
- 25(1) freedoms subject to **public order, morality, health** and **the other provisions of Part III**
- **25(2)(a)**: State may regulate **secular activity** associated with religion, not spiritual belief
- **25(2)(b)**: 'Hindus' includes **Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists**; wearing **kirpans** deemed part of Sikhism
- State may intervene to root out a **social evil**, within constitutional limits
- **Article 26** rights (institutions, property) subject to **public order, morality and health**
- **Exclusivism** hinders religious pluralism

## SEC 2:12:6 | Indian vs Western secularism
- Indian secularism: **equal respect** for all religions; **no discrimination** on religious grounds

- **Western** model: **strict separation**, mutual exclusion; **Indian** model: **principled distance**, equal respect
