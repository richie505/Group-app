# ROW 1:68 | Tripartite struggle for Kanauj - Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas; causes and outcome
## SEC 1:68:0 | Why Kannauj?
- After Harsha, Kannauj = symbol of **imperial sovereignty**; title of **Samrat** of North India
- Commanded the **Doab**'s agrarian wealth and **Arabian Sea** trade via **Gujarat**, bypassing the Arab-held **Indus delta**
- Significance: shows the shift of power to **regional kingdoms** in early medieval India

## SEC 1:68:1 | The three contenders
- Rashtrakuta capital: **Manyakheta (Malkhed)** in the Deccan; the Pratiharas later held Kannauj
- Struggle began (c. **780–800 CE**) when Pratihara **Vatsaraja** clashed with **Dharmapala** and Rashtrakuta **Dhruva**
- **Dhruva** defeated Vatsaraja and Dharmapala, took booty and **returned south** without holding northern territory

## SEC 1:68:2 | Phases
- **Dharmapala** removed the Pratihara nominee **Indrayudha** and installed **Chakrayudha** as puppet at Kannauj (second phase)
- **Nagabhata II** later ousted Chakrayudha
- Rashtrakuta **Indra III** sacked Kannauj c. **916 CE** after defeating Pratihara **Mahipala I**

## SEC 1:68:3 | Inscriptions
### Who defeated whom
| Rashtrakuta | Pratihara defeated |
|---|---|
| **Dhruva** | **Vatsaraja** (not Nagabhata I or II) |
| **Govinda III** | **Nagabhata II** |
| **Indra III** | **Mahipala I** (c. 916 CE) |
- **Nagabhata II** was a **Gurjara-Pratihara** king, not a Pala
### Records
- **Sanjan plates** (**871 CE**, reign of **Amoghavarsha I**) — describe **Govinda III's Himalayan campaign**
- **Gwalior prashasti** of **Mihira Bhoja** — Nagabhata II's victories over **Chakrayudha** and **Dharmapala**
- **Khalimpur inscription** — records **Gopala's selection**
- **Matsya Nyaya** = **law of the jungle**
- **Dharmapala** founded **Vikramashila**; **Param Saugat** = a devout **Buddhist**

## SEC 1:68:4 | Outcome
- ★ The three contenders: **Pala**, **Gurjara-Pratihara** and **Rashtrakuta** (not the Cholas)
- ★ Kannauj compared with **Magadha** of the Gupta period as a prize after Harsha
- ★ Kings and dynasties (only **two** of the four pairs correct):

| King | Dynasty |
|---|---|
| **Nannuka** | **Chandela** (founder) |
| **Jayashakti** | **Chandela** (not Paramara) |
| **Nagabhata II** | **Gurjara-Pratihara** |
| **Bhoja** | **Parmar** (not Rashtrakuta) |
- ★ Chandelas: early feudatories of the **Gurjara-Pratiharas** of Kannauj; fought the Pratiharas, Palas and **Kalachuris of Chedi**; founded early **9th c. CE**
- Kannauj finally held by the **Pratiharas** under **Bhoja** and **Mahendrapala I** (no power kept it long)
- Exhaustion of all three + Pratihara decline after **Indra III's** raid → rise of **Rajput** states: **Chandelas**, **Chauhans**

## SEC 1:68:5 | Exam corner
- Chronology: Pallava **Mahendravarman I** → Pala **Gopala** → Pratihara **Bhoja** → Chola **Parantaka I**
- Non-participants: **Cholas**, **Pallavas**, **Kalyani Chalukyas**

## SEC 1:68:6 | Kannauj → ancient names and causes of the struggle
- ★ **Mahodaya** / **Mahodaya Shri** ('Nagar Mahoday Shri') = **Kannauj** (name in the time of **Mihir Bhoj**)
- Tripartite struggle was for **Kannauj**, not **Ujjain**

## SEC 1:68:7 | Contending dynasties → base, rulers, distractors
- ★ **Cholas** — **not** part of the tripartite struggle

## SEC 1:68:9 | Rashtrakuta rulers → northern campaigns (defeated whom)
- ★ **Govinda III** (successor of **Dhruva**) defeated Pratihara **Nagabhata II** in his North India campaign
- ★ Recorded in the **Sanjan** and **Radhanpur** copper plates

## SEC 1:68:10 | Inscriptions → dynasty and content
- ★ Rashtrakutas fought the **Pratiharas** and the **Palas** to capture **Kanauj**

# ROW 1:69 | Palas of Bengal - Gopala, Dharmapala, Devapala; Nalanda and Vikramshila, Pala art
## SEC 1:69:0 | Pala rulers
### Rulers
- **Gopala** — **elected by notables** (mid-8th c.) to end **matsyanyaya** (anarchy)
  - displaced the later Guptas in **Magadha** and the **Khadgas** of **eastern Bengal**; built **Odantapuri**
- ★ **Dharmapala** (c. 770–810) — founded **Vikramashila** University and **Somapura**
  - defeated by **Dhruva** and by **Nagabhata II** near **Monghyr**
- **Devapala** — granted **five villages** for the Nalanda monastery at the request of the envoy of Shailendra **Balaputradeva** (Nalanda copper plate)
- **Mahipala I** (c. 977–1027) — **revived** Pala power; defeated by **Rajendra Chola**
- **Ramapala** (c. 1072–1126) — last strong Pala; faced the **Kaivarta revolt**; linked with **Jagaddala**
### Centres
- **Nalanda** founded by **Kumaragupta I**; Buddhist texts compiled at Nalanda under Dharmapala
- **Jagaddala** — translations into **Tibetan**
- **Valabhi** — a **Hinayana** centre in Harsha's time

## SEC 1:69:1 | Mahaviharas
| Mahavihara | Present location |
|---|---|
| **Somapura** | **Paharpur**, Naogaon district, Bangladesh |
| **Vikramashila** | **Antichak** |
| **Jagaddala** | **Varendra** |
| **Odantapuri** | **Bihar Sharif** |
- **Nalanda** founded in the **5th century AD** by Gupta ruler **Kumaragupta I**

## SEC 1:69:2 | Mahavihara features
- **Vikramashila** at Antichak — **Bhagalpur** district (not Banka)
- Destruction of Pala centres by **Bakhtiyar Khilji** — c. **1193–1200**
  - **1194** for Nalanda in one tradition; **Jagaddala** sacked in the early **13th c.**
- Odd one out: **Taxila** (ancient, Gandhara) vs Pala centres Nalanda, Vikramashila, Somapura

## SEC 1:69:3 | Acharyas & Tibet
- ★ **Atisha Dipankara Srijnana** — went to **Tibet** in the **11th c.**, revitalised Buddhism there; linked with **Vikramashila**
- **Balaputradeva** — **Shailendra** ruler of **Suvarnadvipa / Suvarnabhumi**; envoy asked **Devapala** for five villages for his Nalanda monastery
- **Shantarakshita** (Nalanda) — founded **Samye** monastery in Tibet
- **Shilabhadra** — headed Nalanda in Harsha's time
- **Sakya Sri Bhadra** — **Jagaddala**

## SEC 1:69:4 | Art & literature
- Pala art: **bronze** and **black-stone** sculpture; illustrated Buddhist manuscripts on **palm leaves**
- **Dhiman** and son **Vitapala** — Pala **artists**, recorded by **Taranatha**
- **Ramacharita** — **Sandhyakar Nandi**; on **Ramapala** and the **Kaivarta** peasant revolt

## SEC 1:69:5 | Sena successors
- **Lakshmanasena** — last **Sena** ruler; court poet **Jayadeva**; ousted by **Bakhtiyar Khilji**

## SEC 1:69:6 | Exam corner
- Chosen, not hereditary: **Gopala** and **Nandivarman II Pallavamalla**; Dharmapala, Devapala, Narasimhavarman I, Mahendravarman I inherited
- **Kautilya** — not a Nalanda acharya
  - Shilabhadra (Nalanda, Harsha's time), Shantarakshita (Samye), Atisha (Vikramashila, Tibet) were
- Andhra's **Dharmakirti** (taught at Nalanda) studied under **Dharmapala of Kanchi**, head of Nalanda (not the Pala king)

## SEC 1:69:7 | Pala rulers → achievements, and the Sena successors
- ★ **Gopala** — founder of the Pala dynasty; born near **Pundravardhana** (per **Taranath**); elected king of Bengal
- ★ Elected / chosen by the public: **Gopala** and **Nandivarman Pallavamalla** (not Harsha or Yashovarman)
- ★ **Khalimpur inscription** of **Dharmapala** — describes Gopala's election to end **Matsya Nyaya**

## SEC 1:69:8 | Acharyas and foreign contacts → institution, Tibet, Southeast Asia
- ★ **Balaputradeva** — ruler of **Suvarnabhumi**; founded a monastery at **Nalanda**; asked **Devapala** for **five villages**

## SEC 1:69:9 | Nalanda and the Pala-era mahaviharas → founder, present location
- ★ **Odantapuri** — founded by **Gopala I**
- ★ **Nalanda** — **Gupta** dynasty; in **Bihar** (ancient Magadha); famed for Buddhist philosophy
- ★ **Vikramshila** — founded by **Dharmapala**; a **Buddhist** centre of learning
- ★ Buddhist-period centres: **Nalanda**, **Vikramshila**, **Vallabhi**

## SEC 1:69:10 | Mahavihara features, destruction and odd-one-out
- ★ **Taxila** — not an early medieval centre; early Buddhist centre, capital of **Gandhara** (Rawalpindi district, Pakistan)
- ★ Studied at Taxila: **Chanakya**, **Chandragupta Maurya**, **Charaka**, **Jivaka**

# ROW 1:70 | Gurjara-Pratiharas - Nagabhata I and II, Mihira Bhoja; resistance to Arab expansion
## SEC 1:70:0 | Name & origin
- **Pratihara** = **door-keeper**; claimed descent from **Lakshmana**, Rama's door-keeper brother
- **Agnikula** legend (**Prithviraj Raso**): **Pratiharas, Chaulukyas, Paramaras, Chauhans** born from the fire-pit at **Mount Abu**

## SEC 1:70:1 | Capitals
- ★ Order: **Vatsaraja** (775–800) → **Nagabhatta II** (800–833) → **Mahendrapala I** (885–910) → **Mahipala** (912–944)

## SEC 1:70:2 | Rulers
- **Nagabhata I** — founder; resisted **Arab** expansion
- **Vatsaraja** — took Kannauj, defeated Dharmapala; defeated by Rashtrakuta **Dhruva** (start of the tripartite struggle)
- **Nagabhata II** — ousted **Chakrayudha**, defeated **Dharmapala** near **Monghyr**, made **Kannauj** the capital
- **Mihira Bhoja** — checked by **Sankaravarman** of Kashmir, Rashtrakuta **Krishna II** and Pala **Devapala**
- c. **916 CE** — **Indra III** defeated **Mahipala I** and sacked Kannauj (not the reverse)
- **Rajashekhara** — court poet of **Mahendrapala I**; also at **Mahipala I**'s court
- Order: Vatsaraja → Nagabhata II → Mihir Bhoja → **Mahendrapala I** → **Mahipala**

## SEC 1:70:3 | Mihira Bhoja
- Son of **Ramabhadra**; grandson of **Nagabhata II**
- Title **Adivaraha** — devotion to **Vishnu**; legend on his **coins**; a Vaishnava
- Captured **Kannauj** c. **836 AD**, made it the capital (629 AD = Hiuen Tsang's journey)
- Pratiharas ruled first from **Ujjain**, later **Kannauj**

## SEC 1:70:4 | Arab travellers
- **Sulaiman** (mid-9th c.) called Mihira Bhoja **Juzr (Al-Juzr)**
- **Al-Masudi** called him **Baura**

## SEC 1:70:5 | Rajashekhara's works
- Rajashekhara: **Kavyamimamsa**, **Karpuramanjari**, **Viddhasalabhanjika**
- **Prabandhachintamani** — by **Merutunga**, not Rajashekhara

## SEC 1:70:6 | Arabs & decline
- **Mahmud of Ghazni** attacked Kannauj in **1018**; with Indra III's raid and feudatory secession, weakened the Pratiharas
- Pratiharas kept Arab rule confined to **Sindh**
- **Muhammad bin Qasim** defeated and killed **Raja Dahir** of Sindh; conquest stayed within **Sindh** and **Multan**

## SEC 1:70:7 | Exam corner
- **Sulaiman** praised the Pratihara **cavalry**
- Sulaiman named Rashtrakuta **Amoghavarsha I** one of the **four great kings** of the world

## SEC 1:70:8 | Pratiharas → name, origin and capitals
- ★ Founder: **Nagabhatta I**
- ★ Rajputs of the **Agni** clan; "Gurjara" from link with **Gurjars**
- ★ Gurjars first mentioned in the **Aihole inscription** of Pulakesin II and **Harshacharita** of Banabhatta

## SEC 1:70:9 | Pratihara rulers → period and achievement
- ★ **Bharatrabhatta I** — **not** a Gurjara-Pratihara ruler
- ★ Pratiharas ruled much of northern India mid-**8th** to **11th** c.; first at **Ujjain**, later **Kannauj**

## SEC 1:70:10 | Mihira Bhoja → title, parentage, capital, extent
- ★ **Mihir Bhoja** (836–885 AD) — the **greatest** Pratihara king; follower of **Vaishnavism**
- ★ Titles **Aadi Varaha** and **Prabhas**
- ★ Others: **Dharmapala** — Pala (770–810); **Mahendrapala I** — Pratihara (885–910); **Harsha** — Pushyabhuti (606–647)

## SEC 1:70:11 | Arab travellers → what they said of the Pratiharas
- ★ **Suleman** visited during the reign of **Bhoja I** (Mihir Bhoja)
- ★ Mihir Bhoj — son of **Ramabhadra** and queen **Appa Devi** (not son of Nagabhatta II)
- ★ Capital **Kannauj** (seized c. 836 AD); **Al-Masudi** called him **King Baura**

## SEC 1:70:12 | Rajashekhara → patrons and works
- ★ **Rajashekhara** — Sanskrit poet and dramatist at the court of **Mahendrapala I**

# ROW 1:71 | Rashtrakutas - Dantidurga, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha (Kavirajamarga), Krishna I

- **Takkolam** (**949 CE**): **Krishna III** defeated the Cholas under **Parantaka I**

## SEC 1:71:7 | Rashtrakuta rulers → period, capital and contribution
- ★ **Dantidurga** — laid the foundation of the Rashtrakuta empire (dynasty c. **753–982 CE**); former Chalukya **feudatory**; **Hiranyagarbha** at **Ujjayini**
- ★ **Kailasa** (Shiva) temple, Ellora — built by **Krishna I** (Rashtrakuta dynasty)
- ★ **Amoghavarsha I** — born in a **military camp** at Shribhavan (800 AD) as father **Govinda III** returned from northern campaigns

## SEC 1:71:8 | Capitals, religion, Ellora and the end of the dynasty
- ★ **Ellora** — **Buddhist, Hindu and Jain** rock-cut caves together
- ★ **Kailasa** temple, Ellora — **Rashtrakutas**; built by **Krishna I** in **Dravidian** style
- ★ A wonder of **rock-cut** architecture; **Cave 16** of Ellora

## SEC 1:71:9 | Battles and conflicts → victor: Vengi, Takkolam
- ★ **Takkolam** — between the **Cholas** (Rajaditya, son of **Parantaka I**) and the **Rashtrakutas** (**Krishna III** with Western Gangas and Vaidumbas)

## SEC 1:71:11 | Literary works → authors; court poets → patrons
- ★ **Pampa** — Telugu-land scholar; wrote **Vikramarjuna Vijayam** (Pampa Bharata) in **Kannada**
- ★ Court poet of Vemulavada Chalukya **Arikesari II**; brother of **Jinavallabha**
- ★ Vikramarjuna Vijaya = **Kannada** version of the **Mahabharata**

# ROW 1:73 | Badami (Vatapi) Chalukyas - Pulakesin I, Pulakesin II, Aihole inscription of Ravikirti
## SEC 1:73:0 | Rulers
### Period and order
- Badami (Vatapi) Chalukyas: c. **543–753 CE** (Pulakeshin I to Dantidurga's overthrow of Kirtivarman II)
- Order: **Pulakesin I** → **Kirtivarman I** → **Mangalesha** → **Vikramaditya I**
### Rulers
- **Pulakesin I** — fortified **Vatapi**, **543 CE** (**Badami cliff inscription**); performed the **Ashvamedha**; Badami as capital
- **Kirtivarman I** — annexed **Kadambas, Gangas, Mauryas of Konkan**
  - 'night of doom' (Aihole) to the **Nalas, Mauryas, Kadambas**
  - left **minor sons** → brother **Mangalesha** ruled as **regent**
- **Mangalesha** — earliest **rock-cut caves** at Badami
- **Pulakesin II** — title **Dakshinapatheshwara**
- **Vikramaditya I** — restored Chalukya power after the Pallava occupation of Vatapi
- **Vikramaditya II** — took **Kanchi three times** but spared it; queen **Lokamahadevi** built the **Virupaksha** temple, Pattadakal
- **Kirtivarman II** — last ruler; overthrown by **Dantidurga** (c. 753–757)
### Capital
- Capital **Vatapi** (Badami, **Bagalkot** district, Karnataka); **Kanchi** was the **Pallava** capital
- Pallava **Narasimhavarman I** destroyed Vatapi in **642 CE** → title **Vatapikonda**

## SEC 1:73:1 | Pulakesin II
- Killed **Mangalesha** at **Elapattu-Simbige** in **Bana** territory (c. 609–10 CE)
- Eastern campaign: **Kosala & Kalinga** → march to **Vengi** → defeat of the **Durjayas** → **Kubja Vishnuvardhana** appointed at Vengi
- **Satara inscription** (**617 CE**) — appointment of brother Kubja Vishnuvardhana as **viceroy of Vengi**
- Kubja Vishnuvardhana declared independence **624 CE** (Eastern Chalukyas), capital **Pithapuram**
- Embassies with Persian king **Khusrau II** (Ajanta Cave 1 painting of it now doubted)
- Checked **Harsha** at the **Narmada**
- **Aihole inscription** (Ravikirti) — **Sanskrit**, **Southern Brahmi** script; key source on Pulakeshin II

## SEC 1:73:2 | Pulakesin II: titles & firsts
- **Paramesvara** — taken after defeating **Harsha** (not Mahendravarman I)
- Other titles: **Dakshinapatheshwara**, **Satyashraya**, **Prithvivallabha**
- **Lohnara inscription** (**630 CE**) — does **not** list Harsha among his defeated enemies
- **Aihole inscription** (**634 CE**) — does record the victory over Harsha

## SEC 1:73:3 | Aihole inscription
- ★ Mentions poet **Kalidasa**; author **Ravikirti** claimed fame like **Kalidasa** and **Bharavi**
- ★ In **Sanskrit**, southern Brahmi; authentic source for **Pulakesin II**

## SEC 1:78:7 | Eastern Chalukyas — origin, period, identity and FIRSTs
- ★ Official language **Sanskrit** (also Vishnukundins, Kakatiyas, Reddis); **Prakrit** under the Satavahanas and Ikshvakus
- ★ **Pancharamas** — built in the **Vengi Chalukya** period, by **Chalukya Bhima I**
- ★ Satavahana capitals: **Kotilingala**, **Pratishthana**, **Dhanyakataka**; **Vengi** — Eastern Chalukyas; **Warangal** — Kakatiyas
- ★ **Cholas** had close political and **matrimonial** ties with the **Chalukyas of Vengi**

## SEC 1:78:8 | Rulers → titles
| Title | Ruler |
|---|---|
| **Rajamahendra** | ★ **Ammaraja I** (Vishnuvardhana VI); Rajahmundry named after it |
| **Vishamasiddhi** ('conqueror of the forest') | ★ **Kubja Vishnuvardhana I** |
| **Narendra Mrigaraja** ('lion among kings') | ★ **Vijayaditya II** — 108 Narendreswara temples |
| **Tripura-Martya-Maheswara** | ★ **Gunaga Vijayaditya** |
- ★ **Ammaraja** — built **Rajahmundry**
- ★ **Chellava** ('Samasta Gandharva Vidyakovida') — court singer of **Chalukya Bhima**
- ★ 108 Saiva temples — Group-2 key: **Vijayaditya I** (other keys: Vijayaditya II)

## SEC 1:78:9 | Rulers → contributions
- ★ **Addanki** inscription of **Pandaranga** — first Telugu poem, in **Taruvoja** metre
- ★ **Pandaranga** — commander of **Gunaga Vijayaditya III**
- ★ **Chalukya Bhima I** — built many temples (Bhimeswara at Draksharama, Samalkot) and the **Pancharamas**
- ★ **Vijayaditya II** (Narendra Mrigaraja) — **108 battles** in 12 years; **108 Shiva temples** (Narendra Mrigeswaralayas) as **penance**

## SEC 1:78:13 | Capitals, towns and other names
- ★ **Vijayaditya III** (Gunaga Vijayaditya) — developed **Kandukuru** like **Bezawada**; built **Pandurangapuram**
- ★ **Yuddhamalla** — **Kartikeya** temple at Bezawada (Bezawada inscription)
- ★ **Birudankaprolu** = **Bikkavolu**; Gunaga Vijayaditya's temple groups

## SEC 1:78:14 | Inscriptions and copper-plate grants → ruler
- ★ **Vipparla** inscription — **Jayasimha Vallabha I**

## SEC 1:78:16 | Religion, queens and Jain patronage
- ★ **Ayyana Mahadevi** — built the Jain **Nedumbasadi (Nedumba Vasati)** near **Vijayawada**; recorded in the **Musinikonda** grant
- ★ Her husband: AP History key — **Kubja Vishnuvardhana**; Group-2 key — **Gunaga Vijayaditya III** (c. 848–892)

## SEC 1:78:17 | Poets, books and court figures → works and titles
- **Chellavva**, court singer and dancer — title **Gandharva-Vidya-Visarada**

## SEC 1:78:18 | Andhra dynasties → period
- ★ **Eastern (Vengi) Chalukyas** — credit for Telugu; Telugu language and literature grew under them though Sanskrit was official
- **Kesari Samudra** lake — **Rudradeva** (APPSC G2 2019 key); trap: **Beta Raja II**
