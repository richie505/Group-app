# ROW 2:43 | Supreme Court - composition, appointment, collegium and NJAC, jurisdictions (original, appellate, advisory, writ)
## SEC 2:43:0 | Basics (Arts 124-147)
### History and placement
- **Federal Court** set up **1937**
- Supreme Court inaugurated **28 January 1950** (two days after the Constitution came into force, 26 January)
- Shifted to **Tilak Marg** building in **1958**
- Constitution: **Part V, Chapter IV** (Articles **124–147**)
### Strength (including the CJI)
| Year | Strength |
|---|---|
| 1950 | **8** |
| 1977 | **18** |
| 1986 | **26** |
| 2009 | **31** |
| 2026 Ordinance | **38** (37 + CJI) |
- **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** — notified **16 May 2026** under **Article 123**; judges other than CJI raised from 33 to **thirty-seven**
### Judges: appointment, tenure, pay
- **Distinguished jurist** route — in the opinion of the **President**; never used; must be a citizen of India
- **NJAC** — six members, to be chaired by the **CJI**; Attorney-General not a member
- **Article 128** retired judge sitting in SC — has SC judge's powers but is **not deemed a regular judge**
- SC judge retires at **65**, resigns in writing to the **President**; HC retirement age **62**
- **CJI** — **no fixed tenure** in years, serves until **65**; oath before and resignation to the **President**
- **HC judges' pension** — charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** (salaries on the State fund)
### Reports and courts
- **Law Commission 214th Report** — submitted **2008** (not 1998)
- **NCRWC** (Justice **M.N. Venkatachaliah**, set up 2000) — report **2002**; recommended a **National Judicial Commission**
- **AP High Court** at **Amaravati** since **1 January 2019**
### Key Articles
- **Article 129** — Court of Record, power to punish **contempt**; **Article 130** — seat at **Delhi**
- **Article 145(3)** — **Constitution Bench**, at least **five judges**: hears substantial questions of constitutional interpretation and **Article 143** references; Article 143 opinion does not bind the President
### CJI tenures
- Shortest-serving CJI — **Kamal Narain Singh**, **18 days** (25 November–12 December 1991)
- **Chandrachud** served **over seven years**

## SEC 2:43:1 | Judges cases
### The Judges Cases
| Case | Year | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| First (**S.P. Gupta**) | **1981** | Consultation **does not** mean concurrence; **executive primacy** |
| Second (**SCAORA v. Union of India**) | **1993** | Reversed 1981; consultation **means concurrence**; created the (two-judge) **collegium**; judges' primacy |
| Third (Presidential reference) | **1998** | Collegium enlarged to **CJI + four senior-most** judges; plural consultation; settled working of collegium |
| Fourth | **2015** | Struck down the **NJAC** |
- Collegium — judicial creation of the **Three Judges Cases**; no express constitutional mention
- Assertion-Reason: Second Judges Case gave birth to the collegium **because** it held consultation means concurrence (R explains A)
### NJAC and the 99th Amendment
- **99th Constitutional Amendment, 2014** — inserted **Article 124A** (NJAC); sought to replace the collegium
- Ratified by **16 States**; assent **31 December 2014**; in force **13 April 2015**
- Struck down in **2015** — executive role infringed **judicial independence**, part of the **Basic Structure** (R explains A)
- Fourth Judges Case **4:1**; lone dissent **J. Chelameswar** (upheld NJAC); Bench led by **J.S. Khehar**
### Appointment, transfer, misconduct
- Formal appointment — **Article 124(2)** (SC judges) and **Article 217(1)** (HC judges), by the President
- Transfer of an HC Chief Justice — **Article 222**
- **In-House Procedure (1999)** — followed **C. Ravichandran Iyer (1995)**; for misconduct not warranting removal
- Retired HC judge may plead only in the **Supreme Court and other High Courts** (**Article 220**); **Article 124(7)** bars a retired SC judge altogether
- Reform ideas: judicial standards and accountability law, permanent disciplinary authority, asset disclosure, RTI (administrative side), transparent appointments — **not** abolition of the collegium by **executive notification**
### Jurisdiction and comparisons
- **Article 131** — federal disputes only; a dispute between **private companies** is outside it
- **Election disputes** concerning Parliament and State Legislatures go to the **High Courts**
- **'Procedure established by law'** (India) — narrower review than US **'due process of law'** (substantive + procedural); US Court called a **'third chamber'**
- Supreme Court replaced the **Federal Court** (1935 Act) and the **Privy Council's** Judicial Committee; Council of States is the Rajya Sabha, not a court

## SEC 2:43:2 | Collegium vs NJAC
- Government may return a name **once**; bound to **appoint** if the collegium **reiterates**
- **HC appointments** initiated by the **HC Chief Justice + two senior-most HC judges**, then to CJI + two senior SC judges
- **SC collegium** — five members: **CJI + four senior-most judges**; no executive member

### Appeals
- **Article 134** — HC reverses acquittal and sentences to **death**: appeal lies **as of right**, no HC certificate
- **Article 136** — special leave **discretionary**, against any judgment of any court or tribunal, except **military tribunals** (136(2)); needs **no HC certificate**
- **Article 137** review = the Court's own judgments, distinct from judicial review of statutes
- **Curative petition** — after dismissal of a review under Article 137; rests on inherent powers under **Article 142**
### Advisory (Article 143)
- **143(1)** — President may refer questions of **law or fact** of public importance; Court **may** give its opinion
- **143(2)** — pre-Constitution treaty/agreement matters: Court **must** give its opinion
- Advice **does not bind** the President
### Appointment and transfer
- **CJI** appointed by the **President** (Article **124(2)**); by convention the **senior-most** judge; collegium only recommends
- **Article 222** — HC judge transferred by the President after consulting the **CJI**
- Collegium in **June 2026** — CJI **Surya Kant**, Justices **Vikram Nath**, **B.V. Nagarathna**, **M.M. Sundresh**, **P.S. Narasimha**; **J.K. Maheshwari** had retired
- **Punjab Cabinet** (September 2026) objected that the State was **bypassed** in appointing the Chief Justice of the **Punjab and Haryana** HC
### Other
- **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** — defines **civil** and **criminal** contempt; the Constitution confers contempt powers but **does not define** contempt
- **e-SCR** — electronic Supreme Court Reports; free digital database of reported judgments, launched **2023**
- **"Imperium in imperio"** — criticism of the collegium as an authority within an authority (opacity, judges selecting judges)
- **Private member's Bill** — social diversity, **90-day** limit for the Centre to notify collegium names, **regional benches** of the SC
- **US Supreme Court** has **no** writ jurisdiction equivalent to **Article 32**
- India synthesises **American judicial supremacy** with **British parliamentary sovereignty**
- Longest-serving CJI — **Y.V. Chandrachud** (16th CJI), 22 February 1978 – 11 July 1985

# ROW 2:44 | High Courts and subordinate judiciary; judicial review, judicial activism, PIL and judicial overreach debate
## SEC 2:44:0 | HC judge vs SC judge
### Constitution of High Courts
- HCs in **Part VI**, **Chapter V**, Articles **214–231**; SC in Part V, Chapter IV, Articles 124–147
- New HC — created by a **law of Parliament**, brought into being by **Presidential notification** (e.g. AP HC under the AP Reorganisation Act)
- **Article 216** — HC strength left to the **President** (no uniform strength); SC strength fixed by **Parliament by law** (Article **124(1)**)
### HC judges: eligibility, oath, tenure
- HC eligibility — **10 years** in judicial office or as an HC advocate; **distinguished jurist** route only for the **SC** (Article **124(3)(c)**)
- **Article 219** — HC judge's oath, taken before the **Governor**; resignation addressed to the **President**
- **Article 220** — restriction on practice after retirement
- **Acting judges** fill in for absent judges or those acting as Chief Justice; appointment ends at **62**
- HC retirement age raised from **60 to 62** by the **15th Amendment, 1963** (R explains A)
- HC judge — salary on the **State's** Consolidated Fund; pension on the **Consolidated Fund of India**
- HC judge removed by the **President's order** after an **address by Parliament** (Article **217(1)**); Governor cannot remove
- SC judge sworn in by the **President** or a person he appoints (Article **124(6)**)
### Articles and review
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **129** | SC Court of Record |
| **215** | HC Court of Record |
| **219** | HC oath |
| **222** | Transfer |
- **UK** — parliamentary sovereignty bars US-style invalidation of Acts; **Canada** and **Australia** have judicial review
- Decisions of President/Governor reviewable on **mala fides** or **irrelevant grounds** (protection under Article **361** is for the office-holder); e.g. **S.R. Bommai**
- **Article 74(2)** — courts cannot inquire into ministers' advice to the **President**; **Article 163(3)** for the **Governor**
- **Articles 31A–31C** and **33** exemptions — constitutional provisions, **not** judicial evolutions
- **Ninth Schedule** — added by the **First Amendment, 1951** (assent 18 June 1951), after land-reform rulings

## SEC 2:44:3 | Judicial review
### Meaning and basis
- Term **'judicial review'** — **not used** in the Constitution; power flows from several Articles; part of the **basic structure**
- Based on a **written Constitution**, **federalism** and **Fundamental Rights**
- Theory — **supremacy of the Constitution** and **limited government**
- Symbol of **federal government** — courts as **umpire** of the Centre-State division
- Basic-structure status — **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**; reaffirmed for tribunals in **L. Chandra Kumar (1997)**
- Power to test laws and executive orders against the Constitution; not a judgment on **policy wisdom**, not an ordinary appeal
- Grounds — violation of FRs, lack of **legislative competence**, contravention of the Constitution or basic structure; **not** disagreement with majority policy
- **Article 372(1)** — continuance of **pre-Constitution laws**, subject to review
- **Article 13(2)** — primary textual foundation for striking down ultra vires laws; **Article 32** part of the Basic Structure
- **Article 137** — review of own judgments; **Article 142** — complete justice; **Article 226** — HC writs
- **Article 21** — **'procedure established by law'**; US uses **'due process of law'**
### Legislature's response and later cases
- Legislature cannot simply overrule a judgment; may **remove its basis** by valid amendment (incl. **retrospective** validating Act) or amend the Constitution subject to the **basic structure**
- **First Amendment, 1951** — response to rulings on **land reform** and **reservation**
- **Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021** — re-enacted provisions struck down in **Madras Bar Association (2025)**
- HC **Chief Justice** — **no absolute immunity**: administrative actions (staff appointments, discipline) open to writs under **Article 226**
### Origins and firsts
- Judicial review began in **Marbury v. Madison (1803)**, Chief Justice **John Marshall** (USA)
- **Parliamentary** form first in **Great Britain**; **presidential** form first in the **USA**
- **Basic Structure** first propounded in **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**
- **Federal Court** inaugurated **1 October 1937** (1935 Act); replaced by SC in 1950
- First PIL considered — **Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar**
