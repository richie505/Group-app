# ROW 1:105 | Slave/Mamluk dynasty - Qutbuddin Aibak, Iltutmish (iqta, Chalisa, Turkan-i-Chahalgani), Razia Sultan, Balban
## SEC 1:105:1 | Rulers in order
### Sultanate dynasties
| Dynasty | Period |
|---|---|
| **Mamluk** | 1206–1290 |
| **Khalji** | 1290–1320 |
| **Tughlaq** | 1320–1414 |
| **Sayyid** | 1414–1451 |
| **Lodi** | 1451–1526 |
### Mamluk rulers
- Mamluk rulers include **Ruknuddin Firuz**, **Alauddin Masud**, **Nasiruddin Mahmud**; **Jalaluddin Khalji** was a Khalji, not a Mamluk
- **Qutbuddin Aibak** — title **Lakh Bakhsh** = "giver of lakhs"; capital at **Lahore**
- **Iltutmish** — shifted capital from **Lahore to Delhi**
- **Nasiruddin Mahmud** (1246–66) — immediately preceded **Balban** (1266–86)
- Under Nasiruddin Mahmud, Balban was **principal minister / naib** and leader of the Forty; briefly displaced by **Imaduddin Raihan**
- Balban broke the power of the Forty by **promoting junior Turks**
- **Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji** crowned himself at **Kilokhari** (1290) — end of Mamluk rule, start of Khalji dynasty

## SEC 1:105:2 | Key rulers
- **Aibak** — rose to **Amir-i-Akhur** (Master of Stables), then general
- **Iltutmish** refused shelter to **Jalaluddin Mangbarni** of Khwarazm — kept **Chengiz Khan** out of India
- **Razia** — discarded **purdah**, wore **male attire**, personally led the army
- Turkish nobles resented Razia's favour to **Yaqut**, an **Abyssinian**; led to revolt under **Altuniya**
- **Balban's rise** — captured by Mongols, bought by Iltutmish → **water-carrier** → member of the **Forty** → **Amir-i-Shikar** (made by Razia)
- Balban created the military department **Diwan-i-Arz**; built outposts and forts against the Mongols

## SEC 1:105:3 | Terms
- ★ **Balban** introduced the Persian festival **Nawruz** in India; Iranian kings his court ideal
- **Balban** — Sultan as **Zil-i-Ilahi** (shadow of God); required **sijda** (greeting lying on the ground) and **paibos** (kissing the feet)
- **Mamluk** — Arabic, "owned"; an **owned military slave** (not a domestic slave)
- **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** (the Forty) — **Turkish military elite**, not Persian revenue clerks

## SEC 1:105:4 | Monuments
- **Qutb Minar** — begun by **Aibak**, completed by **Iltutmish**
- Qutb Minar dedicated to **Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki**
- **Sultan Ghari tomb** (Iltutmish) — Iltutmish called **Father of tomb building**

## SEC 1:105:5 | Exam corner
- End of Mamluk rule: **Balban** (d. **1286**) → grandson **Kaiqubad** (murdered) → infant **Kaimurs** → **Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji** (1290)

## SEC 1:105:7 | Qutbuddin Aibak and Iltutmish → career and contributions
- ★ **Aibak** died **1210** at **Lahore** after a **fall from his horse** while playing **chaugan** (polo)
- ★ Aibak buried in Lahore near **Anarkali Bazaar**; founder of the **Slave Dynasty**
- ★ **Iltutmish** — first Sultan to introduce **regular coins** and declare **Delhi** the capital

## SEC 1:105:8 | Razia, Nasiruddin Mahmud and Balban → career and contributions
- ★ **Balban** (1266–1286) created **Diwan-i-Arz** (military department), separating it from **Diwan-i-Wizarat** (finance)
- ★ **Polo** introduced in India by the **Turks**; Aibak fond of polo (chaugan)

# ROW 1:106 | Khalji dynasty - Jalaluddin; Alauddin Khalji's conquests, Deccan campaigns of Malik Kafur
## SEC 1:106:0 | Jalaluddin Khalji (1290-1296)
- First Sultan with a **benevolent attitude** towards Hindus; lenient to rebels like **Malik Chhajju**
- Crowned at **Kilokhari**; murdered by Alauddin at **Kara** (**1296**)
- Converted, settled Mongols — **New Mussalmans**
- Married his daughter to **Ulugh Khan**, descendant of Chengiz Khan
- Alauddin's **Devagiri raid** (1294–96) — **first Turkish invasion of the south**

## SEC 1:106:1 | Alauddin Khalji (1296-1316)
- Earlier name — **Ali Gurshasp**
- Under Jalaluddin, Alauddin was **Amir-i-Tuzuk** = **master of ceremonies** (Ariz-i-Mumalik = army minister)
- Title **Sikandar-i-Sani** = **the second Alexander**; wished to be a world conqueror
- During Mongol siege of Delhi, sheltered in **Siri** fort; **Zafar Khan** killed at **Kili**

## SEC 1:106:2 | Conquests in order
| Conquest | Year | Ruler defeated |
|---|---|---|
| **Gujarat** | 1299 | **Rai Karan (Karnadeva II Vaghela)** |
| **Ranthambhor** | 1301 | |
| **Chittor** | 1303 | **Ratan Singh** |
| **Malwa** | 1305 | **Mahlak Deva** |
- Gujarat campaign (1299) led by **Ulugh Khan** and **Nusrat Khan**; Queen **Kamala Devi** captured
- Chittor renamed **Khizrabad** after Prince **Khizr Khan**
- Southern policy — rulers **kept thrones on paying tribute**; northern kingdoms annexed
### Malik Kafur's southern campaigns
- **Devagiri** (1307–08, first) → **Warangal** (1309–10) → **Dwarasamudra** (1310–11) → **Madurai** (1311)
### Kakatiya–Delhi relations
- **1303** — Kakatiyas defeated a Delhi army at **Upparapalli**
- Treaty **1309–10**; help against the Pandyas **1311**; tribute stopped after **1316**

## SEC 1:106:3 | Mongol threat
- Alauddin did **not abandon** the north-western frontier: repaired and built forts, founded **Siri**, made **Ghazi Malik** governor of **Punjab**
- Mongol threat → a **large standing army**, paid for by military, revenue and market reforms
- **Genghis Khan** reached the Indus c. **1221** under **Iltutmish**; **1398** invasion was by **Timur**

## SEC 1:106:4 | End of the Khaljis
- ★ Alauddin's conquests in order: **Gujarat / Jaisalmer** (1299) → **Ranthambhor** (1301) → **Chittor** (1303) → **Warangal** (1309–10)
- After Alauddin's death (**1316**), **Malik Kafur** set aside **Khizr Khan** and enthroned the minor **Shihabuddin Umar**
- **Khusrau Khan** subdued Warangal under **Mubarak Shah** — **third invasion**, **1318**
- **Ghazi Malik's** victory over **Khusrau Khan** ended Khalji rule

## SEC 1:106:5 | Exam corner
- **Padmavat** — by **Malik Muhammad Jayasi**, in **Awadhi**, **1540**; narrates the **Padmini** story (some scholars call it legend)

## SEC 1:106:6 | Khalji Sultans → accession, policy, contribution
- ★ **Malik Kafur** ("**Hazar Dinari**", bought for a thousand dinars) — snatched by **Nusrat Khan** in the Gujarat campaign
- ★ **Alauddin** wanted to launch a **new religion**; dropped it on advice (Ulemas opposed); Sikandar-i-Sani on his coins
- ★ **Zafar Khan** — Alauddin's commander who **died fighting the Mongols**
- ★ After **1306**, the **Ravi** was the Sultanate–Mongol boundary; **Malik Kafur** and **Ghazi Malik** stopped Mongols under **Kabak** there
- **Bahlol Lodi** ruled **1451–1489** (not 1491)

## SEC 1:106:8 | Mongol invasions → leaders, battles, defence
- ★ Genghis Khan's original name — **Temujin (Temuchin)**
- ★ Genghis Khan came through the North-West Frontier under **Iltutmish**, chasing **Jalaluddin Mengubirni** of Khwarazm, who sought refuge with Iltutmish

## SEC 1:106:9 | Alauddin's conquests and Malik Kafur's Deccan campaigns → year, ruler defeated, commander
- ★ **1303** — Kakatiya army (**Prataparudra II**) defeated **Alauddin Khalji's** army at Warangal; led by **Fakhruddin Juna** and **Malik Chhajju**
- ★ **Padmini** — wife of **Rana Ratan Singh** (Padmavat, Jayasi, 1540)

# ROW 1:107 | Alauddin Khalji's market control regulations, revenue and military reforms
## SEC 1:107:0 | Why market control
- **Alauddin Khalji** — first **standing army paid in cash**; introduced **dagh** and **huliya**
- Aim of market control — keep **prices low and fixed** so a large army could live on **low, fixed salaries**; curbed merchants
- Effects — low prices and a strong army against the Mongols, but **espionage and repression** caused discontent

## SEC 1:107:1 | The four markets
- **Sarai Adl** — cloth, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil
- **Mandi** — food grains

## SEC 1:107:2 | Officials & departments
- **Shahna-i-Mandi** — superintendent of each market; under the **Diwan-i-Riyasat**, who worked under the **Naib-i-Riyasat**
- **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj** — recovered **arrears** from revenue officials
- **Barids**, **munhiyans** and slave boys monitored market compliance

## SEC 1:107:3 | Military reforms
| Year | Mongols defeated at |
|---|---|
| 1298 | **Jalandhar** |
| 1299 | **Kili** |
| 1305 | **Amroha** |
| 1306 | **Ravi** |
- **Dagh** — branding of horses; **huliya / chehra** — descriptive roll of soldiers

## SEC 1:107:4 | Revenue terms
- State share (**kharaj**) — **one-half** (50%)
- First Sultan to **measure land**, using the **biswa**
- Abolished **kismat-i-khuti** (headman's cess); brought **khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis** under taxation
- **Abolished** (not expanded) intermediaries' privileges; state collected revenue more directly
- **Ghari** — house tax; **Charai** — grazing tax

## SEC 1:107:5 | Barani: revolts & ordinances
- Barani's four causes of revolt: noble **wealth**, noble **intermarriage and gatherings**, **weak spy system**, **drinking**
- Ordinance — noble **marriages and gatherings** needed **royal permission**

## SEC 1:107:6 | Malik Kafur & Warangal
- **Malik Kafur** — **eunuch**, purchased during the Gujarat campaign, rose to **Malik Naib**, led Deccan campaigns
- Second Warangal expedition (**1309–10**) — Kafur with **Khwaja Haji**; Prataparudra submitted; loot sent on **1,000 camels**
- **1303** failed attack — **Potuganti Maili** and **Recherla Venna** defeated **Malik Fakhruddin Juna** at **Upparapalli**
- **Amir Khusrau's Khazain-ul-Futuh** — Warangal fort **double-walled with a moat**, diamond mines; Khusrau went on the **1310** campaign
- Purpose of Deccan expeditions — **plunder and tribute** to pay the army, not annexation

| Kingdom | Ruler |
|---|---|
| Devagiri | **Ramachandra** |
| Warangal | **Prataparudra II** |
| Dwarasamudra | **Vira Ballala III** |
| Madurai (Pandya) | sacked |
- **Tughlaqs** — token currency, capital transfer, canals; **Khaljis** — market control; **Lodis** — first Afghan dynasty

## SEC 1:107:7 | Exam corner
### Alauddin's state policy
- Refused Ulema's guidance — guided by **the interests of the state**
- Unlike Balban, did not depend on **religious sanction**; revived Balban's harsh, centralised rule
- **Kotwal Ala-ul-Mulk** talked him out of **world conquest** and a **new religion**
- **Ariz-i-Mamalik** — head of **army** department
- Reign called the **imperial period** — extensive conquests, centralisation, imperial ambitions
### Market measures
- Aims — price fixation, guaranteed supply, controlled distribution, **abolition of monopolies**
- Main source — **Ziauddin Barani**
- Prohibition of **liquor and gambling**, market control, severe punishment — **Alauddin Khalji**
- **Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah** (1316–1320) abolished price controls, restored confiscated lands
- Significance — first state-run **price-control and rationing** system in India; first **land measurement** for revenue

## SEC 1:107:10 | Military reforms → features and FIRSTs
- ★ Alauddin introduced **price control** in Delhi — not to pay lower wages to palace artisans; **Malik Kabool** was Shahna (market inspector)
- ★ Deccan expeditions were **financial ventures**; he did **not** aim to annex the Deccan states (K.S. Lal: greed of wealth)

## SEC 1:107:11 | Market control → aim, markets, enforcement, supply
- ★ Alauddin's market price control was **highly successful**; meant to maintain a vast standing army

## SEC 1:107:12 | Revenue reforms → terms, share, taxes
- ★ Collected revenue by **measuring cultivated land**
- ★ Measurement limited to **Delhi and nearby areas**, not the entire empire
- ★ **Abolished the powers of provincial governors**

# ROW 1:108 | Tughlaq dynasty - Ghiyasuddin; Muhammad bin Tughlaq's five experiments; Firoz Shah's reforms
## SEC 1:108:0 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
- **Ghazi Malik**, governor of Punjab, killed **Khusrau Shah** and became Sultan in **1320**
- Limited any increase in land revenue to about **one-tenth**, raised gradually; drought remission, waste land cultivated
- Sequence: became Sultan (**1320**) → **Juna Khan** attacked Warangal (**1321–22**) → Warangal fell after five-month siege (**1323**) → died at Afghanpur (**1325**)
- Warangal renamed **Sultanpur** (1323) under Ghiyasuddin
- Died **1325** when a wooden pavilion collapsed at **Afghanpur** (Ibn Battuta suspected **Juna Khan**)
- Built **Tughlaqabad**

## SEC 1:108:1 | MBT's five experiments
- **Transfer of capital** — Delhi to **Devagiri (Daulatabad)**, **1327**; later back to Delhi
- **Token currency** (c. **1329–30**) — **copper and brass** coins, due to **shortage of silver**; forgery → recalled, redeemed at heavy cost
- **Doab taxation** — coincided with famine → **peasant revolts and flight**
- **Qarachil** (Kumaon hills) expedition — to secure the **hill frontier**
- **Khurasan** expedition — Central Asian conquest
- **Swarga-dwari** — famine-relief and cultivation camp on the Ganga near **Kannauj**
- **Daulatabad** — MBT's renamed capital; not a Firoz town (Firoz: **Firozabad, Fatehabad, Hisar Firoza**)

## SEC 1:108:2 | Departments & terms
- ★ **Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi** (MBT) — **agriculture**; bringing **waste land** under cultivation, direct aid to farmers
- **Taqavi / sondhar** loans — for **seeds and wells**
- Ibn Battuta: horse couriers (**uluq**) every **four miles**; foot couriers (**dawa**) three stations to every mile
### Firoz Shah institutions
| Institution | Function |
|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Istihqaq** | pensions and grants |
| **Diwan-i-Khairat** | charity |
| **Diwan-i-Bandagan** | slaves |
| **Dar-ul-Shifa** | free hospital |
- Firoz built the **largest number of canals** among Delhi Sultans; levied irrigation cess **haq-i-sharb**

## SEC 1:108:3 | Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351-88)
### Accession and administration
- Placed on the throne at **Thatta** (**1351**) after death of **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; ruled till **1388**
- Made **iqtas hereditary**; wrote off **taqavi** loans, fixed land revenue, kept the four Islamic taxes
- Made **jizya** a separate tax; extended it to **Brahmins** for the first time (ulema's advice)
- Set up a separate **Public Works Department** for canals, towns, buildings
- Wazir **Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul** — originally the captured Brahmin **Jagannatha Pandit**; paid **compensation** to those who suffered under MBT
### Canals
- **Haq-i-sharb** — **one-tenth** of produce; canals from **Yamuna and Sutlej** to **Hisar**
- First Sultan to dig canals — **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq**; most canals — **Firoz Shah**
### Culture and campaigns
- **Ashokan pillars** brought to Delhi from **Topra** (Ambala) and **Meerut**
- Autobiography — **Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi**
- About **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** from the **Jawalamukhi** temple library (**Nagarkot**); some translated into Persian by **Azizuddin Khalid Khani**
- Two failed Bengal expeditions against **Haji Iliyas**; **Jam Babaniya** of Sind agreed to pay tribute
### Sultan–policy
- **Firoz Shah** — canals, irrigation, public works; **Alauddin** — market control; **Balban** — Blood and Iron; **MBT** — token currency, capital transfer

## SEC 1:108:4 | Ibn Battuta
- Native of **Morocco** (born **Tangiers**, **1304**); came to India in **1333** under MBT
- Made **Qazi of Delhi**; sent as envoy to **China** in **1342**
- **Rihla** (Kitab-ul-Rehla) — in **Arabic**; conditions in the Sultanate and the Deccan
- Recorded **sati** — required the **Sultan's permission**

## SEC 1:108:5 | Revolts & new states
| State | Year | Founder |
|---|---|---|

- ★ Bahmani kingdom founded **1347**, revolting against Delhi; later split into five Deccan sultanates
- Harihara and Bukka had been sent by **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**
- Tughlaq period — **1320 to 1414** (Ghiyasuddin to **Khizr Khan** taking Delhi)
- Timur's invasion (**1398**) — under **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq**, the last Tughlaq; Firoz died 1388; **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq II** and **Abu Bakr** were earlier weak successors
- **Vilasa copper plate** (Prolaya Nayaka) and **Madhura Vijayam** (Gangadevi) — conditions under Tughlaq rule in the south

## SEC 1:108:6 | Exam corner
- **Barani** — MBT a "**mixture of opposites**"; Ibn Battuta — eccentric, severe in temper, generous
- Ibn Battuta — first foreign traveller to Vijayanagara, under **Harihara I**
- **Dagh** (branding of horses) — Alauddin, not Balban; Balban — sijda, paibos, Zil-i-Ilahi, **blood and iron**

- **Daulatabad** — linked with Muhammad bin Tughlaq

## SEC 1:108:7 | Tughlaq rulers → rise, reign, contribution, end — Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
- ★ **1323** — **Ulugh Khan** (later MBT) conquered Orugallu; Warangal renamed **Sultanpur**
- ★ **Feroz Shah Tughlaq** (1351–1388) — **largest canal network**; longest (~**200 km**) from **Sutlej to Hansi**; another Yamuna to Hissar

## SEC 1:108:9 | Muhammad bin Tughlaq's five experiments → cause and result
- ★ **MBT** raised land revenue to **one-half** of produce; called an ill-fated idealist
- ★ **MBT** transferred capital from Delhi to **Daulatabad (Devagiri)**
- ★ First to introduce **token currency** in India — **MBT**; due to lack of silver (Barani)
- ★ MBT issued a new **gold coin**, called **Dinar** by Ibn Battuta; token currency was in **copper/bronze**, not gold, not for West Asian trade
- ★ Token currency failed because the state had **no proper control over currency issue**
- ★ Barani's five schemes: **Doab tax**, **Devgiri capital**, **token currency**, **Khurasan** attack, **Qarachil** expedition

## SEC 1:108:10 | Departments, taxes and terms → meaning
- ★ **Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi** (Diwan-i-Kohi) — **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**, for **agriculture**; trial land of **30 × 30 karohs**
- ★ **Diwan-i-Khairat** — **Firuz Tughlaq**; help to poor Muslims, orphans, widows; marriages of poor Muslim girls
- ★ **Dar-ul-Shafa** (Firuz) — a **free hospital**

## SEC 1:108:11 | Ibn Battuta and historians → statements on MBT
- ★ **Ibn Battuta** — **Moroccan** ("Moorish") traveller (1304–1377); in India **1333–1342** under **MBT**
- ★ Made **Qazi of Delhi**; sent to **China** as ambassador (1342)
- ★ Ibn Battuta gives the detailed description of the Sultanate **postal system**; book **Kitab-ul-Rihla**
- ★ **Badauni** on MBT's death: "the king was freed from his people and they from their king"
- ★ MBT died on the way to **Sindh**, chasing rebel **Taghi** after a revolt in **Gujarat**
- ★ MBT's new gold coin — **Dinar** (Ibn Battuta); silver coin **Adli**; token currency c. **1330** — not in gold

## SEC 1:108:14 | Firoz Shah → revenue, jizya, iqta, books and campaigns
- ★ **Firuz Tughlaq** — initiated state-promoted **canal irrigation**; biggest network of canals
- ★ Canals: Sutlej to Ghaggar (**96 miles**), **Yamuna to Hisar** (**150 miles**), hill of Sirmor to Hansi, Ghaggar to Firozabad
- ★ Separate **department of slaves** — **Diwan-i-Bandagan** (Firuz)
- ★ Levied **jizya on Brahmins** — **Firuz Tughlaq**
- ★ Nagarkot campaign — about **300** Sanskrit manuscripts from **Jawalamukhi** temple, translated into Persian by **Azizuddin Khan**
- ★ **Public Works Department** — **Firuz Shah Tughlaq**; founded towns like **Fatehabad, Hisar, Firozpur, Jaunpur, Firozabad**

## SEC 1:108:15 | Delhi Sultans → distinctive measure; cities of Delhi → founder
- ★ **Ain-ul-Mulk Multani** served Alauddin, MBT and Firuz — **not Iltutmish**; won **Malwa** for Alauddin (1305)
- **Zil-i-Ilahi** theory — **Balban**, not Iltutmish
- **Dahsala** — **Todar Mal**; Abul Fazl wrote Akbarnama and Ain-i-Akbari

# ROW 1:109 | Timur's invasion; Sayyid and Lodi dynasties; the First Battle of Panipat, 1526
## SEC 1:109:0 | From Timur to Babur
- ★ Babur beat Ibrahim Lodi chiefly through **skilled commandants**
- **Alauddin Alam Shah** ended Sayyid rule — **voluntarily gave the throne** to **Bahlol Lodi** (**1451**)
- **Sikandar Lodi** — destroyed temples, imposed **jizya**, yet had a **Sanskrit medical work** translated into Persian
- Babur's descent — **Timur** (father's side), **Chengiz Khan** (mother's side)
- **Khatauli** (1517–18) — Ibrahim defeated by **Rana Sanga** of Mewar; Ibrahim had made **Vikramajit** of Gwalior a vassal
- **Ibrahim Lodi** — only Delhi Sultan to **die on the battlefield**
- Contemporaries of Ibrahim — **Krishnadevaraya**, **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk**, **Rana Sanga** (not Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq)
- **Vasco da Gama** (1498) — Delhi ruler **Sikandar Lodi**

## SEC 1:109:1 | Sayyid order
- ★ Order: **Rukn-ud-din** (1236) → **Mubarak Khan** (1316–1320) → **Firuz Shah Tughluq** (1351–1388) → **Alam Shah** (1445–1451)
- ★ Rukn-ud-din ruled after Iltutmish's death though Iltutmish had named **Raziyya**
- Sayyid order: **Khizr Khan** → **Mubarak Shah** → **Muhammad Shah** → **Alauddin Alam Shah**

## SEC 1:109:3 | Terms & buildings
- ★ **Sikandar Lodi** — Persian poetry under pen name **Gulrukhi**
- **Gaz-i-Sikandari** — Sikandar Lodi's land-measuring yard; also abolished several taxes, used audits and spies
- Sikandar Lodi built **Moth ki Masjid** and royal tombs in the **Lodi Gardens**
- **Mubarak Shah's** octagonal tomb — **blue enamelled tiles**, **guldasta** motifs
- Panipat: **Tulughma** — flanking manoeuvre; **Araba** — carts chained together as defensive line

## SEC 1:109:4 | First Battle of Panipat (1526)
### Before Panipat
- **Timur's route** — crossed the **Indus**, Punjab via **Multan** and **Bhatnair**, sacked **Delhi** (December **1398**)
- Mughal Empire was **not** an effect of Timur — founded by Babur in 1526
- **Khizr Khan** (Timur's viceroy) arrested **Daulat Khan Lodi**, took Delhi in **1414**; ruled as deputy, without the title Sultan
- **Bahlol Lodi** got the throne from **Alam Shah**, the last Sayyid
- **Sikandar Lodi** — rule from **Punjab to Bihar**, subdued **Jaunpur**, defeated **Bengal** rulers and **Rajput** chiefs
### Invitation to Babur
- ★ **Alam Khan** — **uncle of Ibrahim Lodi** and pretender to the Delhi throne; invited Babur
- **Daulat Khan Lodi** (governor of Punjab) and Alam Khan sought Babur's help against Ibrahim; **Rana Sanga** too offered to join (per Babur)
### The battle
- ★ Fought **21 April 1526**, **Babur** vs **Ibrahim Lodi**; Babur won by **skilled warfare** despite a smaller army
- ★ Artillery first used in India at Panipat; Babur first mentions guns at **Bajaur** (**1519**)
- Decisive — **field artillery and muskets**, handled by **Ustad Ali Quli** and **Mustafa**
- Turning point — **ended the Delhi Sultanate**, began the **Mughal Empire**; Mughal rule established by **Babur** (Sher Shah Suri — Sur dynasty)

## SEC 1:109:5 | Babur's battles
| Battle | Year | Defeated |
|---|---|---|
| **First Panipat** | 1526 | Ibrahim Lodi |
| **Khanwa** | 1527 | Rana Sanga |
| **Chanderi** | 1528 | Medini Rai |

- ★ Order: **Panipat → Khanwa → Chanderi → Ghaghra**
- ★ Babur declared **Jehad** at **Khanwa** (**16 March 1527**); took title **Ghazi** after it

## SEC 1:109:6 | Exam corner
- **Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi** — **Yahiya bin Ahmad Sarhindi**, reign of **Mubarak Shah** (1421–34)
- Lodi kingship — Sultan as **first among equals** among Afghan nobles
- **Sikandar Lodi** founded **Agra** (**1504**), shifted capital there from Delhi
- **Lodis** — **first Afghan** and last dynasty of the Sultanate; **Ibrahim Lodi** the last Delhi Sultan

## SEC 1:109:7 | Sayyid and Lodi dynasties → period, founder, succession
- ★ **Agra** founded by **Sikandar Lodi** (**1504**); Sikandar = **Nizam Khan**, third son of Bahlul, crowned **17 July 1489**
- ★ After Timur's invasion — **Sayyid dynasty**; Tughlaqs ended with **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (d. **1412**); **Daulat Khan** Sultan in 1413, defeated by **Khizr Khan**
- ★ Sayyid order: **Khizr Khan** (1414–1421) → **Mubarak Shah** (1421–1434) → **Muhammad Shah** (1434–1445) → **Ala-ud-Din Alam Shah** (1445–1451)
- ★ Lodi order: **Bahlul Lodi** (1451–1489, founder) → **Sikandar Shah** (1489–1517) → **Ibrahim** (1517–1526)
- ★ Mughal-period battles: **Khanwa** → **Ghaghra** → **Chausa** → **Samugarh**

## SEC 1:109:8 | Sayyid and Lodi rulers → contributions
- ★ **Sikandar Lodi** abolished the **tax on grains**; introduced **Gaj-i-Sikandari**; punished embezzlement by nobles

## SEC 1:109:10 | Invasions and battles → year, route, tactics, result
- ★ Mughal Empire founded by **Babur** — beat Ibrahim Lodi at **First Panipat** (**1526**); Akbar consolidated it
- ★ After Panipat Babur captured **Delhi and Agra**
- ★ Ibrahim Lodi — Sultan from **1517**; defeated and killed **21 April 1526**
- ★ Artillery first used by one army at **First Battle of Panipat**
- ★ **Khanwa** (**16 March 1527**) — Babur defeated **Rana Sanga** of **Mewar**; declared **Jehad**, took title **Ghazi**
- ★ Khanwa **more decisive** than Panipat — Rana Sanga a more formidable foe; established Mughal supremacy in North India

## SEC 1:109:12 | Persons → invitation to Babur; contemporaries of Ibrahim
- ★ **Krishnadevaraya** (1509–1529) — contemporary of **Babur**

# ROW 1:110 | Delhi Sultanate - the five dynasties in outline
## SEC 1:110:0 | The five dynasties
### Dynasties
- Delhi Sultanate — **1206 to 1526** (Mamluk founded → defeat of Ibrahim Lodi)
- Order: **Slave/Mamluk** (1206) → **Khalji** (1290) → **Tughluq** (1320) → **Sayyid** (1414) → **Lodi** (1451)
- Shortest — **Khalji**, about **30 years** (1290–1320)
- Longest — **Tughlaq**, **94 years** (1320–1414)
- **Sayyids** — **1414–1451**
### Founders and firsts
| Dynasty / role | Ruler |
|---|---|
| Mamluk founder, **first Sultan of Delhi** | **Qutbuddin Aibak** |
| Khalji founder | **Jalaluddin** |
| Tughluq founder | **Ghiyasuddin Tughluq** (earlier **Ghazi Malik**) |
| Lodi founder, first Afghan ruler | **Bahlul Lodi** |
| First woman ruler | **Razia** |
| Last Sultan | **Ibrahim Lodi** |
- Ghazi Malik became Sultan in **1320** after killing **Khusrau Shah**
- **Khizr Khan** — Timur's **viceroy** in India
- **1323** — **Ulugh Khan** captured **Prataparudra II**; Kakatiya kingdom annexed; Delhi invaded Andhra **five times** (1303–1323)

## SEC 1:110:1 | Rulers & contributions
### Mamluks
- Succession up to Razia: **Aibak → Aram Shah → Iltutmish → Ruknuddin Firoz → Razia**
- **Iltutmish** issued the **tanka** and **jital**; consolidated the Sultanate
- ★ **Qutub Minar** — begun by **Aibak** (only basement), completed by **Iltutmish**; fourth floor damaged in **Firoz Shah's** time; **Alauddin Khalji** did not contribute
- **Razia** (daughter of Iltutmish, 1236–1240) — **first and only woman ruler** of the Delhi Sultanate
### Khaljis
- After Alauddin, **Malik Kafur** set up the child **Shihabuddin**, followed by **Mubarak Shah**
- **Alauddin Khalji** adopted and completed **Balban's frontier policy** against the Mongols
- Alauddin — **Sikandar-i-Sani**; **Gulrukhi** was Sikandar Lodi's pen name; **Ghazi Malik** — pre-accession name of Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq
### Tughluqs
- ★ **Muhammad bin Tughluq** — conquest of **South India** completed (begun by Alauddin through **Malik Kafur**)
- ★ First to introduce **token currency** — **Muhammad bin Tughluq**
- ★ **Badayuni** on MBT's death — "the king was freed from his people and they from their king" (**Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh**)
- ★ **Ibn Battuta** visited India under **Muhammad bin Tughluq**
- ★ **Diwan-i-Kohi** — **Muhammad bin Tughluq**
- **Harihara and Bukka** returned to Hinduism under **Vidyaranya**; founded **Anegondi**, which grew into Vijayanagara
### Lodis
- ★ **Sikandar Lodi** founded a town where **Agra** now stands (**1504**), not Delhi

## SEC 1:110:2 | Titles
- ★ **Alauddin Khalji** raised a powerful army to **protect the empire's boundaries**
- **Aibak** — **Quran Khwan** (recited the Quran melodiously); ruled as **Malik**, never took title Sultan
- **Iltutmish** — **Sultan-i-Azam**; **Balban** — **Zil-i-Ilahi** (Shadow of God)

## SEC 1:110:3 | Key turning points
- **Iltutmish** defeated **Tajuddin Yalduz** of Ghazni at **Tarain** (1215–16); **Qabacha** of Multan and Sindh suppressed **1228**
- Second Tarain **1192**; Chandawar **1194**
- **Timur** (**1398**) — under **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, last Tughlaq ruler
- **Vasco da Gama** at Calicut (**17 May 1498**) — Delhi Sultan **Sikandar Lodi**; Calicut under the **Zamorin**, Vijayanagara under **Immadi Narasimha**
- **Battle of Khatoli** (**1517**) — **Rana Sanga** of Mewar defeated **Ibrahim Lodi**
- **Ibrahim Lodi** — last Sultan of Delhi; defeated at **Panipat** (1526)

## SEC 1:110:4 | Longest and shortest
- **Bahlol Lodi** — longest reign: **1451–1489**, **38 years**

## SEC 1:110:5 | Exam corner
- **Haji Iliyas** made **Bengal independent**; **Firoz** failed to recover it in two campaigns (second **1359–60**)
- **Rajon ki Baoli**, Mehrauli (**1506**) — reign of **Sikandar Lodi** (1489–1517)

## SEC 1:110:6 | The five dynasties → period, founder, origin
- ★ Dynasties in order: **Slave** (1206–1290) → **Khalji** (1290–1320) → **Sayyid** (1414–1451) → **Lodi** (1451–1526)
- ★ **Ghazi Malik** — founder of the **Tughluq** dynasty
- ★ **Sayyids** — called **Kulah-Daran** (wore a pointed cap, **kulah**); fourth dynasty, between Tughlaqs and Lodis
- ★ **Iltutmish** — "**a slave of a slave**" (slave of Aibak, who was Muhammad Ghori's slave); married Aibak's daughter
- ★ **Firuz Tughluq** — **Translation Department** for Hindus and Muslims; Sanskrit texts into Persian
- ★ **Firuz Shah Tughluq** brought **Ashoka's Pillar** to Delhi
- ★ **Firuz Shah Tughluq** — **Employment Exchange** for the unemployed

| Sultan | Period |
|---|---|

## SEC 1:110:7 | Rulers → reign periods and contributions
- ★ **Muhammad bin Tughluq** — **Ministry of Agriculture** (Diwan-i-Kohi)
- ★ **Muhammad bin Tughluq** — first Sultan to join the public celebration of **Holi**
- ★ **Iltutmish** — first Sultan to **shift his capital to Delhi**
- ★ **Alauddin Khalji** — **market reforms** / **market control system**
- ★ **Firuz Shah Tughluq** — **maximum number of canals**
- ★ **Firuz Tughluq** — improved **fruit quality**; planted **1200** orchards near Delhi, restored **30** gardens of Alauddin
- ★ **Sikandar Lodi** — founded **Agra** and made it the capital
- ★ Shortest dynasty — **Khalji** (1290–1320); longest — **Tughluq**
- ★ Longest reign — **Bahlol Lodi** (1451–1489), vs Alauddin (1296–1316), MBT (1325–1351), Balban (1266–1286)

## SEC 1:110:8 | Rulers → succession order
- ★ Afghan rulers: **Bahlol Lodi** (c. 1451–1489, founder) → son **Sikandar Lodi** (1489–1517) → son **Ibrahim Lodi**

## SEC 1:110:9 | Battles, invasions and breakaway kingdoms
- ★ Rivals of **Iltutmish** — **Tajuddin Yalduz** (Ghazni), **Nasiruddin Qabacha** (Sindh), **Ali Mardan** (Lakhnauti); **Malik Altunia** was not one
- ★ Iltutmish took control of Delhi after **Aram Shah's** death
- ★ **Tughril Khan** revolted under **Balban**, against the appointment of **Amin Khan** in Bengal
- ★ Mongols under **Genghis Khan** invaded / first seen on the **Indus** under **Iltutmish**
- ★ **Battle of Khatoli** (**1518**) — **Maharana Sanga** badly defeated **Ibrahim Lodi**

## SEC 1:110:10 | Sultans → titles and epithets
- ★ **Qutbuddin Aybak** — "**Lakh Baksh**"; first ruler of the **Slave Dynasty** (**1206–1210**); capital **Lahore**; born in **Turkistan**
- ★ Aybak patronised **Hasan Nizami** and **Fakhr-i-Mudabbir**
- ★ Balban's full name — **Ghiyasuddin** Balban; ninth Mamluk Sultan; wazir and heir of **Nasiruddin Mahmud**
- ★ **Firuz Tughluq** — **employment office** for the unemployed

## SEC 1:110:11 | FIRSTs and LASTs → ruler
- ★ **Amir Khusrau** — reign of **Alauddin Khalji**
- ★ **Raziyya Sultan** (1236–1240) — first woman ruler of medieval India; only woman on the Delhi throne
- ★ Last Tughluq — **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (1394–1412); **Khwaja Jahan** founded independent **Jaunpur**; challenged by **Nusrat Shah**
- ★ Slave dynasty (1206–90) — **Aibak** (1206–10), **Iltutmish** (1211–36), **Balban** (1266–86); **Ibrahim Lodi** not of it

# ROW 1:111 | Sultanate central administration - Sultan, wazir, the diwans, nobility, theory of kingship
## SEC 1:111:0 | Theory of kingship
### Balban
- ★ **Balban** took the title **Zil-i-Ilahi** (Shadow of God); also **Niyabat-i-Khudai** (representative / deputy of God)
- ★ Balban — policy of **blood and iron**; divine right of kings, autocratic Sultan; Sultan next after the **Paigambar** in dignity
- ★ Balban's real name **Baha-ud-din**; title **Ulugh Khan** from Nasiruddin Mahmud; did **not** introduce the **Iqtadari** system
- Balban introduced **sijda**, **paibos**, **Nauroz**; **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** was created by **Iltutmish**
- Balban **excluded low-born men** from high office (appointing them — **MBT**)
- Din-i-Ilahi — Akbar; **Tuti-i-Hind** — Amir Khusrau
### Sultans and the Caliph
- ★ **Iltutmish** — received **khillat** from the Caliph of **Baghdad** (**1229**); Sultanate became part of **Dar-ul-Islam**
- **Mubarak Shah Khalji** — proclaimed himself **Caliph**
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** — sought investiture from the Abbasid Caliph in **Egypt**

## SEC 1:111:1 | The Sultan & the state
- Succession — no fixed rule; **acceptance by nobles and ulema**, or **military strength**
- **Sharia** — civil matters of Muslims; supplemented by royal decrees, **zawabit**; criminal law per Sultan's rules; Hindus kept personal law
- **Turko-Mongol** — decimal army units, mounted archers; **Perso-Arabic** — title Sultan, sijda, paibos, Nauroz

## SEC 1:111:2 | Central diwans

|---|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Insha** | correspondence, decrees | |
| **Diwan-i-Wizarat** | finance | |
| **Diwan-i-Qaza** | justice | |
| **Diwan-i-Arz** | army recruitment, inspection | **Balban** |
| **Diwan-i-Bandagan** | slaves | **Firoz Tughlaq** |
| **Sadr-us-Sudur** | religious affairs, endowments | |
- Chief Sadr was also the **Chief Qazi**
- **Wizarat's heyday** — **Firoz Tughlaq**, with **Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul** running the government

## SEC 1:111:3 | Officials
- ★ **Khareetadar** — **despatcher of royal decrees**
- **Barid-i-Mumalik** — news and **intelligence**
- **Qazi-ul-Quzat** — Chief Justice

- **Khut** — hereditary rural landholder

## SEC 1:111:4 | Wazirs & key nobles
- **Nizam-ul-Mulk Junaidi** — served **Iltutmish** (not MBT)

## SEC 1:111:5 | Nobility over time
- **Iltutmish** created the **Forty** (Turkish slave-nobles); **Balban** destroyed their power
- **Khalji Revolution** (**1290**) — high offices opened to **non-Turks and Indian Muslims**
- **MBT** — rationalist absolutism; appointed **non-Turks and low-born** persons
- **Lodis** — Afghan tribal nobility; Sultan as **first among equals**
- ★ **Diwan-i-Bandagan** — **Tughlaq** (Firuz)
- ★ Firuz banned inhuman punishments (amputation, molten lead, iron nails); set up **Diwan-i-Khairat**

## SEC 1:111:7 | Exam corner
- Sultans saw themselves as **representatives of the Caliph**; not every Sultan got investiture
- **Alauddin Khalji** — titles **Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin**, without investiture
- **Iltutmish** — investiture from Caliph of Baghdad (c. **1229**)
- **Mir Bakshi** — a **Mughal** office, not Sultanate
- **Muhammad Ghori's** coins — Goddess **Lakshmi** with the **Kalma**; **Mahmud's** silver coins — Arabic and Devanagari Sanskrit

## SEC 1:111:8 | Sultans → inscriptions, coins and relations with the Caliph
- ★ Term **Zillillah** first used by **Balban**
- ★ **Balban** — "**Helper of a Caliph**" in inscription at the mosque at **Garhmukteshwar**
- ★ **Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq** — separate **Agriculture Department**, planned **rotation of crops**
- ★ **Firuz Tughluq** — most slaves (about **1,80,000**); **Diwan-i-Bandgan**; slaves paid **10 to 100 tankas**
- ★ **Alauddin Khalji** — **Alexander the Second**; titles **Yamin-ul-Khilafat**, **Nasir-e-Amir-ul-Momineen** on coins
- ★ Heyday of the **Wizarat** — **Tughluq** dynasty; wizarat inspired by Persia

## SEC 1:111:9 | Sultans → theory of kingship
- ★ **Balban** — first Muslim ruler with a **theory of kingship** like the divine right of kings; Sultan the representative of God on earth, after the Paigambar

## SEC 1:111:10 | Features of the state — legal basis and nature
- ★ Medieval government — fusion of **Perso-Arabic**, **Turko-Mongol** and **Indian** elements

## SEC 1:111:11 | Central departments (diwans) → head, function, founding Sultan
- ★ **Firuz Tughluq** — separate **department for slaves**
- ★ **Balban** — **Diwan-i-Arz** for a large standing army against the Mongols; commander **Imad-ul-Mulk**, freed from the Wazir's financial control
- ★ **Diwan-i-Arz** — **defence**

- ★ **Chaudhary** — chief rural land-revenue official; village headman
- ★ **Muqaddam / Chaudhari** — **village headman**, highest revenue authority in the village (not an accountant)

## SEC 1:111:16 | Universal angles
- ★ **Iltutmish** — first Sultan to receive **investiture** from the Caliph; first **legally independent** Sultan of Delhi
- ★ **1229** — Caliph of Baghdad **Al-Muntasir Billah** gave him the title **Sultan-i-Azam**
