# ROW 1:229 | Gandhian philosophy - truth, ahimsa, satyagraha, means and ends
## SEC 1:229:0 | Key terms
- **Truth** is the end; **non-violence** is the means
- Means are everything: the nature of the end is determined by the **means**
- Between cowardice and violence, Gandhi preferred **violence**; ahimsa is a quality of the **strong**, not the weak
- **Satyagraha** — born **1906** in the **Transvaal**; early English rendering: **passive resistance**
- **Swaraj** — wider than political freedom: **self-rule, self-control** and capacity of the masses to govern themselves without coercion
- ★ Family planning — **self-control** is the best means

## SEC 1:229:1 | Quotations
- ★ **"Wrong means never take us to right ends"** — **M.K. Gandhi**; means and ends are two sides of a coin
- ★ Politics for Gandhi = **activity for public welfare** (social welfare)
  - Key suggestion: **decentralisation of power** — encourage **village panchayats**
- **"God is Truth"** = earlier formulation; **"Truth is God"** = later formulation (truth given independent, central status)
- Politics bereft of religion (morality of truth and non-violence) is a **death-trap**
- **Gandhi's Talisman** — before a step, recall the face of the **poorest and weakest** person

## SEC 1:229:2 | Forms of satyagraha
| Struggle | Year | Gandhi's "first" |
|---|---|---|
| **Champaran** | 1917 | first **civil disobedience** |
| **Ahmedabad** | 1918 | first **hunger strike** |
| **Kheda** | 1918 | first **non-cooperation** |
| **Rowlatt Satyagraha** (hartal) | 1919 | first **all-India mass movement** |

## SEC 1:229:3 | Rules for satyagrahis
- A Satyagrahi must **never insult the opponent**
- Rules require **non-retaliation**, submission to **arrest** and observance of **jail discipline**

## SEC 1:229:4 | Gandhism vs Marxism
- Interrelated principles of **Swaraj**: **swadeshi, ahimsa, satyagraha, decentralisation, education for all**
  - **Class dictatorship** is NOT a Gandhian principle
- **Trusteeship** as basis of property relations — **Gandhism**; Marxism seeks **abolition of private property**

## SEC 1:229:5 | Other comparisons
- ★ **Non-violence** — a way to attain **truth**; ahimsa is the means, truth the end, inseparable
  - Truth = **reality**; described as existence, consciousness and bliss
- Gandhi a **philosophical anarchist** — ideal of **enlightened anarchy**; State = violence in concentrated form
- Socialism of the Indian **Preamble** — blend of **Marxism and Gandhism**, with a **Gandhian** inclination

## SEC 1:229:6 | Exam corner
- ★ **Strike** — the **last** step in the Gandhian strategy of Satyagraha
- ★ **Fasting** — the **first** step; Gandhi called it the **"Fire Arrow"**

## SEC 1:229:7 | Gandhian terms → meaning
- ★ **Heavy industrialisation** — favoured by **Nehru**, not by Gandhi
  - Nehru shared Gandhi's social principles (truth, non-violence, against untouchability) but differed on **economics**

## SEC 1:229:8 | Gandhi → statements and quotations
- ★ Gandhian politics = **morality, religion and humanity**; **no place for authority** — aim a **stateless society**
- ★ Cruellest form of violence — **persistence of poverty**
- ★ **"Which is morally wrong, can never be politically right"** — **M.K. Gandhi**
- ★ Aim of a Satyagrahi is **not to defeat the enemy**; **suffering** is the driving force of Satyagraha

## SEC 1:229:11 | Gandhism compared — Marxism, socialism, anarchism, individualism
- ★ Common goal of Gandhism and Marxism — a **stateless society** (approaches differ)
  - Gandhian stateless society founded on **non-violence, truth, simplicity**

# ROW 1:230 | Gandhian social and economic thought - trusteeship, sarvodaya, swadeshi, village republic, bread labour
## SEC 1:230:0 | Key terms
- ★ **Gandhian Innovation** — **produce more from less input for more people**; coined by **Prof. Prahlad** and **R.A. Mashelkar**
- **Sarvodaya** — welfare/uplift of **all**, reaching the **last person**
- **Swadeshi and khadi** — promote **rural employment** and **self-reliance**; bridge towns and villages; Gandhi popularised the **charkha**

## SEC 1:230:1 | Books behind the ideas
- ★ **Unto This Last** — **John Ruskin**; Gandhi's **Gujarati** paraphrase titled **Sarvodaya** (**1908**)
  - Influenced his ideas on social equality, labour and welfare of all
- **The Kingdom of God is Within You** — **Leo Tolstoy**; Gandhi took **love and non-violence** from it

## SEC 1:230:2 | Roots of the ideas
- **Bread labour** — sources: **Tolstoy** (from **Bondaref**) and **Ruskin's Unto This Last**; linked by Gandhi to **yajna**
- **Thoreau** (Civil Disobedience) — gave the right to **break unjust laws**, not bread labour
- **Trusteeship** — applied to **both capitalists and zamindars** (e.g. **Ahmedabad mill owners**)

## SEC 1:230:3 | Gandhian economy
- Non-violent economy requires **decentralisation** and **self-reliance**; production for **need**, not profit
- **1925** — **All India Spinners' Association**

# ROW 1:232 | Champaran, Ahmedabad Mill and Kheda Satyagrahas
## SEC 1:232:0 | Three early satyagrahas
### Sequence
- Order: **Champaran (1917)** → **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (Feb-Mar 1918)** → **Kheda (1918)** → **Rowlatt Satyagraha (April 1919)**
- Gandhian firsts: Champaran — first **Satyagraha** in India; Ahmedabad — first **fast**; **Kheda** — first **non-cooperation**
- Gandhi first used a **hunger strike** as a weapon in the **Ahmedabad Mill Strike** (1918)
### Champaran (1917, Bihar)
- ★ Related to the **Tinkathia** system — indigo on **3/20** of the land
- ★ **Ram Manohar Lohia** — not related to Champaran
- Year: **1917** (1915 is a wrong attribution)
- Precursor — **Bengal Indigo Revolt of 1859**
- Planters **raised** taxes and imposed illegal levies (did not reduce them); issue from **forced indigo cultivation**
- **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918** — abolished **Tinkathia**; **25 per cent** refund of illegal exactions (on Enquiry Committee's advice)
### Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
- Workers demanded **50%** wage rise (wartime inflation); owners offered **20%**
- Lasted **21 days** (not 35); issues **plague bonus** and wages; workers won **35%**
### Kheda (1918)
- Cause: **crop failure** (drought/famine); peasants sought remission/suspension of land revenue, initially refused
- Field leader — **Vallabhbhai Patel**
- Called off when government agreed: collect revenue only from **those able to pay**, suspend for poor peasants, **return confiscated property**
### Later movements
- Order: **Home Rule (1916)** → **Non-Cooperation (1920)** → **Civil Disobedience (1930)** → **Quit India (1942)**
- **Quit India** — demanded the **immediate end** of British rule; **"Do or Die"** call at **Bombay**, 1942

## SEC 1:232:1 | Champaran sequence
- Gandhi **defied the order to leave**; toured villages with his lawyers **recording peasants' complaints** → government ordered an inquiry
- **Champaran Agrarian Enquiry Committee (1917)** — chaired by **F.G. Sly**; appointed by the **Bihar and Orissa** government; Gandhi represented the ryots

## SEC 1:232:2 | Terms
- Directed against **forced indigo cultivation** and **illegal exactions** by **European planters** (not forest tax)
- **Tinkathia / Teen Kathiya** — indigo on **3/20th** of the land
- **Tawan** — **lump-sum compensation** demanded when indigo lost value
- **Sharahbeshi** — **enhanced rent**
- **Mahadev Desai** — Gandhi's **secretary**; joined the Champaran work in 1917 (not a planters' leader)
- **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan Sangh)** — **1920**, **Anusuya Sarabhai** with **Gandhi**

## SEC 1:232:3 | Persons
- ★ **Mahatma Gandhi and Bihar, Some Reminiscences** — **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**
- Political mentor — **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**; intellectual influences — **Tolstoy, Ruskin, Thoreau**
- **Rajkumar Shukla** — local **indigo cultivator** who persuaded Gandhi to visit Champaran
- Ahmedabad: demand **50%**; Gandhi advised and tribunal awarded **35%**
- Kheda: **Gandhi** — spiritual head; **Patel, Mohanlal Pandya, Narahari Parikh** — mobilisation
- Kheda began the **Gandhi–Patel** alliance
- All three struggles under Viceroy **Lord Chelmsford** (1916–1921)

## SEC 1:232:4 | Gandhi's path to leadership
### Before Champaran
- **Phoenix Settlement** — **1904**, near **Durban**; **Tolstoy Farm** — **1910**, near **Johannesburg**
- Return to India **9 January 1915** — now **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**

### Rise
- ★ First successful Satyagraha after South Africa — **Champaran** (1917, Bihar), against the **Tinkathia** system
- **1917–1919** established Gandhi as a **national leader** and Satyagraha as a **method of struggle**
### Jallianwala Bagh
- Crowd gathered on **Baisakhi**, **13 April 1919**, amid Rowlatt Satyagraha protests
- Dyer gave **no warning**, blocked the **only exit**, fired about **ten minutes**, **1,650 rounds**

# ROW 1:233 | Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh; Hunter Committee
## SEC 1:233:0 | Rowlatt Act, 1919
### Name and origin
- ★ **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919** = **Rowlatt Act**; named after **Sir Sidney Rowlatt**, chair of the **Sedition Committee**
- Extension of the **Defence of India Act, 1915**
- ★ Passed in **1919** — on **10 March 1919**
### Provisions
- ★ Authorised **imprisonment without trial** and **summary procedures** for trial — hence popular indignation
- ★ Aim — curtail **national and revolutionary activities**; criminalise dissent
- In-camera trials by a **three-judge** High Court panel, **no appeal**
- Popularly: **"No Vakil, No Appeal, No Daleel"**; Gandhi's reply — **Rowlatt Satyagraha**
### Protest and repeal
- **Jinnah, Malaviya, Mazhar-ul-Haq** — **resigned** in opposition (did not draft the Hunter report)
- Repealed under Viceroy **Lord Reading**, along with the **Press Act of 1910**

## SEC 1:233:1 | 1919 in dates
| Date (1919) | Event |
|---|---|
| **February** | **Satyagraha Sabha** formed at **Bombay** (Home Rule League networks) |
| **6 April** | nationwide **hartal** — fasting, prayer, protest |
| **9 April** | **Kitchlew** and **Satyapal** arrested (not 13 April) |
| **13 April** | Jallianwala Bagh massacre |
| **18 April** | Rowlatt Satyagraha **withdrawn** — violence in **Punjab and Ahmedabad**; Gandhi's "**Himalayan blunder**" |
| **31 May** | **Tagore** renounced **knighthood** |
| **14 October** | **Hunter Committee** set up |

- ★ **Hunter Committee** (Disorders Inquiry Committee) — appointed after the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre**
  - ★ Headed by **Lord William Hunter**; **7 members** (**4 British, 3 Indians**); examined disturbances in **Bombay, Delhi, Punjab**
- ★ **Tagore** gave up Knighthood in protest at the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre**; **Shankaran Nair** resigned from the Viceroy's Council
- Rowlatt Satyagraha — **first all-India mass strike**; **urban-centred** (not confined to rural Punjab)
- Pairs: **Champaran** (1917) — indigo; **Kheda** (1918) — peasants; **Ahmedabad** (1918) — mill workers; **Dandi** (1930) — Salt March

# ROW 1:234 | Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement - programme, spread, Chauri Chaura and withdrawal
## SEC 1:234:0 | NCM chronology
### Khilafat
- Aim — restore the **Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa** and protect his **territories**
- Opposed the **Treaty of Sevres** (**May 1920**)
- Leaders: **Ali brothers, Abul Kalam Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani, Dr. M.A. Ansari**; **Tej Bahadur Sapru** — not a Khilafat leader (mediator of Gandhi-Irwin Pact, 1931)
- Lost relevance when Turkey abolished the Caliphate — **1924**
- ★ Aitchison: "we could not play off the **Mohammedans against the Hindus**" — **Khilafat and Non-Cooperation (1919-22)**
- ★ Khilafat seen as a once-in-a-hundred-years chance for Hindu-Muslim unity — **Mahatma Gandhi**
  - ★ **Hakim Ajmal Khan** renounced the title **Haziq-ul-Mulk** during the Khilafat agitation
- ★ **Moplah Rebellion (1921)** — offshoot of the **Khilafat Movement**
### Launch
- Gandhi presided over the **All-India Khilafat Conference, Delhi, November 1919** — first proposed non-violent non-cooperation
- Background: **Khilafat issue, Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh** (not the Simon Commission Report)
- ★ Launched **1 August 1920** — day **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** died; **Calcutta special session** approved it (Sept 1920)
- **Nagpur session** (Dec 1920) — presided by **C. Vijayaraghavachariar**
- At Calcutta, **Konda Venkatappayya** first opposed the resolution, then resigned from the **Madras Legislature**
  - Other Andhra participants: **Pattabhi Sitaramayya, Duggirala, A. Kaleswara Rao, Peta Bapayya**
- ★ **M.A. Jinnah** did **not** support the movement
### Course
- Lawyers who gave up practice included **T. Prakasam** (with Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Rajagopalachari, Patel); not Ambedkar
- Tribals and forest users opposed **pullari**; peasants joined **no-tax** campaigns
- **Akali Movement** (1920–25) → **Sikh Gurdwaras Act, 1925**
- Gandhi at **Kakinada**, April 1921 — inspired **Durgabai Deshmukh**
- **Palnadu forest satyagraha** — **Kanneganti Hanumanthu**; boycott of forest officials, refusal of **grazing tax**
- April 1919: Gandhi detained at **Palwal** on the way to Punjab
- NCM made Congress a **mass force** for the first time; Swaraj **not** won in one year
### Chauri Chaura and withdrawal
- ★ Halted after **Chauri Chaura** (February 1922) — **Gorakhpur** district, UP; police station burnt, **22 policemen** killed (4 February 1922)
- Withdrawn via the **Bardoli resolution**, **11–12 February 1922** — turn to **constructive work**
- Defence of the accused — **Madan Mohan Malaviya**
- Withdrawal a **"national calamity"** — **Subhas Chandra Bose**; **M.N. Roy** — weak leadership
- **Marxist** view — halted to prevent a radical **anti-landlord** turn
### Aftermath
- **Great Trial**, Ahmedabad, **18 March 1922** — Judge **C.N. Broomfield**; sedition under **Section 124A** (Young India articles); **six years**; Gandhi pleaded guilty
- Gandhi's papers: **Young India, Navajivan, Indian Opinion, Harijan**; **The Bengalee** was **Surendranath Banerjea's**
- Delhi special session (**September 1923**) let Swarajists **contest elections**
- Gandhi's only Congress presidency — **Belgaum, 1924**
