# ROW 5:112 | Regulation and ethics of biotechnology - GEAC, biosafety, bioethics, biopiracy, IPR and the ethical/social/legal debate
## SEC 5:112:0 | GEAC
### Status and set-up
- **GEAC** (Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee) — **apex statutory body** regulating GMOs in India (not FSSAI, CPCB or ICAR)
- Constituted under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**
- Renamed from "Approval" to "**Appraisal**" Committee in **2010**
- Chair — **Special or Additional Secretary, MoEFCC**; co-chair — senior officer of the **Department of Biotechnology**
### Functions
- Deals with **field trials**, **environmental release**, **commercial cultivation** and large-scale use of hazardous microorganisms and recombinants
- Not its function — fixing the **maximum retail price** of commercial seeds
### Related bodies and debates
- **NGT** — established under the **NGT Act, 2010**
- **CGWA** — set up under **Section 3(3)** of the EPA; **CPCB** — under the **Water Act, 1974**
- **Genetic modification of crops** — flagged for **biodiversity** concerns (APPSC-GS 2023): gene flow, monoculture, loss of diversity
- "**Designer babies**" — ethical concern over genetic technologies used to **select or modify desired traits** in human offspring

## SEC 5:112:1 | Committees under Rules 1989
- **IBSC** — first tier, **institutional level**
- Sequence: **IBSC** → **RCGM** → **GEAC** → **SBCC** → **DLC** → expert committees
- **SBCC** — **state level**; powers to **inspect, investigate and take punitive action**
- **DLC** — **district-level** monitoring
- **GEAC** — environmental release and commercial use

## SEC 5:112:2 | GM crops in India
### Regulators and approvals
- **RCGM** — under **DBT**; monitors ongoing research and advises GEAC (does not approve commercial release)
- **GEAC** (under MoEFCC) — approves **environmental release and commercial use** of GM crops
- **BRAI Bill, 2013** — proposed a **single regulator** for biotechnology; lapsed with the **15th Lok Sabha**
- **BioE3 Policy, 2024** — **single-window clearance** and **regulatory sandboxes**
- **SDN-1 and SDN-2** genome editing — small edits **without foreign DNA**; exempted from GMO rules under the **2022** guidelines
- **SDN-3** — **transgenic**; remains under full regulatory control
### Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2002** | Bt cotton approved |
| **2006** | PIL |
| **2010** | Bt brinjal moratorium; **State NOC** made mandatory for field trials |
| **2014** | GEAC cleared field trials of **11 crops and 21 varieties** |
| **2022** | GEAC cleared environmental release of GM mustard **DMH-11** |
| **July 2024** | Supreme Court **split verdict** on DMH-11; both judges directed Centre to frame a **national GM crop policy** |
### Crops and genes
- **Bt cotton** — initially **three MAHYCO hybrids** approved for three years; the **only GM crop** commercially cultivated in India
- **Bollgard II** cotton — **Cry1Ac and Cry2Ab** from **Bacillus thuringiensis**
- **Bt brinjal** — resistance to the **fruit and shoot borer**
- **DMH-11** (GM mustard) — **barnase-barstar** genes from **Bacillus amyloliquefaciens**; developed by **Delhi University**
### Risks and biosafety levels
- GM crop risks: insect resistance, harm to non-target organisms, weediness, genetic homogenisation, toxicity, allergenicity, antibiotic resistance — **not ozone depletion**
- **BSL-4** — lethal agents with no cure (e.g. **Ebola**)
- **BSL-3** — serious airborne agents (e.g. **TB**); **BSL-2** — moderate-risk agents

## SEC 5:112:3 | Genome-edited crops
- **Pusa Rice DST1** — edited form of **MTU1010**; **drought and salinity tolerance** via **DST gene knockout** using **SDN-1**
- Pusa Rice DST1 — India's **first genome-edited rice** for commercial launch; to be regulated like a **conventional crop**
- SDN-1 and SDN-2 exempted crops — need **IBC approval**, skip the **GEAC** route
- **Golden Rice** — biosynthesis of **beta-carotene**, precursor of **Vitamin A**

## SEC 5:112:4 | IPR in biotech
### Laws
- **GM food** (incl. import) — **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006**; FSSAI GM food framework of **2024**; import without competent authority approval prohibited
- EPA — GMO release; PPVFR Act — plant varieties; Patents Act — patents
### Patents Act sections
- **Section 3(j)** — excludes **plants, animals, seeds and essentially biological processes**; **microbial processes** patentable
- **Section 3(d)** — Supreme Court upheld **Glivec (imatinib)** rejection in **Novartis v Union of India (2013)**
### PPV&FR Act, 2001
- Farmers may save, use, sow, resow, exchange, share and sell farm produce incl. seed — but **not branded seed** of protected varieties
- **FL-2027 (FC5)** potato — **PepsiCo** sued **Gujarat** farmers, later withdrew; PPVFR Authority **revoked** registration in **2021**
- Act **bars registration** of varieties carrying **GURT** (Genetic Use Restriction Technology)
- Farmers' objections to GURT/terminator seeds — **dependency** on companies, **recurring costs**, **loss of seed-saving**

## SEC 5:112:5 | Biosafety & biosecurity
- **Cartagena** Protocol on Biosafety — Cartagena is in **Colombia**
- **WMD Act, 2005** — criminalises **biological weapons** in India
- India is a Party to the **BWC** (opened **1972**, in force **1975**)
- Gap — absence of a **unified national biosecurity framework**; laws lag behind synthetic biology and gene editing
- **National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research, 2017** — **ICMR and DBT**
- "**CRISPR babies**" — **He Jiankui**, **China**, **2018**

## SEC 5:112:6 | Exam corner
- ★ **Maharashtra and Haryana** — farmers defied government and cultivated unapproved Bt cotton and Bt brinjal, protesting regulatory delays
- **Maharashtra** — leading **Bt cotton** State
- Primary environmental concern of GM crops — **cross-pollination / gene flow** to non-GM crops
- GM product approval: **7–10 years** in India vs **3–5 years** globally
  - due to overlapping mandates of **DBT, GEAC, CDSCO, FSSAI, NBA** and no biosafety liability law

## SEC 5:112:7 | GEAC — ministry, Act, chair, role
- ★ **GEAC** — regulates **GMOs** in India; apex statutory body approving environmental release of GM crops
- ★ GEAC permission needed for cultivation of any GM crop (e.g. Bt cotton) — under the Ministry of **Environment and Forests**
- ★ Constituted under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**; renamed Approval → Appraisal in **July 2010**
- ★ **Bt brinjal** — objective: to make it **pest-resistant**
  - gene **Cry1Ac** from soil bacterium **Bacillus thuringiensis**; against lepidopteran pests like brinjal **fruit and shoot borer** (Leucinodes orbonalis)
- **Eco-Sensitive Zones** — activities **regulated, not necessarily prohibited**; buffers protecting nearby sensitive areas

- ★ Law in India **prohibits import of GM food** without approval of the **competent authority**
- ★ India **does import** apples from the **USA**: after **June 2023** settlement of **six WTO disputes** (Mutually Agreed Solutions), India lifted additional duties on **eight** US products incl. **apples, walnuts, almonds**

# ROW 5:113 | Nanotechnology - nature, scope, applications, Nano Mission, health and environmental concerns
## SEC 5:113:0 | Basics
- ★ Nanoparticle size — **1 to 100 nm**
- One nanometre — **10^-9 m** (one billionth of a metre); 0.1–1 nm is **atomic** scale
- Unusual properties — **quantum effects** and very high **surface-area-to-volume ratio**
- **STM** — images atoms on **conducting surfaces**; **AFM** works on non-conducting materials too
- **Bottom-up** synthesis — **self-assembly**; **top-down** — lithography, milling, etching
- **2023 Chemistry Nobel** (**Bawendi, Brus, Ekimov**) — **quantum dots**; size-tunable colour, used in displays and solar cells

## SEC 5:113:1 | Pioneers
### Materials
- ★ **Graphene** — the **strongest** material; made of **carbon only**
  - single layer of carbon atoms in a 2D **hexagonal lattice**; basic element of graphite and other allotropes
- ★ **Fullerene** — an **allotrope of carbon** (not a fluorine compound)
  - **Buckminsterfullerene C60**: hollow sphere with **12 pentagonal and 20 hexagonal** faces
- **C60** — "**buckyball**"; **Kroto, Curl and Smalley**, Nobel Chemistry **1996**
### People
| Person | Contribution | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **Feynman** | lecture that gave the idea | **1959** |
| **Norio Taniguchi** | coined "nanotechnology" | **1974** |
| **Binnig and Rohrer** | STM; Nobel Physics **1986** | **1981** |
| ★ **Sumio Iijima** | carbon nanotubes | — |

## SEC 5:113:2 | Carbon allotropes
### Diamond
- ★ **Diamond** — **hardest** natural substance; least compressible, best thermal conductor among natural materials
- Each carbon bonded to **four** others (tetrahedral 3D network)
- **Electrical insulator** (no free electrons); excellent **thermal conductor** (lattice vibrations) — two separate facts
- **Transparent**, very high **refractive index**
### Graphite
- ★ **Graphite** — **lubricant** for heavy machines; anti-seize, anti-friction
- ★ Soft and slippery — layers held by weak **van der Waals forces**, so they slide
- Opaque, black, good **electrical conductor**
### Graphene, fullerene, nanotubes
- **Graphene** — one-atom-thick **sp²** layer of **graphite**; strong single-atom sheet
- Rolled graphene cylinders — **carbon nanotubes**
- **Fullerene (C₆₀)** — hollow **spherical cage** of 60 carbon atoms
- ★ Most important property of nanomaterials (RAS 2013 key) — **friction**

### Pathogens and diseases
- ★ **Filariasis** — fever in initial days, chronic inflammation, swelling especially in **lower limbs**
- **Lymphatic filariasis** — nematode **Wuchereria bancrofti**
- **Plasmodium falciparum** — most **lethal**; **cerebral malaria**
- **Japanese encephalitis** — caused by **JEV**; Culex-borne; **pigs** amplifying hosts; pigs and wild birds reservoirs; spread by mosquito bite, **not water**
- **Zika fever** — **Zika virus**; spread by mosquitoes, **blood and sexual contact**; fever, rash, joint pain
- **Yellow fever virus** — yellow fever
- **Fascioliasis** — liver fluke **Fasciola**, mainly in **ruminants**; caught from **raw water plants**; damages liver and bile ducts
- **Ascariasis** — **nematode** Ascaris lumbricoides; caught by swallowing its eggs
- **Dracunculus medinensis** — **Guinea worm disease**; humans are hosts, infected by drinking water with Cyclops carrying larvae
- **Leishmania donovani** — kala-azar; **Entamoeba histolytica** — amoebic dysentery; **Candida** — candidiasis
- **Mycoplasma pneumoniae** — **atypical pneumonia**; C. tetani — tetanus; Y. pestis — plague; M. leprae — leprosy
- **Rinderpest** — **Morbillivirus**; **foot-and-mouth disease** — **Aphthovirus**
- Virus pairs: **JEV** — JE; HBV — hepatitis B; HIV — AIDS; Nipah virus — Nipah
### Zoonoses
- **Plague** — rodents, via **rat fleas**; **rabies** — bite of infected animals; **Nipah** — fruit bats; **anthrax** — infected livestock
- **Malaria** — vector-borne but **not zoonotic** (odd one out vs rabies, anthrax, Nipah)
### Programme and AP response
- NVBDCP (now **NCVBDC**) — six diseases: malaria, JE, dengue, chikungunya, kala-azar, LF
  - targeted for **elimination**: **malaria, lymphatic filariasis, kala-azar**; dengue, chikungunya, JE only controlled
  - **Malaria and dengue** — outbreak-prone and **climate-sensitive**
- AP response — **indoor residual spraying**, **LLIN** nets, **source reduction**
- AP **agency (tribal) districts** — most of the State's **malaria and scrub typhus** burden

## SEC 5:114:3 | Zoonoses
### Animal diseases and hosts
- ★ **Bird flu** (avian influenza) — **H5N1**; mainly birds (hens, ducks); **swine flu** — **H1N1** (H5N1 does not cause swine flu)
- **Pigs** — key hosts of **swine flu** (and JE), not rabies or bird flu
- **Rabies** — from **dogs, cats, bats, foxes**; attacks the **nervous system** → throat spasms → **hydrophobia**
- **Plague** — **rat–flea** cycle; **Ebola** — from non-human primates
- **Rabies, Ebola, Nipah** — **zoonotic viral** diseases
- **SARS** (coronavirus) — via **civet cats** as intermediate hosts
- **Ranikhet (Newcastle) disease** — **chickens** and other poultry
- **Rinderpest** — **cattle and buffalo**; caused by **Morbillivirus**; declared **globally eradicated in 2011**
- **Foot-and-mouth disease** — **Aphthovirus**; cattle, goats, pigs, sheep (**cloven-hoofed** livestock)
- **Fascioliasis** — snail-borne **liver fluke** of cattle and sheep (not viral)
- **Cowpox** — **cowpox virus** (not Morbillivirus)
- One Health — **biodiversity loss** raises **zoonotic and pandemic risk**
### Human diseases and spread
- **Leprosy** — **Mycobacterium leprae**; damages **skin and peripheral nerves**
- **Measles** — highly contagious viral, **respiratory** (airborne)
- **Diphtheria and pertussis** — **respiratory route**; gonorrhoea and syphilis — sexually transmitted
- **Tuberculosis** — **bacterial**, airborne
- Most **antibiotics** — **natural or semi-synthetic** (some fully synthetic); many come from **bacteria**, not only fungi

## SEC 5:114:4 | Air pollution & climate
### Air, noise and climate
- Air pollution — mainly the **respiratory system**; also cardiovascular disease, stroke, lung cancer
- WHO (2019) — **4.2 million** premature deaths from **ambient outdoor** air pollution
- **Noise pollution** — **hearing loss, hypertension, sleep disorders and anxiety**
- **3.6 billion** people live in highly **climate-vulnerable** areas
- **DALY** — years of life lost + years lived with **disability** (disease-burden metric)
- India — life expectancy **49.7** (1973) → **70.3** (2023); **NCDs >57%** of deaths; communicable diseases persist — **double burden**
### Diseases and pathogens
- **Chikungunya** — **alphavirus**; malaria destroys **red blood cells**; dengue mainly affects **platelets**
- Plasmodium infecting humans — **all five**: P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. malariae, P. ovale, **P. knowlesi**
- **Wolbachia** — **bacterium**; **cytoplasmic incompatibility** and **pathogen blocking**; naturally **absent** in Aedes aegypti and Anopheles
- **Gambusia** and **guppy** — larvivorous fish; **Bti** — bacterial bio-larvicide
- **Gonorrhoea** — **sexually transmitted** (not droplets)
- **COVID-19** — respiratory and contact routes; attacks the **lungs**; multi-organ complications
- **Rust of wheat** — **Puccinia**; **Ustilago** — smut
### AMR and programmes
- AMR drivers — overuse, self-medication, poor sanitation, **unregulated** veterinary drug use (not regulated, supervised use)
- WHO **Bacterial Priority Pathogens List, 2024** — critical group: carbapenem-resistant **A. baumannii**, **Enterobacterales**, **rifampicin-resistant TB** (Wolbachia not on it)
- **"Antibiotic"** — coined **1942** by **Selman Waksman**; **Fleming** — **penicillin** (**1928**, Penicillium notatum)
- **Mission Indradhanush** — **December 2014**; target over **90%** full immunisation of children and pregnant women
- AP **adult BCG** drive — began **16 May 2024**; **27.10 lakh** doses; AP **first** in India

## SEC 5:114:5 | How AMR builds
### Mosquitoes and AIDS
- ★ Only **female** mosquitoes suck blood; **females have bigger wings**; males feed on flower and fruit juices
- ★ AIDS symptoms — **swollen lymph nodes**, **night sweats**, **memory loss**, **weight loss** (all four); HIV destroys the body's disease resistance
### Biofilms
- **Biofilm** — microbial community in a self-made **EPS** (extracellular polymeric substance) matrix
- EPS shields microbes and blocks antibiotics → **persistent / chronic infection**
- Forms on **medical implants, human tissues, food, food-processing surfaces**
- Food industry — **spoilage and contamination**
### Resistance
- Severity order — **MDR → XDR → TDR** (multi-, extensively, totally drug-resistant)
- Causes — antibiotic use in **livestock**, **repeated exposure**, **mutation**
- **Incomplete course** — resistant survivors multiply → AMR

- **UIP** — **1985**; **U-WIN** — portal registering **every vaccination** under UIP

### Outbreaks and microbes

## SEC 5:114:9 | Vector-borne diseases — disease → pathogen → vector → organ
### Mosquitoes and malaria
- ★ **Culex, Anopheles, Aedes** — globally distributed **blood-feeding** mosquitoes
- ★ Malaria vaccine is hard — caused by **several species of Plasmodium** (falciparum, vivax, malariae, ovale), each with different antigens
- ★ **P. vivax** — about **65%** of malaria cases in India; recurring malaria
- ★ **Malignant malaria** — **Plasmodium falciparum**
- ★ Malaria — **protozoan** Plasmodium; carrier **female Anopheles** (secondary host)
- ★ Malaria vs dengue — differ in **mosquito species**: female **Anopheles** vs female **Aedes aegypti**
### Dengue, JE and others
- ★ **Dengue** — **virus**, carried by **female Aedes** mosquito (A. aegypti); "breakbone fever"; transmitted by insect bite
- ★ Dengue — **platelets** fall rapidly → risk of internal bleeding
- ★ **Japanese encephalitis** — **virus** (JEV, a flavivirus); infects the **brain**
- ★ Keeping **pigs** away from settlements helps eradicate **JE** (pigs and wild birds reservoirs; Culex vector)
- ★ **Chikungunya** — **viral** (CHIKV, alphavirus), by **Aedes** — not bacterial
- ★ **Sleeping sickness** — unicellular **Trypanosoma** (T. b. rhodesiense, T. b. gambiense)
- ★ **Filaria** — caused by **nematodes** (thread-like roundworms)
- ★ **AIDS** spread — **sex, blood transfusion, across the placenta**; not by mosquitoes or blood-sucking insects
- ★ **Measles** — **virus**
### Organs
- ★ **Jaundice** — affects the **liver** (high blood bilirubin)
- ★ **Aflatoxin** — affects the **liver**; produced by **Aspergillus** and **Penicillium** species
- **Amoebiasis** — **large intestine**; **Entamoeba histolytica**
