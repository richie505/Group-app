# ROW 2:18 | Amendment of the Constitution - Article 368, types of majorities, procedure, limitations
## SEC 2:18:0 | Article 368 basics
- **Article 368**, in **Part XX** — Parliament's power to amend the Constitution and the procedure
- Wording: Parliament may, **in exercise of its constituent power**, amend **by way of addition, variation or repeal** any provision (subject to the basic-structure limit)
- Amendment procedure borrowed from **South Africa** (so was the method of electing Rajya Sabha members)
- **Granville Austin** — process appears "complicated" but is "merely **diverse**"; one of the most ably conceived aspects of the Constitution
- **Citizenship** is changed by simple majority under **Article 11**, not under Article 368
- Latest enacted amendment (as of 2026): **106th Amendment (2023)**; the **131st** was only a Bill that failed in the Lok Sabha (April 2026)
## SEC 2:18:1 | Procedure
### Nature
- A **synthesis of rigidity and flexibility** — neither purely rigid nor purely flexible
### Initiation and passage
- Bill may be moved by a **minister or a private member**, with **no prior permission of the President**
- Can start in **either House**; needs a **special majority in each House separately**
- **No joint sitting** (Art. 108 does not apply) for an amendment Bill — its absence is a recognised criticism of the procedure
- Ordinary Bill: simple majority; deadlock between Houses goes to a **joint sitting**
- **24th Amendment Act, 1971** — made the President's **assent obligatory** to an amendment Bill
### Role of States
- A State Legislature **cannot initiate** an amendment; it can only **ratify** certain Bills passed by Parliament
- Amendments to specified **federal provisions** need ratification by **at least one-half of the State Legislatures**
- **No time limit** is prescribed for State ratification
- President's qualifications and tenure need **no** ratification
- Creation/abolition of a Legislative Council follows **Article 169**, outside Article 368
## SEC 2:18:2 | Three routes of amendment
### Special majority
- **Special majority** = **majority of the total membership** of each House + **two-thirds of members present and voting**
- Fundamental Rights and DPSPs need only the **special majority** procedure
### Special majority + State ratification
- Needs ratification: **Seventh Schedule** / distribution of legislative powers, **election of the President**, representation of States
- **101st Amendment Act, 2016** added the **GST Council (Article 279A)** to the provisions needing State ratification
- Ratification only for a **limited group** of federal provisions, **not every amendment**
- Needs only **half the States**, not every State; by **simple majority** of members present and voting in each State Legislature; **no time limit**
- First amendment needing State ratification: **3rd Amendment (1954)**, on **Entry 33 of the Concurrent List**
### Simple majority, outside Article 368
- **Quorum in Parliament**, **MPs' salaries**, **parliamentary privileges**
- **Legislative Councils (Article 169)**
- **Citizenship**, **Fifth and Sixth Schedules**, **number of puisne judges** of the Supreme Court
- The **basic structure** cannot be amended even by the special procedure
### Related Articles
| Subject | Provision |
|---|---|
| Election of the President | **Articles 54 and 55** |
| GST Council | **Article 279A** |
| Representation of States | **Fourth Schedule** |
| Citizenship | **Article 11** |
- **Impeachment of the President** (Article 61 type) — **two-thirds of the total membership** of the House concerned
## SEC 2:18:3 | Bill types compared
- Constitutional amendment Bill: President **must give assent**; can **neither withhold nor return** it (made mandatory by the **24th Amendment, 1971**)
- **Money Bill** — President may assent or withhold, but **never return** it
- **Ordinary Bill** — can be returned **once** for reconsideration
- **Financial Bill (I)** — originates only in the **Lok Sabha**, but the **Rajya Sabha** has full power to **reject or amend** it
## SEC 2:18:4 | Can Parliament amend FRs?
### Sequence
- **Shankari Prasad (1951)** → **Golaknath (1967)** → **24th Amendment (1971)** → **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**
### Cases
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad** and **Sajjan Singh** | Amendments are **not 'law'** under Article 13(2); Sajjan Singh upheld power to amend FRs |
| **Golaknath** | Parliament **cannot amend FRs**; first limited the amending power over FRs |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | FRs and Preamble amendable, but **basic structure** cannot be altered; upheld the **24th Amendment**; first propounded the doctrine |
| **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain** | **First applied** the basic-structure doctrine |
| **Minerva Mills** | Struck down **clauses (4) and (5) of Art. 368**; **limited amending power** is itself a basic feature; struck down the clause giving DPSPs absolute priority |
- Kesavananda Bharati: **13-judge** bench, **7:6** majority; judgment delivered **24 April 1973**
### Amendments
- **24th Amendment (1971)** — amended Arts. 13 and 368; empowered Parliament to abridge/take away FRs; inserted **Article 13(4)** (an Art. 368 amendment is not 'law' for Art. 13)
- **42nd Amendment (1976)** — inserted **clauses (4) and (5)** in Art. 368 to declare amending power unlimited and bar judicial review of amendments
### Other limits
- **Article 32** — amendable by **special majority without State ratification**, though the SC's power under it is basic structure
- **Rajendra N. Shah (2021)** — **Part IXB** provisions on State co-operative societies struck down for want of **State ratification**
- Procedure is a **combination of rigidity and flexibility**; the basic-structure limit permits **judicial review** of amendments
## SEC 2:18:5 | Kinds of special majority
- Rejection by **either House** — the amendment Bill **fails** (no joint sitting)
- Changes under **Articles 2, 3 and 169** — **simple majority**, not deemed amendments under Art. 368
- **Article 4** — laws under Arts. 2 and 3 are **not** amendments under Art. 368
- **Article 2** new States; **Article 3** boundaries; **Articles 5–11** citizenship
| Type / Article | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Article 368** type | Constitutional amendment; removal of SC/HC judges, CAG, CEC, State Election Commissioners; approval of National Emergency (**Arts. 124, 217, 148, 324, 243K, 352**) |
| **Article 61** type | Impeaching the President |
| **Article 249** | Legislating on a State List subject |
| **Article 312** | Creating new **All-India Services** |
## SEC 2:18:6 | Exam corner
- Two-thirds of **528** present and voting = **352**; **298** votes in favour → Bill **fails**
- **129th Amendment Bill, 2024** — **simultaneous elections** (Lok Sabha and State Assemblies); proposes **Article 82A**; referred to a Joint Committee
- **131st Amendment Bill, 2026** — Lok Sabha maximum strength from 550 to **850** (with delimitation); failed in the Lok Sabha on **17 April 2026** (298 for, short of 352)
- Women's reservation — **106th Amendment (2023)**
## SEC 2:18:7 | Article 368 — location, source, nature and persons
- ★ **K.C. Wheare** — Indian Constitution establishes a good **balance between rigidity and flexibility**; called it **quasi-federal**
- ★ Indian Constitution is **partly rigid and partly flexible** (some provisions by simple majority, some need two-thirds under Art. 368)
## SEC 2:18:8 | Amendment procedure → step-by-step rules
- ★ **24th Amendment** — took away the President's veto over an amendment Bill by inserting "**shall give his assent**"
## SEC 2:18:9 | Amendment Bill vs Ordinary and Money Bills
- ★ Amendment can be initiated only in **Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha** — not by State Legislatures or the President
- ★ **Article 368(2)** mentions **two methods** of amendment
- ★ **Constitution Amendment Bill** — must be passed by **each House separately** by **special majority**
- ★ **Joint sitting (Article 108)** — Bill passed by a **simple majority of members present and voting**
## SEC 2:18:10 | Types of majority → requirement → used for
- ★ New State created by a **simple majority** in Parliament
- ★ Ratification needs only **half the State Legislatures**, not all
- ★ Need consent of at least half the States: **federal provisions**, **rights of the Supreme Court**, **process of constitutional amendment**
## SEC 2:18:12 | Simple-majority provisions → Articles (not amendments)
- ★ **Article 169** — creation of Legislative Councils in States
- ★ **Article 3** — only **Parliament** can increase/diminish area, alter boundaries or name of a State; "**a State may include a Union territory**" is not a provision
- ★ New State / altered boundaries — **simple majority**, ordinary legislative process; not an amendment for Art. 368
## SEC 2:18:13 | Federal provisions → State ratification (Art. 368(2))
- ★ Need State ratification: **Union List (Seventh Schedule)**, **extent of the executive power of a State**; not the conditions of the Governor's office
- ★ First amendment ratified by States under Art. 368(2): **Third Amendment, 1954** (re-enacted **Entry 33** of the Concurrent List)
- ★ Not covered by ratification: **qualification**, **conditions of office** and **tenure** of the **President**
## SEC 2:18:14 | Can Parliament amend Fundamental Rights? → cases
- ★ **Article 368** — amending power and procedure of the Constitution
- ★ Amendment may be initiated in **either House** of Parliament
- ★ **Parliament** — competent to amend Fundamental Rights
- ★ **Parliament** — power to admit or include a State into the Union
- ★ **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)** — first gave the **basic structure** doctrine; Preamble part of the Constitution and of basic structure; reversed **Golaknath**
- ★ Parliament got the right to amend FRs — **Kesavananda Bharati** case
  - Earlier **Shankari Prasad (1951)** and **Sajjan Singh (1965)** held FRs amendable; **Golaknath (1967)** reversed this
- ★ Article 368 amended by both the **24th (1971)** and **101st (2016)** Amendments

## SEC 2:19:1 | The Emergency pair
### 42nd Amendment (1976)
- Bill introduced in the Lok Sabha in **September 1976**, during the Emergency
- Known as the '**Mini-Constitution**'
- Extended term of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies from **five to six years**
- Inserted **Part XIV-A** (**tribunals**) and **Part IVA** / **Article 51A** (Fundamental Duties); froze delimitation on the **1971 Census**
- Enabled National Emergency in **a part of India**
- Added '**socialist**' and '**secular**' to the Preamble; SC holds **secularism** is part of the **basic structure** (inherent in the scheme)
- Made ministerial advice **binding** on the President; shifted **education** to the Concurrent List
### 44th Amendment (1978)
- Restored the **five-year** term of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies; restored judicial review in important respects
- '**Armed rebellion**' replaced '**internal disturbance**' as a ground for National Emergency
- **Lok Sabha** can disapprove/revoke a Proclamation of Emergency
- **Articles 20 and 21** cannot be suspended during an Emergency
- Deleted **Arts. 19(1)(f) and 31**; property became a **legal right** under **Article 300A** (Part XII)
- Restored courts' power over election disputes of **President, Vice-President, PM and Lok Sabha Speaker** (reversed the **39th Amendment** bar)
- President may **return** ministerial advice **once** for reconsideration

# ROW 2:21 | Basic Structure doctrine - evolution and content; Kesavananda Bharati and after (Indira Gandhi, Minerva Mills, Waman Rao, I.R. Coelho)
## SEC 2:21:0 | Meaning & origin
- **Dietrich Conrad** (German jurist) — argued in India in **1965** that amending power has **implied limitations**, drawing on Germany's **Basic Law**
- **Justice Mudholkar**'s dissent in **Sajjan Singh (1965)** — first judicial reference to '**basic features**'
## SEC 2:21:1 | Evolution
### Cases
| Case | Point |
|---|---|
| **Golaknath (1967)** | Challenged the **17th** Amendment (not the 24th) |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | **24 April 1973**, **13 judges**, **7:6**; propounded the doctrine, **overruled Golaknath**, upheld the 24th Amendment |
| **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)** | **First application**; struck down clause of the **39th Amendment**; **free and fair elections** a basic feature |
| **Minerva Mills** | Struck down **Arts. 368(4), 368(5)** and the **widened Article 31C** (42nd Amendment) |
| **NJAC case (2015)** | Struck down 99th Amendment and NJAC Act by **4:1**; judicial independence basic |
### Ninth Schedule
- More than **280 entries**; inclusion gives **no blanket immunity** from judicial review (Waman Rao, I.R. Coelho)
- **Waman Rao** cut-off **24 April 1973** — later entries open to **basic-structure review**
- **Article 31C** inserted by the **25th Amendment, 1971** (not the First)
### Basic feature — case
| Basic feature | Case |
|---|---|
| **Welfare state** | **Bhim Singhji** |
| Rule of law | **P. Sambamurthy** |
| Arts. 32, 136, 141, 142 | **Delhi Judicial Service Association** |
| Judicial independence | **NJAC** |
## SEC 2:21:2 | Elements → case
- **Kesavananda Bharati** — **secularism, federalism, separation of powers** among basic-structure principles
- **S.R. Bommai (1994)**, **nine-judge** bench — reaffirmed **secularism** and federalism
- **L. Chandra Kumar (1997)** — High Courts' powers under **Articles 226 and 227**
- Raising voting beyond a statutory right — linked to **democracy and free and fair elections** as basic features
## SEC 2:21:3 | Ninth Schedule story
- Law placed in the Ninth Schedule only by **Parliament through a constitutional amendment**; covers Central and State laws; created mainly for **land-reform** laws
- **76th Amendment Act, 1994** — Tamil Nadu **69%** reservation law placed in the Ninth Schedule
- **I.R. Coelho** — '**rights test**'/'**direct impact test**': post-1973 law invalid if it abrogates essence of **Articles 14, 19 and 21**
- Sequence: First Amendment (1951) → Kesavananda (1973) → **Waman Rao (1981)** → I.R. Coelho (2007)
## SEC 2:21:4 | Who made the doctrine?
### Kesavananda Bharati
- ★ Enunciated the **doctrine of basic structure** (1973, **13 judges**, **7:6**); Parliament cannot **damage or destroy** the basic structure
- ★ Supreme Court interpreted the **basic features** of the Constitution
- Struck down the part of **Article 31C** barring courts from asking whether a law gives effect to the DPSPs; **judicial review** basic
### Nature
- **Judge-made**, evolving case by case; not defined in the Constitution
- **FRs may be amended**, provided the basic structure is not damaged
- Parliament is not sovereign in the British sense — **no unlimited amending power**; written Constitution, FRs, judicial review, federal division restrict it
### Articles and doctrines
- **Article 31C** — protects laws implementing **Art. 39(b) and 39(c)** from challenge under Arts. 14 and 19
- **Article 31A** — estate acquisition, agrarian reform; **Article 31B** — Ninth Schedule laws
- SC-evolved doctrines: **eclipse, severability, pith and substance**
- Created by amendments: Art. 31C primacy, Art. 13(4), unlimited amending power
- **Prospective overruling** — first applied in **Golaknath (1967)**
## SEC 2:21:5 | Supremacy & debate
- Rests on **supremacy of the Constitution** and **judicial review**
- Criticised as **judicial rewriting** — no textual definition; developed case by case by **unelected judges**
- **43rd Amendment Act, 1977** — removed the 42nd's bar on High Courts reviewing central laws
- 129th Amendment Bill, 2024: former CJI **B.R. Gavai** told the JPC it **did not violate** basic structure; **U.U. Lalit** and **Sanjiv Khanna** raised doubts
- **Judicial review** — **inherent** power of constitutional courts; tests laws and executive action
- Basic structure **cannot be destroyed** even under Article 368
## SEC 2:21:6 | Exam corner
- Parliament **may amend** any part, but **cannot damage or destroy** its core features
- **Preamble** — part of the Constitution, **amendable**, subject to the basic structure
- **Secularism** was a basic feature even **before** the 42nd Amendment added 'secular'
## SEC 2:21:8 | Basic structure — meaning, origin, persons and FIRSTs — Origin & nature
- ★ Doctrine propounded in **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**; approved in **Minerva Mills (1980)**
## SEC 2:21:9 | Cases → year → contribution (evolution, 1951 to 2015)
- ★ Concept of '**basic structure**' propounded in **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — beware options that omit it ("**None of the above**")
## SEC 2:21:12 | Elements declared basic structure → case
- ★ Doctrine enunciated in the **Kesavananda Bharati** case, **1973**
| Basic feature | Case |
|---|---|
| **Principle of equality** | **Indra Sawhney (1992)** |
| Free and fair elections | **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain** (and **Kuldip Nayar**) |
| HC powers under Arts. 226–227 | **L. Chandra Kumar (1997)** |
## SEC 2:21:14 | Scope, supremacy debate and criticism
- ★ Source of the basic structure theory — **judicial interpretation**
- ★ Implies certain features are **so essential** that they **cannot be abrogated** (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973; approved in Minerva Mills)
## SEC 2:21:15 | Universal angles
- ★ **I.R. Coelho** case — **Ninth Schedule** ("9th schedule case"); judiciary can review Ninth Schedule laws against the basic structure

# ROW 2:24 | Structure of the Indian government - separation of powers and checks and balances
## SEC 2:24:0 | Core ideas
- ★ Constitutional government = **limited government** — powers defined and restricted by the constitution
- ★ Constitutional government — a government **limited by the terms of the Constitution**
  - Constitution defines, restrains and checks power to prevent **executive tyranny**
- ★ Best safeguard of liberty in a liberal democracy — **separation of powers**
- **Montesquieu** — **division of powers** the most effective protection of liberty
- A **majoritarian** position **cannot override** constitutionalism
- **PIL** widens access to justice for the disadvantaged; **not a precondition** of the rule of law, which existed before PIL via **Arts. 14, 32, 226**

# ROW 2:25 | President of India - election, qualifications, term, impeachment; executive, legislative, financial, judicial powers
## SEC 2:25:0 | Electoral college (Arts. 54, 55)
- Members of **State Legislative Councils** vote in **neither** the Presidential nor the Vice-Presidential election
## SEC 2:25:1 | Election method
### Presidential election
- **Indirect** election; **proportional representation by single transferable vote** — borrowed from **Ireland**
- **Nominated MPs and nominated MLAs** cannot vote; **MLCs** cannot vote
- Nomination needs **50 proposers and 50 seconders**
- MLA vote value = (State population / elected MLAs) × **1/1000** — varies with population
- Population base: **1971 Census**, frozen by the **84th Amendment** till the first Census after 2026
- MP vote value ensures **parity between Union and States**; MLA values ensure **uniformity among States**
- **Article 71** disputes — **Supreme Court only**; acts done before the election is declared void remain valid; vacancy in the electoral college no ground for challenge
### Vice-Presidential election
- **All MPs**, elected and **nominated**; PR by STV, **secret ballot**; State Assemblies do not take part
## SEC 2:25:2 | Qualifications, term, oath
- **Article 58** — Indian citizen, **35 years**, qualified for the **Lok Sabha**, no office of profit
- Vice-President must be qualified for the **Rajya Sabha** (citizen, 35, no office of profit)
- **Article 56** — **five-year** term; **Article 57** re-election; **Article 61** impeachment
- **Article 65** — Vice-President **acts as President**; **72** pardon; **74** aid and advice; **76** Attorney General
- Resignation addressed to the **Vice-President**, who informs the Speaker
- **Article 60** — oath administered by the **CJI**
- Emoluments **fixed by Parliament by law**, listed in the **Second Schedule**; cannot be reduced during the term
- **11th Amendment (1961)** — ended the **joint-meeting** method for electing the Vice-President
## SEC 2:25:3 | Impeachment (Art. 61)
### Impeachment
- ★ Ground: **violation of the Constitution**
- **Article 61** — charge may start in **either House**; resolution signed by **one-fourth** of members after **14 days' notice**
- Both Houses must pass by **two-thirds of total membership** (simple majority not enough)
- Elected and nominated MPs take part; **elected MLAs** (States, Delhi, Puducherry) do **not**
- No President impeached so far
### Term and office
- **No limit** on terms; may be re-elected
- Emoluments **cannot be diminished** during the term; rent-free residence; cannot be a member of any legislature
- Person elected to a vacancy gets a **full five-year term**
- While the VP acts as President, the **Deputy Chairman** presides over the Rajya Sabha
- **Article 54** — electoral college; **52** creates the office; **60** oath (not re-election, which is **57**); **71** election disputes
### Powers and appointments
- **Article 240** — regulations for A&N, Lakshadweep, DNH-DD, and **Puducherry** when its Assembly is **suspended or dissolved**
- **Article 148** CAG; **76** Attorney General; **155** Governors; **324** Election Commission
- CJI, judges, AG, Governors appointed formally on the **PM's** recommendation (Article 74)
- **Joint sitting** — summoned by the **President**, presided by the **Lok Sabha Speaker**
- Nominates **twelve** members to the Rajya Sabha; President part of Parliament (**Article 79**)
- Prior recommendation needed for a **Money Bill** and a Bill under **Article 3**
- **Appointing Ambassadors** — on ministerial advice, not situational discretion
### Pardon and veto
- Only the **President** can pardon a **court-martial** sentence, not the Governor
- **Article 72** covers court-martial, Union-law offences and **all death sentences**, not State-law sentences in every case
- **Suspensive veto** — returns an Ordinary Bill once; **not** for **Money Bills**
### Firsts
- **M. Hidayatullah** — only CJI to act as President (**1969**)
- **V.V. Giri** — won on **second-preference** votes
- **S. Radhakrishnan** — first President from **South India**
- **N. Sanjiva Reddy** (1977–82, from **AP**) — only President **elected unopposed**; earlier Lok Sabha Speaker and AP's first CM

- ★ Minimum proposers for a presidential candidate (as keyed in the PYQ) — **twenty electors**; **Article 55** — uniformity of representation among States
