# ROW 4:11 | Weathering, mass wasting and soil formation; denudation and the cycle of erosion
## SEC 4:11:0 | Denudation
- Denudation sequence: **weathering** (first stage) → mass wasting / erosion → transportation → deposition

## SEC 4:11:1 | Weathering processes
| Process | Nature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydrolysis** | chemical | clay from **feldspar**; **muscovite → vermiculite** (potassium replaced by hydronium) |
| **Oxidation** | chemical | **rusting** of iron-bearing minerals; may give red or yellow soils |
| **Carbonation** | CO2 in rainwater forms **carbonic acid** | dissolves **limestone**; stores carbon as **bicarbonate** |
| **Hydration** | **absorption of water** by minerals, which **swell** | **anhydrite → gypsum** |

## SEC 4:11:2 | Weathering notes
- Weathering is fastest in **hot and wet** climates
- **Exfoliation domes** — onion-like peeling of **granite** (pressure or temperature effects)

## SEC 4:11:3 | Mass wasting
- **Slump** — rotational slip along a **curved surface** in weak, saturated soil
- **Creep** — **very slow** downslope movement from repeated wetting-drying or heating-cooling
- **Rockfall** — free fall of rocks from cliffs
- **Slide** — coherent mass slipping along a surface
- **Debris flow** — fast water-soil-rock mix

## SEC 4:11:4 | Landslide conditions
- India: principal trigger of mass wasting is **rainfall** (southwest monsoon), next **earthquakes**
- Most vulnerable regions: **Himalaya and North-East**

## SEC 4:11:5 | Cycle of erosion
- **Davis's cycle** ends in a near-flat **peneplain** with residual **monadnocks**; emphasised structure, process and time
- **Walther Penck** — stressed **uplift and erosion acting simultaneously**
- **Peninsular plateau** of India — old peneplain, **rejuvenated in places**
- **Inselbergs** — residual island-hills of **arid and semi-arid** regions (wind and sheet-wash erosion)

## SEC 4:11:6 | Zonal soils
- ★ **Loam** — contains **all types of particles** (about 40% sand, 18% clay, 42% silt)
- **Podzol** — cold **taiga** / boreal conifer forests; thin, **acidic**, leached, humus-poor
- **Terra rossa** — **Mediterranean** regions over **limestone**
- Chernozem — temperate grasslands; laterite — hot wet tropics; oxisols — stable equatorial surfaces

## SEC 4:11:7 | Soil formation
### Particles
| Particle | Diameter |
|---|---|
| ★ **Clay** | **less than 0.002 mm** |
| Silt | 0.002–0.05 mm |
| Fine sand | 0.05–2 mm |
- Water-holding capacity: **clay** highest > silt > sand (drains readily)
### Factors
- Five classical factors: parent material, climate, relief/topography, organisms, time — **latitude** is not one
- **Active** factors: **climate** and biological activity; **passive**: parent material and topography
- AP: **bedrock composition does influence** soil formation, along with climate and altitude
### Types and shares
- In-situ (residual) soils = **sedentary soils** (APPSC 2018)
- Transported **alluvial** soil (mainly rivers, North India) covers about **45.6%** of India
- AP cultivated land: **red soil** about **60%**; black soil about one-fourth; laterite about 1%
- Most powerful agent of soil erosion: **water** (sheet, rill, gully); wind dominates only in areas like the Thar

## SEC 4:11:8 | Exam corner
- Rainforest soils poor in nutrients: heavy rainfall **leaches** nutrients and dead matter **decomposes rapidly**

## SEC 4:11:12 | Soil-forming factors — active vs passive; AP soil formation
- ★ **Active** factors: **climate** and **biological activity** (drive weathering and organic processes); parent material, topography, time are the others
- ★ Rainwater contains **carbon dioxide** in solution and **atmospheric oxygen** — both aid chemical weathering
- Higher temperature and greater rainfall increase the rate of chemical weathering

## SEC 4:11:13 | Soil texture data and soils → climate region; in-situ vs transported
- ★ **Clay** holds **maximum water** (smaller particles)
- ★ Clay pore spaces are **small**, not large

# ROW 4:12 | Major landforms - fluvial landforms and river profiles
## SEC 4:12:0 | River course → work → features
### River courses
- ★ **Peneplain** — formed by **rivers**; near-featureless gentle plain reduced almost to base level
- Dominant work: upper course **vertical erosion** → middle **lateral erosion and transport** → lower **deposition**
- Middle course: lateral erosion, transport, wider valleys, **meanders** — vertical erosion not the sole work
- **Canyon** — **upper course** feature (strong vertical erosion, youthful stage)
- **V-shaped valley** — rills → gullies → valleys, mainly by **vertical erosion**
### Agents and landforms
| Landform | Formed by |
|---|---|
| Mountains | **folding, faulting, volcanism, erosion** |
| Plains | deposition and marine processes |
| Valleys | **river, glacier, tectonic faulting, karst** solution |
| Cliffs, caves, arches, stacks, beaches | **waves, currents and tides** |
| Arêtes, horns, U-shaped and hanging valleys, moraines | **glacial** erosion and deposition |
| Dunes, yardangs, mushroom rocks, playas, loess | **wind** erosion and deposition (desert) |
| Sinkholes, dolines, caves, stalactites, stalagmites, blind valleys | **karst** |

## SEC 4:12:1 | Profile terms
- **Stream capture** (river piracy) — headward erosion breaches a neighbouring divide and diverts its upper waters
- **Graded profile** — smooth, **concave** long profile; erosion and deposition broadly balanced
- **Base level** — lowest level a river can erode to; ultimate base level = **sea level**
- **Estuary** — partly enclosed river mouth with **free connection to the sea**; an ecotone
- Young fold mountains: **Alps, Andes, Rockies**; old: Appalachians, Urals, Pennines, Aravallis

## SEC 4:12:2 | Gorge vs canyon
- **Gorge** — deep, narrow, steep-sided river valley formed by river erosion
- **Canyon** — a much deeper gorge; a canyon is a **variant of a gorge** (not the reverse)
- ★ **Grand Canyon** — a gorge carved by the **Colorado** in **Arizona**, USA; about 446 km long, over 1.8 km deep
- **Dihang gorge** (Brahmaputra entry to India) — **Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon** near **Namcha Barwa**

## SEC 4:12:3 | Erosional landforms
- **Plunge pool** — large pothole below a waterfall; potholes drilled by eddy-whirled fragments
- **Incised / entrenched meanders** — **uplift rejuvenates** river; meanders cut deep into hard rock
- **Badland** topography — dense gully network (rills merge and deepen into gullies)
- **Unpaired terraces** — on **one bank** only, often slow uplift; **paired** terraces at same height on both banks

## SEC 4:12:4 | Depositional landforms
- **Braided channel** — sand deposits split the channel into many threads with **riverine islands and bars**
- Meander: **outer** bend concave, **erosional** (cliff); **inner slip-off** side convex, **depositional**

## SEC 4:12:5 | Delta vs estuary
- ★ **Tapti** forms an **estuary** (transition zone between river and ocean)
- **Strong tides and currents** remove sediment — they do **not** favour delta formation
- **Sundarbans** — **Ganga-Brahmaputra delta**; world's largest **mangrove** forest

## SEC 4:12:6 | Delta types
- **Godavari** forms a **delta**; **Narmada** and **Tapi** have estuarine mouths

## SEC 4:12:7 | Drainage patterns
| Pattern | Control | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dendritic** | uniform rock; **tree-like** | northern plains |
| **Trellis** | **folded** rocks; tributaries join at right angles | Appalachians |
| **Centripetal** | streams to a central depression | **Loktak Lake**, Manipur |
| **Rectangular** | **joints and faults**; right-angle bends | joint-controlled plateaus |

## SEC 4:12:8 | AP coast
- Minor deltas: **Vamsadhara** — **Srikakulam**; **Penna** — near **Nellore**
- **Kolleru** — freshwater lake between **Godavari and Krishna** deltas
- **Pulicat** — **saltwater lagoon** on the AP–Tamil Nadu border

## SEC 4:12:9 | Exam corner
- India's classic badlands: **Chambal** ravines; also the **Chos** of Hoshiarpur (Punjab)
- Himalayan river depositional features: **braided channels, alluvial fans, natural levees** (terraces excluded — vertical down-cutting)
- West-flowing Deccan rivers form **estuaries**: **steep gradients**, hard-rock and **short** courses
- Sinking of deltas: **groundwater extraction** and reduced **silt supply**

## SEC 4:12:10 | Landform types at a glance → formation and examples
- ★ Alluvial soils of AP delta regions — formed by **river deposition**

## SEC 4:12:13 | Gorges and canyons; places → erosional landform
- ★ Gorges and canyons are **V-shaped**, deep, narrow, steep-sided valleys; gorges are **not** extended forms of canyons
- ★ Badland topography: **Chambal basin**
- ★ **Gandikota** canyon — **Pennar** River, AP; "Grand Canyon of India"
- ★ **Grand Canyon** (USA) — **Colorado** River, Arizona

## SEC 4:12:15 | Delta vs estuary; delta types → river; largest delta
- ★ World's greatest delta — **Ganga-Brahmaputra** (Ganges / Sunderban Delta; with Meghna)
- ★ About **two-thirds** of the Sunderban Delta in **Bangladesh**, rest in India

## SEC 4:12:16 | Drainage patterns → rock and place
- ★ **Dendritic** — rivers of **South India**; also Ganga plains and **Assam Valley** (wide plains, gentle slopes, heavy rain, many tributaries)
- ★ Dendritic also: Sindhu system, Mahanadi, Godavari

# ROW 4:13 | Major landforms - glacial, arid/aeolian, karst and coastal landforms
## SEC 4:13:0 | Agent → landforms
### Running water
- **Canyon** — step-like sides, wider at top; in **horizontally bedded sedimentary** rocks; gorges in hard rocks
- Old (lower) course: **oxbow lakes**, natural levees; youth: waterfalls, potholes, rapids
- Running-water group: **delta, V-valley, gorge, canyon**; **cirque** is glacial
- Running water also: deltas, meanders, **alluvial fans**; peneplain — prolonged fluvial denudation
- Badlands / ravines: **Chambal Valley**; **Chos** — Hoshiarpur, Punjab
### Other agents
- **Esker** — **glacial** depositional feature; yardang, mushroom rock — aeolian
- **Sand dune** — accumulation of sand built by **wind deposition**
- **Karst** — groundwater dissolving **limestone or dolomite**
- **Blind valley** — ends abruptly where stream sinks into limestone
- **Belum Caves** — **black limestone** with stalactites and stalagmites
- Main agent of coastal erosion: **waves**
- **Groynes** — built across longshore drift to trap sand; hard defences like seawalls, breakwaters

## SEC 4:13:1 | Glacial landforms
| Landform | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Arête** | knife-edged ridge |
| Cirque | bowl-shaped hollow |
| Horn | sharp pyramidal peak |
| Moraine | accumulated unsorted glacial debris |
| **Esker** | winding ridge of **stratified** sediment from a **sub-glacial tunnel** |
| **Drumlins** | oval egg-shaped till hills; field gives **"basket of eggs"** topography |
| **U-shaped valley** | glacier **deepens and widens** a river valley |
| **Hanging valley** | tributary valley left high above the main valley; joins by a **waterfall** |
| **Fjord** | glacial U-valley **drowned by the sea**; Norway, Greenland, New Zealand |
- Horns, eskers, U-valleys, moraines — **glacial** activity
- **Blind valley** — a **karst** feature (not tectonic)

## SEC 4:13:2 | Desert landforms
- **Mushroom rock** — wind-borne sand **abrades the base** more than the top
- **Yardang** — streamlined ridge **parallel to wind**; **zeugen** — tabular ridge, hard cap over soft rock
- **Seifs** — long narrow longitudinal dunes where sand is **scarce**; **barchans** — crescent, horns downwind
- **Playa** — **evaporation** of a temporary desert lake; deflation hollow — wind removal; **loess** — fine silt
- **Uluru** — an **inselberg** (isolated residual hill)
- Peninsular Plateau — **rejuvenated peneplain**
- **Beach nourishment** — **soft** coastal defence; seawalls, groynes, offshore breakwaters are hard

## SEC 4:13:3 | Karst depressions
- Ascending scale: **sinkhole → doline → uvala → polje**
- **Polje** — large enclosed karst depression, often cave collapse; **uvala** — coalesced dolines; swallow hole — where a stream sinks
- **Kethavaram** cave paintings — **Kurnool** district; **deer** most painted; a half-painted deer also found

## SEC 4:13:4 | Karst & caves
### Speleothems
- **Stalactites** hang from the roof; **stalagmites** rise from the floor; meeting forms **pillars / columns**
### Caves
| Cave | Place | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Krem Liat Prah** | Meghalaya | **India's longest** natural cave |
| **Mammoth Cave** | USA | **world's longest** |
| **Belum** | Nandyal | **second-longest** in India; karst |
| **Borra** | **ASR** district, Ananthagiri hills near Araku | limestone karst; **Gosthani** River passes through; Mesolithic rock paintings |
| **Undavalli** | Guntur area | rock-cut temples, **Vishnukundins**; reclining **Anantasayana Vishnu**; not karst |
| **Bojjannakonda** | Sankaram, **Anakapalli** | **Buddhist** rock-cut caves and stupas; not karst |
| Arwah | Meghalaya | |
- AP karst caves: **Belum and Borra**; Undavalli, Bojjannakonda, Erravaram are not karst
- **Devprayag** — confluence of **Bhagirathi and Alaknanda**; the **Ganga** begins

## SEC 4:13:5 | Coastal erosion sequence
- Coastal erosion processes: hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution — **saltation** is wind transport
- Sequence: cliff and wave-cut platform → sea cave → arch → stack → **stump**
- **Sea stack** — **arch collapses**; arch — cave cuts through a headland; sea cave — erosion of weak zones
- Reduce beach sediment supply: **dams**, harbours, beach sand mining, offshore dredging

## SEC 4:13:6 | Coastal deposition
- **Spit** — sand ridge joined to land at **one end**; bar — may cross a bay mouth
- **Tombolo** — joins an **island to the mainland**
- **Lagoon** — shallow water cut off by a bar, reef or sandbar
- **Oxbow lake** — a **meander is cut off**
- **Narmada and Tapi** form estuaries, not deltas
- East coast — soft, low-lying, alluvial; rocky cliffs **not** dominant
- AP severe coastal erosion: **Uppada** (Kakinada) and the **Krishna coast**
- **Pulicat** — **saltwater** lake; **Kolleru** — freshwater

## SEC 4:13:7 | Plain terms & coasts
- **Bhabar** — coarse gravel and pebble belt where Himalayan rivers enter the plains
- **Terai** — marshy, forested belt **south of the Bhabar**; streams reappear
- **Bhangar** — older alluvium above flood level, with **kankar**; **khadar** — new floodplain alluvium, no kankar
- **Doab** — land **between two rivers**; bet land — floodplain; chos — Hoshiarpur ravines; **karewas** — lacustrine deposits of Kashmir
- **Ria** coast — ranges at **right angles** to shore; **Dalmatian** — ranges **parallel**

## SEC 4:13:8 | Exam corner
- **Bird-foot delta** — sediment **lighter than** sea water, carried far; e.g. **Mississippi**
- Arcuate: Nile, Sundarbans; cuspate: Tiber
- **Billa Surgam** — **Kurnool/Nandyal** (not Anantapuramu); Palaeolithic cave site with animal skeletal remains
- **Hanging glaciers** — stranded on steep slopes as the main glacier **thins**; collapse can trigger ice-rock avalanches, LLOFs, GLOFs
- **Erra Matti Dibbalu** — late-Quaternary red dunes, **Visakhapatnam**; UNESCO Tentative List **September 2025**

## SEC 4:13:9 | Agents and landforms; weathering and mass wasting types
- **Carbonation** — chemical weathering; exfoliation, frost wedging, block disintegration are physical
- **Oxidation** — chemical weathering, not mass wasting; creep, landslide, rockfall are mass wasting
- AP: **granite and gneiss** weather into **red soil**; **basalt** into black soil

## SEC 4:13:11 | Fluvial landforms → process and river stage; delta type → condition
- ★ **Bird's foot** delta — **Mississippi** (Gulf of Mexico); **Nile** — arcuate

## SEC 4:13:12 | River-plain terms → meaning and region; plain types
- ★ **Ganga Plain** — described as a **geosyncline** (trough of subsidence filling with sediment)
- ★ **Bhabar** — flat plains along the sub-Himalaya; **8–16 km** wide, parallel to Shiwalik foothills, pebbles, porous beds
- ★ **Imphal Basin** — **lacustrine plain** (former lake); also **Kashmir valley**

## SEC 4:13:13 | Glacial landforms → formation and meaning
- ★ **Hanging valley** — **glacial** erosion; tributary glacier valley left higher than main valley

## SEC 4:13:15 | Karst landforms → meaning; speleothems; longest caves
- ★ **Stalactites and stalagmites** — **groundwater** dissolving and re-depositing calcium carbonate in limestone caves
- ★ **Patland** (central India) — **lava-capped mesas**
- ★ **Blind valley** — **karst** region; deep, narrow, flat-bottomed, abrupt end (steephead valley)
- ★ **Sinkhole** (doline) — most common karst landform; closed depression

## SEC 4:13:16 | Caves → location → karst or not
- ★ **Arwah** caves — limestone formations and fossils; near **Cherrapunji**, **Meghalaya**

## SEC 4:13:18 | Coastal erosion — landforms, agents, processes, defences
- ★ Maximum coastal erosion: **waves** (also tides, currents, tsunamis, human activity)

# ROW 4:14 | Atmosphere - structure (layers) and composition
## SEC 4:14:0 | Composition (% by volume)
### Dry-air gases
| Gas | Share | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen** | about **78.08%** | **permanent** gas |
| Oxygen | **20.95%** (about 21%) | negligible by about **120 km** |
| **Argon** | **0.93%** | **third** most abundant |
| Carbon dioxide | about **0.04%** | reaches only about 90 km |
- Descending order: **nitrogen > oxygen > argon > carbon dioxide** (APPSC-G1 2023)
- Variable gases: water vapour, carbon dioxide, ozone
- Trace gases: **neon, helium, xenon, hydrogen, krypton**
- **Hydrogen** (light) extends higher than nitrogen and oxygen
- Heterosphere, lower to higher by molecular weight: **nitrogen → atomic oxygen → helium → hydrogen**
- Early atmosphere — **reducing**, little or no free oxygen
### Water vapour and dust
- Water vapour up to **4%** in warm, wet tropics; below 1% in deserts and polar air; decreases with altitude
- Water vapour — variable; absorbs insolation and terrestrial heat (**blanket** effect); influences air **stability**
- Dust — concentrated in lower layers; more in **subtropical and temperate** belts (dry winds)
### Ozone and greenhouse gases
- **Ozone** absorbs harmful **UV**; CO2 — photosynthesis and greenhouse effect; oxygen — respiration, combustion
- **Chapman cycle**: UV splits O2 → O atoms + O2 form **O3** → O3 absorbs UV and dissociates
- Excess UV from ozone depletion → **cataract**, skin cancer, immune suppression, lower crop yield
- Greenhouse effect: atmosphere **transparent to incoming** solar, **opaque to outgoing** terrestrial radiation
- Greenhouse gases: **methane, nitrous oxide**; not hydrogen, krypton, neon, helium, argon

## SEC 4:14:1 | Layers (top first)
- Layers divided by **variation of temperature with height**; composition gives homosphere / heterosphere
- Temperature trend upward: troposphere **falls**, stratosphere **rises**, mesosphere **falls**, thermosphere **rises**
| Layer | Height | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Exosphere** | about **600–10,000 km** | outermost; merges into space |
| **Thermosphere** | | **auroras and airglow**; contains the **ionosphere** (reflects **radio waves**, long-distance communication) |
| **Mesosphere** | **50–80 km** | **coldest** (about **−100°C** near 80 km); temperature falls with height; **meteors burn up** |
| **Stratosphere** | up to **50 km** | temperature rises (**ozone absorbs UV**, inversion); little weather, suits **jet aircraft** |
| **Troposphere** | about **8 km poles**, **18 km equator** | lowest; weather; **life** exists here |
- **Mesopause** about **80 km**; **stratopause** about **50 km**; **tropopause** 8–18 km
- **Aurora Australis** — **Southern** Hemisphere; Aurora Borealis — northern

## SEC 4:14:2 | Troposphere & tropopause
- Troposphere average height about **13 km** (8 km poles, 18 km equator)
- Thicker at equator due to **strong convection** lifting air higher
- Normal lapse rate **6.5°C per km** (about 1°C per 165 m)
- About **99%** of atmosphere's mass within **32 km** of the surface
- **Tropopause** — boundary of troposphere and stratosphere; about **−80°C over the equator**, **−45°C over the poles**; caps weather and vertical mixing
- **Homosphere** — gases evenly mixed; **heterosphere** — gases layered by **molecular weight**
- **Stratospheric** ozone shields UV; **ground-level** ozone not necessarily beneficial
- **Kigali Amendment** — adopted **2016**, in force **2019**; **80–85%** HFC cuts

## SEC 4:14:3 | Ionosphere layers
- Ionosphere **expands by day**, **contracts at night** (solar radiation)
- **F layer** — main reflector of **short-wave** radio
- D layer — absorbs medium and long waves; E layer — reflects medium-frequency waves

## SEC 4:14:4 | Ozone layer
- ★ Ozone layer primarily in the **stratosphere** (lower part, about 15–30 km; stratosphere about 10–50 km)
- About **90%** of ozone in the stratosphere; densest about **15–35 km**
- Absorbs harmful **UV-B and UV-C**; shield for life
- Column ozone measured in **Dobson Units** (DU)
- Ground-level ozone — **pollutant** from **NOx and VOCs** in sunlight
- Stratosphere warms with height because **ozone converts UV to heat**
| UV band | Wavelength |
|---|---|
| UV-A | 315–400 nm |
| **UV-B** | **280–315 nm** |
| UV-C | 100–280 nm (shortest, most harmful) |

## SEC 4:14:5 | Depletion
- ★ Primary cause of stratospheric ozone depletion: **halocarbons** (CFCs, HCFCs) releasing **chlorine and bromine**
- **HCFCs do deplete** ozone, as do CFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, methyl chloroform, methyl bromide
- Uses: **CFCs** — refrigeration, ACs, **aerosols**, foam blowing; **halons** — fire extinguishers; **methyl bromide** — fumigant
- CFCs — deplete ozone and potent greenhouse gases; **HFCs** — spare ozone but high global-warming potential
- **Antarctic ozone hole** — **August to November** (southern spring), aided by polar stratospheric clouds
- Ozone hole came into focus in **1985**
- **2025** ozone hole peaked at just over **21 million sq km**, closed early; 2006 record **29 million sq km**

## SEC 4:14:6 | Ozone treaties
- **Montreal Protocol** — **1987**; legally binding; phases out nearly **100** ozone-depleting substances
- **Vienna Convention** — **1985**; framework for research, monitoring, cooperation
- India — **first developing country** to phase out **HCFC-141b** in rigid foam: **44%** cut vs 35% target by **January 2020**

## SEC 4:14:7 | Exam corner
- WMO Greenhouse Gas Bulletin 2025: CO2 in 2024 **423.9 ppm** (up **3.5 ppm**, largest rise since 1957; about 152% of pre-industrial)
- Temperature-inversion (reversal) layers: **stratosphere and thermosphere**
- **Mission SANA** — India's first indigenous stratospheric super-pressure balloon; launched from **Vijayawada**

## SEC 4:14:8 | Composition of the atmosphere — gas → share, type, role
- ★ Descending: **nitrogen (78%) > oxygen (21%) > argon (0.93%) > CO2 (0.04%)**
- ★ Maximum gas: **nitrogen** (78.084%); with oxygen (20.947%) makes 99%
- ★ Permanent gases: **nitrogen**, oxygen, argon; CO2, ozone, neon vary
- ★ **Xenon** — "**Stranger Gas**" (Greek *xenos*); discovered by Ramsay and Travers, **1898**
- ★ CO2 warms the atmosphere by absorbing **infrared** (terrestrial) radiation
- ★ Optimum CO2 for natural balance: **0.03%**; confined to the troposphere
- ★ Ozone depletion gas: **chlorofluorocarbon** (CFC)
- ★ Weather balloons filled with **helium** (inert)

## SEC 4:14:9 | Vertical distribution, variable constituents and evolution of the atmosphere
- ★ Near the surface mainly **nitrogen and oxygen**
- ★ **Infrared** waves largely absorbed by **water vapour** concentrated in the lower atmosphere; atmosphere transparent to short-wave solar radiation
- ★ Dust more in **subtropical and temperate** areas than equatorial and polar; these areas do **not** have less dry winds
- ★ Ascending order: **troposphere → stratosphere → mesosphere → ionosphere**
- Dust particles act as **hygroscopic nuclei** for condensation (cloud formation)

## SEC 4:14:11 | Layers of the atmosphere at a glance → height, temperature trend
- ★ Upward sequence: **troposphere, stratosphere (15–50 km), mesosphere (50–80 km), ionosphere (80–640 km)**, exosphere
- ★ **Ionosphere** — reflects / deflects **radio waves**; telecommunication (ions); cosmic rays and auroras observed
- ★ **Homosphere** — up to about **90 km**, uniform composition; **ionosphere not included**
- ★ Troposphere average height **13 km**
- ★ **Troposphere** — all weather and climate changes; temperature falls **6.5°C per km**; key for biological activity

## SEC 4:14:12 | Mesosphere, exosphere; temperature trend
- ★ Communication (geostationary) satellites about **36,000 km** — in the **exosphere** (above 640 km)
- ★ Wireless signals reflected to Earth by the **ionosphere**

## SEC 4:14:13 | Stratosphere, aurora; phenomena → layer
### Ozone layer
- ★ Ozone layer in the **stratosphere** (maximum concentration); mainly **15–35 km**, max height 55 km
- ★ Ozone layer refers to the atmosphere about **15–20 km** above the surface
- ★ Absorbs **ultraviolet**; protects life on Earth from UV
- ★ Ozone layer shields from UV **and** depletion is linked to **CFCs** — both correct
- ★ Maximum damage to ozone: **chlorofluorocarbons**
### Layers and phenomena
- ★ Troposphere — weather; stratosphere — ozone; ionosphere — radio waves and **aurorae** (not mesosphere)
- ★ **Aurora Borealis** — **ionosphere**; northern; Aurora Australis — southern; charged solar particles
- ★ Stratosphere ideal for jets — **no clouds** and weather (very dry)
- ★ Increasing distance: **troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere**
- ★ **Agra and Darjeeling** — same latitude; Darjeeling colder in January due to **altitude** (1°C per 165 m lapse rate)
- ★ **Rain** cleans a polluted environment (washes out particles)

## SEC 4:14:14 | Troposphere — thickness, lapse rate, weather
- ★ Troposphere thickest over the **equator** (about 16–18 km), about **8 km** at poles — due to **high insolation and strong convection**
- ★ Lowest layer: **troposphere**; average height **13 km**
- ★ Most weather activity: **troposphere**

## SEC 4:14:16 | Thermosphere and ionosphere — layer → height → scientist name; radio communication
- ★ Ozone layer useful — prevents the Sun's **ultraviolet** rays reaching Earth

## SEC 4:14:20 | Universal angles

# ROW 4:15 | Insolation and heat budget; horizontal and vertical distribution of temperature, inversion
## SEC 4:15:0 | Heating processes
- **Insolation** — direct heating by incoming **short-wave** solar radiation
- Atmosphere heated mainly **from below** by outgoing **long-wave terrestrial radiation**
- **Advection** — **horizontal** heat transfer by wind (e.g. the **Loo**); **convection** — vertical, by rising warm air
- Temperature falls with height: ground heats air from below; higher air thinner, less water vapour and dust
- **Clear skies** — greatest night-time terrestrial cooling (and more daytime heating); clouds cut both
- Himalayas cool despite low latitude — **altitude** (about 6.5°C per km)
- **Cold ocean currents lower** coastal temperatures, favour west-coast deserts; warm currents raise them

## SEC 4:15:1 | Insolation
- ★ **Albedo** — **reflecting power**; **fresh snow** / frozen ice highest; forests, oceans low
- Albedo descending: **fresh snow > sand > grass > forest > water**
- Insolation affected by angle of rays, day length, atmospheric transparency, **slope and aspect**
- **Aphelion** — around **4 July**, about **152 million km**
- **Low, thick clouds** reflect sunlight → cooling; high, thin clouds trap outgoing heat
- Arctic warming **3–4 times** faster than global average (Apr 2025; at least 2°C); **ice-albedo feedback**
- **Heat dome** — stalled **high-pressure** system under a slowed, meandering **jet stream**

## SEC 4:15:2 | Heat budget (of 100 units)
| Of 100 units | Units |
|---|---|
| Reflected to space | **35** (clouds **27**, scattering 6, snow and ice 2) |
| Absorbed by atmosphere | **14** |
| Absorbed by Earth's surface | **51** |
- Albedo — share of incoming radiation **reflected** by a surface
- Melting ice exposes **darker ocean** (lower albedo) → absorbs more heat; positive ice-albedo feedback

## SEC 4:15:3 | Greenhouse gases
- Share of greenhouse effect: **CO2 about 60%**, methane 20%, CFCs 15%, N2O 5%
- CO2 sources: fossil fuels, deforestation, cement
- **Water vapour** — most abundant greenhouse gas; important climate **feedback**
- 100-year GWP: CO2 **1**; methane **28–36**; nitrous oxide **265–298**

## SEC 4:15:4 | Distribution & range
- Same latitude: insolation higher over **continents** than oceans; declines poleward
- Heat **surplus** in the **tropics**, deficit at poles; winds and currents carry heat poleward
- Equator gets **direct** rays; poles oblique rays
- January NH isotherms bend **equatorward over land**, **poleward over oceans**
- India winter **20°C isotherm**: Tapi mouth → **Mahanadi delta**; temperature rises north to south
- **Thermal equator** — line of highest mean temperature; mostly **north** of the Equator; shifts with the Sun
- Highest winter diurnal range: **north-western India** (about 14–18°C)
- Normal lapse rate **6.5°C per km**
- **Urban heat island** — **concrete and asphalt** store heat
- Cloudy nights warmer — clouds **reduce terrestrial heat loss**

## SEC 4:15:5 | Temperature inversion
- Highest temperatures in **sub-tropical deserts** (20°–30° N), not Equator — **clear skies and dry air**
- **Valley inversion** — cold air **drains downslope** into valleys; frost pockets; slopes warmer
- **Stratosphere** — a **permanent inversion** layer
- **Interior** areas — **larger** annual range (land heats and cools fast); coasts equable

## SEC 4:15:6 | IMD heatwave thresholds
- Heatwave minimum: **coast 37°C**, plains 40°C, hills 30°C
- Cold wave minimum-temperature threshold: plains **10°C** or below; hills **0°C** or below

## SEC 4:15:7 | Temperature extremes

### Inversion
- **Temperature inversion** — temperature rises with height; favoured by long winter nights, clear skies, calm dry air
- Effects: **fog, frost, smog**, pollutants trapped in the planetary boundary layer

# ROW 4:16 | Atmospheric pressure - pressure belts; planetary, periodic and local winds; jet streams
## SEC 4:16:0 | Pressure belts
| Belt | Latitude | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Equatorial low | 0°–5° | calm rising air |
| **Subtropical high** (**horse latitudes**) | **30°–35°** | **sinking dry air, hot deserts** |
| Subpolar low | 60°–65° | |
| Polar high | | cold sinking air |
- Belts shift **5°–10°** with the overhead sun (north in NH summer)
- **Polar vortex** — cold low around poles, year-round, strongest in **winter**; anticlockwise
- Heated air expands, less dense → **low pressure**
- **El Niño** — AP south-west monsoon expected **below normal**

## SEC 4:16:1 | Planetary winds
- **Trade winds** — subtropical high → equatorial low; **NE** in NH, **SE** in SH
- **Westerlies** — subtropical high → subpolar low; **south-westerly** in NH
- Tropical cyclones: **trade winds** push them **west** onto east coasts; **westerlies** turn them **north-east**

## SEC 4:16:2 | Wind controls
- Closely spaced isobars — **steep pressure gradient**, strong winds; air moves high → low
- Standard sea-level pressure **1013.25 mb** (hPa)
- **Coriolis force** — zero at equator, **maximum at poles**; proportional to wind speed
- No cyclones within about **5°** of the Equator — Coriolis **zero / negligible**
- **Friction** — **slows** surface winds; stronger over land; negligible aloft
- **Geostrophic wind** — upper air, parallel to isobars; pressure gradient = Coriolis, no friction
- **ITCZ** — equatorial low where **trade winds converge**
- **Walker circulation** — east–west cell over the **Pacific**; **Hadley** cell — meridional
- **Roaring Forties** **40°–50° S**; Furious Fifties 50°–60° S; SH westerlies stronger (little land friction)

## SEC 4:16:3 | Jet streams
- **Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet** — **south of Himalayas in winter**, north in summer; withdraws before monsoon burst, returns **October**
- STWJ carries **western disturbances** eastward from the Mediterranean (worsened 2026 monsoon extremes)
- **Tropical Easterly Jet** — about 5°N–20°N; **South China Sea to East Africa**; June to early October
- **Somali (Findlater) Jet** — **low-level** jet off **East Africa**; with Somali Current strengthens monsoon
- Summer: heated land → **low pressure** draws moist sea winds → monsoon

## SEC 4:16:4 | Monsoon & polar vortex
### Theories
- **Halley** (**1686**) — monsoon as a giant **land-sea breeze**
- Modern monsoon theory **1957** — ITCZ shift, jet streams, **Tibet Plateau**, ENSO
- 'Monsoon' from Arabic **'mausim'** = **season**
- Winds reverse due to **differential heating** of land and sea
- **ITCZ** shifts north in northern summer following the overhead Sun; this helps the monsoon (result, not cause)
### Others
- Jet streams first identified in the **1920s** by **Wasaburo Ooishi** (Japan); widely known in WWII
- **Rossby waves** weakening the polar vortex — winds reverse, vortex **splits**, cold air spills into mid-latitudes
- **Sea breeze** by day — land heats faster, lower pressure over land
- **Western Disturbances** — winter rain and snow in **north-western India**
- Warmer western Indian Ocean offsetting El Niño — **positive IOD**

## SEC 4:16:5 | Daily breezes
- **Valley (anabatic)** breeze — **uphill by day**; **mountain (katabatic)** — **downhill at night**
- Katabatic cold air in valleys → **temperature inversion and frost**
- Land and sea breezes — **daily**; monsoon reverses seasonally
- Sea and land breezes **moderate** AP coastal temperatures
