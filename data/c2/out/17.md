## SEC 1:131:3 | Shah Jahan (1628-1658)
### Reign and monuments
- Crowned at **Agra** in **1628**

- **Sambhaji** — executed **1689**; **Rajaram** — resisted from **Jinji**, followed by **Tarabai**; **Shahu** — released by **Bahadur Shah I**

# ROW 1:133 | Mughal administration - mansab (zat and sawar), jagirdari system and the jagirdari crisis
## SEC 1:133:0 | Mansabdari basics
- **Mansab** — a **rank** (military and civil); not a hereditary office
- Ranks and jagirs — **ended with the holder's death**

## SEC 1:133:1 | Zat, sawar & classes
- **Zat** — personal rank and status; **Sawar** — number of **cavalrymen** to be maintained
| Class | Sawar |
|---|---|
| First | equal to zat |
| **Second** | **half of zat** |
| Third | less than half of zat |

## SEC 1:133:2 | Changes by emperor
- Payment: cash — **naqd**; non-hereditary revenue assignment — **jagir**
- Subahs under Akbar: **12** (1580) → **15** (end of reign); Aurangzeb — **21**
- **Jahangir** — **du-aspa sih-aspa**: part of a trusted mansabdar's **sawar rank** counted double, zat not raised
- **Shah Jahan** — half-yield jagir **shashmaha**; quarter-yield **sihmaha**
- **Aurangzeb** — **mashrut**: temporary/conditional increase in sawar for a specific assignment

## SEC 1:133:3 | Army & musters
| Troops | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ahadis** | gentlemen troopers recruited and paid directly by the **emperor** |
| Tabinan | mansabdars' contingents |
| Dakhili | state-paid troops under mansabdars |
| Walashahi | bodyguard |
- Army not exclusively ahadis — bulk was **mansabdars' cavalry**, plus infantry and artillery
- **Dagh** — **branding of horses** to prevent substitution
- **Bitikchi** — prepared revenue papers and accounts

## SEC 1:133:4 | Central officials
| Official | Charge |
|---|---|
| **Wakil** | chief adviser/regent; **largely symbolic** under Akbar |
| **Mir Bakhshi** | **intelligence** (waqia-navis, khufia-navis, harkaras) |
| **Mir Saman** | royal **household** and **karkhanas** |
| **Sadr-us-Sudur** | religion, charity, **madad-i-maash** |
- **Madad-i-maash** — **revenue-free grants** to scholars and religious men, under the **Sadr**
- Province: **Diwan** — revenue; independent check on the **Subahdar** (governance, law and order)

## SEC 1:133:5 | Jagir types
| Jagir | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Altamgha** | **perpetual** |
| Tankhwah | in lieu of salary |
| **Watan** | **hereditary home territory** of a zamindar or Rajput chief; not transferred |
| Mashrut | conditional |
- **Jagir** — land-revenue assignment in lieu of **salary and military service**; no judicial/police duties; no ownership of land

## SEC 1:133:6 | Jagirdari crisis
- Jagirs transferred every **3-4 years** — to stop mansabdars **developing local roots** and becoming feudal lords
- **Bejagiri** — mansabdars **kept waiting without jagirs**; bred discontent and factionalism
- Crisis under **Aurangzeb**: **more mansabdars**, **fewer good jagirs**, more **pressure on peasants**
- Breakdown of the **jagirdari system** — core explanation of Mughal decline

## SEC 1:133:7 | Exam corner
### Administration
- Mansab — **combined military-cum-civil rank**; Mughal state essentially military
- Ascending order: **Village → Pargana → Sarkar → Subah**
- **Chakla** — group of parganas between **pargana and sarkar**; from **Shah Jahan's** time
### Jagirdar and zamindar
- **Jagirdar** — non-hereditary revenue assignment in lieu of salary; frequently transferred
- **Zamindar** — **hereditary** local rights; **intermediary** between state and peasants
### Historians and legacy
| Historian | Explanation of jagirdari crisis |
|---|---|
| **Satish Chandra** | **shortage of jagirs** relative to claimants |
| Irfan Habib | agrarian crisis |
| Athar Ali | influx of Deccani and Maratha nobles |
- **Asaf Jahi state of Hyderabad** — retained Mughal-style jagirdari administration

## SEC 1:133:8 | Mansabdari system → introduction, purpose and PYQ features
- ★ Introduced by **Akbar**; ★ borrowed from **Mongolia** (Genghis Khan's army)
- ★ Common to **military and civil** departments — not all mansabdars military; high civil officials also graded as mansabdars
- ★ Abul Fazl: **66 grades**; in practice **33**
- ★ Main aim — **clean administration**; ★ Akbar's military system based on **Mansabdari**
- ★ Akbar revived **Chehra** (descriptive roll of soldiers) and **Dagh** (branding of horses)
- ★ **10 cavalrymen → 20 horses** — horses rested on march and replaced in war
- ★ **Mir Bakshi** — responsible for smooth functioning of the system
- ★ **'Mansab'** — **Arabic** word meaning rank or position

## SEC 1:133:10 | Emperors → changes in the mansab system
- ★ Not true of the system: **sawar rank could exceed zat**
- ★ Mansabdars in **33 classes**; paid in **cash** by grade
- ★ True: **zat and mansab** designations granted; **three classes** of mansabdars; ★ not hereditary
- ★ **Do-aspa and Sih-aspa** — introduced by **Jahangir**; extra soldiers/horses without raising zat

## SEC 1:133:11 | Army components and musters → terms
- ★ **Chakla** — territorial unit between **Subah and Pargana** (not Sarkar); especially under **Shah Jahan**

## SEC 1:133:14 | Jagir types → features; jagirdar vs zamindar
- ★ **Ahadis** — offered services **singly**; attached to **no chief**; **emperor** as their immediate colonel (not attached to Mirzas)

# ROW 1:134 | Mughal provincial and local administration; the mansabdari-jagirdari-zamindari linkage
## SEC 1:134:0 | Units & heads
- ★ District — **Sarkar**; Suba (province) → **Sarkars** (districts) → **Parganas** (sub-districts) → villages
- Sarkar officials — **Faujdar** and **Amalguzar**; Suba — **Subedar** and provincial **Diwan**
- Ascending: **Pargana → Sarkar → Suba** (UPSC 2021); Pargana ≈ tehsil; **Chakla** — intermediate unit, not the Sarkar
- District revenue collector — **Amalguzar** (or **amil**)

## SEC 1:134:1 | Provincial officials
- Provincial **Diwan** — revenue and finance, independent of the Subedar; a **check on the Subedar**

## SEC 1:134:2 | Revenue officials
| Official | Duty |
|---|---|
| **Potdar** | **treasurer** |
| Bitikchi | assessment and land records |
| Qanungo | pargana-level records |
| Patwari | village accountant |
| **Muqaddam** (Patel) | **village headman**; collected revenue |
| Khazanadar / Fotadar | treasurer at Sarkar level |

## SEC 1:134:3 | Mansab → jagir → zamindar
- Chain: **Mansabdar → Tankhwah jagir → Jagirdar's agents → Zamindars → Peasant**
- Jagirdar's agents collected the assessed revenue (**jama**) through zamindars, who kept a **hereditary share**
- Peasant paid about **one-third** of the produce

## SEC 1:134:4 | Jagirdar vs zamindar
- Local face of the jagirdari crisis — **short-tenure jagirdars** pressed for more revenue; **zamindars resisted/rebelled**; **peasants fled**

## SEC 1:134:5 | Zamindar categories
- **Primary zamindars** — did **not** hold merely temporary, non-hereditary rights

## SEC 1:134:6 | Golconda (AP angle)
- Under **Tana Shah**: tarafs and simalu; **37 Sarkars**, **517 Parganas**
- Revenue **farmed out by auction**
- **Northern Circars** zamindars trace their origin to the **Qutb Shahi** era

## SEC 1:134:7 | Exam corner
- ★ Ascending by size: **Pargana — Sarkar — Suba**
- ★ Suba run by **Subedar** (or Nizam) and **Diwan**; **15 Subas** under Akbar, **22** under Aurangzeb
- **Chakla** — unit between **Suba and Pargana**, not identical with Sarkar; prominent in **Bengal** under **Murshid Quli Khan**
- State's effective rural reach ended at the **Pargana**; villages largely self-governing
- **Jagirs** — in lieu of **salary for state (mainly military) service**; not hereditary; often transferred

## SEC 1:134:9 | Provincial and revenue officials → functions
- ★ **Amalguzar** — revenue collector in charge of a **district**
- ★ **Qanungo** — kept records of the **revenue payable by the villages**

# ROW 1:135 | Mughal economy - agriculture, karkhanas, trade, silver influx, Indian Ocean commerce
## SEC 1:135:0 | Agriculture & revenue
### Crops and market
- About **15 percent** of rural produce reached the market; cash revenue pushed peasants to sell
- **Jins-i-kamil** ("perfect crops") — **commercial crops**: cotton, sugarcane, indigo, oilseeds, opium
- From the New World via the **Portuguese** — **tobacco, maize, chillies**
- **Shahab Nahr** — restored **Rajabwah** canal of **Firuz Shah**
### Land and revenue
| Land | Condition |
|---|---|
| Polaj | cultivated annually |
| Parauti | fallow one year |
| **Chachar** | fallow **3-4 years** |
| Banjar | uncultivated |
- **Zabt** — devised by **Raja Todarmal**; measured with **Ilahi Gaz** (bamboo rod with iron rings)
- Zabt state share — **one-third** of average produce of the **past ten years**
- Cash revenue under zabt — **monetised the rural economy**, linked peasants to markets
- **Patta** — title deed; **Qabuliyat** — deed of agreement
- **Khud-kasht** — cultivated land in own village; **pahi-kasht** — in another village
### Society, crafts, observers
- **Umara** (nobility) — **high mansabdars**: Turanis, Iranis, Hindustanis, Rajputs
- **Karkhanas** — **court luxury goods** (gold/silver articles, fine textiles, arms, jewellery); a Sultanate tradition
- **Jean de Thevenot** — French traveller-physician; reached **Surat** in **1666** (Manucci Italian, Pelsaert Dutch, Mundy English)
- **Francois Bernier** — **no private property in land** (contested by modern historians)

## SEC 1:135:1 | Traders & communities
- ★ **Banjaras** — **traders**; carried **grain in bullock caravans**
- ★ Seth, bohra — long-distance trade; local traders — **banik**
| Group | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sarrafs** | money-changers and bankers; issued **hundis** |
| Mahajans | moneylenders-cum-merchants |
| Baniks | retail traders |
| **Nagarseth** | **head of a merchant community**; dealt with officials |
- **Marwaris** — Oswals, Maheshwaris, Agarwals; **Chettis** — Coromandel merchants
- Sultanate overseas trade — **Multanis and Khurasanis**; inland — Gujarati, Marwari, Bohra merchants
- First European trade centre in Andhra — **Machilipatnam** (**Dutch**, **1605**); Guntur — Tobacco Board; Nizampatnam (Petapoli) — later
- British policy: Indian exports **heavily taxed** in Britain; British goods favoured in India — ruined handicrafts

## SEC 1:135:2 | Coins & credit
- ★ **Vijayanagara** — agriculture through **ponds/tanks, dams and wells**
- **Bima** — **insurance** of goods in transit, by sarrafs, for a premium
- **Chalni** — coins of the current reign; **Khazana** — previous reign's coins, at a discount
- **40 dams** = 1 rupee; dam copper, rupiya silver
- Silver **rupiya** — standardised by **Sher Shah** at **178 grains**; continued by Akbar
- 18th-c. **Bengal** exports — cotton, silk, saltpetre, opium (**not copper**); copper traded at **Motupalli**

## SEC 1:135:3 | Ports
- **Surat** — chief west-coast port; **Bab-ul-Makka** (Gate of Mecca), for Haj
- **Masulipatnam** (Qutb Shahi port) — **Kalamkari (chintz)**
- Masulipatnam order: **Dutch 1605 → English 1611 → (Hugli, Portuguese expelled 1632) → French 1669**
- **Hugli** — Portuguese base in Bengal; expelled by **Shah Jahan**, **1632**
| Product | Centre |
|---|---|
| **Saltpetre** | **Machilipatnam**, Narsapur, Pulicat |
| Damascus swords | **Nirmal** |
| Carpets / durries | **Warangal** |

- ★ **Sitar** — Persian long-necked lute (**setar**) + Indian **veena**
- ★ Non-Islamic vocal style (as per the answer key): **Taraka**
- ★ Title **'Tansen'** — from Raja **Vikramjit** of Gwalior (not Akbar); **'Mian'** — from Akbar; also called **Ramtanu**
- ★ Before Akbar — patron **Raja Ramchandra (Singh)** of **Bhata / Rewa** (Bandhavgarh); Akbar sent a firman for him
- ★ **Tappa** — encouraged at **Muhammad Shah's** court; **Sadaranga and Adaranga** — Khayal composers there; Tarana, Dadra, Ghazal also
