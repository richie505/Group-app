## SEC 3:144:0 | SCS vs package
- Package period: **2015-16 to 2019-20** (five years, the promised SCS period)
| Feature | Under SCS | Under Special Assistance Package |
|---|---|---|
| CSS funding | automatic **90:10** grant | equivalent amount only through **EAP** repayments |
| Use of funds | across all schemes | tied to approved **externally aided projects** |
| Industry | **tax concessions** for industry | only the weaker **Section 94(1)** incentives |
## SEC 3:144:1 | Package components
- **Polavaram** treated as a national project under **Section 90**
- Industrial incentives linked to **Section 94(1)** (unrelated to Polavaram's status)
- Promised **ten-year tax holiday**: delayed, diluted or subsumed in **GST**; not implemented fully
## SEC 3:144:2 | EAP route
- State borrows from agencies like **World Bank** and **ADB**; Centre repays loan and interest up to the **SCS-equivalent amount**
- AP's criticism: **long approval cycles** and heavy compliance, creating red tape and delays
## SEC 3:144:3 | Centre's stand
- **14th Finance Commission**: removed the general-special category distinction and raised devolution to **42%**; Centre said this absorbed SCS benefits
- **Constitutional and technical constraints** on extending SCS to a new State
- Feared similar demands from **Bihar** and **Odisha**

- **50-year interest-free loans** for **capital works** only
  - over and above the **net borrowing ceiling**, outside the **3% of GSDP** deficit ceiling

# ROW 4:0 | The universe and the solar system - origin theories, the Sun, planets, asteroids, comets, meteors
## SEC 4:0:0 | Basics & units
### Universe
- Age of the universe **13.8 billion years**; Earth–solar system **4.6 billion years**
- ★ Most abundant element in the universe: **Hydrogen**; second: **Helium**
- Planet formation: hot inner disc kept only **metals and rocky minerals**; **ices** retained in the cool outer disc (giant planets)
- Early atmosphere: oxygen added mainly by **photosynthesis** (after H-He atmosphere lost; degassing gave water vapour, N2, CO2)
- 2026 study: some geomagnetic pole reversals lasted up to about **70,000 years** (vs assumed 10,000)
### Distance units
- ★ **Light year** = distance light travels in one year (measures **distance**, not time); used for distances between **stars**
- **Parsec** ≈ **3.26 light-years**: largest unit (light-year far larger than an AU)
- **1 AU** (mean Earth-Sun distance) = **149.6 million km**; light crosses it in about **8 min 19 s**
### The Sun and planets
- Sun's diameter about **109 times** Earth's; holds **99.8%** of Solar System's mass
- **Sun's gravitational pull** gives the centripetal force keeping planets in orbit
- Solar System now has **eight** planets; Pluto reclassified by IAU in **2006**
- Pluto a **dwarf planet**: has **not cleared its orbital path**; discovery of **Eris** helped prompt the change
- Outer planets have **longer** periods of revolution than inner ones
- Mean distance from Sun: **Mercury 57.9**, Venus 108.2, Earth 149.6, Mars 228 million km
- ★ Largest year: **Pluto** (farthest; **247.9** Earth years per revolution)
- ★ Smallest planet: **Pluto** by the old view (2,376 km); now **Mercury** (4,879 km)
- ★ Farthest planet: **sometimes Neptune, sometimes Pluto** (Neptune farthest 1979–1999, as Pluto's orbit lies inside Neptune's)
- ★ Hottest planet: **Venus** (464°C); ★ coldest: **Neptune** (–200°C; atmosphere of hydrogen, helium, methane)
- **Uranus** discovered by **William Herschel**
- ★ **Magellan** spacecraft sent to **Venus** (launched 4 May 1989; reached orbit 10 Aug 1990; named after Ferdinand Magellan)
- Asteroid belt: between **Mars and Jupiter**, rocky leftovers, largest body **Ceres**; the icy ring beyond Neptune at 30–50 AU is the **Kuiper Belt**
### Sky and Moon
- **Polaris** (within ~1° of north celestial pole) is in **Ursa Minor**
- Solar flare classes, increasing strength: **A, B, C, M, X**
- **Micromoon**: full Moon at **apogee** (6–7% smaller, 10–14% dimmer)
- **Himalayan Chandra Telescope** at **Hanle**: completed 25 years in 2026

## SEC 4:0:2 | Life cycle of a star
### Stars
- ★ **Supernova**: explosion of a **dying star** at the end of its life cycle
- ★ Black hole: a **collapsed star**; most form from a large star dying in a supernova (NASA)
- ★ Black hole theory first given by **S. Chandrasekhar** (paper to Royal Astronomical Society, London, 11 Jan 1935)
- **Chandrasekhar limit** ≈ **1.44 solar masses**: upper mass limit of a white dwarf
- Massive star after main sequence: **supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black hole**
- Sun after exhausting hydrogen: becomes a **red giant**
- **Absolute magnitude**: brightness at **10 parsecs**; lower number = brighter
### The Sun and Earth
- **Sunspots** (cooler dark magnetic patches) mark the **~11-year** solar cycle; Sun's magnetic poles reverse at its peak
- Sunlight reaches Earth in about **8 min 20 s** (~150 million km)
- Earth lies in the Sun's **habitable zone**
- First mathematically predictive heliocentric model: **Copernicus**; **Aryabhata** held Earth rotates on its axis and explained eclipses
### Missions

## SEC 4:0:3 | The Sun: layers
- Layers, centre outward: **core → radiative zone → convective zone → photosphere → chromosphere → corona**
- **Photosphere** about **5,500–6,000°C**; **corona** can exceed **one million °C**
- Energy: **nuclear fusion** of **hydrogen into helium** in the plasma core (~**15 million °C**)
- **Coronal mass ejections**: **plasma** clouds from the **corona** (not rock); ~**250–3,000 km/s**; reach Earth in **1–3 days**
- Earth's magnetic field: **geodynamo** of churning **liquid-iron outer core**; shields from solar wind
- Southern aurora **Aurora Australis**; northern **Aurora Borealis**
- **Lunar eclipse**: Earth's shadow falls on the Moon; solar eclipse: Moon's shadow on Earth

## SEC 4:0:6 | Asteroids vs comets

# ROW 4:1 | Earth - shape, size, geoid; latitudes and longitudes, the graticule
## SEC 4:1:0 | Shape
- ★ First to state Earth was spherical: **Aristotle** (384–322 BC), in "On the Heavens"
- **Geoid** = **Earth-shaped**; Earth is an **oblate spheroid** (flattened at poles, bulging at Equator)
- Evidence of sphericity: Earth's **circular shadow** on the Moon in a lunar eclipse (also mast-first ships, circumnavigation, satellite images)
- Weight **minimum at the equator**, maximum at the poles: rotation and equatorial bulge reduce effective gravity
- ★ Stars rise **perpendicular to the horizon** for an observer at the **Equator** (celestial poles on the horizon)
- ★ **Arctic** and **Antarctica**: near the **North and South Poles**
- India's nearest point to the Equator: **Indira Point** (6°45' N); Equator does not touch India
## SEC 4:1:1 | Dimensions
- Water : land ≈ **71 : 29**
- Equatorial diameter **12,756 km**; polar diameter **12,714 km**; equatorial circumference ~**40,090 km**
## SEC 4:1:2 | Graticule facts
- ★ First to divide the circle into **360 degrees**: **Hipparchus** (Greek; borrowed from Babylonian maths)
- **Equator**: the only **great-circle** parallel; all other parallels are small circles
- **0° lat, 0° long**: **Gulf of Guinea**, Atlantic Ocean
- Antipode: change N↔S and subtract longitude from 180° — **20°N, 80°E → 20°S, 100°W**
- **179 parallels** (89 N + 89 S + Equator) and **360 meridians**
- One degree of longitude: **111 km** at Equator, **55.5 km** at 60°, zero at poles

# ROW 4:2 | Motions of the earth - rotation and revolution; effects (day and night, Coriolis force, tides)
## SEC 4:2:0 | Rotation vs revolution
### Rotation
- **Rotation**: Earth's spin on its axis, **west to east**, once in **24 hours**; gives day and night
- **Venus and Uranus** spin opposite to Earth
- Linear rotational speed **maximum at the Equator**, zero at the poles
- **Equator**: about **12-hour** days all year; variation grows with latitude
- Coriolis effect comes from **rotation** (not revolution) and shapes **cyclones**
### Revolution
- **Revolution**: elliptical path around the Sun, about **365¼ days**
- **Perihelion and aphelion** belong to revolution; day and night, Coriolis effect, time zones come from rotation
## SEC 4:2:1 | Rotation facts
- Rotation not felt: motion nearly **uniform**; **atmosphere and objects** move along with Earth
- **Sidereal day** (relative to a distant star): **23 h 56 min 4 s**, about 4 min shorter than the 24-hour solar day
- Climate-driven ice melt lengthens the day by about **1.33 ms per century** (mass shifts towards Equator, higher moment of inertia)
- Axis shifts mainly from **mass redistribution**: melting ice, groundwater depletion; solar flares only disturb the upper atmosphere
## SEC 4:2:2 | Evidence & names
- **Foucault pendulum**, **Paris, 1851**: slow turning of the **plane of swing** showed Earth's rotation
## SEC 4:2:3 | Day & night
- Day and night caused by **rotation**; **circle of illumination** (grey line) divides them
- Circle of illumination passes through both poles only at the **equinoxes**
- Six-month polar day and night: **revolution with axial tilt** (also seasons, solstices, equinoxes, opposite seasons)
- **Midnight sun**: Sun above horizon 24 h in summer, **north of the Arctic Circle** and **south of the Antarctic Circle** (e.g. Norway); peaks near summer solstice
- **Twilight**: **shortest at the Equator** (Sun rises and sets nearly vertically), longer towards poles (weeks-long near poles)
- Equator: seasons marked by **wet and dry** periods
## SEC 4:2:4 | Coriolis effect
- Northern Hemisphere: deflection to the **right**; Southern Hemisphere: to the **left**
- NH tropical cyclones rotate **anticlockwise**
- Coriolis force (**2Ωv sin latitude**): **zero at the Equator**, **maximum at the poles**, grows with wind velocity
## SEC 4:2:5 | Centrifugal effect
- **Coriolis effect**: deflection of moving objects
- Centrifugal effect of rotation: greatest at Equator, zero at poles; causes **polar flattening** (equatorial bulge)
- Weight **minimum at the Equator** (effective gravity least)
## SEC 4:2:6 | Tides
- Evidences of rotation: Coriolis deflection, equatorial bulge, apparent celestial motion; Earth-Sun distance is **not** constant (elliptical orbit)
- Passing two tidal bulges a day: **two high and two low tides**; similar tides about **12 h 25 min** apart
- **Neap tides**: Sun and Moon at **right angles**; **spring tides**: at new and full Moon (aligned)
## SEC 4:2:7 | Revolution & leap year
- Centripetal force keeping Earth in orbit: **Sun's gravitational force**
- Extra **quarter day** a year makes a leap day in 4 years: **366** days with **29 February**
- Gregorian rule: century year leap only if divisible by **400**: **2000** leap, 1900 not
## SEC 4:2:8 | Exam corner
- Cyclone approaching AP coast usually steered **north-northwest** (steering winds, trade winds, Coriolis)
## SEC 4:2:9 | Rotation — two motions, direction, period, speed and data
- ★ A **shift** in Earth's rotation and axis is taking place (ETH Zurich, Nature Geoscience, PNAS); length of day has increased slightly
  - ★ Solar flares and **CMEs** bombarding Earth's outermost atmosphere: also true
- ★ **Aryabhatta**: first to explain that **rotation** accounts for daily rising and setting of the Sun (works in Aryasiddhanta)
- ★ Satellites not always launched eastward (polar orbit ones go **north or south**); Earth does rotate **west to east**
- ★ Axis inclined at **23.5° (23°30')** from the ecliptic axis
- ★ Seasons: **revolution** and **inclination of the axis**; Sun perpendicular at Equator on **21 March** and **23 September** (12 h day and night)
## SEC 4:2:10 | Persons and laws → evidence of the motions
- ★ Winds deflected to the right in the NH due to **rotation of the Earth** (Coriolis effect)
## SEC 4:2:11 | Day and night — terms → meaning
- ★ Varying length of day and night through the year: Earth's axis **inclined** to the ecliptic; **revolution on a tilted axis**
## SEC 4:2:12 | Effects of rotation — Coriolis force, centrifugal effect and tides
- ★ Coriolis force: an **apparent force** caused by Earth's **rotation**
- ★ Deflects winds to the **left** in the **Southern** Hemisphere, right in the Northern
## SEC 4:2:13 | Revolution — data and leap year
- ★ High tide recurs about **52 minutes** later each day (lunar day **24 h 52 min**): 8:00 am → **8:52 am**
- ★ Day and night: **rotation** on axis (about 24 h); seasons: **revolution** with axis tilted at **23.5°**

# ROW 4:3 | Concept of time - local and standard time, GMT/UTC, Indian Standard Time, time zones, International Date Line
## SEC 4:3:0 | Longitude → time
- **15° = 1 hour**; **1° = 4 minutes**; **1 minute of arc = 4 seconds** of time
- Places to the **east** are **ahead** in local time (Earth rotates west to east)
- Opposite sides of Greenwich: **add** longitudes — 25°E and 35°W = 60° = **4 hours**
- **Colombo** shares Mumbai/Kolkata/Chennai time (**UTC+5:30**); Nepal is UTC+5:45
- Poles: about **six months** of day and six of night
- Axial tilt gives seasons, unequal day length, tropics and polar circles; **not day and night** (rotation)
- **Vega**: pole star about **14,000 years ago** and again in about **12,000 years**, due to **precession** (axis wobble)
## SEC 4:3:1 | Solving local time
- ★ 10:00 a.m. IST → Shillong (92°E): 92° − 82.5° = 9.5° × 4 min → **10:38 a.m.**
- 10 a.m. at A (60°W) → B (90°E): 150° = **10 hours** ahead → **8 p.m.**
## SEC 4:3:2 | Time terms
- **GMT**: mean solar time at Greenwich; **UTC**: atomic **caesium** clocks, adjusted with **leap seconds**
- Prime Meridian (Royal Observatory, Greenwich) adopted at the International Meridian Conference, **1884**
- Most time zones (with overseas territories): **France** (12); Russia 11
- **China**: single time zone despite large east-west span
- **Daylight Saving Time**: more use of evening daylight in summer; India does not use it
- **NavIC** (7 satellites: 3 GEO + 4 GSO): **rubidium** atomic clocks; IRNSS-1F clock failed
- **Milankovitch cycles**: long-term orbital and axial variations influencing climate
- India's single time zone costs the **north-east** daylight; separate-zone proposals undecided
- Moving **west**, local time **falls behind**
## SEC 4:3:3 | Indian Standard Time
### IST meridian
- IST meridian **82°30′E**: **GMT + 5 h 30 min**; 0000 UTC = **5:30 a.m.** IST
- Reference town: **Mirzapur** (Uttar Pradesh)
- States crossed, west to east: **UP, MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh** (not **West Bengal**)
- IST maintained by **CSIR-National Physical Laboratory**, New Delhi
- Tropic of Cancer and IST meridian meet in **Koriya** district, **Chhattisgarh**, on the **Baghelkhand Plateau**
### India's extent
- Westernmost **68°7′E** (Kutch); easternmost **97°25′–97°30′E** (Arunachal Pradesh)
- Span ~**29°** ≈ 116 min: about **2 hours** difference
### Worked rules
- 30° west of Greenwich: **2 hours behind** GMT
- Depart 10:00 a.m., 3-hour flight, 15° east: **2:00 p.m.** local
### Tropic of Cancer
- Not through **Odisha** (wholly south); crosses 8 States: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram
- 21 June daylight: Tropic of Cancer **13½ h**; Arctic Circle 24 h; Equator ~12 h
## SEC 4:3:4 | International Date Line
- Date Line passes through the **Bering Strait**; deviates around the Aleutians and Pacific island groups
- **Big Diomede** (Russia) and **Little Diomede** (USA): neighbours on opposite sides, different dates
- **Samoa** moved to the Asian side in **2011**
- **Kiribati's Line Islands** (**UTC+14**): among the first to see a new day
- Crossing **eastward**: **subtract** one day
## SEC 4:3:5 | Solstices & equinoxes
### Dates
- Order: **March Equinox → June Solstice → September Equinox → December Solstice**
- Summer solstice (NH) **21 June** = **International Day of Yoga**
- NH winter solstice around **22 December**; autumnal equinox around 22/23 September
- **23 September** (and 21 March): Sun overhead at the **Equator**; equinox: day and night about equal
### Solstice effects
- ★ Winter solstice noon Sun at the Tropic of Cancer: **43°** (Sun overhead at Tropic of Capricorn)
- 21/22 December: Sun overhead at **Tropic of Capricorn**; Southern Hemisphere **longest day**; North Pole **continuous night**
- June solstice: North Pole **continuous daylight**
- March Equinox: spring begins in NH, **autumn** in SH
- 23 September to 21 December: NH days get **progressively shorter**
- Solstice: unequal day and night, maximum seasonal contrast
## SEC 4:3:6 | Tropic of Cancer & tilt
### Tilt
- Seasons: revolution combined with **axial tilt**
- Axis keeps the same orientation due to **angular momentum**
- Axis at **66½°** to the orbital plane (**23½°** tilt from perpendicular)
- Southern summer: **South Pole** tilted towards the Sun
- North Pole six months daylight ↔ South Pole **continuous darkness**
### Tropic of Cancer in India
- At **23°30′N**; mainland India ends at **8°4′N**, northern limit **37°6′N**
- Crosses **8** States; west to east **Gujarat** first, MP third, Jharkhand fifth, Tripura seventh
- **Andhra Pradesh** lies **wholly south** of it
- Near it: **Agartala** (Tripura), Gandhinagar (Gujarat), Ranchi (Jharkhand), Ujjain (MP)
- **Mahi** crosses it twice; rises in MP, through Rajasthan and Gujarat, into the **Gulf of Khambhat**
## SEC 4:3:7 | IST in 2026
- Separate **Bombay** and **Calcutta** times from **1884**; single IST adopted after 1947
- **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026**: in force **180 days** after Gazette publication
- **White Rabbit** protocol developed by **CERN** (not ISRO)
- White Rabbit IST network: synchronised high-precision IST time-stamps for banking, payments, railways, airports, power grids, telecom; guards against **GPS spoofing**
## SEC 4:3:8 | Exam corner
