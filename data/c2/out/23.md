# ROW 1:180 | Administrative changes - civil services, army reorganisation, police, judiciary, local self-government (Ripon)
## SEC 1:180:0 | Civil services: patronage to PSC
### Company era
- **College of Fort William** (Calcutta, **1800**) — set up by **Wellesley** to train recruits of the Company's civil service
- **Charter Act of 1833** — Law Commission under **T.B. Macaulay**; codification of laws
- **Charter Act of 1853** — ended **Directors' patronage**; introduced **open competitive examination** (backed by Trevelyan and Robert Lowe)
- **Macaulay Committee** (**1854**) — examination to be held in **London**; preference for Oxford and Cambridge graduates
### Crown era
- **Indian Civil Services Act, 1861** — limited Indian entry, mainly into **uncovenanted posts** (conditions: seven years' residence, local-language exam)
- **Satyendranath Tagore** — **first Indian in the ICS** (1864; many texts say 1863)
  - **Ram Mohan Roy** (1832) — first to demand Indians in higher posts
- Maximum age cut to **19** in **1878** — under **Lord Lytton**; a major Indian grievance
- **INC** pressed for higher age limit and simultaneous exams from its **first session (1885)**
- **Aitchison Commission** — **1886/87**
### Indianisation and PSC
- **Government of India Act, 1919** — separate ICS exam in **India**, held from **1922** alongside the London exam
- ★ **Lee Commission** (**1923-24**) — progressive Indianisation; **equal representation** of Indians and Europeans in the ICS
- **Public Service Commission** set up — **1926**
- Order: 1919 Act → 1922 exam in India and England → 1924 Lee Commission → 1926 PSC
- **Federal and Provincial PSCs** — **1935**
- **Federal Court** — **Government of India Act, 1935**
  - 1861 Act — High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras; 1919 Act — dyarchy
## SEC 1:180:1 | Service commissions
- **Aitchison Commission** (**1886**, under **Dufferin**) — replaced covenanted/uncovenanted with **Imperial, Provincial and Subordinate** services
- **Lee Commission** (1923-24; **50:50 parity** in the ICS) — appointed under **Lord Reading**
  - **Islington Commission** — under Hardinge II
## SEC 1:180:2 | Persons
- **Lord Cornwallis** — **Father of Civil Services in India**; created a covenanted service, banned private trade and gifts
  - Promotion based on **seniority**
- **Lytton's Statutory Civil Service** (1878-79) — **one-sixth** of covenanted posts for Indians of high families; later abolished
- **Sardar Patel** — called the ICS the **'Steel Frame'** of India
- Wellesley — the "Bengal Tiger"; Bentinck — modern Western education
## SEC 1:180:3 | Judiciary & police
### Judiciary
- **Warren Hastings** (**1772**) — district **Diwani Adalats** (civil) and **Faujdari Adalats** (criminal)
- **Regulating Act of 1773** — **Supreme Court at Calcutta** (**1774**); first Chief Justice **Sir Elijah Impey**
- **Cornwallis** — four provincial courts of appeal: Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad and **Patna**
- **Bentinck** — **abolished** the provincial courts of appeal and circuit courts; work to **Commissioners**; separate **Sadar courts at Allahabad**
| Code | Year |
|---|---|
| Code of Civil Procedure | **1859** |
| Indian Penal Code | **1860** |
| Code of Criminal Procedure | **1861** |
- **Indian High Courts Act, 1861** — abolished the Supreme Courts and Sadar Adalats; set up High Courts
- High Courts at **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras** — **1862**; **Allahabad** — **1866**
### Police
- **Cornwallis** — **thanas** under **darogas**; police duties removed from **zamindars**
- **Daroga** — local representative of the **"aura and authority of Company Bahadur"**; notorious for abuse of power
- **Munro's Judicial Commission** — police under the **Collector**; minor crimes tried by village officers
- **Thomas Munro** (Governor of Madras) — extended the **Ryotwari system**
- **Police Act, 1861** (under **Canning**) — modelled on the **Royal Irish Constabulary**; meant to suppress dissent (not Cornwallis's model)
- **ICS** — direct ancestor of the **IAS**
## SEC 1:180:4 | Ilbert Bill, 1883
- Aim — let Indian **district magistrates and sessions judges** try **European British subjects**; end racial judicial privilege
- **"White Mutiny"** — the fierce European agitation against it
- Supported by the **Indian Association**; exposed British racial attitudes
- Outcome — diluted: Europeans tried by an Indian judge could claim a **jury of at least half European** members
## SEC 1:180:5 | Local self-government
- **Madras** (**1688**) — first municipal corporation in India
- **Lord Mayo's Resolution, 1870** — first step in **financial decentralisation**; divided central and provincial finances
- **Lord Mayo** — also a **statistical survey** of India and the **first census** (**1871**)
- **Lord Ripon's Resolution** — **1882**; provided an **elected non-official majority** in local bodies
- Ripon's Resolution — **'Magna Carta of local self-government'**
- **Lord Ripon** — **Father of Local Self-Government in India**
- After Ripon, local bodies still **controlled by officials** and short of funds (not free of official control)
- **Royal Commission on Decentralisation** (**1907-09**, chaired by **Hobhouse**) — only made recommendations
- **Cantonments Act, 1924** — passed by the **Central legislature**
- Sequence: Mayo 1870 → Ripon 1882 → GoI Act 1919 → GoI Act 1935
## SEC 1:180:6 | Exam corner
- **High Courts** — set up by **Letters Patent** issued under the **Crown's authority**
## SEC 1:180:7 | Civil services — persons → contributions and FIRSTs
- ★ **Lord Cornwallis** — established the **judicial organisation** in India; his criminal and judicial regulations still underpin the system
  - Gave limited judicial powers to Company revenue collectors (already civil magistrates)
## SEC 1:180:8 | ICS chronology, patronage and examinations
- ★ **Cornwallis** (Governor-General **1786-1793**) — created the **Covenanted Civil Service** (later the ICS); "Father of Civil Service in India"
- ★ **Montagu-Chelmsford Report** — recommended ICS exam held **simultaneously in India and England**
- ★ **Government of India Act, 1919** — separate ICS exam in **India**; held from **1922**
- ★ **1922** — British finally agreed to simultaneous ICS exam in India and England
- ★ **1 January 1941** — Indians in the civil service (**617**) exceeded Europeans (**585**)
- ★ **Lee Commission** (**1924**) — recommended an independent, impartial **Public Service Commission**
## SEC 1:180:10 | Judiciary — Governor-General → judicial reform
- ★ **Regulation of 1793** (**Cornwallis**) — District Collector stripped of judicial powers; made **only a revenue collector**
  - Reason: Cornwallis was alarmed at the power concentrated in the Collector; separated judicial from revenue administration
## SEC 1:180:12 | Police — terms and reformers: Cornwallis, Munro, Police Act 1861
- ★ **Daroga** — local representative of the **"Aura and Authority of Company Bahadur"** (second half of 18th c.)
- ★ **1793** — Cornwallis separated landlords from police duties; a **police station every 20-30 sq miles** under a **Daroga**
  - Darogas appointed and supervised by the **Magistrate**
## SEC 1:180:13 | Ilbert Bill controversy, 1883 — aim, "White Mutiny", compromise
- ★ **Ilbert Bill** (**1883**, Viceroy **Ripon**) — **removal of disqualifications** on Indian magistrates in the trial of Europeans
- ★ Would let Indian district magistrates and sessions judges try **Europeans in criminal cases**; Europeans organised a vehement agitation
## SEC 1:180:14 | Local self-government — year → step; Mayo and Ripon
- ★ **Madras** — first **Municipal Corporation** in India, **29 September 1688**
  - ★ Now the **Greater Chennai Corporation**; oldest municipal institution in India
- ★ **Cantonments Act** — passed by the **Central Legislature** in **1924**
- ★ **Lord Ripon** — **Father of Local Self-Government**; resolution of **1882**
- ★ Local self-government — **Ripon**, not **Lytton**
- ★ **Mayo's Resolution of 1870** — **financial decentralisation**; first to bifurcate Central and Provincial finances; provinces given certain services to administer
  - ★ Followed the legislative devolution of the **Indian Councils Act, 1861**
## SEC 1:180:15 | Universal angles
- ★ **Ilbert Bill (1883)** — to put **Indians and Europeans on par** in the **criminal jurisdiction** of courts (Ripon's tenure)
- ★ **Surendranath Banerjea** — **dismissed** from the ICS
  - ★ Selected in **1869**; disqualified over his age, reinstated by court, then dismissed for a minor judicial error

# ROW 1:181 | Revolt of 1857 - political causes (annexations, Doctrine of Lapse, subsidiary alliance)
## SEC 1:181:0 | Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798)
- **Subsidiary Alliance** — devised by **Lord Wellesley**; ally had to give up **independent foreign policy**
- **Nizam of Hyderabad** — **first** to accept (**September 1798**; again **1800**)
  - Mysore 1799; Awadh November 1801; Peshwa via Treaty of Bassein
- **1800** — Nizam ceded the **Rayalaseema** districts to pay for the subsidiary force → the **Ceded Districts**
- ★ **Rani Chennamma** — led the **Kittur revolt**; British refused to recognise her adopted son
## SEC 1:181:1 | States that accepted

- After **1858** — princely states guaranteed in return for accepting British **paramountcy**
## SEC 1:181:2 | Doctrine of Lapse
- Associated with **Lord Dalhousie**; applied from **1848**
- Adopted son could inherit **private property**, but **not the throne**
- **Satara** (**1848**) — first state annexed
- **Jhansi** (**1853**) — annexed; adopted son **Damodar Rao** of Rani Lakshmibai not recognised
- Nagpur — later; **Awadh (1856)** — annexed for **misgovernment**, not lapse
- Abandoned by **Queen Victoria's Proclamation** (**1 November 1858**), read by **Canning** at the **Allahabad Durbar**; recognised adoption
## SEC 1:181:3 | Annexation of Awadh, 1856
- **Wajid Ali Shah** — deposed and **exiled to Calcutta** after the annexation
- ★ **Wajid Ali Shah** (last Nawab of Awadh) — "Daro-deewar pe hasrat ki nazar karte hain, khush raho ahle-vatan ham to safar karte hain"; sung by revolutionaries in prison
- **Summary Revenue Settlement** (1856) — made directly with **village proprietors**; taluqdars lost estates, were disarmed, forts demolished
- **Bengal Army** sepoys alienated — their families in Awadh hit by the new **revenue burden**
- **Begum Hazrat Mahal** — opposed annexation; proclaimed her son **Birjis Qadr** ruler
- ★ **Hazrat Mahal** — led at **Lucknow**; **Nana Saheb** — led at **Kanpur**; **Mangal Pande** — hanged **March 29, 1857**

## SEC 1:181:4 | Other grievances
- **Nana Saheb** (adopted son of Peshwa **Baji Rao II**) — denied his adoptive father's **pension**
- **Dalhousie** — Bahadur Shah's successors to leave the **Red Fort** for the **Qutub at Mehrauli**
- **Canning (1856)** — successors to lose the **imperial title** and become mere **princes**
- **1855** — titles of the **Carnatic Nawab** and **Tanjore Raja** abolished

## SEC 1:181:11 | Centres and leaders of 1857 → British opponent and personal grievance
### Start and character
- ★ Revolt of 1857 — by the **Sepoys**; began as a sepoy mutiny
- **Meerut** — revolt began **10 May 1857**; sepoys marched to Delhi
- ★ **Delhi** — a centre **recaptured** by the English
- ★ **Chittor** — **not affected** by the Revolt
- ★ **Uddham Singh** — **not related** to 1857; a Punjab revolutionary

- ★ **Tatya Tope** — real name **Ramachandra Pandurang** (Tope); **commander-in-chief** of **Nana Saheb**

  - ★ Tatya Tope — **betrayed by a friend**, captured and put to death

- ★ **Thakur Kushal Singh of Auwa** — defeated the joint army of the **British and Jodhpur**
- ★ **Rani Lakshmibai** — Jhansi; **Begum Hazrat Mahal** — Lucknow (Henry Lawrence killed in the siege; Colin Campbell recaptured it in **1858**)
- ★ **Palmerston** — **British Prime Minister** during the revolt (1855-58)

# ROW 1:182 | Revolt of 1857 - economic, socio-religious, administrative and military causes
## SEC 1:182:0 | Economic causes
- **Annexations** — threw officials, retainers and soldiers of princely courts **out of work**
- High, rigid **land revenue** — peasants driven into **debt**, lost land to **moneylenders and traders**
- **Inam Commission** (**1852**) — in **Bombay** (not Madras); confiscated about **20,000 estates** lacking title
- Ruin of artisans — British machine-made goods entered **duty-free**; Indian goods faced **heavy tariffs** (deindustrialisation)
- **'Absentee sovereignty'** and **'drain of wealth'** — transfer of economic resources **from India to Britain**
## SEC 1:182:1 | Socio-religious causes
| Measure | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Abolition of **Sati** | **1829** | under **William Bentinck** (not Dalhousie); resented as interference in custom |
| **Lex Loci Act** (Caste Disabilities Removal) | **1850** | converts to Christianity could **inherit ancestral property** |
| **Hindu Widow Remarriage Act** | **1856** | challenged orthodox norms |
- Charter Act grant — **1813**
## SEC 1:182:2 | Military grievances
### Background grievances
- Lower courts — slow, costly, corrupt; favoured **the rich**
- **Satara** (with Jhansi, Nagpur) — annexed under the **Doctrine of Lapse**; **Awadh (1856)** — for **misgovernance**
- **Wajid Ali Shah** — deposed at Awadh's annexation (1856)
- **Western education, railways and the telegraph** (plus missionaries under police protection) — fears of **mass conversion**
- After **1858** — Crown policy of **religious neutrality**
- **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** — cause: **lack of Indian representation in the Legislative Council**; British misunderstanding of Indian customs
### Army
- **General Service Enlistment Act, 1856** — **Lord Canning**; recruits to serve anywhere, including **overseas** (feared loss of caste)
- **Bhatta** — **foreign service allowance**; withdrawn for service in **Sindh and Punjab**
- Highest rank for an Indian sepoy — **Subedar**
- Sepoys to European soldiers — about **5:1** (2.3 lakh to 45,000)
- **"Peasants in uniform"** — sepoys carried **village grievances** into the army
- **Chapatis** passed village to village across **North India**

# ROW 1:183 | Outbreak and spread - Mangal Pandey, Meerut; centres and leaders of the revolt
## SEC 1:183:0 | Outbreak to Delhi
- **Mangal Pandey** — attacked British officers at **Barrackpore** on **29 March 1857**
- ★ **Hearsey** (Lt Gen Sir John Bennet Hearsey) — British commanding officer at **Barrackpore**
- ★ **10 May 1857** — **3rd Bengal Light Cavalry** and the twentieth regiment revolted at **Meerut**
- ★ **Jhansi** — rising did **not** begin on **11 May 1857**
- Meerut rebels freed the jailed 85 sepoys and marched with the slogan **'Dilli Chalo'**
- At Delhi, rebels entered the **Red Fort** and killed **Commissioner Simon Fraser**
- **Bahadur Shah Zafar** — tried and exiled to **Rangoon** after Delhi fell
- Hindu-Muslim unity of 1857 — later cited against the **two-nation theory**
## SEC 1:183:1 | Centres & leaders
- **General Bakht Khan** — real military leader at Delhi; came from **Bareilly** (reached **3 July 1857**); led a **10-member 'Court of Soldiers'**
- **Begum Hazrat Mahal** (Lucknow) — proclaimed her son **Birjis Qadr** ruler of Awadh
- **Kunwar Singh** — **Arrah** (Bihar); Hazrat Mahal — Lucknow; Nana Saheb — Kanpur; Bahadur Shah Zafar — Delhi
- **Tantia Tope** — mainly **Central India**; Bakht Khan — Delhi; Hazrat Mahal — Awadh
- Order of rising: **Barrackpore** (29 March) → **Delhi** (11 May) → **Lucknow** (4 June) → **Kanpur** (5 June)
