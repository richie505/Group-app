# ROW 1:205 | Morley-Minto Reforms 1909; formation of the Muslim League 1906; Lucknow Pact 1916
## SEC 1:205:0 | Key dates
### 1906: Simla Deputation and the League
- **1 October 1906** — Simla Deputation, led by **Aga Khan III**, met **Lord Minto**
  - Demanded **separate electorates and weightage**, citing Muslims' contribution to the defence of the Empire
- **30 December 1906** — **Nawab Salimullah** of Dacca proposed the All India Muslim League at **Dacca/Dhaka**
  - **Aga Khan** — first president; **Syed Ameer Ali** — London branch
- League's **first annual session** — **Karachi**, 1907 (not its founding); second session — **Amritsar**
- **Jinnah** joined the Muslim League in **1913**
### 1909–1917
- **1909** — legal beginning of **separate electorates**; **1916** — high point of **Congress-League unity**
- **12 December 1911** — Delhi Durbar: annulment of the **partition of Bengal** announced through **King George V**; capital moved from **Calcutta to Delhi**
- ★ **1916** — **Lucknow Pact** between Congress and Muslim League
- **Lucknow session, 1916** — Congress-League alliance, **Moderate-Extremist reunion**, **Tilak's** return
- **Jinnah** — presided over the Muslim League session at **Lucknow, 1916**
- **20 August 1917** — **August Declaration**
### Later milestones
- **1930** — **Iqbal's** Allahabad address; **1933** — **Rahmat Ali** coined 'Pakistan'
- **1935** — Government of India Act prescribed an **All-India Federation**; the **1919 Act** brought provincial dyarchy and bicameralism at the Centre
- **1940** — **Lahore Resolution**: demanded **separate independent states for Muslims** (hence "Pakistan Resolution")
  - Moved by **A.K. Fazlul Haq** (Premier of Bengal); **Jinnah** presided
- **16 August 1946** — **Direct Action Day**; large riots in **Calcutta**
- **Liaquat Ali Khan** — Pakistan's first Prime Minister

## SEC 1:205:1 | Who's who
- **Lord Minto** — Viceroy; "**Father of the Communal Electorate**" (Morley-Minto Reforms gave separate electorates for Muslims)
- **John Morley** — **Secretary of State for India** (Indian Councils Act, 1909)
- **Satyendra Prasanna Sinha** — first Indian member of the Viceroy's Executive Council (**1909**), as **Law Member**
- **Tilak and Jinnah** — principal negotiators of the **Lucknow Pact** (with the League's Young Party)
- Early Muslim League aims — **loyalty** to the British Empire, protection of Muslim political interests, separation from the Congress; **not** armed revolt
- **Charter Act, 1833** — created the **Governor-General of India**
  - Pitt's India Act — **dual control**; 1813 Act — largely ended the Company's trade monopoly
- **Government of India Act, 1858** — ended Company rule; provincial **dyarchy** came only in **1919**

## SEC 1:205:2 | Indian Councils Act 1909
- **Aim** — rally the **Moderates** and reward **Muslim loyalty** (limited concessions, separate electorates)
- **Separate electorates for Muslims** — introduced by the **Indian Councils Act, 1909**; legislative councils enlarged
- **Indirect, multi-tier elections** — not direct universal elections
- Central Legislative Council — additional members up to **60**; **official majority** kept at the Centre
- Members could discuss the budget, ask supplementary questions, move resolutions, but **not vote** on the budget
- Compare the Acts:

| Act | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1892 | **budget discussion**, **indirect election** |
| 1909 | **separate Muslim electorates** |
| 1919 | **provincial dyarchy**, **direct elections** |
| 1935 | **provincial autonomy** |

## SEC 1:205:3 | Lucknow Pact terms
- Congress accepted **separate electorates for Muslims** — major concession
- Common goal — **self-government**; separation of executive and judiciary; elected majorities in provincial legislatures
- **Weightage** — Muslims in minority provinces got more seats than their population share
- Communal veto — bill barred if **three-fourths** of the affected community's members opposed it
- **Provincial Dyarchy (1919)** — subjects split into **reserved and transferred**; abolished by the **1935 Act**

## SEC 1:205:4 | Separate electorates extended
- **1919 Act** — communal representation extended to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans**
- **1935 Act** — extended to **depressed classes, women and labour**; also gave **provincial autonomy**

## SEC 1:205:5 | Exam corner
- ★ **Mohammad Iqbal** — first proposed a separate Muslim state; **Allahabad** League session, **1930**
  - Declared Hindus and Muslims **two separate nations**; Muslim state in the **North-West**
- **Chaudhry Rahmat Ali** — coined "PAKISTAN" in **1933**, pamphlet **Now or Never**
- **Ambika Charan Majumdar** — presided over the **Lucknow Congress session, December 1916**

- **Rowlatt Act** — **1919**, to crush revolutionary activity

## SEC 1:205:11 | British Acts and constitutional changes → provision / electoral feature, 1773-1947
### 1892 and 1909
- ★ **1892** — Indian Councils Act first introduced **indirect elections** (principle of representation)
- ★ **Morley-Minto Reforms** passed **1909** (Indian Councils Act, 1909); **Minto** replaced **Curzon** as Viceroy (1905)
- ★ **1909 Act** — **communal representation** / **separate electorates**: Muslim members elected only by **Muslim electorates**
  - **Lord Minto** — Father of Communal Electorate
- ★ **1909 Act** — most **short-lived** constitutional experiment; **Ramsay MacDonald**: incomplete agreement between democracy and bureaucracy
### 1919
- ★ Post of **Indian High Commissioner** — created by the **Government of India Act, 1919**
### 1935
- ★ **Provincial Autonomy** — introduced by the **Government of India Act, 1935**; Federal Legislature; bicameral legislature
- ★ **1935 Act** abolished **provincial dyarchy**; dyarchy started at the **Centre**
  - Not a feature: dyarchy at the Centre **as well as** in the Provinces
- ★ **India Council** abolished by the **1935 Act**
- ★ 1935 Act — Governor-General's Executive Council restructured with more Indian members; GG kept ultimate authority
- 1935 Act passed **August 1935** — longest British Act of Parliament to that time
