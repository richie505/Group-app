# ROW 2:26 | Veto powers and the ordinance-making power (Article 123); pardoning power (Article 72)
## SEC 2:26:0 | Veto by type of bill
- **Money Bill** — President may **assent or withhold** assent, but **cannot return** it for reconsideration
  - Money Bills are generally introduced with the President's **prior recommendation**
- **Constitutional Amendment Bill** — presidential assent **mandatory** under the **24th Amendment, 1971**; cannot reject or return it

## SEC 2:26:1 | Vetoes actually used
- **Pocket veto** possible because **Article 111** sets **no time limit** in India
  - The **10-day limit** (Bill becomes law if not decided) is a **US** feature, not Indian
- **Governor** has **no pocket veto** — Supreme Court (**2025**): indefinite inaction under **Article 200** is **justiciable**
- **Giani Zail Singh** — pocket veto on the **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986**

## SEC 2:26:2 | Ordinance: basis
- **Article 123** — President's ordinance power; **Article 213** — parallel power of the **Governor**
- Ordinance power is a relic of the **Government of India Act, 1935** (Governor-General's power to issue ordinances)
- Issued only when the legislature is **in recess** and the Head of State is satisfied immediate action is needed
  - None can be issued while **both Houses are sitting**
- Same **force, effect and limits** as an **Act of Parliament / State Legislature**; legislative, not executive
  - **Cannot amend the Constitution**
- **Article 13(2)** — ordinance is **'law'**, so void if it **abridges Fundamental Rights**
  - Can make or change **tax** provisions within legislative power
- **Article 85** — max **six-month** gap between two sessions of Parliament; **Article 174** — same for State Legislatures

## SEC 2:26:3 | Ordinance: can vs cannot
- Within legislative competence an ordinance **can**: amend an **ordinary Act**, operate **retrospectively**, change **taxes**
- An ordinance **cannot amend the Constitution** under **Article 368**

## SEC 2:26:4 | Life of an ordinance
- Must be **laid before the legislature** on reassembly
- Houses reassembling on different dates — **six weeks** counted from the **later** date

## SEC 2:26:5 | Satisfaction & judicial review
### Amendments
- **38th Amendment (1975)** — made the President's **satisfaction final**
- **44th Amendment** — **reversed** that position
- Ordinances are **subject to judicial review**
### Cases
| Case | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar** | **1987** | Routine **re-promulgation** without placing before the legislature = **fraud on the Constitution** (Bihar) |
| **Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar** | **2017** | Re-promulgation can be a **fraud on the Constitution**; **laying** before the legislature is **mandatory** |
### Criticism
- Frequent ordinances **bypass parliamentary deliberation**
- **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** — revived the debate on ordinance misuse and executive overreach

## SEC 2:26:6 | Exam corner
- **Constitutional Amendment Bill** — cannot be returned (assent mandatory)
  - The one-time return power can apply to **Financial Bills of Categories I and II**
- **US President** — only a **qualified veto**, overridden by a **two-thirds** majority of Congress
- MP's unexcused absence (**60 days**, **Article 101(4)**) — periods of **prorogation** and adjournments of **more than four consecutive days** are **left out**

## SEC 2:26:7 | Veto by type of bill and pocket veto
- ★ **Gyani Zail Singh** — exercised the **pocket veto** by withholding assent to the **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill** (**1986**)
- ★ Pocket veto = taking **no action** on a Bill (neither assenting nor returning it); used **only once** in India by the President
- ★ **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** — **absolute veto**, on Council of Ministers' advice, over the **PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954**

## SEC 2:26:8 | Ordinance (Article 123) — basis, conditions and force
- ★ **Article 123** — President promulgates ordinances during **recess** of Parliament
- ★ Ordinance has the **same force and effect** as an Act of Parliament
- ★ President **can withdraw** an ordinance **at any time** (so "can never repeal it" is wrong)
- ★ Promulgating ordinances is a **legislative power** of the President (an integral part of Parliament)
- ★ Ordinances must be approved by Parliament within **six weeks** of its reassembly

## SEC 2:26:9 | Ordinance — scope, limits, laying and life
- ★ **Article 123** — President's power to **promulgate ordinances** (and give assent to them)
  - ★ 2002 ordinance on **electoral reforms**, returned by the Union Cabinet unchanged — assented under **Article 123**
- ★ Ordinance power is available **except when both Houses are in session**, when the President is **satisfied** circumstances need immediate action
- ★ Scope of an ordinance = scope of Parliament's **ordinary legislative power** — can amend **any Central Act**, but **not the Constitution**

# ROW 2:27 | Vice-President; Prime Minister and the Union Council of Ministers - Articles 74, 75, 77, 78
## SEC 2:27:0 | Vice-President: office & election
### Office
- **Article 63** — creates the office; **Article 64** — VP is **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; **66** — election; **67** — term
- ★ **Vice-President** — ex-officio **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; **not a member** of the House
- Office modelled on the **Vice-President of the USA**
- Parliament = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**; the VP is **not a constituent part** of Parliament
### Election and qualifications
- ★ Elected by an electoral college of members of **both Houses of Parliament** (**Art. 66(1)**)
  - **All MPs, elected and nominated**; **PR-STV**, **secret ballot**; **State legislatures have no role**
- Qualifications: **citizen**, at least **35** years, qualified for election to the **Rajya Sabha** (not the Lok Sabha)
- Eligible for **re-election any number of times**
### Removal and Chair
- ★ Removal resolution can be proposed in the **Rajya Sabha only**
- Has a **casting vote** in a tie in the Rajya Sabha
- Does **not preside** while a resolution for his own removal is considered
- While **acting as President**: the **Deputy Chairman** presides over the Rajya Sabha; VP draws the **President's salary**, not the Chairman's
- **Article 67(a)** — VP resigns by writing to the **President**; takes effect **immediately**
### Others
- ★ **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy** — became President **without** having been Vice-President
- **39th Amendment (1975)** — removed the Supreme Court's jurisdiction over Presidential election disputes; **44th Amendment (1978)** — restored it
- **President** removed by **impeachment** (**Article 61**) — **14 days' notice**, **two-thirds of total membership** of each House

## SEC 2:27:1 | Oath, resignation, removal
### Oaths
| Office | Oath Article | Administered by |
|---|---|---|
| President | **Article 60** | **CJI** |
| Vice-President | **Article 69** | **President** |
| Governor | **Article 159** | High Court **Chief Justice** |
| Union Ministers | **Article 75(4)** (forms in **Third Schedule**) | **President** |
- Governor's oath is written into **Article 159** itself (like the President's and VP's), **not** the Third Schedule; the Third Schedule holds the other oaths
- State Ministers and CM — oath of office and secrecy under **Article 164(3)**
- Administering ministers' oaths is **not** a PM duty under **Article 78**
### Resignations and removal
- **Governor** resigns to the **President**
- **Speaker** resigns to the **Deputy Speaker** (**Article 94**)
- State Ministers dismissed by the Governor on the **Chief Minister's advice**
### Deputy PM
- **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** — first **Deputy PM**; office not in the Constitution; holder takes oath as an **ordinary Minister**

## SEC 2:27:2 | Prime Minister
### Appointment and membership
- ★ PM **appointed by the President** — **Article 75(1)**; other Ministers on the PM's advice; **75(2)** — hold office during the **President's pleasure**
  - By convention the leader of the **Lok Sabha majority**
- ★ PM is **generally a member of the Lok Sabha**, but not compulsory — may be from the **Rajya Sabha**
  - ★ **Indira Gandhi** — first PM from the Rajya Sabha at appointment (later joined the Lok Sabha)
- ★ **Chandra Shekhar** — Lok Sabha member (**Balia**) while PM (1990–91); **Deve Gowda**, **I.K. Gujral**, **Manmohan Singh** were not
- PM's oath administered by the **President**
- Non-member Minister (including PM) must enter either House within **six months**
- **UK PM** must belong to the **House of Commons**; **Indian PM** — **either House**
### Tenure and responsibility
- **No fixed tenure** — continues while enjoying the **Lok Sabha's confidence**; formally holds office at the **President's pleasure**
- PM and Council responsible **only to the Lok Sabha** (**Article 75(3)**), even if the PM is a Rajya Sabha member
- During a **National Emergency** the President remains **bound by the Council of Ministers' advice**
### Ministers in Parliament
- **Article 88** — Ministers may **speak** in both Houses and joint sittings
- A Minister can **vote only** in the House he belongs to
- **75(2)** pleasure tenure; **75(3)** collective responsibility; **75(5)** six-month rule
### Bodies chaired and duties
- ★ PM heads the **National Defence Committee**
- ★ PM presides over the **National Development Council**
- PM chairs **NITI Aayog**, **NDC**, **Inter-State Council**, and the **National Integration Council**
- PM's duties towards the President — **Article 78** (not Article 74)
- PM advises the President on **appointing Ministers** and **dissolving the Lok Sabha**; **not** on the CJI (collegium)
- **S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001)** — a non-member **cannot be repeatedly reappointed** Minister without getting elected
### Vice-President items
- VP — ex-officio **President of the Indian Council of World Affairs (ICWA)**
- **Article 68** — VP vacancy filled **as soon as possible**; **no six-month deadline** (unlike the President's)
- VP can **act as President** for at most **six months**

## SEC 2:27:4 | Size limit: 91st Amdt, 2003
- **91st Amendment (2003)** — **Article 75(1A)**: Council of Ministers incl. PM capped at **15%** of Lok Sabha's total strength
- **Article 75(1B)** — member disqualified for **defection** cannot be a **Minister until re-elected**
- **Article 164(1A)** (States) — 294-member Assembly: 15% = 44.1, so max **44** incl. CM (e.g. **West Bengal**)

## SEC 2:27:5 | Ranks of ministers (convention)
### Ranks
- Ranks of Ministers rest on **convention and the PM's choice**; the Constitution does not classify them
- **Cabinet Minister** — heads an important ministry, attends Cabinet meetings; **Deputy Minister** only **assists** a senior Minister
- **Union Cabinet** = **PM + Cabinet-rank Ministers** (about **15–20**); wider Council (about **60–80**) adds Ministers of State and Deputy Ministers
- Word **'Cabinet'** — absent from the original Constitution; inserted in **Article 352(3)** by the **44th Amendment (1978)** (Cabinet's written advice for an Emergency)
- PM as head of the Council: **selects** Ministers, **allocates portfolios**, can ask any Minister to **resign**
### Others
- **1956 Gentlemen's Agreement** — united AP cabinet: **60% Andhra**, **40% Telangana**
- Lok Sabha **quorum** — **one-tenth** of total membership

- **Prime Minister's Office** — not in the Constitution; **extra-constitutional staff agency** of the PM

## SEC 2:27:7 | Exam corner
- ★ PM has **full discretion** in choosing Ministers (**Article 75(1)**); a non-member chosen must become an MP
- **C.P. Radhakrishnan** — elected Vice-President after **Jagdeep Dhankhar** resigned (**July 2025**)

## SEC 2:27:8 | Vice-President — office, qualifications and election (Arts 63, 64, 66)
### Election
- ★ Elected by **both Houses of Parliament**, **PR-STV** (**Article 66**); **not appointed** by the President
- ★ **State Legislatures do not take part** in electing the VP
- ★ State Legislative Assembly takes part in electing the **President**, **Rajya Sabha** members and **Legislative Council** members
### Office
- ★ **Articles 64** and **89(1)** — VP is **ex-officio Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States)
- ★ VP is **not a member** of either House of Parliament
- ★ **Rajya Sabha** — presided over by one who is **not its member**
- ★ **Article 79** — Parliament = **President + Council of States + House of the People**; VP is **not an integral part**
### Removal
- ★ Removal resolution moved in the **Rajya Sabha alone**, passed by a **majority of its members**, then agreed to by the **Lok Sabha**
- ★ Requires **14 days'** prior notice

## SEC 2:27:9 | Vice-President vs President — oath, term, removal
- ★ **Article 67(b)** — VP removed by a resolution of the **Council of States** passed by a **majority of all the then members**, agreed to by the House of the People
- ★ Parliament = **House of the People + Council of States + President**
- ★ **Convention**, not written: PM **resigns if he loses majority** in the Lok Sabha (**Article 75** only says the President appoints the PM)

## SEC 2:27:10 | Acting President (Art. 65) and election disputes (Art. 71)
- ★ President resigns to the **Vice-President** (**Article 56(1)(a)**); VP immediately informs the **Speaker** (**Art 56(2)**)
- ★ Vacancy in the President's office (death, resignation, removal) filled within **six months**
- ★ VP elected for **five years**; ex-officio **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**
- ★ While discharging the President's duties, the VP does **not** perform the duties of **Chairman** of the Council of States
- ★ VP — **second-highest** constitutional office after the President (**Article 63**); acts as President during **casual vacancies**

## SEC 2:27:11 | Vice-Presidents → FIRSTs, records and mid-term vacancies
- ★ **Dr. S. Radhakrishnan** — **first** Vice-President (**1952–1962**); Ambassador to the **USSR** (**1949–1952**)
- ★ **Dr. Zakir Hussain** — **second** VP (**1962–1967**)

  - ★ **V.V. Giri** — High Commissioner to **Sri Lanka** (**1947–1951**); **K.R. Narayanan** — Ambassador to **China** (**1976–1978**)

- ★ PM has **full discretion** in choosing Ministers — President appoints them on PM's advice (**Article 75(1)**)

## SEC 2:27:12 | Prime Minister — appointment, oath and membership (Art 75)
- ★ **President** — "represents the nation but does not lead the nation"; real executive power is with the **PM**
- ★ **PM** — leader of the **Lok Sabha** (leader of the majority party); if not a Lok Sabha member he can nominate another
- ★ **Article 75(5)** — Minister not an MP for **six consecutive months** ceases to be a Minister
- ★ **Manmohan Singh** — PM **2004–2014**; **Rajya Sabha** member from **Assam** (first elected **1991**)

## SEC 2:27:13 | Prime Minister — powers and functions
### Bodies headed by the PM
- ★ **Planning Commission** (set up **1950**) — **PM** is its (ex-officio) **Chairman**
- ★ Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission — equivalent to a **Cabinet Minister**; first was **Gulzarilal Nanda**
- ★ **NDMA** — headed by the **PM**; created under the **Disaster Management Act** (**23 December 2005**)
### Conventions
- ★ Budget **not passed** by the Lok Sabha — PM submits the **resignation of the Council** (loss of majority)
- ★ PM **resigns on losing majority** in the Lower House — **convention**, not explicit in the Constitution

## SEC 2:27:14 | Union Ministers — appointment, tenure and status (Art 75)
- ★ A **Minister who is a Rajya Sabha member** can take part in Lok Sabha deliberations
- ★ **Vice-President** — a non-member who takes part in the proceedings of a House of Parliament
- ★ PM from the Upper House **cannot vote** in the Lok Sabha (can speak, but votes only in his own House)
- ★ **Giriraj Singh** (MP from **Begusarai**, Bihar) — Union Minister of **Textiles** after the **18th Lok Sabha** elections

## SEC 2:27:15 | Oaths, resignations and removal → executive heads and ministers
### Oaths and removal
- ★ Oaths: President — by **CJI**; VP — by the **President**; PM — by the **President**
- ★ **Speaker** — does not take an oath of office
- ★ **Article 67(b)** — VP removal resolution moved first only in the **Rajya Sabha**, then agreed to by the Lok Sabha
- ★ **Governor** — **no impeachment** provision; appointed by the President, holds office at his **pleasure**
  - ★ President removed by **impeachment** (**Article 61**)
### Executive
- ★ **Article 53(1)** — executive power of the Union vested in the **President**
- ★ PM — **appointed by the President**; **head of the Council of Ministers**
- ★ Convention: the person commanding **majority support in Parliament** is invited to head the government
- ★ **Article 54** — election of the **President**; **Article 75** — appointment of PM and Council of Ministers
- ★ A **non-member** can join the Cabinet for up to **6 months** (**Article 75(5)**) — membership is not restricted to MPs
### PM facts

- ★ **Chandra Shekhar** — **never a Chief Minister** before becoming PM

- ★ **Gandhi Peace Prize** jury includes the **PM**, the **CJI** and the **Leader of Opposition** in the Lok Sabha (not the President)
  - ★ Prize instituted in **1995** (**125th** birth anniversary of Gandhi); jury of **five**

## SEC 2:27:16 | Articles 74, 77, 78 → content
- ★ During a **National Emergency** the President does **not** acquire discretionary powers; acts on the **Council of Ministers'** advice
- ★ Real executive power in the parliamentary form — **Prime Minister** (**Article 75**)
- ★ All executive power vested in the **President**
- ★ Council of Ministers **collectively responsible** to the **House of the People** — a **constitutional obligation**, **Article 75(3)**
  - ★ **Article 74(3)** does **not exist**

## SEC 2:27:17 | Council of Ministers — size limit, 91st Amendment

## SEC 2:27:18 | Council of Ministers vs Cabinet; categories of ministers

- ★ **Article 75** — appointment of the Council of Ministers
  - ★ **75(1)** — President appoints PM, and other Ministers on the PM's advice; **75(2)** — office during the **President's pleasure**
- ★ **Article 77(3)** — business rules; the **President allocates portfolios** among Ministers on the **PM's advice**

## SEC 2:27:19 | Non-constitutional posts — Deputy PM, Leader of the House, PMO
- ★ **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** — first **Deputy PM** (**15 Aug 1947 – 15 Dec 1950**); also **Home Minister**; integrated **500+** princely states

# ROW 2:28 | Cabinet system, Cabinet committees, collective and individual responsibility; Attorney General of India
## SEC 2:28:0 | Parliamentary vs Presidential
- **Nominal and real executives**, dissolution of the lower House — **parliamentary** features
- **Separation of powers** — **presidential** system
- **Collective responsibility** of the Council of Ministers — clearest parliamentary marker
- Cabinet form — mechanism ensuring **collective responsibility of the Government to the people** (UPSC 2017 key)

## SEC 2:28:1 | Council of Ministers vs Cabinet
- Council of Ministers (**Articles 74, 75**) rarely meets as a body; the **Cabinet** meets regularly and takes **policy decisions**
- **"Sink or swim together"** — all ministers must **defend Cabinet decisions or resign**
- **Kitchen cabinet** criticised as an **extra-constitutional power centre** that may bypass the Cabinet

## SEC 2:28:2 | Responsibility
- **Article 75(3)** — collective responsibility to the **Lok Sabha**; **no-confidence motion** only there (even Rajya Sabha ministers answer to it)
  - **Article 164(2)** — State Council collectively responsible to the **Legislative Assembly**
- Defeat in the **Rajya Sabha** does **not** by itself force the ministry to resign
- **PM's death or resignation** — the whole **Council of Ministers** goes out of office
- **Article 75(2)** — Minister holds office during the **President's pleasure**; in practice PM can ask him to resign or advise dismissal
- **President** administers oaths of office and secrecy to Union Ministers (**Third Schedule**)
- **Oath of secrecy** — ensures confidentiality of Cabinet deliberations

## SEC 2:28:3 | Cabinet committees (8)
### The committees
- **Eight** Cabinet Committees (April 2025): **Appointments**, **Security**, **Political Affairs**, **Economic Affairs**, **Parliamentary Affairs**, **Accommodation**, **Investment and Growth**, **Employment and Skill Development**
- **Appointments** Committee — senior appointments; **Parliamentary Affairs** — government business and sessions
- PM chairs **Appointments**, **Security**, **Political Affairs**; **Accommodation** — chaired by a senior minister
- **Caste enumeration in the Census** — decided by the **Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs** (not CCEA)
### Related terms
- **Kitchen cabinet** — informal inner circle of the PM's trusted colleagues and friends (some senior ministers and non-ministers)
- **Cabinet dictatorship** — criticism of Cabinet dominance over Parliament in single-party majority eras
- **Chief Secretary** — State equivalent of the Cabinet Secretary
  - **G. Sai Prasad** — took charge as **AP Chief Secretary** in **February 2026**

## SEC 2:28:4 | Organisation of government
- **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar report (1949)** — reorganising the machinery of government (grouping of ministries, Cabinet Committees)
- **Department of Personnel** — came in **1970** out of the **1966 ARC**; placed **under the PM**
- Cabinet Committees — extra-constitutional, formed under the **Transaction of Business Rules, 1961** (**Article 77(3)**)
- **GoMs and EGoMs** abolished in **2014** (criticised for delay, diluting collective responsibility)
- **1989–2014** coalition era — **coordination committees** and **alliance partners' demands** weakened Cabinet cohesion

## SEC 2:28:5 | Cabinet Secretariat & Secretary
- **Cabinet Secretariat** — functions directly under the **PM**; serves Cabinet and its committees; administers the Business Rules
- **Cabinet Secretary** — appointed by the **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet**; senior-most civil servant
  - Term **two years**, extendable up to **four**
  - **First among equals** among Secretaries; **eleventh** in the Table of Precedence
  - Heads the **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC)**

## SEC 2:28:6 | Law officers
### Attorney General
- **Attorney General** — **highest law officer**; **Article 76**; appointed by the **President**
- **No fixed term** — holds office during the **President's pleasure**; by **convention** resigns when the **government changes**
- **M.C. Setalvad** — first Attorney General; **C.K. Daphtary** — first **Solicitor General**
- **Article 88** — AG can **speak** and take part in either House, but **cannot vote**
- **Article 105(4)** — AG enjoys **MPs' privileges** and immunities
- Performs legal duties referred by the **President** (not a Governor)
### Solicitor General and Advocate General
- **Solicitor General** — **statutory** (non-constitutional) post; appointed by the **ACC**; assists the AG
- **Advocate General** — **Article 165**; State's legal adviser; appointed by the **Governor**
- **Article 177** — Advocate General can **speak** in both Houses and sit on **committees**, but **cannot vote**
### Responsibility
- ★ Government's failure in the **Rajya Sabha** — **no consequence** for its survival; responsible to the Lok Sabha (**Article 75(3)**)

## SEC 2:28:7 | Exam corner

## SEC 2:30:32 | Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha — equal and unequal status
- ★ Equal status: considering the **CAG's report**; approving **emergency proclamations**
- ★ **Money Bills** — introduced only in the **Lok Sabha**
- ★ Lok Sabha's exclusive powers: **introducing** a Money Bill, its **rejection or amendment** (**Art. 109**), **accountability** of the Council (**Art. 75(3)**)
- ★ Rajya Sabha **cannot reject or amend** a Money Bill and **cannot vote** on **Demands for Grants**; it **can discuss** the Annual Financial Statement
- ★ Rajya Sabha is **never dissolved**
- ★ Lok Sabha maximum per this question — **552** (530 States + 20 UTs + 2 Anglo-Indians) [**Articles 81, 331**]

## SEC 2:30:33 | Special powers of Rajya Sabha — Articles 249, 312, 67(b)
- ★ **Article 109** — special procedure for **Money Bills**; a Money Bill **cannot be introduced** in the **Council of States**
- ★ Rajya Sabha returns a Money Bill within **14 days**
- ★ Impeachment of the President — can be initiated by **both Houses** (either)

## SEC 2:30:34 | Universal angles
- ★ Lok Sabha — **543** members at present
- ★ **79th Amendment (1999)** — extended **SC/ST** and **Anglo-Indian** reservation in the Lok Sabha and Assemblies by **10 years**
  - ★ Extended again by **10 years** by the **95th Amendment, 2009**
