## SEC 5:108:5 | In-situ vs ex-situ bioremediation
### In-situ vs ex-situ
- **In-situ** — treats contamination at the original site, **no excavation**; **most cost-efficient**, **passive**, but slow
- **Ex-situ** — **landfarming, composting, biopiles**; ex-situ **bioreactors** are fastest
### Techniques
- **Bioaugmentation** — adds selected pollutant-degrading **microorganisms**
- **Bioventing** — supplies air/nutrients to soil through wells
- **Biosparging** — injects air **below the water table**
- Landfarming — ex-situ land treatment, distinct from slurry treatment
### Limits
- Bioremediation is often **slow**; results and **reliability vary** with pollutant, organism, site and co-contaminants
### Stem cells
- **Mesenchymal stem cells** — first found in **bone marrow**; can become bone, cartilage and fat cells (this ability does not explain where they were found)

# ROW 5:109 | Eco-friendly technologies in agriculture - integrated pest management, biofertilisers, biopesticides, organic and natural farming
## SEC 5:109:0 | Sustainable vs harmful
- **Sustainable agriculture** — produce food while **protecting the environment** and conserving resources for **future generations**
- **IPM** — a sustainable practice; harmful: excess chemical fertiliser, pesticide overuse, **monoculture**
- **Organic farming** — main advantage: **reduced chemical input**, **improved soil health** (not guaranteed higher yields)
- **Bordeaux mixture** (fungicide) — copper sulphate + **calcium hydroxide (lime)**
## SEC 5:109:1 | IPM: keep pests below economic injury level
- **IPM** (FAO) — keep pests **below economic injury levels** using all suitable methods; **optimise**, not maximise, control
- In IPM, **chemical control** only when needed — not the first or routine line of defence

## SEC 5:109:3 | Fertiliser & manure facts
### Fertilisers
| Fertiliser | Role |
|---|---|
| **DAP** | **Balanced N and P** supply |
| Urea | Vegetative growth |
| Phosphatic | Roots, flowering, fruiting |
| Potassic | Quality |
- **Neem-coated urea** — slows release of nitrogen → better **nitrogen-use efficiency**
- Excess nitrogen — acidifies soil, leaches **nitrate** into groundwater, causes eutrophication
- Continuous chemical fertiliser use — **decline in soil organic matter**, fewer microbes, lower nutrient density in food
### Manures and soil
- **Green manure** — crop ploughed in while green; **legumes** preferred (fix nitrogen)
- Green manure adds **organic matter** (humus) and fixes nitrogen in **root nodules** → better soil structure and water retention
- **Biochar** — charcoal from **pyrolysis** of biomass; long-term **carbon sequestration**; water/nutrient retention
### Pests
- **Carbosulfan** — proposed ban: breaks down to highly toxic **carbofuran**, harms birds and aquatic life; Registration Committee under the **Insecticides Act, 1968**
- Biological IPM — **green lacewings** against **aphids**; pheromone traps are also IPM tools

# ROW 5:111 | Genetic engineering - recombinant DNA, GM crops (Bt cotton, Bt brinjal, GM mustard), gene editing/CRISPR
## SEC 5:111:0 | Steps of rDNA technology
- **Sequence** — **isolate DNA** → **cut** at specific sites (restriction enzymes) → **join** with DNA ligase (recombinant DNA) → **insert into host cell**
- **Restriction enzymes** (restriction endonucleases) — cut at **specific recognition sequences**, not at random
- **pBR322** — early, widely used **E. coli cloning vector**; DNA ligase — molecular stitcher; PCR — amplifies DNA
- **Gene cloning** — copying a desired gene/DNA fragment; whole-animal cloning copies an organism
- **PCR** — copies **small quantities of DNA** for analysis, diagnostics, research
## SEC 5:111:1 | Tools & vectors
### Tools
- **Agrobacterium tumefaciens** — "nature's genetic engineer"; **disarmed Ti plasmid** transfers **T-DNA** into the plant nucleus
- **Plasmids** — usually **circular, double-stranded** DNA
- Recombinant plasmid enters bacterium by **transformation**; bacteria multiply to yield DNA/product
- **Genetic library** — a collection of genetic material; biochips aid drug discovery
- Microbial production of useful products — **industry**; agriculture: GM crops; medicine: drugs, vaccines; research: biochips
### Biosafety
- **Gene-drive mosquitoes** — trait may spread through wild populations; **no simple recall** mechanism
- **Brazil-nut gene soybean** (for methionine) — never released, **allergenicity**
- **Horizontal gene transfer** — genes moving between **unrelated organisms** (e.g. antibiotic-resistance genes to pathogens)
## SEC 5:111:2 | Firsts
### Firsts
- First GMO — **1973**
- **Insulin** — first human hormone via rDNA; marketed as **Humulin**, **1982**
- Classic GMO pathway — **human insulin gene → bacteria → insulin**
- **Flavr Savr** tomato — **Calgene**, US, **1994**; first commercially sold GM food; **delayed ripening**, longer shelf life
- **CRISPR-Cas9** — demonstrated **2012**
- **Mutation breeding** — **non-GMO**; **71 Trombay** varieties linked to climate resilience
- **Transgenic organism** — carries a foreign gene from another species; purpose — a desired characteristic or product
### CRISPR
- **CRISPR** — Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats; specialised stretches of DNA
- **Cas9** — the **molecular scissors**; **guide RNA** — navigator, leads Cas9 to the target
- Uses — correct **genetic defects**, treat disease, **improve crops**; raises **ethical concerns**
