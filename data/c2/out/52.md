# ROW 2:108 | Kinship - descent, lineage, clan, kinship terminology; North-South Indian kinship contrast
## SEC 2:108:0 | Types & degrees of kin
- **Consanguineal** kinship — based on **blood** (e.g. uncle and nephew)
- **Affinal** kinship — based on **marriage** (e.g. son-in-law and father-in-law)
- Ritual / **fictive** kinship — not a biological tie (a father-son tie by birth is consanguineal, not fictive)
- Degrees: **8** primary, **33** secondary, **151** tertiary kin

## SEC 2:108:1 | Descent rules
- **Patrilineal** descent — **Kondhs, Santhals, Bhils**
- **Matrilineal** descent — **Khasi, Garo, Nair**
- **Double (bilineal)** descent — different attributes through each parent (e.g. movable property through mother, immovable through father)
- **Bilateral** descent — both sides treated equally
- **Parallel** descent — women to daughters, men to sons
- **Cross** descent — mother to son, father to daughter

## SEC 2:108:2 | Descent groups
- **Lineage** — unilineal descent group from a **known ancestor**; exogamous
- **Clan** — believes in a common ancestor without a demonstrated link
- **Phratry** — **two or more related clans**
- **Gotra** — exogamous Hindu clan
- **Moiety** — divides society into **two halves**

## SEC 2:108:3 | Key terms
- **Gotra** — exogamous Hindu clan; same-gotra marriage bar dates from the **Later Vedic** period
- **Sapinda** — kin within the **prohibited degrees** of blood relationship for marriage
### Residence and family
- **Matrilocal** — husband lives with the **wife's family**
- **Patrilocal** — wife lives with the husband's family
- **Avunculocal** — residence with the **maternal uncle**
- **Joint family** — **several generations** living together
- **Nuclear family** — parents and children

## SEC 2:108:4 | Kinship usages
- **Avoidance** — restraint, e.g. daughter-in-law and **father-in-law**
- **Joking relationship** — licensed teasing, e.g. a man and his **wife's younger sister**
- **Teknonymy** — addressing a person through a **third person**, usually a child ("father of my child")
- **Avunculate** — authority / special role of the **maternal uncle** (esp. matrilineal societies)
- **Amitate** — role of the **father's sister**
- **Couvade** — **husband imitates** his wife's pregnancy taboos; associated with the **Khasi** and **Toda**

## SEC 2:108:5 | North vs South kinship
### North India
- **Gotra and village exogamy** with **sasan / sapinda** marriage bars
- Brides go to **higher-status families** and break with the natal family
### South India
- **Cross-cousin** marriage preferred; **parallel cousins** barred
- Marriage with the **elder sister's daughter**, FZD or MBD preferred
- Continuity with the natal family
- Cross-cousin and **uncle-niece** marriage common in **Andhra Pradesh** — Telugu **menarikam**
### Forms of marriage
- **Endogamy** — a rule about the group within which one marries, not a form by number of spouses

## SEC 2:108:6 | Scholars
- **L. H. Morgan** — classificatory vs descriptive terminology; *Systems of Consanguinity and Affinity* (**1871**)
- Kinship terms by **use**: terms of **address** and **reference**
- Kinship terms by **structure**: **elementary, derivative, descriptive**
- **Irawati Karve** — *Kinship Organization in India* (**1953**); **four** zones: North, Central, South, East
- Karve — "**Mother of Indian Kinship Studies**": **first to map** Indian kinship systematically
- **Dumont** — **no sharp line** between consanguines and affines in South India

## SEC 2:108:7 | Exam corner
- Uncle–niece (maternal uncle and sister's daughter) — **consanguineous** (blood tie)
- Husband–wife and brothers-in-law — **affinal** (linked by marriage)
- India's National Family Planning Programme (**1952**) — **first in the world**, hence first in Asia

## SEC 2:109:0 | Varna vs jati
- **Varna** — theoretical **four-fold** scheme associated with the **Vedas**
- **Jati** — actual **local / regional** groups; thousands of sub-castes (not exactly four)

## SEC 2:109:7 | Exam corner

# ROW 2:110 | Caste in modern India - dominant caste, sanskritisation, caste associations, caste and politics
## SEC 2:110:0 | Key concepts
- **Sanskritisation** — lower caste adopts rituals, **vegetarianism**, **sacred thread** of the twice-born; **positional**, not structural, change
- Colonial **Census from 1871** ranked castes → groups formed associations and sought higher classification
- **Caste association** — **voluntary**, membership-based body acting as a **pressure group**
- **Samayas** — caste associations of the **Kakatiya** era

## SEC 2:110:1 | Dominant caste (Srinivas)

- **Mahuva** — I.P. Desai's study of joint family jointness

- Later additions — **Western education**, administrative jobs, urban income
- ★ Pre-independence **Bihar** dominant castes — **Bhumihar, Rajput, Kayastha** (not **Kurmi**)
- Medieval Andhra — **Reddys, Kammas, Velamas, Kapus** Kshatriyaised through the **Nayankara** system

## SEC 2:110:3 | Forces of change

## SEC 2:110:4 | Census & caste counts

## SEC 2:110:5 | State surveys

## SEC 2:110:10 | Changes in caste and caste in politics — forces → effects

## SEC 2:113:2 | Westernisation vs modernisation

## SEC 2:113:4 | Cultural lag (Ogburn)

- **W.F. Ogburn** — **cultural lag**, in *Social Change* (**1922**)

- Lagging factors — **religion, law, tradition** (not politics)
- Cultural lag — **delay** in adjusting to change
- Example — fast spread of **digital technology**, slower norms on children's online safety (**DPDP Act, 2023**: parental consent under 18)
- **Xenophobia** — fear of foreigners
### Sources of change
- **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955** — role of **law**
- **Media** — creates **awareness**, forms **public opinion**, drives change, **cultural diffusion**
- ★ New development model for developing societies — **non-material** indicators (quality of life, equity, wellbeing)
- **Ethnic group** — a group sharing a **common culture** (clan, lineage, sapinda are kinship groups)

# ROW 2:114 | Social stratification and mobility in India
## SEC 2:114:0 | Theories of stratification
| Approach | Thinker |
|---|---|
| **Functionalist** | **Kingsley Davis and Wilbert Moore** |
| Conflict | Marx |
| Multi-dimensional | Weber |
| Ideological (caste) | Dumont |
- **Davis and Moore** — unequal rewards draw **scarce talent** into functionally important positions
- **Melvin Tumin** — stratification **ancient and universal** (not a recent industrial phenomenon)
- Tumin's critique — stratification **wastes talent** and transmits inequality to the next generation
- **Weber** — **class, status, party (power)**; class position and market situation decide **life chances**
- **Marathas, Gujjars, Patels** seek OBC status — pursuit of greater **life chances**
- **Louis Dumont** — *Homo Hierarchicus*, purity-pollution principle
- Ghurye — listed features of caste; Srinivas — coined Sanskritisation and dominant caste

## SEC 2:114:1 | Systems of stratification
| System | Setting |
|---|---|
| **Estate** | **Medieval feudal Europe** |
| Caste | Traditional India; birth-based |
| Slavery | Ancient Greece and Rome; person as property |
| Class | Industrial societies; achieved status |
- **Harappan** society — differentiation (citadel and lower town), no rigid caste; varna crystallised in **Later Vedic** period
- Caste — based on **birth** and endogamy; occupation **inherited**, not freely chosen
- Hereditary caste occupations — **blacksmith**, potter, weaver (IAS officer, doctor, scientist are achieved)
- Class system — **open**, so social mobility is possible
- Sanskritisation (copying upper-caste practices) and dominant caste (economic and political power) — both true, unrelated definitions

## SEC 2:114:2 | Types of mobility
- **Intra-generational** — status change within one person's **lifetime**
- **Inter-generational** — change across generations
- **Upward** (vertical) — e.g. a farmer's son becoming an **IAS officer**
- **Horizontal** — change at the **same level**
- **Structural** — from changes in the **occupational structure**
- **Occupational mobility** — leaving the family's work; weakens **hereditary occupations**
- Caste: **vertical** = ranking by birth; **horizontal** = division into separate caste groups
- **Caste status** — position in the caste hierarchy, fixed by **birth**
- **Srinivas's** dominant caste — numerical strength, sizeable arable land, **fairly high ritual rank**
- **Kshatriyaisation** (Kulke) — martial / landholding groups claiming **Kshatriya** status
- Kshatriyaisation among **Marathas, Jats, Yadavs** — brings mobility but **reproduces the hierarchy**

## SEC 2:114:3 | Ascribed vs achieved
- **Ascribed** status — **caste, gender, family background**
- **Achieved** status — earned; **IAS officer, doctor, scientist**
- Caste status — linked with **occupation**, a **fixed** position, from **birth** (not effort)
- **Horizontal mobility** — clerk moving to an **equal-rank** job
- **Intergenerational mobility** — labourer's son becoming an engineer
- **Sanskritisation** — adopting **upper-caste customs**
- **Westernisation** — adopting Western values and ways

## SEC 2:114:4 | Thorner's agrarian classes
- **Daniel Thorner** — agrarian classes by **nature of land rights** and **type of income**
- Dominant caste — not ritual rank alone; also **numerical strength** and **arable land**

## SEC 2:114:5 | Scholars & books
### Concepts
- **Social stratification** — hierarchical ranking by unequal **wealth, prestige, power**
- Bases — **caste** (traditional); **political power**, **urbanisation** (modern)
- Caste status feature — **fixed social position**; education, urbanisation, globalisation widen achieved status
- **Ethnocentrism** — viewing own culture as **superior**; casteism — loyalty to own caste

# ROW 2:115 | Rural, urban and tribal society in India - features and interlinkages
## SEC 2:115:0 | Village vs town
- **Village** — **agricultural**, small and sparse, **homogeneous**, primary face-to-face relations, **informal control**
- Village-to-town transition begins with **traders and manufacturers** (not wholly dependent on village resources)
- **Tribal society** — relatively isolated, kinship-based; **subsistence economy**; **common control** of land and forest
- **Peasant society** — part of wider caste- and class-stratified, market-linked society; individual land, tenancy

## SEC 2:115:1 | Features & traps
- Rural community elements — **territory**, **we-feeling**, relative **self-sufficiency**, small size, **homogeneity** (not cultural diversity; UPPSC 2021)
- Diversity and heterogeneity — **urban** traits
- Urban life — **competition**, **impersonal** relations, **anonymity**, weak social control (not informal ties)

# ROW 2:116 | Casteism - meaning, causes, manifestations and remedial measures
## SEC 2:116:0 | Caste vs casteism
- **Casteism** — **discrimination and prejudice** based on caste; concentration of power, money, prestige in a few castes
- **Caste** — a **social structure** of hereditary, endogamous identity
- Casteism — **partisan loyalty** to one's own caste and using caste for gain

## SEC 2:116:1 | Causes of casteism
- **Endogamy** — keeps caste boundaries and "purity" intact
- Only about **5%** of Indian marriages are inter-caste
- Electoral politics — caste-based **vote banks**; caste associations as pressure groups
- Urban migrants carry caste prejudice — caste networks, associations, caste stickers on vehicles; identity does **not disappear**

## SEC 2:116:2 | Thinkers & reformers
- **Kaka Kalelkar** — casteism "an overriding, **blind and supreme group loyalty**"
- **B.R. Ambedkar** — caste is a division of labour and a "**division of labourers**"; splits the working class
- Ambedkar — *Annihilation of Caste* (**1936**)
- **Rajni Kothari** — *Caste in Indian Politics* (**1970**): politics **politicises** caste, not just uses it
- **Periyar** — jailed in **Vaikom Satyagraha** (**1924**); **Self-Respect Movement** from **1925**
- **Sree Narayana Guru** — **Aruvippuram** Shiva consecration (**1888**); "**One Caste, One Religion, One God** for Man"; SNDP Yogam

## SEC 2:116:3 | Caste in politics
- Casteist element in electoral politics — traced to **Minto-Morley Reforms, 1909** (separate electorates)
- **Samajwadi Party** and **Rashtriya Janata Dal** — from backward-caste mobilisation
- **Justice Party** (Andhra) — mobilised **Reddys, Kammas, Kapus, Velamas** against Brahmin dominance; won the **Communal G.O.** in **1921**; ground for their post-independence dominance
- Post-**Karamchedu** Dalit mobilisation — through independent **Dalit Mahasabhas** (not the Justice Party)
- Caste census debate — **ossify** caste identities or **expose** existing inequity

## SEC 2:116:4 | Laws & cases

|---|---|

| Article 15 | Bars caste discrimination |

# ROW 2:117 | Communalism - meaning, causes, forms and growth in India
## SEC 2:117:0 | Fundamentalism vs communalism
- **Religious fundamentalism** — mainly **doctrinal**; return to fundamentals, **literal scripture**
- **Communalism** — **political mobilisation** of religious identity against other communities for power and resources
- Fundamentalism can exist **without** inter-group conflict
- Fundamentalism features — group **exclusiveness**, literal reading of scripture, conservatism, resistance to change
- Causes of fundamentalism — **identity crisis**, **social insecurity** (with globalisation); exclusiveness is a feature, not a cause

## SEC 2:117:1 | Scholars & leaders
- **Ram Ahuja** — communalism an **ideology**: religious communities with differing, often **opposing interests**; communal persons practise politics through religion
- **Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe** — "the **political assertiveness** of a community to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation"
- **Bipan Chandra** — followers of one religion share **common social, political and economic interests**
- **Lord Minto** — "**Father of the Communal Electorate**" (separate electorates for Muslims)

## SEC 2:117:2 | Bipan Chandra's three stages
- **Bipan Chandra** — communalism: one religion = common social, political, economic interests
- Stages — **communal nationalism** → **liberal communalism** → **extreme communalism**
- **Liberal** — interests different but **reconcilable** within one nation
- **Extreme** — interests **mutually antagonistic**
- After **1937** elections — Muslim League to extreme communalism based on **fear, hatred and violence**; echoed by Hindu Mahasabha and RSS

## SEC 2:117:3 | Oommen's six dimensions
- **Secessionist** — independent state outside India, e.g. **Khalistan**
- **Separatist** — territorial state **within India**, e.g. **Gorkhaland**, **Bodoland**
- **Retaliatory** — attempts to **harm** members of other communities (riots, revenge cycles)
- **Welfarist** — works for one community's welfare
- **Political** — uses religion for votes
