# ROW 3:14 | Economic growth vs economic development; measures of development
## SEC 3:14:0 | Growth vs development
### Concepts
- **Economic growth** — quantitative concept; rise in actual output
- **GDP / GNP growth** — measures of economic growth
- **Development** — **broader** scope than growth (not narrower)
- **GDP alone** cannot measure development — development also covers health and education
  - **HDI** captures these through life expectancy, education and income
- **Per capita real income** — **oldest** measure of development; may conceal how income is distributed
### Examples
- **Kerala** — **2nd** in HDI among States but **7th** in per capita NSDP: a lower-income State can do better in human development
- **Swarna Andhra @2047** targets: GSDP **₹308 lakh crore**, per capita income **₹55 lakh**, growth **above 15%**, tracked through **651 KPIs**

## SEC 3:14:1 | Models & indicators
### Models
| Model / view | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Gandhian** model | **decentralisation** and **village industry** |
| **Kerala** model | high human development at modest income |
| **Western** model | **economic growth** through rising GDP and **industrialisation** |
| **Trickle-down** approach | benefits of growth reach poorer sections **automatically** |
- **Capability approach** — broader than GDP
- **Directive Principles** — development objectives: **adequate livelihood** and **reduction of inequality** (not merely higher national income)
### New view of development
- **Dudley Seers** — *The Meaning of Development* (**1969**)
- Emphasises reduction in **poverty, unemployment and inequality** — not the simple growth rate alone
- Rising real GNP need not mean development if **poverty and unemployment** increase
### Indicators
- Valid indicators: **institutional change**, **structural transformation**, **inclusive growth**
- **Not** an indicator: a larger share of GDP from the **primary sector**
- **Structural transformation** — shift from **agrarian** activity towards **industrial and service** activities
- **Higher ICOR** — more capital per unit of extra output (capital less efficient) → **lower growth** for a given investment rate
  - growth = saving rate / capital-output ratio
- **Green economy** (UNEP) — **low-carbon, resource-efficient, socially inclusive**; not limited to agriculture

# ROW 3:16 | Human Development Index - components, methodology, India's rank and trend
## SEC 3:16:0 | Origin & idea
### Origin
- First **Human Development Report** — **1990**, published by **UNDP**; based on **Mahbub-ul-Haq's** human development approach
- HDR compares countries on **education, health and per-capita income**
- **GDP** — primarily an **economic** measure; HDI measures human development; MPI multidimensional poverty
- **MPI** first included in the HDR in **2010**; OPHI works with UNDP on the MPI
### The idea
- Human development approach — **broader** than an economic measure; focuses on **people, opportunities and choices**
- **Income (GNI)** is a **means** of development; higher GNI per capita does **not** guarantee a higher HDI
- **Malaysia** has higher GNI per capita than **Chile**, but **Chile** has the higher HDI (better life expectancy, schooling) — policy choices matter
- **Martha Nussbaum** — extended Sen's capability approach with a list of **central human capabilities**
- **Human capital formation** — investment in **education, health, skills** (on-the-job training), **migration and information**
### What HDI leaves out
- HDI components: life expectancy, schooling indicators, GNI per capita — **not environmental quality**
- HDI does **not** capture **human security**, inequality, poverty, empowerment, participation or environment
- India's HDI **0.685** (HDR 2025) — **medium** category (0.550–0.699), just below the high threshold of **0.700**

## SEC 3:16:1 | Dimensions & goalposts
| Indicator | Goalposts |
|---|---|
| Life expectancy | **20–85 years** |
| Expected years of schooling | **0–18** |
| Mean years of schooling | **0–15** |
- **Education index** — **arithmetic mean** of expected-years and mean-years schooling sub-indices
- **Dimension index** = (actual − minimum) / (maximum − minimum)
  - e.g. actual 50, min 20, max 80 → (50−20)/(80−20) = **0.50**
- **MPI** measures multidimensional poverty; HDI measures human development

## SEC 3:16:2 | Method
- ★ HDI indicators: **income level, education, life expectancy** — not condition of environment
- **Logarithm of GNI per capita** — reflects **diminishing returns** to income
- Since **2010**, HDI = **geometric mean** of its three dimension indices — a strong dimension cannot fully offset a weak one
- **IHDI** is **lower** than HDI whenever inequality exists
- ★ HDI 2022 (HDR 2021/22, for 2021), descending: **Hong Kong (4th) > Australia (5th) > Japan (19th) > Russia (52nd)**

# ROW 3:17 | Related UNDP indices - IHDI, Gender Inequality Index, Gender Development Index, Multidimensional Poverty Index
## SEC 3:17:0 | Year introduced
| Index | Year |
|---|---|
| **GDI / GEM** | **1995** |
| ★ **HPI** | **1997** |
| **MPI / GII / IHDI** | **2010** |
- **HPI** (HDR 1997) — deprivation in **longevity, knowledge, decent standard of living**; complemented the HDI

## SEC 3:17:1 | Index → meaning
- **IHDI** — discounts HDI for unequal distribution; **lower than HDI** whenever inequality exists
- **GDI = 1** — **gender parity** (health, education, earned income)
- **GII** — introduced in HDR **2010** to address GDI's shortcomings; **higher GII = more inequality**
- National MPI: **MPI-poor** if deprived in at least **one-third** of weighted indicators
- **Alkire-Foster** method: **MPI = headcount ratio (H) × intensity (A)** — hence small values (e.g. India 0.066)
  - not a sum; not a geometric mean (that is HDI); not a $1-a-day income measure

## SEC 3:17:2 | Dimensions & indicators
- **Capability Poverty Measure** indicators — **child underweight, unattended births, female illiteracy** (not male labour-force participation)
- **GII** reproductive health — **maternal mortality ratio** and **adolescent birth rate**
- Global MPI dimensions:
  - health — **nutrition, child mortality**
  - education — **years of schooling, school attendance** (not living standards)
- **National MPI** — **12** indicators (Global MPI **10**); same three dimensions and Alkire-Foster method
  - health **3**, education **2**, standard of living **7**

## SEC 3:17:3 | Global vs National MPI
- **Global MPI** — **UNDP and OPHI** (Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative)
- **National MPI** — **NITI Aayog** with UNDP and OPHI
- National MPI standard-of-living indicators: cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets, **bank accounts**

# ROW 3:19 | Environmental degradation as a development challenge; green accounting and green GDP
## SEC 3:19:0 | Functions of the environment
- ★ Environment supplies **both renewable and non-renewable** resources — "renewable only" is incorrect
- ★ **Recycling** — not a function of the environment
- ★ "It **disorganizes waste** that benefits human life" — not a function of the environment

## SEC 3:19:1 | Key terms
- **Absorptive capacity** — ability to absorb degradation and neutralise pollutants without harm
- **Resource depletion** — use **faster than regeneration**
- **Trade-off view** — "**grow first, clean up later**" (Environmental Kuznets Curve logic)
- **Green GDP** — conventional GDP adjusted for **resource depletion and environmental degradation**
  - conventional GDP ignores depletion, so growth can hide loss of **natural capital**
- **Uttarakhand** — first State to adopt green GDP as **Gross Environment Product (GEP)**
- **Green economy** — fosters **green industries and green jobs**; decouples development from resource use and pollution

## SEC 3:19:2 | Green Revolution damage
- HYV crops depleted the **water table** through their **irrigation requirements**
  - monoculture and chemicals hurt soil fertility and biodiversity
- **AP Common Zoning Regulations 2026** — development-restricted: **forests, hills, water bodies, eco-sensitive areas, CRZ**

## SEC 3:19:3 | Causes
- India's low **EPI** score — **urban particulate pollution and coal-based power**
- India's two environmental threats — **poverty-induced degradation** and **pollution from affluence and rapid industrial growth**
- Human-driven causes: groundwater over-extraction, industrial emissions, urban expansion — **wildlife conservation** is not one
- A **sustainably managed forest** retaining biodiversity — **not** resource depletion

## SEC 3:19:4 | Green accounting
- **Expert Group on Green National Accounts** — chaired by **Sir Partha Dasgupta**; set up **2011**; reported **2013**
- **NEW** — **Paul Samuelson**; **MEW** — **Nordhaus and Tobin**
- India's environment accounts (UN **SEEA**) — compiled by **MoSPI**; ecosystem and natural-asset accounts since **2018**

## SEC 3:19:5 | Policy tools
- **EIA** in India — first in **1978** for **river valley projects**; EIA Notification **1994**, revised **2006**
- **Green Credit Rules, 2023** — under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**

## SEC 3:19:6 | Data
- More than **60%** of the world's rivers dammed or diverted; about **40%** of land degraded; about one-third transformed
- Largest land restored 2011-2020 — **Telangana** (**4.18 Mha**); AP among the top five; total **21.76 Mha**
- World Bank (**2013**) — India's annual cost of environmental degradation about **5.7% of GDP** (**Rs 3.75 lakh crore**, for 2009)
  - mechanism: **higher healthcare spending** and **lower labour productivity**
- **Green Index** — developed by the **World Bank**
  - MoSPI compiles India's SEEA-based accounts and **EnviStats India**
