# ROW 4:68 | Linguistic groups of India - language families, Eighth Schedule languages, bilingualism
## SEC 4:68:0 | Language families
### Indian families and their areas
| Family | Area in India | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Indo-Aryan** (Indo-European) | north, west and east | Kashmiri (**Dardic** branch), **Halbi** (Bastar), **Gor Boli / Lambadi**, Marathi |
| **Dravidian** | mainly **South India** (not North) | Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam; Kui, Gondi, Kurukh |
| **Austro-Asiatic (Austric)** | **central-east India** and **Meghalaya** | Santhali, Mundari, Ho, Khasi |
| **Sino-Tibetan** | north-east and Himalaya | **Bodo** (Tibeto-Burman) |
- **Dardic** is Indo-Aryan, not Sino-Tibetan
- **Austro-Asiatic** branches — **Munda** (Santhali, Mundari, Ho) and **Mon-Khmer** (Khasi)
- Most tribal languages of **central and eastern India** (Jharkhand-Odisha-West Bengal belt) are **Austric**; Dravidian covers only some central pockets (Gondi, Kui, Kurukh)
- **Kui** — **Dravidian**, of the **Kandhas** of Odisha (not Austro-Asiatic)
- **Khasi** (Mon-Khmer) — evidence that the **Meghalaya plateau** was once part of **Peninsular India**
- **Gor Boli / Lambadi** — Indo-Aryan, spoken by **Banjaras** across several States; **no script** of its own
- **Romani** (Roma/Gypsies) — related to **Indian Indo-Aryan** languages
### Dravidian branches
- **Telugu** — **South-Central Dravidian**
- Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam — Southern; Kui, Gondi, Kurukh — Central or North Dravidian
### Shares and Census 2011
- Older **Tibeto-Burman** estimate — about **0.85%** (20% was the older **Dravidian** figure)
- Census 2011: **Hindi 43.6%**, then **Bengali 8.0%**, Telugu 6.7%, Tamil 5.7%
- Smallest scheduled languages — **Sanskrit, Bodo and Manipuri**; largest — Hindi, Bengali, Marathi
### Telugu and others
- **Telugu** — the "**Italian of the East**"
- Base of standard Telugu — **Coastal Andhra**, especially the **Krishna-Godavari** region
- **Meghalaya** official languages — English, with **Khasi and Garo**
### World families
- **Indo-European** — largest by speakers (about **46%** of humanity)
- **Niger-Congo** — most languages; includes the **Bantu** languages (Sub-Saharan Africa)
- **Austronesian** — second by number of languages (Sino-Tibetan is second by speakers)

# ROW 4:69 | Migration - types, streams, causes and consequences; internal migration in India
## SEC 4:69:0 | Types of migration
| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| **Will** | voluntary or forced |
| Distance | short or long |
| Number | individual or mass |
| Cause | economic, social, political, religious |
| **Time** | e.g. **permanent** |
| **Administrative boundary** | internal or international |
- **Refugee** — forced out by fear of persecution (race, religion, nationality, social or political group); cannot return safely
- **Information networks** (e.g. **Keralites and Sikhs**) influence migration
- Adults — most mobile; drought and conflict push migration
## SEC 4:69:1 | Migration streams
- **Rural to rural** — largest internal stream; contributed most in 2011
- **Rural to urban** — dominated by **males moving for employment**; drives urbanisation
- Migration — main component of rural-urban growth difference, as **natural increase differs little** between them
- Record remittances **US$135 billion** (FY 2024–25) — increasingly from **skilled professionals in developed countries**
- **PDOT** (Jan 2018) — **MEA** programme for emigrants to **ECR** (Emigration Check Required) countries; about 1,72,220 trained by 31 Dec 2024
## SEC 4:69:2 | Push vs pull
- **Political instability** — push factor (not stability); monsoon failure, poor rural infrastructure — push
- **Education** — pull factor
- AP seasonal labour — from drought-prone **Rayalaseema** and **north-coastal** districts
- **Srikakulam** fishermen — seasonal migration to the **Gujarat coast**
- Migration from **Uttarandhra** to end with **Bhogapuram airport** (Chief Minister)
## SEC 4:69:3 | Data
### Definitions and surveys
- **Usual Place of Residence** (PLFS/NSO) — at least **6 months**
- Census — place of birth (lifetime migrants) and place of last residence
- Revised MoSPI **short-term migration** — **15 days–6 months** (earlier 1–6 months)
- **Nationwide Migration Survey** (MoSPI, July 2026–June 2027) — first full migration survey since **2007–08**
### Figures
- **PLFS 2020-21** — overall migration rate **28.9%**; female 47.9%, male 10.7%
- **Census 2011** (last residence) — **455.8 million**, about **37.6%**; 141.2 million moved in 2001-11
- Intra-state rural-to-rural — dominated by **women**, mainly **marriage**; inter-state rural-to-urban — men, for work
- Rural-urban migration encouraged by **lack of rural employment**, high urban labour demand, unbalanced development
- Major in-migration States — **Delhi, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala**
### Schemes and global
- **ONORC** — NFSA ration portability in all **36** States/UTs
- **e-Shram** — 22 languages via **BHASHINI**; One-Stop-Solution linked to 15 central schemes
- **Global Compact for Migration** — endorsed by the **UN General Assembly**; non-binding; international migration only
- **World Migration Report** — by **IOM**; **biennial** since 2000 (not annual); 2026 is the 13th edition
## SEC 4:69:4 | Theories & proponents
- **Everett S. Lee** — **Push-Pull Theory**, "A Theory of Migration" (**1966**); not demographic transition
- **Wilbur Zelinsky** — **mobility transition** model
- **Ravenstein** — laws of migration; **Griffith Taylor** — migration zone theory
- Demographic transition — Notestein, Thompson; diet-based population theory — Doubleday
- Forced migration — persecution, development-induced displacement, exploitation; **weighing push and pull** is voluntary migration
## SEC 4:69:5 | Consequences
- Source area left with an **ageing population** and **more women**
- **Social remittances** — transfer of **skills, knowledge and ideas** by migrants
- "**Son of the soil**" — hostility towards migrants in the destination (e.g. against "Bihari bhaiya")
- COVID-19 lockdown — about **122 million** jobs lost; exposed lack of social security for migrants
## SEC 4:69:6 | Laws & schemes
- **Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RECS) Act, 1979** — subsumed in the **OSH Code, 2020**
  - Registration if **5** or more inter-State migrant workers; displacement allowance **50%** of monthly wages plus journey allowance
- **OSH Code, 2020** — covers self-migrating workers, **annual travel allowance**, portability of PDS and social security (no abolition of contractor licensing)
- Four Labour Codes — consolidated **29** laws; in force **November 2025**
- **Global Compact for Migration** — adopted at **Marrakech**, **December 2018**
## SEC 4:69:7 | Diaspora & AP
- About **3.5 million** Indians in the **UAE**; India's diaspora — world's largest, over **35 million**
- India **2024** — top remittance recipient, about **US$137 billion**, only country above US$100 billion
- AP Gulf migration source districts — **Anantapur, Kadapa, Chittoor** (domestic workers, unskilled labour)
- **Konaseema** to GCC (about 15,000 by Dec 2025) — over 70% **women**, mostly **housemaids**
- **APNRT** (AP Non-Resident Telugu Society) — Gulf help desk for stranded Telugus (Mar 2026)
- Nationwide Migration Survey — run by **NSO/MoSPI**
## SEC 4:69:8 | Exam corner
- **UAE** — highest share of international migrants in its population
- **Europe** — largest number of migrants; **Oceania** — highest percentage
## SEC 4:69:12 | Migration theories and terms → proponent
- ★ **Everett Lee** — migration theory; migration as a permanent or semi-permanent change of residence
## SEC 4:69:16 | Acts, codes, schemes and compacts on migrants — Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act 1979
- ★ **Garib Kalyan Rojgar Abhiyaan** (GKRA) — launched **2020** for returnee migrant workers
## SEC 4:69:18 | Acts, codes, schemes and compacts on migrants — Global Compact for Migration (GCM)
- ★ **GCM** — first intergovernmental agreement under UN auspices covering all dimensions of international migration
- ★ Endorsed by the **UN General Assembly** through a **vote** on **19 December 2018** (not adopted by all UN countries)

# ROW 4:70 | Urbanisation in India - trends, class-size of towns, million-plus cities, urban agglomerations
## SEC 4:70:0 | Urban share by Census
| Census | Urban share |
|---|---|
| **1901** | **10.84%** |
| 1931 | 11.99% |
| 1961 | 17.97% |
| 2001 | 27.81% |
| 2011 | 31.16% |
- Phases — 1901-31 slow, 1931-61 medium, **rapid from 1961** (78.94 to 377.11 million)
- Towns — **1,827** (1901), 2,049 (1931), **5,161** (2001), **7,935** (2011)
## SEC 4:70:1 | The 2001-11 jump
- 2001-11 — first time since Independence urban **absolute increase** (about **91 million**) exceeded rural
- Urban growth 2001-11 — 286 to 377.1 million, about **31.8%** (not 41.8%)
- Principal reason — **reclassification** of rural areas as urban; **census towns 1,362 to 3,894**
- Statutory towns 2001-11 — **3,799 to 4,041**
- Three components of urban growth — natural increase, net rural-urban migration, **reclassification**
- **EAC-PM** — about **63%** urbanised (2015), from **satellite data**; World Bank (2010) — 55.3%
- Urban population by **2031** — about **60 crore**
- **Urban Challenge Fund** — competition-based, **₹1 lakh crore**
- **PMAY-U 2.0** — **1 crore** families, **₹2.30 lakh crore**, up to 2028-29
## SEC 4:70:2 | Class-size of towns
| Class | Population |
|---|---|
| I | 1,00,000 and above |
| **II** | **50,000–99,999** |
| V | 5,000–9,999 |
| VI | below 5,000 |
- "**Top-heavy**" — **Class I** cities hold about **70%** of urban population
- **Article 243P** — metropolitan area: **10 lakh** or more
- **Municipal Corporation** — larger urban area; Municipal Council — smaller; **Nagar Panchayat** — area in transition
- **Economic Survey 2025-26** (Ch. 15) — cities as "**critical economic infrastructure**"; scale but lack liveability
## SEC 4:70:3 | Largest UAs, 2011
- 2011 order — **Greater Mumbai** (18.4 m), **Delhi** (16.3 m), **Kolkata** (14.1 m); only these three mega cities (10 million+)
- **Delhi** overtook Kolkata (2001 order had Kolkata ahead)
- **Chennai** — about 8.7 million, not a mega city
- Diversified towns — **Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai**
  - Resort — Shimla, Ooty, Araku; mining — Raniganj, Digboi, Khammam; religious — Tirupati, Varanasi, Rameswaram
- "**Administered, not governed**" — too many agencies (ULBs, parastatals), low own-source revenue
- **AMRUT** — **25 June 2015**; 500 cities (485 after mergers); ₹1 lakh crore
- **HRIDAY** (2015) — 12 heritage cities, incl. **Amaravati**
- **UN-Habitat World Cities Report 2026** — released at World Urban Forum, **Baku**; about 40% (3.4 billion) face housing shortage, 1.1 billion in slums
## SEC 4:70:4 | Million-plus & UAs
- **Statutory town** — notified with municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee; run by a ULB
- **Urban agglomeration** — continuous urban spread of a town and its outgrowths, or of contiguous towns
- Million-plus cities/UAs — **53** (2011), up from **35** (2001)
- AP million-plus UAs (2011) — **Visakhapatnam** and **Vijayawada**
- **Vijayawada** — transport/trade town; Udhampur — garrison; Bhadrachalam — religious; Pilani — educational
- 1981 town criteria criticised for male bias, uniform thresholds, density on administrative boundaries (not satellite imagery)
## SEC 4:70:5 | States, 2011

- Least urbanised southern State — **Andhra Pradesh** (**29.47%**)
- AP districts — most urbanised **Visakhapatnam** (47.45%), least **Srikakulam** (16.16%)
- Most towns (Census 2001) — **Uttar Pradesh**
- **UN-Habitat** (1978) — HQ **Nairobi**; Centre of Excellence for Urban Affairs at Amaravati
## SEC 4:70:6 | Towns by function (APPSC-G2 2025)

- **Chandigarh** — planned city of **Le Corbusier**

# ROW 4:71 | Metropolitan regions and megacities of India; problems of large cities
## SEC 4:71:0 | Terms & coiners
- ★ **Megalopolis** — term first used by French geographer **Jean Gottmann** (1961), for the cities of the north-eastern seaboard of the USA
- **Megalopolis** — chain of merged metropolitan areas; **conurbation** — continuous urban area of merged towns
- **Megacity** — **10 million** (1 crore) or more; million-plus city — 10 lakh or more
- **Primate city** — **Mark Jefferson**; **Rakesh Mohan** — three processes of Indian urbanisation
## SEC 4:71:1 | World's largest cities
- **WUP 2025** — most populous: **Jakarta**, then Dhaka, Tokyo, New Delhi
- Jakarta replaced Tokyo due to a **new city-definition method** (not Tokyo's decline)
- **Economic Survey 2025-26** — 16 major cities (2000-2020) grew **horizontally**, periphery faster than core

## SEC 4:71:5 | Urban flooding

## SEC 4:71:6 | Problems of large cities
- Municipal solid waste — about **62 million tonnes** a year; about 70% collected, 30% processed
- **Eight** of the world's ten most polluted cities in India; **Delhi** — most polluted capital
- Urban planners — about one per **one lakh** people (developed countries one per 5,000)
- **Urban heat island** — dense construction, asphalt, loss of tree cover
## SEC 4:71:7 | City rankings

# ROW 4:72 | Human development - concept, HDI components and India's position
## SEC 4:72:0 | Persons & concept
- **Human Development Reports** — by **UNDP** (not World Bank); first HDR **1990**, conceived by **Mahbub ul Haq** with Sen
- **Amartya Sen** — "Development as Freedom" (**1999**); Nobel in Economics **1998**
- Human development — enlargement of **human choices**; quantitative and qualitative
