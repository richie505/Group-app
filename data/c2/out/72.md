# ROW 3:86 | Non-performing assets - causes, twin balance sheet problem, recognition and provisioning
## SEC 3:86:0 | Asset classification
- **Sub-standard asset** — NPA for **12 months or less**
- **Doubtful asset** — NPA for **more than 12 months**
- **Special Mention Accounts (SMAs)** — early-warning accounts, not yet NPAs

| Category | Overdue days |
|---|---|
| SMA-0 | 1–30 |
| **SMA-1** | **31–60** |
| SMA-2 | 61–90 |

## SEC 3:86:1 | Definition
- **NPA** — interest or principal overdue for **more than 90 days**
- Agricultural loans: NPA after **two crop seasons** (short-duration crops); **one crop season** (long-duration crops)
- Income recognition: interest on an NPA booked as income **only when actually received** (part of **IRACP** framework)

## SEC 3:86:2 | Key terms
- **Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** = **provisions held ÷ Gross NPAs**
- **Net NPA** = Gross NPA − provisions
- **Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR)** = **EBIT ÷ interest due**; shows a borrower's present and emerging risk
- **Evergreening** — fresh loans given to repay old loans, **concealing the default**

## SEC 3:86:3 | Twin balance sheet problem
- **Twin balance sheet problem** — **over-leveraged corporates** + **capital-starved, NPA-laden banks** at the same time
- Over-leveraged firms find it **hard to invest** → private investment falls

## SEC 3:86:4 | Causes & response
- Aggressive lending **2010-15** went largely to **infrastructure, steel and power**, without strict appraisal → stalled projects
- NPA vicious cycle: aggressive lending and shocks → NPAs → **provisioning and capital erosion** → weak credit growth
- Twin balance sheet problem framed in **Economic Survey 2016–17** (CEA **Arvind Subramanian**)
- **Asset Quality Review (AQR)** by RBI — **2015**
- Government's **4R strategy** — **Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise, Reform**
  - Response included the IBC, recapitalisation and NARCL

## SEC 3:86:5 | ECL model (from 1 April 2027)
- After the **AQR**, disclosed NPAs rose sharply and **peaked in 2018**
- RBI shifting from **incurred-loss model** to forward-looking **3-stage Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model**; the 90-day overdue test stays
- **Stage 1** assets — provision for **12-month** expected loss
- **Stage 2** assets — provision for **lifetime** expected loss
- Borrower-level NPA tagging: account upgraded **only after all arrears on every facility** are paid

## SEC 3:86:6 | SCB GNPA trend
- SCB GNPA peak — about **11.2%** in **March 2018** (after the AQR)
- **March 2026**: GNPA **1.93%**, NNPA **0.39%** — a **decadal low**
- **Andhra Pradesh Grameena Bank** (FY26): GNPA **0.91%**, NNPA **nil**
- SCB credit: about ₹67 lakh crore (2015) → about **₹181 lakh crore** (2025), nearly tripled; CRAR about **17.3%**

## SEC 3:86:8 | NPA — definitions, classification and types
- ★ **NPA** — loan or advance in default or arrears, i.e. **interest or principal not paid**

# ROW 3:87 | NPA resolution - SARFAESI, DRTs, IBC 2016, NARCL/bad bank, Prompt Corrective Action framework
## SEC 3:87:0 | Recovery channels
### Tribunals and forums
- **Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs)** — set up under the **Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 1993**; also used under SARFAESI
- DRTs and DRATs handle debts **above ₹20 lakh**
- **Lok Adalats** — statutory forums under the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**
- **NCLT** replaced the **Company Law Board**, **BIFR** and **AAIFR** (not the Securities Appellate Tribunal)
- **NCLT** — adjudicating authority for **corporate insolvency** under IBC 2016; **DRT** for individuals and partnership firms
### How they differ
- **SARFAESI** — secured lenders recover **without court intervention**
- **IBC** — collective, NCLT-led resolution
- **DRTs** — tribunal-based recovery
- FY 2024 recoveries: **IBC 48%** (largest), SARFAESI 32%, DRTs 17%, Lok Adalats 3%

## SEC 3:87:1 | SARFAESI Act 2002
### SARFAESI
- SARFAESI Act 2002 — based on the **Narasimham Committee**
- **Section 13(2)** — demand notice of **60 days**
- **Section 13(8)** — borrower can redeem the asset only by paying the **full dues** (not 25%)
- **Section 17** — appeal to the DRT within **45 days**
- **Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs)** — acquire NPAs, generally **at a discount**; regulated by the **RBI** (not SEBI)
### Related points
- **ICR below 1** — firm **cannot service its interest from earnings** (ICR = EBIT ÷ interest payable)
- **NCLAT** hears appeals from NCLT, IBBI, **CCI** (from 26 May 2017) and **NFRA** (from 7 May 2018)
- IBC waterfall: resolution costs, **workmen's dues** and secured creditors' claims rank **above government dues**
- **PCA**: the **RBI** may restrict lending, branch expansion, dividends and pay, and order capital infusion, a management change or a merger
- Andhra Pradesh: **NCLT** bench at **Amaravati**; **DRT** bench at **Visakhapatnam**

## SEC 3:87:2 | Restructuring schemes
| Scheme | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate Debt Restructuring (CDR)** | **2001** | |
| **5/25 scheme** | **2014** | |
| **Strategic Debt Restructuring (SDR)** | **2015** | lender debt converted into **51% or more equity** |
| **S4A** | **2016** | splits debt into **sustainable and unsustainable** parts |
| **Project Sashakt** | **2018** | **Sunil Mehta Committee**; plan binds all lenders once **66%** approve; lead bank drives it |

- Chronological order: CDR → 5/25 → SDR → S4A
- **Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets**: lenders review the account within **30 days** of default; **Inter-Creditor Agreement mandatory**; resolution within **180 days**

## SEC 3:87:3 | IBC 2016 basics
### Background
- IBC 2016 — based on the **T.K. Viswanathan (Bankruptcy Law Reforms) Committee, 2015**
- IBC shifted control from **debtor-in-possession** to **creditor-in-control**
- **Section 29A** — bars **wilful defaulters** and promoters of NPAs from submitting resolution bids
### CIRP thresholds and timelines
- Minimum default to trigger **CIRP** — **Rs 1 crore**; triggered by financial or operational creditors
- CIRP ordinarily **180 days** with extension; outer limit **330 days** including litigation
- **Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP)** for MSMEs — **2021**, **120-day** limit
- **IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026**: liquidation cut to **180 + 90 days**; adds hybrid **Creditor-Initiated Insolvency Resolution Process** (Secs 54C–54P)
### Outcomes and ratios
- Deterrent effect: **settlement before admission** of **30,310 cases** worth **₹13.78 lakh crore**
- Higher **PCR** (provisions ÷ gross NPA) → bank is **more resilient**
- March 2026 SCB GNPA of **1.93%** — a **decadal low**

## SEC 3:87:4 | IBC institutions
- **NCLT** — **Section 408**, Companies Act 2013
- **NCLAT** — **Section 410**, Companies Act 2013; sits at **New Delhi and Chennai**; hears appeals from NCLT under **Section 61** of the IBC
- **IBBI** regulates **insolvency professionals**, insolvency professional agencies, **information utilities** and registered valuers

## SEC 3:87:5 | Bad bank & 2026 amendment
### NPA decline
- Reasons for the fall in NPAs: **4R strategy**, **higher provisioning**, **IBC** and **NARCL**
### IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026
- NCLT must admit or reject a CIRP plea within **14 days**
- NCLT must rule on resolution plans within **30 days**
- 'may' → **'shall'** in **Section 7(5)(a)**: admission of a financial creditor's plea mandatory once default is proved; overturned the **Vidarbha Industries** ruling (which had read 'may' as NCLT discretion)
### NARCL ('bad bank')
- **NARCL** (National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited) — announced in **Budget 2021–22**
- Pays **15% in cash**, **85% in Security Receipts**; Government guarantee of **Rs 30,600 crore** for **5 years**
- Takes over stressed loans **above ₹500 crore**, targeting about **₹2 lakh crore** of fully or partly provided assets
### Related
- **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018** — cases of **₹100 crore or more**

## SEC 3:87:6 | Prompt Corrective Action
- **Evergreening** — fresh loan used to repay an old loan so the **NPA stays hidden**
- **PCA** — **Prompt Corrective Action**; RBI framework to intervene in banks with weakening financial health
- PCA introduced by the RBI in **2002**
- PCA indicator: **asset quality** → **net NPA ratio**; **Tier-1 leverage ratio** → measures leverage

## SEC 3:87:7 | Exam corner
- ★ **IBC** enacted and in force in **2016**
- **Insolvency** — inability to pay debts as they fall due (a financial state); **bankruptcy** — the legal declaration or process that follows
- Approval of a resolution plan does **not automatically release personal guarantors**; they remain liable — **Lalit Kumar Jain (2021)**
- IBC criticisms: **protracted litigation**, limited **NCLT capacity**, deep **haircuts**, **guarantor liability** (not eliminated)

## SEC 3:87:12 | Schemes → restructuring, CDR 2001 to Prudential Framework 2019
- ★ **S4A** — RBI scheme for reworking the financial structure of big corporates facing genuine difficulties; banks segregate **sustainable and unsustainable** debt; introduced **2016**, ended **February 2018**
- ★ **Inter-Creditor Agreement (ICA)** — signed **July 2018** by **33** banks/FIs; faster resolution of stressed assets of **₹50 crore or more** under consortium lending

## SEC 3:87:13 | IBC 2016 — definitions, enactment and FIRSTs
- ★ **Insolvency** — financial state of being unable to pay debts as they become due
- ★ **Bankruptcy** — legal process where the debtor's assets are **liquidated or reorganised** for creditors

## SEC 3:87:18 | Prompt Corrective Action framework — full form, triggers, restrictions
- ★ **PCA** — RBI framework restricting operations of **commercial banks** with weak financial health, mainly to contain rising **NPAs**

# ROW 3:89 | Financial markets - money market instruments and capital market segments
## SEC 3:89:0 | Money vs capital market
- ★ **Call money market** — most volatile part of the organised money market; repayable on demand, overnight to **14 days**
- **Money market** (up to one year) — regulated by the **RBI**; **capital market** (above one year) — by **SEBI**
- **Hybrid** instruments — **convertible preference shares**, partly or optionally convertible debentures; equity shares are pure; T-Bills and CPs are money market

## SEC 3:89:1 | Money market instruments
- **Commercial Paper (CP)** — **unsecured** short-term promissory instrument of eligible corporates; **7 days to 1 year**
- **Vaghul Committee** — CP (**1990**) and CDs (**1989**)
- **Call money** — overnight inter-bank; **notice money** — **2 to 14 days**; **TREPS** — **tri-party repo**
- **Industrial securities market** — **primary and secondary** segments
- **Budget 2026–27**: market-making for corporate bonds, **derivatives on corporate bond indices**, bigger municipal bond markets

## SEC 3:89:2 | Government securities
- **G-Sec** — tradeable instrument acknowledging debt of the **Central or a State Government**; issued by the RBI under the **Government Securities Act, 2006**
- **Treasury Bills** — sold by **RBI auction** for the Centre; **91, 182 and 364 days**; **zero-coupon**
- **Cash Management Bills** — Centre's **temporary cash mismatches**
- **Dated securities** — **one year or more**; issued by the **Central Government**
- **State Development Loans (SDLs)** — State Governments' long-term borrowing / dated securities
- **Gilt-edged market** — Treasury Bills and Government bonds; default risk almost **zero**

## SEC 3:89:3 | T-bills & gilt market
- ★ **Gilt-edged market** — market of **Government securities**; interest and redemption on due date are certain
- **SBI DFHI** — largest **primary dealer**; primary dealers are RBI-regulated underwriters and market makers in G-Secs
- OMO: RBI **sells** G-secs to **absorb** liquidity; purchases inject liquidity
- Treasury Bills first issued in India — **1917** (First World War)
- **Yield** — investor's return at market price; **face value** — nominal value printed on the security

## SEC 3:89:4 | SEBI AIF categories
- **Category III AIF** — hedge funds, **leveraged trading** (may trade commodity derivatives); Category II — private equity and debt funds
- **September 2025** — SEBI added an AIF category only for **accredited investors**
- **REITs** pool **real-estate** assets; **InvITs** pool **infrastructure** assets
- Bond terms: **bondholder** — **creditor** to the issuer; **maturity** — repayment date; **coupon** — interest; face value — nominal value

## SEC 3:89:5 | Bond types
- **Masala bond** — rupee-denominated, issued overseas; **investor** bears currency risk
- **Sovereign Gold Bond** (2015) — **2.5%** interest plus gold-price-linked returns; fresh issuance discontinued
- First green bond — **European Investment Bank**, **2007**; World Bank labelled green bond 2008
- Treasury Bills — **zero-coupon**; Government bonds pay interest
- Bond **price and yield** move in **opposite directions**

## SEC 3:89:6 | What moves yields
- **RBI OMO purchases** of G-secs → **lower** yields (support prices, inject liquidity)
- Higher policy rates, inflation and government borrowing → **raise** yields

## SEC 3:89:7 | Exam corner
- ★ **Commercial paper** — credit source for **corporate industry**; introduced **1990**; unsecured promissory note; minimum **7 days**
- CCP regulators: **RBI** — **CCIL** (money, G-Sec, forex markets); **SEBI** — **NSCCL** and **ICCL** (capital markets); IFSCA only IFSC entities

## SEC 3:89:8 | Money market vs capital market — definitions and instruments
- ★ **Capital market** — long-term funds (debt and equity): **Government Bond Market** and **Stock Market**
- ★ **Money market**: Call Money Market, Treasury Bill Market
- ★ **CBLO** (Collateralised Borrowing and Lending Obligation) — **money market** instrument; by **CCIL** from **20 January 2003**; needs collateral

## SEC 3:89:9 | Government securities — types, Treasury Bills and bonds
- ★ **T-Bills** maturities — **91, 182 and 364 days**
- ★ T-Bills sold by the **RBI** on auction basis; first issued **1917**
- ★ Per the PYQ key, **RBI** is not counted as a money-market participant; money market covers overnight to **one year**

## SEC 3:89:10 | Gilt-edged market and government securities — types, Acts, participants
- ★ A **US debt default** does not nullify Treasury bondholders' **legal claims** to payment

## SEC 3:89:11 | Capital market — structure, instruments and AIF categories
- ★ Promoting **venture capital** — **entrepreneur development** measure
- ★ **Venture capital** — **long-term start-up capital** for new companies with growth potential; a form of private equity
- ★ **AIFs**: **hedge funds** and **venture capital** (not bonds, stocks); privately pooled from sophisticated investors; defined under **SEBI Regulations, 2012**
- ★ **InvIT** dividends and interest income — **fully taxable** at the investor's slab rate

# ROW 3:92 | Financial instabilities - crises, systemic risk, shadow banking, macroprudential regulation
## SEC 3:92:0 | Crises and India's response
- **Harshad Mehta scam (1992)** — bank funds diverted into stocks via **ready-forward G-Sec deals**; followed by statutory SEBI and **NSE screen trading**
- **IL&FS (2018)** — infrastructure NBFC defaults
- **East Asian crisis (1997)** — collapse of the **Thai baht**, short-term foreign debt; India's impact limited by **capital controls** and low short-term debt

## SEC 3:92:2 | Key terms
- **Teaser loans** — **low fixed rate** early, higher later → future default risk
- **Credit Default Swap** — insurance-like **derivative on bonds**; seller pays on default; **counterparty risk**
- **NBFC asset-liability mismatch** — **short-term borrowing** (CP, mutual fund money), **long-term lending** (infrastructure, housing); exposed by the **IL&FS** default
- **Shadow banking** — financial activity of **non-bank entities** (e.g. NBFCs) outside regular bank regulation
- **PMC Bank** failure — hidden exposure to **one realty group**; weak oversight from **dual control** (RBI and State Registrars)

## SEC 3:92:3 | Systemically important
- **D-SIBs** (RBI): **SBI, ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank** — extra **CET1 surcharge**
- **G-SIBs** — identified by the **Financial Stability Board**; **no Indian bank** among them
- IRDAI's **D-SIIs**: **LIC, GIC Re, New India Assurance**

## SEC 3:92:4 | NBFCs & co-op banks
- **NBFCs** — registered under the **Companies Act**; cannot take **demand deposits**, issue cheques or join the payment system; deposits not DICGC-covered
- **Scale-Based Regulation** (October 2022) — **Base, Middle, Upper, Top** layers
- **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020** — **urban and multi-state cooperative banks** under RBI supervision
- **FSDC** — **coordinates** among financial regulators; RBI regulates banks and NBFCs, IRDAI insurers

## SEC 3:92:5 | Stability bodies
- **FSDC** — set up **December 2010**; chaired by the **Union Finance Minister**; not a NITI Aayog organ
- **Financial Stability Report** (RBI) — **half-yearly**, June and December
- **PCA** — for **weak banks**, monitored on capital, asset quality (NPAs), leverage; can restrict lending, branch expansion, dividends
- **CFSA** — co-chaired by an **RBI Deputy Governor** and the **Finance Secretary**; RBI Governor chairs the FSDC sub-committee

## SEC 3:92:6 | Exam corner
- **Global Financial Crisis (2007-09)** — began with **US sub-prime mortgages**; India saw **FPI outflows**, set up FSDC (December 2010)
- Sub-prime chain: weak borrowers → securitisation → rising rates → defaults → US bank failures (not falling oil prices)
- **FSDC** — macroprudential supervision and inter-regulatory coordination; also **financial literacy and inclusion**; not a NITI Aayog organ
- **Andhra Pradesh** credit-deposit ratio **157.40%** — lending very high relative to deposits

## SEC 3:92:7 | Financial crises and scams → year, cause, India impact
