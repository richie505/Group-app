# ROW 2:2 | Sources of the Indian Constitution - borrowed features and their origin countries
## SEC 2:2:0 | GoI Act 1935: largest source
- **Government of India Act, 1935** — the **single largest source** of the Constitution
  - federal scheme, Governor's office, judiciary, Public Service Commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details
- 1935 Act's **Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists** → Constitution's **Union, State and Concurrent Lists**
- 1935 Act's administrative provisions → the Constitution's **bulk and extensive administrative detail**
- Language of the **Preamble** — from **Australia**; 1935 Act gave the structural and administrative backbone
- Criticisms
  - **'Bag of borrowings'** — many provisions drawn from foreign constitutions and earlier laws
  - **'Elephantine'** — length
  - **'Lawyers' paradise'** — legal detail

## SEC 2:2:1 | Country → features
### Britain
- **Parliamentary system** and its conventions (Britain has no single written Constitution)
- **Single citizenship**, **bicameralism**, **cabinet system** (USA has dual citizenship)
- **Prerogative writs**; parliamentary privileges; office of **Speaker**
### USA
- **Fundamental Rights**, **judicial review**, **independence of judiciary**, **impeachment of the President**
- Idea of the **Preamble**
### Canada
- **Federation with a strong Centre**
- **Residuary powers with the Centre** (not Australia, not South Africa)
### Australia
- **Concurrent List**
- **Joint sitting** of the two Houses (Article 108) — presided over by the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**
- **Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse** (not Canada)
- **Language of the Preamble**
### USSR
- **Fundamental Duties** (not France)
- Ideals of **social, economic and political justice** in the Preamble (not Australia)
  - Fundamental Duties and the justice ideals are two separate USSR borrowings
### Others
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency | **Weimar Germany** |
| Emergency provisions (except financial) | Germany's **Weimar** Constitution |
| Directive Principles | **Ireland** (Ireland took the idea from **Spain**) |
| Method of electing the President | **Ireland** |
| Procedure established by law | **Japan** |
| Republic; liberty, equality, fraternity | **France** |
| Amendment procedure | **South Africa** (not Ireland) |

- General **emergency scheme** — associated with the **1935 Act**
### Parts of the Constitution
- **Fundamental Rights** and **Directive Principles** — the **philosophical** part
- **Parliamentary system** — the **political** part

## SEC 2:2:2 | Article → feature
- ★ Indian **federalism** — based on the pattern of **Canada**
- **Residuary powers** in India — with **Parliament** (**Article 248**), on the Canadian model (not the States)
- **Article 21** — uses **'procedure established by law'** (from **Japan**), not 'due process of law'
- **Fundamental Duties** — introduced by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**; the **eleventh** duty added by the **86th Amendment**

## SEC 2:2:3 | Look-alike pairs
- **Integrated judicial system** — a single hierarchy of courts enforces both Central and State laws
  - unlike the separate federal and State courts of the **USA**

## SEC 2:2:4 | Emergency source dispute
- Suspension of Fundamental Rights in a National Emergency — idea from **Weimar Germany**
- **Articles 20 and 21** cannot be suspended
  - Article 20 — protection in respect of conviction for offences; Article 21 — life and personal liberty
- **Article 19** — the right most readily suspended

## SEC 2:2:5 | Exam corner
- Seventh Schedule (as stated): Union **98**, State **59**, Concurrent **52** subjects
- Original Constitution: **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules**
- Today: about **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules**

## SEC 2:2:6 | GoI Act 1935 → the single largest source
- ★ **British Constitution** — credited (per answer key) with the **most profound influence**: parliamentary government, cabinet system, rule of law
  - the **GoI Act, 1935** is also widely cited as a major contributor
- ★ Indian Constitution — the **longest** written Constitution in the world (originally 22 Parts, 395 Articles, 8 Schedules)
  - **Fundamental Rights** chapter borrowed from the **USA**, but that is not the reason for its length
- ★ Tradition of written constitutions began in **America** — USA has the world's **first** written Constitution
  - also the world's **shortest** written Constitution — only **7 Articles**

## SEC 2:2:7 | Feature → source country (parliamentary, rights, Preamble)
- ★ **Fundamental Rights** — from the **USA**
- ★ **Directive Principles of State Policy** (Part IV) — from **Ireland**

## SEC 2:2:8 | Feature → source country (federal, judicial, amendment)
- ★ **Article 21** — the Article tested for 'due process of law'; its text: no deprivation of life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**

## SEC 2:2:9 | Country → features (match list)
| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| ★ **Concurrent List** | **Australia** |
| ★ **Election of members of the Rajya Sabha** | **South Africa** |
| ★ **Cabinet system** | **Britain** |
| ★ **Directive Principles** | **Ireland** |
| ★ **Fundamental Rights** | **USA** |
| ★ **Union of States with a strong Centre** | **Canada** |

- ★ Concurrent List — both Parliament and State Legislatures legislate on same subjects; **Union law** prevails in conflict
- ★ Amendment of the Constitution — **not** borrowed from **France**
- ★ GoI Act, 1935 — did **not** provide for a **written Constitution** for the country
  - demand for a constitution accepted in principle in the **August Offer, 1940**
- ★ Supreme in the Indian political system — **the Constitution** (supreme law of India)

## SEC 2:2:10 | Articles → borrowed feature (number game)
- ★ **Concurrent List** (Seventh Schedule) — from **Australia**
- ★ **Election Commission** — not inspired by any other constitution
- ★ Method of election of the **President** — inspired by **Ireland**
- ★ **Articles 20 and 21** — cannot be suspended during a National Emergency

## SEC 2:2:12 | Universal angles
- ★ **Federal system** — adopted largely from the **Government of India Act, 1935**
  - division of powers and administrative framework drawn from the Act

# ROW 2:3 | Salient features of the Indian Constitution - lengthiest, blend of rigidity and flexibility, quasi-federal etc.
## SEC 2:3:0 | Number game
- New provisions inserted as **lettered Articles** (e.g. **21A**, **51A**) — numbering still ends at **Article 395**

## SEC 2:3:1 | Parts added / repealed
| Part | Subject | Added by |
|---|---|---|
| **IVA** | Fundamental Duties | **42nd Amendment, 1976** |
| **IX** | Panchayats | **73rd Amendment, 1992** |
| **IXA** | Municipalities | **74th Amendment, 1992** |
| **IXB** | Co-operative Societies | **97th Amendment, 2011** |
| **XIVA** | Tribunals | — |

- **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** — created the **third (local) tier**, added to the original dual polity

## SEC 2:3:2 | Why so lengthy
- Long partly because it **includes detailed administrative provisions**
- **'Elephantine size'** — **H.V. Kamath** (APPSC 2018 key)
  - 'Elephantine' criticises **great length and bulk**, not brevity
  - **Granville Austin** — Assembly 'a one-party body in an essentially one-party country'
  - **Viscount Simon** — 'a body of Hindus'
### Parliamentary system
- Council of Ministers — collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**
- Preferred because **familiar** through the **GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935**
  - also suited to India's size and diversity; avoids executive–legislature conflict
- India described as a **Union of States** with a parliamentary system

## SEC 2:3:3 | India vs USA vs Britain
- ★ **Republic** — **no hereditary rulers**; head of State elected (in India, by **indirect election**)
  - head of State, the **President**, elected, not hereditary; ultimate authority rests with the people
- Amending power of Parliament limited by the **basic structure doctrine** (Parliament a creature of the Constitution)
- India — **representative (indirect) democracy**; no referendum, initiative or recall nationally
- Indian Parliament — **not sovereign** in the British sense: bound by written Constitution, Fundamental Rights, judicial review
- Judicial review **narrower than the USA** — Article 21 uses **'procedure established by law'**
- **Single integrated court system** enforces both Central and State laws
- India has a **parliamentary**, not presidential, form of government

## SEC 2:3:4 | Other features
- **Right to Property** — moved out of Fundamental Rights; now under **Article 300A**
- Emergency provisions (**Arts. 352-360**) — **Part XVIII**: National, State (President's Rule), Financial
- Voting age **21 → 18** — **61st Amendment, 1988** (not 42nd, 44th or 73rd)
- India — **secular** state; **no state religion**

## SEC 2:3:5 | Constitutionalism
- **Constitutionalism** — government power **limited by a supreme Constitution**, exercised by rules, not the ruler's will
- **Supremacy of the Constitution** — a basic-structure element
  - with rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power

## SEC 2:3:6 | Exam corner
- Original Constitution — **8 Schedules** (395 Articles, 22 Parts); **12 Schedules** now
- **Written Constitution** — associated with the **USA**
  - strong Centre — Canada; Concurrent List — Australia; amendment procedure — South Africa
- Indian States — **no separate constitutions**, **no dual citizenship** (the USA has both)

## SEC 2:3:7 | Lengthiest Constitution — counts, Parts and FIRSTs — Written, single document — who said
- ★ Originally **395 Articles**

## SEC 2:3:9 | Terms → meaning: constitutionalism, supremacy, democracy, republic
- ★ **Constitutional government** — government **limited by the terms of the Constitution**
- ★ India is a **Parliamentary Democracy**
- ★ **Republic** — the **head of the State is elected**

## SEC 2:3:10 | Parliamentary form — features and comparisons with the USA and Britain
- ★ Cardinal features: **democratic republic**, **parliamentary** form, **supreme power vests in the People**
  - **not** a unified authority — separation of powers
- ★ **President** elected **indirectly**; **parliamentary system combined with republicanism** (no hereditary ruler)

## SEC 2:3:12 | Which is NOT a feature — odd-one-out sets
- ★ **Presidential Government** — **not** a feature
  - features: Parliamentary Government, Independent Judiciary, Federal Government

# ROW 2:4 | Preamble - text, keywords, 42nd Amendment; is the Preamble part of the Constitution (Berubari, Kesavananda)
## SEC 2:4:0 | From resolution to Preamble
### Objectives Resolution
- ★ Preamble — a modified version of the **Objectives Resolution** of **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- Moved **13 December 1946**; adopted **22 January 1947**
- ★ Preamble **non-justiciable**
### Dates
- **26 November 1949** — date in the Preamble ('this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949') — **adoption** of the Constitution
- **26 January 1950** — commencement
- Preamble **enacted after** the rest of the Constitution, to conform to the text
- **Constitution Day** — **26 November**, observed **since 2015**
### Content
- ★ Original 1949 Preamble — **Sovereign, Democratic, Republic**
  - ★ **Socialist**, **Secular**, **Integrity** — added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**
- Source of the Constitution — **the people of India** themselves ('We, the people of India ... give to ourselves this Constitution')
- Preamble reflects the philosophy, ideals and **objectives** of the Constitution

## SEC 2:4:1 | Sources
| Element | Source |
|---|---|
| Idea of a Preamble; **'We, the people'** | **United States** Constitution |
| Language of the Preamble | **Australia** |
| Justice | **Russian Revolution** |
| Liberty, equality, fraternity | **French Revolution** |

## SEC 2:4:2 | Keywords
- **Fraternity** — sense of brotherhood, promoted by single citizenship; possibly from **Article 1 of the UDHR (1948)**
- 1949 Preamble — **'Sovereign Democratic Republic'**; 'unity of the Nation'
- 42nd Amendment inserted Socialist and Secular **between 'Sovereign' and 'Democratic'**
  - now 'Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic'
### Meanings
- **Secular State** — **no official/State religion**; equal respect for all religions; not hostile to religion
- **Secularism** in India — **equal respect for all religions**
- 'Secular' before 1976 — appeared in **Article 25(2)(a)** (secular activities associated with religious practice)
- **Democratic** — not political democracy alone; covers **social and economic** democracy too
  - 'Democratic' as a mix of **Fabian Socialism and American Populism** — **not valid** (APPSC G1 2024 key)
- **Socialist** — aims at **reducing inequality**
- **Republic** — **elected head of State**

## SEC 2:4:3 | Objectives for all citizens
| Objective | Of |
|---|---|
| ★ **Justice** | **Social, economic and political** |
| **Liberty** | thought, expression, belief, faith and worship |
| **Equality** | **status and opportunity** |
| **Fraternity** | assuring the **dignity of the individual** |

- Not in the Preamble — 'Federal', 'Fundamental Duties', 'adult franchise'
- **Liberty** — absence of restraints plus opportunities to develop personality; not freedom from every restraint

## SEC 2:4:4 | Is it part of the Constitution?
| Case | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **Berubari Union** | **1960** | Preamble **not part** of the Constitution; **'key to the minds of the makers'**, aid to interpretation where ambiguous |
| **Golaknath** | **1967** | dealt with amending Fundamental Rights |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | **1973** | Preamble **is part** of the Constitution; amendable under **Article 368**, but basic structure cannot be destroyed (overruled Berubari) |
| **Minerva Mills** | **1980** | struck down 42nd Amendment clauses on unlimited amending power and excluding judicial review |
| **S.R. Bommai** | **1994** | reaffirmed **secularism** as part of basic structure |
| **LIC of India** | **1995** | reaffirmed Preamble is part of the Constitution |
| **Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India** | **25 Nov 2024** | upheld **'Socialist'** and **'Secular'**; rejected challenges to 42nd Amendment insertions |

- **Minerva Mills** — struck down clauses giving DPSPs absolute precedence over Fundamental Rights; restored balance of Parts III and IV, reinforcing basic structure
- **Secularism** — part of basic structure **even before** 'Secular' was inserted in 1976
- Kesavananda Bharati came **before** the 1976 amendment

## SEC 2:4:5 | 42nd Amendment 1976
- ★ **'Socialist'** added by the **42nd Amendment** (with secular and integrity)
- ★ Objective of the Constitution — reflected in the **Preamble**
- 42nd Amendment — enacted during the **Emergency (1975-77)** under **Indira Gandhi**
  - **curtailed** judicial review, tried to exclude it in some matters — did **not abolish** it entirely
- **Fraternity** — in the **original 1949** text, not added by the 42nd
- Preamble amended **only once** — **42nd Amendment, 1976** (Socialist, Secular, Integrity)
- **Berubari Union** — Preamble a **key to the framers' minds**; non-justiciable, aid to interpretation
- Preamble — **non-justiciable**, cannot be enforced in court; Berubari (1960) held it not part of the Constitution

## SEC 2:4:6 | Who called it
- **'Political horoscope'** — **K.M. Munshi**
- **'Identity card'** — **Palkhivala**
- **'Soul'** — **Thakurdas Bhargava**; **Hidayatullah** also called the Preamble its soul
- Article 32 **'heart and soul'** — **Ambedkar**

## SEC 2:4:8 | Making of the Preamble — Objectives Resolution and sources → country
- ★ Liberty in the Preamble — of **thought, expression, belief, faith and worship** only
  - ★ **'Political Liberty'** — not embodied as a standalone category
  - ★ **Economic liberty** — **not** embodied
- ★ Chief source of political power — **the People** ('We, the People of India'); sovereignty vests in them

## SEC 2:4:9 | Preamble text — order of words, date, original vs absent words
### Status and order
- ★ Status on **26 January 1950** — **'A Sovereign Democratic Republic'**
  - adopted 26 November 1949; in force 26 January 1950
- ★ Now — **'Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic'** (that sequence)
- ★ Opens with **'We, the People of India'** — indicates **people's sovereignty**; found in the **Preamble**
- ★ Preamble's date — **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949** (adoption)
- ★ **Third reading** of the Constitution in the Assembly started — **14 November** (key: **1948**)
### Words absent from the Preamble
- ★ **Public Welfare**, ★ **Federal**, ★ **Religious Justice**, 'People's'
- ★ **Three** types of justice — social, economic, political
### Descriptions
- ★ Preamble — called the **'Soul'** of the Constitution
  - **K.M. Munshi** — 'Horoscope of our sovereign democratic Republic'
- ★ Declares India **Secular** — the **Preamble** ('Secular' added by 42nd Amendment, 1976)
- ★ **Communist State** — **not** the philosophy of the Constitution; socialist state is an ideal
- ★ India — democratic polity, sovereign state, democratic society and **welfare state** (welfare state via **DPSP**)

## SEC 2:4:10 | Keywords → meaning
- ★ 'Democratic' as a mix of **British Fabian Socialism and American Populism** — **not valid** (answer: none of the statements valid)
- ★ **'Freedom'** — **not** used in the Preamble; it uses **Liberty**, Equality, Justice, Fraternity

## SEC 2:4:11 | Objectives secured for all citizens → kinds and meaning
- ★ Liberty secured — of **thought, expression, belief, faith and worship**
- ★ Preamble secures to all: **Justice** (social, economic, political), **Liberty** of thought and expression, **Equality** of opportunity, **Dignity of the Individual** — all of these

## SEC 2:4:12 | Is the Preamble part of the Constitution? — landmark cases
- ★ **Kesavananda Bharati** — Preamble regarded as part of the **basic structure**
  - 13-judge bench outlined the **Basic Structure doctrine** (1973)
- ★ **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)** — Preamble an **integral part** of the Constitution

## SEC 2:4:13 | Amendability and secularism as basic structure
- ★ **'Secular'/'Secularism'** — inserted by the **42nd Amendment**

## SEC 2:4:14 | Status and legal effect of the Preamble
- ★ Preamble — **part of the Constitution**, but **no legal effect independently** of other parts
  - not enforceable in court; confers no substantive powers (Kesavananda Bharati)

## SEC 2:4:15 | 42nd Amendment, 1976 → words added and other changes
- ★ **42nd Amendment** — added **Socialist, Secular** (and **Integrity**) to the Preamble
  - ★ **Integrity** — not in the Preamble in 1975; added **1976**
  - ★ not added: Gram Swarajya, Sovereign
- ★ **Fundamental Duties** — added by the **42nd Amendment**
- ★ **42nd** — termed the **'Mini Constitution'**
- ★ 'Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic' — substituted by the 42nd Amendment for 'Sovereign Democratic Republic'; amendment held valid

## SEC 2:4:16 | Persons → descriptions of the Preamble (who said)
- ★ Mind of the makers of the Constitution — reflected in the **Preamble** (**Berubari Union**, 1960)
- ★ **Berubari** — first declared the Preamble **not a part** of the Constitution
- ★ **'Horoscope of our sovereign, democratic republic'** / 'political horoscope' — **K.M. Munshi**

## SEC 2:4:17 | Universal angles
- ★ Preamble amended by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**
  - 'Sovereign Democratic Republic' → 'Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic'
  - 'Unity of the Nation' → 'Unity and **Integrity** of the Nation'
- ★ Words added — **Secular and Socialist** (and Integrity)
- 42nd Amendment based on the **Swaran Singh Committee** recommendations

# ROW 2:7 | Citizenship - Articles 5-11; Citizenship Act 1955, acquisition and loss, OCI, CAA 2019
## SEC 2:7:0 | Articles 5-11 (Part II)
- Citizenship — **Part II, Articles 5–11**; identifies citizens at commencement (**26 January 1950**)
  - Part I (1–4) Union and territory; Part III (12–35) Fundamental Rights

| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **5** | at commencement — domiciled + born in India, or parent born in India, or **5 years'** residence before 1950 |
| **6** | migrants **from Pakistan to India** — cut-off **19 July 1948** |
| **7** | migrants **to Pakistan** — **1 March 1947** relevant |
| **10** | **continuance** of citizenship |
| **11** | **Parliament** regulates acquisition and termination by law |

- Parliament alone regulates citizenship (**Citizenship Act, 1955**; Rules under **Section 18**); **Election Commission** has no such power
- Born in India **26 Jan 1950 – 30 June 1987** — citizen by birth regardless of parents' nationality, except children of **foreign diplomats** and **enemy aliens**
- **Descent** — born **outside India**, **either parent** Indian at birth; birth registered at an Indian consulate

## SEC 2:7:1 | Acquisition: 5 modes (B-D-R-N-I)
- Born on/after **3 December 2004** — **one** Indian-citizen parent enough if the other is not an **illegal migrant**

| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| **3** | **birth** |
| **5** | **registration** |
| **7** | **incorporation of territory** |
| **14A** | **National Register of Indian Citizens**; national identity card |

- **PIO** — foreign citizen whose ancestors were Indian nationals; **NRI** — Indian citizen residing abroad
- **Article 1** — 'Union of India' = **States only**; 'Territory of India' = States + UTs + territories acquired
- **NRC** — citizens only; **NPR** — all usual residents
- Basis shifted **from jus soli to jus sanguinis**
  - **birth** — **jus soli**; **descent** — **jus sanguinis**; naturalisation — residence and character; incorporation — new territory

## SEC 2:7:2 | Loss: 3 modes (R-T-D)
### Citizens and rights
- **Article 19** freedoms — **citizens only** (like Articles 15, 16, 29, 30)
- **Single citizenship** for Union and States; no separate State domicile
### Acquisition rules
- **Naturalisation** — **oath of allegiance** required
- Registration (person of Indian origin) — **7 years'** residence; spouses of Indian citizens also a category
### Loss
| Section | Mode |
|---|---|
| **7A** | OCI cardholders |
| **8** | **renunciation** — voluntary, by declaration |
| **9** | **termination** |
| **10** | **deprivation** — compulsory, by the Centre |

- Renunciation — minor children lose citizenship; may **resume within one year** of majority
- Deprivation — only citizens by **naturalisation, registration or Article 5(c)**
  - grounds: fraud, disloyalty, aiding/unlawful trade with an enemy, **two years'** imprisonment within **five years** of naturalisation/registration
### CAA 2019
- Communities: Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, Christian — **not Muslim**
- Naturalisation residence reduced to **five years**; citizenship effective from date of entry
### OCI
- OCI — foreign nationals of Indian origin; **lifelong visa**; **cannot vote**
- Barred if self, parent or grandparent was a citizen of **Pakistan or Bangladesh**
- **Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2026** — **e-OCI cards**, central OCI database, online application and renunciation
### Territory
- **Mizoram** — State on **20 February 1987**, **State of Mizoram Act, 1986** and **53rd Amendment**
- **Puducherry, Goa** — **acquired** territories (cession, treaty or conquest)
- **Berubari** reference — led to the **9th Amendment, 1960** (cession needs Article 368)
- UTs — **eight** after merger of Dadra and Nagar Haveli with Daman and Diu (**26 January 2020**)
- **Aadhaar** — not proof of citizenship — **Section 9** of the Aadhaar Act
