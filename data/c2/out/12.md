# ROW 1:99 | Musunuri Nayaks and the Velama (Racherla) chiefs of Rachakonda-Devarakonda
## SEC 1:99:0 | Liberation to fall
### Fall of Warangal (1323)
- Warangal fell in **1323**; Muhammad bin Tughlaq renamed it **Sultanpurti**
- Andhra placed under **Malik Burhanuddin**, assisted by **Malik Maqbul**
- **Prolaya Nayaka**, **Recherla Singama** and **Prolaya Vema Reddi** — surviving generals of **Prataparudra**
### Liberation movement
- **Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka** led the liberation confederacy (**Vimochanodyamam**) that freed Andhra from Tughluq rule
  - Recherla Singama, Prolaya Vema Reddi and Kolani Rudra took part but did not lead
- Prolaya Nayaka ruled from **Rekapalli** — on the **Godavari**, forest region near **Bhadrachalam**
- **Vilasa copper-plate grant (1330)** — issued by **Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka**
  - records Prolaya's **rise**, **Tughluq atrocities** and his **restoration of agraharas**
  - Kaluvacheru grant (1423) — issued by **Anitalli**
### Kapaya Nayaka
- **1336** — Kapaya Nayaka with **Hoysala Veera Ballala III**, **Prolaya Vema Reddi** and **Singama** took Warangal from **Malik Maqbul**
- Kapaya helped **Hasan Gangu** (Zafar Khan) found the **Bahmani kingdom** at **Gulbarga** in **1347**
- **Bhuvanagiri** lost in **1356**
- Truce of **1364–65** — Kapaya ceded **Golconda fort** with huge wealth; Golconda became the boundary
- **Battle of Bhimavaram (1367–68)** — **Anapota Nayaka** defeated and killed Kapaya; end of Musunuri rule
- **Tarikh-i-Ferishta** (Persian) calls Telangana **Tilang**; describes Bahmani–Kapaya conflicts

## SEC 1:99:4 | Recherla Velamas (1325-1475)
### Kingdom
- Recherla kingdom — c. **1325–1475** (about 150 years); began as loyal feudatories of the early Kakatiyas
- Reddi state — **1325–1448**
- Founder of the Recherla lineage — **Betala Nayudu**
- 1336 recovery: **Kapaya** recovered Warangal; **Singama** cleared **Nalgonda–Mahabubnagar**; **Prolaya Vema Reddi** cleared **Kammanadu–Pakanadu**
### Rulers
- **Singama Nayaka I** — founded the independent Velama kingdom; expanded to the **Krishna** and the **Krishna-Tungabhadra doab**
  - killed fighting the chiefs of **Jallipalli** (not at Bhimavaram)
- **Madanayudu** and nephew **Naganayudu** invaded the Reddi kingdom to avenge **Singama**
- **Anapota Nayaka** (son of Singama I) — shifted capital from **Amanagallu** to **Rachakonda**; defeated Kapaya at Bhimavaram
- **Devarakonda** (Nalgonda) — **second branch capital** of the Recherla Velamas
- ★ **Potana** refused to dedicate his Bhagavatam to **Sarvajna Singama Nayaka II**; dedicated it to Lord Rama

### Kakatiya capital
- **Anamakonda (Hanamakonda)** — only the first Kakatiya capital; later moved to **Warangal (Orugallu)**
## SEC 1:99:5 | Velama alliances

# ROW 1:100 | Gajapatis of Orissa in Andhra; Bahmani incursions into Andhradesa
## SEC 1:100:0 | Bahmani kingdom
- ★ Bahmani kingdom founded **1347** by **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** — **Hasan Gangu** / **Zafar Khan**
  - capital **Gulbarga**, named **Hasanabad**
  - four regions: Gulbarga, Daulatabad, Berar, Bidar
- ★ Capital shifted from **Gulbarga** to **Bidar** — **Ahmad Shah I**
- **Muhammad Shah I** fought Kapaya Nayaka
- **Muhammad III** ruled with **Mahmud Gawan** as minister
## SEC 1:100:1 | Bahmani incursions
- **Muhammad Shah I** burned Kapaya's son **Vinayaka** alive in **1361**
- **Mahmud Gawan** (with Muhammad III) — suppressed Telangana revolts from **1453**, subdued **Orugallu**, took **Kondaveedu**
  - executed **1481** or **1482** (dates differ)
- **Machareddi** repulsed Firoz Shah at **Kandukuru** (**1398**)
- **Gajarao Tippa** beat Firoz Shah at **Kambhammetta** (**1396**)
## SEC 1:100:2 | Gajapati rulers
- Gajapati rule — c. **1434–1541**
- Order of Andhra campaigns: **Kapilendra → Purushottama → Prataparudra**
- **Kapilendra** — attacked **Tiran Andhra** in **1448**; captured **Kondaveedu** c. **1450**
  - Kondaveedu under Vijayanagara till **1449**, fell to the Gajapatis c. **1450**
- **Hamvira**, son of **Kapileswara Gajapati** — defeated the Bahmani army attacking **Devarakonda**
- ★ **Prataparudra Gajapati** — defeated by **Krishnadevaraya**; his daughter **Annapurna Devi** married Krishnadevaraya
  - lost **Udayagiri**, **Kondaveedu** and **Kondapalli** to Krishnadevaraya (**1513–1519**)
## SEC 1:100:3 | Krishnadevaraya's campaign (1513-1519)
- Campaign began with **Udayagiri** → Kondaveedu → Kondapalli → Bejawada → march to **Cuttack** (**1518**)
## SEC 1:100:4 | Peace and administration
- **Naidus** — revenue officers in southern Andhra under the Gajapatis
- **Nayakas and samantas** — collected revenue, kept order; Reddi-era social order retained
- Peace terms (Krishnadevaraya–Prataparudra): **marriage alliance**, **Balakrishna idol**, lands **north of the Krishna** returned to the Gajapati
  - not annexation of Cuttack / the Gajapati heartland
- **Madala Panji** — temple chronicle of **Jagannath at Puri**; material on Gajapati history

# ROW 1:101 | Telugu language and literature to the 14th century - Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana, Palkuriki Somanatha
## SEC 1:101:0 | Earliest Telugu records
### Earliest inscriptions
- ★ **Kalamalla inscription** (Kadapa, c. **575 CE**) — first Telugu inscription; **Renati Chola** ruler **Erikal Mutturaju Dhananjaya**
- **Erragudipadu inscription** (Punyakumara, Renati Cholas) — first **fully available** Telugu inscription; **song / geya** style
- **Addanki** — first Telugu **poetic** inscription, **Taruvoja** metre; issued by **Panduranga**, commander of Eastern Chalukya **Gunaga Vijayaditya III**
- **Bezawada** — first **Madhyakkara** poem; issued by **Yuddhamalla II**
- **Pitikayagulla** — 8th c., post-Badami Chalukya influence on early Telugu script (not the first poetic inscription)
- Chejarla inscription — **Kubja Vishnuvardhana**
- Pre-Nannaya written Telugu: **Kalamalla, Vipparla, Addanki, Bezawada** (not **Motupalli**, a later Kakatiya-era record)

## SEC 1:101:6 | Language facts
### Family and status
- Telugu — **Dravidian** family; one of the **22** Eighth Schedule languages
- Sub-branch — **South-Central Dravidian** (with Gondi and Kui); Tamil, Malayalam — South Dravidian
- **Classical language** since **2008**
- Telugu Language Day, **29 August** — birth anniversary of **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy** (not Gurajada)
- **Trilinga** theory — Shaiva centres **Srisailam, Kaleshwaram, Draksharama**
### Script
- **Bhattiprolu** stupa inscriptions (c. **400 BCE**, Guntur) — Prakrit, variant of **Brahmi**, early Telugu place and personal names
- Then a common Old Kannada–Telugu script; separate Telugu script in the **11th–14th centuries**
### Court languages
- Prakrit age — royal language **Prakrit** (APPSC G2 2011 key); golden age under **Hala**
- **Gatha Saptasati** — compiled by **Hala**; Telugu words **atta, addam, potta, pilla**
- **Eastern Chalukyas** — official language **Sanskrit**; Telugu the people's language
  - bilingual records: eulogy in Sanskrit, grant details in Telugu
- Kakatiya period — Telugu grammar, poetics and **desi-metre** tradition developed; **Sanskrit** remained the inscriptional language
- **Nannaya's Bharatam** — fixed grammar and literary register of classical Telugu; began the **Kavitrayam** tradition
