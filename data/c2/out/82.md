# ROW 4:17 | Humidity, evaporation, condensation, clouds and forms of precipitation
## SEC 4:17:0 | Humidity terms
### Measures of moisture
- **Specific humidity** — water vapour per unit weight of air (**g/kg**); unaffected by changes in air volume
- **Absolute humidity** — water vapour per unit volume (**g/m³**)
- Air is **saturated** at relative humidity of **100 per cent**
- **Dew point** — temperature at which air becomes saturated; condensation begins (vapour → liquid or ice)
- Same vapour content + temperature rises → relative humidity **falls** (warm air holds more vapour); rises on cooling
- Water vapour **decreases** with altitude; up to about **4 per cent** in humid tropics, under **1 per cent** in deserts and polar areas
- **Isobar** — equal atmospheric pressure; **isotherm** — equal temperature
### Latent heat
- **Evaporation** absorbs latent heat; **condensation** releases it — powers **thunderstorms and cyclones**
- Latent heat of vaporisation of water — **540 cal/g** (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg); latent heat of fusion of ice — **80 cal/g**
- Steam burns more severely than boiling water — **latent heat released on condensing**
- **Refrigerator** refrigerant — **absorbs heat on evaporation** (inside), **releases it on condensation** (at the back)
- Higher pressure → boiling point **increases** (pressure cooker cooks faster); lower pressure at altitude lowers it

## SEC 4:17:1 | Uplift chain
- ★ Clouds result from **condensation** — collection of tiny water droplets or ice crystals
- **Condensation** — water vapour changing into liquid or ice particles
- **Precipitation** — fall of grown droplets as rain, snow or sleet
- Chain: uplift of warm air → **cooling** → **condensation** → clouds and precipitation
- Rising air cools **adiabatically** to the **dew point**, where condensation begins
- **Corona** — coloured rings close to Sun or Moon; **diffraction** by small water droplets in thin clouds

## SEC 4:17:2 | Clouds
### Cloud types
| Cloud | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Cirrus** | feathery; high, fair-weather cloud |
| **Cirrocumulus** | ripples |
| **Cirrostratus** | thin milky sheet; causes **halo**; high-altitude |
| **Cumulus** | heaped **cauliflower** tops, flat bases; fair weather; convective; can grow into cumulonimbus |
| **Nimbostratus** | low, dark, thick, shapeless; steady, continuous / **prolonged precipitation** ('nimbus' = rain-bearing) |
| **Cumulonimbus** | towering, up to about **15 km**, **anvil-shaped** top; thunderstorms and hail |
### Related phenomena
- **Waterspout** — funnel from a cumulus or cumulonimbus cloud over a water body
- **Blood rain** (coloured rain) — **dust or spores** mixed with raindrops; e.g. Sirocco dust over Italy, **Kerala's red rain of 2001**
- **Western disturbances** — about **10–15 cm** winter precipitation in north-western India; useful for rabi crops
- **Mango showers** — pre-monsoon convectional rain; **Mawsynram** — windward side (orographic rain)

## SEC 4:17:3 | Forms of condensation
### Dew, frost, fog, smog
- **Dew** — liquid moisture on cool surfaces, dew point above 0°C; **frost** — ice crystals, dew point at or below **0°C**
- Dew and frost favoured by **clear skies, calm air, high relative humidity and long cold nights**; clouds and wind prevent them
- **Fog** — ground-level cloud, visibility under **1 km**; **mist** thinner and wetter, visibility over 1 km
- **Los Angeles smog** — **photochemical** (needs sunlight, can form **PAN**); **London smog** — sulphurous, cool and humid
### Precipitation forms
- **Hail** — layered ice balls in cumulonimbus with strong updrafts
- **Sleet** — rain frozen into pellets; **snow** — ice crystals formed below 0°C
- **Rain** — drops exceeding **0.5 mm**; **drizzle** — fine drops under 0.5 mm, commonly from stratus clouds
### Water budget and rainfall
- **Combustion** is not a stage of the hydrological cycle; evapotranspiration, sublimation, groundwater flow are
- India — about **4,000 BCM** annual precipitation; about **1,139 BCM** utilisable (690 BCM surface, 449 BCM groundwater)
- Wet-bulb temperature above about **31.5°C** — body cannot cool by sweating; wet-bulb = dry-bulb only at 100% RH
- AP average annual rainfall about **966 mm** — about two-thirds from **South-West monsoon**, one-third from North-East monsoon
- Eastern side of Western Ghats — **leeward**, rain shadow, descending warm dry air; peninsular rainfall decreases west to east

## SEC 4:17:4 | Precipitation types
- **Convectional** — mango showers, daily equatorial afternoon showers
- **Frontal** — warm air lifted over cold air along a front; typical of temperate regions
- **Relief (orographic)** — contrast between windward and leeward sides
- **Rain-shadow area** — the **dry leeward** side of a mountain range
- **Aravallis** — little orographic rain in Rajasthan; winds run roughly **parallel** to them
- Intense rainfall: accumulated water falls when updrafts **weaken**

## SEC 4:17:5 | Optical phenomena & extremes
### Optics
- **Sun halo** — ring of about **22°**; refraction through hexagonal ice crystals in high **cirrostratus**
- **Rainbow** — Sun **behind** the observer, drops in front; refraction, dispersion and internal reflection
- **Secondary rainbow** — **two** reflections per drop; colours reversed, fainter (primary: one)
- Primary rainbow — **red** outside, violet inside
### Extremes
- **Silver iodide** — cloud-seeding agent; crystal structure **mimics ice**; needs suitable clouds already present
- **Acid rain** — pH below **5.6**; term coined by **Robert Angus Smith** (**1872**); mainly from SO2 and NOx
- **Cloudburst** — about **100 mm per hour** or more over **20–30 sq km**; usually from cumulonimbus

# ROW 4:18 | Air masses and fronts - classification and associated weather
## SEC 4:18:0 | Air mass basics
- **Air mass** — large body of air with relatively uniform **temperature and humidity**; narrow boundary between air masses = **front**
- **Bergeron** classification — first letter = **moisture**: c = continental, dry; m = maritime, moist
- Source regions: polar ocean **mP**, tropical ocean **mT**, tropical land **cT**, polar land **cP**
- Over ocean an air mass **gains** moisture; land drier; warm surfaces unstable; cold surfaces stable
- Loses source character by **exchanging heat and moisture** with surfaces it passes over
- Air masses control **temperature, humidity, stability and precipitation**
- **Atmospheric pressure** — force exerted by air

## SEC 4:18:1 | Air mass types
- **cA** (very cold, very dry) — **Arctic basin and Greenland ice**
- **cT** — Sahara and Arabia; **mE** — equatorial oceans; **mP** — high-latitude oceans

## SEC 4:18:2 | Modification
- Warm air over a colder surface — **cooled from below**, becomes **stable**; fog, stratus, drizzle

## SEC 4:18:3 | Types of front
- **Stationary front** — neither air mass advances; cloud and rain may persist **for days**
- **Warm front** — warm air rises **gently** over cold air; gradual uplift, gentle slope
- **Cold front** — steep; steep uplift
- **Occlusion** — lifts warm air off the ground

## SEC 4:18:4 | Passage of a front
- Approaching warm front clouds: **cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus**
- Warm front — falling pressure, steady rain; warm sector — warm humid air; after cold front — **cold, clear air**
- **Wind** — high pressure to low pressure; steeper pressure gradient → stronger

## SEC 4:18:5 | Polar front theory
- **Polar Front Theory** — **Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes** (Bergen School, Norway); wave cyclones on the polar front
- Mid-latitude cyclones form along **fronts**
- Temperate cyclone life cycle: **wave on front → warm and cold fronts → occlusion → dissipation**
- **Frontolysis** — dissolution of a front; **frontogenesis** — formation or sharpening
- **Arcus** (shelf/roll) clouds — leading edge of **thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts**

## SEC 4:18:6 | Air masses over India
### Climatic controls
- **Himalayas** block cold Central Asian air — north India about **3–8°C** warmer in winter than same latitude
- Winter **anticyclonic cell** — clear skies, light northerly winds, low humidity, large daily temperature range
- **South-west monsoon** — moist maritime Indian Ocean air fed by the low-level **Somali Jet**
- **AP** — dual influence; **north-east monsoon** from winter air returning over the Bay of Bengal
- **Leeward** slope — **rain shadow**; windward — orographic rain (e.g. Western Ghats)
### Air mass effects
- **Hygroscopic** nuclei — dust and salt particles for condensation
- New air mass can cause **sudden** rise or fall in temperature
- **Front** forms where contrasting air masses meet; lighter warm air rises over denser cold air

## SEC 4:18:7 | Exam corner
- **Polar anticyclones** (Siberian High, Canadian High) — cold, dense **cP and cA** air; cold-core, strongest near surface; clear skies, fog, frost
- **Arctic amplification** — meandering jet stream carrying warm air into the Arctic
- **Cold front** — advancing cold air lifts warm air sharply; cumulonimbus, heavy short showers, squalls, sudden temperature fall
- **Atacama** (Chile) — snow for first time in over a decade from an unusually far-north **Antarctic high**

## SEC 4:18:13 | Air masses affecting India; climatic effects; relief
- ★ Strong **Polar Vortex** — keeps **cold air up north** and **warm air down south**

# ROW 4:19 | Atmospheric disturbances - tropical cyclones, temperate cyclones, anticyclones, thunderstorms, tornadoes
## SEC 4:19:0 | Tropical cyclone formation
### Conditions
- Sea-surface temperature above about **26.5–27°C** over a large area — most essential (APPSC-GS 2025: form over **warm oceans**)
- Need **low vertical wind shear**; moisture favourable
- Belt of origin: about **5°–30°** N and S, especially **western** parts of tropical oceans
- Not at the Equator — **Coriolis force too weak** within about 5°
- Absent in **South Atlantic and south-east Pacific** — cool waters, strong wind shear
- Not over rivers or lakes; cold currents suppress them
### Structure and behaviour
- Rotation: **anticlockwise** (Northern Hemisphere), **clockwise** (Southern) — Coriolis
- Lowest pressure — **centre (eye)**; strongest winds — eyewall; spiral rainbands bring rain and wind
- In a cyclone, air moves **inward and rises**
- Weakens after landfall — **cut off from warm, moist oceanic energy**
- **Cloudburst** — about **100 mm per hour** over **20–30 sq km**; Himalaya during **June–August**

## SEC 4:19:1 | Tropical vs temperate
- Tropical cyclones pushed **westward** by **trade winds**; mature ones may recurve north-east under westerlies
- Tropical cyclones — **smaller and more intense**; temperate — larger, gentler winds
- Temperate cyclones — clear **frontal system** (warm and cold fronts; occluded front may develop)
- Temperate cyclones move **west to east** with the westerlies
- **East coast** of India (Odisha, AP, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu) — more cyclone-prone

## SEC 4:19:5 | Anticyclones, tornadoes, Western Disturbances
### Cyclone structure
- **Eyewall** — ring of towering cumulonimbus around calm eye; strongest winds, heaviest rain
### Tornadoes and thunderstorms
- **Tornado** — small, short-lived funnel tied to a thunderstorm; winds **105–322 km/h**, moves up to about 90–100 km/h
- Tornadoes spin from **severe rotating convection** — supercells
- Ordinary thunderstorm — short-lived **single-cell cumulonimbus**; rotating supercells give tornadoes
- **Waterspout** — tornado-like funnel over water; seen over Brahmaputra, **Barpeta, Assam** (26 August 2026)
- **Thunder** — air expanding explosively after heating by lightning
- **Damini App** — **lightning** alerts in 15 languages; **104** detection locations
- **Kaal Baisakhi / Nor'westers** — **Bihar, Odisha, West Bengal, Assam**; approach from north-west
- **Cloudburst** — very local disturbance (like thunderstorms, tornadoes, squalls); cyclones and anticyclones large-scale
### Anticyclones
- High-pressure centre, sinking air flowing **outward**; **clear, dry, calm, settled** weather
- Northern Hemisphere anticyclone rotates **clockwise**
- **Fronts** belong to **temperate cyclones** only
### Western Disturbances
- From the **Mediterranean**; affect NW India in **winter**; about **10–15 cm** rain for rabi crops, snow on mountains
- Western Disturbances and wind discontinuity lines — **synoptic** factors in hailstorms; plateaus and hills boost convection
