# ROW 2:13 | Cultural and Educational Rights - Articles 29-30; the right to property and the 44th Amendment
## SEC 2:13:0 | Articles 29-30
- **Article 30(1)** — right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice for **all minorities, religious or linguistic**
  - State may regulate standards but may not take away their minority character
- **Article 30(1A)** (inserted by **44th Amendment**) — **compulsory acquisition of property** of a minority educational institution
  - Amount fixed must not restrict or abrogate the Article 30 right
- **Article 350A** — instruction in the **mother tongue at the primary stage** for children of linguistic minority groups
- Six notified religious minorities (NCM): **Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis, Jains**
  - **Linguistic minorities** are not one of the six notified religious categories

## SEC 2:13:1 | Who is a minority?
- **'Minority'** — used in Articles **29, 30, 350A, 350B** but **not defined** in the Constitution
  - **NCM Act, 1992** also does not define it
- Unit for determining a linguistic minority — **the State**
  - Numerical inferiority and non-dominant status are relevant
- **Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v. Union of India** (**2014**) — RTE Act's **25 per cent quota** does not apply to minority schools

## SEC 2:13:3 | Right to property: journey
- **17th Amendment (1964)** — land under personal cultivation acquired only on paying **market value**
- **44th Amendment, 1978** — deleted **Articles 19(1)(f) and 31** from Part III
  - Made property a constitutional legal right under **Article 300A**

## SEC 2:13:4 | Article 300A & LARR 2013
- **Article 300A** — no person shall be deprived of his property **save by authority of law**
- **LARR Act, 2013** — replaced the **Land Acquisition Act, 1894**
  - Makes **Social Impact Assessment** mandatory (a feature, not the reason it replaced the 1894 Act)
- **GGR Housing v. State** (**Andhra Pradesh High Court**) — strict **statutory due process** in land acquisition under Article 300A

## SEC 2:13:5 | FR or not?
### Legal / statutory rights
- ★ **Right to vote** — a **legal (statutory) right**, not a constitutional or fundamental right
  - ★ **Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India** (**2009**) — SC held voting is merely a statutory/legal right
  - Statutory right under **Section 62** of the **Representation of the People Act**
- ★ **Right to vote and to be elected** — **legal rights**
- ★ **Right to information** — a **legal right**
  - Also read into **Article 19(1)(a)**
- ★ **Right to property** — a **legal right**
  - Constitutional legal right under **Article 300A**; available to **citizens, non-citizens and companies**
  - Not a Fundamental Right enforceable under **Article 32**
### Other classifications
- **Right to Work (Article 41)** — a **Directive Principle**, not a Fundamental Right
- Recognised under **Article 19(1)(a)** though voting is statutory (the **'voting paradox'**)
  - **Right to know candidate details**, **NOTA**, **secrecy of the ballot**

## SEC 2:13:6 | Exam corner
- ★ **44th Amendment Act, 1978** — deleted right to property from the list of Fundamental Rights (Part III)
  - ★ Made it a **legal right** under **Article 300-A** in **Part XII**
  - Removal was primarily to prevent property rights from obstructing **land reform**
- ★ At present, right to property is a **legal right**
- Fundamental Rights originally **seven**; after deletion of right to property (Articles 19(1)(f) and 31) — **six**

## SEC 2:13:7 | Minorities — meaning, types, Articles and notified minorities
- ★ **Article 350-A** — every State and local authority to endeavour to provide adequate facilities for instruction in the **mother tongue at the primary stage** for children of linguistic minority groups
- ★ **Article 30** does **not define** the term 'minorities'
  - ★ All minorities, whether based on **religion or language**, have the right to establish and administer educational institutions

## SEC 2:13:8 | Articles 29-30 — minority rights and educational institutions
- ★ **Article 30** — all minorities have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice

## SEC 2:13:9 | Right to property — Amendments → year → change
- ★ **Right to Property** — **not a fundamental right**
- ★ **Land Acquisition Act, 1894** — enacted by British rulers; came into effect on **1 March 1894**
  - ★ Purpose was **not land reform**, but land acquisition for **public institutions, organizations and companies**

## SEC 2:13:10 | Article 300A and LARR Act 2013 — provisions
- ★ Right to property — a **legal right available to any person**
  - ★ Originally one of the **seven FRs** under **Article 31**; one of the most controversial rights
  - ★ Removed as a fundamental right by the **44th Amendment Act, 1978**

## SEC 2:13:11 | Fundamental vs constitutional/legal right — classification
- ★ **Right to education** — a **Fundamental right**
- ★ **Right to vote and right to be elected** — **legal rights under an Act**
  - ★ A legal right is the offshoot of the **ordinary law**
- ★ **Right to Property** — a **statutory right**

# ROW 2:14 | Right to Constitutional Remedies - Article 32; the five writs and their scope
## SEC 2:14:0 | Article 32: clauses
### Status and significance
- ★ **Article 32** — called the **"heart and soul"** of the Constitution by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**
  - Makes the **right to a remedy** itself a Fundamental Right; direct access to the Supreme Court
- **'A right without a remedy is a worthless declaration'** — explains significance of **Article 32**; makes Part III effective, not merely declaratory
- Article 32 is part of the **basic structure** — cannot be abridged even by constitutional amendment
### Clauses
- **Article 32(2)** — **five writs**: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto
- **Article 32(3)** — Parliament may empower **any other court** to exercise SC's Article 32 powers; **no such law** made yet
- **Article 32(4)** — right **cannot be suspended** except as the Constitution provides (not by an ordinary law)
- SC jurisdiction under Article 32 — **original but not exclusive** (High Courts also issue writs under **Article 226**)
### Article 32 vs Article 226
- Both SC and High Courts can issue **all five writs**
- Article 226 — writs for Fundamental Rights and **'any other purpose'** → HC wider in **subject matter**; SC wider **territorially**
- HC can issue writs beyond its territory where the **cause of action**, wholly or partly, arises within its territory — after the **Fifteenth Amendment (1963)**
- Before **1950**, only the **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** High Courts had writ jurisdiction
- Writs originated as **prerogative remedies** of **English common law**
### Writ points
- **Habeas Corpus** — lies against **public authorities and private individuals**; no release where detention is lawful
- **Quo Warranto** — tests a claim to a **substantive public office** created by statute or the Constitution
  - **Any member of the public** (any interested person) can seek it; literally **'by what authority or warrant'**
- **Mandamus** — not ordinarily against **private bodies**, nor for discretionary or legislative functions or non-statutory instructions
- **Certiorari** — generally post-decision, but may also be used to **transfer a pending case**
### PIL and access
- **PIL** relaxes the rule of **locus standi** (only the directly aggrieved person may approach the court)
- **S.P. Gupta (1981)** — key PIL case; Justices **P.N. Bhagwati** and **V.R. Krishna Iyer** associated with PIL
- **Epistolary jurisdiction** — letters/postcards treated as writ petitions; **Sunil Batra** (prisoner's letter)
- SC examined an SOP for **24x7 urgent hearings** under Articles 32 and 226 in matters of **threats to life and liberty**

## SEC 2:14:1 | The five writs
### Meanings
| Writ | Literal meaning |
|---|---|
| Habeas Corpus | 'to have the body' |
| Mandamus | ★ **'we command'** |
| Prohibition | 'to forbid' |
| Certiorari | **'to be certified or informed'** |
### Mandamus
- ★ **Mandamus** — orders a public authority/institution to **perform a duty** it has refused or neglected
  - ★ Judiciary orders the **Executive** to perform its duty
  - Lies against **public officials, statutory bodies, inferior courts and tribunals**; not ordinarily against **private individuals**
### Certiorari
- ★ **Certiorari** — issued by a higher court to examine the functioning of a **subordinate court**; case record delivered to the higher court for review
- Quashes an order **already passed** by a judicial, quasi-judicial or administrative body
  - Grounds: lack or excess of **jurisdiction**, or **error of law** apparent on the face of the record
- Since **1991** — may lie against certain **administrative bodies** whose actions affect rights; not against private persons or legislative bodies
### Prohibition and Habeas Corpus
- **Prohibition** — directs a lower judicial body to **stop continuing proceedings** in excess of jurisdiction (acts while pending)
- **Habeas Corpus** — the **bulwark of individual liberty**; prompt remedy against unlawful detention
  - Lies against both public authorities and **private individuals**; the only writ available against private persons
- **Habeas Corpus** is among the five writs under **Article 32**; review, advisory opinions and special leave are other kinds of jurisdiction

## SEC 2:14:2 | Writ limits
- **Mandamus** cannot compel the Government to **make a law or policy**, but can enforce the terms of a policy **once laid down**
- Mandamus does **not** lie against the **President or Governors**, or private bodies without public duties
- Mandamus **lies against** a **government company or statutory corporation** performing a public function

## SEC 2:14:3 | Prohibition vs Certiorari
- **Prohibition** — **preventive** writ; acts while the case is pending
- **Certiorari** — **curative** writ; acts after an order is made

## SEC 2:14:5 | Cases & amendments
- **Article 32A** (barred challenges to State laws under Article 32) — inserted by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**
  - **43rd Amendment** omitted Articles **32A, 131A, 144A, 226A and 228A**
- **L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)** — judicial review under **Articles 226 and 227** is part of the **basic structure**
- **20 January 2026** — SC (Justices **Dipankar Datta** and **S.C. Sharma**) agreed to examine whether the **Enforcement Directorate can invoke writ jurisdiction**
  - Appeals by **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** against a **Kerala High Court** ruling

## SEC 2:14:6 | PIL & res judicata
- HC dismisses a writ petition **on merits** → same plea under Article 32 **barred by res judicata**; remedy lies in **appeal**
  - If dismissal was **not on merits**, Article 32 may be invoked

## SEC 2:14:7 | Exam corner
- **The Judiciary** — guardian of Fundamental Rights; Supreme Court is their **protector and guarantor**

## SEC 2:14:8 | Article 32 — clauses, scope, status and FIRSTs
- ★ **Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32)** — described by **B.R. Ambedkar** as the **"heart and soul"** of the Constitution
  - ★ Ambedkar: the Article without which the Constitution would be a **nullity**
- ★ **Article 32** — empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for enforcement of **Fundamental Rights**
- ★ **Article 32(2)** — extensive **original jurisdiction**: writs of habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto
- ★ SC does **not** have **exclusive** power to issue writs; High Courts too under **Article 226**
- ★ **Certiorari** — known as **'postmortem'** (higher court re-examines the subordinate court's decision)
- ★ **Quo warranto** — inquires into the legality of a person's claim to a **public office**

## SEC 2:14:9 | Writs → literal meaning, purpose and scope
- ★ **Habeas Corpus** — literally **'to have the body of'**; protection of **personal freedom/liberty**
  - ★ Can be issued by a **High Court** to secure the liberty of the individual
- ★ **Prohibition** ('to forbid') — to a **lower court**, prohibiting **continuation of proceedings**
  - ★ Issued when proceedings are **pending**; prevents a lower court from exceeding its jurisdiction
  - Mandamus directs activity; Prohibition directs inactivity
- ★ **Certiorari** — superior court to an **inferior court** to **transfer the record** of a case for review

## SEC 2:14:10 | Writs — PYQ match, Habeas Corpus and Quo Warranto
- ★ **Habeas Corpus** — lies against government authorities **and private individuals** who illegally detain
  - ★ Requires the detained person to be **produced before the court**
- ★ **Habeas Corpus** — the **greatest safeguard** of the liberty of a person

## SEC 2:14:12 | Article 32 vs Article 226 — SC and HC writ jurisdiction
- ★ **Article 32** — Supreme Court safeguards the **Fundamental Rights** (Right to Constitutional Remedies, Part III)
- ★ Only **Fundamental Rights** can be enforced under Article 32
- ★ **Enforcement of Fundamental Rights** — jurisdiction of **both** SC (Article 32) and HC (Article 226)
  - ★ High Courts have **broader** writ jurisdiction under Article 226
- ★ Power to enforce Fundamental Rights — **Supreme Court and High Courts**
  - **Article 13** — power of **judicial review** to SC and HCs
- ★ Guardian of Fundamental Rights — the **Judiciary** / the **Supreme Court**, not the **President**
- ★ **Supreme Court** — protector of fundamental rights and **Guardian of the Constitution**

## SEC 2:14:13 | Persons, cases and amendments on writ jurisdiction
- ★ **P.N. Bhagwati** — Chief Justice when **PIL** was introduced; with **V.R. Krishna Iyer**, liberalised **locus standi**
- ★ Ambedkar described **Article 32** as the **very soul** of the Constitution — it gives an effective remedy against violation of fundamental rights
- ★ SC does **not** have exclusive jurisdiction on **constitutional validity of Central laws**; **High Courts** also have it
- ★ **Kesavananda Bharati** (13 Judges) — judicial review under **Articles 32, 226 and 227** declared part of the inviolable **basic structure**
- ★ High Courts in a **better position** to protect rights — SC issues writs only for **fundamental rights**; HC under Article 226 for **any other matter** too

## SEC 2:14:14 | Universal angles
- ★ HC writ power covers **all five writs** — Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari
  - ★ SC's writ power limited to **Part III** (Fundamental Rights)

# ROW 2:15 | Directive Principles of State Policy - Articles 36-51, classification, implementation
## SEC 2:15:0 | Basics
- **Part IV** (**Articles 36–51**) — DPSPs; fundamental in governance but **not enforceable** by courts
- Immediate model — Constitution of **Ireland (1937)**, which had copied it from the **Spanish** Constitution
- Antecedents — **Instrument of Instructions** (**Government of India Act, 1935**) and **Karachi Resolution (1931)**
- Called the **'Instrument of Instructions'** and the **'conscience of the Constitution'** (**Granville Austin**)
- Aim — **social and economic democracy** and a **welfare state**
- **Article 36** — **definition of 'State'** for Part IV (as **Article 12** for Part III)
  - **Article 37** makes DPSP non-justiciable; **38 and 39** set welfare and policy goals

## SEC 2:15:1 | FR vs DPSP
- FRs — **legal sanction** enforced by courts; DPSPs — **moral and political** sanction

## SEC 2:15:2 | Classification (academic, not in Constitution)
### The classification
- Constitution does **not** classify DPSPs; **Socialist, Gandhian, Liberal-intellectual** is an **academic** arrangement
- **Gandhian** — Articles **40, 43, 43B, 46** (also **48**)
- **Liberal-intellectual** — **seven** Articles, incl. **44, 45, 48, 48A**, and **50**

| 45 | Early childhood care and education |

## SEC 2:15:3 | Article 39 clauses
### Articles 38–39
- **Article 38** — social order based on social, economic and political **justice**; **minimise inequalities** of income, status, facilities, opportunities
- **Article 39(a)** — **adequate means of livelihood** for men and women equally
- **Article 39(b)** — distribution of **material resources** of the community for the **common good**
- **Article 39(c)** — prevent **concentration of wealth** and means of production
- ★ **Article 39(d)** — **equal pay for equal work** for both men and women (a DPSP)
- **Article 39(e)** — health and strength of workers and children from unsuited work
- **Article 39(f)** — children develop in **freedom and dignity**; protected against **exploitation and abandonment**
  - Present wording via the **42nd Amendment**; it is a DPSP (Articles **24** and **21A** are FRs)
### Other Articles
- **Article 42** — **humane conditions of work** and **maternity relief**
- **Article 43A** — workers' participation in management of **industries** (not agriculture)
- **Article 45** — **early childhood care and education** until **six years**
  - **Article 21A** — FR to education for ages **6–14**; **Article 51A(k)** — related Fundamental Duty
- **Article 44** — Uniform Civil Code; **nutrition** is **Article 47**
- **Article 350B** — **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities** (350A — mother-tongue education)
- Courts use DPSPs as **interpretive aids**, reading welfare principles into **Article 21**

## SEC 2:15:5 | Implementation
- FRs — mostly **negative** obligations; DPSPs — mostly **positive** obligations on the State
- Directives **outside Part IV** (non-justiciable): **Article 351** — development of **Hindi**; **Article 335** — SC/ST claims to services
- **Article 40** (village panchayats) — implemented through the **73rd Amendment, 1992** (not the RTE Act)

- **Gujarat UCC Bill, 2026** — exempts **Scheduled Tribes**; Gujarat **second State** after **Uttarakhand**
  - Provides for **monogamy** and **equal inheritance**

## SEC 2:15:8 | DPSP — basics, definitions, source and antecedents
### Source and nature
- ★ Concept adopted from the Constitution of **Ireland**
- ★ **Instrument of Instructions** (**GoI Act, 1935**) → incorporated as the **DPSPs**
- ★ DPSPs are **fundamental in the governance** of the country (**Article 37**)
- ★ DPSPs are **non-justiciable** (FRs justiciable)
### Aims
- ★ Purpose — **social and economic democracy**
- ★ **Welfare State** ideal — enshrined in the **DPSPs** (Part IV, Articles 36–51)
  - ★ Also found in the **Preamble** — welfare state idea in **both**
- ★ **Economic Justice** — provided in the **Preamble** and the **DPSPs** (e.g. **Article 38**)
- ★ **Concentration of wealth** violates the DPSPs — **Article 39(b) and 39(c)**
- ★ Not an objective — a **Religious State** (aim: secular, democratic state)
### Contents
- ★ **International peace and security** — in the DPSPs
- ★ **Equal Pay for Equal Work** — DPSP, **Article 39(d)**
- ★ **Panchayati Raj** — laid down under the DPSPs (Part IV)
- ★ Classified for study into **socialistic, Gandhian, liberal-intellectual**; the Constitution itself has no classification
- ★ Provisions bearing on **Education** — **DPSPs, Rural and Urban Local Bodies, Fifth, Sixth and Seventh Schedules** (all five)
  - ★ **Article 45** (after **86th Amendment, 2002**) — early childhood care and education until age **six**
  - ★ **Eleventh Schedule** includes education; Panchayats and municipalities under **Articles 243G and 243W**

## SEC 2:15:9 | Article 37 — non-justiciability and the FR–DPSP balance
- ★ DPSPs **not enforceable** by courts (**Article 37**); FRs are enforceable
- ★ DPSPs are **guide posts for legislation** — duty of the State to **apply them in making laws**
- ★ DPSPs do **not** impose a **legal duty** on the State

# ROW 2:16 | Conflict and harmony between Fundamental Rights and DPSP - Champakam, Golaknath, Kesavananda, Minerva Mills
## SEC 2:16:0 | FR vs DPSP
- FRs — mostly **negative**, restraining the State; aim at **political democracy**
- DPSPs — **positive** directions; aim at **social and economic democracy**
- Law violating an FR — **void** under **Article 13** (to the extent of inconsistency)
  - Law cannot be struck down **merely** for violating a DPSP
- First FR–DPSP conflict case — **Champakam Dorairajan (1951)**
- Last word on the Ninth Schedule — **I.R. Coelho (2007)**

| **Champakam Dorairajan** | 1951 | FRs above DPSP |
| **In re Kerala Education Bill** | **1958** | **Harmonious construction** — both Parts given effect as far as possible; FRs prevail in direct conflict |
| **Golaknath** | **1967** | **Parliament cannot amend FRs**; FRs 'transcendental'; **prospective overruling** |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | 1973 | FRs amendable but not the **Basic Structure** |
| **Minerva Mills** | **1980** | **Balance** between FRs and DPSP |
| **Sanjeev Coke** | **1983** | Came after Minerva Mills |
- Champakam did **not** adopt harmonious construction
### Interpretation
- Courts use DPSPs to enrich FRs — **Article 48A with 21** (healthy environment), **Article 45 with 21**
  - Non-justiciability does **not** exclude DPSPs from interpretation

## SEC 2:16:2 | Amendments answering courts
### First Amendment and Ninth Schedule
- **First Amendment (1951)** — added **Articles 31A, 31B** and the **Ninth Schedule** to protect land-reform and welfare laws
- **Article 15(4)** (First Amendment) — response to **Champakam Dorairajan**
- **Ninth Schedule** — later grew to **over 280 entries**; not confined to land reforms
- **I.R. Coelho** — decided **2007**
### Amending power and Article 31C
- **Seventeenth Amendment** — widened 'estate' in Article 31A; land under personal cultivation
- **Twenty-fourth Amendment (1971)** — response to **Golaknath**; Parliament can amend **any part**, including FRs
  - Added **Article 13(4)** and **Article 368(3)**; made **presidential assent mandatory**
- **Twenty-fifth Amendment** — inserted **Article 31C**; replaced **'compensation' by 'amount'**
- **Twenty-sixth Amendment** — abolished **privy purses**
- **42nd Amendment** — attempted to extend Article 31C to **all DPSPs**; **struck down** in **Minerva Mills**
### Article 21
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** — widened **Article 21**; procedure must be **fair, just and reasonable** (due process)

## SEC 2:16:3 | Article 31C story
- Original **Article 31C** — shielded laws giving effect to **Articles 39(b) and 39(c)** from **Articles 14, 19 and 31**
  - Put the declaration that they implemented the policy beyond judicial question
- **Kesavananda Bharati** — **first part upheld**; **second part** (excluding judicial review) **struck down**

## SEC 2:16:4 | Ninth Schedule (Art. 31B)
- **Waman Rao (1981)** — Ninth Schedule laws after **24 April 1973** (date of Kesavananda judgment) open to challenge
- **I.R. Coelho (2007)** — rejected **blanket immunity**

## SEC 2:16:5 | Art. 39(b): material resources
- **Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra (2024)** — reaffirmed that **original Article 31C** (as upheld in Kesavananda) continues in force
- **Material resources of the community** — private ownership alone does **not** automatically bring a resource within Article 39(b)
  - Depends on its **nature, scarcity**, effect on **community well-being** and concentration of wealth
- **7:2** majority of the **nine-judge** Bench — written by **CJI D.Y. Chandrachud**
  - Rejected Justice **Krishna Iyer**'s **1977 Ranganatha Reddy** minority view

## SEC 2:16:6 | Exam corner
- DPSPs — **not enforceable** in courts but **fundamental in the governance** of the country (**Article 37**)
  - Duty of the State to **apply them in making laws**

## SEC 2:16:7 | FR vs DPSP — differences, legal status and doctrine
- ★ **Ninth Schedule** — introduced under PM **Jawaharlal Nehru** via the **First Amendment, 1951** (inserting **Article 31B**)
- **"FRs are the means and DPSPs are the ends"** — FRs provide the framework through which DPSP social and economic objectives are pursued

## SEC 2:16:8 | Landmark cases → position: Gopalan (1950) to Sanjeev Coke (1983)
### Cases
- ★ Chronology — **A.K. Gopalan (1950)** → **Golaknath (1967)** → **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** → **Minerva Mills (1980)**
- ★ **Champakam Dorairajan (1951)** — **FRs prevail** over DPSPs in case of conflict
- ★ **Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)** — first time SC **limited Parliament's power to amend**
  - ★ **Second largest bench** (**11 Judges**); largest — **Kesavananda Bharati** (**13 Judges**)
- ★ **Basic structure** doctrine first propounded — **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**
- ★ **A.K. Gopalan** — **personal liberty** (Article 21), **not** right to property
  - ★ Golaknath, Kesavananda Bharati, Minerva Mills concerned **property rights** and amending power
### Principles
- ★ **Preamble** — may be invoked to determine the ambit of **FRs and DPSPs**
- ★ Parliament **may amend FRs** to implement DPSPs

## SEC 2:16:10 | Ninth Schedule and Art. 31B
- ★ **First Amendment (1951)** — **protection of agrarian reforms** in certain States
  - ★ Added **Articles 31A, 31B** and the **9th Schedule**

# ROW 2:17 | Fundamental Duties - Article 51A, Swaran Singh Committee, enforceability
## SEC 2:17:0 | Basics
### Placement
- **42nd Amendment, 1976** — inserted **Part IVA**, single **Article 51A**, clauses **(a) to (j)**
- **Part IVA** — one of four Parts added after 1950: **IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA**
- Recommended by the **Swaran Singh Committee**
- Addressed to **citizens of India only** (not foreigners)
  - Unlike FRs under Articles **14, 20, 21, 25** available to non-citizens
- **Non-justiciable** moral obligations; 'available against the State' describes FRs
- Comparable list of citizens' duties among major democracies — **Japan**
  - Socialist States give rights and duties relatively equal weight
### Specific duties
- **Article 51A(k)** — only duty addressed to **parents or guardians**
  - Clauses **(b), (f), (h), (j)** generally regarded as **moral** duties
- **Article 51A(i)** — citizens protect **public property** (not Article 48A)
### Environment
- **Article 48A** — **Directive Principle** addressed to the State; citizens' duty — **51A(g)**; Article 21 read to include a clean environment
- **42nd Amendment, 1976** — inserted **51A(g)**, added **48A**, moved **forests** and **wild animals and birds** to the **Concurrent List** (Entries **17A, 17B**)
  - Followed the **1972 Stockholm Conference**

## SEC 2:17:1 | How the duties came
- **Swaran Singh Committee** — set up by the **Congress party** in **1976**, chaired by **Sardar Swaran Singh**
  - Need felt during the **Internal Emergency (1975-77)**, not 1962
  - Suggested **8 duties**; 42nd Amendment added **10**
- **10** duties after the 42nd Amendment; **11** at present
- **86th Amendment, 2002** — inserted **Article 21A**, substituted **Article 45**, added **51A(k)**
- **Justice J.S. Verma Committee (1999)** — **operationalising** Fundamental Duties; identified existing legal provisions

## SEC 2:17:2 | The eleven duties
### Clauses
| Clause | Duty |
|---|---|
| (a) | Respect for Constitution, **Flag** and **Anthem** |
| **(b)** | Cherish noble ideals of the **freedom struggle** |
| (c) | Uphold **sovereignty, unity and integrity** |
| (d) | **Defend** the country |
| **(e)** | Harmony, common brotherhood; renounce practices **derogatory to women** |
| (f) | **Composite culture** |
| (g) | **Natural environment** |
| **(h)** | **Scientific temper**, humanism, spirit of inquiry |
| **(i)** | **Safeguard public property**, **abjure violence** |
| (j) | **Strive towards excellence** |
| **(k)** | Parent/guardian to provide education to child aged **six to fourteen** |
- ★ **Eleven** Fundamental Duties; originally **10**, 11th added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**
### Interpretation and links
- **51A(g)** — natural environment includes **forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife**; **compassion for living creatures**
  - **Corporations**, as legal persons, may also carry the 51A(g) duty
- **Article 19(1)(a)** (free speech) correlative with **51A(c)** (sovereignty, unity, integrity)
- **UAPA, 1967** — duty to protect **sovereignty and integrity**
- **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** — promotion of **harmony**

## SEC 2:17:3 | Swaran Singh: not accepted
- Not accepted — Parliament's power to prescribe **punishment/penalties** for non-compliance
- Proposed duty not included — **payment of taxes**
- List incorporated in a **modified** form

## SEC 2:17:4 | Enforceability & cases
### Nature
- ★ **Rights are correlative with duties** (every right has a corresponding duty)
  - ★ **Holland** — the forbearance of others is called a duty
- Inspired primarily by the Constitution of the **former USSR**; FRs drew substantially on the **US** Constitution
- **Non-justiciable** — no direct court enforcement or penalty
  - **Legislation** may enforce them; courts may use them in **interpretation**
  - Courts may consider them in judging **reasonableness** of a law restricting an FR
- Called a **'code of moral precepts'** — non-justiciable, no direct sanctions; critics call them **superfluous**
- Criticisms — **not exhaustive** (voting, taxes left out), an **appendage to Part IV**, **vague**
- Significance — remind citizens of obligations, aid courts on reasonableness, support action against **anti-national** activities
  - Do **not** provide a basis for suspending Fundamental Rights
### Laws and cases
- **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971** — gives effect to **51A(a)** (Constitution, Flag, Anthem)
- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** — based on **Article 253** (implementing international agreements)
- **M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India** — **CSR** includes environmental protection; linked to **51A(g)**
- **Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala (1986)** — duties **cannot override** FRs; no **compelled participation** for respect of symbols
- **AIIMS Students' Union v. AIIMS (2002)** — rights and duties **correlative**; duties **as important** as rights
- **Vande Mataram** debate — duties under 51A **cannot curtail** **Articles 25 and 26** (freedom of religion)

## SEC 2:17:5 | Duty vs look-alike
- **51A(h)** — scientific temper, humanism, inquiry and reform; **protection of monuments** is **Article 49** (DPSP)
- **51A(e)** — **harmony and common brotherhood**; the **Preamble** uses **fraternity**
- Environment — **48A** and **51A(g)**; education — **45, 21A, 51A(k)**; heritage — **49** and **51A(f)**

## SEC 2:17:6 | Criticism
- Not exhaustive — omits **voting**, **paying taxes**, **family planning**

## SEC 2:17:7 | Exam corner
- **Protecting the elderly, differently abled and children** — **not** in Article 51A (APPSC Group 2, 2025 distractor)
  - Scientific temper **51A(h)**, excellence **51A(j)**, public property **51A(i)**

## SEC 2:17:8 | Part IVA — basics and amendments
- ★ Added on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee (1976)**; partly inspired by the **USSR**
- ★ Introduced by the **42nd Amendment** in **1976** — the **'10 commandments'**
  - ★ 42nd Amendment added a new chapter on **Fundamental Duties**; they are **not part of Fundamental Rights**
- ★ Enumerated in **Article 51-A**
- ★ In **Part IV-A** (not Part VI-A)
- ★ Currently **11** duties
- ★ **Right to Education** became an FR — **86th Amendment**
- ★ Parts — **IV A**: Fundamental Duties; **VI**: The States (**Articles 152–237**); **Part XI** is **not** The Municipalities

## SEC 2:17:9 | Swaran Singh Committee, 1976
- ★ **12 members** — chairman **Swaran Singh**
  - ★ Members included **A.R. Antulay**, **S.S. Ray**, **C.M. Stephen** (not Haridev Joshi)
  - Others: Rajni Patel, H.R. Gokhale, V.S. Sayed Muhammed, V.N. Gadgil, D.P. Singh, Dinesh Goswami, Vasant Sathe, B.N. Banerjee

## SEC 2:17:10 | Article 51A (a) to (k) — the eleven duties
- ★ **51A(a)** — **abide by** the Constitution and **respect** its ideals, institutions, Flag and Anthem
- ★ **51A(f)** — **value and preserve** the rich heritage of our **composite culture**
- ★ **51A(g)** — **protect and improve the natural environment**, including forests and **wildlife**
- ★ **51A(h)** — **scientific temper**; **51A(j)** — strive towards **excellence**
- ★ Not duties — **protect weaker sections from social injustice**; **strive towards abolition of untouchability** (Article **17**, FR)

## SEC 2:17:11 | Article 51A — distractors and look-alikes
- ★ **51A(c)** — **uphold and protect sovereignty, unity and integrity** (a Fundamental Duty)
- ★ Not Fundamental Duties:
  - ★ **To vote in public/general elections**
  - ★ **To protect monuments and places of national importance** — DPSP, **Article 49**
  - ★ **To protect the elderly, differently abled and children** — closest is **51A(k)** (parents educating children)
- ★ Real duties include scientific temper **51A(h)**, safeguard public property **51A(i)**, abide by Constitution **51A(a)**

## SEC 2:17:12 | Applicability, source and comparison
- ★ Concept taken from the **Russian (former Soviet Union)** Constitution; added by **42nd Amendment, 1976**

## SEC 2:17:14 | Enforceability — non-justiciable nature and cases
- ★ Fundamental Duties **cannot be enforced by writs**; moral obligations of citizens
- ★ Swaran Singh Committee recommended **penalties for non-compliance** in certain situations
- ★ **Ernest Barker** — **"Rights without duties are like men without shadows"**
- ★ Enumerated in **Part IV A**
- ★ Not Fundamental Duties — **organising Village Panchayats** (DPSP, **Article 40**); **respecting parents and teachers**
