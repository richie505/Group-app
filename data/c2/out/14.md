# ROW 1:112 | Sultanate provincial and military administration - iqta system, dagh and huliya, army organisation
## SEC 1:112:0 | Iqta system
### Origin
- Origin in the Caliph's empire: land granted to army officials for **limited periods** in lieu of a regular wage
- **Turkish**, non-indigenous institution (not an ancient Indian one)
- First iqta in India: **Hansi** (some sources say **Kuhram**) — granted by **Muhammad Ghori** to **Qutbuddin Aibak**
- ★ **Iltutmish** introduced the iqta in the Delhi Sultanate and built it into a **systematic** administrative institution
### Nature and working
- Iqta = **revenue assignment**, not private ownership; given to officials in place of salary
- Financed the **army**: linked military obligations with administration of assigned territories
- **Muqti** duties: maintain troops, collect revenue, preserve **law and order**, remit the **surplus** to the centre (not exempt from law and order)
- **Khalisa** = crown land, revenue for court and royal household; **inam** = grants to religious men and institutions

## SEC 1:112:1 | Changes by Sultan

| Sultan | Change |
|---|---|
| **Balban** | appointed **khwajas** (auditors) to check muqtis' accounts |
| **Alauddin Khalji** | large-scale **cash payment** to standing army |
| **Ghiyasuddin Tughluq** | improved postal system with **runners and horsemen** |
| **Firoz Tughlaq** | made iqtas **hereditary**; **revived** the jagir system (did not abolish it); returned to assignment-based payment |

- Before Firoz: iqtas **periodically transferable** between muqtis
- Firoz's revival **adversely affected army efficiency**
- Iltutmish and Balban paid through iqtas; Balban and Alauddin tightened control over the system
- Iqta = ancestor of the Mughal **jagir and mansab** system

## SEC 1:112:2 | Decimal army ranks
- Ranks, lowest to highest: sar-i-khail → sipah-salar → amir → malik → **khan**
- **Khan** = highest rank

## SEC 1:112:3 | Army & dagh-huliya
- **Alauddin Khalji** introduced **dagh** (branding of horses) and **huliya** (descriptive roll of each soldier)
- **Sher Shah** later revived dagh
- Purpose: prevent **substandard horses** and **proxy/substitute soldiers** at muster
- Turkish military innovations: **iron stirrup**, horse-based warfare, mounted archery, armour, stronger fortifications

## SEC 1:112:4 | Post & intelligence
- **Ulaq** = horse post; used even to bring **Khorasan fruit** to Delhi
- **Dawa** = foot post
- Alauddin Khalji's daily market reports: **barids** and **munhiyans** (munhiyans = secret spies)

## SEC 1:112:5 | Land & revenue terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mahrousa** | protected town |
| Khalisa | land under state administration (crown land) |
| Shiq | a portion or division |
| Iqta | revenue assignment |
| **Dagh** | branding of horses |
| Zabt | Mughal revenue system |
| **Madad-i-Mash** | revenue-free grants to **saints, religious institutions and mashaikhs** |

- Mansabdars were paid through jagirs or cash
- **Tanka** (Iltutmish) = **silver coin**, not a tax
- Maratha claims: **Sardeshmukhi** = **one-tenth**; **chauth** = one-fourth

## SEC 1:112:6 | Exam corner
- Sultanate land broadly = **khalisa** (crown land) and **iqta** (revenue assignments)
- Jagir belongs to the Bahmani, Qutb Shahi and Mughal systems
- **Amaram** = **Vijayanagara** assignment system (comparable to iqta and Mughal jagir)

| State | Army / bodyguard |

| Shivaji | cash-paid standing army |

## SEC 1:112:8 | Sultans → changes in the iqta and army pay
- ★ **Alauddin Khalji** developed **Khalisa** land on a large scale; took back lands held as **Milq**, Idrarat (pension) and **Waqf**; aim: a powerful, autocratic state
- ★ Iqta revenue was **not** deposited directly in the Sultan's account; the **iqtadar/muqti** collected revenue and taxes in his area

## SEC 1:112:11 | Land assignment terms → meaning and dynasty
- ★ Early Turkish administration was essentially **military**, because the country was parcelled out as **iqtas** among military leaders (who collected revenue and kept troops for the Sultan)

| ★ **Jagir** | Mughals |

## SEC 1:112:12 | Other medieval revenue terms → meaning
- ★ **Madad-i-Mash** (Mughal) — **revenue-free land** granted to learned/pious persons as charity; also to support saints

# ROW 1:113 | Sultanate economy - kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams; agriculture, urbanisation, trade and craft
## SEC 1:113:0 | Sharia taxes
### The four Sharia taxes
- ★ **Khams** (Khama) = state's **one-fifth (1/5)** share of **war booty** and treasure-troves
- **Kharaj** — land tax paid mainly by non-Muslim cultivators; usually **one-third**
- **Ushr** — land tax on Muslim-held land; **one-tenth** of produce
- **Jizya** on non-Muslims; **zakat** on Muslims
- **Firoz Tughlaq** abolished illegal cesses, kept only **kharaj, jizya, zakat and khams**
- Not Sharia: ghari, charai, haq-i-sharb (cesses), taccavi (loans), iqta/inam (land assignments)
### Related terms
- **Iqta** = land assigned to officials **in place of salary**
- **Ulema** = custodians and interpreters of religious law; **khuts** and **muqaddams** collected revenue from peasants
- **Silk and porcelain** imported from **China**; West Asia supplied textiles, glassware and horses
- Kakatiya land: **Maagani** = irrigated; **Metta** = rain-fed

## SEC 1:113:1 | Revenue & cesses
- **Alauddin Khalji** raised land revenue from one-third to **one-half** of produce
- **Alauddin Khalji** introduced **Ghari** (house tax) and **Charai** (grazing tax on milch cattle)
- ★ **Sharb** (Haqq-i-Sharb) = **irrigation** tax of **Firuz Tughluq**, at **10%** of yield, on recommendation of religious scholars

## SEC 1:113:2 | Agriculture & irrigation
### Irrigation
- ★ **Araghatta** = a large **wheel with earthen pots** tied to the outer ends of its spokes (Persian wheel); garland of pots on the rim; animal power lifts water from open wells
- ★ Word: **Ar** (spokes/wheel) + **Ghata** (pot); used in ancient and early medieval India
- Turks popularised the **Persian wheel (saqiya)**
- **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** — **first canal-builder** of the 14th century
- **Firoz Tughlaq** — built the **most canals**; longest from **Yamuna to Hisar**
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** — **Diwan-i-Kohi**; **taccavi** (sondhar) **loans** to cultivators to extend cultivation
### Crops and labour
- **Maize** not grown in 13th–14th c.; introduced later by the **Portuguese**
- **Ibn Battuta** mentioned slave markets; **Firoz Tughlaq** kept a separate **department for slaves**

## SEC 1:113:3 | Towns & founders
### Founders

| City / fort | Founder |
|---|---|
| **Jahanpanah** | **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** |
| **Siri** | Alauddin Khalji |
| Tughlaqabad | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq |
| Agra | Sikandar Lodi |
| Firozabad, Fatehabad, Hisar-Firoza, Jaunpur | Firoz Shah Tughlaq |

- Mughal centres: **Agra** and **Lahore** (imperial centres); **Allahabad** (Prayagraj) — Akbar's fort, provincial seat
### Alauddin's economy
- ★ **Alauddin Khalji** began the **Public Distribution System**
- Price-control markets: **military needs** shaped economic policy (maintaining the army)
- **Gold dinars** popular after his **South Indian conquests**
- Booty: **Alauddin** and **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** took **four-fifths**; **Firoz Tughlaq** restored the **one-fifth** (sharia) share
### Other
- **Muhammad Ghori** — gold coins with goddess **Lakshmi**, copying the **Gahadavala** type
- **Firuz Tughlaq** took about **1,300 Sanskrit manuscripts** from the **Jawalamukhi** temple library at **Nagarkot**

## SEC 1:113:4 | Trade & crafts
- **Paper** strengthened bureaucratic **record-keeping**
- **Khorasan fruit** reached Delhi by the **horse-post** system
- **Banjaras** — bulk goods such as grain on **oxen**
- **Multanis** and **Khurasanis** — overseas trade and financing; **Bohras** — inland trade

## SEC 1:113:5 | Coins
### Sultanate coins and crafts
- Crafts that flourished: **leather, metal-work, carpet-weaving**, textiles (not printing, tea, rubber)
- **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** ran about **36 royal karkhanas** (luxury goods for the court)
- **Tanka** = silver, **175 grains**; **jital** = copper; **shashgani** = **six jitals**
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**'s **token currency** (c. 1329–30): brass and copper tokens at par with silver tanka; failed due to **widespread forgery**
### Sher Shah
- **Rupiya** = **173 grains** of pure silver; currency in pure gold, silver and copper
- 1 Rupiya = **64** copper **Daams** (1 : 64)
- His surveyed revenue system = model for **Todar Mal's bandobast**

## SEC 1:113:6 | Exam corner
- **Dehliwal** — coin minted at Delhi before the Sultanate by the **Tomaras** and **Chauhans**
- Hindu society: **caste order** continued; **sati** and **purdah** spread
- **Amir Khusrau**: wealth and diamonds of Kakatiya **Warangal** attracted the **Delhi invasions**

## SEC 1:113:8 | Taxes and cesses → nature, payer, Sultan — Land revenue and land categories
- ★ **Alauddin Khalji** — first to charge **Ghari** (tax on houses and huts); also grazing tax on milk-giving animals
- ★ **Firuz Tughluq** — first to levy **Haqq-i-Sharb** (irrigation tax)

## SEC 1:113:9 | Agriculture — crops, irrigation, agrarian measures
- ★ **Araghatta** = **waterwheel** (Persian wheel) for irrigation of land
- ★ Operated by animals (**bullocks, buffaloes, camels**); lifts water from open wells
- ★ Mentioned in the **Panchatantra** and **Rajatarangini**

## SEC 1:113:13 | Coins → Sultanate and Sur rulers
### Sultanate
- ★ **Iltutmish** — first regular, standardised currency: silver **Tanka** and copper **Jital**; declared **Delhi** capital
- ★ Metals: **Tanka** silver, **Shashgani** silver, **Jital** copper
- ★ **Muhammad Ghori** — coins with **Devi Lakshmi**; Kalma in Arabic on some others
### Sher Shah and Akbar
- ★ **Sher Shah** — pure silver **Rupiya** (**178 grains**) and copper **Daam** (380 grains)
- ★ Exchange rate Rupiya : Daam = **1 : 64**
- ★ Sher Shah introduced pure **gold, silver and copper** coins; abolished mixed-metal coins; system continued under the Mughals, basis of British currency
- ★ **Akbar**, like Sher Shah, regulated the currency: gold coins, silver rupees and copper **dams** circulated

# ROW 1:114 | Sultanate society - nobility, ulema, position of women, slavery; conversion and syncretism
## SEC 1:114:0 | Social groups
- **Kulah-daran** (pointed-cap wearers) = **Sayyids**; **dastar-bandan** (turban-wearers) = ulema
- Golconda: foreign Muslims = **Afaqis**; Brahmins received **agraharas** and **Mustajir** (tax-farming) rights

## SEC 1:114:1 | Sultans & religion
- **Jalaluddin Khalji** — first Sultan with a **benevolent** attitude towards Hindus
- **Alauddin Khalji** — reduced **ulema interference**; told **Qazi Mughisuddin** he would decree what was good for the state
- **Nasiruddin Khusrau Shah** — Hindu gods worshipped in the royal palace (**1320**)
- **Sikandar Lodi** — **orthodox**: destroyed temples at **Mathura**, broke **Jwalamukhi** images at Nagarkot, imposed **jizya**

## SEC 1:114:2 | Ulema, jizya, Barani
- **Qazi-ul-Quzat** = chief judge; qazis administered the Sharia as interpreted by the ulema
- **Barani**: ideal of governance in **Fatawa-i-Jahandari**; his history = Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi
- **Jizya** = protection tax on non-Muslims in place of **military service**; exempt: women, children, old, disabled
- **Qutb Shahis** of Golconda **never imposed jizya**; Shia rulers who patronised Hindu temples

## SEC 1:114:3 | Women & slavery
### Religious policy
- **Firoz Tughlaq** — followed ulema, theocratic principles, respected the **Egyptian Caliph**, extended **jizya to Brahmins**
- Temple destruction: also **political** (royal legitimacy) and temple wealth — not only religious
- **Bhakti** movement: indigenous roots in **Alvars and Nayanars**; Islam reinforced its egalitarian, anti-ritual side
### Women
- **Sati** documented by **Ibn Battuta**
- **Jauhar** against Alauddin: **Ranthambhor (1301)**, **Chittor (1303)**
- **Razia Sultana** (1236–40) — only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate; faced resistance due to gender norms
- Not Delhi rulers: Lal Ded (female saint of Kashmir), Rudrama Devi (Kakatiya ruler), Chand Bibi (defended Ahmadnagar)
### Slavery
- Alauddin's third regulated market: **horses, slaves and cattle** (besides grain market and cloth market **Sarai-i-Adl**)
- **Firoz Tughlaq** — about **1,80,000 slaves**; created **Diwan-i-Bandagan**
- **Aibak, Iltutmish, Balban** were slaves (**bandagan**) → name **Slave/Mamluk** dynasty

## SEC 1:114:4 | Conversion theories
- **'Sword' theory** (forced mass conversion) rejected as a **colonial trope**; coercion isolated
- Patronage and social liberation = partial factors
- **Sufi influence** + **agrarian expansion** — major factor in **Bengal and Punjab** (through pirs and dargahs)

## SEC 1:114:5 | Syncretism: who did what
- **Al-Biruni** compared the Sufi idea of divine love with the **Gita** (not the Arthashastra)
- **Sheikh Nuruddin** — founded Kashmir's **Rishi** movement; vegetarian; served all castes
- **Chishtis** (Muinuddin, Nizamuddin Auliya, Baba Farid) — spoke to people in **Hindavi**
- **Padmavat** — **Malik Muhammad Jayasi**
- **Amir Khusrau** — credited with the **sitar** and **qawwali**

## SEC 1:114:6 | Exam corner
- **Gesu Daraz** (Bandanawaz) — moved to **Gulbarga** under **Firoz Shah Bahmani**; wrote **Mirqat-ul-Ashyaq** in Persian and early **Dakhni**
- **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** — granted villages to the **Bhadrachalam** temple

## SEC 1:114:8 | Sultans and the ulema → religious policy — Ulema, qazis and the Islamic character of the state
- ★ **Barani** did not see the Sultanate as truly Islamic: Sultans **supplemented Muslim law with their own regulations** (**zawabit**), not ruling strictly by sharia
- ★ Also given as Barani's reason: the **majority of the population did not follow Islam**

## SEC 1:114:14 | Sufis and scholars → contributions to syncretism
- **Dalail-i-Firozshahi** — Sanskrit texts translated in **Firoz Tughlaq's** reign (not linked with Baba Farid)

# ROW 1:115 | Indo-Islamic architecture - arcuate style, Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza, Tughlaqabad, provincial styles
## SEC 1:115:0 | Arcuate style terms
- **Arcuate** style = **arch, dome and vault**; trabeate = lintels, pillars, corbels
- **Surkhi** = **brick dust** in the lime mortar (for rubble masonry)
- **Mihrab** = **prayer niche**; minbar = pulpit; jali = pierced stone screen
- Decoration moved away from **human and animal figures** → geometric patterns, arabesque, Quranic calligraphy
- **Sayyid and Lodi**: Hindu-style gateways, **plaster ornament**, external **drip-stones**

## SEC 1:115:1 | Architectural firsts

| First | Building |
|---|---|

| Double domes on tombs (pioneers) | **Lodis** |

- **Sultan Garhi** — built by **Iltutmish** as tomb of his son **Nasiruddin Mahmud**
- Red Palace — Balban; Jamat Khana Masjid — Alauddin Khalji; Firoz Shah Kotla — Firoz Tughlaq's citadel at Firozabad

- **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq's tomb** — **sloping (battered) walls** and **marble dome**
- Chronology: **Qutb Minar** (early 13th c.) → **Tughlaqabad** (1320s) → **Lodi Garden tombs** (15th–early 16th c.) → **Fatehpur Sikri** (1570s)
### Qutb complex
- 'Qutb Minar and its Monuments' WHS: Qutb Minar, Quwwat-ul-Islam, Alai Darwaza, Iron Pillar, Iltutmish's tomb, Alauddin's tomb and madrasa, **Imam Zamin's tomb**; Tughlaqabad Fort is separate
- **Iron Pillar** — **Gupta** period; king Chandra = **Chandragupta II**; rust resistance
### Provincial
- Bengal: **Eklakhi tomb, Dakhil Darwaza, Chota Sona Masjid**
- **Lal Darwaza** Mosque = **Sharqi**; Moth ki Masjid = Lodi

## SEC 1:115:3 | Qutb Minar storeys
- **Five** tapering storeys; about **72.5 m**; bands of Quranic calligraphy
- **Aibak** built the first storey; **Iltutmish** completed it
- **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** replaced the **lightning**-damaged top storey with **two storeys** (marble and sandstone)
- Dedicated to Sufi saint **Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki**
- **Jahanpanah** (and a fortress at Allahabad) — **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; Siri Fort — Alauddin; Firoz Shah Kotla — Firoz

## SEC 1:115:5 | Qutb Shahi Hyderabad
### Dynasty features
- **Khalji** — **horseshoe arch** with **lotus-bud** fringe
- Tughlaq — battered walls; domes and false arches show **Syrian and Byzantine** influence
- Lodi — octagonal tombs, double domes; Sharqi — propylon screen
### Hyderabad and Golconda
- Hyderabad founded **1591**; capital moved from Golconda in **1592**
- Planned on the model of **Isfahan** by **Mir Momin Astarabadi**
- **Charminar** — marks the **end of a plague**; four arches, four minarets; Persian arches + Deccani stucco
- **Golconda Fort** — **acoustic system** and **piped water**
- **Qutb Shahi Tombs**, Ibrahim Bagh — **seven** rulers; **onion domes on square bases**
- Qutb Shahi style = **Indo-Saracenic** blend of **Persian and Hindu** traditions

## SEC 1:115:6 | Exam corner
- Golconda Jami Masjid gateway — **Nastaliq** calligraphy (Yazdani); Naskh for Quranic verses around the mihrab
- **Rajon ki Baoli** (Mehrauli) — **Daulat Khan Lodi**, **1506**, under Sikandar Lodi

## SEC 1:115:8 | Architectural FIRSTs and dynasty → style features
- **Sadak-e-Azam** of **Sher Shah Suri** = **Grand Trunk Road**; one of Asia's oldest major roads

## SEC 1:115:9 | Dynasty → style features
- ★ **Horse-shoe arch** first introduced in the **Alai Darwaza** (built by **Alauddin Khalji**)

## SEC 1:115:10 | Sultans → monuments built (Slave and Khalji)
- ★ **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque, Delhi — **Qutb-ud-din Aibak**; built with materials from demolished temples; near the Qutb Minar; earliest Indo-Islamic architecture in India
- ★ **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra** — a **mosque**; Quwwat-ul-Islam and Adhai Din ka Jhonpra are at **Delhi and Ajmer** respectively
- ★ Chronology: **Qutb Minar** (Aibak 1206–10, Iltutmish 1211–36) → **Tughluqabad fort** (Ghiyasuddin Tughluq, 1320–25) → **Lodi Garden** (Lodi dynasty, 1451–1526) → **Fatehpur Sikri** (Akbar, 1556–1605)

## SEC 1:115:11 | Sultans → monuments built (Tughlaq onwards), chronological order
- ★ **Qila-i-Kuhna**, Delhi — built by **Sher Shah** in **1541**; noted for elegant architecture

## SEC 1:115:13 | Qutb complex, Delhi — site → features
- ★ **Qutab Minar** named after Sufi saint **Khwaja Qutb-ud-din Bakhtiyar Kaki** (commonly believed; debated)
- ★ Begun by **Qutb-ud-din Aibak**, completed by **Iltutmish**

## SEC 1:115:14 | Provincial styles — region / dynasty → monuments
- ★ **Gol Gumbaz**, Bijapur (Vijayapura), Karnataka — one of the **largest domes** in the world; massive unsupported dome, **second-largest** after **St. Peter's Basilica**
- ★ 17th-century mausoleum of Sultan **Mohammad Adil Shah**

## SEC 1:115:16 | Universal angles
- ★ **Rajon ki Baoli** — 16th-c. stepwell, **Mehrauli** Archaeological Park, Delhi; recently conserved by **ASI** with the **World Monuments Fund India**
- ★ Four-tiered; built c. **1506** by **Daulat Khan Lodi** under **Sikandar Lodi**; Lodi-period water engineering
- ★ **Tomb of Balban** (Mehrauli) — **first true arch** in India
