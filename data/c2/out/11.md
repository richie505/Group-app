# ROW 1:90 | Hoysalas - Vishnuvardhana and Ramanuja; Belur, Halebid and Somanathapura temple architecture
## SEC 1:90:0 | Capitals
- ★ **Dwarasamudra** — capital of the **Hoysalas**
- ★ Dwarasamudra = modern **Halebid / Halebidu**, **Hassan district**, Karnataka
- **Belur** (also in Hassan) — the **earlier** Hoysala capital, before Dwarasamudra

| Capital | Dynasty |

| Madurai | Pandya |

## SEC 1:90:1 | Southern capitals
- Hoysalas — region: **Karnataka** (capitals Belur, later Dwarasamudra/Halebidu)
- Constant wars with the **Yadavas of Devagiri** and the **Pandyas**
  - **Singhana** defeated the Hoysalas; **Mahadeva Yadava** was defeated by them
  - **Narasimha III** was defeated by the Pandyas

## SEC 1:90:2 | Vishnuvardhana
- Original name **Bittideva** (also **Bittiga**)
- Converted from Jainism to Vaishnavism under **Ramanuja** (who had fled Chola persecution)
- Queen **Shantala Devi** remained a Jain
- Title **Talakadugonda** — after defeating the **Cholas at Talakad**
- **Veeragallu** (hero stone, Kannada inscription) at **Neeragunda**, Arsikere taluk, found **28 June 2026** — may settle the disputed **date of Vishnuvardhana's death**

## SEC 1:90:3 | Rulers & the end
- **Veera Ballala II** (1170–1220) — defeated **Yadava Bhillama**; made the Hoysalas **independent**
- **Narasimha III** — defeated by Pandya **Maravarman Kulasekhara**; Keshava Temple, Somanathapura built in his reign
- **1311** — **Malik Kafur** took Dwarasamudra (campaign hit Yadavas, Kakatiyas, Hoysalas, Pandyas)
- **1342** — death of **Ballala III**; Hoysala territory passed to **Vijayanagara**
- **Kanchi** — capital of the **Pallavas** (Warangal Kakatiya, Dwarasamudra Hoysala, Madurai Pandya)

## SEC 1:90:4 | Sacred Ensembles (UNESCO 2023)

| Temple | Builder | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Chennakeshava, Belur | **Vishnuvardhana** | 1117 |
| Hoysaleswara, Halebid | **Ketamalla**, officer of Vishnuvardhana | c. 1121; a **Shiva** temple (not Vishnu) |
| Keshava (Vishnu), Somanathapura | **Somanatha**, general of **Narasimha III** | **1268** |

- **Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas** — UNESCO World Heritage List in **2023**

## SEC 1:90:5 | Hoysala style
- Style: generally **Vesara** in exam keys (also described as an evolution of Dravida)
- Material: soft **chlorite schist (soapstone)**
- **Stellate (star-shaped) plan**, peaked in the **12th century**
- Raised platform of about one metre — **jagati**
- **Lathe-turned pillars**, finely **polished** (not left rough); three or four sections on one base, leaping animal heads; influenced **Vijayanagara** architecture
- **Madanika (Salabhanjika)** bracket figures — **Belur** (not Somanathapura)

## SEC 1:90:6 | Exam corner
- ★ Hoysala monuments — **Halebid and Belur** (Karnataka)
  - ★ Hoysaleshvara temple, Halebidu — dedicated to **Shiva**; Chennakeshava temple at Belur also Hoysala style
- Best-preserved **star-shaped plan** — **Keshava Temple, Somanathapura**

## SEC 1:90:10 | Hoysala temples → builder, deity, site and UNESCO listing
- ★ **Hoysaleshwara Temple** (Halebidu, Karnataka) — example of **Vesara** style
  - ★ Built in **dark schist** stone by the Hoysala king; "Lord of the Hoysalas"
  - ★ Hoysala temples called **hybrid / Vesara** — neither fully Dravida nor Nagara

## SEC 1:90:11 | Hoysala style — features, terms and sculptors
- ★ Hoysala, Gahadavala, Kakatiya, Yadava — **none** established their kingdoms in the early 8th century AD
- ★ Hoysalas ruled parts of present **Karnataka** and most of the **Tamil** region
- ★ Achieved **independence** by the close of the **12th century**; ended at the beginning of the **14th century**

# ROW 1:92 | Kakatiyas - origins, Beta I, Rudradeva, Ganapatideva; Kakatiya-Yadava-Hoysala relations
## SEC 1:92:0 | Origins
### Progenitor and religion
- Progenitor: **Kakara Gundana of Magallu** or **Venna Bhupati of Bayyaram**
- Early Kakatiyas — **Jains**, later **Shaivas**; sovereign phase **1163–1323**
- **Kakati** = **gourd**; **Kakatamma / Kakati Devi** linked with the Jain **Kushmandini**
### Base and first mention
- Early base — **Hanamkonda Vishaya** (modern Karimnagar, Warangal, Khammam)
- Served the **Rashtrakutas** first, then the **Kalyani Chalukyas**
- **Magallu grant** (**956**) of Eastern Chalukya **Danarnava** — **first mention** of the Kakatiyas (through **Gundyana**)
- **Prola I** — emblem changed from **Garuda** to **Varaha**
- **Rudradeva** — first sovereign (**1163**); **Prataparudra II** — last ruler

## SEC 1:92:2 | Ganapatideva
- **1210** — Ganapatideva and **Choda Tikka of Nellore** killed **Prithviswara**, ending the **Velanati** kingdom; title **Prithviswara Shirakhandika Kreeda Vinoda**
- Married **Pinnachodi's** daughters **Narama and Perama**; their brother **Jayapa** made **Gajasahani** (chief of elephant forces)
- **1257** — with **Akkana and Bayyana**, killed **Mummadi Rajendra Chola** — final end of the **Velanati Chodas**
- **Tikkana** — envoy to Ganapatideva; Ganapatideva restored **Manumasiddhi II** to the **Nellore** throne
  - Tikkana's **Nirvachanottara Ramayana** — dedicated to **Manumasiddhi** (not Ganapatideva)
- **Battle of Muttukur (1263)** — **Pandyas** defeated Ganapatideva; **Nellore** fell to them
- **Rudrama Devi** (ruled **1262–1289**) — trained by her mother **Kupama Devi**; **Mailamba** was Ganapatideva's sister
  - Defeated the **Pandyas**, **Eastern Gangas**, **Seunas of Devagiri** — not the **Western Chalukyas** (declined long before)

# ROW 1:93 | Rani Rudrama Devi and Prataparudra; Marco Polo's account; fall to the Delhi Sultanate

# ROW 1:94 | Kakatiya administration - Nayankara system, tank irrigation, trade, Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam
## SEC 1:94:0 | Administrative units
- Largest to smallest: **Rashtra / Mandala → Nadu / Bhumi → Sthala → Grama**
- **Ayagar** system — **twelve** village functionaries (APPSC G2 2012); paid by produce shares and service lands

## SEC 1:94:1 | Officers & terms
- **Talari** — village **watchman**
- **Karanam** — accountant
- **Sunkadhikari / Sunkamanya** — collected tolls
- **72 Niyogas** (central departments) — under the **Bahattara Niyogadhipati**

## SEC 1:94:2 | Nayankara system
### Kakatiya Nayankara
- Reorganised / introduced by **Prataparudra II**
- **Nayakasthalam** — villages given to Nayakas **instead of salary**; kept a fixed army, paid a fixed annual sum
- **72 Nayankaras** (one account), each guarding a fort; together held **one-fourth** of the land

## SEC 1:94:3 | Military & persons
