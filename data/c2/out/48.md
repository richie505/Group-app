# ROW 2:75 | Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013 - structure, jurisdiction, working; Central Vigilance Commission
## SEC 2:75:0 | Road to the Lokpal
### Ombudsman idea
- **Ombudsman** — first set up in **Sweden** in **1809** (Sweden's 1766 law relates to right to information, not the Ombudsman)
- Indian equivalents: **Lokpal** (Union) and **Lokayukta** (State)
- Term **"Lokpal"** coined by **Dr. L.M. Singhvi**
### Committees
- **First ARC** (set up **1966**) — recommended two-tier **Lokpal-Lokayukta** system in **1966**; **Second ARC** reiterated it in **2007**
- **Santhanam Committee** — associated with the **CVC** and the **CBI**, not the Lokpal
### Bills and the Act
- ★ First **Lokpal Bill** presented in **1968** (4th Lok Sabha)
- Passed by Lok Sabha in **1969**; lapsed on dissolution of the Lok Sabha before Rajya Sabha passed it
- **India Against Corruption** — led by **Anna Hazare**; central to the **2011** campaign for the **Jan Lokpal Bill**
- Bill re-introduced **2008**; Parliament passed the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Bill** in **December 2013**
- President's assent **1 January 2014**; Act came into force **16 January 2014**
- Chronology: Sweden ombudsman (**1809**) → first Lokpal Bill (**1968**) → Maharashtra's first Lokayukta (**1971**) → first Lokpal appointed (**2019**)
### Investigation
- Lokpal **Inquiry Wing** — preliminary inquiry into offences under the **Prevention of Corruption Act**
- AP **Anti-Corruption Bureau** — police agency investigating **Prevention of Corruption Act** offences, incl. **trap** and **disproportionate-assets** cases

## SEC 2:75:1 | Composition
- Lokpal is a **statutory body**, not constitutional
- Chairperson/member **cannot be an MP or MLA**
- Chairperson: former **CJI**, Supreme Court judge, or an **eminent person** with **25 years'** relevant experience
- Term: **five years** or till **70 years** of age, whichever earlier; **no reappointment**
  - A member may become Chairperson within the total five-year period
- Act also covers public servants **outside India**; bars complaints filed after **seven years** from the alleged offence

## SEC 2:75:2 | Selection Committee (5)
- Chaired by the **Prime Minister**
- Members: **Speaker**, **Leader of the Opposition** in Lok Sabha, **CJI** (or a Supreme Court judge he nominates), an **eminent jurist**
- **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** — **not** a member
- Act also covers public servants **outside India**

## SEC 2:75:3 | Jurisdiction & time limits
### Structure
- Lokpal = Chairperson + not more than **eight** members; at least **half judicial** members
- **Search Committee** — at least **seven** persons
- Asset declaration: **2016 amendment** left form and manner to be prescribed by the **Central Government** (not the Constitution)
### Jurisdiction
- Central officials of **Groups A, B, C and D**; **Group C and D** complaints referred to the **CVC**, which reports back to the Lokpal
- **NGOs, trusts, societies** receiving **foreign contributions** above **₹10 lakh** — covered
- **Anything said or any vote** by an MP in Parliament — **excluded**
- PM: allegations on **international relations, security, public order, atomic energy, space** — excluded
- Inquiry against PM on other matters: **full Bench**, at least **two-thirds** majority; held **in camera**
### Time limits
| Stage | Limit |
|---|---|
| Preliminary inquiry | **90 days** + 90 days (reasons in writing) |
| Inquiry Wing's report | **60 days** |
| Investigation | **6 months** + 6 months |
| Special Court trial | **one year**, extendable up to **two years** |
### Lokayuktas
- Governor consults **Chief Justice of the High Court** and **Leader of the Opposition** (not the CEC) when appointing a Lokayukta
- Lokayukta recommendations — **binding in some States only**, advisory in many
- AP: **Lokayukta** handles higher functionaries, **Upa-Lokayukta** the rest; coverage includes **CM and MLAs**
- **Section 17A, Prevention of Corruption Act** — **prior approval** before investigating decisions/recommendations of public servants; Supreme Court **split verdict**, "sanction paralysis" debate

## SEC 2:75:5 | State Lokayuktas

- Annual consolidated report submitted to the **Governor**
- Weakness: many lack an **independent investigating wing**; depend on **State police** or **ACB**

# ROW 2:76 | Central Information Commission and State Information Commissions; CBI and its legal basis
## SEC 2:76:0 | Status & origin
- **CBI** — set up **1963** on the **Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption** (1962–64) recommendation
- **CVC** — set up **1964** by executive resolution; statutory under the **CVC Act, 2003**; apex vigilance body, not constitutional
- **No constitutional bar** on CBI investigating economic offences — it does handle them

## SEC 2:76:1 | Key milestones
### CBI
- Not a **constitutional body** — set up by **MHA resolution**; powers from the **DSPE Act, 1946**
- Sequence: **Special Police Establishment (1941)** → **DSPE Act (1946)** → **CBI (1963)** → CVC (1964)
- Handles corruption plus **economic** and **special crimes**
- Director: minimum tenure **two years**, extendable up to **five years** (**2021**)
- **Director of Prosecution** — appointed on **CVC's** recommendation for **two years**
- **BHARATPOL** (**January 2025**) — real-time **Interpol** assistance requests; developed by the **CBI**
### Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1997)
- Gave the **CVC superintendence** over the CBI; fixed **two-year** tenure for the CBI Director
- Did **not** transfer investigation decisions to the CVC — **CBI alone** decides whether to investigate
- DSPE Act revised in **2003** accordingly
### RTI
- **RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019** — Centre may fix tenure and salaries of CIC and SIC members
- **2019 Rules** — Information Commissioner's term fixed at **three years**

## SEC 2:76:2 | Who picks whom
| Post | Appointed by | Committee |
|---|---|---|
| **CBI Director** | Central Govt | **PM**, **LoP** in Lok Sabha (or leader of largest opposition party), **CJI** or SC judge nominated by him |
| **Central Vigilance Commissioner** | President | **PM**, **Union Home Minister**, **LoP** in Lok Sabha |
| **Chief Information Commissioner** | President | **PM**, **LoP** in Lok Sabha, **Union Cabinet Minister** nominated by PM (no CJI) |
| **CEC and ECs** (2023 Act) | President | **PM**, a **Union Cabinet Minister**, **LoP** in Lok Sabha (no CJI) |

## SEC 2:76:3 | CBI
- Founder Director — **D.P. Kohli**; annual memorial lecture in his name
- **K. Santhanam** — headed committee recommending the CBI; **Vineet Narain** — petitioner (1997); **Vohra** — 1993 committee
- CBI — India's **National Central Bureau** for **Interpol**
- Other units: **OCSAE** — online child sexual abuse material; **BHARATPOL** — international assistance requests; **economic offences** cells — bank securities and fraud
- **Section 6, DSPE Act** — CBI needs State **consent** (general or specific) as **police** is in the **State List** (Entry 2)
- **Vineet Narain** decided **1997**; DSPE Act revised **2003**

## SEC 2:76:4 | CVC
- Present PM-headed CBI Director selection committee — introduced by the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013** (until 2014, DSPE Act alone)
- Composition: **one Central Vigilance Commissioner** + up to **two Vigilance Commissioners**
- Tenure: **four years** or till **65 years**, whichever earlier
- Appointed by the **President**
- Supervises the **CBI** in corruption cases; competent authority under the **Whistle Blowers Protection Act**
- Statutory, not constitutional
- Does **not conduct trials** of CBI corruption cases — superintendence and recommendations only

# ROW 2:78 | Centralization vs decentralization - concepts, arguments and Indian experience
## SEC 2:78:0 | Forms: weakest to strongest
- **Delegation** — entrusts tasks to **semi-autonomous bodies**
- **Devolution** — transfers powers to **elected local governments** with their own mandate and **revenue**

## SEC 2:78:1 | Dimensions
| Dimension | Concerns |
|---|---|
| **Fiscal** | power to **spend** and **raise funds and taxes** |
| **Political** | **decision-making** |
| **Administrative** | **implementation** through staff and functions |
- India: **political** power transferred, some **administrative**, **fiscal** power **least** — the decentralisation gap
- Elected local bodies **without fiscal decentralisation** may remain **powerless**; elections alone are insufficient

## SEC 2:78:2 | Meaning
- Features: **autonomy**, **people's participation**, **empowerment** of local communities (not "instilling non-confidence")
- **Subsidiarity** — function at the **lowest competent level**; moves up only when that level cannot perform it
- **Three Fs** of PRIs — **Funds, Functions, Functionaries**; effective devolution needs all three
- **Local self-government** = **democratic decentralisation** — because it brings **community participation** and **local accountability**
- Local functions: **public health**, **sanitation**, **water supply**; **judicial review** — higher courts, not local

## SEC 2:78:3 | Views on the village
### Gandhi, Nehru, Ambedkar
- **Gandhi** — **Gram Swaraj**: republic of **self-sufficient villages** with decentralised economic and political power
- **Nehru** — **centralised planned state**, **heavy industry** (National Planning Committee, Bombay Plan)
- **Ambedkar** (**4 November 1948**) — village a seat of **caste domination**; "sink of **localism**, den of **ignorance**, **narrow-mindedness** and **communalism**"
### Article 40 and the start of Panchayati Raj
- **Article 40** — **Directive Principle** (Part IV), **non-justiciable**
- Article 40 — **compromise**: Gandhian panchayats named as objective; non-justiciability accommodated **Ambedkar's** reservations
- Nehru inaugurated Panchayati Raj at **Nagaur, Rajasthan**, on **2 October 1959**
  - AP followed on **1 November 1959**; CDP **1952**; Balwant Rai Mehta Committee appointed **January 1957**

## SEC 2:78:4 | Articles & Bills
- **64th Amendment Bill (1989)**, Rajiv Gandhi — passed **Lok Sabha**, failed in **Rajya Sabha** amid fears of **centralisation**
- **Article 40** — directs the State to organise **village panchayats** as units of self-government
  - Articles **243A, 243B, 243G** came later with the **73rd Amendment**

## SEC 2:78:5 | Arguments
- For **centralisation** after Independence: **nation-building after Partition**, **unity** and **security**
- **Elite capture** by dominant local castes — cited **for centralisation**, not decentralisation

## SEC 2:78:6 | Exam corner
- Power to **withdraw at will** → **delegation**, not **devolution** (the decisive test)
- Incomplete devolution of three Fs → PRIs stay **implementing agencies**, not **planning units**
- Historical village autonomy in Andhra — **12-Ayagar** system under the **Kakatiyas** and **Vijayanagara**

## SEC 2:78:7 | Centralisation and decentralisation — definitions, forms and dimensions
- ★ Local government attributes: **statutory status**, **local community participation**, **power to tax**
  - ★ **Central control** — not an attribute (aim is decentralisation)

## SEC 2:78:9 | Articles, Amendments and Bills on local self-government
- ★ **Article 40** — organise **village Panchayats** and endow them with powers to function as units of **self-government**
- **Article 33** — Parliament's power to modify Fundamental Rights (under Rights to Constitutional Remedies)

## SEC 2:80:2 | Balwant Rai Mehta vs Ashok Mehta
### Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
- Examined the **CDP (1952)** and **NES (1953)** — not IRDP
- Central idea: **democratic decentralisation** through **three-tier elected local bodies**
- **Direct** election to the Gram Panchayat; **indirect** to Panchayat Samiti and Zila Parishad
- **Panchayat Samiti** — **executive** body; **Zila Parishad** — **advisory, coordinating, supervisory**
- **District Collector** as **chairman** of the Zila Parishad
  - Balwant Rai Mehta, First ARC, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao seen as cutting the Collector's development role; **Hanumantha Rao** the exception
### Ashok Mehta Committee
- Appointed **December 1977** by the Janata Govt of **Morarji Desai**; reported **August 1978**
- **Two-tier** system; **four-year** term; **Mandal Panchayat** for a group of villages; **Zila Parishad** — district-level executive
- **District** — first point of decentralisation below the State
- Supersession: elections within **six months**
- **Constitutional recognition** for PRIs; a **Minister for Panchayati Raj** in the State cabinet; **official participation of political parties**
- First to seek constitutional recognition and party-based elections (sources differ: APPSC 2018 key credits **L.M. Singhvi**)
- Not implemented: **Janata government fell** before completing its term
- Panchayats formed after 1977 on its lines — **Second Generation Panchayats**
### Other committees

## SEC 2:81:2 | Compulsory vs voluntary
### Basics
- ★ **Grama Sabha** — example of **direct democracy** (all registered voters participate directly)

  - 39(b) resources, 44 common civil code, 48 agriculture/animal husbandry

### Finance Commissions
- **SFC** — constituted every **five years** by the **Governor**; recommendations **advisory**
