# ROW 2:62 | Election Commission of India - composition, appointment (2023 Act), powers and functions
## SEC 2:62:0 | Status & composition
### Constitutional base
- **Article 324** — Election Commission; lies in **Part XV** (Articles 324-329)
- Article 324(1): ECI superintends elections to **Parliament, State Legislatures, President, Vice-President**
  - Does NOT cover **Panchayats and Municipalities** — these go to the **State Election Commissions**
- **ECI** decides every step: from announcement of schedule to declaration of results
- **Model Code of Conduct** — not a statute; rests on **Article 324**; applies from **announcement of the election schedule** (Harbans Singh Jalal, 1997)
### Members
- **CEC and ECs** — **equal powers**; differences settled by **majority**
- Salary on par with a **Supreme Court judge**; service conditions cannot be varied to their disadvantage
- Demand: ECI expenses be charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** (ECI depends on DoPT, Law and Home Ministries)
### Related provisions and bodies
- **Article 71** — disputes on Presidential and Vice-Presidential elections
- **329(a)** bars court challenges to delimitation; **329(b)** requires an election petition
- **Chief Electoral Officer** — State-level officer under ECI control; does NOT conduct local-body polls (State Election Commission does)
- **J&K delimitation (2022)** — Commission under Justice **Ranjana Desai**; **Assam** delimited by the ECI
### National Voters' Day
- ★ **25 January** — National Voters' Day, observed since **2011**; marks ECI's foundation day (**25 January 1950**)

## SEC 2:62:1 | Milestones
- ★ **Golden Jubilee** of the ECI — **2001**
- **Diamond Jubilee** — **2010**; **75 years** completed in **2025**
- **61st Amendment** lowered voting age from 21 to 18; then two ECs added in **1989**
- **V.S. Ramadevi** — first woman CEC, served in **1990**; **T.N. Seshan** — tenth CEC
- ECI is a **multi-member** (three-member) body continuously since **1 October 1993**; members paid like Supreme Court judges

## SEC 2:62:2 | Cases & Acts
- **Article 324 as reservoir** — used where **no law holds the field**; cannot ordinarily override an existing law
- **T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995)** — upheld multi-member EC and **majority rule**
- **Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023)** — interim selection panel: **PM, Leader of Opposition (or largest opposition party leader) in Lok Sabha, CJI** (until Parliament made a law)
- **2023 Act** — replaced the **CJI** with a **Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM**
- **Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968** — party recognition and symbol allotment; issued under the RPA 1951 and Article 324

## SEC 2:62:3 | Appointment (2023 Act)
### CEC and Other ECs Act, 2023
- **Search Committee** — headed by the **Law Minister**; two Secretary-rank members; shortlists **five** names
- **Selection Committee** — may pick names **outside the shortlist**; **recommends to the President**
- Government majority of **2:1** on the Selection Committee — challenged by **ADR** and **Jaya Thakur**; SC said CJI's place was only temporary, pending a law
### Other points
- **Tarkunde Committee** — set up by **Jayaprakash Narayan**; proposed a three-member EC
- Post-retirement bar: **CAG** (Article 148(4)) and UPSC Chairman (Article 319); **no express bar** for retiring Election Commissioners
- ECI advises the **Governor** on disqualification of State legislators under **Article 192**; the **President** under **Article 103**
  - Anti-defection cases go to the Speaker or Chairman
### Election machinery
- Each State: **Chief Electoral Officer**; removed by the **Election Commission** (not like a HC judge)
- Only the **State Election Commissioner** has High Court judge-like protection
- District level: **Collector/District Election Officer** appoints Returning and Presiding Officers
- **Gram Panchayat** (all three tiers) elections — conducted by the **State Election Commission**, not the ECI

## SEC 2:62:4 | Tenure & removal
- **CEC** — removed in the same manner and on the same grounds as a **Supreme Court judge**; safeguard in **Article 324(5)**
- Removal motion: at least **100** signatures (Lok Sabha) or **50** (Rajya Sabha)
- **Election Commissioners and Regional Commissioners** — removed only on the **CEC's recommendation**
- Constitution prescribes **no qualifications** and **no fixed term** for EC members
- ECI: one CEC + two ECs, appointed by the **President**; term **six years or age 65**, whichever earlier
- **State Election Commissioner** — removed like a **High Court judge**

## SEC 2:62:5 | Functions
- **Special Intensive Revision** of rolls — ordered under **Article 324** and **Section 21 of the RPA, 1950**
- ECI cannot review a declared result and does not try petitions; challenge only through an **election petition**

## SEC 2:62:6 | ECI vs SEC
- **Article 243ZA** — municipal elections (SEC); **243K** — Panchayat elections; **324** — ECI
- Chairman of a State Legislative Council — elected by the **Council itself** under **Article 182**, not by the ECI
  - Presiding officers are chosen by their own Houses

## SEC 2:62:7 | Exam corner
- Election petitions on Parliament / State Legislature elections — tried by the **High Court**; appeal to the **Supreme Court**
- **T.N. Seshan era** reforms did NOT include **transferring election petitions to the EC** (they stay with High Courts)
- **Gyanesh Kumar** — CEC facing the first removal motion against a CEC appointed under the **2023 Act**
- Andhra Pradesh: **Anil Chandra Punetha** — new **State Election Commissioner** for local-body polls

## SEC 2:62:8 | ECI — constitutional status and composition
- ★ ECI conducts elections to **Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President**; Constitution vests **superintendence, direction and control** of elections in it
- ★ CEC and ECs appointed by the **President** — **Article 324(2)**, under law made by Parliament
- ★ ECs removed only by the **President on the recommendation of the CEC**
- ★ CEC term — up to **6 years or 65 years** of age

## SEC 2:62:18 | ECI vs State Election Commission
### State Election Commission
- ★ Conducts elections to **local bodies** (Panchayati Raj institutions and Urban Local Bodies) — **Articles 243K and 243ZA**
- ★ SEC is a **constitutional authority** — from the **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)**; rural local body polls are NOT overseen by the ECI
- ★ Functions: **prepares electoral rolls** and **conducts elections** for Panchayats and Municipalities (Articles **243K(1)** and **243ZA(1)**)
- ★ All Panchayat elections — conducted by the **State Election Commission** (Article 243K)
- ★ State Election Commissioner — appointed by the **Governor of the State**
- ★ PYQ key (MPPSC 2019) for urban-body elections: **Article 243K**
- ★ Decision to conduct Panchayat elections — **State Government**; Panchayat is entry **5** of the **State List** (7th Schedule)
### Election Commission of India
- ★ Provisions for elections — **Part XV** (Articles **324-329**), titled "Elections"
- ★ ECI conducts elections to **Parliament, State Legislative Assemblies, President and Vice-President** (Article 324(1))
  - ★ Recommending **President's Rule** is not among its functions
- ★ **State Assembly elections** — conducted by the **ECI** (Article 324)
- ★ **Local bodies** — elections NOT conducted by the ECI
- ★ Status of CEC (PYQ key) — equal to the **Governor of a State**; CEC removable like a Supreme Court judge

## SEC 2:62:19 | State machinery — Chief Electoral Officer vs State Election Commission
- ★ **State Election Commission, Rajasthan** — constituted in **July 1994** (not December); **one-member** body
- ★ Its **secretary** is also the **Chief Electoral Officer** for the State

# ROW 2:63 | Elections - delimitation, model code of conduct, EVM-VVPAT, NOTA, election petitions
## SEC 2:63:0 | Electoral systems
### First-past-the-post
- **FPTP** (Lok Sabha) — winner needs only a **plurality** (most votes) in a single-member constituency, not a majority
- **State Assembly** — directly elected from **single-member territorial constituencies**, by FPTP
- Reasons cited against **proportional representation** for Lok Sabha: difficulty for voters, fragmentation, unstable governments, expense, no by-elections, weaker voter–MP link
  - "Guarantees direct election in every single-member constituency" is NOT such a reason
### Rolls
- **Special Intensive Revision** — rebuilds the roll comprehensively; **summary revision** is annual
### NOTA in indirect elections
- **NOTA** not applicable in **Rajya Sabha** and **Legislative Council** elections — struck down by the Supreme Court in **2018**
- NOTA applies only to direct **Lok Sabha and Assembly** elections
### Vice-President
- Elected by **all MPs, elected and nominated**, by single transferable vote — **Article 66**
- **State Legislatures have no role** in electing the VP
- **President** — elected by elected MPs and MLAs — **Article 54**

## SEC 2:63:1 | Voters' rights
### Constitutional provisions

| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **324** | Election Commission |
| **325** | **One general electoral roll**; no exclusion on religion, race, caste or sex |
| **326** | **Adult suffrage** |
| **329** | Court-related bars |

- Article 326 disqualifications: **non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, corrupt or illegal practice**
- **61st Amendment Act, 1988** — voting age lowered from 21 to **18**
- **70th Amendment (1992)** — elected MLAs of **Delhi and Puducherry** added to the Presidential electoral college
- **Article 83** (Parliament's duration), 85, 172, 174, 356 — may need amending for **simultaneous elections**
### Right to vote
- **Kuldip Nayar (2006)** — right to vote is a **statutory right**, not fundamental; right to contest also **statutory**
- Old **Rule 49-O** — voter entered the booth but **refused to vote before the Presiding Officer**
  - Struck down: recording refusal in **Form 17-A** broke secrecy; NOTA is a button on the EVM
### Rolls and enrolment
- Qualifying dates: **1 January, 1 April, 1 July, 1 October** (not 1 December)
- **Overseas elector** — enrols at the **address in the Indian passport** (**Section 20A**, RPA 1950)
- **Sahulat app** — **Jammu and Kashmir**, for **elderly voters**
- **Meghalaya** — first State with **Braille-enabled ballot papers** at all booths
### Other
- **Security deposit** forfeited on securing **one-sixth or less** of valid votes
- Delimitation principle — **"one person, one vote, one value"**; uniformity of representation under **Article 81(2)**
- **Assembly elections** — conducted by the **Election Commission of India**

## SEC 2:63:2 | Delimitation
### Delimitation Commissions

| Act | Commission | Chair |
|---|---|---|
| 1952 | 1952 | — |
| 1962 | 1963 | — |
| 1972 | **1973** | Justice **J.L. Kapur** |
| 2002 | 2002 | Justice **Kuldip Singh** |

- Commission set up under an **Act of Parliament**; headed by a **retired Supreme Court judge**; CEC and State Election Commissioners as members
- Orders **cannot be questioned in court** and **cannot be modified by legislatures** — two separate safeguards
- Delimitation redraws boundaries using **recent census** figures for **equal representation**
### Freeze and amendments
- **42nd Amendment (1976)** — froze inter-State allocation on **1971 Census** till **2000**, to encourage population control
- **84th Amendment (2001)** — extended freeze till the **first census after 2026**; rationalisation using the **1991 Census**
- **87th Amendment (2003)** — boundary redrawing switched to the **2001 Census**
- **131st Amendment Bill, 2026** — proposed raising Lok Sabha ceiling from 550 to **850**, delimitation on 2011 Census; **defeated**
- AP–Telangana seat increase under **Section 26** awaits the **first census after 2026**
- **Tamil Nadu Assembly resolution** — permanent freeze at **543** seats, retain **2.2:1** ratio, women's reservation from **2029** (not linked to delimitation)
### Seats and Articles
- Lok Sabha: **543** elected seats against a ceiling of **550** (530 States, 20 UTs)
- **Article 170** — readjustment of Assembly constituencies; **Article 82** — Lok Sabha; **Article 81(2)** — uniformity of representation
- **Article 102** — disqualifications for Parliament; **84** qualifications; **173 and 191** for State Legislatures
- **Panchayat and municipal polls** — State Election Commissions under **243K** and **243ZA**
### RPA links
- **RPA 1950** — Articles **81, 82, 170, 171, 325, 326**; seats and rolls
- **RPA 1951** — Articles **84, 102, 173, 191, 324, 329**; nomination, scrutiny, counting
- **Election Symbols Order, 1968** — recognises parties, reserves symbols
### Lok Sabha years
- **1st** Lok Sabha **1952**; 9th 1989; 11th 1996; 16th 2014

## SEC 2:63:3 | Reserved seats
### Reservation
- **Article 334A** — women's one-third reservation (**106th Amendment**); operates only **after the next census and delimitation**
- **Article 330** — SC/ST seats in Lok Sabha; **331** — former Anglo-Indian nomination
- **104th Amendment** (2019, in force Jan 2020) — SC/ST reservation under Article 334 extended to **2030**; ended Anglo-Indian nomination
  - Article 334 originally 10 years (till 1960); **95th Amendment** gave the previous extension
- Reserved constituencies have a **common electorate** — **all voters** may vote
- AP Lok Sabha: **Araku** — ST-reserved; **Amalapuram, Tirupati** — SC-reserved
- AP Assembly: **29 SC**, 7 ST seats; Telangana 19 SC, 12 ST
### RPA 1951 and machinery
- **Sections 77–78** — election expenses; **Section 78**: account to the **DEO** (usually Collector) within **30 days**
- **Section 8** — disqualifications; conviction disqualification continues **six years after release**
- **Section 100** — grounds for declaring an election **void** (disqualification, corrupt practice, improper rejection of nomination)
- **Section 14** — Lok Sabha poll notified by the **President** on ECI recommendation; ECI fixes schedule
- **Article 324** — superintendence, direction and control of elections; also backs the **Model Code** (ECI guidelines, not a statute)
- ECI **controls officers on poll duty** — may transfer them or act against partisan conduct
- **Delimitation Commission** — constituted under an **Act of Parliament**; orders cannot be modified by Lok Sabha or Assemblies
### Party recognition (Election Symbols Order, 1968)
- **State Party** — 3% of Assembly seats or **three** seats, whichever more
- **National Party** — recognised as State party in **four** or more States
### Voting facilities and initiatives
- **Postal ballot** — persons in **preventive detention**, service voters, special voters, electors on election duty
- Absentee senior citizens — **85 years or above** (raised from 80)
- **cVIGIL** — reporting Model Code violations (geo-tagged photo/video); **Saksham** — voters with disabilities
- **IIIDEM** — trains election managers; institute of the **ECI**
### Other
- **Mid-term election** — after premature dissolution; **by-election** fills a single vacancy, within six months
- **17th Lok Sabha** — **2019**; 16th 2014; 18th 2024
- Population-based delimitation would shift seats to **Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan**

## SEC 2:63:4 | Model Code of Conduct

## SEC 2:63:5 | NOTA, EVM & ink
### Indelible ink
- Made by **Mysore Paints and Varnish Ltd**; based on **silver nitrate**
- Formula from the **National Physical Laboratory**; applied on the **left forefinger**
- First used in the **1962** general election
### NOTA
- **PUCL v Union of India** (**27 September 2013**) — NOTA button on all EVMs
- Secret negative vote linked to **Article 19(1)(a)**
- Symbol (ballot paper with black cross) designed by **NID Ahmedabad**; first Lok Sabha use **2014**
- NOTA is **symbolic** — even if it tops the count, the candidate with most votes wins; no fresh poll
- Using NOTA is **active participation** (voting), **not abstention**
### EVM / VVPAT
- **EVM** — electronic vote recording and counting apparatus; not an electoral system
- **EVMs** first used at **Parur, 1982**; **VVPAT** first at **Noksen, 2013**
- Assembly candidate can win without 50% — **FPTP**
### Offences
- From **1 July 2024**: bribery, undue influence, personation in **BNS Chapter IX (Sections 169–177)**; earlier IPC Chapter IXA

## SEC 2:63:6 | Election petitions (RPA 1951)
### Petitions
- **Article 329** — election challenged only by an **election petition**; delimitation laws cannot be questioned in court
- **Section 81** — petition presented within **45 days**; **Section 80A** — trial by the **High Court**
- High Court should decide within **six months**; appeal to the **Supreme Court**
- Election disputes — only **courts**; President/VP disputes — **Supreme Court** under **Article 71**, not the ECI
### Parties and polls
- **Section 29A** — **registration** of parties by the ECI (distinct from **recognition** under the Symbols Order, 1968)
- **Section 20B** observers — **general, police, expenditure** (not judicial)
- **Section 126** — no opinion polls in the **48 hours** before close of poll; **126A** — exit polls
- **Exit poll** — of voters immediately after leaving the polling station; differs from an opinion poll
- By-election six-month rule does NOT apply if remaining term **less than one year**, or ECI certifies difficulty after consulting the Centre
- **ECINET** — unified platform merging **40+** ECI apps and websites (not only for petitions)
- **First general election — 1951–52**; it and 1957, 1962, 1967 held simultaneously with Assembly polls

## SEC 2:63:7 | Exam corner
- **Floating constituency** — no fixed geographical boundary; e.g. **Sangha** (Sikkim): only registered **Buddhist monks** vote and contest
- **NETA app** (rate representatives) — built by a **private start-up**, not the ECI
- **Article 171** — composition of State Legislative Councils; superintendence of elections is **Article 324**
- **Corrupt practices** and disqualifications — **RPA, 1951**; voter registration — RPA 1950

## SEC 2:63:8 | Electoral systems — President, Vice-President, Parliament and State Legislatures

# ROW 2:64 | Electoral reforms - state funding, electoral bonds, criminalisation of politics, RPA 1951 provisions
## SEC 2:64:0 | Committees → subject
### Electoral-reform committees

| Committee | Year | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| **Tarkunde** | — | proposed selection panel: **PM, Leader of Opposition, CJI** (not the Speaker) |
| **Dinesh Goswami** | **1990** | broad electoral reform: flagged **musclemen**, urged **rotation of SC/ST reserved seats**, recommended **State funding** and **EVMs** (led to 1996 RPA amendment) |
| **Vohra** | **1993** | **crime-politics nexus** |
| **Indrajit Gupta** | **1998** | **State funding** — **partial funding in kind** to recognised parties and candidates (not adopted) |
| **Ram Nath Kovind HLC** | 2023-24 | simultaneous elections; members incl. **Amit Shah, N.K. Singh** |

- Goswami Committee did NOT deal with Legislative Council seat allocation
- **Indrajit Gupta** — NOT a Centre-State relations body (APPSC-G2 2025, UPPSC 2015)
  - Centre-State bodies: **Rajamannar** (Tamil Nadu, 1969-71; State-level autonomy initiative), **Sarkaria** (1983-88), **Punchhi** (2007-10; federal dialogue and coordination)
### Law Commission
- **255th Report (2015)** — limited power for ECI to **deregister** non-compliant parties; recommended **totalisers**
- **170th Report (1999)** — internal party democracy, partial list seats; full State funding desirable only if parties **barred from other funding sources**
- Simultaneous polls held **1951-52 to 1967**; backed by the 170th Report and the 2018 draft report
### State funding and money
- Level-field argument for State funding — equality under **Article 14**; curbing black money also for; fiscal burden against
- **State of Karnataka v. Prathik Parasrampuria (2026)** — removing black money from elections is a core responsibility of the **ECI**
- **Totaliser** — combines several EVM counts to **conceal booth-wise trends**; ECI: no legal framework exists
### Other
- **Section 126A**, RPA 1951 — ban on **exit polls**
- **Tenth Schedule** disqualification — decided by Speaker/Chairman, subject to **judicial review** (SC, 1992)
- **Security deposit** lost on securing no more than **one-sixth** of valid votes
- Death of a candidate — since the **1996 amendment**, does NOT automatically cancel the election; recognised party may name a replacement

## SEC 2:64:2 | Landmark cases
### Criminalisation and disclosure
- **Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)** — convicted legislator disqualified **immediately**; no protection during appeal
- **ADR v. Union of India (2002)** — mandatory disclosure of **criminal cases, assets, liabilities, education**
- **PUCL** — voter's right to know under **Article 19(1)(a)**
- **ADR v Union of India (2024)** — **Electoral Bond Scheme** struck down: violated voters' **right to information** under **Article 19(1)(a)** (five-judge bench, unanimous)
### EVM, VVPAT, NOTA
- **Subramanian Swamy v ECI (2013)** — **VVPAT** necessary for free and fair polls; phased introduction
- **ADR v ECI (2024)** — refused return to **paper ballots** and 100% VVPAT counting

## SEC 2:64:4 | Electoral bonds

### Funding and other reforms

# ROW 2:66 | Party system in India - one-party dominance, multi-party and coalition eras
## SEC 2:66:0 | Nature
- Original Constitution had **no specific provision** on political parties
- ECI classification: **national**, **State**, **registered-unrecognised** parties (cadre/mass is an organisational classification)
