# ROW 3:100 | Sharing of resources and devolution - horizontal and vertical devolution criteria, grants
## SEC 3:100:0 | Finance Commission (Art. 280)
### Constitution and report
- FC constituted by the President every **fifth year or earlier**; quasi-judicial; recommendations **advisory**
- **Article 281** — President lays the FC report before each House of Parliament with an **explanatory memorandum** on action taken
### Duties under Article 280(3)
- **Clause (b)** — principles governing **grants-in-aid under Article 275**
- **Clause (d)** — any other matter **referred by the President**
- Augmenting State Consolidated Fund for **Panchayats and Municipalities** — added by **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)**
- NOT an FC duty: **maintaining income-expenditure accounts** of Union and States
- **GST revenue sharing** — dealt with by the **GST Council**, not the FC
### Concepts
- **Vertical devolution** — sharing net tax proceeds between **Union and States**
- **Horizontal devolution** — distribution of the States' share **among individual States**
- **Article 275 grants** — for revenue deficits, local bodies, disasters, tribal welfare; NOT for financing **every CSS**
### Sixteenth FC and recent
- 16th FC — linked CSS to **measurable outcomes**; proposed a high-powered committee to close **inefficient schemes**
- 16th FC — State fiscal deficit capped at **3% of GSDP**
- **SASCI** — **50-year interest-free capex loans**; prevented **pro-cyclical compression** of State capital expenditure (Economic Survey)

## SEC 3:100:4 | Divisible pool & grants data
### Divisible pool
- FC recommendations — **advisory, not binding** on the Government
- **80th Amendment (2000)** — gave effect to 10th FC's **Alternative Scheme of Devolution** (29%); made **all central taxes** shareable, effective **1 April 1996**
- Excluded from divisible pool — **cesses and surcharges**, and **cost of collection**; pool taxes shared under **Article 270**
- Divisible pool — about **81% of gross tax revenue** in 2025–26
- Largest-ever rise in States' share — **14th FC** (32% to **42%**, +10 points)
- 16th FC **retained 41%**; demand of **18 of 28 States** for **50%** not accepted
- 16th FC — shift from **entitlement-based** to **performance-oriented** transfers
### Transfer channels and grants
- Channels (Economic Survey): tax devolution, FC grants, CSS and other transfers; **Article 123** (ordinance power) is NOT a channel
- **Article 282 grants** — **discretionary**, mostly **tied**, routed through **Union ministries**
- **Article 275 grants** — statutory, **charged on the CFI**
- 15th FC revenue-deficit, sector-specific and State-specific grants — about **₹10.1 lakh crore** (vs ₹9.47 lakh crore under 16th FC)
- Recommended to be notified as disasters — **heatwave and lightning**
- 15th FC — **₹4,800 crore** (2022-23 to 2025-26) to reward **educational outcomes**
### Gadgil formula
- Introduced in **1969**; formula-based plan transfers lapsed when **NITI Aayog** replaced Planning Commission (**1 January 2015**)
- Initial Special Category States — **Assam, Jammu and Kashmir, Nagaland**
- Special Category States — **90% grant, 10% loan**; general-category States — **30% grant, 70% loan**

# ROW 3:102 | Public expenditure - revenue and capital, developmental and non-developmental, trends; subsidies
## SEC 3:102:0 | Revenue vs capital expenditure
### Revenue expenditure
- Does not create assets — salaries, pensions, **subsidies**, interest, administrative expenses
- ★ Most important item of Union revenue expenditure — **interest payment**
- **Maintenance** of existing assets and **interest payments** — revenue expenditure
- Grants to States — revenue expenditure
- **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure **minus** revenue receipts
### Capital expenditure
- **Creates assets or reduces liabilities**
- **Loans and advances to States** — capital (affects financial assets); **repayment of loans** — capital (reduces liabilities)
- Defence equipment purchase — can be capital outlay
- Builds long-term capacity — acquires, creates, upgrades or extends life of **assets**
- **Effective capital expenditure** = capex + **grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets**
- Union capex 2026-27 — **₹12,21,821 crore** (+11.5%) = **capital outlay** ₹9,43,042 crore + **loans and advances** ₹2,78,780 crore
- **SASCI** — **50-year interest-free loans** to States for capex
- **Golden Rule** — borrow only for **asset creation** (capital expenditure)
## SEC 3:102:1 | Laws of expenditure growth
- **Wagner's law** (law of increasing state activity) — public expenditure rises **faster than national income** as economy industrialises
- **Peacock-Wiseman hypothesis** — **displacement effect**: wars and crises raise tolerable tax burden, stepwise rise in spending

# ROW 3:103 | Public debt - internal and external, debt-GDP ratio, sustainability, state debt and off-budget borrowing
## SEC 3:103:0 | Internal vs external debt
- Classified by **source of borrowing**
- **Internal debt** — borrowed within the country (domestic); **96.7%** of public debt; G-Secs over **90%** of Central debt
- **External debt** — from foreign sources; may involve **foreign-currency** obligations
- Market loans — internal; loans from foreign governments — external
- **Small savings, post-office deposits** — **other liabilities** in the Public Account
- **Treasury Bills** — short-term; G-Secs — long-term; SDLs — issued by States
- **G-Secs and T-Bills** — internal-debt instruments
## SEC 3:103:2 | Debt-GDP targets
### Debt composition
- **NSSF special securities** — **non-marketable internal debt** (like saving/compensation bonds, 14-day intermediate T-Bills)
- Bank deposits reach government borrowing via **SLR holdings**
- External debt end-Dec 2024 — **USD 718.2 billion**, **18.4% of GDP**
- Most external debt — **non-sovereign** (companies, banks); Government's external debt about **2.6% of GDP**
### Targets and institutions
- Centre's new debt target — **50 ± 1%** of GDP by **31 March 2031**
- FRBM (amended **2018**) — Centre **40%**, general government **60%**, States 20% (**N.K. Singh Committee**)
- **PDMC** — set up **2016** in **Department of Economic Affairs** (headed by Joint Secretary (Budget)), not RBI
## SEC 3:103:3 | Sustainability & borrowing
- **Market borrowings** — largest source of **capital receipts**
- **Zero primary deficit** — borrowing needed only for **interest payments**
- If **r > g** — a **primary surplus** is needed to stabilise debt
- Interest payments — about **40% of revenue receipts**; squeeze other spending (debt as fiscal anchor)
- State debt sustainability — **debt-GSDP** and **interest payments-to-revenue receipts**
- Off-budget borrowings and guarantees — make consolidated debt **appear lower**; may weigh on sovereign ratings
## SEC 3:103:4 | State debt (Art. 293)
- **Article 293** — States borrow only **within India**, on security of the **State's Consolidated Fund**, via **SDLs**
- External loans to States — **routed through the Centre** since **2005-06** (back-to-back for general category States)
- 16th FC — State fiscal deficit capped at **3% of GSDP** for 2026-31

|---|---|
| **Punjab** | **42.9%** |
| West Bengal | 38.3% |
| Rajasthan | 37.9% |
| Andhra Pradesh | 34.6% |
- AP total debt 2025-26 RE — **₹6,35,177 crore**, **36.04% of GSDP**; market borrowing (₹4,83,093 crore) largest component
- **"India's External Debt: A Status Report"** — **Department of Economic Affairs**, Ministry of Finance; RBI compiles quarterly data
## SEC 3:103:5 | Off-budget borrowing
- Budget 2026-27 — **debt-GDP ratio** replaced fiscal deficit as principal fiscal anchor
- Classic case — **FCI borrowing from NSSF** for unpaid food subsidy; **Budget 2021-22** ended it, food subsidy fully on-budget
- FRBM — new Central guarantees capped at **0.5% of GDP** a year (contingent liabilities)
- 16th FC — **stop** off-budget borrowing, bring existing debt **on-budget**, widen definitions of deficit and debt
## SEC 3:103:6 | Debt institutions
- Statutory **PDMA** — **not yet created**; **RBI** still manages public debt
## SEC 3:103:7 | Exam corner
- **Article 292** — Union borrowing against the security of the **Consolidated Fund of India**
- **Consolidated Fund** — main account; receives loan recoveries
- **Public Account** — money held **in trust**; repaid or used by rule; records other liabilities
- **Contingency Fund** — urgent needs; **NIF** — permanent corpus
- Largest component of external debt historically — **commercial borrowings**
## SEC 3:103:8 | Public debt — meaning, internal vs external, Centre's liabilities
- ★ Internal debt — **market borrowings**, **Treasury Bills**, **special securities issued to RBI**
- ★ World Bank classified India — **less indebted country**
- ★ **Provident Fund** — **contractual savings**
## SEC 3:103:9 | Government funds — Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, Public Account
- ★ Maximum resources for plan expenditure — from **borrowings**
- ★ **State Provident Funds** money — credited to the **Public Account**
- Three funds — Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, Public Account of India

# ROW 3:104 | Deficit financing - meaning, methods and consequences
## SEC 3:104:0 | Meaning: Indian vs Western usage
- ★ Deficit financing — **inflationary** in character (raises money supply)
- ★ Most inflationary — **creation of new money** to finance a budget deficit (monetising the deficit)
- **Indian usage** — borrowing from the **RBI**, which **creates new money**
- **Western usage** — any financing of a deficit
## SEC 3:104:1 | Purpose & phases
- Used mainly to finance the **Five Year Plans** (resources for development)
- Limited use until the late 1960s; heavier from the **1970s**
## SEC 3:104:2 | Curbs on deficit financing
- FRBM **escape clause** — deviation capped at **0.5% of GDP** a year
- Since **2018** — monetised borrowing allowed only on escape-clause grounds
- Ad hoc T-bills phase-out began **September 1994**; **March 1997** agreement ended them
- **Ways and Means Advances** — from **1 April 1997**
## SEC 3:104:3 | Mode → inflationary effect
- Post-Independence need — **low tax base**, poor compliance, weak privately owned banking, **low domestic savings** (not a high savings rate)
- Inflationary order — **creation of new money** (most) > bank borrowing > public borrowing
- **Debt repayment** — withdraws money
## SEC 3:104:4 | Consequences
- **Crowding out** — heavy market borrowing raises interest rates, squeezes **private investment**
- **Debt trap** — borrowing to pay interest
- **Demand-pull inflation** — money supply and demand rise without matching rise in goods and services; burden falls most on the **poor**
## SEC 3:104:5 | Current data
- AP fiscal deficit 2024–25 — **5.05%** (above the **4%** ceiling)
- **Market loans** — **75%** of AP's debt
## SEC 3:104:6 | Exam corner
- **OMOs** — **sale** of G-Secs absorbs liquidity; **purchase** injects liquidity
- RBI urges shift from **subsidies** to **capital expenditure**
## SEC 3:104:7 | Deficit financing — meaning, purpose and plan phases
- ★ In India, deficit financing raises resources for **economic development**
- ★ Most inflationary — **creating new money** to finance a budget deficit (monetisation)
- ★ Creates additional paper currency; if it fails, generates **inflation**
- ★ **Fiscal deficit** (Union budget) — **budgetary deficit + net increase in internal borrowings** and other liabilities

# ROW 3:105 | Fiscal policy - objectives, instruments; fiscal, revenue, primary and effective revenue deficits
## SEC 3:105:0 | Deficits: formula
### Formulas
| Deficit | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Primary deficit** | fiscal deficit **minus interest payments** |
| **Fiscal deficit** | primary deficit **plus interest payments** |
| **Revenue deficit** | revenue expenditure **minus** revenue receipts |
| **Effective revenue deficit** | revenue deficit **minus grants for creation of capital assets** |
| Old **budget deficit** | total expenditure minus total receipts **including borrowings** |
- ★ Fiscal deficit minus interest payments — **primary deficit**
- ★ Primary deficit + interest payment — **fiscal deficit**
- Worked: FD ₹1,50,000 crore − interest ₹1,20,000 crore = PD **₹30,000 crore**
- ERD is **less than or equal to** RD (never always greater)
- Budget deficit is NOT FD minus interest (that is primary deficit)
### Meaning of each
- **Revenue deficit** — borrowing for **day-to-day consumption**
- **Fiscal deficit** — total borrowing requirement; **headline marker** of fiscal health
- **Monetised deficit** — part financed by the **RBI** through **high-powered money**
- High **RD/FD ratio** — borrowing finances **consumption/revenue expenditure**
- **AP** — still runs a **primary deficit**
### Chronology
- **1994** RBI-Government agreement on ad hoc T-bills; abolished from **1 April 1997**; ERD introduced **2011-12**; added to FRBM by **2012** amendment
### Effects
- Deficit financing (India) — drawing down **cash balances**, borrowing from **RBI**, issuing **new currency**
- Higher fiscal deficit widens **CAD** — dissaving reduces **national savings**, widens savings-investment gap
- **Fiscal multiplier** largest for **capital expenditure**
## SEC 3:105:1 | Musgrave's three functions
- Fiscal policy — revenue and expenditure to influence **aggregate demand, allocation and distribution**
- **Richard Musgrave** — popularised **fiscal federalism** (**1959**)
| Function | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Allocation** | providing **public goods** |
| **Distribution** | reducing inequality |
| **Stabilisation** | controlling inflation and unemployment |
## SEC 3:105:2 | Fiscal vs monetary policy
- ★ Monetary policy — **complementary** to fiscal policy
- ★ Fiscal policy — policy on **taxation and government expenditure**
- Fiscal policy promotes **full employment and growth** — via expenditure and taxation affecting **aggregate demand**
- Fiscal policy components — **taxation, expenditure, public debt**; objectives — growth, employment, price stability, equity (objectives do not explain components)
- Fiscal deficit financed by market borrowings, small savings/NSSF, provident funds, external loans; **PSU dividends** are non-tax revenue, NOT a financing source
- **Pump priming** — public expenditure to stimulate activity; **crowding out** — displaces private investment; **crowding in** — public capex attracts private investment
- Expenditure-side deficit cut — **rationalising subsidies through DBT**; tax-base expansion and disinvestment are receipts-side
## SEC 3:105:3 | Fiscal rules
### Origins
- ★ **J.M. Keynes** — fiscal policy as tool against the **Great Depression**; **General Theory (1936)**
- **Long-Term Fiscal Policy** — **1985**, FM **V.P. Singh**
### Targets
- **FRBM Act 2003** — fiscal deficit **3% of GDP**, revenue deficit **eliminated**; amended **2018**
- **N.K. Singh Committee** — general government debt **60%** (Centre **40%**, States **20%**)
- Central debt target — **50 ± 1%** by **March 2031**; projected **55.6%** in BE 2026-27
- From **2006-07** — RBI barred from **subscribing to primary issues** of G-Secs (except escape clause)
### Other
- **Money supply and liquidity** — monetary policy objective, NOT fiscal
- Fiscal objectives — employment, price stability, growth, redistribution, mobilisation, regional balance
- Centre + States public expenditure — about **28% of GDP** (ES 2016-17); Union alone 13.6% (BE 2026-27)
## SEC 3:105:4 | Deficit glide path (% GDP)
### Glide path and data
- FY 2021-22 glide path — FD below **4.5%** by 2025-26 (met at **4.4%**); from 2026-27 FD set to keep **debt-GDP falling**
- FD **9.2%** (2020-21 peak) to **4.3%** (2026-27 BE)
- Earlier peak — **8.4%** in **1990-91**
- 9.2% to **4.4%** — linked to three rating upgrades in **2025** (**Morningstar DBRS, S&P, R&I**)
- Worked: FD ₹4 crore / GDP ₹400 crore = **1%**
### Instruments
- Fiscal policy objectives — **resource mobilisation**, **equitable distribution**, **balanced regional development**, growth
- **Repo rate** — monetary tool of RBI/MPC, NOT fiscal
- **MPC** meets **bi-monthly** — faster than the budget cycle
- **Ad hoc T-bills** (automatic monetisation) replaced by **WMA** from **1 April 1997**
- WMA beyond the limit — **overdraft**
### Stance
- **Counter-cyclical** — deficits in slumps (e.g. **Atma Nirbhar Bharat, 2020**), consolidation in booms
- **Expansionary** stance — used in **recession**
- **Tax concessions, cheap credit** — **hidden subsidies**
## SEC 3:105:5 | Key relations & terms
- ★ **Zero primary deficit** — borrowing **just equals interest payments** (debt-trap signal)
- Public borrowing — **internal** (bonds, T-Bills, small savings) and **external** (multilateral, sovereign bonds)
- **External borrowing** — exposes to **currency risk**
- Most desirable CAD financing — **FDI**; FPI volatile
- **Fiscal multiplier** — change in GDP ÷ change in government spending or taxes
- **Crowding out** — heavy borrowing raises interest rates, **private investment falls**
- Fertiliser **DBT (2018)** — subsidy released after retail sale on **PoS machines** with **Aadhaar** authentication

# ROW 3:106 | FRBM Act - targets, escape clause, N.K. Singh Committee; fiscal consolidation path
## SEC 3:106:0 | FRBM Act, 2003
- ★ FRBM Act aims to augment **fiscal discipline** (cutting fiscal and revenue deficits)
- Introduced by FM **Yashwant Sinha**; Rules in force **5 July 2004**
- Original targets — FD **3%** by **31 March 2008**; revenue deficit eliminated by **31 March 2009**
- Objectives — inter-generational equity, macro stability, eliminate RD, cut FD, transparency, balanced budget over time; **fixing repo rate** is NOT one
- **No penal provisions** for missing targets; does provide deficit limits, disclosures, fiscal statements
- Additional guarantees against Consolidated Fund — capped at **0.50% of GDP** a year
- Aim **fiscal discipline and prudence** — by cutting FD, RD and public debt
## SEC 3:106:1 | Statements laid in Parliament
| FRBM statement | Content |
|---|---|
| **Macro-Economic Framework Statement** | growth, inflation, external balance, assumptions, risks |
| **Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement** | rolling targets |
| **Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement** | fiscal strategy |
| **Medium-Term Expenditure Framework Statement** | expenditure projections |
- Three-year rolling targets **suspended from 2021-22** (post-pandemic flexibility)
- **AP FRBM** limits — restrain **open-market borrowing**
## SEC 3:106:2 | Committees
- **2004 Task Force** on FRBM implementation — **Vijay Kelkar**; recommended tax reform and **comprehensive GST**; later headed **13th FC**
- **FRBM Review Committee** — **N.K. Singh**; formed **May 2016** (announced Budget 2016-17); report **January 2017**; reflected in **Budget 2017-18**
- Proposed **Fiscal Council** — **independent, advisory** body (no binding powers)
- Deviation statement — Government acknowledged missing **3% fiscal deficit** and **40% Central debt** targets
## SEC 3:106:3 | Amendments & episodes
### N.K. Singh Committee and 2018 amendment
- Principal anchor — **debt-to-GDP ratio**; fiscal deficit the operational target; general government debt **60% by 2023**
- Debt anchor and escape clause added by the **2018 amendment**
- 2018 targets — FD **3% by 31 March 2021**; general government debt **60%**, Central **40%** by **31 March 2025**
- **Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement** — **three-year** rolling targets (RD, FD, tax-GDP, total outstanding liabilities)
### Chronology
- **2004** Rules notified; **2008-09** suspension (global financial crisis); **2012** ERD target and MTEF Statement added; **2018** debt anchor and clauses
### Escape clause
- Grounds — national security, war, calamity, **agricultural collapse**, structural reforms, sharp output fall; NOT routine tax shortfall
- Government must place a **justification** and a **path back** to target (deviation capped at 0.5% of GDP)
### Recent
- COVID 2020–21 FD — **9.2% of GDP**
- Halving commitment met at **4.4% of GDP in 2025-26** (a commitment, not a legislative target)
- New debt anchor — **50 ± 1%** of GDP by **2031** (criticised vs FRBM's 40%)
- **16th FC** — State fiscal rules "**effectively unenforced**"; sought wider deficit definition incl. off-budget borrowing
- States — own **fiscal responsibility legislation**; Union FRBM does not automatically apply
### First Budget
- **James Wilson** — India's first Budget, **1860**
- **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty** — free India's first Budget, **1947**
## SEC 3:106:4 | Escape vs buoyancy clause
- **Escape clause** trigger — real output growth falls **3 percentage points** below average of previous four quarters
- **Buoyancy clause** (2018) — sharp rise in growth requires FD cut of at least **0.25% of GDP**; never an automatic increase
## SEC 3:106:5 | States & AP
- **16th FC** — FD of Centre **3.5% of GDP**, States **3% of GSDP** by **2030-31**
- **AP FRBM Act** — **2005**; eliminate RD, FD limited to **3% of GSDP**
- **12th FC** — debt relief linked to State FRLs via **Debt Consolidation and Relief Facility**

|---|---|
| 2021-22 | 4% |
| 2022-23 | **3.5%** |
| 2023-24 to 2025-26 | 3% + 0.5% for power-sector reforms |
## SEC 3:106:6 | Exam corner
- FRBM significance — made fiscal discipline a **legal duty**; added **disclosure statements** to the Budget
## SEC 3:106:7 | FRBM Act, 2003 — origin, FIRSTs, objectives and original targets
- ★ FRBM Act concerns **both fiscal and revenue deficit** (reduce FD, eliminate RD, manage debt)

## SEC 3:107:5 | Receipts classified
### Receipts
- Revenue estimates — **CBDT and CBIC** under **Department of Revenue**

- **Capital receipts** — create liability or reduce assets
- **Recovery of loans** — **non-debt capital receipt**; market borrowings — debt capital receipt
- **Disinvestment** — capital receipt that **reduces assets** (not a liability); **borrowings** create a liability
- **Treasury Bills** — **short-term** domestic borrowing
- **Paper taxes** — **wealth tax, gift tax, estate duty** (abolished); GST is not
### Expenditure and deficit
- Capital expenditure — creates assets or reduces liabilities: capital works, loans given, **repayment of debt**
- Salaries, allowances — revenue expenditure
- **Fiscal deficit** — total expenditure minus total receipts **excluding borrowings**
