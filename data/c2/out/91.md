## SEC 4:52:1 | Formation & properties
### Red soil
- ★ Red colour — **iron oxides**, particularly **ferric oxide** (oxidised under well-drained conditions)
- ★ Formed by weathering of ancient **crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks**
- ★ Short of lime, phosphates, nitrogen and humus; fairly **rich in potash**; mainly siliceous
- AP red soils — **low moisture-retention** capacity
- Rayalaseema red soils — **shallow**, low moisture retention, low organic matter
### Black soil
- AP black soils — from **Deccan Trap basalt**, crystalline schists and **basic gneiss**
- Rich in calcium, potash, magnesium; poor in **nitrogen, phosphorus** and humus
- Colour from **iron and titanium** compounds; clayey
- Neutral to strongly alkaline
### Alluvial and laterite
- AP deltaic alluvium — laid by **Godavari and Krishna**, renewed with silt each flood; rich in potash and lime
- Alluvium — rich in potash, phosphoric acid, lime; poor in **nitrogen and humus**; ideal for rice and wheat
- Laterite — rich in **hydrated iron and aluminium oxides**; poor in organic matter, low fertility
- Laterite — **acidic** conditions
- Laterite **less fertile**; **poor in nitrogen** (heavy leaching washes out nutrients)
### Other
- Much of AP underlain by **granitoid-gneiss** bedrock
- Desert/arid soil — **little moisture**; sandy, low humus, often saline; Thar and Gujarat

## SEC 4:52:5 | Problem soils & schemes
- ★ **Halophytes** — saline soils
- ★ Cryophytes — cold soil; lithophytes — rocks; xerophytes — tropical deserts; hygrophytes — waterlogged; hydrophytes — aquatic
- **Salinity** — lowers yields, may break down soil structure
- Alkalinity — reduces nutrient availability; linked to limestone, basalt or alkaline irrigation water
- Alkaline soils reclaimed by **gypsum**; saline soils need drainage and leaching
- **Gujarat** — largest saline area; **Uttar Pradesh** — most alkali land
- **Uppada** — coastal erosion (sea-level rise, waves); Podu topsoil loss — ASR and Parvathipuram Manyam
- Soil Health and Fertility — under **RKVY** since **2022-23**
- Soil Health Card Scheme — launched **19 February 2015** at **Suratgarh**

- ★ Laterite develops under high temperature and heavy rainfall; **low humus**, nitrogen, phosphorus

# ROW 4:59 | Demographic dividend, ageing and population policy
## SEC 4:59:0 | Demographic transition stages
### Stages
- ★ Sequence with development: **high birth–high death → high birth–low death → low birth–low death**
- Five stages: **high stationary, early expanding, late expanding, low stationary, declining**
- **Declining** stage — birth rate **below** death rate
- ★ India's **population explosion** — **1951–81** (death rates fell, birth rates high) — **Stage 2**
- India overall now — **Stage 3**; AP — Stage 4; Japan, Germany — Stage 5
### History
- British period — high birth and death rates, **famines and epidemics**
- Life expectancy around 1951 — about **32 years**
- **1921** — "great divide"; decline in 1911–21, continuous growth after; about 0.19% a year in 1891–1921
### Dividend and elderly
- Dividend channels — labour supply, savings and investment, lower dependency ratio, human capital; **rising dependants** works against it
- RBI state-finances study — **Kerala and Tamil Nadu** crossed the demographic turning point; Bihar, UP have a wider window
- About **78%** of elderly have **no** pension cover

## SEC 4:59:1 | Thinkers
- Malthus — **Essay on the Principle of Population (1798)**; population **geometric** (1, 2, 4, 8), food **arithmetic** (1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Positive checks** — **famine**, disease, war; preventive — moral restraint, late marriage
- **Optimum population** — size at which **per-capita income is maximum**; **Edwin Cannan**
- **Thompson** — demographic transition theory; **Notestein** — formal three-stage version; **Ehrlich** — The Population Bomb
- Marxian view — overpopulation a product of **capitalism** (relative surplus population, unemployment)
- UP draft Population (Control, Stabilization and Welfare) Bill — **2021**
- Mission Parivar Vikas — **2016**; national family planning programme — **1952**

## SEC 4:59:2 | Dividend: key terms
- **Demographic winter** — persistent decline, fertility below **2.1**, inverted age pyramid
- Working-age (UNFPA) — **15–64** years; Indian projections often 15–59
- Economic Survey **2018-19** "demographic nightmare" — without **health, education, skills and jobs**
- **Replacement TFR** — **2.1**; births per 1,000 a year is the crude birth rate
- NFHS-5 India TFR — **2.0** (NFHS-4: 2.2)
- Southern States fear **lower tax devolution** and **fewer seats** after delimitation

## SEC 4:59:3 | Dividend window estimates
- Window opens at **different times in different States**
- Peak — ES 2018-19: about **2041**; ES 2025-26: about **2030**
- UNFPA window — **2005–06 to 2055–56** (five decades)
- 15–59 workforce peaks **2041**; window closes after that
