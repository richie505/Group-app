# ROW 2:31 | Presiding officers - Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Chairman; Secretariat; sessions, adjournment, prorogation, dissolution
## SEC 2:31:0 | Presiding officers
### Speaker and Deputy Speaker
- Speaker must already be an **elected Lok Sabha member**; the six-month relaxation applies only to **Ministers**
- Speaker and Deputy Speaker take **only the oath as MPs**; no separate oath as presiding officer
- Removal of Speaker — **Article 94(c)**: **effective majority** (majority of all the then members), not simple majority
- Removal notice — at least **14 days**
- Speaker resigns to the **Deputy Speaker**, not to the President
- Deputy Speaker's removal also needs an **effective majority** (not two-thirds) after **14 days'** notice
- Deputy Speaker post vacant throughout the **17th Lok Sabha** (and the 18th)
### Panel of Chairpersons
- Nominated by the **Speaker**; up to **ten** members
- A panel member presides when **both Speaker and Deputy Speaker are absent**, not when the Speaker's office is **vacant**
### Rajya Sabha and others
- Deputy Chairman resigns to the **Chairman**; removed by an **effective majority** after 14 days' notice
- When the Vice-President acts as President, the **Deputy Chairman** performs the Chairman's duties
- **Governor** — removed by the President at pleasure, **no notice**; the other removals need 14 days' notice

## SEC 2:31:1 | Speaker: status
- Salaries of Speaker and Deputy Speaker — charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** (**Article 97**)
- Speaker's election date fixed by the **President**; Deputy Speaker's by the **Speaker**
- Deputy Speaker — elected by the House from among its members; Opposition member is only a **convention**, not a constitutional requirement
- Only the **Speaker** stays in office after dissolution; election by the House is common to both, so it does not explain the difference
### Pro-Tem Speaker
- Usually the **senior-most member** of the House
- **Appointed by the President** (**Article 95(1)**), not elected by the House; sworn in by the President
- Swears in the new MPs (**administers oath**) and conducts the election of the regular Speaker
- Serves until the regular Speaker is elected

## SEC 2:31:2 | Normal sitting vs own removal (Art. 96)
| Situation | Speaker | Deputy Speaker |
|---|---|---|
| Normal sitting (**Art. 100**) | No vote in first instance; **casting vote** in a tie | Out of the Chair: ordinary member — can debate, move motions, vote in first instance |
| Own removal resolution (**Art. 96**) | **Cannot preside**; may speak, take part and **vote in first instance**; no casting vote | Cannot preside; may speak and **vote in first instance**; no casting vote |

## SEC 2:31:3 | Speaker's powers
### Articles
- **Article 93** — House chooses two members as Speaker and Deputy Speaker
- Speaker's provisions — **Articles 93–96** (election, vacation, removal), **100** (casting vote), **110** (Money Bill certificate), **118** (joint sitting rules)
- **Article 86** — President may address either/both Houses, require attendance and **send messages on pending Bills**
### Powers
- ★ **Speaker** certifies the Money Bill; decision **final** — cannot be questioned in any court, either House or by the President
- Speaker decides **Tenth Schedule** cases, but these remain **subject to judicial review**
- Leader of the Opposition recognition (Speaker's direction) — party needs at least **one-tenth** of the House's strength; office statutory since the **1977 Act**
- **President** appoints the Pro-Tem Speaker; adjourning **sine die**, Tenth Schedule decisions and fixing the Deputy Speaker's election date are the **Speaker's**
### Deputy Speaker and joint sitting
- Deputy Speaker is **not subordinate** to the Speaker while exercising the office's functions
- Deputy Speaker's ruling in the Chair **cannot be appealed** to the Speaker
- Joint sitting (**Art. 118(4)**) — presided by Speaker, then Deputy Speaker, then the **Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman**

- **Meira Kumar** — first woman Speaker; **Sumitra Mahajan** — second
- Failed removal motions — **G.V. Mavalankar (1954)**, **Hukum Singh (1966)**, **Balram Jakhar (1987)**; motion on **Om Birla** listed for debate in **2026**
### Rajya Sabha

### Secretariat and Money Bill
- Secretariat staff rules (absent a law) — made by the **President** after consulting the **Speaker** and **Chairman**
- Lok Sabha Secretariat — directly under the **Speaker's** control, not the executive
- **Article 110(4)** — Speaker alone certifies a Money Bill; certificate **final**; Rajya Sabha **cannot overrule** it

## SEC 2:31:5 | Ending a sitting or session
### Terms
- **Adjournment** — suspends work in a sitting for a specified time
- **Prorogation** — ends a **session** only (not the House); by President under **Article 85(2)(a)**
- **Recess** — period from prorogation to the next session
- **Dissolution** — ends the Lok Sabha itself; **Article 85(2)(b)**; Article 123 is ordinances
### Effects
- Bills **do not lapse** on prorogation; pending notices lapse, except notices to introduce bills
- On dissolution pending business lapses, subject to **Article 107**; the Rajya Sabha is permanent
- Speaker does **not vacate** office on dissolution; continues until just before the new House **first meets**
- Quorum — **one-tenth** of total membership, including the presiding officer (**Article 100(3)**)

## SEC 2:31:6 | Sessions & Secretariat
### Sessions
- Max gap of **six months** — from the **last sitting** of one session to the **first sitting** of the next
- Constitution fixes **no number of sessions** and no minimum sitting days; at least **two** sessions a year follow from the six-month gap
- Budget, Monsoon and Winter sessions — a **convention**
- President may **summon the two Houses separately**; each must meet within six months of its last sitting
- **Article 87** — President's special address at the first session after each general election and the first session of each year; not after every prorogation
- President's address debated through a **Motion of Thanks**
### Secretariat
- **Article 98** — each House has **separate secretarial staff**; **common posts** for both Houses may be created
- **Secretary-General** — appointed by the **presiding officer** of the House, not the Union Government
- Deputy Speaker's election date — fixed by the **Speaker** under **Rule 8(1)**; communicated to members by the **Secretary-General**

## SEC 2:31:8 | Speaker → constitutional basis, resignation, dissolution and removal
### Election and oath
- ★ **Article 93** — Speaker is **chosen** by the House from among its members
- ★ Elected by **all the then members** of the Lok Sabha
- ★ No non-member can be chosen; Speaker must be a member **at the time of election** (no six-month window)
- ★ Speaker and Deputy Speaker do **not take an oath of office**
- ★ Pro-tem Speaker — **administers oath** to newly elected members
### Resignation, dissolution, removal
- ★ **Article 94** — Speaker resigns in writing to the **Deputy Speaker**
- ★ On dissolution the Speaker does **not vacate** office until immediately before the **first meeting** of the new House
- ★ Removed by a **resolution of the Lok Sabha** passed by a majority of **all the then members** — **Article 94(c)**
### Joint sitting and Secretariat
- ★ Joint session called by the **President**; presided by the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**
- ★ Lok Sabha Secretariat — under the direct control and supervision of the **Speaker**; authority exercised through the Secretary (**Article 98**)
### Persons
- ★ **G.V. (Ganesh Vasudev) Mavalankar** — Speaker of Provisional Parliament (1951) and first Lok Sabha Speaker (**15 May 1952**)
- ★ Speakers in order: **Mavalankar** (1952-56), **M. Ananthasayanam Iyengar** (1956-62), **Hukum Singh** (1962-67), **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy** (1967-69)
- ★ **N. Sanjiva Reddy** — only President who was earlier Lok Sabha Speaker
- ★ **P.A. Sangma** — first **tribal** Speaker (11th Lok Sabha, **23 May 1996**)

## SEC 2:31:9 | Presiding officers → oath and resignation/removal table

- ★ Indian Parliamentary Group — **former MPs** can become its members
- ★ **Vitthalbhai Patel** — first (elected) Speaker of the Central Legislative Assembly (**1925–1930**)

## SEC 2:31:10 | Speaker → normal sittings vs own removal resolution; Pro-Tem Speaker
- ★ Pro-tem Speaker — one of the **senior-most members**; list sent to the **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs** for appointment
- ★ Speaker presides over a **joint sitting** (Art. 118(4)); President **summons** it (Art. 108)
- ★ Every Money Bill endorsed with the Speaker's certificate (**Art. 110(4)**)
- ★ **Article 100** — Speaker's **casting vote** on an equality of votes
- ★ **Article 179** — State Speaker continues after dissolution until immediately before the **first sitting** of the new Assembly

## SEC 2:31:11 | Speaker → powers and Articles
- ★ **Speaker of Lok Sabha** decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill; decision **final** (**Article 110(3)**)
- ★ Speaker votes **only in case of a tie** (Article 100(1))
- ★ **Speaker** presides over the joint sitting of both Houses

## SEC 2:31:12 | Deputy Speaker → Articles 93 to 96, powers and position
- ★ Casting vote — used only **in a tie** (Article 100(1))
- ★ **Article 95** — when the Speaker's office is vacant, the **Deputy Speaker** performs its duties
- ★ A resigned Minister makes a statement in the Lok Sabha with the **Speaker's** permission; cannot be compelled to
- ★ Joint session presided by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha**

- ★ **Meira Kumar** — first **female** Speaker of Lok Sabha

- ★ **G.V. Mavalankar** — first Speaker (15 May 1952 till death, 27 Feb 1956); "**Father of the Lok Sabha**"

## SEC 2:31:15 | Secretariat and sessions → Articles
- ★ Recruitment rules for Lok Sabha secretarial staff — **President** after consultation with the **Speaker** (**Article 98(3)**); appointment orders issued by the Speaker

## SEC 2:31:16 | Summoning and the six-month rule — Article 85
- ★ **Article 85(1)** — six months must not intervene between the last sitting of one session and the first of the next
- ★ Interval between sessions — at most **six months**; Parliament must meet at least **twice** a year

## SEC 2:31:17 | Secretariat → Article 98
- ★ Parliament Secretariat — **independent of the Government**; idea mooted in **1926** by **Vithalbhai J. Patel**
- ★ **Article 98(1)** — separate secretarial staff for each House; **common posts** may be created; **Parliament** may regulate their service
- ★ Joint session presided by the **Speaker** (Art. 118(4)); in his absence the **Deputy Speaker**
- ★ President's address prepared by the **Union Cabinet** (real executive power)

# ROW 2:32 | Parliamentary devices - Question Hour, Zero Hour, motions (adjournment, no-confidence, censure, calling attention)
## SEC 2:32:0 | Question Hour vs Zero Hour
### Question Hour
- **Question Hour** — most effective **day-to-day** vigilance over the executive; Ministers must answer
- Formal, provided in the Rules; for **questions to Ministers**
### Zero Hour
- Informal **Indian innovation**, in use since **1962**; **not mentioned** in the Rules of Procedure
- Begins **immediately after Question Hour**; urgent matters raised without prior notice
- Member **intimates the Presiding Officer on the same day**; no fixed duration in the Rules
- Ministers are **not bound to reply**
### Timings since 2014
| House | 11 AM – noon | Noon – 1 PM |
|---|---|---|
| Rajya Sabha | **Zero Hour** | **Question Hour** |
| Lok Sabha | Question Hour | Zero Hour |
### Other
- **Calling Attention** — Indian innovation in the Rules of Procedure, from **1954**
- **Whip** — based on **parliamentary conventions**; not in the Constitution, Rules or any statute
- **Confidence Motion** — admissible only in the **Lok Sabha**

## SEC 2:32:1 | Types of questions
### Three kinds of questions
| Type | Answer | Supplementaries | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starred** | **Oral** | Allowed | Marked with an **asterisk**; max **20** a day; green paper |
| **Unstarred** | **Written** | Not allowed | No asterisk; no daily limit; white paper |
| **Short notice** | Oral | Allowed | Notice **less than 10 days**; **urgent** public importance; light pink paper |
- Ordinary questions need at least **15 clear days'** notice
- Starred and unstarred questions — matters of **general** public importance
- Question to a **private member** — **yellow** paper (not blue)
- No "adjournment question"; adjournment is a **motion**
### Other devices
- ★ **White Paper** — official Government/Ministry document explaining policy on a subject; collects feedback before a bill
- Adjournment Motion — only for a **definite matter of urgent public importance**; sets aside normal business
- Private Members' Resolutions — **alternate Fridays**, afternoon
- **Yielding the floor** — member speaking gives way to another member or the Chair
- Question Hour + supplementary questions + adjournment motions — the "**accountability triangle**"

## SEC 2:32:2 | Indian innovations
### Rules of the Lok Sabha
| Rule | Device |
|---|---|
| **Rule 193** | Short-duration discussion, **no voting** |
| **Rule 184** | Discussion followed by **voting** |
| **Rule 377** | Matters not raisable under any other device; written notice + Speaker's consent; up to **30** a day since **2015** |
- Lok Sabha proceedings governed mainly by the **Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business**; the Constitution gives only the framework (No-Confidence Motion and Rule 377 come from the Rules)
### Dates
- Short-duration discussion — **1953**; limited to **two hours**, no vote
- Calling Attention — **1954**
- Zero Hour — **1962**; lasts at most one hour
### Calling Attention and adjournment
- **Calling Attention Motion** — in **both Houses**; seeks an **authoritative statement** from a Minister
- Calling attention notices **narrowed the scope** of the **Adjournment Motion**
- Zero Hour and calling attention grew from Indian practice; **neither is in the Constitution**
- Adjournment motion admitted only when other routes (e.g. short duration discussion) would be **too late**; privilege questions cannot be raised this way
- Question Hour's purpose — enforce **accountability of the Council of Ministers**

## SEC 2:32:3 | Calling attention vs adjournment
### Motions
- **Adjournment Motion** — **Lok Sabha only**; implies **censure**
- **Calling Attention Motion** — **no censure**; needs presiding officer's permission; either House
- **No-Confidence Motion** — most powerful weapon of accountability; leave needs **50 members**; flows from **Article 75(3)**; Lok Sabha only; no reasons needed
- Privilege motion — referred to the **Committee of Privileges**
- Point of order — not debated; the **Presiding Officer** rules on it
- Zero Hour — **after** Question Hour (noon–1 PM in Lok Sabha); informal; at most one hour
- Three kinds of question — **starred, unstarred, short notice**
### Committees
- Committee of Privileges — Lok Sabha **15** (Speaker nominates); Rajya Sabha **10** (Chairman nominates)
- Speaker — ex-officio Chair of the **Business Advisory**, **Rules** and **General Purposes** Committees
- **Business Advisory Committee** — timetable; Rules Committee — rule changes; Assurances Committee — Ministers' promises
- **Select Committee** — one House; **Joint Committee** — both Houses, formed on a motion
- Subordinate Legislation Committee — **15** members in each House; **Ministers barred**
- **2G JPC** — chaired by **P.C. Chacko**; **30** members (**20** LS + **10** RS); the 15 + 7 split is the **PAC**
### Other
- Speaker's removal (**Art. 94(c)**) — **effective majority**, not simple majority
- Quorum — **one-tenth** of total membership (**Article 100**)
- First Lok Sabha election — **489** seats contested; first Speaker **G.V. Mavalankar**

## SEC 2:32:4 | Survival motions
| Motion | Moved by | Reasons | Effect if passed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No-confidence** (**Rule 198**) | **Opposition**; Lok Sabha only; **50** members | **Not needed** | Government must resign |
| **Censure** | — | **Must be stated** | Does **not** compel resignation |
| **Confidence** | **Government**, to prove majority | — | If defeated, the **Government falls** |
- ★ Censure Motion **differs** from the No-confidence motion in purpose and procedure; no separate "leave of the House" needed
- First no-confidence motion — against **Nehru** in **1963** by **J.B. Kripalani**
### Privilege motion
- Against a Minister **withholding or distorting facts**; censure motion is disapproval of specific policies
- **Rule 222** (Lok Sabha) and **Rule 187** (Rajya Sabha)
- Needs presiding officer's consent; goes to the Committee of Privileges; does **not** force resignation
### Other
- **Rule 184** — discussion ending in a formal motion and **vote**; defeat can threaten the ruling party
- **Statutory resolution** — moved under a constitutional or statutory provision; **Government resolution** — moved by a minister (e.g. approve a treaty)
- Rule 180A-E (RS) — **Special Mentions**

## SEC 2:32:5 | Financial committees
| Committee | Members | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimates** | **30**, all **Lok Sabha**; **largest** | "**Continuous economy committee**"; present form 1950 (**John Mathai**); Chairman from ruling party |

- AP Assembly — **biometric attendance** for MLAs from the **Budget Session 2026**

## SEC 2:32:6 | Other committees
### Committees

- **Ad hoc** committees — end when the task is done
- PAC, Estimates, COPU and DRSCs — "**watchdogs over the executive**"
- SC/ST Welfare Committee — **no minister** can be a member

### Other
- **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs** — keeps contact with Chief Whips in both Houses
- Resolution on the same subject barred for **one year**; always a substantive motion, put to vote, withdrawn only with leave
- **Second ARC** — code of ethics, Right to Public Services, e-governance; **Shanta Kumar** — FCI/PDS; **Ashok Dalwai** — farmers' income; **Kasturirangan** — education
- **Article 85** — gap between sessions max **six months**, so at least **two sessions** a year (a consequence, not the explanation)

## SEC 2:32:7 | Closures & whips
### Closures
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Simple closure | Matter declared sufficiently discussed and put to vote |
| **Kangaroo closure** | Only **important clauses** debated; others taken as passed |
| **Guillotine** | Allotted time expires; **all remaining** clauses/demands put to vote at once |
### Whips
| Whip | Meaning |
|---|---|
| One-line | Informative |
| **Two-line** | Direction to **attend** |
| Three-line | Strict **voting** direction |
- Defying the Whip — disqualification under the **Tenth Schedule**
### Other
- **Half-an-hour discussion** — matter already sufficiently debated needing **factual elucidation**; no motion or vote
- Cut motions — **economy cut**: by a specified amount; **policy cut**: to **Re 1**; **token cut**: by **Rs 100**
- PAC recommendations — **not binding**; checks spending within the demand, not policy
- **Urjit Patel** committee — inflation targeting **4% ± 2%**; **Nachiket Mor** — financial inclusion

# ROW 2:33 | Legislative procedure - ordinary, money, financial and constitutional amendment bills; joint sitting
## SEC 2:33:0 | Government vs private member's bill
| | Government Bill | Private Member's Bill |
|---|---|---|
| Notice before introduction | **7 days** | **1 month** |
| Drafted by | **Concerned department** with the **Law Ministry** | The member |
- Only **14** Private Members' Bills have become law; the last in **1970**

## SEC 2:33:1 | Ordinary bill stages
- ★ **General discussion** on a Bill — during the **second reading** (first stage: leave to introduce, then introduction)
- **Second Reading** — general discussion, committee stage and **clause-by-clause** voting; **Third Reading** — the Bill as a whole
- Second House may **pass, amend, reject** or keep it pending **six months** (possible deadlock)
- **Suspensive veto** — President returns a non-money Bill; must assent if **repassed**; no time limit fixed for the **pocket veto**; no **qualified veto** for the Indian President
- Money Bill (**Art. 111**) — President may **assent or withhold**, but **cannot return** it

## SEC 2:33:2 | Money Bill (Arts. 109, 110)
### Scope (Art. 110)
- **Article 110(1)** — imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of **any tax**
- **Article 110(2)** — not a Money Bill merely for **fines or penalties**, licence fees or local taxes; taxes, borrowing and the Consolidated Fund are Money Bill matters
### Procedure
- ★ Money Bill presented in the **Lok Sabha**
- Introduced only in the **Lok Sabha**, only by a **minister**, only on the **President's recommendation**
- **No joint sitting** for a Money Bill (governed by **Article 109**)
- Speaker's certificate needed for a **Money Bill**, not for a **Financial Bill-I**; joint sitting allowed for Financial Bills I and II
- President **cannot return** a Money Bill; **can return** Financial Bills I and II
- **Article 274** — Bills affecting taxation in which **States are interested**; expenditure from the CFI needs recommendation under **Article 117(3)**
- No joint sitting for a Bill that has **lapsed** on dissolution
### Cases and States
- **K.S. Puttaswamy (2018)** — Aadhaar Act upheld as a Money Bill; **Rojer Mathew (2019)** — issue referred to a **seven-judge** bench
- AP Legislative Council — only **recommendations within 14 days** on a Money Bill; cannot reject or amend finally
- **November 2025** advisory opinion — courts **cannot fix timelines** or declare **deemed assent**; did not uphold the Tamil Nadu deemed assent

## SEC 2:33:3 | Money vs Financial Bills
### Bill types
| Bill | Article | House of introduction | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Money Bill** | 109–110 | Lok Sabha only | Cannot amend/reject |
| **Financial Bill-I** | **117(1)** | **Lok Sabha only**, on President's recommendation | **May amend or reject** |
| **Financial Bill-II** | **117(3)** | **Either House**; President's recommendation at **consideration stage** | May amend or reject |
| Ordinary Bill | 107–108 | Either House | — |
| **Constitution Amendment** | **368** | — | — |
- **Article 110(3)** — **Speaker's** decision on a Money Bill is final; certificate endorsed on the Bill to the RS and the President
- **Finance Bill** — gives effect to the Budget's **tax proposals**; every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, not vice versa
- **Article 113** — Demands for Grants; **Article 114** — Appropriation Bill
### Private Members' Bills
- Lok Sabha — time allotted by the **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions**; defeat has no bearing on the government
- Rajya Sabha — no such committee; **Business Advisory Committee** allots the time
### Joint sitting and dissolution
- Six-month count (Art. 108) excludes prorogation and adjournments over **four** consecutive days; other grounds: rejection, final disagreement on amendments
- Joint sitting presided by Speaker → Deputy Speaker → **RS Deputy Chairman** → a member chosen by those present; the RS **Chairman** does not preside
- Lapses on dissolution — a Bill **passed by the Lok Sabha, pending in the Rajya Sabha**
- **Article 79** — Parliament = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**; President is not a member of either House

## SEC 2:33:4 | Joint sitting (Art. 108)
- Amendment **reducing or abolishing** a tax — **no** President's recommendation needed; **increasing** a tax needs it
- President does not summon a joint sitting — the Bill **comes to an end**
- Decision — **simple majority** of members of both Houses **present and voting** together
- Excluded from joint sitting — **Money Bills** and **Constitution Amendment Bills**; Financial Bills I and II can go

|---|---|

| **1978** | Banking Service Commission Repeal |

## SEC 2:33:6 | Lapse on LS dissolution
- **Adjournment / adjournment sine die** — Bills **do not lapse**; prorogation ends no Bill
- **Dissolution** — all pending Lok Sabha business lapses
- Lapse: Bills pending in the **Lok Sabha**, or passed by it and **pending in the Rajya Sabha**
- Survives: Bill pending in the Rajya Sabha **never passed by the Lok Sabha**
- Survives: Bill for which a **joint sitting was notified** before dissolution

## SEC 2:33:7 | Exam corner
- **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024** (simultaneous elections) — proposes **Article 82A**; still before the **JPC**, not enacted
- **Article 200** — Governor **cannot return a Money Bill** for reconsideration

## SEC 2:33:8 | Bills → basics; government vs private member's
- ★ Indian Parliament = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**
- ★ A Bill becomes an Act after the **President's assent** (**Article 111**)
- ★ Ordinary legislative Bill — in **either House**; Money Bill only in the **Lok Sabha**; Bill creating **All-India Services** only in the **Rajya Sabha**

## SEC 2:33:9 | Ordinary bill → stages and President's assent (Art. 111)
- ★ Money Bill **cannot be returned** for reconsideration by the President (not a feature)
- ★ Money Bill — only in the **Lok Sabha**, with the President's prior recommendation; RS cannot amend/reject, only suggest within **14 days**
- ★ Joint sitting — held for an **Ordinary Bill**

## SEC 2:33:10 | Money Bill → Articles 109 and 110, procedure and cases
### Definition and introduction
- ★ Money Bill defined in **Article 110**; deemed a Money Bill if it contains **only** Art. 110 matters (taxes, borrowing, etc.)
- ★ Introduced only in the **Lok Sabha**; **Article 109(1)** — not in the Rajya Sabha
- ★ Speaker's decision on a Money Bill is **final**; no obligation to consult; no joint session
- ★ Every Money Bill is a Finance Bill, but **not every Finance Bill is a Money Bill**; both introduced only in the Lok Sabha on the **President's recommendation**
### Rajya Sabha's role
- ★ Rajya Sabha **can amend or reject** a Finance (financial) Bill; **cannot** alter a Money Bill
- ★ Financial bills — **Money Bills (Art. 110)**, **Financial Bills (I) (Art. 117(1))**, **Financial Bills (II) (Art. 117(3))**
- ★ RS may detain a Money Bill at most **14 days**; cannot amend or reject it (**Art. 109**)
- ★ Lok Sabha is **not bound** to accept the Rajya Sabha's recommendations
### President and joint sitting
- ★ President **cannot return** a Finance (Money) Bill — not his prerogative (**Article 111**)
- ★ Joint sitting — **Article 108** (not 109); presided by the **Speaker**

## SEC 2:33:12 | Money Bill vs Financial Bills (I, II) vs Finance Bill
- ★ Rajya Sabha returns a Money/Finance Bill within **fourteen days**; ordinary Bill — no time limit
- ★ Bill merely involving **expenditure** (no Art. 110 matter) — can be initiated in **either House**
- ★ "No bill imposing tax without the President's recommendation" — **Article 117**
- ★ Special provisions on Financial Bills — **Article 117**; **Art. 119** — regulation by law; **Art. 121** — restriction on discussion

## SEC 2:33:13 | Articles → House of introduction and President's prior recommendation
- ★ Rajya Sabha **cannot reject or amend** a Money Bill
- ★ Rajya Sabha **cannot vote** on the **Demands for Grants**, but **can discuss** the Annual Financial Statement
- ★ President's **prior sanction** needed for Bills on: **new State**, **taxation in which States are interested**, **altering State boundaries**, **Money Bill**

## SEC 2:33:14 | Joint sitting → Article 108 provisions and instances
- ★ Joint sitting — **Article 108**; Article 109 is the Money Bill procedure
- ★ Applies only to **ordinary bills**; not to Money Bills or **Constitution Amendment Bills** (Art. 368)
- ★ **Summoned by the President**; requires the **intention of the President**
- ★ Grounds — Bill **rejected** by the other House, or Houses **finally disagree** on amendments, etc.
- ★ Presided by the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (**Art. 118(4)**)

## SEC 2:33:15 | Lapsing of bills — dissolution, prorogation, adjournment
- ★ Joint session already summoned, then Lok Sabha dissolved — joint session **held at the scheduled time**
- ★ Bill **already lapsed** on dissolution — **no joint sitting** can be summoned

## SEC 2:33:16 | Universal angles
- ★ **Appropriation Bill** — a **Money Bill**; not mandatory to be passed by the RS — deemed passed if not returned within **14 days**
- ★ Money Bill substantially amended by RS — **Lok Sabha may still proceed**, accepting or not accepting the recommendations (**Art. 109(2)**)

# ROW 2:34 | Budget in Parliament - stages, cut motions, appropriation and finance bills, Consolidated/Contingency/Public Account
## SEC 2:34:0 | Budget basics
- ★ Article 112's **Annual Financial Statement** is commonly called the **Budget**; the word "Budget" does not occur in the Constitution
- ★ First Union Budget of independent India — **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty**, on **26 November 1947**
- **Budget Circular** — generally issued in **September**; printing of Budget documents marked by the **Halwa ceremony**
- Prepared by the **Budget Division**, **Department of Economic Affairs**, Ministry of Finance
- AP Budget 2026-27 (**14 Feb 2026**) — **Payyavula Keshav**; separate agriculture budget — **K. Atchannaidu**

## SEC 2:34:1 | Budget Articles in order
| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| **112** | Annual Financial Statement |
| **113** | Estimates procedure / Demands for Grants |
| **114** | Appropriation Bill |
| **115** | **Supplementary, additional or excess** grants |
| **116** | **Vote on account**, **vote of credit**, **exceptional grants** |
- ★ Stages of normal financial legislation — **presentation**, **discussion**, **Appropriation Bill**, **Finance Bill**; vote on account, vote of credit and exceptional grants are not stages
- Second batch of Supplementary Demands for Grants 2025-26 (approved by Lok Sabha) — **Article 115**

## SEC 2:34:2 | Three funds
### Funds
| Fund | Article | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Consolidated Fund** | **266(1)** | Union revenues, loans raised (incl. **Treasury Bills**, market and foreign loans), loan repayments; withdrawal only by an **appropriation law** |
| **Public Account** | **266(2)** | Provident funds, small savings, remittances, **savings bank deposits** (held in trust); **no prior vote** of Parliament needed |
| **Contingency Fund** | **267(1)** | Held by the **Finance Secretary**, **Department of Economic Affairs** (not Expenditure) |
- Budget covers all three funds; presented as **Revenue Budget** and **Capital Budget**
- **CAG** audits all three funds of both the **Union and the States**
### Appropriation
- President cannot withdraw alone; **Article 114** requires an Appropriation Act
- **Article 114(3)** — Appropriation Act for **every** withdrawal, even **charged** expenditure
- No amendment may vary a voted grant or charged expenditure; Appropriation Bill has an **automatic repeal clause**
### Grants
- Cut motions — only in the **Lok Sabha**, during **voting on Demands for Grants**; if passed, signal loss of confidence
- **Vote on account** — since the **1 February** Budget (2017), usually needed only in **interim-Budget** years
- **Excess grant** — spending exceeded the grant; needs prior approval of the **PAC**
- **Token grant** — linked to **re-appropriation**; must be voted by the Lok Sabha

## SEC 2:34:3 | Cut motions (LS only)
| Cut | Reduces demand | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy cut** | To **₹1** | Complete disapproval of the policy |
| **Economy cut** | By a **specified amount** | — |
| **Token cut** | By **₹100** | Air a specific grievance |

## SEC 2:34:4 | Charged on CFI (non-votable)
- Charged expenditure — **not put to vote**, but **both Houses can discuss** it
- **Supreme Court judges'** salaries — charged on the **CFI**
- **High Court judges'** salaries and allowances — **State's Consolidated Fund**; their **pensions** — **CFI**
- President's emoluments and **debt charges** — charged on the CFI
- **Third Schedule** — forms of **oaths and affirmations**; **Second Schedule** — emoluments, allowances, privileges

## SEC 2:34:5 | Salaries: charged or voted

- **CAG** — salary, pension and office expenses **charged** on the CFI
- **Election Commission** — salaries and running costs **voted**, not charged
- **PM and Union Ministers** — salaries **voted** through annual Demands for Grants
- **Vice-President** — **no salary as Vice-President**; paid only as **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**

## SEC 2:34:8 | Budget → Article 112, terms and FIRSTs
- ★ Budget preparation and presentation — **Department of Economic Affairs** (Ministry of Finance)
- ★ **Article 112** — President causes the **Annual Financial Statement** to be laid before both Houses each year
- ★ Financial year — **1 April** to **31 March**
- ★ Estimates of expenditure submitted as **Demands for Grants** (**Art. 113(2)**); Lok Sabha may pass, reject or reduce them
- ★ "**Budget**" does not appear in the Constitution
- ★ **Economic Survey** — presented **before the Budget** each year

## SEC 2:34:9 | Funds → Articles 266-267
### Consolidated Fund
- ★ All revenues by taxes and other receipts — credited to the **Consolidated Fund of India** (**Article 266**)
- ★ CFI also gets loans via **treasury bills**, ways and means advances, and loan repayments
- ★ Withdrawal from the CFI — authorised by the **Parliament of India** (**Article 114**, Appropriation Bill)
- ★ Right to issue money from the CFI (UPPCS 2000 key) — "**none of the above**"
- ★ Parliamentary control over finance — placing the **Annual Financial Statement**; withdrawal only after the **Appropriation Bill**; supplementary grant provisions
### Vote on Account
- ★ Needed when the regular Budget is **not expected to be passed** in time (e.g. elections near)
- ★ Permits withdrawal from the **CFI** pending the Budget (**Article 116**)
### Contingency Fund
- ★ **Article 267** — Contingency Fund, an **imprest** at the President's disposal, set up by Parliament by law
- ★ For **unforeseen expenditure**; President spends **before authorisation** of Parliament
- ★ State Contingency Fund — established by the **State Legislature**; operated by the **Governor**

## SEC 2:34:10 | Cut motions → terms
- ★ **Cut motion** — proposal to **reduce expenditure** (demands for grants) in the Budget; moved in the Lok Sabha
- ★ Object — draw the House's attention to the matter specified

## SEC 2:34:11 | Articles → Appropriation Bill and charged expenditure (114)
- ★ Appropriation Bill — carries an **automatic repeal clause**; authorises withdrawals from the CFI
- ★ Charged expenditure (e.g. salaries of constitutional authorities) — **cannot be voted** but **can be discussed**

## SEC 2:34:12 | Charged expenditure → meaning and items on the Consolidated Fund
- ★ Supreme Court judges' salaries — **Consolidated Fund of India**
- ★ High Court judges' salaries — **Consolidated Fund of the State** (**Art. 202(3)(d)**); pension from the **CFI** (**Art. 112(3)(d)**)
- ★ Charged expenditure (**Art. 112(3)**) — **not voted** by Parliament; can be discussed in either House

## SEC 2:34:13 | Constitutional salaries → who fixes, who pays, charged or voted
- ★ Not charged on the CFI — salary of the **Vice-President** (paid as RS Chairman)
- ★ Not charged on the CFI — salary of the **Prime Minister**
- ★ Not a mandatory charged expenditure — salary and pension of **Election Commission** members
- ★ Charged (Art. 112(3)) — **President's** emoluments; **RS Chairman/Deputy Chairman** salaries; **CJI**'s salary; **HC judges' pensions**
- ★ Governor of two or more States — emoluments **allocated among the States** as the **President** determines (**Art. 158(3A)**)
