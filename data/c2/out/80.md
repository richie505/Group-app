# ROW 4:4 | Seasons - inclination of the axis, solstices and equinoxes, distribution of insolation
## SEC 4:4:0 | Why seasons occur
- Seasons (Sun's angle + day length) result from **axial inclination**, **parallelism of the axis**, **revolution** and the **spherical shape** of the Earth
- Factors affecting distribution of insolation: **angle of incidence**, **duration of day**, **atmospheric transparency**, **land configuration**
  - **Direction of ocean tides** is NOT a factor

## SEC 4:4:1 | Summer vs winter
- Hemisphere **tilted towards the Sun** = **summer** hemisphere (e.g. Northern Hemisphere tilted towards Sun → summer)
- Summer hemisphere heats more: **more direct rays** + **longer days**; higher Sun, concentrated energy
- **Oblique rays** → lower heating: same energy spread over a **larger area**
- **Vertically overhead Sun** → **no shadow** at noon
  - Higher Sun → shorter shadow; lower Sun → longer shadow
  - **Midnight sun** = Sun visible above the horizon at midnight

## SEC 4:4:2 | Distance is not the cause
- Seasons caused mainly by **axial tilt** (solar angle + day length), not distance
- Earth is **nearest the Sun** during **northern winter**

## SEC 4:4:3 | Insolation
- Greatest seasonal differences in day length and insolation: **high latitudes** (very large annual swings)
- Insolation generally **decreases from the tropics towards the poles**
- Equator is **not always** the maximum: cloudless **subtropical deserts** can receive the maximum

## SEC 4:4:4 | Seasons by latitude
- ★ **Christmas in Australia** falls in **summer** — Australia is in the **Southern Hemisphere** (December = summer there; Northern Hemisphere has winter)
- Axis tilted about **23½°**
- Southern Hemisphere's longest day: around **22 December**; Northern Hemisphere's: around **21 June**
- **Four distinct seasons**: **mid-latitude** regions
  - Low latitudes: wet and dry periods; subtropics: hot summers, mild winters

## SEC 4:4:5 | Shadows & India
### Overhead Sun
| Date | Sun overhead at |
|---|---|
| 21 June (June solstice) | **Tropic of Cancer** |
| December solstice | **Tropic of Capricorn** |
| Equinoxes | **Equator** |

- ★ On **21 June** the Sun **does not set** at the **Arctic Circle** (traces a full circle above the horizon)
- On 21 June, objects at the **Tropic of Cancer** cast **no noon shadow**
- ★ Noon shadow is **smallest** on **June 21** (in north India)
- Only places **between the Tropics** get the overhead Sun; **Delhi** (north of **23½°N**) never does
- **Andhra Pradesh** (about **12°41′N–19°07′N**, between Equator and Tropic of Cancer) gets the overhead noon Sun **twice a year**
### Day length and hot season
- On 21 June day length increases northward: **Srinagar** > Delhi > Hyderabad > Chennai > Kanyakumari
- About **50°C** recorded at **Ganganagar** in **1935** (March–May hot weather season)
- IMD's four seasons: winter → pre-monsoon → South-West monsoon → **post-monsoon**

## SEC 4:4:6 | AP's seasons
- Coldest place in AP: **Lambasingi** (about **2°C**)

## SEC 4:4:9 | Summer vs winter and insolation — factors and distribution
- ★ Noon temperature at a place (e.g. Delhi) is higher in summer because in **winter** the Sun's rays **bend more** (fall more obliquely)
- Sun's rays perpendicular at the Equator twice a year: **21 March** and **23 September**
- 21 March – 23 September: Sun between Equator and **Tropic of Cancer** → **summer** in Northern Hemisphere

## SEC 4:4:10 | Seasons by hemisphere and latitude zone → features
- ★ Seasonal differences are maximum in the **mid-latitudes** — large summer–winter temperature differences; **four distinct seasons**

## SEC 4:4:11 | Shadows and places in India → overhead Sun, day length, hot season
- ★ Longest summer sunlight among the cities: **Srinagar** (higher latitude)

## SEC 4:4:-1 | (whole topic)
- ★ "**Yellow Sea** is the coastless sea of the world" is a **wrong** statement
- ★ Southern Hemisphere has **summer in January** (its hottest month)
- ★ **Stratosphere** contains the ozone layer — called the **Ozonosphere**

# ROW 4:5 | Interior of the earth - crust, mantle, core; discontinuities and seismic evidence
## SEC 4:5:0 | Sources of information
- **Direct** sources: **surface rocks from mining areas**, **magma** and **lava**
- **Indirect** sources: **meteorites**, seismic waves, gravity anomalies, magnetic surveys
- Deepest hole (~**12 km**): drilled by the **USSR** in the **1970s** on the **Kola Peninsula**
  - Mponeng and TauTona mines (South Africa): only about **3.9 km**
- Surface seismic waves = **L-waves** (P = primary, S = secondary)

## SEC 4:5:1 | Seismic waves
- ★ Earthquakes generate **P, S and L** waves
  - **P-waves** travel **fastest** (arrive first); P and S = **body waves** (travel through the interior)
- **P-waves**: **longitudinal** (push–pull) — particles vibrate parallel to propagation
- **S-waves**: **transverse/shear** — particles vibrate perpendicular; travel **only through solids**, stop at liquids
- **Surface waves** do the most damage

## SEC 4:5:2 | Shadow zone
- **P-wave shadow zone**: about **103°–142°** from the epicentre
- **S-waves** absent beyond **105°** → S-wave shadow zone larger than P-wave shadow zone
- S-waves **disappear at the outer core** because the **outer core is liquid** (S-waves cannot pass through liquids)

## SEC 4:5:3 | Layers
### Order and state
- Order: **crust** → **mantle** → **outer core** → **inner core** (centre)
- Seismic waves reveal discontinuities: speed changes with **density** → **reflection and refraction** at boundaries
- **Mantle**: mostly **solid** but **flows plastically** over geological time (drives convection)
- **Outer core**: **liquid** iron and nickel (why S-waves stop); **inner core**: **solid** (iron)
- Core is mainly **iron and nickel**; core begins at about **2,900 km**
### Numbers
| Layer | Value |
|---|---|
| Oceanic crust | about **5 km** thick; **basaltic**, denser, richer in Fe–Mg |
| Continental crust | about **30 km** thick (more beneath mountains); thicker, generally older |
| Mantle | **83%** of volume, **68%** of mass |

- Density increases downward: crust **2.8–3.0**, mantle **4.6**, inner core **13.3–13.6** g/cm³
- **Moho** discovered in **1909** (Andrija Mohorovicic); about **30 km** below continents, **5 km** below oceans
### Layering schemes
- **Compositional** layers: crust, mantle, core
- **Mechanical** layers: lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere, outer core, inner core
- Beneath **Tibet**, the dense lower layer of the **Indian plate** is reported to be **delaminating** into the mantle at about **100 km** depth

## SEC 4:5:4 | Discontinuities (top down)
| Discontinuity | Separates |
|---|---|
| **Conrad** | upper and lower crust |
| **Mohorovicic (Moho)** | crust and mantle |
| **Gutenberg** | mantle and outer core |
| **Lehmann** | outer and inner core |

- Order from surface: **Conrad → Moho → (Repetti) → Gutenberg → Lehmann**
- At the Moho, P-wave velocity rises from about **6.9 to 8.1 km/s**
- **410 km** and **660 km** discontinuities: mineralogical zones / **mineral phase changes** in the mantle

## SEC 4:5:5 | Lithosphere vs asthenosphere
- **Lithosphere**: rigid; **crust + uppermost mantle**
- **Asthenosphere**: weak, partly molten zone in the **upper mantle** (~**100–200 km**); major source of **magma**
- Lithospheric plates **glide over the asthenosphere**
- Sequence: lithosphere → asthenosphere → mesosphere → **barysphere** (= the **core**)

## SEC 4:5:6 | Suess: SIAL, SIMA, NIFE
- **NIFE** = **nickel (Ni)** + **ferrum/iron (Fe)** — main metals of the core (not nitrogen and fluorine)

## SEC 4:5:7 | Crust, core & heat
### Crustal composition
| Element | % by weight | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| **Oxygen** | 46.60 | 1 (most abundant element) |
| **Silicon** | 27.72 | 2 (not a metal) |
| **Aluminium** | 8.13 | 3 (most abundant **metal**) |
| **Iron** | about 5 | 4 |
| Calcium | about 3.6 | — |
### Core and magnetism
- Core: about **16%** of volume, **32%** of mass
- Core = iron + nickel (NIFE) with lighter elements **silicon**, **sulphur**, **oxygen** (not aluminium, sodium, potassium)
- Magnetic field generated by **convection** in the **liquid outer core**; changes in flow linked to **field reversals**
- Magnetic field **shields life** from **solar wind** and **cosmic radiation**
- Magnetic axis inclined about **11.5°** to the geographic axis
### Heat, pressure, density
- **Temperature, pressure and density** all generally **increase with depth**
- Upper-crust geothermal gradient: about **1°C per 32 m** (slows deeper)
- Inner core stays **solid** due to **immense pressure** of overlying rocks
- Average density of Earth: about **5.5 g/cm³** (densest planet)
- Geothermal sites: **Bakreshwar** — **West Bengal**; **Cambay** — **Gujarat**

## SEC 4:5:8 | Exam corner
- Diamonds reach the crust from the mantle through **kimberlite pipes**
- About **90%** of India's diamond reserves: **Madhya Pradesh**

## SEC 4:5:9 | Sources of information on the interior — types and places
- Direct source: **surface rocks from mining areas** (the material itself is examined); seismic waves, gravity and magnetic surveys are indirect

## SEC 4:5:11 | Seismic waves — types, evidence and shadow zone
- ★ Outer layer of the Earth: **crust** — outermost solid layer, thin; **oceanic** and **continental** types
- ★ Seismic waves are recorded by the **seismograph**, a **vibrating rod**

## SEC 4:5:12 | Layers — position, composition, thickness, density and state
- ★ Core (below the mantle) mainly made of **iron** (with nickel — NiFe)
  - ★ Outer core about **2260 km** thick, mostly liquid iron and nickel; inner core radius about **1220 km**, mostly iron
- ★ Unlike Earth's fluid core, the **Moon's core** is **solid**

## SEC 4:5:13 | Discontinuities → layers separated
- ★ Boundary between crust and mantle: **Moho** — discovered by Croatian seismologist **Andrija Mohorovicic** in **1909**

## SEC 4:5:15 | Composition — SIAL, SIMA, NIFE and crustal elements
- ★ Interior layer (**Nife**) made of **nickel and ferrum** (iron)
- ★ Most abundant element in the crust: **oxygen** (46.60%) — then silicon 27.72, aluminium 8.13, iron 5.00

# ROW 4:6 | Rocks and minerals - igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic; the rock cycle
## SEC 4:6:0 | Three rock types
### Igneous
- ★ **Granite** is **igneous**, not metamorphic; **granite = intrusive**, **basalt = extrusive**
### Sedimentary
- ★ **Coal** is **sedimentary**; sedimentary rocks cover **75%** of Earth's surface; hold most fossils and mineral oil
- All sedimentary: **sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate**
- Called **stratified** rocks: sediments deposited, compacted, cemented in successive layers (**strata**)
- Modes: **chemically** formed — **rock salt**; **mechanically** — sandstone; **organically** — coal
### Metamorphic
- ★ **Marble** = metamorphic, from **limestone** by heat and pressure
- From igneous: granite → **gneiss**; basalt → **amphibolite**, **schist**
- **Marble, slate, quartzite** metamorphic; **limestone** is sedimentary (odd one out; parent of marble)
- Agents of metamorphism: **heat and pressure** (complete melting not required — would give magma)
- Typical metamorphic minerals: **garnet, kyanite, sillimanite**; **halite** is not (chemical sedimentary)
### Mineral
- **Quartz** = **silicon dioxide (SiO2)**; hard, weathering-resistant; in granite and sandstone

## SEC 4:6:1 | Igneous: texture & make-up
- **Intrusive** rocks cool slowly at depth → **coarse grains** (granite, **gabbro**)
- **Felsic**: silica-rich, **light-coloured**, relatively low density; **mafic**: rich in Fe, Mg, Ca — dark and **dense**

| Rock | Type |
|---|---|
| **Rhyolite** | felsic extrusive |
| Granite | felsic intrusive |
| Diorite | intermediate intrusive |
| Basalt | mafic extrusive |

- **Lithology**: physical character of a rock unit — **texture, grain size, composition**

## SEC 4:6:2 | Parent → metamorphic
| Parent | Metamorphic |
|---|---|
| **Limestone** | **Marble** |
| Shale | Slate |
| Sandstone | Quartzite |
| Coal | Graphite |

## SEC 4:6:3 | Rock cycle
- Weathering → erosion → deposition → **lithification** → sedimentary rock; heat and pressure → metamorphic; melting → magma
- Exposed metamorphic rocks can weather into **sediment**; any rock type can become another
- CO2 in rainwater forms **carbonic acid** → weathering
- **Enhanced rock weathering**: crushed **basalt** spread on farmland; locks CO2 as **bicarbonate**

## SEC 4:6:4 | Minerals
### Abundance
- Crust: **oxygen** (~**46.6%**) first, **silicon** (~**27.7%**) second; **iron** = second-most abundant metal (~5.0%)
- Earth as a whole by mass: **iron** 32.1% > **oxygen** 30.1% > **silicon** 15.1% > **magnesium** 13.9%
- **Feldspar**: about **one-half** of the crust
- Suess: **SIAL** (silicon-aluminium) — granitic, continental; **SIMA** — basaltic; **NIFE** — nickel-iron core
### Classes and crystallisation
- **Muscovite** — **phyllosilicate**; albite — tectosilicate; hornblende — double-chain, augite — single-chain inosilicate
- Bowen's series: **quartz** crystallises **last**, most stable
- Discontinuous branch: **olivine** (first) → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite; mafic minerals form first at highest temperature
- Mohs hardness: **quartz 7**; apatite 5, orthoclase 6, topaz 8
### Time scale
- Geological time scale developed in **1760** by **Giovanni Arduino**; Standard scale set in **Italy** in **1881**; Indian scale advocated by **T.H. Holland**

## SEC 4:6:5 | Rock systems of India
### Order (older → younger)
- **Archaean → Dharwar → Cuddapah → Gondwana → Deccan Trap → Tertiary** (Tertiary youngest)
- **Cuddapah** and **Vindhyan**: **Purana** (Proterozoic) group
- Vindhyan series lower → upper: **Semri, Kaimur, Rewa, Bhander**
### Systems and resources
| System | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Dharwar** | ★ most mineralised; major source of **metallic minerals** — high-grade **iron ore, manganese, copper, lead, gold** |
| **Gondwana** | ★ main source of coal (over 90%; nearly **98%**); **Damodar, Mahanadi, Godavari** valleys |
| **Cuddapah** | mainly **clay** rocks (shales, slates) + quartzite, limestone; ~1,400–600 million yrs; barytes, limestone, asbestos, copper, nickel, cobalt — **no coal** |
| **Deccan Trap** | **end-Cretaceous basalt** from **fissure eruptions**; **black cotton (regur)** soil |
| **Tertiary** | **Neyveli lignite** (not Gondwana) |

- Gondwana coal vs Carboniferous coal: **younger, lower in carbon**; also occurs in Assam and Sikkim
- ★ Oil and petroleum in AP rock systems: **Pleistocene**
- **Sedimentary rocks**: principal repositories of **fossils**; hold coal, petroleum, natural gas
- **Dravidian (Palaeozoic)**: abundant **marine fossils**; Archaean: formed before life, fossil-free
- AP's Deccan Trap patches: **basalt** → **black cotton soil**

# ROW 4:7 | Earth movements - continental drift, sea-floor spreading, plate tectonics and plate boundaries
## SEC 4:7:0 | Theories in order
### Continental drift
- **Alfred Wegener** — continental drift, **1912**
- **Pole-fleeing force** linked to Earth's **rotation**; **tidal force** to Sun and Moon — both **too weak**, so theory not accepted
- **Tillites** (glacial deposits) in India and southern continents → **ancient glacial conditions**, landmasses once connected
### Convection and sea-floor spreading
- **Arthur Holmes** — **convection current** theory (**1930s**); driven by **radioactive decay** in the mantle
  - Rising limbs split crust at **ridges**; sinking limbs drag plates at **trenches**
- **Harry Hess** — **sea-floor spreading** (early **1960s**): new crust at mid-ocean ridges, destroyed at trenches
- Normal and reversed magnetic bands lie **symmetrically** on both sides of a ridge
- Direct observation of a rapid episode → spreading may occur in **episodic bursts**, not at a uniform rate
### Plate tectonics
- Put together in **1967** by **McKenzie and Parker**, and independently by **Morgan**
### Folds by rising pressure
- **Symmetrical → asymmetrical → (overfold) recumbent → nappe**

## SEC 4:7:1 | Pangaea & parts
- **Panthalassa**: mega-ocean around Pangaea
- **Tethys Sea**: between northern **Laurasia** and southern **Gondwanaland**
- **Gondwanaland**: South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica, Madagascar, Arabia
- **Mesosaurus** fossils: **South Africa** and **Brazil** (not India and Australia)
- **Indo-Gangetic Plain** bounded by the **Himalayan Frontal Fault** (north) and a **normal fault** (south)

## SEC 4:7:2 | Evidences
- Ancient rock belts (~**2,000 million years**) on the **Brazil** coast match those of **Western Africa**
- **Ghana**'s coastal **placer gold** has its source rock in the **Brazil plateau** → once joined
- Away from a mid-ocean ridge: **age, density, thickness** of oceanic crust and sediment cover **increase**

## SEC 4:7:3 | Plate boundaries
- **Convergent** boundaries: **earthquakes** from subduction-related **friction**; mountain building and subduction
- **Divergence** adds new crust; **sea-floor spreading creates** (does not destroy) crust
- **San Andreas Fault**: **transform** boundary (not divergent)
- **Ladakh Magmatic Arc**: **northward subduction** of the **Neo-Tethys** plate; between **Indus** and **Shyok** suture zones

## SEC 4:7:4 | Plates
- Plates (rigid lithosphere) float on the **semi-fluid asthenosphere**
- Seven major plates: **Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic**
- **Nazca** = minor plate, between **South American** and **Pacific** plates
- Plate movement monitored with **GPS** data

| Feature | Setting |
|---|---|
| **Sagaing Fault** (Myanmar) | transform boundary, **Indian–Eurasian** plates |
| **Red Sea**, **East African Rift** | divergence of **Nubian, Somalian, Arabian** plates near the **Afar** triple junction |
| **Himalayas**, Indo-Gangetic foreland basin | **India–Eurasia collision** (also India's earthquake risk) |

## SEC 4:7:5 | Indian plate's journey
- **Gondwanaland** broke up about **225 million years ago**
- **Deccan Traps** erupted as the Indian Plate crossed near the **equator** over the **Reunion hotspot**
- **India–Eurasia collision** began about **4–5 crore** (40–50 million) years ago; older estimates 50–55 million
- Indian Plate moves north at about **5 cm per year** → Himalayas still rising
- **Delamination** (southern Tibet): denser lower layer of the plate peels off and sinks into the mantle at about **100 km**

## SEC 4:7:6 | Faults & folds
- **Anticline**: up-arched fold; **syncline**: down-troughed fold (horst and graben are fault blocks)
- **Normal** fault: hanging wall moves **down**; **reverse**: moves **up**; **strike-slip**: horizontal (shear)
- **Thrust fault** = **low-angle reverse fault**; Himalayan **MCT, MBT, HFF** are thrust faults
- **Aravallis**: **old**, worn-down fold mountains, among India's oldest
- **Rockies**: **young** fold (North America); **Appalachians**: old fold
- **Satpura**: **block mountain**, flanked by rift valleys of **Narmada** and **Tapi**

## SEC 4:7:7 | Exam corner
- **Peninsular Plateau** (old Gondwana shield) part of the African landmass until the **Cretaceous**
- **Siwaliks**: **youngest** Himalayan range
- **Lake Malawi**: southern end of the **East African Rift**; **Lake Victoria** between the two branches, not a rift lake
- **Eastern Ghats**: old, highly **eroded**, **discontinuous** hills of the stable **Gondwana shield** (not young fold)

## SEC 4:7:8 | Theories → proponents, year and mechanism
- ★ **Plate tectonics**: crust pieces in constant slow motion driven by **mantle** movement; plates are rigid parts above the asthenosphere
- ★ Original single landmass: **Pangea** (supercontinent)
- ★ Continental drift: **Alfred Wegener** (German)
- ★ Plate tectonics was **not** given by H.H. Hess in 1967 alone — developed in the **1960s** by **Wilson, Morgan, Parker, McKenzie, Hess** and others
- ★ Plate tectonics is **based on sea-floor spreading**

## SEC 4:7:10 | Evidences — continental drift and sea-floor spreading
- ★ Evidence of continental drift (all three statements correct):
  - ★ ~**2,000 million-year** rock belt of the **Brazil** coast matches **West Africa**
  - ★ **Ghana** coast placer **gold**, no local source rock; origin in the **Brazil plateau** (gold-bearing veins in Brazil) when the continents were joined along the Atlantic
- ★ **Himalayas still rising** because the **Indian plate collides with the Eurasian plate** (R explains A)
  - ★ Himalayas: young fold mountains of the **Alpine** orogeny; rising about **5 mm per year** (some sources over 1 cm)

## SEC 4:7:11 | Plates → location and monitoring
- ★ **Cocos Plate**: between **Central America** and the **Pacific Plate**

## SEC 4:7:12 | Plate boundaries → motion and examples
- ★ **Seafloor spreading** continually **adds new crust**; **subduction** destroys old crust — the two roughly balance

## SEC 4:7:13 | India in Gondwanaland and the Indian plate — drift, collision, new findings
- ★ Indian subcontinent originally part of **Gondwana** (Gondwanaland), the southern supercontinent
- ★ **Pangea** (Carboniferous) surrounded by **Panthalassa** (named by Wegener); split into north and south parts
- ★ **Gondwanaland**: Antarctica, South America, Africa, Madagascar, Australia, Arabian Peninsula, Peninsular India — **not North America**

# ROW 4:8 | Folding, faulting, mountain building; isostasy
## SEC 4:8:0 | Endogenic chain
- ★ Evidence of the Himalayas as **young fold mountains**: **deep gorges**, **U-turn river courses**, **parallel ranges**, **steep gradients** causing landslides (all four)
- ★ Youngest mountain chain: **Shiwaliks** — outermost (**outer Himalayas**), last stage of formation
- ★ Active volcanoes: **Cotopaxi** (Ecuador), **Etna** (Italy), **Fujiyama** (Japan); **Aconcagua** (Argentina) not active
- Himalaya: **Tethys** sediments uplifted by the northward **Indian Plate** (**Eocene to Pliocene**); uplift began ~**70 million years** ago, continues today
- **Endogenic processes** produce **mantle dynamics**; mantle dynamics produce **plate movement** (both true, R not the explanation)

## SEC 4:8:1 | Diastrophism
- **Orogenic**: mountain building by **horizontal (tangential)** forces in narrow belts
- **Epeirogenic**: **vertical** uplift or subsidence of large areas — e.g. **Peninsular Block** (transgression, regression)
- **Barchan**: crescent-shaped dune; **seif**: long narrow dune parallel to wind

## SEC 4:8:2 | Mountain types
| Type | Formed by | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Young fold** | recent **compression** (folding); high, rugged, sharp | ★ Himalaya, **Alps**, Andes, Rockies — world's **highest peaks** |
| **Old fold** | ancient folding + long denudation; **lower, rounded, eroded** | Aravallis (Precambrian), Appalachians, **Urals** (Palaeozoic) |
| **Block** | **faulting** | **Vosges**, **Black Forest**, **Sierra Nevada** (not young fold) |
| **Volcanic** | erupted lava | ★ **Mt. Etna** (Sicily), Kilimanjaro, Fujiyama, Mount St. Helens, Cotopaxi |
| **Dome** | magma bulges crust **without erupting** | **Black Hills**, **Bighorns** |
| **Residual** | erosion | Aravalli, Eastern Ghats |

- ★ Everest and Mont Blanc are **fold** mountains, not volcanic
- **V-shaped valley**: river (vertical) erosion; U-shaped, hanging = glacial; rift = tectonic

## SEC 4:8:3 | Mountain-building theories
- ★ **Appalachian**: old range (**Caledonian** period) — odd one out vs Tertiary/Alpine Alps, Andes, Himalayas, Rockies, Atlas
- **Isostasy**: balance of crustal blocks **floating** on denser material below
- **Airy**: **uniform density**, varying thickness, deep **roots**
- **Pratt**: varying density, common compensation depth; **higher blocks are lighter**
- **Kober**: **geosynclinal** theory — long shallow troughs between rigid forelands (**kratogens**)
  - Stages: **lithogenesis** (sedimentation) → **orogenesis** (folding) → **gliptogenesis** (uplift, denudation)

## SEC 4:8:4 | Folds by rising pressure
- **Nappe**: recumbent fold **broken and thrust far forward**
- **Cuddapah** sediments laid down in **synclinal troughs**
- **Siwaliks**: simple **Jura-type** anticline–syncline folds, **not** block mountains

## SEC 4:8:5 | Horsts, grabens, rifts
- **Horst**: uplifted fault block; **graben**: down-faulted block
- **Rift valley**: central block **sinks between faults** as crust pulls apart
- ★ **Narmada valley** = **rift valley**; Narmada forms **no delta** (deposits no silt)
- Narmada flows west between the **Vindhyas** (north) and **Satpuras** (south)
- **Vosges** (France) and **Black Forest** (Germany): **horsts** flanking the **Rhine graben**
- Grabens: **Great Rift Valley**, **Death Valley**, **Rhine Rift Valley**; **Sierra Nevada** is a horst

## SEC 4:8:6 | Aravalli
- **Diastrophism**: all crustal movements that move, raise or build up the crust (incl. **earthquakes**, **plate movements**)
- **Aravalli**: **Precambrian** fold mountain (Appalachians Palaeozoic; Himalaya, Alps Tertiary)
- Highest peak: **Guru Shikhar**, **1,722 m**, Mount Abu
- Aravalli **hill** threshold: **100 m** above local relief (not 500 m)
- **Supreme Court paused** new Aravalli mining leases (**November 2025**)
- Collapse of hanging glaciers in seismic Himalayan zones → landslide-lake outburst floods and **GLOFs**
- **Tombolo**: joins an island to the mainland; **spit**: built by longshore drift

## SEC 4:8:7 | Exam corner
- Diastrophism includes **orogeny, epeirogeny, earthquakes, plate tectonics** — **heterogenic movement** not included
- AP's **Eastern Ghats**: old, heavily eroded residual hills of the **Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt**
- **Andes** (~**7,000 km**): **longest** mountain range; Himalayas: **highest**

## SEC 4:8:8 | Diastrophism — endogenic processes, definition and types
- ★ **Orogeny** = **mountain building**, not continent building (endogenic: volcanism, diastrophism; exogenic: weathering, erosion)
- ★ Key (M.P.P.C.S. 2023): **plate tectonics** given as not under diastrophism; endogenic forces from **thermal asymmetry** inside the Earth
- ★ **Epeirogenic** forces result from **vertical** earth movements

## SEC 4:8:11 | Mountain types → formation and examples
- ★ **Mount St. Helens**: active stratovolcano, Washington, **USA**
- ★ Highest volcanic mountain among options: **Cotopaxi** (**5911 m**); Kilimanjaro 5881 m, Pinatubo 1486 m, Taal 311 m (Philippines)
- ★ Most recent range: **Himalayas** (Great Himalayas = **Himadri**); Aravalli most ancient in India
- ★ Folding results from **orogenic** force (plate interaction)
- ★ **Appalachians**: not Tertiary orogeny (Caledonian)
- ★ **Volcanic cone**: a **basic landform** (ejecta, lava, ash around a vent; central crater); residual mountains are eroded forms
- ★ **Sierra Nevada** (California): **block** mountain, not young fold
- ★ **Andes**: young fold, not old (old: **Ural, Aravalli, Appalachian**)

## SEC 4:8:12 | Fold mountains by age, key facts and the Aravalli
- ★ **Aravalli**: oldest mountain range of India; **Proterozoic**, formed about **570 million years** ago; highly eroded, broken hills
- ★ Aravalli Ranges located in **Rajasthan**
- ★ **Guru Shikhar** (**1722 m**): highest Aravalli peak, near Mount Abu, **Sirohi** district, **Rajasthan**
- ★ **Kumbhalgarh** (**1224 m**): highest among options when Guru Shikhar is absent
- ★ **Old mountains**: **Pennines** (Europe), **Appalachians**, **Aravallis**
- ★ **Residual mountain**: **Aravalli** (remnant worn down by denudation)
- ★ World's highest peaks (incl. Everest): **young folded** mountains

## SEC 4:8:13 | Block mountains and rift valleys — horst, graben → location
- ★ Correct pairs (only two): **Appalachians** — North America — fold; **Andes** — South America — fold
  - ★ **Vosges** is a **block** mountain (Europe), not Central Asia fold; **Alps** are young **fold**, not block
- ★ **Death Valley** (California, USA): **rift valley**
- ★ **Narmada** forms **no delta** (A false) because it flows through a **rift valley** (R true)
- ★ **Black Forest**: block mountain in **Germany** (highest peak **Feldberg**, 1493 m); **Vosges** in **France**
- ★ **Damodar**: east-flowing river with a rift valley due to **down warping**
- **Stalagmite** rises from cave floor; **stalactite** hangs from roof; lapiaz and dolines are surface karst
- **Estuary**: drowned river mouth (tides mix with fresh water); delta builds by deposition

# ROW 4:9 | Volcanoes - types, materials, landforms, global distribution
## SEC 4:9:0 | Terms
- **Magma**: molten rock below the surface; **lava**: once it reaches the surface
- **Caldera**: cauldron-shaped depression from **summit collapse** after the magma chamber empties
- **Crater**: funnel-shaped mouth at the top of a cone
- Most abundant volcanic gas: **water vapour**, then CO2 and SO2
- A volcano releases lava, ash and gases; magma produces volcanism

## SEC 4:9:1 | Lava types
- **Acidic (felsic)** lava: **silica-rich**, **viscous**, **explosive** eruptions; steep cones, stratovolcanoes
- **Pyroclastic flows** and **lahars** (volcanic mudflows): **stratovolcanoes**
- **Basaltic** lava plateaus (e.g. **Deccan**) weather into **fertile black soil**

## SEC 4:9:2 | Volcano forms
- **Hayli Gubbi** (Ethiopia): **shield volcano**; erupted **November 2025** after ~**10,000–12,000 years** dormant
- **Composite (strato)** volcanoes: steep, alternating **lava and ash** layers, explosive

## SEC 4:9:3 | World belts

# ROW 4:10 | Earthquakes - causes, seismic waves, measurement, distribution, effects
## SEC 4:10:0 | Basics
- **Focus**: point of **origin** underground
- **Epicentre**: surface point **directly above the focus**; shaking generally strongest
- **Seismic doublet**: **two independent** quakes of **similar magnitude**, close in time and space (unlike tapering aftershocks)
- **John Milne**: **Father of Modern Seismology** (early horizontal seismograph)
- **Richter**: magnitude scale; **Mercalli** scale: intensity; **seismograph** records shaking

## SEC 4:10:1 | Causes
- **Tectonic earthquake**: fault rupture when stress exceeds rock strength; divergent and convergent boundaries
- **San Andreas Fault**: transform boundary; **Ring of Fire**: volcanic and convergent-margin activity (not transform)
- **Latur 1993**: quakes can occur in **stable regions** along **hidden faults**
- **Koyna 1967**: India's classic **reservoir-induced** earthquake
- **2025 Myanmar (Mandalay)** quake (**M 7.7**): strike-slip **Sagaing Fault**; **Turkey–Syria 2023**: **East Anatolian Fault**
- Earthquake-prone: **Kutch**, **North-East**, **Andaman and Nicobar**; **Visakhapatnam**: low-risk **stable shield**
- Volcanic gases: mostly **water vapour**, then CO2, SO2, H2S
- **Extrusive** landforms: **lava plateau**, cinder and composite cones, craters, calderas, lava flows; **intrusive**: batholiths, lopoliths, phacoliths

## SEC 4:10:2 | Seismic waves
- **Love** and **Rayleigh** waves = **surface waves**
- **Seismic seiches**: **standing waves** in rivers, reservoirs, ponds (reported in the Myanmar quake)

## SEC 4:10:3 | Magnitude vs intensity
- **Intensity**: qualitative, **observed effects** at a place; varies with distance
- **Magnitude**: **energy released** at the focus; one value per quake
- **Richter scale**: **Charles F. Richter**, **1935**; measures magnitude
- +1 unit: **10×** wave amplitude, about **31×** energy
