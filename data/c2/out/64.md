## SEC 3:37:1 | Anti-poverty programmes
### Direct-attack programmes
- **IRDP, NREP, RLEGP and JRY** — direct-attack / target-group poverty programmes
- ★ **MRTP** is not an anti-poverty programme — MRTP Act (**1969**) controls monopolies and restrictive trade practices
- **JRY (1989)** — formed by merging **NREP** with **RLEGP** (rural landless employment)
- **SGSY** replaced IRDP's individual-beneficiary approach with an **SHG (group) approach**
### Antyodaya and self-employment
- ★ **Antyodaya** — first started in **Rajasthan** (**1977**, 1977-78), by the Rajasthan State Government
  - means development (Udaya) of the people at the bottom: the poorest of the poor
- **RUDSETI** — set up at **Ujire, Karnataka** in **1982**; trains rural youth for self-employment; model for present **RSETIs**
### Women's programmes
- **Swalamban** — training and employment of **poor women**; former **NORAD** scheme
- ★ **Mahila Samridhi Yojana** — **2 October 1993**; micro-finance to uplift rural women
- ★ **Mahila Swa-shakti Project** = Women Self-Empowerment Plan ("Women Empire Plan – 1989" is a wrong match)

## SEC 3:37:2 | Restructuring chain
- **SGSY** — launched **1 April 1999** by merging six programmes: **IRDP, TRYSEM, DWCRA, SITRA, Ganga Kalyan Yojana, Million Wells Scheme**
- **Ganga Kalyan Yojana** — **bore-well and tube-well irrigation** for small and marginal farmers (not urban employment guarantee)
- Sequence: **SGSY → NRLM 'Aajeevika' → DAY-NRLM**
  - NRLM (Aajeevika) restructured **2010**, launched **2011**; renamed DAY-NRLM in **2016**

## SEC 3:37:3 | DAY-NRLM structure
- DAY-NRLM three tiers: **SHG → Village Organisation → Cluster-Level Federation**

## SEC 3:37:4 | DAY-NRLM basics
### Earlier schemes
- **IRDP** — subsidy plus bank credit to lift rural families above the poverty line; all blocks from **2 October 1980**
- **Swayamsiddha (2001)** — SHG-based, integrated empowerment of women
  - **Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (1993)** — micro-credit; **STEP** — skills and employment
### DAY-NRLM features
- Centre-State funding: **60:40** general; **90:10** North-Eastern States incl. Sikkim; **100%** for UTs
- Up to **30%** of SHG members may be slightly above the poverty line
  - beneficiaries identified by **PIP**, vetted by the **Gram Sabha**
- **Lakhpati Didi** — SHG member earning **₹1 lakh** or more a year
- **SVEP** (Start-up Village Entrepreneurship Programme, **2016**) — **non-farm enterprises** for SHG households
### Andhra Pradesh links
- **Indira Kranthi Patham (2004-05)** — merger of **Velugu** (2000, World Bank-aided) and **DWCRA**; not Velugu's earlier name
- **Unnati** (SERP) — the **poorest SC/ST SHG households**
- **Stree Nidhi** — credit to SHG members in rural and urban areas
- AP's **SERP/Velugu** model — template for **NRLM**; AP leads in SHG-bank linkage

## SEC 3:37:5 | DAY-NULM (urban)
- **NULM (2013)** replaced the urban **SJSRY (1997)**; later renamed **DAY-NULM**
  - SGSY (rural) → NRLM; IRDP → SGSY (1999); PMRY → **PMEGP** (2008)
- Ministry: originally **Housing and Urban Poverty Alleviation**, now Housing and Urban Affairs
- **PAISA** — Portal for Affordable Credit and Interest Subvention Access
- AP implementing agency: **MEPMA** — Mission for Elimination of Poverty in Municipal Areas

## SEC 3:37:6 | SHG pioneers
- **SHG-Bank Linkage Programme** — pilot in **1992** by **NABARD**; world's largest microfinance programme
- NABARD role: **refinances banks**, trains and promotes SHGs; does **not** replace commercial banks as direct lenders
- **MYRADA** — pioneered bank linkage of SHGs in the **mid-1980s** (not the national programme)
- **RBI** — regulator; **SIDBI** — lends to small industries

## SEC 3:37:7 | SHG movement in AP
- **SERP** — Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty; registered society of the AP Government, **2000**
- **SAPAP (1995)** — **UNDP**-supported, in **Mahabubnagar, Kurnool, Anantapur**; World Bank aided **Velugu (2000)**
- **Indira Kranthi Patham** — **2004-05** merger of **Velugu and DWCRA**; implemented by SERP
- **Pavala Vaddi** — interest subsidy for SHGs, **2004**
- **Vaddi Leni Runalu** — rewards **prompt** repayment
- SHG challenges: uneven quality, weak market links, debt stress from poor financial literacy, **political interference**

# ROW 3:38 | Social security and DBT - PM-JDY, JAM trinity, Aadhaar-enabled transfers, pension schemes
## SEC 3:38:0 | PM Jan Dhan Yojana
- ★ **PMJDY** — launched **28 August 2014**; implemented by the **Department of Financial Services**, Ministry of Finance

- **PMSBY** — **accidental death and disability**; death from any cause — **PMJJBY**
- **NSAP (1995)** — under the **Ministry of Rural Development**

# ROW 3:39 | Food security - NFSA 2013, PDS reform, buffer stocks, One Nation One Ration Card, PMGKAY
## SEC 3:39:0 | Concept
- ★ **FAO** headquarters — **Rome**; UN agency fighting hunger, improving nutrition and food security
- **World Food Summit, 1996** — food security: all people, at all times, physical and economic access to **sufficient, safe and nutritious food** for an active and healthy life
- Dimensions: **availability, accessibility, affordability/access, utilisation/absorption, stability** — **accountability** is not one
- **Amartya Sen** (entitlement approach) — famine from a collapse of people's **command over food** (income, exchange, transfers), not just lower food availability
- **National** food security — aggregate production and stocks; **household** food security — each household's actual access and affordability

## SEC 3:39:1 | Buffer stock mechanism
- **Strategic reserve** — **5 million tonnes** above operational stocks
- Minimum buffer stock levels for each quarter fixed by the **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs**

## SEC 3:39:2 | NFSA 2013 entitlements
### Entitlements
| Group | Entitlement |
|---|---|
| Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) household | **35 kg** per household per month |
| Priority Household (PHH) | **5 kg** per person per month at **₹3** rice / **₹2** wheat / **₹1** coarse grain |
| Pregnant women and lactating mothers | free meal + maternity benefit of at least **₹6,000** |
| Children 6 months – 6 years | take-home ration or hot meal through **ICDS/anganwadi** |
### Food support channels
- **Food-for-Work** — grain as **wages**
- **PDS** — grain via Fair Price Shops; **ICDS** — supplementary nutrition at anganwadis; **PM POSHAN** — hot cooked meals
### Grievance and accountability
- **Section 15** — District Grievance Redressal Officer (DGRO)
- **State Food Commission** in each State (an existing body may be designated); monitors implementation, hears appeals
- Accountability: **social audits, mandatory disclosure of PDS records, vigilance committees**
### PMGKAY and hunger index
- **PMGKAY** — launched **2020** under the COVID-19/Atmanirbhar package
  - **5 kg** free grain **per person**, **on top of** the NFSA entitlement
- **Global Hunger Index** objections: **three of four** indicators concern children only; undernourishment from a small **opinion-poll-type sample** (it does not use only income indicators)
### Andhra Pradesh PDS
- AAY: **35 kg** free rice per card; PHH: **5 kg** per unit
- **Ragi** (all areas) and **jowar** (Rayalaseema) — up to **3 kg** per card free in lieu of rice

## SEC 3:39:3 | NFSA basics
- NFSA commenced **July 2013** (not 2014)
- Coverage: up to **75%** rural and **50%** urban = about **two-thirds** (≈ **67%**) of the population
- Ration card head: **eldest woman** aged **18** or above
- **Draft NFS (Amendment) Bill, 2026** — AAY entitlement to **7 kg per person**, overall ceiling **35 kg** per household
  - aim: remove the inequity of **smaller households** getting more grain per head

## SEC 3:39:4 | PDS to PMGKAY
- **PDS** — began as a **World War II rationing** measure (background: Bengal famine)
- **Buffer stocks** — formally introduced in the **Fourth Five-Year Plan** (1969-74)
- From **1 January 2023** — PMGKAY subsumed in NFSA; **entire NFSA allocation free** ("One Nation–One Price–One Ration")
- Free grain extended **1 January 2024 – 31 December 2028** (five years)

## SEC 3:39:5 | Laws & bodies
- **RPDS** June 1992 → **TPDS** June **1997** → NFSA 2013
- **FCI** — created under the **Food Corporations Act, 1964**; Essential Commodities Act (1955) regulates essential goods
- **NCCF** — set up **1965**, under the **Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution**
- **Bharat-brand** atta, rice, dal — sold via **NAFED, NCCF, Kendriya Bhandar** outlets and **mobile vans**

## SEC 3:39:6 | Andhra Pradesh
- **Mee Mart** (AP) — to be linked with the **Digital Rupee (CBDC)** for food-subsidy delivery

## SEC 3:39:7 | Exam corner
- Food security needed even in **surplus** years — starvation can come from **unequal distribution**
- **OMSS** — FCI sells surplus central-pool grain at predetermined prices: raises supply, moderates prices, offloads stocks, cuts carrying costs
  - does **not** replace FCI procurement with private procurement
- NFSA objective: **food and nutritional security** — adequate quality food at affordable prices, a life with **dignity**
- Criticism: **issue prices frozen since 2013** while subsidy costs rise

## SEC 3:39:8 | Food security — definitions, dimensions and FIRSTs
- ★ Dimensions: **Availability, Access, Affordability, Utilization, Stability** — **Accountability** is not a dimension
- ★ "National food security but not household security" — **sufficient food stock**, but not all households can obtain it

## SEC 3:39:9 | Economists and indices → views on hunger
- ★ In India **both rich and poor** are malnourished — rich eat a **wrong diet**, poor eat a **poor diet**
- ★ Food security necessary even in **surplus** years — starvation from **unequal distribution** and lack of access

## SEC 3:39:10 | Buffer stocks and OMSS — mechanism, norms and stock data

# ROW 3:41 | Rural credit and rural non-farm sector; migration and rural distress
## SEC 3:41:0 | Rural credit milestones
- **1951** — moneylenders supplied about **70%** of rural credit
- **All India Rural Credit Survey Committee** (RBI, **1951**) — headed by **A.D. Gorwala**; led to **SBI (1955)**
  - verdict: "Rural credit has **failed**, but **cooperation must succeed**"

## SEC 3:41:1 | Sources of rural credit
- Non-institutional: **traders**, relatives; institutional: **commercial banks, PACS, RRBs**

## SEC 3:41:2 | Kisan Credit Card
- **KCC** — open to **tenants, sharecroppers, SHGs, JLGs** (not only landowners)
- **Small Finance Banks** lend to small businesses, small and marginal farmers, rural entrepreneurs — **regulators** (FSDC members) are not borrowers

## SEC 3:41:3 | Key terms
- ★ **Claudia Goldin** — **2023** Nobel (Sveriges Riksbank Prize) in Economic Sciences; prize set up **1968**, awarded since **1969**
  - **U-shaped curve** of women's work: high in agriculture in the **1980s**, fall **2004-05 to 2012** (mechanisation), rise after **2020** (partly distress)
- Informal loans by repayment: **temporary** (repaid after harvest), **chronic**, **debt-trap** (new loan to repay old)
- **Distress migration** — forced by **debt, landlessness, crop loss**; **seasonal** migration — for a season, then return
- **Feminisation of agriculture** — rising share of **women** among cultivators, labourers, unpaid family workers as men move to non-farm/urban work

## SEC 3:41:4 | Rural non-farm sector
- DAY-NRLM microfinance route: **SHGs, JLGs, BC Sakhis**
- **SVEP** (DAY-NRLM) — funds rural **non-farm enterprises**
- **PMEGP** — implemented by **KVIC** (statutory, KVI Act 1956, Ministry of MSME)
  - NABARD — apex rural development bank; SIDBI — finances MSMEs

## SEC 3:41:5 | Migration: push vs pull
- **Pull**: **better wages** in cities; **push**: crop failure, poor rural healthcare, rural unemployment
- Parliamentary panel on **DDU-GKY**: **low wages and drop-outs**

## SEC 3:41:6 | Distress data (ES 2025-26)
- **KVIC** — statutory body under the **KVI Act, 1956** (amended 1987, 2006); Ministry of MSME
- **NREGA** — passed 2005, in force **2006**; renamed **MGNREGA** in **2009**
  - **100 days** per household; wages within **15 days** (not 30); **one-third** participation for women
- **26.8%** of rural Indians were migrants in **2020-21** (ES 2025-26)
- Rural women — **80%** of the rise in female workers, **2017-18 to 2023-24**
- Feminisation of agriculture ≠ **land ownership** by women; it is about participation in farm work

## SEC 3:41:7 | Exam corner
- AP farm indebtedness: **NABARD** study — about **50%** of AP farmers depend on non-institutional credit (not 25%)
- AP distress-migration belts: **Rayalaseema** and north Andhra (**Srikakulam**)
- AP **Gulf migration**: mainly **YSR Kadapa** and **Godavari/Konaseema** districts

## SEC 3:41:12 | Data — rural distress indicators and the ES 2025-26 debate
- ★ Rural-urban migration — combination of **push** (rural poverty, few jobs, poor services) and **pull** (urban jobs) factors
- ★ **MGNREGA** positive results: higher **purchasing power** of rural poor, **less distress migration** (not lower bargaining power of labour)

# ROW 3:42 | Urban development - urbanisation trends, challenges (housing, slums, transport, waste, finance)
## SEC 3:42:0 | Statutory vs census town
- **Statutory town** — notified by the State, with a **municipal corporation, municipality, cantonment board or notified town area**; run by an urban local body
- **Census town** — population above **5,000**; at least **75%** of male workers non-agricultural; density at least **400** per sq km

## SEC 3:42:1 | Census 2011 facts
### Urbanisation level
- India's urbanisation: **27.81%** (2001) → **31.16%** (2011)
- India — **third** in absolute urban population, after **China** and the **USA**
- ★ Most urbanised State: **Goa** — **62.2%** (2011); 49.8% (2001)
  - then Tamil Nadu 48.4%, Maharashtra 45.2%, Gujarat 42.6%
- ★ Least urbanised State: **Himachal Pradesh** — **10%**; Odisha 16.7%, Jharkhand 24.0%, Rajasthan 24.9%
- Least urbanised southern State: **Andhra Pradesh** (undivided, about **33%**)
- Highest decadal urban growth 2001-2011: **Sikkim**
### Urban population size
- ★ Highest urban population: **Maharashtra** (50.8 million); lowest: **Sikkim** (**1,53,578**)
  - after Sikkim: Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Mizoram
- ★ Top three States in urban population (2001 and 2011): **Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Tamil Nadu**
- ★ **Uttar Pradesh** — largest number of towns
### Million cities and megacities
- ★ Million-plus cities: **35** (2001) → **53** (2011)

### Town classes
- **Six** population-size classes (**Class I to VI**)
- ★ Small towns: **Class IV, V, VI** (under **20,000**)
- ★ Highest population growth — **Class I** towns (Census 1991); **Class III** (Census 2001)
### Urban challenges and projections
- Urban population projected about **60 crore** by **2031**; cities: about **3%** of land, about **60%** of GDP

- Governance challenge: **fragmented authority**, limited **fiscal autonomy** of cities
- **16th Finance Commission** — only **half** of basic grants to ULBs tied to sanitation, waste, water
