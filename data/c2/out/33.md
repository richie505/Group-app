# ROW 1:249 | Direct Action Day, Mountbatten Plan, Indian Independence Act 1947; partition and its consequences
## SEC 1:249:0 | Road to freedom
### Sequence, 1946-47
| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| **Direct Action** call by Muslim League | **29 July 1946** |
| First **Interim Government** formed | **2 September 1946** |
| **Attlee Statement** | **20 February 1947** |
| **Mountbatten Plan** | **3 June 1947** |
| Independence | **15 August 1947** |
- League called **Direct Action** after it **withdrew its acceptance of the Cabinet Mission Plan**
- Attlee Statement: British rule to end by **30 June 1948**, even without agreement among Indian parties
- Order: Attlee Statement → **Mountbatten replaces Wavell** as Viceroy → Mountbatten Plan → Independence
- ★ Mountbatten Plan: **Punjab and Bengal** Legislative Assemblies to meet in two groups (Hindus, Muslims) to vote on partition; referendum provision covered **all** the provinces asked
- **Indian Independence Act, 1947** — gave **legal force to the Mountbatten Plan** (introduced 4 July, royal assent 18 July, effective 15 August 1947)
  - **GoI Act 1935**, as adapted, continued as interim law
### Gandhi, 1947-48
- After Noakhali, Gandhi went to **Bihar** (March 1947) to calm anti-Muslim riots
- 15 August 1947: Gandhi fasting and praying at **Beliaghata, Calcutta**
- Assassinated by **Nathuram Godse** in **New Delhi**, **30 January 1948**
## SEC 1:249:1 | Mountbatten (3 June) Plan
- Transfer of power advanced from June 1948 to **15 August 1947**
- **Paramountcy lapsed**; the **565** princely states could **accede to India or Pakistan**
- **No third option** of remaining independent was given to the princely states
## SEC 1:249:2 | Alternative schemes
- **Plan Balkan** (Mountbatten, May 1947) — rejected by **Nehru**: could lead to Balkanisation of India
- **United Bengal plan** (1947) — proposed by **Sarat Chandra Bose** and **H.S. Suhrawardy**; a **sovereign, undivided Bengal**
  - **Both Congress and League rejected** it
- **V.P. Menon** — suggested immediate transfer of power on **dominion status**, without waiting for a new constitution
- Congress accepted partition to **avoid Balkanisation**, secure a **strong Centre** and **end communal bloodshed**
## SEC 1:249:3 | Independence Act stages
- Bill introduced in House of Commons — **4 July 1947**
- **Royal Assent** — **18 July 1947**
- Came into effect — **15 August 1947**
## SEC 1:249:4 | Act: provisions
- Interim governance under the **Government of India Act, 1935, as adapted**, until new constitutions
- Constituent Assemblies given **sovereign** power — could repeal any British law, even the Independence Act
- Offices ended: **Viceroy** and **Secretary of State for India**; paramountcy lapsed; **boundary commissions** provided
- **Dominion status** — India and Pakistan **free before framing their constitutions**
## SEC 1:249:5 | Heads at independence
- ★ **J.B. Kripalani** — a **teacher** by profession (Rajendra Prasad basically a lawyer)
- **J.B. Kripalani** — Congress President on 15 August 1947
- **C. Rajagopalachari** (June 1948 - Jan 1950) — last Governor-General and only Indian GG
  - Mountbatten: last Viceroy, first GG of free India; **Jinnah** first GG of Pakistan
- **Clement Attlee** — **Labour Party**; British PM 1945-1951
## SEC 1:249:6 | Partition consequences
- ★ **Mountbatten** called Gandhi the **'one-man boundary force'** (50,000 soldiers in Punjab vs one man in Bengal)
- **NWFP referendum** (1947) — boycotted by the **Khudai Khidmatgar** movement; NWFP joined Pakistan
- **Radcliffe award** announced **17 August 1947**, after independence
- Pakistan's share of assets and liabilities — **17.5 per cent** (Partition Council)
- **10-15 million** people crossed the new borders
- **Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation** — refugee camps, resettlement, evacuee property
- **M.A. Jinnah** — first Governor-General of Pakistan (August 1947)

# ROW 1:251 | Ambedkar's contribution to the making of the Constitution - Drafting Committee and key interventions
## SEC 1:251:0 | Making the draft
- Before Partition, Ambedkar elected from **Bengal (Jessore-Khulna)** with **Jogendra Nath Mandal**'s support
- **Drafting Committee** set up **29 August 1947** — **seven** members, chairman **Ambedkar**; **Nehru not a member**
- **Draft Constitution** published **21 February 1948** — **315 Articles**
- Final speech in the Constituent Assembly — **25 November 1949**
  - Three warnings: against **violent/extra-constitutional methods**, **hero-worship**, failure to build **social democracy**
  - Did **not** call for abolishing the parliamentary system
- Criticised as a **'copy of the Government of India Act, 1935'** and a **'lawyers' paradise'**
## SEC 1:251:1 | Drafting Committee: 7 members
- **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar** — co-author of the **Munshi-Ayyangar** language formula
- **Saadullah** — from **Assam**
- **Madhava Rao** replaced **B.L. Mitter**; **T.T. Krishnamachari** replaced **D.P. Khaitan**
- **K.M. Munshi** — originally the **only Congress member**; also chaired the **Order of Business Committee**

# ROW 1:252 | Ambedkar's social thought - annihilation of caste, state socialism, conversion to Buddhism
## SEC 1:252:0 | Annihilation of Caste (1936)
- Caste had **religious sanction** in the **Shastras and Vedas** — so the **whole caste system** had to be destroyed
- Reform limited to untouchability — **inadequate**
- Gandhi and Ambedkar differed on **whether the whole caste system should be annihilated** (both stressed uplift and education)
## SEC 1:252:1 | Gandhi vs Ambedkar
- **B.R. Ambedkar** — caste is "not just a division of labour, but a **division of labourers**"
- **'Harijan'** — Gandhi's term; Ambedkar rejected it for **Dalit / Scheduled Castes**
- Poona Pact later felt by Ambedkar to make Dalits **dependent on caste Hindus**
- Ambedkar rejected **Gram Swaraj** and **trusteeship** — favoured a **strong, centralised constitutional democracy**
- Criticised Congress for refusing to treat **caste as a political issue**
- 1932: **Gandhi** opposed separate electorates and fasted; **Ambedkar** demanded them but settled for **reserved seats** (Poona Pact)
## SEC 1:252:2 | Books & themes
| Work | Year | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| **Who Were the Shudras?** | **1946** | Shudras originally **Kshatriyas**, degraded after conflict with Brahmins (denied upanayana) |
| **States and Minorities** | **1947** | memorandum for the **Scheduled Castes Federation**; **State socialism** |
| **The Untouchables** | **1948** | origin of untouchability; **"broken men"** theory |
| **The Buddha and His Dhamma** | **1957** (posthumous) | |
- State socialism: State ownership of **key industries** and **agricultural land**, nationalised **insurance**
  - To be written into the Constitution so a **legislative majority could not undo it**
- Suggested **Hyderabad** as a **second capital**; proposed dividing Maharashtra, warned against huge states
- **Navayana** (Neo-Buddhism) — his reinterpretation of Buddhism; gave rise to the **Dalit Buddhist Movement**
## SEC 1:252:3 | State socialism & reforms
- Village a **'sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism'** — **4 November 1948**; individual, not village, the basic unit
- Resigned in **1951** when the **Hindu Code Bill** (inheritance, marriage, divorce) stalled
- **Independent Labour Party** — **1936**; Labour Member **1942-46**
## SEC 1:252:4 | Road to Buddhism
- **B.S. Moonje** — supported **Sikhism** as an alternative to Hinduism; Gandhi, Rajagopalachari, Malaviya opposed
- About **five lakh** followers, mostly **Mahars**, converted with him
- **Supreme Court, 24 March 2026** — conversion out of **Hinduism, Sikhism or Buddhism** means immediate loss of SC status (**Clause 3**, Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, **1950**)
## SEC 1:252:5 | Exam corner
- **B.R. Ambedkar** — "Nothing can emancipate the outcaste except the **destruction of the caste system**" (APPSC-G1 2018)
- **Political power** necessary for the Depressed Classes to protect their interests
- Accepted **"one state, one language"**, not "one language, one state"
- **Yeola**, **13 October 1935** (Depressed Classes Conference) — born a Hindu, would not die a Hindu
## SEC 1:252:6 | Ambedkar → statements on caste, village and religion
- ★ **B.R. Ambedkar** — "Nothing can emancipate the outcaste except the **destruction of the caste system**"
- ★ **Annihilation of caste** essential for emancipation of the marginalised
## SEC 1:252:8 | Ambedkar vs Gandhi and Congress — caste, electorates, village
- ★ **Neo-Buddhism** advocated by **Ambedkar** — the **Dalit Bauddha Movement**; new sect **'Navayana'** ("new vehicle")
- ★ Navayana rejects monasticism, **Karma**, rebirth, samsara, meditation, enlightenment and the **four noble truths**

## SEC 1:254:2 | Five Year Plans
### Planning

## SEC 1:255:0 | Causes of the movement
### Grievances
- Telugus ~**40 per cent** (2 crore) of the Madras Presidency (c. 1910)
- Jobs: only **8** high State jobs vs **56** for non-Telugus; **21** Deputy Collectors, **30** District Munsifs, **no** District Judge
- 1915: **no engineering, medical or law college** in Andhra (though 8 colleges, 163 secondary schools)
- Rayalaseema grievance: surplus revenue of the Andhra deltas spent on **Tamil areas**
- **Krishna and Guntur** — most enthusiastic supporters of a separate province
- Early demands also: a **university**, agricultural and medical colleges, a **port at Visakhapatnam**
### Bihar and Orissa precedent
- ★ Bihar separated from Bengal — announced at the **Delhi Durbar** (12 Dec 1911); **Bihar and Orissa** province came into existence **1 April 1912**
- Showed provinces **could be redrawn on linguistic lines**
### Press
