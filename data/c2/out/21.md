# ROW 1:166 | Anglo-Mysore Wars I-IV - Haidar Ali, Tipu Sultan, Treaty of Mangalore and Srirangapatnam
## SEC 1:166:0 | Four wars at a glance
| War | Mysore side / allies | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| First | **Haidar Ali** | Haidar on top near Madras; ended by **Treaty of Madras (1769)** |
| Second (1780-84) | Haidar allied with **the Nizam and the Marathas** | British disaster at **Pollilur (1780)** reversed by Coote's win at **Porto Novo (1781)**; ended by **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)** with **Tipu Sultan** |
| Third | Tipu vs British + **Nizam + Marathas** (grand alliance) | Both Nizam and Marathas with the British |
| Fourth | Tipu | Tipu killed defending Seringapatam on **4 May 1799** |
- Treaty of **Madras (1769)** — with **Haidar Ali**; Treaty of **Mangalore (1784)** — with **Tipu Sultan**

## SEC 1:166:1 | Key battles
| Battle | Year | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Pollilur** | 1780 | Haidar and Tipu defeat **Baillie** |
| **Porto Novo** | July 1781 | **Sir Eyre Coote** defeats Haidar Ali (Second War) |
| Tipu vs **General Meadows** | 1790 | Tipu defeats the British army under Meadows (Third War) |
| **Seringapatam** | 1792 | **Cornwallis's** coalition wins; Tipu sues for peace |
| Seringapatam | 1799 | British win; Tipu killed |

## SEC 1:166:2 | Haidar & Tipu
### Haidar Ali
- Rose under the Wodeyars; **Faujdar of Dindigul (1755)** under minister **Nanjaraja**
- Adopted Western military techniques; **modern arsenal at Dindigul** with **French** help
### Tipu Sultan
- **Jacobin Club** at Seringapatam set up **1797**; Tipu joined as **'Citizen Tipu'**, planted a **Tree of Liberty**
- Used **war rockets** (did not discontinue them)
- Titles: **'Tiger of Mysore'**, 'Citizen Tipu', **'Padshah'** — 'Lion of Mysore' is not among them
- Sent **ambassadors to France** and built a **navy** (APPSC-G1 2018 key) — Tipu, not Haidar or the Wodeyars
- **Third Anglo-Mysore War** — ended Tipu's **supremacy in southern India**
- **Ceded Districts** came to the British in **1800** as a consequence of the Mysore wars (not the Third War's stated significance)

## SEC 1:166:3 | Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
- Tipu ceded about **half his territory**, paid **₹3 crore** indemnity, gave **two sons** as hostages
| Party | Share |
|---|---|
| **British** | **Malabar, Coorg** (protectorate), **Dindigul, Bara Mahal** |
| Nizam | **Krishna-Pennar** area |
| Marathas | **Tungabhadra** area |
- **Bara Mahal** (Salem region) — where **Read** and **Thomas Munro** began the **Ryotwari** system

## SEC 1:166:4 | Nizam & Marathas each war
- First War — Haidar's diplomacy **won over the Marathas** and **detached the Nizam** from the British
- Second War — Nizam in an **anti-British confederacy**, later **bought off**

## SEC 1:166:5 | After 1799
- **Gumbaz** (Srirangapatna) — **mausoleum** of Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan
  - **Daria Daulat Bagh** — summer palace; **Jamia Masjid** — Masjid-e-Ala
- Wodeyars restored with boy ruler **Krishnaraja Wodeyar III**, under a **Subsidiary Alliance**
- Nizam's share of Mysore lands — the **Ceded Districts**: **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary** (to the British in **1800**)
- Fourth War removed the **French-backed threat**; brought **Mysore and Andhra** (via Ceded Districts) under the Company

## SEC 1:166:6 | Exam corner
- **Velu Nachiyar** of **Sivaganga** — recovered her kingdom with **Haidar Ali's** help; called the **first queen to wage war against the British** (APPSC-G1 2022)
  - Chennamma (Kittur) and Lakshmibai (Jhansi) fought later; Durgavati fought the Mughals
- **Thomas Munro** served under **Hector Munro** and **Sir Eyre Coote** in the early wars

## SEC 1:166:7 | Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan → contributions
- ★ **Tipu Sultan** — ambassadors to **France** for European technology; tried to build a **navy** and modernise his army
- ★ **Tipu Sultan** — set up **embassies in foreign countries on modern lines**: Ottoman Empire/Istanbul, **France**, Iran, Afghanistan, Arabia
- ★ Tipu's capital — **Srirangapatna**; member of the **Jacobin Club** (founded by French officials); planted the **"tree of liberty"**
- ★ **Haidar Ali** — modern armoury at **Dindigul (1755)**
  - Mysore army under **Venkata Raya** conquered Dindigul in **1742**; **18 Palayams** under Dindigul Semai

## SEC 1:166:8 | Other persons → roles in the Mysore wars
- ★ **Sir Eyre Coote** — defeated Haidar Ali at **Porto Novo**
- ★ **Colonel Baillie** — defeated by Haidar and Tipu at **Pollilur** (10 September 1780); before the war Haidar formed a front with the **Marathas and Nizam**

## SEC 1:166:9 | Anglo-Mysore Wars and their battles — year, war, result
- ★ **Pollilur (1780)** — British army under **Col. William Baillie** defeated (after Haidar's **Siege of Arcot**, 1780)
- ★ First War **1767-69** — **Treaty of Madras** (4 April 1769); Bengal Governor **Harry Verelst**
- ★ Second War **1780-84** — **Treaty of Mangalore**
- ★ **Fourth** War — Tipu defeated; Srirangapatna captured; Tipu killed in its defence in **1799**
- ★ Third War (1790-92) — **Treaty of Srirangapatna**, March **1792**, between **Tipu Sultan** and **Lord Cornwallis**

# ROW 1:167 | The Nizam of Hyderabad, Subsidiary Alliance, and the cession of the Northern Circars
## SEC 1:167:0 | Circars & Andhra to the Company
### Northern Circars
- Mughal firman granting the Circars — **Shah Alam II**, **1765** (some sources 1766)
- **Nizam Ali** leased the Circars in **1766** — after Salabat Jung's grant (**1759**), before the Treaty of Masulipatnam (**1768**)
- **Treaty of Hyderabad (1766)** — Circars leased for **military aid** or **₹9 lakh** a year
- Circars handed over in 1766 included **Mustafanagar, Eluru, Rajamahendravaram, Srikakulam** — not **Kurnool** (Ceded Districts, 1800)
### Guntur and after
- **Basalat Jung** died **1782**; Nizam Ali got **7 lakh rupees** and handed Guntur over on **18 September 1788**
- **Padmanabham (10 July 1794)** — Company defeated **Chinna Vijayaramaraju** of Vizianagaram
- **Chandurti (1758)** — Conflans beaten
- Ceded territories (Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur, Bellary) — **Dattamandalalu** / **Ceded Districts** (1800)
- **Telangana** stayed under the Nizam; Northern Circars + Ceded Districts ≈ **Coastal Andhra** and **Rayalaseema**

## SEC 1:167:1 | Circars & Guntur
- **Nizam Ali** at first **refused** Shah Alam II's firman; agreed to lease the Circars in **1766**
- **Kandregula Jogipantulu** proposed **₹15 lakh** (using the **Gollapudi** pay mutiny); 1766 treaty fixed **₹9 lakh** a year
- **Guntur Circar** — main conflict area; held by Nizam Ali's brother **Basalat Jung**
  - The **fifth** Circar; also called **Kondaveedu** or **Murtazanagar**

## SEC 1:167:2 | Asaf Jahi Nizams
| Nizam | Point |
|---|---|

| **Sikandar Jah** | **Secunderabad** cantonment named after him |

- ★ **Salar Jung I** — abolished **revenue farming** ('Agricultural Tax')
- **Salar Jung I** served **Nasir-ud-Daula, Afzal-ud-Daula, Mir Mahbub Ali Khan**; died **1883**
- **Doctrine of Lapse** — **Dalhousie**; Subsidiary Alliance — **Wellesley**; Blue Water Policy — **Francisco de Almeida**

## SEC 1:167:3 | Road to 1798
- Nizam's defeat by Marathas at **Kharda (1795)** → blamed **Sir John Shore's** neutrality, dismissed British battalions, relied on **French troops under Raymond** → Wellesley forced the alliance
- Resident **James Kirkpatrick** — reported the Nizam's French battalions; pushed him into the **1798** alliance
- **Awadh** — buffer state shielding **Bengal** from the **Marathas and Afghans**

## SEC 1:167:4 | Subsidiary Alliance: terms & effects
- Wellesley's system (**1798**) — counter **French influence**; British **paramountcy without direct administration**
- Terms: accept a **Resident**, surrender **foreign affairs**, maintain the **subsidiary force**, employ **no other Europeans** without Company consent
- Rajput alliances (**1818**) — protection from **Marathas and Pindaris**, accept British paramountcy, isolate them from Maratha powers
- **Satara (1848)** — **first** annexation under the Doctrine of Lapse
  - Punjab — conquest after Second Anglo-Sikh War; Awadh — misgovernment; Berar — **subsidy arrears**
- **Doctrine of Lapse** — annexation of states **without a natural male heir**
- **Ring Fence Policy** — **buffer states** to protect Company territories

## SEC 1:167:5 | Order of signing
### Working of the alliance
- **Resident** at Hyderabad — controlled the Nizam's **foreign relations**; supervised the subsidiary force
- Failure to pay for the force → **cession of territory** (e.g. the Nizam's **Ceded Districts**)
- British intervention justified by succession disputes, misgovernment and **international commitments**
### Order of acceptance
| State | Year |
|---|---|
| **Hyderabad** (first) | **1798** |
| Mysore, Tanjore | **1799** |
| Awadh | **1801** |
| Peshwa | **1802** |
| Bhonsle | 1803 |
| Scindia | **1804** |
| **Holkar, Bharatpur, Jodhpur, Jaipur, Bundi** (Rajput states) | **1818** |
- **Tipu** refused it; **Holkar** — **last** Maratha power to sign (1818)
- **Charles Metcalfe** — negotiated the 1818 Rajput treaties under **Lord Hastings**
### Andhra and annexations
- **Nellore and Chittoor** taken from the Carnatic Nawab in **1801** → whole Andhra under the Company
- **Turrebaz Khan** — attacked Hyderabad Residency **17 July 1857** to free **Chida Khan**; repulsed by **Davidson**
- Lapse order: **Satara (1848) → Sambalpur (1849) → Jhansi (1853) → Nagpur (1854)**
- **Awadh (1856)** — annexed for **maladministration**

# ROW 1:168 | Andhra region under the Company - Machilipatnam, Ceded Districts, early administration and revenue
## SEC 1:168:0 | Chronology
### Circars and the lease
- Five Circars — **Chicacole, Rajahmundry, Ellore, Mustafanagar, Guntur**; **Masulipatnam** was a Company station, not a Circar
- **Kandregula Jogipantulu** — **dubashi** and envoy between the Nizam's court and the Company's men at Machilipatnam
  - Used the **Gollapudi mutiny** to get the Circars leased for **15 lakh rupees** a year (**1766**)
- **Treaty of Masulipatnam (1768)** — Company kept the Circars for a reduced peshkash of **9 lakh** a year + military help to the Nizam
- Guntur payment (1788) — **7 lakh**
### Bobbili and the zamindars
- **Bobbili War (1757)** — **Vijayarama Raju** of Vizianagaram took help of the **French under Bussy**
- **Tandra Papayya** (brother-in-law of Bobbili **Ranga Rao**) killed Vijayaramaraju to avenge Bobbili; Ranga Rao was killed in the battle
- Hereditary zamindari families of the Circars — **Velamas, Reddis, Kshatriya Rajus**
### Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1759 | Salabat Jung's grant |
| 1766 | Circar lease |
| **1768** | **Treaty of Masulipatnam** |
| 1788 | Guntur handover |
| **1794** | **Battle of Padmanabham**; **District Collectors** replace the **Chief-and-Council** system in the Circars |
| **1798** | **Subsidiary Alliance** with the Nizam (Treaty of Hyderabad) |
| **1800** | **Ceded Districts** ceded (Nizam could not pay the subsidy in cash) |
| **1801** | **Nellore-Chittoor** — last to come under Company rule |
| **1802** | **Permanent Settlement** (Cornwallis, 1793) applied to Andhra zamindaris — fixed demand replaced auctions |
| 1820 | Ryotwari extended across Madras under **Munro** |
| **1948** | **Zamindari abolished** in Madras Presidency |
- The 1794 Collector change is not explained by the 1802 Permanent Settlement (it came later)
### Land systems and effects
- **Zamindari** — Northern Circars; **Ryotwari** — Rayalaseema, later most of Andhra
- Ryotwari effects — **over-assessment** (often over **50%** of produce), **moneylender** debt, **land alienation**
- Collectors relied on **dubashis**; **Munro** and **C.P. Brown** — rare officials who learned Telugu
- **Dharmavaram** and **Madanapalle** — present-day silk hubs; sericulture promoted by the British in the Ceded Districts
- Fertile, educated Circars vs arid, neglected Ceded Districts → later **Sri Bagh Pact** debate

## SEC 1:168:1 | Regions acquired
- **Robert Clive** secured the Circar firman from **Shah Alam II** (1765)
- Ceded Districts (**1800**) — **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur** (now AP) + **Bellary** (now Karnataka)
- Name **'Rayalaseema'** — suggested by **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao**; adopted at **Andhra Mahasabha, Nandyal, 1928** (APPSC G2 2012 key)
- After the **Second Carnatic War** the British installed **Muhammad Ali** as Nawab of the Carnatic (secure only after 1763)
