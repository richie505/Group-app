# ROW 1:173 | Company's constitutional framework - Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784, Act of 1786
## SEC 1:173:1 | Regulating Act, 1773
### Background and the Act
- **Select Committee** of 1772 on Company affairs — headed by **Burgoyne** (with a Secret Committee)
- **Regulating Act, 1773** — **first** parliamentary step to control the East India Company; recognised its political and administrative functions
- Did **not** abolish the **Court of Directors**; Directors had to report to the British Government on revenue, civil and military affairs
- **Supreme Court at Calcutta** (1774) — one Chief Justice and **three judges**; civil and criminal jurisdiction
### Act of 1786
- Passed to meet **Lord Cornwallis**'s condition for accepting office: GG could override his Council and act as Commander-in-Chief
- Corrected the 1773 defect of the GG (incl. Hastings) being **repeatedly outvoted by his Council**
### Not a British-era landmark
- ★ **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** — post-independence; earlier the **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955**; amended and renamed in **1976**
## SEC 1:173:2 | Acts compared
- ★ **Regulating Act, 1773** — set up India's **first Supreme Court** at Fort William, **Calcutta**; Chief Justice and three puisne judges
- ★ **Sir Elijah Impey** — first Chief Justice
- Order: Regulating Act **1773** → Amending Act **1781** → **Pitt's India Act 1784** → Act of **1786**
- **Amending Act, 1781** (Act of Settlement) — Court to respect **Hindu and Muslim personal laws**; exempted official acts
- **Double Government** of Pitt's India Act (1784) — ended by the **Government of India Act, 1858**
## SEC 1:173:3 | Defects of 1773 → remedies
- Under the 1773 Act the GG had **no veto** and **could be outvoted by his Council**; override power came only with the **Act of 1786**
## SEC 1:173:5 | Exam corner
- **Ministry of Law and Justice** (oldest organ of GoI) — traces origin to the **Charter Act of 1833** (Law Member, first Law Commission)
## SEC 1:173:7 | Acts → year → provisions (1773, 1781, 1786, 1793)
- ★ **Regulating Act** — passed **1773**; appointed a Governor-General of Fort William in Bengal with supervisory powers
- ★ **Act of 1786** — empowered **Lord Cornwallis** to **overrule his Council** (right to reject the Council's decision); passed to get Cornwallis to accept the post
  - Cornwallis wanted the powers of both **Governor-General** and **Commander-in-Chief**

# ROW 1:174 | Charter Acts of 1793, 1813, 1833 and 1853 - provisions and significance
## SEC 1:174:0 | Charter Acts compared
### Charter Act, 1813
- ★ First **financial allocation for education** — annual grant of **Rs 1 lakh**
- Ended Company's trade monopoly in India; kept exclusive trade in **tea** and **with China**
- Allowed **Christian missionaries** to enter and preach (Evangelical pressure: **Wilberforce**, **Charles Grant**)
### Charter Act, 1833
- Abolished the remaining monopoly over **tea** and **China trade**
- Laws of the GG-in-Council called **Acts** (not Regulations); legislative power for all British India to the GG-in-Council
### Charter Act, 1853
- Abolished Directors' **patronage**; **open competition** for civil-service recruitment
- **Last** Charter Act; the **GoI Act 1858** then transferred authority to the Crown
## SEC 1:174:1 | FIRSTs and LASTs

# ROW 1:175 | Land revenue systems - Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari and Mahalwari; areas and effects
## SEC 1:175:0 | Three settlements compared
### Originators and years

| System | Planned / devised by | Year / start |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent Settlement | **Cornwallis** with **John Shore** | **1793** (Bengal, Bihar, Orissa) |
| Ryotwari | **Alexander Read** and **Thomas Munro** | from **1792**, **Baramahal** |
| Mahalwari | **Holt Mackenzie** | proposed **1822**; revised **1833** under Bentinck with **R.M. Bird** |

- **Baramahal** — Salem, Krishnagiri, Dharmapuri, Tiruvannamalai; came under Company after Third Mysore War, **1792**; main Ryotwari area: Madras
- First Ryotwari demand — **50%** of estimated produce (not 30%)
- **Madras and Coorg** — Ryotwari, not Permanent Settlement
### Comparison

| Feature | Permanent Settlement | Ryotwari | Mahalwari |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue relation with | **zamindar** | **ryot** | **village / mahal** |
| Collector | zamindar | individual farmer | **lambardar** (village headman) |
| Assessment | fixed forever | **periodically revised** | **periodically revised** |
| Default | estate auctioned | cultivator's land seized | **village collectively liable** |
| Share of British area | about **19%** | about **51%** | about **30%** |

- **Sunset Law** — estate auctioned if revenue not paid **by sunset** of the due date
- Ryotwari: revenue fixed on **land**, not crop; revised about every **thirty years** (the two are separate features)
- Mahalwari unit — the **mahal** (village or estate); revenue collected through headman or village community
- **Mahalwari areas** — **North-Western Provinces** (Agra-Oudh), parts of **Punjab**, Central India
  - Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Northern Circars — Permanent Settlement; Madras, Bombay — Ryotwari
### Andhra
- Mahalwari-type arrangement in Andhra — **Nellore and Chittoor** (some accounts: Nellore alone)
- Permanent Settlement implemented in Andhra in **1802** — **auction system abolished**, tax fixed; zamindari abolition only in **1948**
- **Village-lease settlements** (headmen as lessees) — replaced ryotwari in the Ceded Districts after **Munro left in 1808**; lasted till ryotwari restored about **1820**
### Effects and criticism
- Rigid **cash** revenue even in crop failure → ryots borrowed from **Mahajans**, mortgaged or lost land
- Cash revenue → **commercial crops**: indigo, cotton, opium, jute
- **Merchant-landlords** — Zamindari areas; **moneylender-landlords** — Ryotwari and Mahalwari areas
- Ryotwari problems — **over-assessment**, inadequate records, unclear boundaries; revenue often heavy, not fixed low
- **Alice Thorner** — Ryotwari **destroyed the village system** and isolated farmers from the government
## SEC 1:175:1 | Permanent Settlement
- Began as a **decennial** (ten-year) settlement, later made permanent
- Gains from agriculture went mainly to **zamindars**, not the Company
- Old estates sold for arrears → new landed class of **urban merchants and moneylenders**
- Effects — **absentee landlordism**, **sub-infeudation**, **rack-renting**; peasants reduced to tenants-at-will
- Rent distribution: state **10/11**, zamindar **1/11** (some accounts 9/10 and 1/10)
- Rationale for fixed demand — assured income, lower collection cost, loyal landlord class, land improvement; it did **not** let the Company gain from later price rises
- **Coastal Andhra** — Haveli lands (direct Company control) and Zamindari areas, whose zamindars paid **peshkash**
- **Madras Inam Commission** — **1858**; investigated rent-free inam titles
- All three settlements made land a **transferable commodity**; created loyal landlord class; fed rural indebtedness and peasant revolts
- State as **'ultimate landlord'** — criticism of the **Ryotwari System**
- **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885** — rights of **landlords and tenants** in Bengal and Bihar
## SEC 1:175:2 | Thomas Munro
- ★ **Thomas Munro** — **first Principal Collector** of the Ceded Districts (**Rayalaseema**); developed Ryotwari there; direct **ryot–state** relationship
- ★ Munro suppressed the **Palegars** of Rayalaseema, assisted by officer **Campbell**, before Ryotwari
- Brought Ryotwari to the **Ceded Districts** in **1802**; Northern Circars mainly zamindari
- Headquarters as Collector — **Anantapur**
- Career: assistant to **Alexander Read** in Baramahal → Principal Collector, Ceded Districts → President, Judicial Commission → **Governor of Madras**
- Died of cholera at **Pattikonda** (Kurnool), **1827**; buried at **Gooty**
- Andhra pattern — **Zamindari** in the Circars, **Ryotwari** in the Ceded Districts, **Mahalwari**-type in Nellore
## SEC 1:175:3 | Ryotwari & Mahalwari features
- Ryot could **sell, mortgage, gift or lease** the land as long as assessment was paid
- After a bad harvest — no automatic exemption; occasional **remission or reduction** possible
- **Holt Mackenzie** devised Mahalwari; **R.M. Bird** revised it (survey-based); **James Thomason** carried on Bird's settlement
- Common ownership under Mahalwari — **Bhai Chara** or **Mahali**
## SEC 1:175:4 | Terms
- Zamindars could seize tenants' property — Regulations of **1799** (**Haftam**) and **1812** (**Panjam**)

- ★ **Thomas Munro** — started Ryotwari; introduced the revenue policy in **Rayalaseema** (Ceded Districts)

## SEC 1:179:4 | Vernacular Press Act, 1878
### The Act
- Passed by **Lord Lytton** amid anger over the **1876–77 famine** and the lavish **Delhi Durbar** (1877)
- Exemption — by **submitting proofs to a government censor**; magistrates could demand bonds; no appeal to court
- Applied only to Indian-language papers, **not English** papers
- **Amrita Bazar Patrika** — turned **English overnight** to escape it
- Repealed **1882** (Ripon) — pre-censorship dropped; a **press commissioner** appointed
- **Indian Press Act 1910** revived VPA features (security deposits); **1908 Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act** — magistrates could seize presses; both Minto II era, not cause and effect
