# ROW 5:102 | Soil, noise, thermal, marine and radioactive pollution - sources, effects and control
## SEC 5:102:0 | Type → major source
### Thermal pollution
- ★ **Nuclear energy** harnessing often causes **thermal pollution**
- Thermal pollution = change in water **temperature**; main cause: discharge of **hot water** (coolant) from **thermal and nuclear power plants** and industries
- **Warmer water** holds **less dissolved oxygen** (oxygen-holding capacity falls as temperature rises)
### Other sources
- **Ghost nets** (marine pollution) — **abandoned, lost or discarded fishing gear** that keeps trapping marine life
- Radioactivity: **strontium-90** fallout from **weapons testing** is **man-made**; cosmic rays, **radon**, **potassium-40** are natural
- **Cell towers** — **non-ionising** radiation; **X-ray machines** — ionising radiation

| Pollution type | Major primary source (APPSC-G2 2025 key) |
|---|---|
| Water | **Sewage discharge** |
| Air | Industrial emissions |
| Soil | Chemical fertilisers and pesticides |
| Noise | Construction |

### Constitution
- **Article 51A(g)** — **fundamental duty** of citizens to protect and improve the natural environment
- **Article 48A** — **Directive Principle** for the State on protection and improvement of the environment; separate provisions (48A does not explain 51A(g))

## SEC 5:102:1 | Soil pollution
### Definition and pollutants
- **Soil pollution** — build-up of **toxic chemicals or radioactive material** in soil; causes toxic crops, less vegetation

| Pollutant | Source / association |
|---|---|
| **Mercury** | **Chlor-alkali** effluent and **fungicides**; **Minamata disease** |
| Cadmium | Phosphate fertilisers and batteries; **itai-itai** disease |
| Chromates | Tanneries |

- **DDT** — persistent organic pollutant under the **Stockholm Convention**
- **DDT** and **mercury** **biomagnify** in marine food chains; dissolved oxygen does not (not a pollutant)
### Rules and law
- **Contaminated Sites Rules** (under Environment (Protection) Act, 1986) — test sites against **189** hazardous chemicals
- **Fly ash** — must be used in bricks, cement and roads within **300 km** of thermal plants; **50 km** = limit for using treated **STP wastewater**
- **IMO Ballast Water Management Convention** (invasive species via ballast water) — in force **2017**
- **APPCB** set up **1976** under the **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974** (Air Act 1981, EP Act 1986, Noise Rules 2000 came later)
### Control
- Soil-pollution controls: **crop rotation**, **biofertilisers**, **integrated pest management**; **watersheds** are NOT a soil-pollution control (APPSC 2019 trap)
- **Biostimulation** — adds **nutrients** to boost native microbes; **bioaugmentation** — adds pollutant-degrading microbes
- **Oil Zapper** — **bacterial consortium** from **TERI** (bioremediation agent, not a chemical dispersant); consumes **oily sludge and crude oil** from oil spills
- Alternatives to conventional pollution control: green technology, bioremediation, carbon capture and storage; **incineration** is itself conventional, not an alternative (APPSC-G2 2025 trap)

## SEC 5:102:2 | Noise Rules 2000 (dB(A))
- **Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000** — framed under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**

| Zone | Night limit |
|---|---|
| Residential | **45 dB(A)** |
| Silence zone | 40 dB(A) |
| Industrial | 70 dB(A) |

- **Silence zone** — at least **100 metres** around hospitals, educational institutions and courts
- Effects of prolonged noise: constricted blood vessels, digestive spasms, **impaired night vision** (it does not improve night vision)

## SEC 5:102:3 | Noise facts
- Noise intensity measured in **decibels (dB)** (APPSC 2025); **dB(A)** weighted to human hearing
  - Hertz = frequency; sievert = biological radiation dose; becquerel = radioactivity
- Rise of about **10 dB** roughly **doubles** perceived loudness
- **Rustling leaves** ~**20 dB**; **rocket engine** ~**180 dB**
- **CPCB** bans firecrackers above **125 dB** at **4 m** from point of bursting
- **NANMN** (CPCB Real Time National Ambient Noise Monitoring Network) — **70 stations** in **seven metros**: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Lucknow, Hyderabad
- **Green mufflers** — rows of **trees and shrubs** along roads that absorb noise

- **GloLitter Partnerships** (2019) — run by **IMO and FAO**, funded by Norway; sea-based marine plastic litter
- **Microplastics** — plastic pieces smaller than **5 mm**

## SEC 5:102:6 | Radioactive pollution
### Radon and sources
- ★ **Radon gas** — most important **indoor air pollutant**; colourless, odourless, radioactive; natural decay product of **radium** from soil
- ★ Radon (**Rn-222**) — leading cause of **lung cancer** after smoking
- Radon — radioactive **noble gas** from the Earth's crust used in **cancer treatment** (APPSC-G1 2018)
- **Medical** sources: **X-rays and isotopes**; natural sources: cosmic rays, radon-222
- **2010 Mayapuri** (Delhi scrap market) incident — unclaimed **cobalt-60**
- **Tummalapalle** uranium mine and mill (UCIL) — **YSR Kadapa** district (not Kurnool)
### Regulation and emergencies
- **AERB** (1983, under Atomic Energy Act, 1962) — India's **independent regulator** for nuclear and radiation safety; approves **radioactive waste handling and disposal**
  - Environmental Survey Laboratory only monitors radiation; **DAE** — nodal technical agency with the Crisis Management Group
- **MRDS** (NDMA) — for metros and State capitals to detect **unclaimed (orphan) radioactive material** (APPSC 2021, asked twice); precedent: Mayapuri
- **NDMA** National Guidelines on Management of Nuclear and Radiological Emergencies — **2009**
- **District Collector** — Incident Commander in an **off-site** nuclear emergency

# ROW 5:103 | Solid waste, plastic waste, e-waste, biomedical and construction waste - rules and management
## SEC 5:103:0 | SWM rules compared
### Basics
- **Solid waste** — discarded material in solid, liquid, semi-solid and **containerised gaseous** form
- **Biodegradable** waste ~**55–60%** of Indian MSW
- **Anaerobic** decomposition → **methane**/biogas; aerobic → compost
- Expanded **5R** hierarchy adds **Refuse** and **Repurpose** (or Recover)
- Biodegradable: **sewage**; non-biodegradable: plastic, DDT, asbestos, **PVC** (PHBV, starch, proteins are biodegradable polymers)
- Rural waste shifting from biodegradable towards **plastic, sanitary** and other non-biodegradable waste
- **Invisible e-waste** — **vapes, USB cables, LED accessories** thrown in regular waste
### Rules timeline
| Rules | Key point |
|---|---|
| MSW (Management and Handling) Rules, **2000** | Primary goal: **promoting segregation** (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| E-waste (M&H) Rules, **2011** | **EPR** first introduced |
| SWM Rules, **2016** | **3 streams**: biodegradable, dry, domestic hazardous; **EPR** for producers and brand owners; user fees, spot fines; no untreated waste in landfills; **criteria for landfill and waste-processing sites** |
| SUP ban, **2022** | Single-use plastic |
| SWM Rules, **2026** | **4 streams**: **wet, dry, sanitary, special care** |

- 2016 Rules: used **diapers/sanitary napkins** wrapped and put in **dry** bin; 2026 Rules give a separate sanitary stream
- 2026 Rules: **wet** bin = food scraps, peels, garden waste; paper, plastics, metals → **dry** bin
- 2026: special care = domestic hazardous waste
- 2026: **Bulk Waste Generator** must process wet waste on site or obtain an **EBWGR certificate**
- 2026: **Environmental Compensation** follows the **polluter-pays** principle (not precautionary)
- (Kigali Amendment = HFC phase-down; Rotterdam = prior informed consent)
### Treatment
- **Sanitary landfills** — not for all biodegradable waste without segregation
- **Incineration** burns waste **in oxygen** (may produce steam for power), not in its absence
- **RDF** (shredded, dehydrated high-calorific MSW) — used in **cement kilns and thermal power plants**
- **Integrated Low Cost Sanitation** — dry latrines → **pour-flush** latrines; liberation of **manual scavengers**
### Plastic, e-waste, C&D
- **PWM (Amendment) Rules, 2026** — unmet targets carried forward up to **three years** (not five)
- **Microbeads** in cosmetics prohibited under PWM Rules; **FSSAI** study found microplastics in all tested sugar and salt samples (unrelated)
- **E-waste** (ITU) — discarded electrical and electronic equipment and parts **not meant for reuse**
- **Global E-waste Monitor** — **ITU and UNITAR**
- **E-waste Rules, 2022** EPR — producers buy digital EPR certificates from **registered** recyclers
- **C&D Waste Management Rules, 2025** — **EPR** framework (MoEFCC)
### AP and courts
- AP urban set-up: **17** Municipal Corporations, **113** municipalities, **7** cantonment boards
- **Supreme Court, 18 August 2026** — **generators**, not only sanitary workers, responsible for waste at the threshold

## SEC 5:103:1 | Disposal method → waste
### MSW processing
- Collection chain: door-to-door → segregated transport → **transfer station** → **MRF** → processing/recycling → residual landfill
- **Indore method** — **aerobic** composting, turned at intervals; **Bangalore method** — **anaerobic** pits, alternate layers of MSW and night soil
- **Vermicompost** — produced by **earthworms**, hence a biological method
- Non-recyclable **high-calorific dry waste** → **RDF** / co-processing in cement kilns, not landfilling
- **Incineration** — controlled high-temperature burning; **pyrolysis** — heating **without oxygen**
- Waste-to-energy: **pyrolysis**, plasma gasification; compost and vermicompost give manure, not energy
- **Oilzapper** — bacteria mix for **oil spills and oily sludge**; **microbial fuel cell** treats wastewater; **EPR** = producer duty
- **UV purification** — kills microorganisms in drinking water
- **Hydrogen** — clean fuel; fuel cells emit only **water vapour**
### Healthcare and hazardous waste
- Healthcare waste: ~**85%** general non-hazardous, ~**15%** hazardous (infectious, chemical, radioactive)
- **BMW treatment** — mainly to **prevent infection spread** and protect human health (APPSC-GS 2025)
- Hazardous waste — four characteristics: **ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity** (not biodegradability)
- Household hazardous waste: **paints and batteries**
- **Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991** — **no-fault** insurance and immediate relief for victims of hazardous-substance accidents
- Hazardous-waste disposal: **landfill = last resort**; APPCB prefers **co-processing in cement plants** and waste exchange between industries

## SEC 5:103:4 | E-waste, C&D, hazardous
### Legacy waste and treaties
- **Legacy waste** — old MSW in dumpsites, emitting methane, polluting groundwater; **biomining** separates it into recyclables, RDF, bio-soil, inert fill → **circular economy** (recovering materials for reuse)
- AP inherited ~**108 lakh tonnes** of legacy waste (CM, Feb 2026); **Ongole** biomining Guthikondavaripalem dump, RDF to cement plants
- **INC-5.2** (Geneva, Aug 2025) — **no agreement**; sticking points: production caps, chemicals of concern, finance
### E-waste
- Old computers release **beryllium, cadmium, chromium, mercury, lead**, PCBs, PBBs (not plutonium)
- **Cadmium** — kidney and bone damage; persistence and endocrine effects = **brominated flame retardants (PBBs)**
- India — **third**-largest e-waste generator (after China, US); ~**2.2 million tonnes** in 2025 (3.54 lakh t = collected/processed FY 2021)
- **E-waste (Management) Rules, 2022** — notified Nov 2022, in force **1 April 2023**; **106** EEE items, **include solar PV** waste
- **Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022** — replaced **2001** rules; EV, portable, automotive, industrial batteries under EPR
- Informal e-waste hubs: **Seelampur** (Delhi) and **Moradabad**
- Citizens: give old devices to **authorised recycler/collection centre** after **wiping data**
### Biomedical, hazardous and industrial
- **BMW Management Rules, 2016** replaced **BMW (M&H) Rules, 1998** (ten categories)
- **Minamata Convention** — phases out mercury-added products (fluorescent lamps); Stockholm = POPs
- **Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016** — **prevention** at the top of hierarchy; co-processing later, before safe disposal

- **C&D Waste Management Rules, 2025** — effective **1 April 2026** (1 Oct 2025 was the draft SWM Rules date)

## SEC 5:103:10 | Waste — definitions, categories and biodegradable vs non-biodegradable — Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable
### Biodegradable
- Biodegradable pollutants — broken down into simpler, harmless substances by **micro-organisms** (bacteria)
- ★ **Sewage** — biodegradable pollutant; also domestic garbage, urine, agricultural residues, cattle dung
- ★ **Wool** — biodegradable (natural fibre of **keratin**); also vegetable peels, fruit waste, paddy husk, cow-dung
- ★ **Rubber** — biodegradable (synthetic rubber is not)
- **Biodegradable polymers** — eco-friendly because **microorganisms** degrade them
### Non-biodegradable
- ★ **Tin** / tin scrap — metal, not broken down by microorganisms
- ★ **Plastic**, tin, lead — not destroyed by bacteria
- Aluminium foil, glass, synthetic rubber — non-biodegradable
- ★ **Polythene** — a **polymer** of **ethylene (C2H4)** (carbon and hydrogen); non-biodegradable, so bags cannot be destroyed
- ★ **PVC** (polymer of vinyl chloride) — not biodegradable; cellulose, starch, protein are biodegradable natural polymers
### BPA
- ★ **Bisphenol A (BPA)** — industrial chemical used in **food-packaging** materials; in **polycarbonate** plastics and **epoxy resins**

# ROW 5:104 | Carbon emissions, carbon footprint, carbon sequestration, carbon credits and carbon trading
## SEC 5:104:0 | Footprints & scopes

| Scope | Emissions |
|---|---|

- **Carbon fertilisation** — increased plant growth because higher CO₂ raises **photosynthesis**

## SEC 5:104:1 | Sinks & sequestration
- **Tropical peatlands** — most carbon per hectare of any terrestrial ecosystem

- **Carbon sequestration** — capturing CO2 from industrial processes and storing it (APPSC-GS 2024; carbon capture also accepted)
  - CO2 fertilisation = plant greening; **carbon leakage** = emissions shifting abroad (CBAM concern)
- **Blue carbon** — carbon stored by **oceans and coastal ecosystems**: **mangroves, seagrass, salt marshes**
- **Geological storage** — coal seams, depleted oil and gas reservoirs, deep saline formations (separate from blue carbon)
- **Bhutan** — carbon-negative country

## SEC 5:104:2 | Capture & removal
- **BECCS** — Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage; **net-negative** emissions

## SEC 5:104:3 | Carbon FIRSTs

## SEC 5:104:4 | Carbon markets & pricing

# ROW 5:106 | Environmental hazards and disaster management - DM Act 2005, NDMA and NDRF, Sendai Framework, disaster types and mitigation
## SEC 5:106:0 | Disaster management cycle
- **DM Act, 2005** — shift from **relief-oriented** action to **risk reduction**
- DM Act gives **PRIs and ULBs** a **legal role** in local plans, relief and capacity building
- **ISRO's DMS** programme and **NDEM** (GIS database) — support management of all hazards

## SEC 5:106:1 | Key terms
- **Hazard** — potentially damaging event; **disaster** — when hazard meets **vulnerability** and causes serious harm
- **Resilience** — ability to resist, absorb and recover quickly; **vulnerability** — susceptibility to damage
- **Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability / Capacity**
- Risk assessment dimensions: **hazard, vulnerability, exposure, capacity** (understanding risk in all dimensions)
- **Geophysical** hazard: **volcanic eruption**; epidemics — biological; thunderstorms — meteorological; forest fires — climatological

## SEC 5:106:2 | Classification
### Types
- **Biological**: epidemics, pandemics, **pest attack**; earthquakes — geological; cyclones — hydro-meteorological
- Drought, heat wave, cold wave — **climatological** (not biological)
- **Man-made**: industrial, chemical, nuclear; e.g. **fire from careless gas-cylinder handling** (APPSC 2025)
- Man-made subtypes: **technological**, **transport**, **social or complex**
- **Emerging hazards**: **cyber attacks**, space weather, AI failures
### Administration
- Notified disasters for **SDRF/NDRF** aid — **14** after August 2026 (**heatwave** and **lightning** added, 16th Finance Commission); no man-made calamity
- **Ministry of Agriculture** — drought, hailstorm, frost, pest attack
- **IMD** — forecasts heatwaves; NDMA sets guidelines; NDRF responds

## SEC 5:106:3 | Structural vs non-structural mitigation
- ★ Earthquake-resistant design: door and window openings **small and centrally located**
- Embankments, mangroves, land-use zoning — **mitigation** (pre-disaster)
- **Structural**: **retrofitting**, earthquake-resistant buildings, sea walls; **non-structural**: awareness, building codes
- **IS 1893** — **earthquake-resistant design**
- Heatwave: **adjusted working hours** — administrative; shade structures — structural; ORS — public-health/awareness
