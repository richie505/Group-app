# ROW 5:60 | Hydropower and pumped storage; large dams and their trade-offs
## SEC 5:60:0 | Energy conversion in a hydro plant
- Hydro plant sequence: **potential → kinetic → mechanical (turbine) → electrical**
- ★ **Turbines and dynamos** convert **mechanical** energy to electrical
- ★ Wind energy — **only kinetic** (energy of air in motion)
- **Mechanical energy** = **kinetic + potential** energy

| Device / process | Conversion |
|---|---|
| **Solar cell** | **light → electrical** |
| Battery | chemical → electrical |
| Generator | mechanical → electrical |
| **Free-falling body** | **potential → kinetic** (also pendulum, descending roller coaster) |

- **Dr NTTPS** (Vijayawada) — Stage V **1x800 MW** unit, **December 2023** (total **2,560 MW**); RTPP Muddanur **1,650 MW**; Simhadri belongs to **NTPC**

## SEC 5:60:1 | Types & classification

- **Run-of-the-river** — uses natural flow, little or no storage, does not much alter the watercourse

### Pumped storage
- **Fastest-responding large-scale storage** — balances renewables and peak demand
- **Off-stream closed-loop** projects — exempted from **CEA** concurrence
- Sequence: surplus power **pumps water up** → **stored** in upper reservoir → **released through turbines** at peak

# ROW 5:61 | Nuclear energy - three-stage programme, types of reactors, PHWR/FBR/AHWR, thorium strategy
## SEC 5:61:0 | Three stages
### The programme
- **Homi Bhabha**'s three-stage programme — **self-reliance** through a **closed fuel cycle**; overcome scarce **uranium** using abundant **thorium**
- Sequence: **PHWR → FBR → AHWR/thorium reactors**

| Stage | Reactor | Fuel / feature |
|---|---|---|
| I | **PHWR** | natural uranium |
| II | **FBR** | plutonium-based fuel; **liquid sodium** coolant, **no moderator** (fast neutrons needed) |
| III | **AHWR**/thorium reactors | thorium |

- FBR: fast neutrons convert **U-238** blanket into **Pu-239** — breeds more fuel than it consumes
- **AHWR** — bridge between **Stage I and Stage III** (thorium use without Stage II)
### Reactors & milestones
- **PFBR** — **India's first indigenous Fast Breeder Reactor**
- **IGCAR** 2005 — **reprocessing** of FBTR's **mixed-carbide** fuel
- **Bharat Small Reactors** (NPCIL) — based on **220 MW PHWR**; captive plants for industry
- **Light Water Reactors** — light water as **both moderator and coolant**; over **85%** of world nuclear capacity
### Nuclide terms
- **Isotones** — same number of **neutrons** (³H and ⁴He: 2 neutrons each)

## SEC 5:61:1 | Fuels: fissile vs fertile
- **U-238** — **fertile**, converted into Pu-239; fuel of **Stage III** and **KAMINI** — **U-233**
- **Enrichment** — raising the share of **U-235** (fissile) by **isotope separation**

## SEC 5:61:2 | Fission, fusion & radioactivity
### Fission & fusion
- **Fission** — splitting of a heavy nucleus; **fusion** — joining of two light nuclei
- All present-day nuclear electricity — **fission**; fusion in Sun and stars, power still at R&D stage
- Fusion releases **more** energy per unit mass than fission (needs very high temperature and pressure)
- ★ **Atom bomb** — **nuclear fission** (uncontrolled)
- **Hydrogen bomb** — fusion triggered by a **fission explosion**
- ★ **Edward Teller** — "father of the **hydrogen bomb**"; first US fusion bomb test **November 1952**, **Eniwetok** (Marshall Islands)

| City | Bomb | Material |
|---|---|---|
| **Hiroshima** | **Little Boy** | **U-235** |
| Nagasaki | Fat Man | **Pu-239** |

- **ITER** (tokamak) — **500 MW** output from **50 MW** input (about ten times)
- **Aditya** (India's first tokamak) and **SST-1** — **Institute for Plasma Research, Gandhinagar** (not BARC)
### Radioactivity
- ★ Measured by **Geiger Counter** (Geiger–Muller); detects alpha, beta, gamma
- Radioactivity — a **nuclear** (not chemical) phenomenon; emits alpha, beta, gamma
- **Half-life** — time for **half** of the radioactive nuclei to decay

| Radiation | Shielding |
|---|---|
| Alpha | paper or skin |
| **Beta** | **a few mm of aluminium** |
| Gamma | lead or concrete |

- Positive beta — **positron**; negative beta — electron; alpha — +2 helium nucleus
- **Neptunium series** — **artificial**, not natural
- **Xenon-135** — "**nuclear poison**": **absorbs neutrons**
- **Tritium** — made by bombarding **lithium** with neutrons
### Fuels & Indian programme
- ★ **Helium** — **not** a fuel element (inert gas)
- **Stage I** — **PHWRs** with natural uranium; spent fuel yields **Pu-239**
- **AHWR** — intended for large-scale **thorium** use, not plutonium-bearing spent fuel
- **ANEEL** fuel (Clean Core Thorium Energy, US) — **thorium + HALEU**; usable in existing PHWRs
- **NPCIL**: **25 reactors**, **8,880 MW**; target **22.38 GW** by **2031–32**
- **SMR** refuelling interval — about **3–7 years**; factory-built
- **IGCAR** — **Kalpakkam** (FBR technology); **AMD** — Hyderabad

## SEC 5:61:9 | Nuclear fuels — isotopes, fissile vs fertile, enrichment, non-fuels
### Nucleus
- ★ Nucleus constituents — **protons and neutrons**
- ★ **Mass number** — sum of **neutrons and protons**
- ★ **Pu-242** (Z = 94) — **148** neutrons
- ★ **Hydrogen** (protium) — **no neutron**; deuterium 1, tritium 2
### Isotopes & isotones
- ★ **Isotopes** — same **protons** (atomic number), different **neutrons** (mass number)
- ★ **Protium, deuterium, tritium** — isotopes of **hydrogen** (not carbon)
- ★ Isoneutronic (isotones): **6C14, 7N15, 8O16** (8 neutrons each)
- ★ **Heavy water** — isotope (deuterium) takes the place of hydrogen
### Fuels
- ★ **Thorium-232** + neutrons → fissile **Uranium-233**
- ★ **Enriched uranium** — U-235 proportion increased from natural level by isotope separation
- ★ **Cadmium** — not a nuclear fuel; **neutron absorber** in control rods and shields
- ★ **Zirconium** (Z = 40) — **not radioactive**; elements above Z 80 generally radioactive, except Technetium (43) and Promethium (61) also radioactive
- ★ Atom bomb — fission of uranium; **hydrogen bomb** — **fusion of deuterium** (and tritium) into helium
- ★ Half-life **5 years** → after 20 years **1/16** remains

## SEC 5:61:10 | Terms → meaning — fission, fusion, bombs and radioactivity
- ★ Reactor vs atom bomb — in a reactor the **chain reaction is controlled** (moderators, control rods)
- ★ **Stars / Sun** — energy from **nuclear fusion** (hydrogen fuses into helium)
- ★ Energy generation in stars — **not** fission
- ★ Breaking apart of a nucleus — **fission** (not fusion)
- ★ Fusion reactors — **fusion of light nuclei**, usually **Tokamak** design, **very high temperature** (thermonuclear)
- ★ Helium nucleus — **two protons and two neutrons**
- ★ Half-life **10 days** → **3/4** decays in **20 days**
- ★ **Fast breeder reactor** — electricity by **fission**; produces more fissile material than it burns
- ★ **Dhruva**, **Purnima**, **Cirus** — Indian research reactors; Purnima I–III indigenously designed, decommissioned

## SEC 5:61:11 | Terms → meaning — fission, fusion, bombs and radioactivity — Atomic vs hydrogen bomb
### Bombs
- ★ **Otto Hahn** (with **F. Strassmann**, **1939**) — atom bomb principle of **nuclear fission** (U-235)
- ★ **Atomic bomb** — **fission**; **hydrogen bomb** — **uncontrolled fusion** (not fission)
- ★ **Nagasaki** (9 August 1945, **Fat Man**) — **plutonium**
- ★ Nuclear bombs — **uranium**
### Particles & radiation
- ★ **Alpha particle** — helium nucleus (2 protons, 2 neutrons, +2); mass nearly equal to a **helium atom**/nucleus
- ★ **Alpha ray** — positively charged particle emitted by a radioactive element
- ★ **Beta particles** — carry **negative** charge (electrons or positrons)
- ★ Zero charge — **neutrino**, **neutron**
- ★ **Deuteron** (a proton + a neutron) — **not** a sub-atomic particle
- ★ Radioactive substance emits **alpha, beta and gamma**
- ★ Radioactivity — a **nuclear** property
### Fuels
- ★ **H. C. Urey** — discovered **heavy water** and deuterium; Nobel Chemistry **1934**
- ★ **U-235** — used in power plants; natural uranium ~**0.7%** U-235; reactors need **3–5%**
- ★ India's reactors — **natural uranium** (about **99.28% U-238**)
- ★ Not atomic fuels — **lead**, **calcium**; thorium usable via breeding to **U-233**
- ★ **Uranium** — metal used to produce electricity
