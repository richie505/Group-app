# ROW 3:20 | Sustainable development - definition, Brundtland Report, SDGs and India's progress
## SEC 3:20:0 | Milestones
- ★ **Limits to Growth** (1972) — propounded by the **Club of Rome**
  - Club of Rome born from a meeting of 30 people from 10 countries at the Accademia dei Lincei, Rome (**April 1968**)
- **Rio+20 Conference (2012)**, UN Conference on Sustainable Development — decided to develop the **SDGs**
- **Agenda 2030** adopted in New York on **25 September 2015**

## SEC 3:20:1 | Brundtland & core idea
- **Brundtland Commission** = World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED), chaired by **Gro Harlem Brundtland** (PM of **Norway**)
- **WCED** — first formal definition of sustainable development, in **Our Common Future**
- Three pillars: **economic growth**, **social inclusion** and **environmental protection** (balance, not maximising one)
- Basic goal — meet **present needs** while preserving choices for **future generations**, within Earth's carrying capacity
- **Carrying capacity** — maximum population an environment can support indefinitely without degradation
- **Earth Overshoot Day** — date when humanity's annual demand on nature exceeds what Earth can regenerate that year

## SEC 3:20:2 | Three pillars: overlaps
- Three pillars: economic growth, **social inclusion or equity**, environmental protection

## SEC 3:20:3 | Rio 1992 principles
- Endorsed at Rio 1992: **precautionary principle**, **polluter pays principle**, **common but differentiated responsibilities**
- **Unrestricted resource exploitation** — NOT endorsed; contrary to sustainable development

## SEC 3:20:4 | Agenda 2030
- **Universal character** — Agenda 2030 applies to all countries (developed, developing, least developed)
- Specific focus: ending all discrimination and violence against **women and girls**
- Two roots: **Millennium Development Goals** and the **Rio+20** outcome
- **17 SDGs**, **169 targets**

## SEC 3:20:5 | India's SDG progress
| Report / event | India |
|---|---|
| SDSN Sustainable Development Report 2025 | **99th** of 167 (score about 67) — first time in **top 100** |
| SDSN Sustainable Development Report 2026 | **94th** — 18 places better than 2015; lagged on 13 SDGs |
| SDG India Index 2023-24 | national score **71**; Kerala and Uttarakhand **79**; Bihar **57** |
| First Voluntary National Review (VNR) | **2017**, covered 7 SDGs |

- SDSN 2026: India ranked behind **Bhutan, Maldives, Nepal and Sri Lanka**; **SDG 2** identified as main area of concern
- **NITI Aayog** prepared the VNR 2025

## SEC 3:20:6 | Exam corner
- Centre of Excellence for Urban Affairs, **Amaravati** (MoU with **UN-Habitat**) — aligned with **SDG 11** (sustainable cities and communities)

## SEC 3:20:7 | Sustainable development — milestones and FIRSTs
- ★ **Prioritize individual wealth accumulation** — NOT an objective of sustainable development
- ★ Concept popularised and formally defined in the **1987 Brundtland Report** ('**Our Common Future**', by WCED)
  - The Brundtland Report is NOT "The Limits to Growth"
- ★ **Sustainable development** — balancing the need to use resources and conserve them for the future
- ★ **1980** — term 'Sustainable Development' introduced; **IUCN** World Conservation Strategy

## SEC 3:20:9 | Terms → meaning
- ★ Brundtland definition (meet present needs without compromising future generations) is intertwined with **carrying capacity**
  - Carrying capacity — maximum number, density or biomass of a population an area can support sustainably
- ★ Base of sustainable development — **environmental approach**

## SEC 3:20:10 | Agenda 2030 and the SDGs — roots, principles, number game
- ★ Agenda 2030 emphasises eliminating all discrimination and violence against **women and girls**

# ROW 3:22 | National income concepts - GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP, factor cost vs market price, GVA, deflator
## SEC 3:22:0 | GDP vs GNP
- ★ **NNP at factor cost** = **National Income**
- **State Domestic Product** (APSES) — value of all goods and services produced within the State in a period, without duplication

| Aggregate | Formula |
|---|---|
| **GNP** | GDP + **NFIA** |
| **NDP** | GDP − depreciation |
| **NNP** | NDP + NFIA |

## SEC 3:22:1 | From GDP (MP) to National Income
- **GNP** = NNP + **depreciation**
- National income = **NNP at factor cost**

## SEC 3:22:2 | Key links
### Per capita income
- ★ **Per capita income** — average income of a country = national income ÷ population
- Per capita income rises only when **income grows faster than population**
- **AP** per capita income computed on **Net State Domestic Product (NSDP)**
- ★ Sign of economic growth — sustained increase in **real per capita income**
### Deflator and GVA
- **GDP deflator** = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100, i.e. GDP at current prices / GDP at constant prices × 100
- Rising GNP/GDP deflator — higher price level, **decrease in purchasing power** of money
- **GVA** — production-side measure; **GDP** = GVA + taxes on products − subsidies (wider measure)

## SEC 3:22:3 | Terms
### NFIA, GDP and GNP
- **NFIA** = factor income received from abroad − factor income paid abroad; added to GDP to get GNP
- Indian resident's factor income earned in Saudi Arabia — counts in India's **GNP only**
- India's GNP usually lower than GDP — NFIA is **negative**
- **GDP** — location-based: all production **inside the country's borders**; does NOT include NFIA
- Residents' production inside and outside the country — **GNP** (nationality-based)
### Other terms
- **Production tax** (levied irrespective of output) — **stamp duty**, property tax; product taxes — GST, excise, sales tax
- **Personal disposable income** = personal income − personal direct taxes − non-tax payments (fines, fees)
- PPP under International Comparison Program — **NSO** is India's nodal agency; **World Bank** the global implementing agency

## SEC 3:22:4 | Included vs excluded
- National income at factor cost — **wages, rent, interest and profits** of the country's normal residents
- **Commission on resale** — payment for productive service, included; NOT a transfer payment
  - Transfer payments: **pensions**, **scholarships**, **unemployment allowances**
- **Value added at each stage** — included; intermediate goods excluded to avoid **double counting**
- Excluded: **second-hand sales**, **interest on public debt**

## SEC 3:22:5 | Worked example
### Concepts
- **National wealth** — a **stock** (point of time); national income — a **flow** (over a period)
- National income = **NNP at factor cost**; NDP = GDP − depreciation; NNP = GNP − depreciation
- **GDP at FC** = GDP at MP − **indirect taxes** + **subsidies** (not direct taxes)
### Worked examples
- GDP(MP) ₹500 cr, depreciation ₹50 cr, NFIA −₹10 cr, net indirect taxes ₹40 cr → GNP(MP) ₹490 cr → NNP(MP) ₹440 cr → national income **₹400 crore**
- Nominal GDP ₹1,000 → ₹1,200 cr with price index 100 → 120 — real GDP **unchanged**
### Personal income and AP
- Personal income = national income + **government and business transfer payments**; deducted: undistributed profits, social security contributions, corporate tax
- AP per capita income 2024-25 (FAE) — **Rs. 2,68,653**; India **Rs. 2,00,162**

## SEC 3:22:6 | Real GDP, deflator, PPP
- **GDP** — value of all final goods and services produced within a country's geographical boundaries in a year, irrespective of who produces them
- **Depreciation** deducted — output that only replaces worn-out capital is not new output
- **Real GDP** — at **constant (base-year) prices**; nominal GDP at current prices
- **Real GVA** — base year used to **remove the effect of price changes**
- **GDP deflator** — most comprehensive measure of inflation (covers all goods and services in GDP; WPI, CPI, CPI-IW price fixed baskets)
- 2022-23 series: **GVA at basic prices** continues to be used; **double deflation** replaced single deflation for manufacturing and agriculture (separately deflates output and input prices)
- India's PPP GDP much larger than market-exchange-rate GDP — **prices are lower in India**

## SEC 3:22:7 | Exam corner
- **Transfer earnings** — not payments for factor services; **excluded** from national income
  - Rent, productive interest, profit — factor incomes

## SEC 3:22:8 | GDP and GNP — meaning and difference
- ★ **Real GDP** — value of production at prices of a **single (base) year**
- ★ National income — **money value of final goods and services** produced (no intermediate goods)
- ★ Prices and money income both doubled — real income **unchanged**

## SEC 3:22:9 | Terms → meaning (national income, SDP and other terms)
- ★ **Income method** GDP = domestic factor incomes − **transfer payments**
- ★ Main difference between GDP and GNP — **net factor income from abroad**
- ★ National Income = **Net National Product at factor cost**
- ★ **NDP** = GDP − **depreciation** (not plus)

## SEC 3:22:10 | Aggregates → formula
- ★ **GNP** — Gross National Product
- ★ GDP at MP = GNP at MP − net income from abroad
- ★ National Income = GNP at MP − depreciation − indirect taxes + subsidies
- ★ NI = NNP at MP − (**indirect taxes − subsidies**)
- ★ **NNP at MP** = GNP at MP − depreciation

## SEC 3:22:11 | Aggregates → formula — Market price vs factor cost
- ★ Difference between GDP at FC and GDP at MP — **indirect taxes and subsidies**
  - GDP at MP = GDP at FC + indirect taxes − subsidies

## SEC 3:22:15 | Nominal vs real GDP and the GDP deflator
- ★ **GNP deflator** — ratio of nominal GNP to real GNP, as a percentage
- ★ Rise in GNP deflator — **decreases purchasing power** of money (rising prices)
- ★ Per capita income growth at current prices higher than at constant prices — constant prices account for **increase in price level**
- ★ **Hindu growth rate** — related to **GDP**

## SEC 3:22:16 | GVA — meaning, headline measure, PPP and India's rank
- ★ Most appropriate measure of economic growth — **per capita real income** (per capita product / per capita NNP)
- ★ Not considered in economic growth — **growth in financial aid from World Bank**; considered: GDP, GNP, per capita GNP
- ★ Highest GSDP growth in 2010-11 (among the States given) — **Bihar**
- ★ Highest per capita income, World Development Report 2000 — **Luxembourg** ($44,640); in 2024, **Singapore** highest per capita GNI (PPP)
- ★ Third largest economy (U.P.P.C.S. 2007) — **Japan**
- PPP — compares currencies through a **basket of goods and services**

## SEC 3:22:17 | Universal angles
- ★ Highest per capita income among Indian states — **Goa**

## SEC 3:22:-1 | (whole topic)
- ★ **NNP** = GNP − depreciation; **GNP** = GDP + NFIA; so NNP = GDP + NFIA − depreciation

# ROW 3:23 | Methods of measuring national income - product, income and expenditure methods; difficulties
## SEC 3:23:0 | Three methods
- **Product (value added) method** — sums the **value added by every producing unit**
- **Value added** = value of output − **intermediate consumption**
- **Income method** — compensation of employees + operating surplus (rent, interest, profit) + **mixed income**
- **Double counting** (cotton → cloth → garment) avoided by counting only the **final product** or the **value added at each stage**

## SEC 3:23:1 | Method → sectors in India
- **Production method** — agriculture, mining, manufacturing
- **Income method** — **services**: trade, transport, banking, real estate, public administration

## SEC 3:23:2 | Circular flow
- **Real flow** (two-sector) — **factor services**: households → firms; **goods and services**: firms → households

## SEC 3:23:3 | Definitions of NI
| Economist | Definition of national income |
|---|---|
| **Alfred Marshall** | **national dividend** — net aggregate of commodities and services produced by labour and capital acting on natural resources |
| **Irving Fisher** | **consumption**-based — services received by ultimate consumers |
| **Kuznets** | added net additions to capital stock |
| **Pigou** | included income from abroad |

- **Expenditure method** — **C + I + G + (X − M)**

## SEC 3:23:4 | In NI or not?
- **Change in inventories** = production − sales; **included** (unsold production counts as output)
- Included: **imputed rent**, **self-consumed farm output**
- Excluded: **unpaid services of housewives**, tax revenue, interest on public debt
- Second-hand car sold via dealer — only the **dealer's or broker's commission** is included

## SEC 3:23:5 | Expenditure side
- **Injections** — investment, government spending, **exports**; **leakages** — saving, taxes, imports
- **Private consumption** — about **59%** of GDP, largest expenditure component; makes India less exposed to global swings

## SEC 3:23:6 | Difficulties in India
- **Informal sector** hard to measure — lacks proper accounts
- One person as farmer and weaver — absence of **occupational specialisation**
- **IMF review (Dec 2025)** — production/expenditure GDP gaps point to only **partial** capture of informal activity
- **Supply and Use Tables** — reduce the **discrepancy between production and expenditure** estimates
- New series improves real GVA via **double deflation** and **volume extrapolation**

## SEC 3:23:7 | State income (AP)
- **APSES 2024-25** figures at **2011-12** base prices
- **Income originating** excludes residents' income from outside the State (that is **income accruing**)
- AP GSDP computed in **9 sectors**
- GSDP estimated by the **production or income-originating** approach
- AP GSDP compiled by the **Directorate of Economics and Statistics, AP** (NSO compiles national accounts)

## SEC 3:23:8 | Exam corner
- **Cost and accounting method** — NOT a method of measuring national income
- Expenditure method also called **consumption-and-investment** or **final expenditure** method
- **Interest on a productive loan** — factor payment, included; pensions, scholarships, gifts — transfer payments

## SEC 3:23:10 | Terms → meaning (circular flow, value added, double counting)
- Open-economy equilibrium: **S + T + M = I + G + X** (leakages = injections)

## SEC 3:23:11 | Methods → what is summed (product, income, expenditure)
- ★ **Fixed capital** — long-term assets used over many years: **farmer's plough**, **computer**; **working capital** — **yarn**, **petrol**
- ★ **Diminishing cost method** — NOT a method to calculate GDP

## SEC 3:23:15 | State income — AP terms, formulae and compilation
- ★ **Factor income** — income accruing to a factor of production in return for **services rendered** to the production unit

## SEC 3:24:2 | NAS base years

## SEC 3:24:3 | 2011-12 vs 2022-23 series
### 2011-12 series
- Headline measure — **GVA at basic prices**
- Criticism — unusually high **manufacturing growth** not matching physical indicators; overestimated private corporate sector
- **Arvind Subramanian** — 2011-17 growth about **4.5%**, not official ~7%
### 2022-23 series
- Follows UN **SNA, 2025 edition**
- Four institutional sectors — **government**, **public corporations**, **private corporations**, **households** (not exports)
- Household / unincorporated sector — benchmarked on **ASUSE**, extrapolated with indicators; **MCA-21** covers companies; PLFS gives employment
- Debates — coverage of the **informal sector** and **MCA-based manufacturing GVA**; IMF data concerns
- **Quarterly GDP** released about **two months** after quarter ends

## SEC 3:24:4 | New series: revisions
### Bodies
- **National Income Committee** (August **1949**) — chaired by **P.C. Mahalanobis**; members Gadgil and Rao; first report **1951**
- **NSO** (2019, CSO + NSSO) — under **MoSPI**, headed by the **Chief Statistician of India**
- NOT an NSO function — **monetary policy**
- **ASI** — conducted by the **Industrial Statistics Wing, Kolkata**; ESD compiles IIP; National Accounts Division handles GDP
### Base year and revisions
- **Base year** — year whose prices value **real GDP at constant prices**; index base = 100
- **CPI** base now **2024** (old base calendar year **2012**); **IIP** rebased to 2022-23
- 2022-23 chosen — free of **GST transition** (2017-18), **COVID** (2019-21) and 2021-22 base effects
- Growth revised: **2023-24** 9.2% → **7.2%**; **2024-25** 6.5% → **7.1%**
- New series **cut GDP by 3-4%**: nominal 2022-23 GDP about Rs 269 → **Rs 261 lakh crore**
- **PLFS 2025** — **monthly** all-India estimates (not only annual)

## SEC 3:24:5 | Estimate stages

# ROW 3:25 | Trends in India's national income, per capita income and sectoral shares; inter-state GSDP comparison

- Largest source of national income: by sector — **services**; by institution — **household sector**
- At Independence the largest contributor to GDP — **agriculture**; industry only about **13%** (1950-51); services now exceed **50%**
- India — **lower-middle-income** economy (World Bank **Atlas method** GNI per capita, updated each 1 July)

- GDP growth **1950-51** — about **3.6%**
- Industry share of GDP **1950-51** — about **13%**
