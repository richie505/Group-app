# ROW 1:30 | Spread and decline of Buddhism; Buddhist and Jain architecture - stupa, chaitya, vihara
## SEC 1:30:0 | Patrons & decline
### Missions and patrons
- Ashoka's mission to **Suvarnabhumi** — **Sona and Uttara**; Mahinda and Sanghamitta to Sri Lanka
- Lay patronage in Andhra — mainly **queens, merchants, guilds and artisans**
  - e.g. deposit of **2,000 karshapanas** with a **weavers' guild** for monks' robes
- **Veerapurushadatta** (Ikshvaku) — rebuilt the **Nagarjunakonda Maha Chaitya** and **Sriparvata University**
- **Naganika** — Naneghat; **Gautami Balashri** — Nasik cave
- **Upasika Bodhisri** — outstanding service to Buddhism in the reign of Ikshvaku king **Ehuvala Chantamula**
- **Mahavamsa** — **Mahadeva Bhikshu** took Buddhism **from Andhra to Sri Lanka** (not the reverse)
- **Vishnukundins** — last dynasty to patronise Buddhism in Andhra; **Indrapalanagara** vihara of this phase
### Decline
- Hostile rulers — **Pushyamitra Shunga, Shashanka, Mihirakula**
- **Hiuen Tsang** (7th c. CE) — found **Sriparvata** functioning **without patronage**
- **Vikramashila** — Pala (**Dharmapala**) Vajrayana centre

# ROW 1:31 | Other heterodox sects - Ajivikas (Makkhali Gosala), Charvaka/Lokayata materialism
## SEC 1:31:0 | Six heterodox teachers
| Teacher | Doctrine |
|---|---|
| **Purana Kassapa** | **Akriyavada** — actions produce neither merit nor demerit |
| **Ajita Kesakambalin** | **Materialist** |
| **Makkhali Gosala** | Ajivikas |
| **Sanjaya Belatthiputta** | Skeptic |
- **Makkhali Gosala** — most important leader and populariser of the Ajivikas (third chief)
- Decline of heterodox sects — **Puranic-Bhakti revival** (incl. under Pallavas) and loss of **royal patronage**

## SEC 1:31:1 | Ajivika doctrine & life
- Ajivikas — **determinists**: actions **fixed before birth**; no free will; karma cannot alter destiny
- Gosala — Mahavira's companion for about **six** years
- **Atomic theory** of matter — influenced the later **Vaisheshika**
  - seven eternal elements — **Pakudha Kaccayana**; Pratityasamutpada — Buddhist
- Ajivika ascetics — called **Sudra sannyasins**; extreme austerities; no caste discrimination

## SEC 1:31:2 | Ajivikas & rulers
- Ajivikas popular under **Bindusara**
- **Barabar caves** — donated by **Ashoka** to the Ajivikas (not built by Chandragupta); among the **earliest rock-cut caves** in India
- **Seventh Pillar Edict** (Delhi-Topra) — names Ajivikas with **Brahmanas, the Sangha and Nirgranthas**

## SEC 1:31:3 | Charvaka / Lokayata
- Other names — **Lokayata**, **Barhaspatya**; Niyativada is Gosala's Ajivika doctrine
- ★ "**The conscious body is the soul**" — **Charvaka**; no separate soul survives death
- Only pramana — **perception**; Nyaya accepts perception, inference, comparison and testimony
- Lokayata — **no surviving texts**; known mainly through **criticisms of rival schools**

## SEC 1:31:4 | Sources
- **Samannaphala Sutta** (**Digha Nikaya**) — Ajatashatru reports the views of the six teachers to the Buddha
- **Bhagavati Sutra** (Jain Anga) — fullest account of **Gosala** and his quarrel with Mahavira

## SEC 1:31:5 | Heterodox schools compared
| School | View |
|---|---|
| **Jainism** | eternal **jiva** in all things |
| Buddhism | anatta |
| Ajivika | predetermined fate |
| Charvaka | rejects Vedic authority; **karma does not determine rebirth** |

## SEC 1:31:8 | Ajivikas → doctrine and features
- ★ Ajivikas — belief in **determinism** (fatalism) and **austerity**; order of reincarnation **cannot** be changed
- ★ Sect believing in the abiding nature of **destiny** — **Ajivikas**

## SEC 1:31:9 | Rulers → patronage, Barabar caves, survival and decline
- ★ "**Fate assigns everything, human is incapable**" — **Ajivikas**

## SEC 1:31:10 | Charvaka/Lokayata → names, tenets, aphorism
- ★ Accepts only **perception** — **Charvaka** (originally Lokayata); rejects Vedas, Vedic ritualism and supernaturalism
- ★ Highest value for Charvaka — **Kama**

## SEC 1:31:12 | Heterodox schools compared → karma, soul, fate, Vedas
- **Shramana** movements (6th c. BCE: Jainism, Buddhism, Ajivika, Charvaka) — arose amid **urbanisation** and **Magadhan patronage**

# ROW 1:32 | Persian and Macedonian invasions - Alexander's campaign, Battle of Hydaspes, effects on India
## SEC 1:32:0 | Achaemenid rulers
- **Cyrus** — founder of the Achaemenid Empire; first to campaign into the Indian north-west (**Gandhara**)
- **Darius I** (**522–486 BC**) — annexed **Punjab and Sindh** west of the Indus; became the **twentieth satrapy**
- **Sudas** (Bharatas) — fought a confederacy of **ten kings**; **Rigveda Mandala VII**, near the **Parushni (Ravi)**

## SEC 1:32:1 | Persian legacy
- **Herodotus** — Indian satrapy most populous and fertile; tribute of **360 talents of gold dust** (one-third of empire's revenue)
- **Kharosthi** (from Aramaic) — written **right to left**
- Ashokan edicts — terms **lipi / dipi**; third-person opening shifting to first person (**Darius** model); Aramaic edicts, satrapy system
- **Persian** contact (6th–5th c. BC) — lasting influence on **script and art**; Alexander's **nineteen-month** campaign — almost **no lasting political mark**

## SEC 1:32:2 | Why Alexander succeeded
- North-west divided into **small independent states** — **Taxila**, **Porus's Punjab**, **Gandhara**
- Help from Indian allies like **Ambhi**, political disunity, superior army
- **Porus** (defeated at the Hydaspes, **326 BC**) — not executed; kingdom **restored and later enlarged**
- **Twelve stone altars** — northern bank of the **Beas**; army refused to go beyond the **Beas** (not the Ravi)
- Withdrew down the Indus; **Nearchus** led the fleet
- Conquered area divided into **three** parts under Greek governors; most states returned to rulers

## SEC 1:32:3 | Indian responses
### Submitted
- **Ambhi** (Omphis) of **Taxila** — **first to surrender** and helped Alexander; Abhisara submitted
### Resisted
- **Porus (Puru)** — ruled between the **Jhelum** and **Chenab**; fought at the Hydaspes
- **Assakenoi (Ashvakas)** — capital **Massaga** (Swat valley); **women took up arms** after heavy losses
- **Malloi / Malavas** and other gana-sanghas — fought him during his **retreat**
### Magadha
- **Nanda** kingdom's **huge army** deterred Alexander from marching east; **Dhana Nanda** unpopular for heavy taxation
- Army refused beyond the Beas — war weariness, homesickness, disease, fear of the **Nanda army**
- **Agrammes** — Greek name of **Dhanananda**; **Bhaddasala** — his commander

## SEC 1:32:4 | Alexander's campaign
- Crossed the **Hindu Kush** in **327 BC**; entered India through the **Khyber Pass** in **326 BC**
- Left in **325 BC**; return point — **Patala (Paatal)**

## SEC 1:32:5 | Effects
- **Bucephala** (after his horse) and **Nicaea** — founded near the Hydaspes battlefield
- Opened **direct contact** and new **land and sea routes** between Greece and India
- Weakened small NW states — helped **Chandragupta Maurya**, who expelled the Greek satraps with **Porus (Parvataka)** as ally
- **Sandrokottas** — Greek name of **Chandragupta Maurya**; fixes early Mauryan chronology (c. **322 BCE**)
- Alexander's invasion — **first dated event** of Indian history

## SEC 1:32:6 | Writers
- **Nearchus** — Alexander's admiral; **first-hand account**; Arrian and Plutarch — later writers
- **Deimachus** — later envoy of **Antiochus I** to **Bindusara**; did not accompany Alexander

## SEC 1:32:7 | Exam corner
- ★ **Takshashila** — between the **Indus and Jhelum**; Rawalpindi district, Pakistan
- **Hydaspes** — **Jhelum (Vitasta)**; battle in **326 BC**
- **Massaga** — capital of the **Ashvakas**

## SEC 1:32:8 | Achaemenid (Persian) rulers → Indian territory
- ★ First Persian ruler to capture part of India — **Darius I** (522–486 BC); Indus lands, Rajputana desert, parts of Punjab (Herodotus)

## SEC 1:32:10 | Political situation on the eve of invasion → causes of Alexander's success
- ★ Magadha ruler contemporary of Alexander — **Dhanananda**
- ★ Dynasty ruling North India at Alexander's invasion — **Nanda**; Dhanananda its last ruler; Greek **Agrammes**; Bhaddasala his commander

## SEC 1:32:11 | Alexander and Indian rulers → submitted or resisted
- ★ **Hydaspes** — Greek name of the **Jhelum**; battle 326 BC
- ★ **Porus** — defeated on the Jhelum; realm between Jhelum and Chenab; state returned and enlarged

## SEC 1:32:12 | Events → year: route, Hydaspes, retreat, death
- ★ Alexander left India in **325 BC** after nearly two years, from **Paatal**; **not defeated by Chandragupta Maurya**

## SEC 1:32:13 | Ancient battles → year, leaders, result
- **Kalinga War** — **261 BC**; turned Ashoka to Buddhism and **Dhamma**

# ROW 1:33 | Sources for the Mauryas - Arthashastra, Indica of Megasthenes, Mudrarakshasa, edicts
## SEC 1:33:0 | Arthashastra
- **Arthashastra** (Kautilya) — rediscovered at **Mysore** in **1905** by **R. Shamasastry**; published **1909**
- **15 books** called **adhikaranas**
- Subject — **administration, economy, espionage and taxation**

## SEC 1:33:1 | Indica of Megasthenes
### Indica
- ★ **Indica** — written by **Megasthenes**
- Pataliputra called **Palibothra** (also Polibrotha)
- Society in **seven classes**; endogamy and hereditary occupations; not the fourfold varna scheme
- Claimed **no slavery** in India — contradicted by the **Arthashastra** and Ashokan edicts (which mention **dasas**)
- First foreign work to mention the **Andhras** — **30 fortified towns**, **1,00,000 infantry**, besides cavalry and elephants
  - Strabo and Arrian only preserved fragments; Deimachus was Antiochus I's envoy to Bindusara
### Kautilya
- Other names — **Chanakya** and **Vishnugupta** (not Pushyagupta)
- **Pushyagupta** — governor associated with the **Sudarshana** lake

## SEC 1:33:2 | Literary works
- ★ Number of **Puranas** — **18**
- **Vishnu Purana** — usually cited for Mauryan **king lists and genealogies**
- **Mudrarakshasa** (Sanskrit drama) — **Vishakhadatta**; overthrow of the **Nandas**, rise of **Chandragupta Maurya**
- **Mamulanar** (Sangam) — refers to the Mauryas' **southward push**

## SEC 1:33:3 | Buddhist & Jain texts
| Source | Subject |
|---|---|
| **Parishishtaparvan** | **Chandragupta's Jain end** |
| Puranas | genealogies |
| Divyavadana | Ashokan legends |
| Mahavamsa / Dipavamsa | Buddhism in Lanka |

## SEC 1:33:4 | Inscriptions & archaeology
- **James Prinsep** — deciphered the Ashokan edicts in **1837**
- **Greek-Aramaic** edict — **Shar-i-Kuna (Kandahar)**; Girnar/Junagadh is linked with **Rudradaman**'s inscription
- **Junagadh inscription** of Rudradaman I (c. **150 CE**) — governor **Pushyagupta** built the **Sudarshana lake** under Chandragupta Maurya
- **80-pillared hall** of Pataliputra — **Kumrahar** near Patna

## SEC 1:33:5 | Exam corner
- Ashoka called his edicts **Dhammalipi**

## SEC 1:33:7 | Indica of Megasthenes → observations and errors
- ★ First to mention **forts in Andhra** — **Megasthenes** (Indika, 4th c. BC): **30 fortified towns**, a lakh of infantry, **2,000 cavalry**, **1,000 elephants**
- ★ **Polibrotha / Palibothra** — **Pataliputra** (Patna)
- ★ **Indica** — states **no slavery** in India; gives **city administration** of the Mauryas; mentions society in **seven classes**
- ★ Pataliputra administration — **six committees** of **five members** each; town officials called **Astynomoi**
- ★ Author of Indica — **Megasthenes**

# ROW 1:34 | Chandragupta Maurya and Bindusara - conquests, treaty with Seleucus, Chanakya
## SEC 1:34:0 | Names & titles
### Chanakya
- ★ **Chanakya** — also **Kautilya** and **Vishnugupta** (childhood name Vishnugupta); son of sage **Chanak**; guided Chandragupta; associated with **Taxila**
- Arthashastra names its author **Kautilya**, one verse calls him **Vishnugupta**
- Taranath and Jain tradition — Chanakya continued as **Bindusara**'s minister
### Sandrokottos
- ★ **Sir William Jones** — identified **Sandrocottus / Sandrokottos** with **Chandragupta Maurya** (**1793**)
### Bindusara
- Son of Chandragupta and **Durdhara**; birth name **Simhasena**
- **Amitraghata** — "**slayer of foes**"; Greek form **Amitrochates**
- Ashoka's mother — **Subhadrangi**

## SEC 1:34:1 | Chandragupta takes Magadha
- Took the Magadhan throne in **321 BC** from **Dhana Nanda**, with **Chanakya**'s help
- **Mamulanar** — **Tamil poet** on the Mauryas' southward expansion (not a Greek chronicler)
- **Justin** (Roman historian) — called Chandragupta's army a "**gang of dacoits**"
- Chandragupta's army (classical accounts) — **6,00,000 infantry** and **30,000 cavalry**

## SEC 1:34:2 | Treaty with Seleucus (c. 305 BCE)
- ★ **Megasthenes** — ambassador of **Seleucus**
- Ceded territories — **Aria, Arachosia, Paropanisadai, Gedrosia** (not **Bactria**)
- Chandragupta gave Seleucus **500 war elephants**
- Seleucus's daughter **Helena** married **Chandragupta** (not the reverse)
- Megasthenes' seven classes — **philosophers, farmers, herdsmen, artisans, soldiers, overseers, councillors** (no **merchants**)

## SEC 1:34:3 | Chandragupta & Jainism
- Jain teacher — **Bhadrabahu** (sixth Jain Thera)
- Abdicated, became an ascetic; died by **Sallekhana** at **Shravanabelagola**
- **Twelve-year famine** — split into **Digambaras** (Bhadrabahu) and **Svetambaras** (**Sthulabhadra**)
