# ROW 5:36 | Propulsion and future vehicles - cryogenic engine, semi-cryogenic, reusable launch vehicle, scramjet
## SEC 5:36:0 | Rocket propellants
| Category | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Solid** | high initial thrust; normally cannot be throttled or stopped |
| **Hypergolic** | ignite on contact; useful for spacecraft manoeuvring; highly toxic |
| **Hybrid** | can be stopped or restarted |
| **Cryogenic** | highly efficient but complex |
- Hydrazine-family + **N2O4** — hypergolic
- **UH25 + N2O4** — earth-storable liquid; **refined kerosene + LOX** — semi-cryogenic; **LH2 + LOX** — cryogenic
## SEC 5:36:1 | Cryogenic technology
### Uses
- ★ Cryogenic engines — **rocket technology** (space launch vehicles)
- ★ Used in the **space shuttle**
- Mainly in rocket **upper stages**; unsuited to military missiles (low-density LH2 needs huge tanks, cannot be stored long)
### Basics
- ★ **Cryogenics** — Greek **'Kryos'** (frost); temperatures below about **–150°C**
- ★ Cryogenic engine — uses a cryogenic **fuel and oxidiser**; super-cooled liquids like **liquid hydrogen** and **oxygen**
- **LH2** (fuel) stored at about **-253°C**; **LOX** (oxidiser) at about **-183°C**; exhaust only **water vapour**
- Specific impulse about **450 seconds** — highest of the propellant categories
### Russian deal
- **1991 Glavkosmos** agreement to sell engines and technology; **US MTCR-based sanctions (1992)** stopped transfer; Russia supplied **seven engines** only
## SEC 5:36:2 | Indian cryogenic programme
- **CE-20 bootstrap-mode** start — engine starts on its own propellant flow, no external gas; supports **in-space restart**
## SEC 5:36:3 | ISRO engines
- Cryogenic Upper Stage Project — began **1994**, cost **₹300 crore**; full cryogenic stage tested **2006**
- **SE2000** — **semi-cryogenic** (refined kerosene + LOX, not LOX + LH2)
- SE2000 — for **LVM3-SC** (replacing the **L110**) and for the **NGLV**
- NGLV **'Soorya'** — about **20,000 kg** to LEO (not 10,000 kg)
## SEC 5:36:4 | Private engines
- **Agnilet** (Agnikul Cosmos) — world's first **single-piece 3D-printed semi-cryogenic** engine
- Agnilet flown **2024** from **Dhanush**, Sriharikota — India's first **private launch pad**
- **EVEREST** (Astrobase, August 2026) — India's first private **800 kN full-flow staged combustion LOX-methane** engine
- **LOX-methane** — cleaner and cheaper; methane can be produced from **Martian resources**
- Skyroot **Vikram-1** — India's first private orbital rocket; reached orbit on Mission **Aagaman**; three solid + one liquid stage; **290 kg** to 500 km SSO

# ROW 5:38 | Navigation satellites - IRNSS/NavIC constellation and GAGAN; applications and limitations
## SEC 5:38:0 | GPS vs NavIC
- **GPS** (US DoD, **1973**) — **24** satellites in **6** orbits at about **20,200 km**; each circles Earth twice a day
- **NavIC** — **seven** satellites: **three** geostationary + **four** inclined geosynchronous at about **29°**
- NavIC original signals — **L5 and S** bands (L1 and L2 are GPS)
- NavIC — regional: India + about **1,500 km** beyond its borders; GPS is global
- Nodal authority for NavIC and GAGAN — **Satellite Navigation Programme Office** under ISRO
## SEC 5:38:1 | NavIC basics
- **1 microsecond** atomic-clock error ≈ **300 metres** position error
- NavIC (formerly IRNSS) fully operational — **2018**; navigation satellites launched since **2013**
- Genesis — **Kargil (1999)** GPS denial
## SEC 5:38:2 | Satellites & clock troubles
- **IRNSS-1** series — launched by **PSLV** from **Sriharikota**
- **NVS-01** — **GSLV-F12**, May 2023; first with an **indigenous rubidium atomic clock**
- **NVS-02** — **GSLV-F15**, January 2025; **100th** Sriharikota launch; valve glitch left it in the wrong orbit
- First-generation IRNSS rubidium clocks — Swiss firm **SpectraTime** (**Switzerland**); ISRO switched to indigenous clocks in **2018**
- **IRNSS-1F** last clock failed **13 March 2026** — only **three** PNT-capable satellites left (**IRNSS-1B, 1I, NVS-01**), below the **four** needed
## SEC 5:38:3 | Applications
- **Elena Geo Systems** — India's first NavIC-based device for precise directions on infrastructure projects
- MoRTH **AIS-140** — NavIC-enabled trackers compulsory in **public transport vehicles** (not all private cars)
- **Snapdragon** chipsets support NavIC
- GPS uses — **timing** (power, telecom, banking), surveying, fleet tracking, **precision farming**, **disaster management**
- Reconfigured **IRNSS-1A** — broadcasts **cyclone, tsunami and high-wave** warnings to fishermen
## SEC 5:38:4 | GAGAN
- **GAGAN** — **GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation**
- Developed by **ISRO** and **Airports Authority of India** (not DRDO)
- GAGAN payloads — **GSAT-8, GSAT-10, GSAT-15**
- **SBAS** — broadcasts real-time corrections for **orbit, clock and ionospheric delay**; improves accuracy, integrity, availability

# ROW 5:39 | Earth observation - IRS series, Cartosat, Resourcesat, Oceansat, RISAT, EOS series and applications
## SEC 5:39:0 | Passive vs active sensors
- **SAR** — **active** sensor: sends its own microwave signal, reads the echo; works through **clouds** and in **darkness**
- **LiDAR** (laser) — **active**; sends its own laser pulse
- Multispectral cameras, infrared radiometers, visible cameras — **passive** (record reflected sunlight or emitted heat)
## SEC 5:39:1 | Remote sensing basics
### Basics
- ★ Remote sensing uses **electromagnetic waves** (part of the EM spectrum)
- ★ Remote sensing — detects physical characteristics of an area by measuring **reflected and emitted radiation** at a distance (satellite or aircraft)
- Information about the Earth **without physical contact**, via reflected or emitted EM radiation
### Techniques
| Technique | Use |
|---|---|
| **Hyperspectral** | identifies materials/chemicals with **hundreds of narrow bands** |
| **NDVI** | vegetation health from **red vs near-infrared** reflectance |
| **InSAR** | ground deformation |
| **LiDAR** | laser ranging for elevation and canopy height |
| **Thermal infrared** | heat of volcanic eruptions, forest fires, urban heat |
### Orbits & NavIC
- **Sun-synchronous** polar orbit — about **500-900 km**; same local solar time (uniform lighting)
- After **IRNSS-1F**'s last rubidium clock stopped (**13 March 2026**, 3 days after its 10-year design life) — only **three** PNT-capable satellites (IRNSS-1B, 1I, NVS-01); a PNT fix needs at least **four**
- **NVS-02** never reached its circular orbit (Jan 2025)
## SEC 5:39:2 | IRS milestones
