# ROW 3:73 | International trade - composition and direction of India's exports and imports; trade deficit
## SEC 3:73:0 | Trade terms
### Theory and strategy
- **Absolute advantage** — **Adam Smith**
- **Comparative advantage** — **Ricardo** (lower opportunity cost)
- **Export promotion** — outward-looking; **import substitution** — inward-looking
### Types of trade
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intra-industry trade** | Import and export of the same product (e.g. cars) |
| Inter-industry trade | Exchange of different products (comparative advantage) |
| **Entrepot trade** | Import, store/trade, then re-export |
| Dumping | Exporting below home-market price or cost |
### Trade remedies and controls
- **Countervailing duty** — offsets **foreign subsidies**
- Anti-dumping duty — imports below home-market price
- Rules of origin — decide the national source
- **Tariff** — tax on imports; import quota — quantity restriction; import licensing — administrative restriction
### Institutions
- **Department of Commerce** — frames and monitors Foreign Trade Policy
- SEZ duty-free enclave — treated as **foreign territory** for trade and tariff purposes

## SEC 3:73:1 | Terms of trade & exchange rate
### Devaluation
- ★ Devaluation — **imports costly, exports cheaper**; increase of exports, decrease of imports
- Immediate effect — exports cheaper abroad, imports dearer at home
- **J-curve** — trade balance **first worsens** (import bills rise), improves after volumes adjust
- **Marshall–Lerner condition** — devaluation improves trade balance if sum of export and import demand elasticities **> 1**
### Terms and concepts
- **Net terms of trade** = export price index / import price index × 100
- Gross terms of trade — import quantity / export quantity
- ★ Payment of foreign trade — related to **Balance of Payment**
- **Closed economy** — neither exports nor imports; open economy has both
- **SEZs** promote exports, investment, jobs, infrastructure **because** of simplified procedures and business-friendly environment

# ROW 3:75 | Balance of Payments - structure, current and capital account, convertibility, forex reserves
## SEC 3:75:0 | BoT vs BoP
### Definitions
- **BoP** — complete record of all economic transactions between residents and rest of world over a period (usually a year)
- **Balance of Trade** — **goods only**; not the complete record
- **Current account** — goods, services, transfers, income; includes the **Balance of Trade**
- **Capital account** — cross-border capital movements; **FDI**, foreign loans, portfolio investment (FDI is **not** current account)
- **CAD** — payments exceed receipts from goods, services, transfers, net income
### Framework
- **IMF (BPM6)** — current, capital and financial accounts; **RBI** combines capital and financial as "capital account"
- BoP identity — current + capital + financial balance + errors and omissions = **zero**
- Positive overall balance **adds to reserves**; negative draws them down (insufficient reserves → BoP crisis)
- **FY24** external recovery — moderation in merchandise exports, **sharp fall in imports**

## SEC 3:75:1 | Structure of BoP
- Current-account balance = exports − imports of goods and services + **net primary income** + **net secondary income**
- **FCNR(B)** deposits and **ECBs** — **temporary** inflows; **FDI** supports a self-sustaining capital surplus

| Item | BoP placement |
|---|---|
| **Software services exports** | Current account (services) |
| Foreign loans | Capital account |
| Remittances | Current transfers |
| Crude oil imports | Merchandise trade |

## SEC 3:75:2 | CAD trend
### Concepts
- ★ Goods trade 14.2% of GDP (1990-91) → **almost three times** by 2011-12; 30.4% (2023), 29.7% (2024)
- Shipping, banking, tourism, software — **non-factor services** (invisibles, with factor income and transfers)
- **Accommodating transactions** — reserve changes, official borrowing; fill gap left by autonomous transactions
- **Temporary global slowdown** — cyclical cause of CAD; oil/gold imports, weak competitiveness, savings-investment gap are structural
### Record
- CA **surplus** three consecutive years — **2001-02 to 2003-04**
- Q4 2023-24 surplus — surge in **services exports**
- CAD peak — about **4.8% of GDP** in **FY13** (taper tantrum); 1990-91 crisis about 3.1-3.2%
- H1 FY26 CAD — **0.8%** of GDP
- 2013 taper tantrum — Morgan Stanley's **'Fragile Five'**
### Vulnerability and support
- **Hot money** dependence increases vulnerability; adequate reserves, low short-term debt are safeguards
- **Extended Fund Facility**, Stand-By Arrangement — IMF windows; RBI swaps and SRVAs are not IMF instruments

## SEC 3:75:3 | Cushions & weak spots
- FY25 record services surplus **$188.8 bn** — financed about **two-thirds** of merchandise deficit
- FY25 remittances — **$135.4 bn**, about **3.5% of GDP**; India world's largest recipient
- Remittances — **counter-cyclical**; no debt or repayment obligation

## SEC 3:75:4 | Correcting a BoP deficit
### Recording and shocks
- Residents buying foreign assets — capital-account **debit**; sale of Indian assets to foreigners — **credit**
- US Fed rate hikes, strong dollar — hot money leaves emerging markets; **rupee weakens**
### Correction tools
- **Expenditure-switching** — **devaluation**, import tariffs, export incentives, import substitution, higher **gold import duty**
- **Expenditure-reducing** — **tight monetary policy** (higher interest rates)
- **IMF** — lender of last resort when reserves cannot cover BoP deficit
- Forex reserve components — **FCA**, **gold**, **SDRs**, **reserve position in IMF** (not "fiscal deficit deposits")

## SEC 3:75:5 | IMF facilities
- IMF core function — **surveillance** of global and member economies
- **RCF** — concessional help to low-income countries

## SEC 3:75:6 | Convertibility & law

## SEC 3:75:7 | Exam corner

## SEC 3:75:8 | Balance of Payments — definition, BoP vs BoT
- ★ BoP — better picture than BoT; BoT covers only visible goods, BoP all transactions
- ★ BoP — systematic record of all import/export transactions over a period, normally **a year**
- ★ BoP — complete record of all economic transactions with the rest of the world
- ★ IMF main function — help solve **balance of payments problems**
- ★ Devaluation — to fix the **balance of trade**

## SEC 3:75:9 | Balance of Payments — current and capital account, recording rules
### Accounts
- ★ BoP — **current account** and **capital account**
- ★ BoP includes visible trade, invisible trade, borrowings
- ★ **Capital account** — **foreign loans**, **FDI**, **portfolio investment**
- ★ **Private remittances** — current account

# ROW 3:76 | Exchange rate systems and rupee movement; currency internationalisation
## SEC 3:76:0 | Four terms
- **Flexible** exchange rate — set by **demand and supply**; **fixed** — set by government, RBI intervenes

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Devaluation** | Official **lowering** of value (fixed regime) |
| **Revaluation** | Official **raising** of value (fixed regime) |
| **Depreciation** | Market-driven fall (floating) |
| Floating rate | Market-set value |

- $1 = ₹60 → $1 = ₹65 under floating rate — **rupee depreciation**

## SEC 3:76:1 | Key terms
### Quotes and market terms
- **Exchange rate** — price of one currency in terms of another
- **Direct quote** — domestic currency per one unit of foreign currency (e.g. ₹95 = $1)
- ★ **Arbitrage** — buy currency where cheaper, simultaneously sell where dearer
- Hedging — covering exchange-rate risk (forward contracts); speculation — open position on expected moves
- **Depreciation/appreciation** — market-driven, flexible regime (not official changes)
- **Managed float** — market determines rate; central bank intervenes when necessary (forex or interest rates)
### NEER and REER
- Rise in **NEER** — **rupee appreciation**; imports cheaper, exports less competitive
- **REER** — NEER adjusted for **relative inflation differentials**; better competitiveness indicator
- High/rising REER — **overvalued** rupee; reduces export competitiveness
### Determinants
- ★ **Inflation** — lowers rupee's value; does not improve the exchange rate
- **PPP** — long-run rate linked to **inflation differential**
- **Imported inflation** limits trade gains after depreciation (also costlier oil imports, ECB repayments)
- Large **capital inflows** — currency **appreciates** (linked with **Dutch disease**)
- Hawkish stance / repo hike — supports rupee by attracting **carry-trade** capital
- **Impossible Trinity** — fixed exchange rate, free capital flows, independent monetary policy
- Deliberate undervaluation (e.g. China) — **currency manipulation**

## SEC 3:76:3 | Devaluation effect (A-R key)
- **Import cover** — months of imports reserves can pay for
- Devaluation — **expenditure-switching**; domestic goods cheaper for foreigners, imports costlier at home
- Devaluation **lowers** export prices in foreign currency (does not raise them); tends to improve balance of trade
- Devaluation does **not necessarily** improve trade balance — import costs rise too
- High **inflation** and large **fiscal deficit** — **weaken** a currency
