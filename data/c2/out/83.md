# ROW 4:21 | Climate change - evidence, natural and anthropogenic causes, global warming, IPCC assessments, impacts
## SEC 4:21:0 | Evidence of warming
### Temperature
- **2024** — hottest year on record, about **1.55°C** above pre-industrial
- **2025** — warmest **La Niña** year
- India warmed about **0.7°C** since 1900 — **less** than global land areas (about **1.59°C**)
### Ice, sea and Sun
- **Gangotri Glacier** — retreated about **1,700 m** since **1935**; cause: global warming
- Global sea level up about **11 cm** since **1993**
- Sea-level rise comes from **thermal expansion** and **melting ice**; higher **snow albedo** is not a cause (only reflects more radiation)
- Solar output: **no net increase** since **1978** (satellites), so recent warming is not a brighter Sun

## SEC 4:21:1 | Natural causes
### Ice ages
- ★ **Great Ice Age** — **Pleistocene** (major glaciations) and **Pliocene** (preceded it, onset of cooling)
- ★ Glaciation of **peninsular India** — **Pleistocene** Ice Age
- ★ **Little Ice Age** — PYQ key: **1650–1870 A.D.**; much colder winters in Europe and North America than in the 20th century
- **Little Ice Age** also placed approximately **1500–1850**
### Astronomical (Milankovitch) theory
- ★ Propounded in the **1920s** by **Milutin Milankovitch**: **eccentricity**, **axial tilt** and **precession** of Earth's orbit drive climate (orbital forcing)
- ★ **Solar irradiance** is not part of the astronomical theories

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Precession** | wobble of the rotational axis |
| Eccentricity | orbital shape |
| Obliquity | axial tilt |

### Volcanoes
- **Sulphur dioxide** and ash form aerosols that reflect sunlight — short-term cooling

## SEC 4:21:2 | IPCC basics
- **195** members; assesses existing literature, does no research of its own
- **2007 Nobel Peace Prize** — shared with **Al Gore**

| Working Group | Subject |
|---|---|
| I | physical science basis |
| II | impacts, adaptation and vulnerability |

## SEC 4:21:3 | Reports & findings
- **SR1.5** — global CO2 emissions to fall about **45%** from **2010** levels by **2030**; **net zero** by **2050**
- **AR6 Synthesis Report** — emissions must peak **before 2025**
- **UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2025** — current policies lead to about **2.8°C**; full NDC implementation about **2.3–2.5°C**
- **CMIP7** scenarios drop the worst-case **SSP5-8.5** pathway; world near **2.8°C** by 2100

## SEC 4:21:4 | Impacts
- Ocean acidity up about **30%** since the Industrial Revolution
- AR6: up to **25%** of mountain species may face extinction
- Warmer, wetter conditions widen the range of **vector-borne diseases** (**malaria**, **dengue**)
- Tipping points: **1.2°C** warm-water **coral reef** threshold already crossed
- Beyond about **2°C** parts of the **Amazon** could shift to savanna and become a carbon **source** (not a sink)

## SEC 4:21:5 | Geoengineering
- ★ **Cirrus cloud thinning** and **stratospheric sulphate aerosol injection** — proposed to reduce **global warming**
- **Stratospheric aerosol injection** — copies the cooling of **volcanic eruptions**: SO2 forms reflective **sulphuric-acid aerosols**
  - main risk: **termination shock** if stopped suddenly
- **Cirrus cloud thinning** — seeds high cirrus clouds so more **outgoing heat escapes** (less insulating effect)
- **Marine cloud brightening** — sea-salt spray acts as **cloud condensation nuclei**

## SEC 4:21:6 | Exam corner
- **Net zero** — human-caused emissions balanced by **removals** (natural sinks or technological capture)
- **2021 Physics Nobel** — **Syukuro Manabe** and **Klaus Hasselmann** (with Giorgio Parisi) for physical modelling of Earth's climate
- **COP31** — **Türkiye**, **November 2026**
- AP: **Uppada** (Kakinada region) — **coastal erosion**; **Palnadu**, **Kurnool**, **Anantapur** — heatwaves

## SEC 4:21:7 | Evidence of warming — data: warmest years, glaciers, sea level, sea ice
- ★ Global warming (IPCC) increases frequency and severity of: glacier **melting**, **sea-level** rise, **floods** and **droughts**, **storm** intensity — all of these

## SEC 4:21:11 | Impacts of climate change → effects
- ★ **Milutin Milankovich** (Serbian, 1920s) — shifting of Earth on its axis (eccentricity, axial tilt, precession) as a factor in climate change

- **Salinity reduces evaporation** (APPSC GS 2018); evaporation raises salinity

# ROW 4:24 | Ocean movements - waves, tides (types and causes), ocean currents of the world
## SEC 4:24:0 | Waves vs tides vs currents
| Movement | What it is | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **Waves** | wind-driven oscillations | mainly transfer **energy** |
| **Tides** | periodic rise and fall of sea level, **once or twice a day** | caused by **gravity of the Moon and Sun**; **tidal energy** |
| **Currents** | continuous flow | deflected by the **Coriolis effect** |

- Storm surges — irregular, weather-driven (not tides)
- **Tidal energy** — **barrages** across estuaries (tidal range) and **underwater turbines** (tidal stream)
- **Coriolis effect** — deflects currents to the **right** in the Northern Hemisphere
  - westerlies drive mid-latitude currents; coastlines redirect them; floor relief steers them

## SEC 4:24:1 | Waves & tsunamis
### Wave terms
- ★ **Wavelength** — distance between two successive points of the same phase; crest to crest
- **Wave height** — crest to trough; **period** — time between crests
- **Swells** — slow, steady, **long-period** waves from **distant storms**
- Wave **breaks** when water depth is **less than half its wavelength** (friction slows it)
- **Rip current** — narrow, fast channel flowing **away from the shore**
### Special waves
- **Kallakkadal** (Kerala coast) — swell surge from long-period swells of **southern Indian Ocean** storms
- **Tsunami** — Japanese for "**harbour wave**"; set off by sudden displacement of the sea floor
- **RTGS Vijayawada** — relays satellite **cyclone, high-wave and fish-shoal** alerts to fishermen

## SEC 4:24:2 | Spring vs neap tides
- **Neap tide** — **first or third quarter** Moon (pulls partly cancel)
- **Spring tide** — **new moon** and **full moon**

## SEC 4:24:3 | Tide causes & types
### Causes and timing
- **Moon** — greater tidal influence than the Sun
- **Direct tide** — bulge facing the Moon; opposite bulge linked with **centrifugal force**
- High tides recur about every **12 hours 25 minutes** (lunar influence)
- **Perigean tides** — unusually high; Moon **closest** to Earth
- **Tidal currents** — tidal water channelled into bays and estuaries
### Types of tide
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Spring** | **highest tidal range** |
| Semi-diurnal | two highs, two lows a day |
| Diurnal | one high, one low a day |
| Mixed | unequal heights |

### Tidal range places
- ★ Gulfs with **wide fronts and narrow rears** get the highest tides (funnelling), e.g. **Bay of Fundy**
- **Bay of Fundy** (Canada) — world's **greatest tidal range**
- **Gulf of Khambhat** — tidal range about **5–10 m**; tidal-energy potential about **7,000 MW**
### Currents
- Drivers: **density differences**, solar heating, wind, gravity, Coriolis force
- Near the equator, solar heating **expands** water — slight slope down which water flows
- Cold water sinking at the poles moves **slowly** towards the equator
- Low and middle latitudes: warm currents along **eastern** coasts of continents
### El Nino and warning systems
- **El Nino** — "**the Christ child**"; recurs about every **3–7 years**
- IMD: El Nino when central equatorial Pacific is at least **0.5°C** warmer for five consecutive overlapping three-month periods
- **Super El Nino** — anomaly above **+2.0°C** in the central Pacific
- **SAMUDRA** app — **INCOIS**; PFZ advisories and marine hazard alerts
- **ITEWS** — run by **INCOIS, Hyderabad**; NIOT, Chennai runs the **OMNI** buoys

## SEC 4:24:4 | Gyres
### Gyres and counter current
- Gyres turn **clockwise** in the Northern Hemisphere, **anticlockwise** in the Southern
- **Equatorial Counter Current** — flows **west to east** between North and South Equatorial Currents; through the calm **doldrums**
### Sargasso Sea
- ★ **Sargasso Sea** — has **no coastline**; bounded by currents, not land
- Boundaries: **North Atlantic Drift** (north), **Gulf Stream** (west), **Canary Current** (east), **North Equatorial Current** (south)
- Calm, warm, salty; floating **Sargassum** weed; North Atlantic
### Currents and places
- **Kuroshio** — warm, North Pacific; Humboldt and Falkland — cold, southern hemisphere
- **North Atlantic Drift** (extension of the Gulf Stream) — mild NW Europe; ice-free ports of **Norway** and **Murmansk**
- **Humboldt (Peru)** — Pacific, west coast of South America
- **Agulhas** — off south-east Africa; **Benguela** — off south-west Africa; **Gulf Stream** — North Atlantic
- **Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich** — tracked an eastward **Kelvin wave** (warm water moving east), an early El Nino signal

## SEC 4:24:7 | El Nino vs La Nina
### Winds and upwelling
- **Trade winds** drive equatorial currents **westward**; westerlies drive drifts eastward
- Cold, nutrient-rich **upwelling** supports fisheries off **Peru** and **Somalia** (Somali/Findlater system)
- **La Nina** — trade winds **stronger** than usual; strong eastern Pacific upwelling; usually good rain over India
- **Bjerknes feedback** (El Nino) — weaker easterlies let warm water move **east**, weakening the trades further
### Monsoon and indices
- El Nino usually **delays and weakens** the Indian monsoon
- **SOI** = **Tahiti** minus **Darwin**; sustained negative SOI indicates El Nino
- May 2026: IMD warned a developing **super El Nino** could **weaken** the 2026 SW Monsoon
- IMD criterion: at least **0.5°C** warmer for **five** consecutive overlapping three-month periods (central equatorial Pacific)

# ROW 4:25 | Ocean-atmosphere interaction - El Nino, La Nina, ENSO, Indian Ocean Dipole, Walker circulation
## SEC 4:25:0 | El Nino basics
- **El Nino** — abnormal warming of the **central and eastern** tropical Pacific; oceanic part of ENSO
- Name — "**little boy**" or "**Christ child**"; appears around **Christmas**
- Recurs about every **3–7 years**; not every year
- Off Peru, cold nutrient-rich **upwelling stops** — fisheries collapse
- El Nino and La Nina — **climate patterns**, not storms
- El Nino **weakens** the **Walker circulation** and shifts it east (less convection over India); La Nina strengthens it
- **MJO** convection rises to about **10 km**

## SEC 4:25:1 | Bjerknes feedback
- El Nino onset: trade winds **weaken or reverse**; warm western Pacific water shifts east
- Air descends over the Indian Ocean — **high pressure**; delayed or weak Indian monsoon

## SEC 4:25:2 | El Nino vs La Nina
- ★ **La Nina** — intense cold in north India, **January 2012**
- **El Nino** — weak/below-normal Indian monsoon; **La Nina** — normal to above-normal
- La Nina: **Australia and Indonesia** — heavy rain and floods; colder North India winters; more severe Bay of Bengal cyclones
- **INCOIS** study: El Nino may **reduce** post-monsoon Bay of Bengal cyclones

## SEC 4:26:3 | Coral basics
### Corals and zooxanthellae
- Reef corals live in symbiosis with **zooxanthellae**; grow about **30°N–30°S**, water above about **20°C**
- Zooxanthellae give **food and colour**; coral gives **shelter and nutrients** (mutualism)
- Coral reefs lie around **25°N–25°S** (to about 30°); among the **richest** ecosystems; grow from polyp skeletons
### Conditions and roles
- **Heavy sediment** and freshwater at muddy river mouths **inhibit** coral growth
- Reefs **break storm waves** and prevent coastal erosion; nursery grounds, fisheries, tourism
- "**Rainforests of the Sea**" (APPSC 2024) — because of **species richness**, not wave-breaking (both true; R not the reason)
### Bleaching
- **Bleaching** — polyps expel **zooxanthellae**, lose pigments; stressed, not necessarily dead
- Contributors: **pollution**, extreme **low tides**, **ocean acidification**; not more carbonate
