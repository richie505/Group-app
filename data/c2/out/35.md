# ROW 1:271 | Post-independence consolidation - land reforms, tribal and border policy, national integration
## SEC 1:271:0 | Land reform measures
### Background
- **Prakasam Committee** (**1938**) — recommended **abolition of intermediaries**
- **Kumarappa Committee** (**1949**) — set the land reform agenda; Phase I of land reform **1947–70**
- **First Constitutional Amendment** — shielded **zamindari abolition** measures from legal challenge
### Measures and results
- **Abolition of intermediaries** — most successful reform; brought **two crore tenants** into direct contact with the State
- Telangana: **Jagir** and **Sarfekhas** lands became **Diwani (ryotwari)** lands
- **1967** extension of the Andhra Inams Act to Telangana — **struck down** by the High Court
- **Operation Barga** (1977) — **West Bengal** (not Kerala); exception to failed tenancy reform
- Ceiling guidelines **1972** — unit = the **family**, not the individual
- **Land consolidation** — success in **Punjab, Haryana, western UP**
- **Benami transfers** — land in others' names to defeat ceilings; oral leases and exemptions also weakened reform
- Failure on ceilings and tenancy — contributed to the **Naxalbari revolt (1967)**
### Andhra Pradesh
- **AP (Andhra Area) Tenancy Act, 1956** — written lease deeds; minimum lease **six years**; in force **1961**
- **AP Land Reforms (Ceiling) Act** — **1973**, after Land Reforms Bill under CM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**; ceiling **10–27 acres wet** or **35–54 acres dry** per family unit
- First residential school (**1971**) — **Sarvail**, Nalgonda, under P.V. Narasimha Rao
- **Rs 2 per kg rice** scheme — **N.T. Rama Rao**, **1983** (with subsidised housing, Janata cloth)
- Planned **AP New Tenancy Act, 2024** — tenants get rights over the **crop** (not land), entitlement cards, crop loans

# ROW 2:0 | Constitutional evolution - Regulating Act 1773 to the Government of India Act 1935 (act-by-act provisions)
## SEC 2:0:0 | Company era Acts
### Before 1773
- **Act of 1767** — Company to pay British Government **£400,000** a year
- Parliament intervened in Bengal in **1772** — **famine**, falling revenue, Company's financial crisis
### Regulating Act, 1773
- Governor-General of Bengal (**Warren Hastings** — first) with a **four-member** Executive Council
- **Supreme Court at Calcutta** (1774) — one Chief Justice (**Sir Elijah Impey**) + **three** judges
- Banned **private trade** and **bribes** by Company officials
- Did NOT abolish the **Court of Directors** (survived until the 1858 Act)
### Pitt's India Act, 1784 and after
- **Pitt's India Act, 1784** — **Dual Control**: **Board of Control** (political), **Court of Directors** (commercial); Council cut to **three**
  - territories called "**British possessions in India**"; Company subordinate to the State
- **Act of 1786** — Governor-General (**Cornwallis**) may **override his Council**; may also act as **Commander-in-Chief** (two separate powers)
### Charter Acts
| Act | Key provisions |
|---|---|
| **1793** | Board of Control salaries charged to **Indian revenues**; override power extended to all future GGs and Presidency Governors; more control over Bombay and Madras |
| **1813** | trade monopoly ended **except tea and China trade**; **missionaries** admitted; **Rs 1 lakh** a year for education |
| **1833** (Saint Helena Act) | monopoly **completely** ended; Company **purely administrative**; created **Governor-General of India**; **Law Member** (**Macaulay**); **Law Commission** under Macaulay (origin of Law Ministry); legislation centralised in GG-in-Council; final step to centralisation |
| **1853** (last Charter Act) | **six** legislative members added; legislative separated from executive; territories held **in trust for the Crown**; **open competition** put into operation |
- Order: Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act 1784 → Charter Act 1793 → Charter Act 1813
- ★ The Charter Act of **1813** being called the "Magna Carta" — marked the **not-correct** statement
- 1833 Act only **attempted** open competition — not implemented
- First **Governor-General of India** — **William Bentinck** (1833 Act created the post; he merely was first holder)
- First **Viceroy** — **Canning**; last Viceroy — **Mountbatten**
- First **Law Member** — **Macaulay** (not Impey)
### Later Acts (for comparison)
- **1892** Indian Councils Act — **indirect election**; recommendations from **universities, municipalities, chambers of commerce**
- Nomination of Indians began **1861**; separate electorates **1909**
- **Simon Commission** — appointed **1927** (two years early); all **seven** members British; boycotted

## SEC 2:0:1 | Crown rule: 1858 and 1861
### Government of India Act, 1858
- Transferred rule from the Company to the **Crown**; Governor-General became **Viceroy**
- Crown's powers via **Secretary of State for India** + **15-member Council of India**
- **Board of Control** and **Court of Directors** abolished
### Indian Councils Act, 1861 and courts
- Restored legislative powers of **Bombay** and **Madras**
- **Portfolio system**; Viceroy's **ordinance** — at most **six months**
- First three Indians nominated by **Canning** — **Raja of Benaras**, **Maharaja of Patiala**, **Sir Dinkar Rao**
- **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** — criticised absence of Indians in the council (lesson of 1857); NOT one of the nominees
- **Indian High Courts Act** — **1861**; High Courts of Calcutta, Bombay, Madras — **1862**
- **1909** Act — separate electorates for Muslims (not transfer to Crown)

## SEC 2:0:2 | 1892 vs 1909
### 1892
- **Indian Councils Act, 1892** — first **indirect election**, via recommendations of universities, municipalities, chambers of commerce
### 1909 (Morley-Minto)
- **John Morley** — Secretary of State; **Lord Minto** — Viceroy
- **Separate electorate** for Muslims; formal elected representation
- **Lord Minto** — "**Father of Communal Electorate**"
- Additional Central members raised to **60** (total **69**); official majority kept at Centre
- Indians first in the **Viceroy's** and **Governors' Executive Councils**; **S.P. Sinha** — first Indian Law Member
- Allowed **supplementary questions**
### Other Acts compared
- **Chamber of Princes** — 1919 Act (not 1909)
- **Rs 1 lakh** education grant — **Charter Act, 1813**
- **Provincial autonomy** — **1935** Act; provincial **dyarchy** — 1919 Act

## SEC 2:0:3 | GoI Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford)
- Basis — **Montagu's declaration**, **20 August 1917**
- Central legislature **bicameral** for the first time — **Council of State** (upper), **Legislative Assembly** (lower)
- **Provincial dyarchy**; first used the term "**responsible government**"
  - **Reserved** (Governor + Executive Council) — **police, finance, land revenue**
  - **Transferred** (Governor + Ministers) — education, agriculture, public health, local self-government
- Provided **High Commissioner for India in London**, a **Public Service Commission**, separate provincial budgets
- **Chamber of Princes** — 1919 Act (not 1935); **120** members; first Chancellor **Maharaja Ganga Singh of Bikaner**
- 1935 Act bicameral provinces (six) — **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces**; NOT **Punjab**
- 1935 Act residuary powers — **Governor-General** (under the Constitution: Parliament)

## SEC 2:0:4 | GoI Act 1935
### Basis and structure
- Based on the **Third Round Table Conference** and the **White Paper**
- **321 sections**, **14 parts**, **10 schedules**
### Federation and Centre
- Proposed **All-India Federation** and **Central Dyarchy** — neither implemented (no princely state joined; accession optional)
- Central legislature — **Federal Assembly** + **Council of States**
- **Defence** and **foreign affairs** — reserved subjects under the **Governor-General**
- **Section 42** — model for **Article 123** (ordinances)
- **Council of India** (1858) — **abolished**; Secretary of State given advisers
### Provinces and other changes
- Provincial dyarchy abolished; responsible ministries from **April 1937**
- Communal representation extended to **depressed classes, women, labour**
- Separation of **Burma** from India, **Sindh** from Bombay, **Orissa** from Bihar
- **Federal Court** (inaugurated **1 October 1937**) — first CJ **Sir Maurice Gwyer**; replaced by the Supreme Court in 1950
- **RBI** formed **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act, 1934**
- Carried into the Constitution — **federal scheme**, Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions, administrative details
### Related
- **Public Accounts Committee** — first set up **1921** (1919 Act)
- **Article 175** — Governor's address; NOT mandatory in every circumstance
- Provincial dyarchy introduced by — **1919 Act**

# ROW 2:1 | Indian Independence Act 1947; the Constituent Assembly - demand, composition, functioning
## SEC 2:1:0 | Indian Independence Act 1947
### The Act
- Ended **British sovereignty**; combined sovereignty with **partition**; quick transfer of power under **dominion status**
- 15 August 1947 — India a **Dominion**, British monarch as nominal head
- Governor-General (common or separate) to act on advice of **Dominion ministers**
- Constituent Assembly — **sovereign constitution-making body** and **Dominion legislature**; Central Legislative Assembly ceased
- Paramountcy lapsed — about **565** princely states theoretically free
- **Instrument of Accession** — ceded **defence, external affairs, communications** (not finance)
- **Kashmir** acceded — **26 October 1947**, after the tribal invasion
- Madras Assembly (four months before independence) — divide Presidency into **Andhra, Tamil, Karnataka, Kerala**
### The Assembly and the Constitution
- Elected on the **restricted franchise** of the 1935 Act — **tax, education, property** qualifications
- Strength fell from **389** to **299** — members from areas that became **Pakistan** withdrew
- ★ Constitution **adopted** by the **Constituent Assembly** (11th session) — **26 November 1949**; full effect **26 January 1950**
- **284** of 299 members signed — **24 January 1950**
- **26 January** — commemorates **Purna Swaraj** (Lahore Congress 1929, observed 1930)
- After 26 January 1950 — **Provisional Parliament** until elections of **1951–52**
- **Ambedkar** — "**grammar of anarchy**", closing speech **25 November 1949**
- ★ **Ambedkar** — called the Constitution a **sacred document**
- "**Patchwork**" criticism — largely **borrowed** from other constitutions
