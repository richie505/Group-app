# ROW 1:62 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya, Kumaragupta, Skandagupta; the Hunas and Gupta decline
## SEC 1:62:0 | Gupta line
### Rulers and dates
| Ruler | Period | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Sri Gupta** | c. 240–280 CE | **founder**; title **Maharaja** |
| Ghatotkacha | c. 280–319 CE | |
| **Chandragupta I** | 319–335 CE | started the **Gupta era (319–20 CE)** |
| **Samudragupta** | c. **335–375 CE** | |
| Chandragupta II | c. 380–413 CE | |
| **Vishnugupta** | c. 540–550 CE | **last** of the main Gupta line |
- Order: **Ramagupta → Chandragupta II → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta** (Ramagupta's reign brief)
### Titles and coins
- ★ First Gupta ruler with the title **Parama Bhagawat** — **Samudragupta**
- **Vikramaditya** — used by both **Chandragupta II** and **Skandagupta** (principal one: Chandragupta II)
- Chandragupta II also called **Devagupta** and **Devaraja**
- **Baladitya** — Narasimhagupta
- Gold coin with king **feeding a peacock** + **Kartikeya** — **Kumaragupta I**
### Sources
- **Damodarpur copper plates** — guilds could act as **banks**
- No Gupta coins/inscriptions in **western Malwa and Saurashtra after Skandagupta** — **loss of Gupta control** there

## SEC 1:62:1 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya
### Saka conquest
- Defeated Saka ruler **Rudrasimha III** — annexed **western Malwa and Gujarat**; **Ujjain** his second capital
- ★ After conquering the Sakas, introduced **silver** coins; Saka-model silver coins weighed about **33 grains**
- Saka annexation brought the empire into touch with **western sea ports**
- Title **Vikramaditya** — linked with victory over the **Sakas**
- **Udayagiri cave inscription** of **Virasena** (his minister) — records his Saka campaign
  - **Harishena** was **Samudragupta's** court poet (Allahabad Prashasti), not Chandragupta II's minister
- Title **Simhavikrama** — **lion-slayer** coins (his father: tiger-slayer coins)
### Marriage alliances
- ★ Daughter **Prabhavati (Gupta)** married **Rudrasena II** of the **Vakataka** dynasty; ruled the Vakataka kingdom as **regent** after his death
  - Extended Gupta influence into the **Deccan** without annexation
- **Kuberanaga** — a **Naga** princess
- **Ramagupta episode**: Ramagupta agreed to surrender queen **Dhruvadevi** to a Saka king; Chandragupta II killed the Saka king and Ramagupta and married her
### Court and visitors
- ★ **Kalidasa** — in the reign of **Chandragupta II**
- Traditional **Navaratnas** include **Kalidasa**, **Amarasimha**, **Varahamihira**; **Fa-Hien** was not one
- **Fa-Hien** — Buddhist pilgrim; did **not name** the reigning king
### Other facts filed here
- **Gwalior inscription** — **Mihirakula**; **Sondani** — Yashodharman; **Maukhari seal** — Sarvavarman; **Chausath Yogini** inscription — Devapala
- **Bindusara** extended Mauryan power into the **Deccan**; Chandragupta Maurya founded the empire
- **Nalanda Mahavihara** founded by **Kumaragupta I**; Harsha patronised Banabhatta; Skandagupta resisted the Hunas

## SEC 1:62:2 | Kumaragupta I vs Skandagupta
- **Kumaragupta I** — titles **Shakraditya** and **Mahendraditya**
- **Kumaragupta I** — founded **Nalanda University**
- **Skandagupta's** gold coins — **bow and arrow**, **Garuda** standard and **Lakshmi**
- **Bhitari pillar** (Ghazipur, UP) — **Skandagupta's** victories over the **Pushyamitras** and the **Hunas**
- **Skandagupta** — repulsed the Hunas **twice**; took title **Vikramaditya**
  - First Huna attack came late in Kumaragupta I's reign; repulsed by **Prince Skandagupta**
- **Kadambas of Banavasi** (Karnataka) — founded by **Mayurasharma**

## SEC 1:62:3 | Huna waves
- **Hunas** = **Hephthalites** / **White Huns**; came from Central Asia through the **Oxus valley** (mid-5th c.)
- **First** Huna attack (reign of Kumaragupta I) — repelled by **Prince Skandagupta**
- ★ **Toramana** — a **Huna**; led the **second** Huna invasion
- **Eran boar inscription** — Toramana's **first regnal year**; records Huna rule over **Eran**
- **Mihirakula** — **son** of Toramana; tyrant and iconoclast who **persecuted Buddhists**
- Mihirakula defeated by **Yashodharman of Malwa** (and Narasimhagupta Baladitya), **not Skandagupta**
- Yashodharman's victory pillars at **Sondani** (near Mandasor) — c. **532 CE**; commemorate victory over Mihirakula

## SEC 1:62:4 | Causes of decline
- Under **Skandagupta**: central control weakened; governors became **hereditary feudatories**
- **Land grants** encouraged the growth of **feudatories**
- Land grants reduced state **revenue** — weakened its ability to keep a professional army
- Loss of **western India** — reduced trade income, incl. **Roman silk trade** revenue
- Later Gupta gold coins **debased** — **less gold, more alloy**; coins gradually gave way to payments in land

## SEC 1:62:5 | Successor powers
- **Yashodharman's** victory over Mihirakula — rise of a **regional power**; further weakened Gupta authority (did not revive the empire)
| Successor power | Seat |
|---|---|
| **Maitrakas** | **Valabhi** |
| Aulikaras | Mandsaur |
| **Pushyabhutis** | Thanesar |
| Later Guptas | Magadha |
- **Maukharis**, **Maitrakas**, **Pushyabhutis** — Gupta successor powers in north and west; **Paramaras** of Malwa a **later** power

## SEC 1:62:6 | Exam corner
- **Huna** invasions, growth of feudatories, loss of western trade revenue — causes of decline; **Arab invasion was not**
- Gupta decline — move from a single empire to **regional kingdoms** and a **feudal order**; not a return to Mauryan centralisation

## SEC 1:62:7 | Gupta rulers → period (chronology)
- ★ Order: **Samudragupta → Chandragupta II → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta**
- ★ **Prayag Prashasti** — military campaign of **Samudragupta**
- ★ **Devagupta** — another name of **Chandragupta II** (evidence: **Sanchi** and **Vakataka** inscriptions); also **Devaraja** (Sanchi inscription) and **Devashri**
- ★ Gupta dynasty ruled — **319–500 AD** (exam key); dynasty established by **Srigupta** c. **275 AD**
  - First powerful ruler **Chandragupta I** (319–335 AD), title **Maharajadhiraja**
- ★ Gupta ruler who defeated the **Hunas** — **Skandagupta**

## SEC 1:62:8 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya → accession, titles, conquests, alliances, court — Ramagupta episode and accession
- ★ **Chandragupta II** — took the throne after killing his elder brother **Ramagupta** (son of Samudragupta), who could not resist the Sakas; married his wife **Dhruv Devi**
- ★ **Napoleon of (Ancient) India** — **Samudragupta** (335–375 AD); so called by **V.A. Smith** in *Early History of India*
- ★ **Saka-conqueror** — **Chandragupta II**; defeated the last Saka king **Rudrasimha III** (first decade of 5th c.); title **Sakari**; issued silver coins (**Rupak**)

## SEC 1:62:9 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya → accession, titles, conquests, alliances, court — Sakas and second capital
- ★ First Gupta ruler to issue **silver** coins — **Chandragupta II**
- ★ Strongest evidence of the **Saka victory** — his **silver coins**; weight about **30–33 grains**

## SEC 1:62:10 | Chandragupta II Vikramaditya → accession, titles, conquests, alliances, court — Fa-Hien and court
- ★ **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** visited India in the reign of **Chandragupta II**
- ★ Came via the **Silk Route** through Central Asia, to India and **Sri Lanka**; c. **399–412 CE**, about **ten years** in India
- ★ First of three Chinese pilgrims (5th–7th c.); the others **Xuan Zang (Hsuan Tsang)** and **I-Qing (I-tsing)**, both 7th c.

## SEC 1:62:11 | Kumaragupta I and Skandagupta → titles and contributions
- ★ **Nalanda University** — founded c. **450 AD** in the reign of **Kumaragupta (I)**
  - ★ An APPSC Group-1 key credits **Asoka**; its own explanation notes Kumaragupta I is the standard attribution
- **Alexander Cunningham** identified the Nalanda site (1915–16)
- ★ Destroyed by **Muslims** — army of **Bakhtiyar Khalji**, end of the **12th century**
- ★ Gupta emperor who defeated the **Hunas** — **Skandagupta**

## SEC 1:62:12 | Huna invasions → leader, Gupta reign, opponent
- ★ **Mihirakula** (Huna, Hephthalite) — defeated by **Yashodharman**, Aulikara king of Malwa, in **528 A.D.**
- ★ **Gwalior inscription** — shows Mihirakula's territory incl. Gwalior
- ★ Hunas invaded during the reign of **Skandagupta**; he **conquered** them

## SEC 1:62:13 | Inscriptions → what they record
- ★ **Sondani (Sondhni) inscription** of Yashodharman — composed by **Vasula Kavi**; records his victories and governance
- ★ Dynasty most distracted by the Huna invasion — **Gupta**; first invasion under **Skandagupta**, who defeated them; it intensified Gupta decline
- ★ **Sarvavarman** (Maukhari) — copper **seal** found at **Asirgarh** (north of Burhanpur, MP), not a Gaya copper plate; gives Maukhari genealogy from **Harivarman**

## SEC 1:62:14 | Causes of Gupta decline — factor → effect
- ★ **Arab invasion** — **not** a cause of Gupta decline (Arabs came in the medieval era)

## SEC 1:62:15 | Successor powers and regional kingdoms → region
- ★ **Maharaja Dharapatta** and **Guhasena** — **Maitrakas of Valabhi**
- ★ Powers in north India between the Guptas and Harsha: **Later Guptas (Guptas of Magadha)**, **Pushyabhutis of Thanesar**, **Maukharis**; not the **Paramaras of Malwa**
  - Later Guptas ruled **Magadha** mid-6th c. to c. 675 CE; not of the main imperial line
- ★ Dynasty that donated most villages to **temples and Brahmins** — **Gupta**
- ★ **Mandsaur Prashasti** — achievements of **Kumaragupta I**; composed by **Vatsabhatti** at Malwa; silk workers built a **sun temple** there
- ★ **Prabhavati Gupta** — queen of the **Vakataka** dynasty (Deccan); daughter of Chandragupta II
- **Vakatakas** — **Vidarbha** and northern Maharashtra; Vakataka ruler **Harishena** patronised **Ajanta**
- **Mayurasharma** founded the **Kadambas** of Banavasi, not Nalanda

# ROW 1:63 | Gupta administration - feudalism debate, land grants, agraharas, revenue and local bodies
## SEC 1:63:0 | Kingship & decentralisation
- **Samudragupta** equated with **Kubera** (Dhanada), **Varuna**, **Indra** and **Yama** (Antaka)
- **Samudragupta** reinstated some defeated rulers as **feudatories**
- Kingship **hereditary** without firm primogeniture; many offices hereditary
- Officials' salaries often paid in **land or kind**
- Gupta administration relied more on **feudatories and grantees** than the centrally regulated Mauryan state
- Tax trend: **land taxes rose**, **trade taxes fell**

## SEC 1:63:1 | Officials
| Official | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sandhivigrahika** | minister of **peace and war** |
| **Dandapashika** | **district police** officer |
| Mahadandanayaka | chief judicial or military officer |
| **Akshapataladhikrita** | **royal records** |
| Pustapala | land-transfer records |
| Dutaka | spy or messenger |
- **Kumaramatyas** — high-cadre officers ranked with princes; **ministers and commanders** chosen from them
- ★ **Amatya** (Satavahana period) — **minister** who advised the king
- Granted villages: grantees got revenue, administrative, judicial and police rights; royal officials often **barred**; grants did **not** bring direct royal administration

## SEC 1:63:2 | Units (UPPSC 2023)
- Largest to smallest: **Bhukti → Vishaya → Vithi → Grama**
| Unit | Head |
|---|---|
| Bhukti | **Uparika** |
| Vishaya | **Vishayapati** |
| Vithi | **Ayuktaka** (esp. eastern India) |
| Village | Gramika / Gramadhyaksha |
- Village-level land transactions needed consent of the **village headman**

## SEC 1:63:3 | Town board (adhikarana)
| Member | Represented |
|---|---|
| **Nagarashreshthi** | **chaired** the board |
| **Prathama kulika** | **chief artisans** |
| Prathama kayastha | chief scribe |
| Sarthavaha | caravan trader |

## SEC 1:63:4 | Revenue terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bhaga** | main land revenue; king's share of produce — **one-sixth** per law books |
| **Bhoga** | periodic supplies in kind — **fruits, firewood, flowers** |
| **Sulka** | customs duty / **toll** |
| **Vishti** | **forced labour** (army, officials); a source of state income |
| **Hiranya** | **cash** payment instead of produce |
| Bali | changed from voluntary to compulsory |
| Kara | periodic tax |
| **Halivakara** | **plough** tax |
| **Udakabhog** | **irrigation** tax |
| Udrang | land revenue |
| Uparnika | tax on border-land farmers |
| Valiam | commodity tax |
| Sita | revenue from crown land |
- Tax-free land went to **Brahmins** and religious functionaries

## SEC 1:63:5 | Land grants & terms
- **Kuliyavapa** and **dronavapa** — **measures of land**
| Land term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Khila** | **untilled waste** land |
| Kshetra | cultivated land |
| Vastu | house-site land |
| Aprahata | uncultivated forest land |
- **Devagrahara** — grants to **temples and monasteries**; **brahmadeya** and **agrahara** to Brahmanas; secular grants to officials/feudatories

## SEC 1:63:6 | Effects of land grants
- Grants in forest frontiers brought virgin land under cultivation; absorbed **tribes** into the caste order as new **sub-castes**
- Peasants became **semi-servile** — tenant farming, sharecropping, tenancy at will, many taxes
- **Satavahana and Ikshvaku** brahmadeya grants barred **royal officials** from interfering

## SEC 1:63:7 | Feudalism debate
- **R.S. Sharma** — Indian feudalism via **land grants, sub-infeudation, vishti, decline of trade, urban decay**
- **Harbans Mukhia** — **no serfdom**; Indian peasants largely free and autonomous
- Evidence for 7th-c. feudalism — decline in **long-distance trade and gold coinage**, shrinking towns, closed village economy

## SEC 1:63:8 | Exam corner
- Gupta law separated civil and criminal more clearly: **property disputes — civil**; theft, adultery — criminal
- **Damodarpur copper plates** — a **district council** assisting the district officer
- Essentials of feudalism — **control of land**, **lord–vassal** ties, **weak central authority**

## SEC 1:63:12 | Revenue terms → meaning
- ★ **Vishti** (forced labour) — a **source of income for the State**, a sort of tax; enforced by state, governor or local chief
  - ★ **Junagadh inscription** mentions Vishti as a form of tax
- ★ Gupta land taxes rose, trade taxes (shulka/tolls) fell; king took **one-fourth to one-sixth** of produce; new agricultural tax **uparikara**
- ★ Tax-free agrarian land granted to **Brahmins**
- ★ **Bhaga** and **Bali** — **sources of revenue**; per Arthashastra the king owned the land and took Bhaga as his share
- ★ Irrigation tax — **Udakabhaga**
  - ★ **Hiranya** — cash tax (Mauryan); **Udranga** — land tax in Gupta inscriptions; **Uparnika** — tax on farmers living on the land
- ★ Gupta land revenue rate — **one-sixth** of production; called **Udranga** and **Bhag** in inscriptions

## SEC 1:63:13 | Land terms and measures → meaning
- ★ **Kulyavapa** and **dronavapa** — **measurement of land** (Gupta period)
| Land term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kshetra** | cultivated land |
| **Khila** | uncultivable land |
| **Aprahata** | jungle / forest land |
| **Gopata Sarah** | pasture land |

# ROW 1:64 | Gupta society and economy - varna and jati, position of women, Fa-Hien's account, trade decline
## SEC 1:64:0 | Fa-Hien's account
- In India c. **399–414 CE**, reign of **Chandragupta II**, but never **named** the king; main interest **Buddhism**
- Came **overland** via **Central Asia and Gandhara**; returned **by sea** via **Sri Lanka** (through Tamralipti)
- Account — **Fo-kwo-ki** (Record of Buddhist Kingdoms)
- ★ Free **hospital** at **Pataliputra**, endowed by citizens; poor patients given doctors, food and medicine
- **20 viharas** at **Mathura**; decay at Kapilavastu, Gaya and Kushinagar
- **Chandalas** — lived outside villages and towns; **struck a piece of wood** to announce approach; exception to the general food restrictions

## SEC 1:64:1 | Social groups
- Castes multiplied — **foreigners and tribes** absorbed into the caste structure (esp. via land grants)
- **Kayasthas** — emerged as **scribes and record-keepers** of land grants
- **Narada Smriti** — **15** types of slaves
- Chandalas lived outside the varnas and settlements
| Land type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Maru** | **desert** |
| Urvara | fertile |
| Usara | barren |
| Aprahata | fallow |
- Main exports — **gems, spices and textiles**

## SEC 1:64:2 | Women
- Dharmashastras advocated **pre-puberty marriage**
- Widows to remain celibate; **svayamvara** gave way to arranged marriage; women generally denied education
- Women lacked general property rights but controlled **stridhana** — **marriage gifts**
- **Eran inscription (510 CE)** — earliest evidence of **sati**; wife of **Goparaja**, general of **Bhanugupta**

## SEC 1:64:3 | Land & crafts
- **Amarakosha** (Amarasimha) — **12** types of land
- **Sudarshana lake**: built by **Pushyagupta** (under Chandragupta Maurya) → repaired by **Rudradaman** → repaired by **Parnadatta** under **Skandagupta**
- Silk weavers of **Lata** (Gujarat) moved to **Mandsaur** (Malwa)
- **Vidisha** — **cloth and ivory** work

## SEC 1:64:4 | Guilds (shreni)
- Some guilds kept **their own armed bodies** for protection
- Guild charity built temples, rest houses, halls, gardens
- Head of a guild — **Sreshthi** or **Setti**; **Sarthavaha** — caravan trader

## SEC 1:64:5 | Guild inscriptions
| Inscription | Guild / content |
|---|---|
| **Indore copper plate** | **oil-men's** guild; temple lamp endowment |
| **Dashpur (Mandsaur)** | **silk-weavers'** guild |
| Damodarpur plates | guild banking |
- Dashpur silk-weavers came from **Lata (Gujarat)**; built a **Sun temple** at Dashpur
- Weavers changing profession — falling silk demand
- Dashpur inscription — "**oldest advertisement**"; composed by **Vatsabhatti** (not Bhava)

## SEC 1:64:6 | Ports & coins
### Ports
| Port | Location |
|---|---|
| **Ghantasala**, **Kadura** | **Krishna district** (Andhra east coast) |
| Kalyan, Chaul | Konkan coast |
| **Bharuch (Bhrigukachchha)** | **largest west-coast** port; mouth of the **Narmada** |
| **Tamralipti** | **eastern** port in **Bengal** |
| **Sopara**, Barbaricum | **western India** (trade with Rome, SE Asia) |
| Pratishthana | Deccan |
- Satavahana weavers' guild — **Kolika Shreni**
### Coins
- **Rupaka** — **silver**, issued after conquest of the Western Satraps
- Gold **dinaras** — issued in the largest numbers; later ones had less gold
- Ordinary daily exchange — **cowries**; copper issues few

## SEC 1:64:7 | Trade decline
- Silk exports to the Eastern Roman Empire fell — **Romans learnt to rear silkworms**
- Causes of north Indian trade decline — collapse of the **West Roman Empire**, decline of the **Sassanids**, **Huna** disruption of the north-western route
- Loss of the north-western route and western India — less inflow of **gold and silver**
- Eastern Roman trade continued until about **550 CE**; stagnation c. 750–1000 CE
- **Chinansuka** — **Chinese silk**; shows growth of trade with China
- **Urban decay** thesis — **R.S. Sharma**: shrinking towns, rural self-sufficiency, scarce gold coins
- **Vishti** and feudal obligations — basis of the **urban decay and feudalism critique**
- **Arikamedu** (near Puducherry) and **Kaveripattanam** (Puhar) — both South Indian international trade centres, incl. with Rome

## SEC 1:64:8 | Exam corner
- Gupta guilds were **autonomous** — framed rules, kept seals, had executive committees, settled disputes

## SEC 1:64:10 | Castes and social groups → status
- ★ **Kayastha** — caste of **scribes** and accountants

## SEC 1:64:13 | Crafts and guilds — centres, features, terms
- ★ Post-Gupta agrarian structure — development of **feudalism**; reason: rise of a **landowning middle class** and a **dependent peasant** class
  - Land grants to officials, temples, nobles created landlords; cultivators paid rent and rendered services

## SEC 1:64:14 | Inscriptions → guild and trade content
- ★ **Dashpur (Mandsaur) inscription** of **Kumaragupta I** and **Bandhuvarman** (his governor) — **silk-weavers' guild** (Tantuwayas) from **Lata** built the **Sun temple** at Dashpur
- ★ Not composed by **Bhavabhuti**
- ★ Called the **first advertisement of the world** — describes silk fabrics / silk trade

## SEC 1:64:15 | Ports → region; exports and imports
- ★ Port handling **North Indian trade** — **Tamralipti** (modern **Tamluk**, West Bengal); traded with SE Asia, China, Lanka, Java, Sumatra
- ★ **Broach (Bharuch / Bhrigukachchha)** — western port also used for North Indian trade
- ★ **Ghantasala**, **Kadura**, **Chaul** — **ports handling foreign trade**
  - ★ **Chaul** — 23 miles south of Mumbai (west coast); **Ghantasala** = Kontakossylla (Andhra coast)
  - ★ Other Gupta-era ports: Tamralipti, Arikamedu, Muziris, Sopara
- ★ Early maritime links with SE Asia across the Bay of Bengal — **monsoon winds** made voyages possible

## SEC 1:64:16 | Coins → metal, name and use
- ★ Gupta **gold** coins — **Dinara**; per Fa-Hien used for daily business

## SEC 1:64:17 | Trade decline and urban decay — causes, effects, timing
- ★ Gradual **decline of towns** — feature of the **Gupta period**
  - ★ **Pataliputra** had become a village when Hiuen-Tsang came; Mathura, Sonpur, Hastinapur, Sravasti declined
- ★ After the Huna invasion ended the Roman Empire (3rd c. AD), Indian merchants relied more on **South-East Asian trade**

# ROW 1:65 | Gupta religion - Bhagavatism, Vaishnavism, Saivism; beginnings of temple architecture (Deogarh, Bhitargaon)
## SEC 1:65:0 | Bhagavatism / Vaishnavism
- ★ Earliest epigraphic evidence of Bhagavatism — **Garuda Pillar at Besnagar**
- **Heliodorus**, envoy of Indo-Greek king **Antialcidas** at the court of Sunga ruler **Bhagabhadra**, set up the Garuda pillar to Vasudeva
- Bhagavatism — worship of **Vasudeva-Krishna** of the **Vrishni (Satvata)** clan; based on karma, bhakti, ahimsa
- Gupta Vaishnavism — theory of **ten** incarnations (avataras) of Vishnu
- Gupta kings — title **Parambhagavata**; **Garuda** emblem
- Colossal **Varaha panel at Udayagiri** — under **Chandragupta II**; the cave was his personal temple

## SEC 1:65:1 | Puranic Hinduism & Shaktism
### Religious change
- **Theistic and devotional** Hinduism replaced **sacrificial Brahmanism**; temple idol worship common
- Shift from sacrifice to **temple worship and bhakti**; Gupta age also called the **Golden Age of art**
- **18 Mahapuranas** (final shape in Gupta period) — e.g. Vishnu, Bhagavata, Matsya, Vayu, Brahmanda, Markandeya, Shiva, Kurma
- **Durga, Amba, Kali, Chandi** — worshipped as **mother goddesses**
- **Tantrism** — spread from about the **fifth** century; open to women and Shudras; centred on shakti
- **Mathura** — major centre of **Jain art** (Tirthankara images)
### Temples
- Earliest surviving **free-standing Hindu temples** — **Gupta** period
- Gupta temple elements: square **garbhagriha**, pillared mandapa, pradakshina path, raised **jagati** platform
- **Pedavegi inscription** of **Nandivarman II** — first inscription in AP on **Vishnu temples**

## SEC 1:65:2 | Saiva sects
- **Pashupata** — **oldest** Shaiva sect; founded by **Lakulisha (Nakulisha)**; pashu = individual soul, pati = Supreme soul
- **Kalamukha** and **Pashupata** — later flourished under the **Kakatiyas**
- **Lingayats** — followed **Basava**
- **Nathpanth** — Matsyendranath and Gorakhnath

## SEC 1:65:3 | Buddhist scholars
- **Buddhaghosa** — **Visuddhimagga**
- **Vasubandhu** — **Abhidharmakosa**
- **Dinnaga** — founded **Buddhist logic**
- **Bhitargaon** — Gupta **Hindu** temple (third stage of temple evolution), not a Buddhist chaitya

## SEC 1:65:4 | Buddhism, Jainism, tolerance
- Gupta kings patronised **Bhagavatism**; yet **Samudragupta** let **Meghavarman** build a monastery at **Bodh Gaya**
- **Nalanda** — founded by **Kumaragupta I** (Shakraditya)
- Buddhism persecuted by Huna ruler **Mihirakula**; Mahayana images still flourished in Gupta art

- Vardhana dynasty also named **Pushyabhuti** after its founder; rulers once Gupta feudatories
- **Harshacharita** — not by Harsha but **Banabhatta**; Harsha wrote **Ratnavali**, **Priyadarshika** (romantic comedies) and **Nagananda** (Buddhist, self-sacrifice)
- State revenue divided into **four** parts — king's use, public servants, scholars, religious activities
- Harsha — last great **Buddhist patron-king** of north India before the **Rajput states**

## SEC 1:67:9 | Vakataka rulers → contributions
- ★ Vakatakas rose after the weakening of the **Satavahanas** (in Dakshinapath)
- ★ Founder **Vindhyashakti**; **Pravarasena I** was his **son** (not the first king)
- ★ **Pravarasena I** — performed **four Ashwamedhas**
- ★ **Pravarasena II** — interested in literature; wrote **Setubandha**

## SEC 1:67:10 | Vardhana rulers → lineage, titles and capitals
- ★ Vardhana dynasty founded by **Pushyabhuti** at **Thaneshwar** (per Harshacharita)
- ★ Harsha shifted capital from Thaneshwar to **Kannauj**

## SEC 1:67:11 | Maukharis and Harsha's conquests → allies, rivals and vassals
- ★ Capital of Harsha's empire — **Kannauj**; shifted from Thanesar to help sister **Rajyashri** administer Kannauj

## SEC 1:67:13 | Sources and books → authors — Literary works
### Harsha's own works and poet
- ★ Harsha wrote **Ratnavali**, **Priyadarshika**, **Nagananda** (not Harshacharita)
- ★ Poet of Harsha's time (606–647 CE) — **Banabhatta**
- ★ **Harshacharita** — **Banabhatta**; major source of the Vardhana dynasty
- ★ Information on Harsha also in **Kalhana's Rajatarangini**
- ★ **Vidyanatha** (Prataparudra Yasobhushanam) — court poet of **Prataparudra(deva)**, as Bana was of Harsha
### Chinese travellers
- ★ **Hiuen Tsang** visited in **Harsha's** reign; left from **Changan** (Tang capital) in **629 AD**
- ★ Hiuen Tsang visited **Bhinmal**; work **Sei-Yu-Kei**; a Buddhist
- ★ Studied at **Nalanda** (637 AD) under **Acharya Sheelbhadra** (Silabhadra); learned **Yogashastra** about one and a half years
- ★ **Fa-Hien** came under **Chandragupta II**, **not Harsha**; Hiuen Tsang under **Harsha**, not Chandragupta II

## SEC 1:67:14 | Court poets and scholars → patrons and works
- ★ **Bana (Banabhatta)** — patronised by **Harsha** (7th c.); wrote **Harshacharita** and **Kadambari**
- ★ **Siladitya** — title of **Harshavardhana**
- ★ Correct pairs: **Mrichchhakatika — Shudraka**, **Mudrarakshasa — Vishakhadatta**, **Harshacharita — Banabhatta**; **Buddhacharita — Ashvaghosha** (not Vasubandhu)
- ★ **Bhavabhuti, Hastimalla, Kshemeshvara** — **playwrights**
  - ★ **Bhavabhuti** (8th c.) — court poet of **Yashovarman of Kannauj**; **Malatimadhava**, **Mahaviracharita**, **Uttararamacharita**
- ★ Two great conventions — **Kannauj and Prayaga**; Kannauj council to show excellence of Mahayana; rulers of 20 countries attended
- ★ **Harisena** — compiler of **Prayaga Prashasti**, secretary of **Samudragupta** (not Harsha's poet)
- ★ **Nagananda, Ratnavali, Priyadarshika** — **Harshavardhana**
- **Mattavilasa Prahasana** — Pallava **Mahendravarman I**; Dandin — Dashakumaracharita; Bharavi — Kiratarjuniyam

## SEC 1:67:16 | Harsha's religion and the Kannauj and Prayag assemblies
- ★ **Prayag Assembly** (quinquennial **Maha Moksha Parishad**) — convened by Harsha; a charitable and religious gathering honouring **Buddha, Surya and Shiva**, not only to popularise Mahayana
- ★ Harsha's great Buddhist convention in U.P. — at **Prayag**
- ★ Hiuen Tsang: **Mathura** famous for **cotton cloth**; **Thanesar** prosperous through trade

## SEC 1:67:17 | Harsha's administration → revenue, officials, punishments
- ★ No one sent Hiuen-Tsang as emissary to Harsha; Tang emperor **Taizong** had banned foreign travel, yet he came by the **Silk Route** in **629 AD**; returned after 13 years and was welcomed

## SEC 1:67:18 | Nalanda in Harsha's time → founder, head, curriculum
- ★ Hiuen Tsang remembered chiefly for his work **Sei-Yu-Kei** — rich information on ancient India

## SEC 1:67:19 | Hiuen Tsang → profile and observations
- ★ Order: **Satavahanas → Vakatakas → Chalukyas**
  - ★ Satavahanas founded by **Simuk** (1st c. B.C.); Vakatakas by **Vindhyashakti** (3rd c. A.D.); Chalukyas of Gujarat by **Mulraj I** (10th c. A.D.)

## SEC 1:67:20 | Hiuen Tsang's places → what he found (incl. Andhra)
- ★ **Sun temple of Multan** — mentioned by **Hiuen Tsang**, **Al-Masudi** and **Alberuni**
- ★ Pilgrimage to **Sambapur** — **Sun worship**

## SEC 1:67:21 | Universal angles
- ★ Post-Gupta main business centre — **Kannauj** (shifted from **Ujjain**, Chandragupta II's second capital)
- ★ **Vakatakas** ruled central and southern India from the **3rd c. CE** to the end of the 5th / early 6th c.; founder **Vindhyashakti**, succeeded by **Pravarasena**
