# ROW 1:243 | Subhas Chandra Bose - Tripuri crisis, Forward Bloc, escape, Azad Hind Government, INA campaigns
## SEC 1:243:0 | Bose: life events
### Early life
- ★ Born **23 January 1897** at **Cuttack** (Odisha division of Bengal province)
- Graduated from **Scottish Church College** (1919); selected in **ICS** in **1920**
- Resigned from ICS in **1921** to join the Congress
- Political guru — **C.R. Das**
- **'The Indian Struggle'** — Bose's account of the national movement, published **1935**
### Congress presidency
- **National Planning Committee** (1938, under Bose) — chaired by **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- Tripuri 1939: Gandhi — **"Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat"** (after Pattabhi Sitaramayya lost to Bose)
### Escape and INA
- Escaped house arrest in Calcutta — **January 1941**; via **Kabul** and **Moscow** to **Berlin**; reached **Singapore** July 1943
- INA anthem — Hindustani version of 'Jana Gana Mana' translated by **Abid Hasan 'Safrani'**
- Reportedly killed in a plane crash at **Taipei** on **18 August 1945**; later subject of official inquiries

## SEC 1:243:1 | Tripuri crisis, 1939
### Tripuri session
- Bose re-elected president defeating Gandhi-backed **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** (**1,580 to 1,377**)
- **Pant Resolution** — Bose to form the Working Committee according to the wishes of **Gandhi**
- Working Committee deadlock — Bose resigned **April 1939**; **Rajendra Prasad** became President
### Related facts on this page
- **Madduri Annapurnaiah** — 'Andhra Netaji'; Forward Bloc member, jailed for the article **'Chicharapidugu'**
- **August Offer (1940)** — accepted **dominion status** as goal; expanded Executive Council, war advisory council; rejected by Congress
- Quit India underground leadership — **Rammanohar Lohia**, Jayaprakash Narayan, Sucheta Kripalani, Biju Patnaik
- **Matangini Hazra** (shot leading a flag procession, Quit India) — **Tamluk**, Bengal; Kanaklata Barua was from Assam
- ★ **Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy** died in a helicopter crash in the **Nallamala** forest on **2 September 2009**

## SEC 1:243:2 | Organisations
### Forward Bloc
- Aim (1939) — **unite radical, left-wing and anti-imperialist forces** within the Congress, after Bose's resignation
- ★ Andhra member — **Madduri Annapurnaiah**
- Andhra branch — **Madduri Annapurnaiah** and **Peta Bapayya**
### In Europe
- **Free India Centre** — **Berlin**
- **Free Indian Legion** — from **Indian prisoners of war** captured by **Rommel** in North Africa
- **Azad Hind Radio** — started in **Germany**, broadcast from Berlin
### In South-East Asia
- INA also known as **Azad Hind Fauj**; Azad Hind Government (1943) was a separate provisional government
- ★ **Rani Jhansi Regiment** — women's regiment of the INA, formed **1943** by **Subhas Chandra Bose**; named after Rani Lakshmi Bai
- Rani of Jhansi Regiment — all-women combat unit under **Captain Lakshmi Sahgal**
- ★ "Avenge the blood of Indian Martyrs... Stand up against the entire Anglo-American enemy" — **Ras Behari Bose**
### Quit India
- **Usha Mehta** — ran the secret **Congress Radio** in **Bombay**

## SEC 1:243:3 | INA phases
- Bose on withdrawal of Non-Cooperation (1922) — **"sprinkling cold water on the spirits of the people"**
### First INA
- ★ Brainchild of **Giani Pritam Singh Dhillon** with **Mohan Singh** and **Major Fujiwara**; formed under **Mohan Singh** (1942)
- First INA — **Capt. Mohan Singh** with political association of **Rash Behari Bose**

- Bose took command **July 1943**; Supreme Commander on **25 August 1943**
- HQ — **Singapore** initially, later **Rangoon**
- Order: first INA (1942) → Bose takes command (July 1943) → Azad Hind Government (21 Oct 1943) → Red Fort trials (Nov 1945)

## SEC 1:243:4 | Azad Hind Government

- Andaman and Nicobar Islands handed over by **Japan** (not Germany)

## SEC 1:243:5 | Red Fort trials (Nov 1945)

# ROW 1:244 | INA trials and the naval mutiny of 1946; impact on British decision to quit
## SEC 1:244:0 | Events in order
- **INA Day** — **12 November 1945**
- RIN Mutiny — **18 February 1946**, ratings of **HMIS Talwar**, Bombay
- Cabinet Mission announced by Attlee — **19 February 1946**; arrived **24 March 1946**
- Attlee's statement of **20 February 1947** — British to leave by **June 1948**; power actually transferred August 1947

## SEC 1:244:2 | Trial & defence
- Sentences of Sahgal, Shah Nawaz Khan and Dhillon remitted **January 1946** by C-in-C **Claude Auchinleck**
- **INA Defence Committee** — set up by the **Indian National Congress**
- Lead counsel — **Bhulabhai Desai** (his last major contribution)
- **Tej Bahadur Sapru** and **Asaf Ali** in the defence; **Motilal Nehru** did not appear (dead by then)

## SEC 1:244:3 | RIN mutiny: who did what
- **M.S. Khan** — president of the **Naval Central Strike Committee**
- **Patel** and **Jinnah** persuaded the ratings to **surrender**

## SEC 1:244:4 | RIN: scale & causes
- Spread to **Karachi** too — not confined to Bombay
- About **20,000** personnel, **78** ships, **20** shore establishments
- 80th anniversary observed **18 February 2026**

## SEC 1:244:5 | Impact on British exit
- ★ Order: **Second Round Table Conference** (Sept–Dec 1931) → **Quit India** resolution (8 Aug 1942, Gowalia Tank) → **RIN Mutiny** (18 Feb 1946)
- RIN revolt was of **naval ratings**, not civilian workers; sections of the **RIAF** and **Army** also showed disaffection
- British depended on the **2.5-million**-strong Indian Army — INA trials and RIN revolt showed it **could no longer be fully trusted**

## SEC 1:244:6 | Exam corner
- **Attlee**: Gandhi's role in the immediate transfer of power minimal compared with the **loss of military loyalty**

# ROW 1:248 | Wavell Plan and Simla Conference; Cabinet Mission Plan 1946; Interim Government
## SEC 1:248:0 | Sequence 1944-46
### 1944–45
- **Gandhi-Jinnah talks** (Bombay, **9–27 September 1944**) — based on the **C.R. Formula** (post-war plebiscite in contiguous Muslim-majority areas)
- **Desai-Liaquat Pact** — equal Congress and League nominees, **20 per cent** for minorities; never formally accepted
- **Simla Conference** (June–July 1945) — on the **Wavell Plan**; closed **14 July 1945**, giving the League a de facto veto
### 1946
- Order: Red Fort trials (Nov 1945) → RIN revolt (**18 Feb 1946**) → Cabinet Mission arrives (**24 March 1946**) → Plan (**16 May 1946**)
- **Tanguturi Prakasam** — premier of the Congress ministry in **Madras**, 1946
- ★ Rejection of the **Cabinet Mission** — last opportunity to avoid partition; Union to control only defence, foreign affairs, communications
- ★ **Gandhi** was totally in favour of the Cabinet Mission Plan
- Princely-state members of the Constituent Assembly — **nominated by rulers**; provincial members elected community-wise by **PR-STV**
- Constituent Assembly — under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**, elections **July 1946**; first met **9 December 1946**
- Constituent Assembly demand: **M.N. Roy** 1934 → **Congress 1935** → August Offer accepted it 1940
- League called for Direct Action — **29 July 1946**; **Direct Action Day 16 August 1946** — **Great Calcutta Killings**; spread to Noakhali, Bihar
- Wavell invited Nehru — **12 August 1946**; Interim Government formed **2 September 1946**, right after Direct Action Day
- Interim Government head — **Nehru** (Vice-President of the Executive Council); **Lord Wavell** Viceroy; Liaquat Ali Khan Finance
- Mountbatten replaced Wavell — **March 1947**
- Missions: first **Jesuit** mission to Akbar **1580**; **Serampore** Mission **1800**; **Cripps** Mission **1942** (not 1945)
- Cripps offered **Dominion Status**; Cabinet Mission planned the Constituent Assembly

## SEC 1:248:1 | Wavell Plan & Simla, 1945
- Wavell Plan — **parity** between **caste Hindus and Muslims** in the Executive Council; Viceroy and C-in-C excluded
- Simla failure — **Jinnah** insisted the **League alone** nominate all Muslim members (denying Congress and Punjab Unionists)
- During the Interim Government — **Wavell** remained President of the Executive Council; Nehru Vice-President
- **August Offer (1940)** — core proposal: an **Indian-framed constitution after the war**

## SEC 1:248:3 | Three-tier plan
### Cabinet Mission structure
- Union — only **Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications**
- Three tiers — Union, groups of provinces, provinces; **residuary** subjects with the **provinces**
- Group A — Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa; Group B — **Punjab, NWFP, Sindh**; Group C — **Bengal, Assam**
### Other facts
- **C.R. Formula** — plebiscite and joint agreements after separation; **Jinnah** demanded only Muslims vote and **no common centre** (not in the Formula)
- 1945–46: League ministries in **Bengal** (under **H.S. Suhrawardy**) and Sindh; Congress in most provinces incl. **NWFP**
- **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** — chaired both **Punjab** and **Bengal** Boundary Commissions
- **Dickie Bird Plan** — provinces as independent successor states; rejected by Nehru as **Balkanisation**

## SEC 1:248:4 | Grouping trap & Constituent Assembly
- 1945–46 elections — League won all **30** Muslim seats in the Central Legislative Assembly; Congress swept general seats
- Non-Muslims — **38%** in the north-west, **48%** in the north-east zone; one reason the Mission rejected full Pakistan
- Constituent Assembly seats — British India **296**, princely states **93**; about one seat per **ten lakh**
- July 1946 results — Congress **208**, League **73**, others **15**
- Grouping — Congress: **optional**; League: **compulsory**
- League withdrew acceptance after **Nehru's statement of 10 July 1946** (Congress could modify the scheme via its majority)
