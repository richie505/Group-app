## SEC 2:99:1 | RBI & Monetary Policy Committee
### Monetary Policy Committee & inflation targeting
- MPC — **six** members; **three external** members appointed by the Centre for **4 years**
- **Governor** chairs the MPC, with a **casting vote**
- **Section 45ZA**, RBI Act — Centre, consulting the RBI, sets the inflation target every **five years**
- Target — **4% CPI-Combined**, tolerance band **± 2%** (CPI-Combined, not WPI)
- **Price stability** is the primary objective; **growth** is secondary
- Recommended by the **Urjit Patel Committee** (2014); framework adopted in **2016**
  - Narasimham — banking reforms; Malhotra — insurance reforms; Raghuram Rajan — financial-sector reforms
### RBI functions
- **One-rupee note and coins** — issued by the **Ministry of Finance**, not RBI
- RBI — monetary authority, foreign-exchange management under **FEMA**, regulation and supervision of banks and **NBFCs**
- **Housing finance companies** — regulated by the **RBI since 2019**; **NHB** remains the apex housing refinancer; Centre took full ownership of NHB in **2019**
- **DICGC** (**1978**) — wholly-owned subsidiary of the **RBI** (not Ministry of Finance); insures bank deposits; **NBFC deposits not covered**
- **Banking Ombudsman Scheme** — began **14 June 1995**, under **Section 35A** of the Banking Regulation Act; **quasi-judicial**
### Other bodies
- **SEBI** — set up by resolution in **1988**; statutory under the **SEBI Act, 1992**
- SEBI replaced the **Controller of Capital Issues**
- **FSDC** (**2010**, non-statutory) — chaired by the **Union Finance Minister**; financial stability and inter-regulatory coordination

## SEC 2:99:2 | SEBI appeal route
- Appeals against SEBI orders — **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)**, then the **Supreme Court** (not NCLAT)

## SEC 2:99:3 | Insurance FDI cap
- Insurance FDI cap — **26%** (2000) → **49%** (**2015**) → **74%** (2021) → **100%** (2025-26)
- **Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha Act, 2025** — inserted **Section 16A** in the IRDA Act: **Policyholders' Education and Protection Fund**; also allows up to **100% FDI** in insurers

## SEC 2:99:4 | Committees & councils
- **FSDC** — set up **2010**; **non-statutory** apex body; chaired by the **Union Finance Minister** (not RBI Governor)
- **Financial Sector Legislative Reforms Commission** — chaired by **Justice B.N. Srikrishna**
- FSLRC proposals — single **Indian Financial Code**, **Unified Financial Agency**, **Public Debt Management Agency**
  - Not proposed: a separate constitutional court for financial disputes

## SEC 2:99:5 | Who regulates it now

- **NFRA** — oversees auditing and accounting standards under the **Companies Act, 2013**
- PFRDA's **NPS e-Shramik** — pension coverage for **informal workers**

## SEC 2:99:6 | Recent actions (2025-26)

# ROW 2:102 | Regulatory governance issues - independence, capacity, accountability, regulatory capture, appellate structures
## SEC 2:102:0 | Key terms
- Regulators as **"mini-states"** (Economic Survey 2025-26) — combine rule-making, investigation, adjudication: **legislative, executive and judicial** functions

- **FSLRC** — headed by **Justice B.N. Srikrishna**; reported **2013** with a draft **Indian Financial Code**; area-based → **task-based** regulation

# ROW 2:105 | Indian society - salient features, unity in diversity, continuity and change
## SEC 2:105:0 | Salient features & diversity
### Features and stratification
- Indian society — **multi-ethnic**, **multi-lingual**, **multi-class**, broadly **patriarchal**
- Matrilineal exceptions — **Khasi**, **Jaintia**, **Garo**
- **Caste** — birth-based, closed, **cumulative** inequality; **class** — open, mobility, **dispersed** inequality
- **Andre Beteille** — shift from **cumulative** to **dispersed** inequality
- Features of social structure — large village population, many religions, many castes; **poverty** is an economic condition
- **Power elite** — **political**, **military** and **corporate** leaders (not sociologists)
### Diversity and unity
- **Census 2011** — about **122** languages; Constitution recognises **22**
- Hindus a minority in **eight** States/UTs; **six** major religions
- Bonds of unity — shared festivals, **Bhakti-Sufi** syncretism, **Khanqahs**; India not linguistically homogeneous
- **Article 350-B** — **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities** (not a Finance Commission)
- Caste and family change — **Article 17**, Caste Disabilities Removal Act, **Hindu Code** Acts
- Harappan continuity limited — script **undeciphered**; urban collapse around **1900 BCE**
- **Education** — promotes individualism, privacy, rights; questions joint-family customs
- **Urbanisation and industrialisation** — nuclear households; family becomes a **consumption** unit

## SEC 2:105:1 | Comte's Law of Three Stages
- **Theological stage** — events explained by supernatural forces: rain by **God's will**
- **Metaphysical stage** — rain by **nature's force**
- **Positivism** — knowledge rests on observation, experiment, scientific evidence; rejects supernatural explanation
- **Marx** — **historical materialism**: the economic base shapes society, politics and culture
- **Verrier Elwin** — tribes should develop by **their own culture**; opposed rapid assimilation into Hindu society (unlike Ghurye)
- **Simmel** — society exists because **individuals interact**; interaction is central
- **Religion** strengthens **social solidarity** (Durkheim); social cohesion is one of its functions
- **Family** promotes social stability — the **primary agent of socialisation**

## SEC 2:105:2 | Western founders
### Auguste Comte
- **Father of Sociology**; coined the word **sociology**; founded **positivism** (1798–1857)
- Motto — **'Order and Progress'** (on Brazil's flag)
### Karl Marx
- **Father of Scientific Socialism**; a **conflict** theorist
- Ideas — **surplus value** (workers produce more value than wages), history as **class conflict**, revolution to a **classless society**
- *Communist Manifesto* — **1848**, with **Engels**; *Das Kapital* — **1867**
- **Alienation from oneself** — losing one's human nature (**species-being**); forced, monotonous work = alienation from the **production process**
### Emile Durkheim
- **Father of Modern Sociology**
- **Social facts** — external forces shaping behaviour, e.g. **laws, customs, religion, traditions**
- **Collective conscience** — shared beliefs that unite society (a concept, not a book)
- **Anomie** — **normlessness** from rapid social change; crime, suicide, unrest
### Max Weber (1864–1920)
- **Verstehen** — **interpretive understanding** (Weber, not Durkheim)
- **Class-status-power**, life chances, ideal bureaucracy; wrote ***The Religion of India*** and *The Protestant Ethic*
- Social action — **value-rational** (values), instrumentally rational (goals), **traditional** (custom), **affective** (emotion)
- **Bureaucracy** — hierarchy, specialisation, **impersonality** (not charisma)
- **Traditional authority** — custom; **charismatic** — personality and leadership; appointment by qualifications = merit system
### C. Wright Mills (1916–1962)
- American; *The Sociological Imagination* (**1959**); not known for social stratification
- **Sociological imagination** — connecting personal experience with wider social forces and history; personal troubles linked to public issues
- **Biography** — the individual's own life and personal troubles
### Georg Simmel
- German; studied **social interaction** and **urban life**; formal sociology
- **Dyad** — group of **two**; **triad** — group of **three**
- City life **increases social distance**; individualism and competition rise
### Social stability
- Society keeps **peace, order, harmony, continuity** and working institutions despite change
- Promote stability — **welfare**, **social justice**, **religious and cultural tolerance**, **democratic institutions**, education and values
- Threat — **regionalism**; inclusive development is a feature, not a threat

# ROW 2:106 | Family - types, functions and changing structure in India
## SEC 2:106:0 | Types of family
### Residence and descent
- **Neo-local** — couple sets up a separate residence away from both sets of parents
- **Duo-local** — spouses stay with their **own natal families**
- **Matrilineal** — descent through the **mother's** line
- **Family of orientation** — the family one is **born** into; **family of procreation** — set up after marriage
### Family as institution
- **Family** — the basic, **primary** social institution; first place of socialisation
- Family — the child's **first school** (language, customs, values, norms)
- Rise of single-parent and female-headed households — **widowhood**, **marital instability**, **male migration**
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu** — urged a return to the **joint family** and incentives for **two or more children** (declining fertility)

## SEC 2:106:1 | Functions by scholar
- **Talcott Parsons** — two irreducible functions: **primary socialisation** of children, **stabilisation of adult personality**
- **George Peter Murdock** — four universal functions: **sexual regulation**, **reproduction**, **economic cooperation**, **education**
  - Reproductive = birth of children; economic = cooperation; educational = socialisation
- Family **encourages** acceptable behaviour; passes on **religious beliefs and rituals**; **protects** from social and economic risks
- **Dysfunction** — persistent failure of family functions (e.g. abuse, alcoholism)
- **High-risk families** — severe social, economic, health, psychological or environmental challenges harming members
  - Economic causes — poverty, debt, job insecurity; social — breakdown, migration, exclusion; psychological — stress, depression, addiction

## SEC 2:106:2 | Joint family studies
- **Karve** — jointness rests on **functional unity and obligations**, more than co-residence; may involve **common property**
- **Tharawad** (Tiyans, some Moplas, Bants) — a woman, her siblings and her **sisters' children**; husband not in the unit
- **Proprietary class** (urban) — joint families, strong **elder-male authority**

## SEC 2:106:3 | Matrilineal systems
- **Khasi** (Meghalaya) — youngest daughter inherits: **ultimogeniture**
- **Garo** — **Machong** = matrilineage; household headed by the heiress's husband, the **nokma**
- **Nair tharavad**, Kerala — **Marumakkathayam**; abolished by the **Joint Family System (Abolition) Act, 1975**

## SEC 2:106:4 | Family laws
- **Mitakshara** — **Vijnaneshwara's** commentary on the **Yajnavalkya Smriti** (Kalyani Chalukya court)
- **Dayabhaga** — **Jimutavahana**
- **Hindu Code** (incl. **Hindu Succession Act, 1956**) — main advocate **B.R. Ambedkar**
- **2005** amendment, **Section 6**, HSA — daughters **coparceners by birth**
- **Vineeta Sharma (2020)** — applies **whether or not the father was alive in 2005**
- **Andhra Pradesh** succession amendment — **1985**, 20 years before the central 2005 amendment
- **Family Courts Act, 1984** — followed the **59th Law Commission Report** (**1974**)
- Guardianship — **Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act**; adoption — Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act
- Family provides socially approved sexual relations; regulating sex **prevents social disorder**

## SEC 2:106:5 | Census data & change
### Scholars
- **Murdock** — family defined by **common residence**, **economic co-operation**, **reproduction**
- **Ogburn** — **seven** functions (incl. status-giving, affectional); all except **affection** passing to outside agencies ('loss of functions')
- **Ramkrishna Mukherjee** — joint families over-represented in **trade and higher occupations**
- **I.P. Desai** — studied jointness in **Mahuva**; **five** degrees of jointness
- **A.M. Shah** — *The Household Dimension of Family in India* (**1973**); family vs household; **developmental cycle** of households
- **Leela Dube** — family **reproduces patriarchy**; **Kapadia** pioneered family studies
- **M.S. Gore** — emotional bonds **persist** as nuclear households rise
- **Khasi** and **Garo** — **Meghalaya**; in matriliny power usually stays with **men** (matriliny is not matriarchy)
- **Dayabhaga** (Bengal, Assam) — **Jimutavahana**; son's right arises only on **father's death**
### Forces of change
- **Industrialisation** — family from **production** unit to **consumption** unit; economic independence of youth
- **Urbanisation and migration** — limited housing, rural-urban migration → **break-up of joint families**
- Urban families studied by **livelihood, authority structure, social milieu, value pattern** (not only household size)
- Urban nuclear households **fell** from **54.3%** to **52.3%** (Census 2001 → 2011)
### Data and law
- **NFHS-6** (2023–24) — released by MoHFW; nodal agency **IIPS, Mumbai**; overall TFR **2.0**
- **NFHS-5** — urban TFR **1.6**, rural **2.1**
- Elderly care — **filial piety**, strained by nuclearisation and migration
- **BNSS, 2023** — maintenance for wives, children, indigent parents, divorced wives
- **Inheritance of property** — passing on wealth and property (collective consumption is a separate function)
- **Social exclusion** — a **cause** of high-risk families; substance abuse, single-parent households, conflict are characteristics

## SEC 2:106:8 | Family functions → socialization, identity, biological, protective, economic, emotional
- Family gives **caste, religious, language and national** identities; **nationality** = national identity (not language identity)

# ROW 2:107 | Marriage - forms, rules of endogamy and exogamy, changing trends and legislation
## SEC 2:107:0 | Forms & terms
- **Sambandham** — consensual **informal** union among **Namboothiris and Nairs**, Kerala's matrilineal system
- **Niyoga** — wife cohabits with a **designated man** to beget a child when her husband is impotent or dead
- **Later Vedic** — child marriage emerged; **Swayamvara** declined; widow remarriage discouraged; same-**gotra** marriage prohibited
- **Anuloma** — higher-varna man + lower-varna woman (tolerated) = **hypergamy**
- **Pratiloma** — lower-varna man + higher-varna woman (condemned) = **hypogamy**
- **Polygyny** — one man, many wives; **fraternal polyandry** — marriage to **brothers**
- **Levirate** — widow marries her **husband's brother**

## SEC 2:107:1 | Manu's eight forms
- Manu Smriti — **eight** forms: four approved (**Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya**), four disapproved (**Gandharva, Asura, Rakshasa, Paisacha**)

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Brahma** | **highest**; daughter given to a learned, virtuous groom, no dowry or bride price |
| **Daiva** | daughter given to a **priest** officiating at a sacrifice |
| **Arsha** | marriage after **symbolic gifts** |
| **Prajapatya** | duty, family welfare, children |
| **Gandharva** | no parental approval; **conditionally accepted** (disapproved but tolerated) |
| **Asura** | **purchase** of the bride with wealth |
| **Rakshasa** | forceful **abduction**; linked with **Kshatriyas**; examples in the epics |
| **Paisacha** | deceit, intoxication, force, no consent; **most condemned** |

- **Satyashodhak** marriage (started by **Savitribai Phule**) — **no dowry**, **no Brahmin priest**

## SEC 2:107:2 | Rules of mate selection
- **Ghurye** — **endogamy** the defining feature of caste

- **North India** — **caste endogamy**, **gotra exogamy**, **village exogamy**
- **Section 3(f)**, HMA — sapinda: **three** generations (mother's side), **five** (father's side)
