## SEC 1:139:2 | Grants & treaties

# ROW 1:140 | Rise of the Marathas - Shivaji's career, Ashtapradhan, revenue system, chauth and sardeshmukhi
## SEC 1:140:0 | Shivaji's career
### Early life
- **Born** at **Shivneri** in **1630** to **Shahaji Bhonsle** (Maratha general) and **Jijabai**
- ★ Birth / Chhatrapati title keyed as **1627, 1674**
- **Dadaji Kondadev** — guardian of the **Pune jagir** and Shivaji's **tutor**
- **Torna** — taken from Bijapur in **1646**; his **first fort**
### Conflicts
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1659 | killed Bijapur general **Afzal Khan** with a **wagh nakh** at the foot of **Pratapgad** |
| 1663 | **night raid on Shaista Khan** at **Pune** — Khan lost his fingers |
| 1665 | **Treaty of Purandar** with **Raja Jai Singh** (Mirza Raja of Amber) — surrendered **23 of 35 forts** |
| 1666 | escaped house arrest at **Agra** with **Sambhaji**, hidden in baskets of sweets and fruit |
| 1670 | second **Surat** raid |
| 1674 | **coronation** at **Raigad** |
| 1677 | treaty with **Tana Shah** (Abul Hasan) of Golconda |
| 1680 | death |
- Golconda treaty — Golconda to give **money** for the southern conquests and **share the spoils**
- Southern campaign — took **Gingee**, **Vellore** and **Ballari**; refused to share the spoils
### Coronation
- Officiated by **Gaga Bhatt**, a Brahmin of **Banaras**, with Vedic rites
- Title **"Shri Raja Shiva Chhatrapati"**; also **Haindava Dharmoddharak** (not Hindavi Sultan)
- Started the **Rajabhisheka Shaka** era to assert sovereignty
- Raigad — coronation; Agra — house arrest (1666); **Jinji** — Rajaram's base; **Kolhapur** — Tarabai's line
### Forts
- **Giri Durg** (hill forts) — **Raigad, Pratapgad, Torna, Sinhagad**; **Vishalgad** — land fort

## SEC 1:140:1 | Ashtapradhan
| Minister | Portfolio |
|---|---|
| Sumant (Dabir) | **foreign affairs**; received envoys; master of ceremonies |
| Nyayadhish | **chief justice** |
| Amatya | **finance** / accounts / treasury and revenue |
| Panditrao | **religion**; grants |
| Mantri | **intelligence** |

- ★ **Sumant** — Shivaji's **foreign minister**; diplomacy, treaties, foreign envoys
- **Dabir** handled foreign affairs, **not** treasury and revenue
- Every minister reported directly to the **Chhatrapati**; posts **not hereditary**; ministers could be transferred or dismissed
- **Peshwa** post — became hereditary and supreme **only later**, under the Peshwas
- **Chauth + sardeshmukhi** together — financed military expansion and gave political hegemony **without the cost of direct governance**

## SEC 1:140:2 | Chauth vs sardeshmukhi
- **Chauth** — **one-fourth (25%)** of land revenue; **protection** levy
- **Sardeshmukhi** — additional **10%**; king's **hereditary right** as Sardeshmukh
- Chauth collected from territories **outside direct Maratha rule** (Mughal and Deccan Sultanate lands)
- **1719** Mughal grants — recognised **Shahu**, confirmed **Swarajya** lands, gave chauth and sardeshmukhi of the **six** Deccan subas
- **Big deshmukhs** opposed Shivaji — he curbed the hereditary rights of **deshmukhs and deshpandes**; they did not back an independent Maratha state
- Maratha Empire at its peak — **Attock to Cuttack**; **Gulf of Khambhat** to beyond the **Kaveri**

## SEC 1:140:3 | Revenue & administration
### Revenue
- **Ryotwari** type — state dealt **directly with the cultivator**; officials paid in **cash**
- **Saranjam** — service land grants **abolished** by Shivaji; returned under **Shahu and the Peshwas**
- **Bagayat** — **irrigated** land; **Jirayat** — dry land; **Kathi** — measuring rod
### Units and officials
| Unit | Official |
|---|---|
| Prant | **Subedar / Sarsubedar** |
| Pargana, Tarf | Deshmukh, Deshpande |
| Mauza | Patil, Kulkarni |
### Coins, language, records
- Coins — gold **Hon** and copper **Shivrai**
- **Rajvyavahar Kosh** — replaced **Persian** official terms with **Sanskrit**
- Marathi and Sanskrit promoted over Persian; **Modi** script for Marathi records
### Character of the state
- **Centralised, non-hereditary, cash-paid**
- Ashtapradhan **advisory**; Mughal wazir system **hierarchical**
### Battles and later events
- **Salher (1672)** — Marathas defeated the **Mughals** in open battle
- After the **1795** war the Nizam paid chauth → joined the **Subsidiary Alliance** in **1798**

## SEC 1:140:4 | Military
- **Ganimi Kava** — **guerrilla** hit-and-run warfare in the **Sahyadri** terrain
- Each fort under three officers of equal rank — **Havaldar, Sabnis, Sarnobat**; Sabnis kept records
- **Bargirs** — cavalry given horses and equipment by the **state**; **Shiledars** — kept their own horses
- Sea forts **Sindhudurg, Kolaba, Vijaydurg** — guarded the **Konkan** coast; Shivaji called **Father of the Indian Navy**

## SEC 1:141:4 | Third Panipat, 14 January 1761
- **14 January 1761** — **Marathas vs Ahmad Shah Abdali** (APPSC-G1 2018)
  - Not Mughals vs Abdali; **Shah Alam II** was emperor, not a combatant; **Nadir Shah** invaded in 1739

- Abdali's allies — **Najib-ud-Daula** and **Shuja-ud-Daula** of Awadh
- Causes of defeat — **no support from Jats and Rajputs**, supply failure, heavy baggage, alienation of regional powers

# ROW 1:142 | Bhakti Movement - origins, Alvars and Nayanars, Nirguna and Saguna streams, social base
## SEC 1:142:0 | Origin & roots
- **Bhakti** — from root **bhaj**, "to **share or participate**"
- Roots — **Upanishads**, **Bhagavad Gita**, **Bhagavata Purana** (not the **Arthashastra**)
- **Bhagavata Purana** — put bhakti at the centre; **ninefold bhakti** (kirtana, smarana, atmanivedana ...)
- Began in the **Tamil** region (**6th-9th c.**, Alvars and Nayanars); reached north India by the **15th c.**; overall span 6th-18th c.
- **Alvars and Nayanars** — from **both high and low castes**; challenged Brahmanical dominance

## SEC 1:142:1 | Alvars vs Nayanars
| | Alvars | Nayanars |
|---|---|---|
| Devotees of | **Vishnu** | **Shiva** |

- **Tiruvasagam** — **Manikkavachakar**
- **Periya Puranam** — **Sekkizhar**; 12th book of the Tirumurai
- Why bhakti arose — **gyana and karma margas** too difficult for ordinary people; reaction to ritualism, priestly dominance, caste

## SEC 1:142:2 | Nirguna vs Saguna
- **Nirguna** (formless God) — **Kabir, Guru Nanak, Dadu Dayal, Ravidas**
- **Saguna Vaishnavas** — split into **Ramamargis** and **Krishnamargis**; **Tulsidas** — Ramamargi
- **Dadu Panth** — founded by **Dadu Dayal** in **Rajasthan**, in Kabir's tradition (not by Kabir)
- Social background — **Kabir** weaver; **Dhanna** Jat cultivator; **Sena** barber; **Raidas** shoemaker
- **Raskhan** — Muslim poet devoted to **Krishna** (not Rama); wrote **Prem Vatika**
- **Mirabai** sang of Krishna

- **Akkamahadevi** — 12th-c. woman saint of **Karnataka**
- Saints placed devotion above **birth and social status**
- Social impact — brought **Hindus and Muslims closer**, weakened caste bondage, but did **not end caste**
