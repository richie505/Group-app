=== ROW 28 | Cabinet system, Cabinet committees, collective and individual responsibility; Attorney General of India

## Parliamentary form — features and defining principle | from: S0
**Features of the parliamentary form (UPSC) - India vs USA**

| **Parliamentary (India)** | **Presidential (USA)** |
|---|---|
| **Nominal and real executives** - nominal (President) and real (PM) | President is both head of State and government |
| **Majority party rule** | Fixed-term executive, independent of legislature |
| **Collective responsibility** to Lok Sabha | No legislative responsibility of executive |
| Ministers are MPs (dual membership / double membership) | Secretaries are not members of Congress |
| **Leadership of PM**; LS can be dissolved (dissolution of the Lok Sabha) | No dissolution |
| Political homogeneity; secrecy of procedure | Separation of powers |

- **Defining feature:** India = parliamentary because of **collective responsibility** (not separation of powers or judicial review) (APPSC 2025)
- **Necessary features:** Majority party rule + collective responsibility + PM as centre of power (APPSC 2019)
- **Why chosen:** Experience under the **1919 and 1935 Acts** + to avoid a personality cult
- **Underlying principle of Cabinet form:** **a mechanism of parliamentary democracy for ensuring collective responsibility of the Government to the people** (UPSC 2017 key)
- ⚠ Defining feature = **collective responsibility**; separation of powers = the **USA** column

## Terms → meaning: Cabinet, Council of Ministers, kitchen cabinet, GoMs | from: S1, S6

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Kitchen cabinet** | Informal inner circle of the PM → a few senior ministers + trusted non-ministers (colleagues and friends) where real decisions are shaped; example: Indira Gandhi's advisers |
| **Groups of Ministers (GoMs/EGoMs)** | Ad hoc groups that examine an issue and report to the Cabinet; existing GoMs/EGoMs **abolished in 2014** |

**Council of Ministers vs Cabinet (incl. Group-II table)**

| **Council of Ministers** | **Cabinet** |
|---|---|
| Wider body, 60-70 ministers of all ranks: Cabinet Ministers, MoS (independent charge), MoS, Deputy Ministers (IYB) | Smaller body (nucleus), Cabinet Ministers only |
| Constitutional - created by **Arts. 74-75** | Word appears only in **Art. 352**; rest by convention |
| Rarely meets as a body | Meets regularly; takes policy decisions |
| Collectively responsible to LS | Directs and supervises the Council |

## Articles → collective and individual responsibility | from: S1, S2

| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 352(3)** | Only place the **word "Cabinet"** appears; inserted by the **44th Amendment, 1978** (APPSC 2018) → Emergency only on the **written** advice of the Union Cabinet |
| **Arts. 74-75** | Create the Council of Ministers |
| **Art 75(3)** (States: **Art 164(2)**) | CoM collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha** |
| **Art. 75(2)** | Ministers hold office at the President's pleasure → individual responsibility |
| Third Schedule | Oaths of **office and secrecy**, administered by the President |

# 1. Collective responsibility
- **Basis:** **Art 75(3)** → ministers "sink or swim together"
  - Not to both Houses, not to the President
  - Ministers from the **Rajya Sabha** are also collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**
- **No-confidence motion:** Only in the **Lok Sabha**
- **Loss of LS confidence** (no-confidence motion passed, defeat on a money bill/major policy) → **whole ministry resigns**
  - Defeat in the **Rajya Sabha** does not bring down the government / force resignation
- **PM's death/resignation:** **Dissolves** the Council
- **Binding decisions:** Cabinet decisions **bind all ministers** even if they differed inside; a dissenting minister unwilling to defend a decision **must resign** (UPSC)
- **Homogeneity & secrecy:** Political homogeneity; **secrecy** of Cabinet deliberation ensured by the oath of secrecy → differences ironed out behind closed doors, public show of unity (UPSC)
# 2. Individual responsibility
- **Basis:** **Art. 75(2)**
  - PM can ask a minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him (UPSC)
- **Personal statement:** A resigned minister may explain his resignation in the House, with the Speaker's consent
- ⚠ **75(3)** = collective (to LS); **75(2)** = individual (President's pleasure)

=== ROW 29 | Types of legislatures - unicameral and bicameral; arguments for a second chamber

## Legislature types — definitions and country examples | from: S0
- **Definitions:** **Unicameral** = one House vs **bicameral** = two Houses
- **India:** Parliament is bicameral → **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** + **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**
- **Why bicameral:** Large and diverse countries prefer two Houses → represent all sections and regions
- **Unicameral examples:** **China** (National People's Congress), **Israel** (Knesset), **New Zealand**, **Sweden**, **Denmark**

**Upper and lower house names in other countries**

| **Country (Parliament)** | **Upper House** | **Lower House** |
|---|---|---|
| UK (Parliament) | House of Lords | House of Commons |
| USA (Congress) | Senate | House of Representatives |
| Japan (Diet) | House of Councillors | House of Representatives |
| Russia (**Federal Assembly**) | **Federation Council** | **State Duma** |
| Australia (Parliament) | Senate | House of Representatives |

- **PYQ (UPSC 2005):** Only "Russia's Parliament is the Federal Assembly" is correct
  - Federation Council = **upper** house; Duma = **lower** house

## Parliament — components, President's place and strength of Houses | from: S0, S1
# 1. Article 79 - components
- **Art. 79:** Parliament = **President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha**
  - Covered in Arts. **79-122**, Part V
- **Why President is part of it:**

## Term of each House — dissolution, extension, permanence | from: S4
# 1. Lok Sabha term
- **Art 83(2):** LS term **5 years** from its first meeting
- **Early dissolution:** By the President on PM's advice → cannot be challenged in court
  - Dissolved before full term: **4th (1970)**, **6th (1979)**, **9th (1991)**, **11th (1997)**, **12th (1999)**, **13th (2004)**
- **Emergency extension:** By law, **1 year at a time**; not beyond **6 months** after the emergency ends
  - **5th Lok Sabha** (**1971-77**) extended during the **1975-77** Emergency
- **Count:** **18 Lok Sabhas** constituted so far (2026)
# 2. Rajya Sabha permanence
- **Permanent House (Art 83(1)):** RS is **never dissolved**; one-third retire **every second year**

| **Point** | **Rajya Sabha** | **Lok Sabha** |
|---|---|---|
| Dissolution | **Not subject to dissolution** - permanent/continuing House (**Art. 83(1)**) | Dissolved by the President; term **5 years** from the date of its first meeting (**Art. 83(2)**) |
| Rotation | About **one-third retire every second year** | Whole House elected together |
| Member's term | **6 years**, fixed by the **RPA 1951**, not by the Constitution (CDI) | 5 years |
| Extension | - | During a National Emergency, by law, **1 year at a time**, not beyond **6 months** after the Emergency ends (IYB) |

- **First Rajya Sabha (1952):** President curtailed terms of initial members to start the one-third cycle → who retired decided by **lottery**
- ⚠ RS member's **6 years** = **RPA 1951**, not the Constitution

## Qualifications and disqualifications — Articles 84, 102, 103, Tenth Schedule | from: S5
# 1. Qualifications

| **Condition** | **Rajya Sabha** | **Lok Sabha** |
|---|---|---|
| Citizenship | Citizen of India | Citizen of India |
| Minimum age (Art 84; IYB) | **30 years** | **25 years** |
| Oath | Before an EC-authorised person (Third Schedule form) | Before an EC-authorised person (Third Schedule form) |

- **Office of profit:** Parliament can exempt offices by law
- **Domicile (RS):** Removed in 2003 (Kuldip Nayar case, 2006)
# 2. Disqualifications
- **Art. 102 grounds:**
  - Office of profit under the Union/a state (exempted posts apart)
  - Unsound mind declared by a court
  - Undischarged insolvent
  - Not a citizen / acquired foreign citizenship
  - Disqualified by a law of Parliament (RPA **1951**)
  - Defection - Tenth Schedule
- **Art. 103:** **President** decides, after obtaining the **Election Commission's opinion** and acting on it
- **Tenth Schedule (defection):** Decided by the **Speaker** (LS) / **Chairman** (RS), not the President
  - Subject to judicial review → Kihoto Hollohan (1992)
  - Grounds: voluntarily giving up party membership; voting/abstaining against the whip without permission and not condoned within **15 days**; independent joining a party; nominated member joining a party after **6 months**
- ⚠ **Art. 102** grounds → **President** (on EC opinion); defection → **Speaker / Chairman**

## Vacation of seats — Article 101 | from: S6

| **Situation** | **Result** |
|---|---|
| Elected to both Houses | Intimate choice within **10 days**, else the **Rajya Sabha** seat (**RS seat**) falls vacant |
| Sitting member of one House elected to the other | Seat in the **first House** falls vacant |
| Elected to two seats in one House | Must choose one, else **both** fall vacant |
| Elected to Parliament and a state legislature | MP must resign the state seat within **14 days**, else the **Parliament** seat falls vacant (Prohibition of Simultaneous Membership Rules, 1950) |
| Absence for **60 days** | Without permission → House may declare the seat vacant |

- **Resignation:** In writing to the Speaker/Chairman; subject to acceptance (**Art 101(3)(b)**), unlike judges or the President (22 Jul 2026)
  - May be rejected if not voluntary or genuine → 33rd Amendment streamlined this
- ⚠ **10 days** = both Houses; **14 days** = Parliament + state legislature; **60 days** = absence

## Sessions — terms → meaning and procedural Articles | from: S8
# 1. Sessions
- **Art. 85(1):** **President summons** each House; Houses need not be summoned together
- **Calendar:** **No fixed calendar**; normally **three sessions** → Budget, Monsoon, Winter
- **Six-month gap (Art. 85):**
  - Keeps the government **accountable** + legislative business moving (PYQ APPSC-GS 2025)
  - Sets **no minimum number of sitting days**
# 2. Session terminology

| **Term** | **Who** | **Effect** |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | Speaker / Chairman | Suspends the sitting for a specified time (hours, days) [GK] |
| **Adjournment sine die** | Speaker / Chairman | Ends the sitting **without fixing a date** for the next; then the President prorogues (LENSD, 14 Aug 2026) |
| **Prorogation** | **President** | Ends the **session**, usually after adjournment sine die (LENSD, 14 Aug 2026) |
| **Recess** | - | Period between **prorogation and reassembly in a new session** (PYQ APPSC-G2 2017) |
| **Dissolution** | President | Ends the life of the **Lok Sabha** only; the RS is never dissolved (CDI) |

- **Termination of a sitting:** By adjournment (or adjournment sine die), or by prorogation/dissolution
# 3. Procedural provisions

| **Provision** | **Article** |
|---|---|
| President's right to address/send messages | **Art. 86** (VIS 2026) |
| Separate secretarial staff for each House | **Art. 98**[GK] |
| Quorum - **one-tenth** of total members | **Art. 100**[GK] |
| Rules of procedure | **Art. 118**[GK] |
| Language - **Hindi or English**; mother tongue with the presiding officer's permission | **Art. 120**[GK] |

- **Secretariats:** Independent of the executive
  - Lok Sabha Secretariat → under the **Speaker**
  - Rajya Sabha Secretariat → under the **Chairman**

## Functions of Parliament — legislative to electoral | from: S9
# 1. Functions

| **Function** | **Content** |
|---|---|
| Legislative | Laws on Union and Concurrent Lists (and State List in special cases); **only Parliament can change an existing law** (PYQ APPSC-GS 2024); delegated legislation is reviewed (APP; IYB) |
| Executive control | Questions, motions, no-confidence motion (LS); ministers collectively responsible (APP) |
| Financial | Budget, taxes, grants; no tax without LS approval (APP) |
| Constituent | Amends the Constitution under **Art. 368**, but cannot alter the basic structure (UPSC) |
| Judicial | Impeaches the President, removes the VP, recommends removal of judges, CEC, CAG; punishes breach of privilege (CDI) |
| Electoral | Elects the VP, takes part in the President's election, elects presiding officers (CDI) |
| Deliberative | Highest forum of debate; represents regional, social and religious diversity (APP) |

# 2. Electoral functions - detail
- **President & VP:** **Elects the Vice-President** (all members of both Houses); **participates** in electing the President (elected members only) (UPSC)
- **Presiding officers:**
  - **Lok Sabha** elects the **Speaker and Deputy Speaker**
  - **Rajya Sabha** elects only the **Deputy Chairman** (VP is ex officio Chairman) (UPSC)
- **Election laws:** **RPA 1950 and 1951**; **Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952**

## Parliamentary privileges — Art. 105, collective and individual, Art. 122 | from: S10
# 1. Basis - Art. 105
- **Art. 105:** Freedom of speech in Parliament; no court proceedings for anything said or any vote given in the House or its committees (immunity)
- **Codification debate:** Privileges are not codified → still rest on **Art. 105(3)** (originally the British House of Commons' privileges)
  - Codification urged to protect press freedom and citizens' rights
- **Bribery:** Sita Soren (2024) → no privilege for a bribe; overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998)
# 2. Collective and individual privileges

| **Collective (House as a whole)** | **Individual (members)** |
|---|---|
| Publish its reports, debates and proceedings; prohibit others from publishing [GK] | **Freedom of speech** in the House (Art. 105(1)-(2)) [GK] |
| Exclude strangers; hold secret sittings [GK] | Freedom from arrest in **civil** cases 40 days before and after a session and during it - not in criminal or preventive detention cases [GK] |
| Make its own rules; punish members and outsiders for **breach of privilege or contempt** (CDI) [GK] | Exemption from **jury service**; may refuse to give evidence / attend as **witness** while Parliament sits [GK] |
| Be informed of a member's arrest, detention, release [GK] | - |
| No arrest or legal process within precincts without the Presiding Officer's permission [GK] | - |
| Courts cannot inquire into its proceedings - **Art. 122**[GK] | - |

- **Duration:** Privileges are **co-terminous with membership**
- **Extension:** Also to ministers, Attorney-General and others who speak in the House
- **PYQ trap (APPSC-G2 2017):** "Only individual privileges, the House as a whole has none" is **wrong** → both exist
> Exam angle: "only members enjoy privileges, not the House" is the **incorrect** statement (APPSC-G2 2017).

## Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha — equal and unequal status | from: S11

| **Equal with Lok Sabha** | **Lok Sabha superior / Rajya Sabha weaker** |
|---|---|
| Introduction and passage of **ordinary bills** | **Money Bill** only in LS; RS cannot amend/reject it, must return in **14 days** |
| **Constitutional amendment bills** - special majority in each House, no joint sitting | **Financial Bill (I)** only in the LS |
| Election and impeachment of the President; election of the VP | **Speaker** certifies/decides what is a Money Bill; presides over **joint sitting**, where LS numbers prevail |
| Approval of ordinances and all three emergencies (Emergency proclamations) | RS may discuss the budget but **cannot vote on demands for grants** |
| Removal of SC/HC judges, CEC, CAG | Only LS can resolve to **discontinue a National Emergency** |
| Consideration of **CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission reports** | **No-confidence motion** only in LS; CoM responsible to LS - RS cannot remove the Council of Ministers |

- **Financial Bill (I):** Introduced only in LS, but both Houses have equal powers on its passage
- **PYQ (APPSC G1 2024):** Equal status = **consideration of the CAG's report** + approval of Emergency
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Not money bills, not initiating the VP's removal

## Special powers of Rajya Sabha — Articles 249, 312, 67(b) | from: S12

| **Article** | **Power** | **Condition** |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. 249** | Lets Parliament legislate on a **State List** subject in the national interest | Resolution by **2/3 of members present and voting** (**two-thirds of members present and voting**); valid **1 year**, extendable 1 year at a time |
| **Art. 312** | Create new **All-India Services** (common to Union and states) | Same two-thirds resolution |
| **Art. 67(b)** | **Initiate removal of the Vice-President** | Resolution can be moved **only in RS** (**only in the Rajya Sabha**) |

- **LS dissolved:** Emergency proclamation (Arts. **352, 356, 360**) stays alive if the **Rajya Sabha approves** it (approved by RS alone for the time being)
  - New Lok Sabha must approve within **30 days** of its first sitting
- **PYQ trap (APPSC-G1 2017):** RS does **not** "authorise State Assemblies" to legislate → it authorises **Parliament** (Art. 249)
  - Returning a money bill with recommendations IS its power

**Other Articles for Parliament's power over State matters**

| **Article** | **Power** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 3** | Form new states, alter areas, boundaries, names - bill needs the **President's recommendation**; state's views not binding |
| **Art. 250** | State List law during a National Emergency |
| **Art. 253** | Law to implement **international treaties** on any subject |
| **Art. 356-357** | During President's Rule, Parliament legislates for the state |
