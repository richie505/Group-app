=== ROW 133 | SC/ST rights - Articles 15, 16, 17, 46, 164, 244, 330, 332, 335; abolition of untouchability

## Untouchability and Scheduled Castes — definitions, terms and FIRSTs | from: S0, S2, S8, S13
# 1. Article 17 — provision
- **Core provision (Art. 17):**
  - "Untouchability" is **abolished**; its practice in any form **forbidden**
  - Enforcing any disability arising from it = **offence punishable by law**
- **Category:** A right in the **Right to Equality** group
- **Legislative power:** **Art. 35(a)(ii)** → only **Parliament**, not State legislatures, can make laws prescribing punishment for untouchability (Art. 17) offences (Parliament legislates under **Art. 35**)
- **Horizontal application:** Binds **private persons** as well as the State; State must act against private violators (**PUDR v Union of India**, **1982**)
# 2. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Untouchability** | **Not defined** - **Neither the Constitution nor any Act defines "untouchability"**; **Mysore HC:** covers the practice **as it developed historically**, not the literal sense; social boycott of a few individuals is excluded |
| Two angles of untouchability | Stigma of **ritual pollution** attached to people + practices the rest of society uses to "protect" itself from it |
| **Structural violence (Johan Galtung)** | Harm built into social structures |
| **Two-tumbler system** | Separate tumblers in tea shops - a form of untouchability |
| **Scheduled Castes** | Term first used by the **Simon Commission**; given shape in the **GoI Act 1935**; earlier "**Depressed Classes**" |
| Manual scavenging | "the **worst surviving symbol of untouchability**" |
- **Forms of untouchability:** Denial of **temple entry**, separate **wells/water sources**, **two-tumbler system** in tea shops, residential segregation, forced "polluting" work
- UPPSC 2019 A-R: "Untouchability is the worst form of structural violence" (A) + "it is practised in the name of religious sanction" (R) → **both true, R explains A**
# 3. FIRSTs
| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| First official SC list | **1936** |
| First SC list after the Constitution | Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, **1950** |
| **Art. 16(4)** | In the original Constitution |

| **Untouchability (Offences) Act 1955** | First law enforcing Art. 17 |

- ⚠ **Simon Commission** first used "Scheduled Castes"; **GoI Act 1935** gave it shape (the FIRST-list names the **GoI Act 1935**); first official list **1936**

## Articles 341, 342 and 330, 332, 334 — SC lists and reserved seats | from: S2, S3
# 1. SC/ST lists — notification procedure
- **Notification:** **President**, **after consultation with the Governor** (**after consulting the Governor**), specifies SCs for a State/UT by public notification
- **Later changes:** Only **Parliament by law** (**Act of Parliament**) can include or exclude a caste
- **States' power:** None - **Supreme Court** (**July 2024**) struck down **Bihar's 2015 notification** (resolution) merging **Tanti-Tantwa** into the SC list
> Exam angle: CDX AP History (p.424) credits "Article 344" with adding castes to the list - that is wrong; Art. 344 is the Official Language Commission. The power is **Art. 341** (and 342 for STs).
# 2. Religion and converts
- **ST status:** **Religion-neutral**
- **Chinthada Anand v State of AP (SC):** **Christian converts cannot claim SC status**
  - Reconversion needs proof of original SC status + bona fide reconversion + community acceptance
# 3. Reserved seats — Arts 330, 332
- **Articles:** **Art. 330** (Lok Sabha) + **Art. 332** (Assemblies)
- **Basis:** Seats in proportion to population; no separate electorates - all voters elect (CDI Block04)

## Commissions → role (NCSC, NCST and others) | from: S2, S6
# 1. Evolution of the national commission
- **Original Art. 338:** A **Special Officer** (Commissioner for SCs and STs) to investigate safeguards + report to the President
- **1978 Resolution:** Non-statutory multi-member **Commission for SCs and STs**; **1987** Resolution modified it
- **1990:** Joint national commission became **constitutional** (**NCSC**, Art. 338)
- **89th Amendment (2003):** Bifurcated it → **NCSC (Art. 338)** + **NCST (new Art. 338A)**
  - **NCST** from **19 Feb 2004**; separate NCSC functioning from **2004**
# 2. Composition and functions
- **Composition:** Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + **three** members each
  - NCST: Chairperson = **Cabinet rank**; Vice-Chairperson = **MoS rank**; members = **Secretary rank**
- **Tenure:** President fixes tenure and service conditions
- **Functions:** Investigates safeguards, inquires into complaints, advises on planning, reports to the **President** (laid before Parliament)
- **Powers:** **Civil-court powers** while inquiring
# 3. Other commissions
- **K.G. Balakrishnan Commission:** SC status for Dalit converts to **Christianity and Islam**; extended till **10 June 2026**
- **AP State SC Commission:** Statutory **State SC Commission**; chairman **K.S. Jawahar** reviewed **Nellore** schemes (CDCA Aug 2026)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** CDCA says **Art. 338(9)** empowers the President to create State commissions (the basis for State SC Commissions) - WRONG; Art. 338(9) requires the Union and States to **consult** the NCSC on major policy; State commissions are created by **State law**

=== ROW 134 | SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 and amendments; Civil Rights Protection Act 1955

## PCR Act 1955 and PoA Act 1989 — definitions, terms and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1, S3
# 1. Origin and scope — PCR Act
- **Origin:** Enacted under **Art. 17** as the **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955**; amended extensively + **renamed in 1976** as the **Protection of Civil Rights (PCR) Act, 1955**
- **Extent:** **Whole of India** (APPSC 2019 key)
- **Rules:** **PCR Rules 1977** (notified **1977**)
# 2. Enactment and applicability — PoA Act
- **Timeline:** Passed **1989**; in force **30 January 1990** (IYB: "**1990**"); **23 sections**
- **Applicability:** Only offences by **non-SC/ST persons** against SCs/STs
- **BNS/BNSS:** Since **1 July 2024**, references to **IPC** and **CrPC** read as corresponding **BNS/BNSS** provisions
- **Jammu & Kashmir:** ⚠ UPSC note that the Act excludes J&K is outdated - applies there since **2019**
# 3. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| "Untouchability" | **Not defined** in the Constitution or the Act; **Mysore HC** read it as the historically developed caste practice |
| **Economic boycott** / **Social boycott** | Defined + made atrocities by the 2015 amendment ("**economic boycott**" and "**social boycott**"); "**Economic boycott**" definition in Sec. **2(1)(bea)** (added 2015) [GK] |
| Atrocity | Offences **not in the PCRA/IPC** (now BNS) by **non-SC/ST** offenders (Sec. 3) |
| **Collective fine** | Fine on inhabitants of an area (PCR Sec. **10A**) |
| **Atrocity-prone areas** | Areas identified by States; arms licences of potential offenders can be cancelled there |
- ⚠ **PCR 1955** (renamed **1976**, Rules **1977**) vs **PoA 1989** (in force **30 January 1990**, Rules **1995**)

# 2. PCR offences and punishment
- **Nature:** Offences **cognizable**

- **Public servants:** **Enhanced minimum punishment** (plus Sec. 4 above)

## Amendments and cases → outcome (2015, 2018, Mahajan, Chauhan) | from: S3, S4
# 1. PoA Amendment Act, 2015
- **Enforcement:** Amendment Act **2015**, **in force from 2016**; many new offences + rephrased old ones
- **New atrocities:**
  - Tonsuring; garlanding with footwear
  - Denying irrigation access
  - Forcing to **carry or dispose of carcasses**, dig graves, or do manual scavenging
  - Dedicating an SC/ST woman as a **devadasi**
  - **Abusing by caste name**
  - **Witchcraft** atrocities (calling a woman a **witch**)
  - **Social or economic boycott** - "**economic boycott**" and "**social boycott**" defined + made atrocities
  - Obstructing voting; preventing SC/ST candidates from contesting
- **Courts:**
  - **Exclusive Special Courts** + **Exclusive Special PPs**
  - Trial, as far as possible, **within two months of the charge sheet**
  - Special Courts can take **direct cognizance**
- **Victims:** New chapter on **rights of victims and witnesses**
# 2. Dilution and restoration
- **Subhash Kashinath Mahajan v State of Maharashtra (20 March 2018):** Seen as **diluting** the Act (APPSC G2 2024 key)
  - **Anticipatory bail** allowed where no prima facie case
  - **Preliminary enquiry** before FIR
  - Prior approval before arrest
- **2018 Amendment Act:** Inserted **Sec. 18A**
  - **No preliminary enquiry**; **no approval** needed before arrest; FIR registered **immediately** (Madras HC ruling applied it)
  - **Anticipatory bail barred** notwithstanding any court order
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPCA calls it "**Section 4A**"; the correct number is **18A**
- **Review (Oct 2019):** SC recalled the Mahajan directions
- **Prithvi Raj Chauhan v Union of India (Feb 2020):** Upheld the 2018 amendment; courts may grant anticipatory bail or quash only where **no prima facie case** exists
# 3. Case / law → outcome
| **Case / law** | **Outcome** |
|---|---|
| **Subhash Kashinath Mahajan v State of Maharashtra (2018)** | Directions to curb misuse: **preliminary inquiry by DSP** before FIR; anticipatory bail if no prima facie case; no arrest of public servants without the **appointing authority's approval** (UPSC) |
| **PoA Amendment Act, 2018** | New **Sec. 18A** - no preliminary inquiry, no prior approval for arrest; anticipatory bail barred "notwithstanding any court order" (IYB, UPSC) |
| Prathvi Raj Chauhan v Union of India (2020) | Upheld the 2018 amendment [GK] |
| **Kiran v Rajkumar Jivaraj Jain (2025)** | Cancelled an HC anticipatory bail; Sec. 18 bar applies where a prima facie case exists; no "mini-trial" at bail stage (LENS Sep 2025) |
> Exam angle: G2 2024 - the case "alleged to have diluted" the Act = **Subhash Kashinath Mahajan**.
- ⚠ **2018** sequence: **Mahajan** (Mar **2018**) → **Sec. 18A** (2018 Act) → review (Oct **2019**) → **Chauhan** (Feb **2020**) upheld
