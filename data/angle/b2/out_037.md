=== ROW 143 | Transgender persons - NALSA judgment, Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019

## Transgender identity — definitions and terms | from: S0, S2, S4, S5, S6
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **"Third gender"** | Legal status of transgender persons recognised by **NALSA (2014)** |
| Gender identity (NALSA) | **Self-determined** (**right to self-identify** gender), not by biology or state verification |
| Transgender person - **Definition (2019)** | Person whose gender does not match that assigned at birth; includes trans men/women, intersex variations, genderqueer |
| Socio-cultural identities | **kinner, hijra, aravani, jogta** and others |
| Transgender person - 2026 definition | Limited to socio-cultural identities, specified intersex/congenital variations, and those **forced** to present as transgender; **excludes self-perceived identity** |
| SEBC | **socially and educationally backward** classes → NALSA's basis for reservation |
| **Vertical** quota | NALSA's OBC-type quota in education and jobs |
| **Horizontal** reservation | Community demand; quota cutting across SC/ST/OBC/General |
| **"Bhiksha Vritti Mukt Bharat"** | Begging-free India → goal of SMILE |
- **Objects (2019 Act):** Cited NALSA + the **right to self-perceived gender identity**
- ⚠ **2019** = self-perceived identity in the Objects; **2026** = **self-perceived identity excluded**

## Cases and Bills → year, mover, holding | from: S0, S1, S4
# 1. NALSA v. Union of India (2014)
- **Delivered:** **15 April 2014** by **Justices K.S. Radhakrishnan and A.K. Sikri**
- **Core holding:** **NALSA (2014)** recognised transgender persons as the **"third gender"**
  - Gender identity is **self-determined**, not by biology or state verification
- **Directions:**
  - Legal recognition of self-identified gender
  - Treat as SEBC (**socially and educationally backward**) for reservation in education and jobs → a **vertical** OBC-type quota
  - Separate public toilets, health facilities and welfare schemes
  - **Arts 14, 15, 16, 19(1)(a) and 21** apply to them
- **Criticism:** 2026 amendment said to go against NALSA
# 2. Rights of Transgender Persons Bill 2014
- **Mover:** Private member's bill by **Tiruchi Siva** (DMK)
- **Fate:**
  - Passed by the **Rajya Sabha in April 2015** → first private member's bill passed by RS in over four decades
  - **not passed by Lok Sabha**
# 3. Challenge to the 2026 Amendment
- **Challenge:** In the **Supreme Court** by Laxmi Narayan Tripathi and Zainab Javid Patel → contrary to NALSA
- **SC, Aug 2026:** Centre assured ID cards issued before the 2026 Act remain valid
- ⚠ **2014** = NALSA judgment + Tiruchi Siva Bill; **2015** = RS passes the Bill

## Acts and Rules → provisions: 2019 Act vs 2026 Amendment | from: S2, S4
# 1. Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019
- **In force:** **January 2020** (**10 Jan 2020**) (UPSC notes)
- **Administered by:** **MoSJE**
- **Certificate of identity:** Issued by the **District Magistrate**; revised certificate after surgery
  - **Rules 2020:** Set application procedure for the certificate through the national portal
- **Offences:** Forced/bonded labour, denial of public places, removal from household, abuse = **6 months to 2 years** + fine
# 2. Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Act 2026
- **Passage:** Bill introduced by **Virendra Kumar** in Lok Sabha, **13 Mar 2026** → President's assent by Apr 2026

| **Aspect** | **Change** |
|---|---|
| Definition | Limited to socio-cultural identities, specified intersex/congenital variations, and those **forced** to present as transgender; **excludes self-perceived identity** |
| Section 4(2) | **Omitted** - ends legal recognition of self-determination |
| Certificate | DM issues it **after a designated medical board's recommendation** |
| Offences | Adds **kidnapping** and **forcing a person to present as transgender** |
| Council | Rotating regional State members |

- **Key changes:** **self-perceived identity excluded**; Sec. 4(2) omitted; DM issues certificate on a **medical board's** advice
- ⚠ Certificate: **2019** = DM alone; **2026** = DM on a **medical board's** recommendation

=== ROW 144 | Minority rights - Articles 29 and 30, National Commission for Minorities, Sachar Committee, schemes

## Minority — meaning, notified communities, FIRST/last | from: S0, S5, S8
# 1. Meaning
- **Definition:** "Minority" **not defined in the Constitution**; only **religious and linguistic** minorities recognised
- **Constitution:** Uses "minority" in **Arts 29, 30, 350A, 350B** but never defines it
- **NCM Act:** Does not define "minority" → under **Section 2(c)**, a minority = any community **notified by the Central Government**
- **Limits:** Minority rights are **not absolute** (APPSC G2 2017) → State may regulate standards, not take away management
# 2. Unit for minority status
- **State-level:** Religious and linguistic minorities identified **at the State level** (**State-wise**), not nationally → the **State**, not the whole of India, is the unit for status (T.M.A. Pai, 2002)
  - Yet Sec. 2(c) of the NCM Act lets the Centre notify minorities nationally → critics: Sec 2(c) gives Centre "unbridled power"
- **State power:** States may notify (designate) their own minorities → Jains were State minorities in **11 States** before 2014 (UPSC)
> Exam angle: G2 2017 - minority status is decided by the State's demography (true); "minority rights are **absolute**" is the untrue statement (APP's "absolute right" wording for Art. 30 should not be used in MCQs).
# 3. Notified minority communities
| **Notified community** | **Year notified** |
|---|---|
| Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis (Zoroastrians) | **1993** (five) |
| Jains | **27 January 2014** (sixth) |

- **Latest addition:** **Jains added 27 January 2014** → **six**
- **Share:** Six notified minorities ≈ **19%** of India's population (UPSC notes); **6 notified minorities** (19%+ of population)
- **Linguistic minorities:** **not** notified under the NCM Act → come under the Special Officer (Article 350B)
- ⚠ **1993** = five communities notified + first statutory NCM; **2014** = Jains (sixth)

## Articles → provisions: 29, 30, 347, 350, 350A, 350B | from: S1, S3
# 1. Articles 29 and 30
| **Article** | **Protection** |
|---|---|
| **29(1)** | Any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture has the right to conserve it |
| **29(2)** | No denial of admission to State-maintained or State-aided institutions on grounds of religion, race, caste or language |
| **30(1)** | **All minorities, religious or linguistic**, may establish and administer educational institutions of their choice ("Charter of Education Rights"); no discrimination in grant-in-aid |
| **30(2)** | The State shall not discriminate against minority-managed institutions in granting aid |

- **Art. 29:** Protects any section of **citizens** (not only minorities) → wider; covers religious and linguistic minorities; applies only to citizens
  - Art. 29 protects minorities but is not confined to them
- **29(2):** No citizen denied admission to a State-maintained or State-aided institution on grounds **only of religion, race, caste or language**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** sex and place of birth are not listed
- **Language promises:** Political promises to conserve a section's language ≠ "corrupt practice" under the Representation of the People Act (RPA, 1951; some notes say "1957")
- **Art. 30:** Applies only to **minorities**; Madrasas are run under Art. 30
- **44th Amendment:** Added Art. **30(1A)** → removing the right to property does not affect minorities' right to establish institutions; compensation on acquisition
- ⚠ **Art. 29** = all citizens; **Art. 30** = only minorities
# 2. Linguistic minority Articles
| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| 347 | The President may recognise a language spoken by a substantial proportion of a State's population |
| 350 | Right to submit a representation for redress of grievances in any language used in the Union or State |
| **350A** | Every State and local authority to provide instruction in the **mother tongue at the primary stage** |
| **350B** | **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities**, appointed by the **President**, to investigate and report on safeguards |

- **Origin:** Arts 350A and 350B inserted by the **7th Constitutional Amendment, 1956** (States Reorganisation)
- **Art. 350B:** Special Officer investigates safeguards for linguistic minorities + **reports to the President**
  - President lays reports before Parliament + sends them to the States concerned

## Acts → provisions: Waqf laws | from: S14
# 1. Laws and Central Waqf Council
- **Evolution:** Waqf Act **1954** → **Waqf Act 1995** (current parent law)
- **Central Waqf Council:** Statutory, **1964**
  - Under Section 8A of the 1954 Act → now Section 9(1) (Sec. 9) of the **Waqf Act 1995**
  - Advises the Centre on State Waqf Boards
- **Wakf (Amendment) Act, 2013:** Amended the 1995 Act (CDX says "replaces" - loose)
  - Bars sale, gift, mortgage, exchange, transfer of waqf property (leasing allowed except mosques, dargahs etc.)
  - Central Waqf Council can direct State Boards
# 2. 2025 changes - key focus
- **Waqf by user:** Survives only for properties registered before the law
- **Non-Muslim members:** Allowed in Central Council and State Boards (at least two in each Board)
- **Disputed land:** Officers above Collector rank decide if it is government property
- **Central portal:** All waqf properties on a **central portal within 6 months** (UMEED portal launched June 2025)
- **Limitation Act:** **Limitation Act** now applies (Sec. 107 of 1995 Act repealed); appeal to the HC within 90 days
- **Supreme Court interim order (Sep 2025):**
  - Stayed the **5-years-practising-Islam** condition + the **Collector's power**
  - Capped non-Muslims at **3 in State Boards and 4 in the Central Council**
  - Did not stay removal of waqf-by-user; accepted the Limitation Act change

## Acts and Codes → provisions | from: S1, S4, S5, S6
# 1. Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act, 2008
- **Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act, 2008:** Set up a National Social Security Board; welfare schemes in Schedule I
- **Schedule I schemes:** IGNOAPS, National Family Benefit Scheme, Janani Suraksha Yojana, Janashree Bima Yojana, Aam Aadmi Bima Yojana, RSBY + schemes for weavers, artisans, fishermen
- **Status:** **merged into the Code on Social Security, 2020**; critics say limited impact
# 2. Code on Social Security 2020 - gig and platform workers
- **Recognition:** **Code on Social Security 2020** = first law to recognise "**aggregators**", "**gig workers**", "**platform workers**"
- **Registration:** Gig workers from **age 16**; National and State Social Security Boards provided
- **Limits:** **no employee status**, no minimum wage guarantee, no industrial-dispute remedies
- **Rajasthan Platform-Based Gig Workers (Registration and Welfare) Act, 2023:** Welfare board funded by a cess on transactions
- **Proposed:** **Karnataka** (Bill 2024) and **Telangana**
# 3. Industrial Relations Code
- **Prior government permission:** **layoff, retrenchment and closure** → only for **300 or more workers** (earlier 100)
  - Standing Orders threshold also = **300**
- **Contract labour:** Threshold raised **20 to 50** workers
- **Fixed-term employment:**
  - **same wages and benefits** as permanent workers
  - Gratuity after **1 year**
- **Strike notice:**
  - No strike without notice **within 60 days** before striking
  - Or **within 14 days** of giving notice
  - Unions: weakens Article 19(1)(c)
- **20 Aug 2026:** SC Constitution Bench revisited Bangalore Water Supply (1978) on "industry" → Code's definitions read on their own terms
- ⚠ **300** = layoff/closure + Standing Orders; **50** = contract labour; **60/14** days = strike notice
# 4. Bonded labour - Article 23 and the 1976 Act
- **Constitutional basis:** **Article 23** bans begar and forced labour → **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976**
- **Vigilance Committees:** District-level **Vigilance Committees** identify and release bonded labourers
- !! Central Sector Scheme for Rehabilitation of Bonded Labourers (2016) [GK]. Not in your sources: the current rehabilitation assistance amounts.

## e-Shram portal → features | from: S3
- **Launch:** **26 August 2021** by the **Ministry of Labour & Employment**
- **Nature:** India's first Aadhaar-seeded **National Database of Unorganised Workers (NDUW)**
