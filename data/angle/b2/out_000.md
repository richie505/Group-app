=== ROW 0 | Constitutional evolution - Regulating Act 1773 to the Government of India Act 1935 (act-by-act provisions)

## Company-rule Acts → year → provisions (1767–1853) | from: S0, S1, S2, S3
| **Act** | **Key provisions** | **Trade monopoly** |
|---|---|---|
| Act of **1767** | Company to pay the British Government **£400,000 a year** (APPSC-G2 2017 key) | - |
| **Regulating Act 1773** | First step by Parliament to **control the EIC** (first parliamentary control); based on the report of a committee under **Lord North**; Governor of Bengal made **Governor-General of Bengal** (Warren Hastings) with a **4-member Executive Council** (a **4-member council**); Bombay and Madras made subordinate; **Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)**; Court of Directors to report on revenue, civil and military affairs | - |
| Amending Act **1781** (Act of Settlement) | Separated judicial and executive functions | - |
| **Pitt's India Act 1784** | **Dual control / double government** - Court of Directors (commerce / commercial) and new **Board of Control** (politics / political affairs) | - |
| Act of **1786** | Governor-General (Cornwallis) given power to **override his council**; also made Commander-in-Chief | - |
| **Charter Act 1793** | GG's control over Bombay and Madras (presidencies) increased / tightened; Board of Control salaries charged to (paid from) Indian revenue; C-in-C not ordinarily a council member | Continued - **monopoly extended 20 years** |
| **Charter Act 1813** | **Missionaries** allowed in; **Rs 1 lakh** a year for literature, science and education; asserted **Crown sovereignty** over Company territories (UPSC 2019 key); civil-servant training compulsory | Ended **except tea and trade with China** (**except tea and the China trade**) |
| **Charter Act 1833** (Saint Helena Act) | GG of Bengal became **Governor-General of India** (William Bentinck); legislative power centralised in the GG-in-Council - laws for all British India; legislative powers of Madras and Bombay reduced; **Law Commission** under Macaulay (origin of the **Law Ministry**); open competition attempted; "final step towards centralisation"; permanent presence of missionaries, Anglican hierarchy expanded | **Ended completely** - **all commercial functions ended**; Company made **purely administrative** |
| **Charter Act 1853** | **Separated legislative and executive functions** of the GG's Council (**Legislative and executive functions of the GG's Council separated**); added **6 legislative members**; **open competition** for civil services; Company's rule extended with **no fixed period**; territories held **"in trust for Her Majesty... until Parliament should otherwise provide"** (APPSC-GS 2018) | - |

# 1. Regulating Act 1773 - why Parliament stepped in
- **Trigger:** Famine + fall in revenue + Company's financial crisis → British Parliament stepped in (**1772**), took control of Bengal away from the Company
  - Company sought a loan of about **£1 million** (1772)
# 2. Pitt's India Act 1784 - status and council
- **Status:** Company territories = **"British possessions in India"**; Company became a subordinate department of the State
- **GG's council:** Reduced to **3**
# 3. Override power and Charter Act renewals
- **Override power:** **Act of 1786** → **Lord Cornwallis**; extended to all future GGs + presidency Governors by Charter Act **1793**
- **Renewal:** Every **20 years** (1793, 1813, 1833); the 1853 Act fixed no term
- **1833 trade:** Ended **all** trade
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes say 1833 kept "tea and China" trade - that was the 1813 position
# 4. Indians' share in administration
- **Charter Act 1833:** No Indian debarred from office by religion, birth, descent or colour; attempted open competition (not implemented)
- **Charter Act 1853:** **Open competition** for civil services introduced, Indians included
  - Set up by **Macaulay Committee (1854)**

## Crown-rule Acts → year → provisions (1858–1935) | from: S4
| **Act** | **Key provisions** |
|---|---|
| **GoI Act 1858** | Company rule ended; **Crown** took over; **Viceroy**, **Secretary of State**, **Council of India** (details below) |
| **Indian Councils Act 1861** | Beginning of **representative institutions**; Viceroy **nominated Indians** (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao); legislative powers restored to Bombay and Madras; **portfolio system** (Canning) recognised; **ordinance** power; new councils Bengal (1862), NWFP (1866), Punjab (1897) |
| **Indian Councils Act 1892** | **Indirect election** (nomination on the recommendation of universities, municipalities, chambers); **budget discussion** and questions, but no vote [GK] (see table below) |
| **Indian Councils Act 1909** (Morley-Minto) | Councils enlarged; central council to **60**; official majority at the Centre; **separate electorate for Muslims**; formal elected representation; **Satyendra Sinha** became the first Indian on the Viceroy's Executive Council; budget discussion, supplementary questions, resolutions |
| **GoI Act 1919** (Montagu-Chelmsford) | **Dyarchy in provinces**; **bicameral** central legislature with **direct elections**; 3 of 6 Executive Council members Indian; communal representation extended; **High Commissioner for India** in London; **Public Service Commission**; provincial budget separated from central budget; statutory commission after 10 years |
| **GoI Act 1935** | **14 parts, 321 sections, 10 schedules** (APP calls them "Articles"); **All-India Federation**; **provincial autonomy**; dyarchy moved to the **Centre**; three Lists; bicameralism in 6 provinces; **RBI**, **Federal Court**, PSCs; **Council of India abolished** |

## Indian Councils Acts 1861, 1892, 1909 → provisions compared | from: S5, S6
# 1. Indian Councils Act 1861
- **Passed:** **1 August 1861** → beginning of **representative institutions**
- **New provincial councils:** **Bengal (1862)**, **Punjab (1897)**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes say "NWFP 1866"; actually North-Western Provinces' council, 1886
# 2. 1861 vs 1892
| **Feature** | **1861** | **1892** |
|---|---|---|
| Indians | **Nominated** as non-officials - **Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao** | More non-officials in central and provincial councils [GK] |
| Election | None | **Indirect election** begins (principle not named "election") [GK] |
| Budget | No discussion | **Budget discussion** allowed, no vote [GK] |
| Executive | **Portfolio system** - a council member given final say over his department(s) | - |
| Other | Viceroy's **ordinance** power (6 months) | Right to ask questions [GK] |

- **1892 indirect election:** Practice began in **1892** (APPSC GS 2019 key)
  - 1909 = first **formal** election; 1892 = indirect start (APP notes say 1909 "first introduced elected members")
- **Reception:** Congress called it inadequate (grievance began with the "sham" Councils Act of 1861)
> Exam angle: "Indirect election launched in?" The answer is **1892** (APPSC GS 2019).
# 3. Indian Councils Act 1909 - Morley-Minto Reforms
- **Central Council:** **Additional members to 60**; total **69 - 37 officials, 32 non-officials**
  - Official majority at Centre; non-official majority in provinces, blunted by the veto
- **Separate electorate:** **Separate electorate for Muslims** introduced
- **Councils:** Members could discuss the budget, ask supplementary questions and move resolutions
> Exam angle: "Which Act..." one-liners; the 2018 key: the Act that allowed some **elected representation** = **1909**.
- ⚠ **Indirect election** = **1892**; **separate electorate** + formal election = **1909**

## GoI Act 1919 and dyarchy → provinces vs Centre | from: S7, S8
# 1. Montagu's declaration → 1919 Act
- **Date:** **20 August 1917**
- **Aim:** **Gradual introduction of responsible government** → basis of the 1919 Act
# 2. Central legislature (1919)
- **Council of State** (upper) + **Legislative Assembly** (lower)
- Mostly **directly elected**; limited franchise (property, tax, education)
# 3. Dyarchy - introduced by, ended by
| **Level** | **Introduced by** | **Ended by** |
|---|---|---|
| **Provinces** | **GoI Act 1919** - subjects split into **transferred** (Governor + Ministers) and **reserved** (Governor + Executive Council) | **GoI Act 1935** - replaced by **provincial autonomy** and responsible government |
| **Centre** | **GoI Act 1935** (reserved and transferred subjects under the GG) | Never took effect - the Federation never came up [GK] |

- **Provincial autonomy:** Dyarchy in the provinces **abolished**
  - Passed 1935 → responsible governments in force from **April 1937**
  - Key for "dismantled dual rule in the provinces"
- **PYQ:** Responsible governments in the provinces after ending dual rule = **GoI Act 1935** (APPSC-GS 2025; paper printed no key)
- **Dyarchy at the Centre:** Reserved + transferred subjects → never came into operation
  - **Defence and foreign affairs** = **reserved subjects under the Governor-General** (GG), not the federal legislature
- ⚠ Dyarchy: **1919** = provinces; **1935** = Centre (never worked)

## GoI Act 1935 → features, implemented or not | from: S9, S10
# 1. Basis and federation
- **Basis:** **3rd Round Table Conference and the White Paper**
- **All India Federation:** Provinces + princely states as units
  - Joining **optional for the states** → none joined → **federation never came into being**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "All India Federation of provinces and princely states" = **correct**; "Defence and Foreign Affairs under the federal legislature" = WRONG → answer "1 only" (UPSC 2024)
> Exam angle: UPSC 2024 paired "All India Federation" (correct) with "Defence and Foreign Affairs under the federal legislature" (wrong).
# 2. Lists and executive
- **Three lists:** Federal, Provincial, Concurrent; **Residuary powers** with the Governor-General
- **Central executive:** Vested in the **Governor-General** (APPSC 2007 key: "Governor-General in Council")
# 3. Legislatures and representation
- **Bicameral centre:** Federal Assembly + Council of States
- **Bicameral provinces (6):** Bengal, Bombay, Madras, **Bihar**, Assam, United Provinces
  - Bihar's Legislative Council (bicameral Bihar) came from the **1935 Act** (BPSC 2024 key)
- **Communal representation:** Extended to **depressed classes, women and labour**
- **Franchise:** Rose from **3% to 14%** of the population (some texts say about **10%**)
- **Separations:** **Burma separated** from India; **Sindh** from Bombay; Orissa from Bihar
# 4. Provisions - implemented or not
| **Provision** | **Implemented?** |
|---|---|
| Federal Court at Delhi (chief justice + judges) | Yes, set up **1937**[GK] |
| **Council of India** (from the 1858 Act) abolished; Secretary of State given advisers | Yes (UPSC, APP) |
| All India Federation | No - none of the states joined |
| Dyarchy at the Centre | No - never came into operation |

# 5. Borrowed into the Constitution
- **Features taken:** Federal scheme, office of **Governor**, judiciary, PSCs, **emergency provisions**, administrative details
- **Example:** Sec. **63** (Governor's address) → **Art. 175**

=== ROW 1 | Indian Independence Act 1947; the Constituent Assembly - demand, composition, functioning

## Indian Independence Act 1947 → dominion status, Governor-General, princely states | from: S0, S1
# 1. Dominion status
- **Dominion status:** India a **Dominion** from 15 August 1947, British monarch as nominal head; Pakistan too
  - Crown lost its **veto** over dominion laws
- **End:** Dominion status ended when India became a **Republic on 26 January 1950**
# 2. Governor-General
- Plan kept **one Governor-General** possible for both dominions (existing GG could be reappointed); each got its own GG in the event
- GG to act on dominion ministers' advice
# 3. Princely states - options and instruments
- **Paramountcy lapsed:** About **565** states in theory free; in practice expected to join **India or Pakistan** (APP says no independence option)
| **Instrument** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Instrument of Accession** | Ceded **defence, external affairs and communications** to the dominion |
| **Standstill Agreement** | Existing administrative arrangements continued until final terms; Hyderabad signed one in **November 1947** |

- **Kashmir:** Acceded **26 October 1947** after the tribal invasion; UN-arranged ceasefire **1 January 1949**

=== ROW 2 | Sources of the Indian Constitution - borrowed features and their origin countries

## GoI Act 1935 → the single largest source | from: S0
- **Single largest source:** **Federal scheme**, **office of Governor**, **judiciary**, **PSCs**, **emergency provisions**, **administrative details**
- **Three Lists:** Federal, Provincial, Concurrent → Union, State and Concurrent Lists
- **Bulk:** Its influence is one reason for the Constitution's bulk (administrative detail other countries leave to ordinary law)
- **Integrated judiciary; Federal Court as model:** GoI Act 1935

## Universal angles | from: S7 | universal
- **Significance:** "Drawn from various sources" (a listed salient feature)
  - Philosophical part (FRs, DPSPs) + political part (parliamentary system) borrowed; structure largely from the 1935 Act
- **Criticism & debates:** Called a "**borrowed constitution**" or "**bag of borrowings**"
  - Reply: features adapted to Indian conditions; many provisions India's own (DPSP as a "novel feature", the federal-unitary switch)
- **Comparative note:**
  - Single citizenship (Britain) vs dual citizenship of the USA
  - Ireland itself took DPSP from Spain
  - Original text **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules** → now about **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules**
