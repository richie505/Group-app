=== ROW 90 | Right to Information Act 2005 - structure, exemptions, amendments and issues

## RTI landmarks, FIRSTs and persons — Sweden 1766 to the 2005 Act | from: S0, S1, S6
| **Order** | **Year** | **Global/Indian landmark** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1766** | **Sweden, 1766**: world's FIRST freedom-of-information / RTI law (Freedom of the Press Act) |
| 2 | 1948 | **UN, 1948** (UDHR Art. 19) recognises the right to seek and receive information; treats information as a human right |
| - | 1990s | **MKSS** (Aruna Roy, Nikhil Dey, Shankar Singh) in Rajasthan: jan sunwais demanding muster rolls and bills |
| - | **1997** | **Tamil Nadu**: first state RTI law (APPSC 2023); Goa the same year |
| 3 | **2002** | India - **Freedom of Information Act 2002**: never notified; repealed by the RTI Act 2005 (APPSC 2023) |
| 4 | 2005 | India - Right to Information Act, **2005** |

- **Order (APPSC 2025):** Sweden → UN → FOI Act → RTI Act
- **Predecessor:** Replaced the **Freedom of Information Act, 2002** (never brought into force)
- **Earlier state laws:**
  - First: **Tamil Nadu and Goa (1997)**
  - Then: Rajasthan, Karnataka (2000), Delhi (2001), Maharashtra, Assam (2002)
- ⚠ **Trap:** **1766** = Sweden (world first); **1997** = Tamil Nadu + Goa (Indian state first); **2002** FOI Act = never notified
# 1. Persons and bodies → role
| **Person / body** | **Role** |
|---|---|
| **MKSS (Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan)** | Rajasthan, led by **Aruna Roy**, Nikhil Dey, Shankar Singh; public hearings (**jan sunwai**) on muster rolls started the RTI movement |
| **Wajahat Habibullah** | First CIC |

# 2. Enactment timeline
- **Passed:** By Parliament in **May 2005**
  - Lok Sabha: **11 May 2005**
  - Rajya Sabha: **12 May 2005**
  - **President's assent 15 June 2005**
  - Fully in force: **12 October 2005**
- **Immediate commencement:** Some sections (e.g. 4(1), 5, 12, 13, 15, 16, 24) came into force at once
- **CIC set up:** **2005** (constituted 12 October 2005)
- ⚠ **Trap:** **11 May** LS → **12 May** RS → **15 June** assent → **12 October** in force
# 3. Preamble and nature
- **Preamble:** **Informed citizenry** + transparency to **contain corruption** + hold governments **accountable**, harmonised with other public interests and confidentiality needs
  - Also listed: transparency, accountability, reduced corruption, participation, better service delivery
- **Nature:** **Statutory (legal) right** under the Act, giving effect to the fundamental right to know that the SC reads into **Article 19(1)(a)**
- **Judicial roots:** Right to know is part of **Art. 19(1)(a)** (and Art. 21) (cases on the cases page)

## RTI Act sections → content; terms → meaning | from: S2
| **Section** | **Subject / content** |
|---|---|
| 2(f) | Definitions: **"information"** |
| **2(h)** | **"Public authority"**: bodies set up by the Constitution, a law or a government notification, and bodies **substantially financed or controlled** by government (May 2026) |
| 2(j) | **"Right to information"** |
| 3 | Every citizen has the right to information |
| 4 | **Proactive (suo motu) disclosure** (APPSC 2025); 4(1)(b) lists what must be published |
| 5 | Designation of **PIOs/APIOs** in every public authority |
| 6 | Request for information - written/electronic; **no reason need be given** (6(2)) |
| 7 | Disposal of request: 30 days / 48 hours |
| 8 | **Exemptions**; 8(2) public-interest override; 8(3) 20-year rule |
| 9 | Rejection where disclosure would infringe **copyright of a person other than the State** |
| 11 | **Third-party information** |
| 12 / 15 | Constitution of **CIC** / **SIC** |
| **18** | Commission's powers and functions: receive and inquire into **complaints** |
| 19 | **Appeals** (two-tier: First Appellate Authority, then Information Commission) |
| **20** | **Penalties** on PIOs; disciplinary action |
| 22 | Overriding effect: Act **overrides the Official Secrets Act, 1923** and other laws |
| 24 | Act does not apply to intelligence and security organisations in the **Second Schedule** (IB, RAW), except for **corruption and human-rights** allegations |
| **25** | **Monitoring and reporting**: annual report to government, laid before the legislature |

# 1. Terms → meaning
- **Information (2(f)):** Any material in any form
  - Records, documents, e-mails, opinions, advices, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, samples, models, electronic data
  - + information on a **private body accessible to a public authority under law**
- **Right to information (2(j)):**
  - **Inspection** of work, documents, records
  - Taking **notes, extracts or certified copies**
  - Taking **certified samples** of material
  - Obtaining information in electronic form
# 2. Filing a request
- **Request:** By post, in person or e-mail, in **Hindi, English or the local official language**
  - Only **citizens** can apply; information free / fee waived for **BPL** applicants
- ⚠ **Trap:** **18** = complaints; **19** = appeals; **20** = penalties; **22** = overrides OSA 1923; **25** = annual report

|---|---|

## Exemptions — Section 8(1) clauses, 8(2), 8(3), Sections 9 and 24 | from: S4
# 1. Section 8(1) exemptions
| **Clause** | **Exempt information** |
|---|---|
| (a) | Sovereignty, security, **strategic, scientific or economic interests**, foreign relations, incitement |
| (b) | Forbidden by a court |
| (c) | Breach of privilege of Parliament/Legislature (**legislative privilege**) |
| (d) | Commercial confidence, trade secrets, **intellectual property** (IP) |
| (e) | **Fiduciary relationship (8(1)(e))** |
| (f) | Information from a foreign government in confidence |
| (g) | Danger to life/safety or identity of a source |
| (h) | Would impede investigation or prosecution |
| (i) | **Cabinet papers** |
| (j) | **Personal information (8(1)(j))** |

# 2. Overrides and other exemptions
- **Section 8(2):** Commission/authority may disclose if **public interest outweighs the harm** to protected interests, even if covered by the **OSA**
- **Section 8(3):** Exempt information disclosed after **20 years**
  - Except: cabinet deliberations, security/strategic/economic interests, foreign relations, incitement
- **Section 9:** Copyright of a person other than the State
- **Section 24 / Second Schedule:** Intelligence and security bodies (May 2026)
  - **IB, R&AW**, DRI, ED, NCB, BSF, CRPF, NSG
  - **CBI added in 2011**
  - Exception: information on **corruption and human-rights violations** must still be given
- ⚠ **Trap:** **8(1)(e)** = fiduciary; **8(1)(i)** = cabinet papers; **8(1)(j)** = personal information

| **Courts** | Supreme Court/CJI's office is covered (2019) |

| **MPLADS** | Works are covered by RTI |

# 1. Political parties under RTI - CIC order and compliance
- **2005 position:** Act did not name political parties; parties claimed exemption
- **CIC, June 2013:** Six national parties (INC, BJP, CPI, CPI(M), NCP, BSP) declared **public authorities** under 2(h)
  - Reason: indirect government funding (land, bungalows, free airtime)
  - Parties **did not comply**; question pending before the Supreme Court
  - RTI (Amendment) Bill 2013 to exclude them **lapsed** (some notes say the 2013 Bill removed them)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Older notes say a 2018 SC verdict made parties disclose funding under RTI - no such final ruling; follow the CIC-2013 fact
- **Why activists want it:** Prevent corruption + crony capitalism; check illegal foreign contributions; parties enjoy state privileges (free airtime on DD)
- **2026 demand:** Bring **recognised national parties under RTI** with a transparent, auditable electoral-funding system

## Information Commissions — composition, appointment, term, removal (vs CVC) | from: S6, S15
| **Item** | **Central Information Commission** | **State Information Commission** |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | Chief IC + **up to 10** ICs | State Chief IC + **up to 10** ICs (LENS Oct 2025) |
| Appointing authority | **President** (Section 12) | **Governor** (Section 15) |
| Recommending committee | **PM (chair)**, **Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha**, a **Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM**[GK] | **Chief Minister (chair)**, **Leader of Opposition in Assembly**, a **State Cabinet Minister nominated by the CM**[GK] |
| Removal | President, for proved misbehaviour/incapacity after **Supreme Court inquiry**; also insolvency, conviction (moral turpitude), paid employment, infirmity [GK] | Governor, same grounds [GK] |

# 1. Nature and composition
- **Nature:** CIC and SICs are **statutory bodies** created by the RTI Act (not constitutional)
  - Hear second appeals and complaints; **powers of a civil court**
- **Composition:** **Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 Information Commissioners** (both levels)
- **SIC selection committee:** CM, LoP, Minister
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Chief Justice of the High Court is **not** on the SIC committee (G2 2025 key)
- **SIC removal (Section 17):** By the **Governor**; for proved misbehaviour or incapacity only after a **Supreme Court inquiry** on Governor's reference
# 2. Eligibility and term
- **Eligibility:** Persons of eminence in public life with experience in **law, science and technology, social service, management, journalism, mass media or administration**
  - Cannot be MP/MLA, hold an office of profit, be connected with a political party, or carry on a business or profession
- **Term:**
  - Originally **5 years or age 65**, no reappointment
  - After 2019 amendment: Centre notifies term = **3 years** (RTI Rules, 2019)
- **PIOs:** Designated by each public authority (Section 5), not by the President/Governor
# 3. Comparison - CVC
| **Item** | **CVC** |
|---|---|
| Composition | A **Central Vigilance Commissioner** + up to **two Vigilance Commissioners** |
| Term | **4 years or age 65** |
| Removal | By the President for misbehaviour or incapacity after a **Supreme Court inquiry**; also on grounds like insolvency |

- ⚠ **Trap:** CIC/SIC = **up to 10** members, term now **3 years**; CVC = **two** VCs, **4 years or age 65**

## Penalties on PIOs and compensation — Sections 20 and 19(8)(b) | from: S7
- **Penalty (Section 20):** Imposed by the **CIC/SIC** on the PIO
  - **₹250 per day** until application received or information furnished
  - Maximum **₹25,000**
- **Grounds:**
  - Refusing to receive an application
  - Not replying in time
  - **Malafide denial**
  - Knowingly giving incorrect/misleading information
  - Destroying information; obstructing
- **Procedure:** PIO must be heard first; burden of proof on PIO
  - Commission can also recommend **disciplinary action**
- **Compensation:** Commission can require the public authority to compensate the applicant for any loss (**Section 19(8)(b)**)
- ⚠ **Trap:** **₹250**/day, cap **₹25,000** = Section **20**; compensation = **19(8)(b)**

## Amendments → changes — RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 and DPDP Act 2023 | from: S8, S9
# 1. RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 - tenure and salaries
| **Item** | **Original 2005 Act** | **After RTI (Amendment) Act 2019** |
|---|---|---|
| Term of CIC/ICs | Fixed **5 years** or age 65 [GK] | As notified by the **Central Government** (3 years under 2019 Rules) [GK] |
| Salary, central level | CIC = **Chief Election Commissioner**; IC = Election Commissioner [GK] | Fixed by the Central Government [GK] |
| Salary, state level | State CIC = Election Commissioner; State IC = **Chief Secretary** | Fixed by the Central Government |
| State ICs | Fixed in the Act [GK] | Also decided by the Centre [GK] |

- **Criticism / autonomy concern:** The **2019 amendment** let the Centre fix tenure and salaries of Information Commissioners - weakens Commissions' autonomy by putting tenure and pay in executive hands
# 2. DPDP Act 2023 and Section 8(1)(j)
- **DPDP Act 2023, Section 44(3):** Rewrote **Section 8(1)(j)** into a blanket exemption for "personal information"
  - Original clause: withheld personal information only if unrelated to public activity or an unwarranted invasion of privacy, with a **public-interest override**
  - Amendment: exempts personal information outright; drops "no relationship to public activity" test + override
- **Timeline:**
  - Centre notified amendment in **Nov 2025**
  - Attorney-General opined it does not dilute RTI (Jan 2026)
  - **Supreme Court referred the challenge to a Constitution Bench** (Feb 2026), noting its "constitutional sensitivity"
  - 7 Aug 2026: SC agreed to examine whether DPDP's "en bloc embargo" is repugnant to RTI
- ⚠ **Trap:** 2019 = tenure and salaries (Centre); 2023 DPDP **Section 44(3)** = rewrote **8(1)(j)**

## Issues and debates — misuse, pendency, activists' safety | from: S12, S13, S14
# 1. Achievements vs misuse
- **Achievements:**
  - Breaks the **culture of secrecy**; officials **accountable**
  - Reduces corruption and misuse of public funds
  - Strengthens citizen participation + service delivery
  - Has **raised the sense of responsibility in the bureaucracy**, but "miles to go" (UPPSC 2010 key: both true, R not the explanation)
- **Misuse:** Act enacted for **transparency and accountability**, misused **by both the public and public authorities** (APPSC 2019 key: both A and R true)

| **Misuse by applicants** | **Misuse by public authorities** |
|---|---|
| Frivolous, vexatious or bulk applications; RTI used for personal disputes or blackmail | **Excessive, bad-faith use of Section 8** to deny information (CDI) |
| Clogging offices with indiscriminate demands | Delays, incomplete or misleading replies; weak proactive disclosure |

> Exam angle: Assertion-Reason pairs of "purpose of the Act" with "misuse" or "limited impact" - usually both true.
# 2. Pendency, vacancies and the re-examination debate
- **Pendency:**
  - Over **4 lakh** RTI appeals/complaints pending across commissions (Jul 2026)
  - Over **26,000** RTI cases pending as of 2025, as cited by the Congress president
  - **Massive backlog of appeals** → long delays
- **Vacancies:** **Chronic vacancies** in CIC/SICs
- **Satark Nagrik Sangathan 2025 report:** Flagged significant gaps in Information Commissions as the Act approached its **20th anniversary (2025)**
- **Debate 2026 - Economic Survey 2025-26:** Asked for "re-examination" of RTI (p.134)
  - India's "uniquely broad" RTI regime → **chilling effect** on honest decision-making
  - Exempt confidential service records + draft/exploratory policy discussions via a possible **"ministerial veto"**
  - Opposition called it dilution of accountability
# 3. Activists' safety
- **Activists:** **More than 100 RTI activists killed**
- **Threats:** **Harassment, legal intimidation and physical attacks** on activists and whistle-blowers
- **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014:** Exists but not operationalised
  - A 2015 amendment bill sought to dilute it
# 4. 2nd ARC recommendations
- Narrow Sec 8; maximise Sec 4 disclosure; protect activists
- Link RTI firmly with **whistle-blower protection law** to prevent victimisation
  - Call repeated on 30 Jul 2026 to implement 2nd ARC whistle-blower recommendations

## Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 and the 2018 amendment → provisions | from: S1, S2
# 1. PCA 1988 - scope
- **Origin:** Tougher bribery laws (Santhanam Committee) → consolidated as **PCA 1988**
- **Public servant (Sec 2(c)):** Any government or local-authority employee, **any Judge**, or anyone whose office requires a **public duty**
- **Offences:** **Bribery**, **undue advantage**, **criminal misconduct**
# 2. Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, 2018
- **Purpose:** Align with **UNCAC**
  - UNCAC adopted 2003; signed by India 2005; India **ratified it in 2011**

| **Change** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| **Bribe-giving** | Made a direct offence, up to **7 years**. A coerced bribe-giver is protected if they report within **7 days**[GK] |
| **Commercial organisations** | Liable/punishable for bribes offered on their behalf; offence framed around **"undue advantage"** (LENS Jul 2026) |
| **Sec 17A** | **Government's prior approval** needed before inquiry or investigation into a public servant's decisions in official functions (VIS 2026) |
| **Punishment** | Bribed public servant = **3 to 7 years** + fine |
| Trial | Within **2 years**, extendable to a maximum of 4 years [GK] |

- ⚠ **Trap:** UNCAC **2003** adopted → **2005** signed → **2011** ratified

# 2. Definition and competent authority
- **Whistleblower / whistle-blower:** A **whistle-blower** is any public servant or other person (including an NGO) who makes a **public interest disclosure** to the public or a higher authority (APPSC 2019 key)
  - On **corruption, wilful misuse of power/discretion or a criminal offence** by a public servant

| **Public servant complained against** | **Competent authority** [GK] |
|---|---|

# 3. Protections, complaints and penalties
- **Protection:**
  - Act **protects the complainant's identity**: **identity kept confidential** (complainant's identity goes to the competent authority, which keeps it secret)
  - Protection against **victimisation**
- **Complaints:**
  - Must be made within **7 years**
  - No anonymous complaints
  - False or frivolous complaints punishable
- **Penalties:**
  - Revealing complainant's identity = up to **3 years** + **₹50,000**
  - Knowingly false complaint = up to **2 years** + **₹30,000**
- **Exclusion:** The **SPG**
- ⚠ **Trap:** Identity revealed = **3 years + ₹50,000**; false complaint = **2 years + ₹30,000**
# 4. 2015 Amendment Bill and weaknesses
- **2015 Amendment Bill:** Passed by the **Lok Sabha** in **May 2015**
  - Would bar disclosures in RTI Sec 8(1)-type categories (sovereignty, security, Official Secrets, Cabinet papers)
  - **Lapsed in 2019** with the 16th Lok Sabha
- **Weakness:** "Victimisation" not defined
- **Context:** More than **100 RTI activists** killed

## LPG — terms → meaning; PSU categories | from: S0

## 1991 crisis — persons, figures, stabilisation vs structural adjustment | from: S2
# 1. Persons → role

# 2. Crisis data and immediate steps
- **Trigger:** **NRI deposit flight**; forex reserves down to **$975 million (June 1991)** = about two weeks of imports

  - **67 tonnes of gold** pledged (Bank of England, UBS)

| **Aspect** | **Stabilisation** | **Structural adjustment** |
|---|---|---|

| Measures | Devaluation, fiscal deficit cuts [GK] | Delicensing, trade, financial, tax and PSU reforms [GK] |

## 1991 reform measures — part of LPG or not | from: S3
# 1. Measures - part of LPG or not
| **Measure** | **Part of 1991 LPG?** |
|---|---|
| Industrial **de-licensing** | Yes: licensing kept only for alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, electronic aerospace/defence, drugs (APP) |

| **Current account convertibility** | Yes: achieved in **1994**[GK] |
| **Capital account convertibility** | **No**: never fully adopted; studied by Tarapore committees [GK] |

- ⚠ **Trap:** Current account convertibility = **1994**; capital account = **No**

- **Exports:** Export duties removed
