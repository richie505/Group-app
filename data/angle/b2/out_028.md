=== ROW 98 | Constitutional, statutory, executive, regulatory and quasi-judicial bodies - definitions and how to distinguish them

## Body types — definitions and spotting the odd one out | from: S0, S1, S2, S7
| **Type** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional body** | Provided for by the Constitution itself; composition, powers, independence set in an Article/Part (e.g. Part XV, **Art. 324**, Election Commission) |
| **Statutory body** | Created by an Act of Parliament → changeable by amending that Act |
| Executive / non-statutory body | Resolution-based, advisory (NITI Aayog, Planning Commission, NDC, Law Commission) |

| **Constitutional** | **Statutory** | **Executive (neither)** |
|---|---|---|
| Finance Commission, CAG, **NCBC** (since 2018), **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities**, **Inter-State Council** (Art. 263) | **NHRC** (1993 Act), **NCM** (1992 Act), Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act 1956) | **NITI Aayog**, **Planning Commission**, NDC |

- **Odd one out:** **NITI Aayog** among NCBC, CAG, Finance Commission (APPSC-GS 2025)
- **Count question:** among NITI Aayog, NCM, Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, NHRC → **only one** constitutional = Special Officer, **Art. 350B** (APPSC-G2 2023)
- **Linguistic minorities:** not notified under NCM Act → overseen by Art. 350B officer (LENS, 21 Jul 2026)
- ⚠ **NCM** = statutory (1992 Act); **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities** = constitutional (Art. 350B)
> Exam angle: "which is NOT a constitutional body" and "how many are constitutional" counts.

## Quasi-judicial bodies → features and civil court powers | from: S4, S2
- **Definition:** **non-judicial** body that can interpret + apply law, examine, **summon**, impose sanctions
  - Authority from a statute, but **not a court**
- **Examples:** **NHRC, SEBI, CIC**, **Mental Health Review Commission**
- **Bodies with civil court powers** (summon witnesses, requisition records):
  - NHRC
  - NCM
  - **NCBC** (since 2018)
  - NCSC and NCST
  - NCW
  - **CIC** (under RTI Act)
- **Not quasi-judicial:** advisory bodies such as Law Commission, NITI Aayog
  - Law Commission: **Advisory** research body that makes recommendations to the Government. Not constitutional, not statutory, **not quasi-judicial**; reports to the **Ministry of Law and Justice**, not the President [GK]
> Exam angle: APPSC-G1 2024 keyed "All incorrect" for statements calling the Law Commission a quasi-judicial body that advises the President.

## Functions and powers → RBI, MPC, SEBI, IRDAI | from: S1, S2, S3
# 1. RBI functions
- **Functions:**
  - Monetary authority
  - Currency issue, except the **₹1** note and coins (issued by Ministry of Finance)
  - Regulator and supervisor of banks and NBFCs
  - Forex manager under **FEMA 1999**
  - **Banker and debt manager** to the government via its **Public Debt Office**
- **Public Debt Management Cell:** Set up to reduce RBI's conflict of interest between monetary policy and debt management
# 2. Monetary Policy Committee
- **MPC:** Statutory **six-member** body under the RBI Act, 1934; fixes the **repo rate**
- **Composition:**
  - **3 from the RBI**: Governor (chair, casting vote) + a Deputy Governor + one officer
  - **3 external members** appointed by the Centre for **4 years**
- **Objectives:** **Price stability** primary; growth secondary
# 3. SEBI mandate & powers
- **Regulates:**
  - Securities market and stock exchanges
  - Listed companies and intermediaries
  - Mutual funds
- **Predecessor:** Replaced the **Controller of Capital Issues**
- **Serves:** Issuers, investors and intermediaries
- **Quasi-judicial powers:** Investigates, summons, passes orders and penalties
- **Appeal route:** **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)** → **Supreme Court**
  - IRDAI and PFRDA orders also appealable to SAT since **2014**
# 4. IRDAI
- **Statutory body:** Under the **IRDA Act, 1999**; apex regulator of **insurance and reinsurance** companies
- **Private entry:** The Act opened insurance to private participation

## Issues → independence, accountability and overlap | from: S0, S1, S4
- **Standard safeguards for independence:** Fixed tenure + selection-committee appointment + removal only on statutory grounds
- **Accountability mechanisms:** Annual reports + accounts laid before Parliament; examined by **Departmentally Related Standing Committees**
- **Regulatory overlap and conflicts:**
  - **FSDC** (2010) coordinates financial regulators
  - **CCI vs sector regulators:** CCI penalised **WhatsApp** for abuse of dominance via data sharing; **DPDP Act** separately restricts cross-use of data
  - **TRAI vs MIB:** resolved by the TV Ratings Policy, 2026

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Significance:** Regulation = key point where the State meets the economy → regulatory capacity is part of State capacity (ES 2025-26)
- **Current relevance:** Joint Committee's draft report on **VBSA Bill** flagged risks to institutional autonomy (Jul 2026)
