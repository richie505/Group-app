## High Courts — Articles, number, FIRSTs and common High Courts | from: S0
# 1. Articles on High Courts

| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 214** | A High Court **for each State** |
| **Art. 230** | **UTs:** **7th Amendment Act, 1956** let Parliament extend an HC's jurisdiction to a **UT** or exclude it (APPSC-GS 2023) |

## Judicial review — meaning, origin, theory and basis | from: S6
# 1. Meaning and term
- **Meaning:** courts test laws and executive orders against the Constitution → declare them **ultra vires / void**
- **Term:** "Judicial review" is **not used** in the Constitution → power flows from several Articles
# 2. Origin and other countries

| **Point** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| **Origin / Source** | **USA** - Marbury v. Madison (**1803**, Chief Justice John Marshall) |
| Roots | Sir Edward Coke's resistance to King James I (17th century England) (TH, 31 Jan 2026) |
| **Other countries** | written constitutions (e.g. Canada, Australia) |
| Britain | none over Acts of Parliament (parliamentary sovereignty) |

# 3. Theory and basis
- **India's model:** a **synthesis** of **American judicial supremacy** + **British parliamentary sovereignty** (parliamentary supremacy)
- **Basis:** **Written constitution** + **federalism** + Fundamental Rights
- **Symbol of:** **federal government** (APPSC key)
  - Courts = umpire of Centre-State division + guardian of a **written, supreme Constitution**
- **Theory:** **supremacy of the Constitution / limited government**
- **Basic structure:** **Part of the basic structure** (Kesavananda 1973; L. Chandra Kumar 1997)
- ⚠ Judicial review = symbol of **federal government** (APPSC key); theory = **supremacy of the Constitution**

## Limits, amendments and overcoming a judgment | from: S7, S8, S9
# 1. Limits of judicial review
- **Narrower than USA:** Art. 21 "**procedure established by law**" (validity + procedure) vs US "**due process of law**" (procedural + substantive fairness)
  - US SC called a "third chamber"
- **Political questions:** courts avoid policy/political questions
  - Delimitation review limited but possible if capricious (**Kishorchandra Chhanganlal Rathod, 2024**; TH, 17 Apr 2026)
- **Writs discretionary:** refused where an effective alternative remedy exists (TH, 29 Jan 2026)
# 2. Amendments → effect on judicial review

| **Amendment** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **1st Amendment (1951)** | followed early land-reform and reservation rulings (e.g. Kameshwar Singh on land reform) |
| **38th Amendment (1975)** | made President's/Governor's satisfaction under Arts. 123, 213, 352 non-justiciable |
| **42nd Amendment (1976)** | barred HCs from reviewing central laws |
| **43rd (1977)** | restored the power |
| **44th Amendment (1978)** | reversed it (the 38th Amendment bar) |

- **Still reviewable:** decisions on mala fides or irrelevant grounds - e.g. President's Rule in **S.R. Bommai (1994)**
- ⚠ **42nd** barred → **43rd** restored; **38th** barred → **44th** reversed
# 3. Overcoming a judgment
- **Core rule:** Legislature **cannot simply overrule** a judgment
- **Options:**
  - **Ordinary/validating law:** **Remove the basis** of the judgment by amending the law, retrospectively if needed (validating Act)
  - **Constitutional amendment / Amend the Constitution:** circumvents the decision, subject to the **basic structure**
  - **Review petition:** under **Art. 137**; curative petition as last resort
- **Limit:** amendment and law both valid, but neither may breach the **Basic Structure**
- **Example:** **Tribunals Reforms Act 2021:** re-enacted conditions the Court had struck down → struck down again in **Madras Bar Association (2025)** (TH 12 Aug 2026)

## Judicial activism and PIL — terms → meaning and instruments | from: S10, S11, S12
# 1. Terms → meaning

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Judicial activism** | wider judicial intervention shaping public policy; **Also called:** **judicial dynamism** |
| **Judicial restraint** | **antithesis** of activism; also developed in the USA |
| **Judicial review** | tests laws against the Constitution |
| **PIL** | **Definition (APPSC 2019):** Legal action for enforcement of **public or general interest** in which the public or a class has a pecuniary or other interest |
| **Social Action Litigation (SAL)** | **Other name** of PIL (APPSC-G2 2023); outcome of **judicial activism**, not judicial restraint |
| **Locus standi** | Relaxed ("place to stand") → any public-spirited citizen can move court for those who cannot |
| **Epistolary jurisdiction** | **Letters, postcards and newspaper reports** treated as petitions |

- **PIL filed under:** **Art. 32 (SC)** or **Art. 226 (HC)**
- ⚠ PIL = SAL = outcome of **judicial activism**, not judicial restraint
# 2. Judicial review / restraint vs judicial activism

| **Basis** | **Judicial review / restraint** | **Judicial activism** |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Interpretative, legalistic | Creative, proactive, reform-oriented |
| Question | "Is the law valid?" | "Is the law just and adequate for today?" |
| Judge's role | Declare what the law is; follow framers' original intent | Force legislature/executive to perform duties |

# 3. Instruments of activism

| **Instrument** | **Use** |
|---|---|
| Suo motu cognisance | Court acts on its own; advised only in exceptional cases of constitutional importance (CDI) |
| Continuing mandamus | Case kept open to monitor compliance |
| Guidelines | Fill legislative vacuum - **Vishaka Guidelines (1997)** (**Vishaka (1997):** workplace harassment) before the **POSH Act 2013** |
| Art. 142 "complete justice" | Interim remedy for legislative delay; resolves deadlocks between branches (LENSD, 27 May 2026) |
| Expert committees | Court-appointed panels to inform and monitor directions [GK] |

## Judicial activism and PIL — origin, FIRSTs, stages and doctrines | from: S10, S11, S12
# 1. Origin and FIRSTs

| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | **Judicial activism** began and developed in the **USA** (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| Term coined | in **1947** by **Arthur Schlesinger Jr.** (American historian) |
| India | took root **post-Emergency, mid-1970s** |
| **First case** (PIL) | **Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)** → undertrials' right to **speedy trial**; prisoners' case filed on behalf of others by **Kapila Hingorani** ("Mother of PILs") |
| **Era** | CJI **Y.V. Chandrachud**'s tenure (1978-85); Bhagwati became CJI in 1985 |

# 2. Stages in India

| **Stage** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| Mid-1970s (post-Emergency) | Krishna Iyer, Bhagwati open access; activism emerges as the "new feature" of Indian judiciary |
| 1979 to 1980s | PIL/SAL; Art. 21 expanded - livelihood (Olga Tellis), education, health, clean environment |
| 1990s onward | Governance and accountability - CVC case, **2G**, **coal block cancellation**, Jharkhand Assembly case; candidates' affidavits on assets and education (Affidavit case: candidates to disclose assets and education) |
| 2017 | Privacy a fundamental right (Puttaswamy) |

# 3. Doctrines evolved by the Supreme Court
- **Genuine SC evolutions:** **Basic Structure** (Kesavananda 1973), **PIL**, **removal of handcuffs** (prisoners' dignity), continuing mandamus, expansion of Art. 21
- **Not an SC evolution:** "exemptions to Fundamental Rights" - written in the Constitution (e.g. Arts. 31A-31C, 33) (APPSC 2017)
- ⚠ "Exemptions to Fundamental Rights" = written in the Constitution, **not** an SC doctrine (APPSC 2017)

## Judicial overreach — concerns and debates | from: S11, S13
# 1. Overreach and concerns
- **Overreach:** Courts entering the executive or legislative domain → strains **separation of powers**
- **Upendra Baxi:** Courts must fill the vacuum, not replace democratic institutions; ideal = **judicial prudence**

| **Concern** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **General** | Violation of **separation of powers**, courts doing executive work, weak enforcement of PIL orders, flood of letter petitions, delays in other cases, "judicial adventurism" |
| **PIL misuse** | Publicity, political or personal motives; policy directives in **M.C. Mehta** cases |
| **Art. 142 worries** | Undefined "complete justice"; bypasses democratic scrutiny |

# 2. PIL — assessment
- **Positives:** Democratised access to courts
- **Negatives:** Overburdened courts; increasingly **misused**

## Tribunals — definition, nature, merits and criticism | from: S0

## Articles 323A and 323B — Amendments, subjects, who establishes | from: S1
- **Origin:** both articles in **Part XIV-A**, inserted by **42nd Amendment, 1976** (Swaran Singh Committee)
- **75th Amendment, 1993:** added rent/tenancy clause (h)

| **Feature** | **Art. 323A** | **Art. 323B** |
|---|---|---|

## Cases → judicial review of tribunals: S.P. Sampath Kumar (1987) to Madras Bar Association (2025) | from: S5, S6
# 1. L. Chandra Kumar (1997) - High Court review and the appeal route
- **L. Chandra Kumar (1997):** SC struck down exclusion clauses **Art. 323A(2)(d)** and **323B(3)(d)**
  - Judicial review under **Arts. 32 and 226** = **basic structure**
  - **Route:** tribunal (incl. CAT) decisions lie before the **High Court (Division Bench)** → SC; not directly to SC
  - Tribunals **supplement** HCs, do not replace them
- **Earlier:** **S.P. Sampath Kumar** case (**1987**) also upheld review (TH, 12 Aug 2026)
# 2. Case law timeline

| **Case** | **Year** | **Held** |
|---|---|---|
| **S.P. Sampath Kumar** | 1987 | Tribunals may replace HCs if an equally effective substitute; SC's review kept [GK] (TH, 12 Aug 2026 groups it with L. Chandra Kumar) |
| **L. Chandra Kumar** | 1997 | Exclusion clauses struck down; HC Division Bench review (above) |
| **R. Gandhi** | 2010 | Tribunal members performing judicial functions must be independent of the executive |
| **Rojer Mathew** | 2019 | First recommended an independent **National Tribunals Commission (NTC)** |
| **Madras Bar Association** | 2021, **Nov 2025** | Struck down Tribunal Reforms provisions re-enacting rejected conditions (details below) |

- ⚠ **Sampath Kumar (1987)**: tribunals may replace HCs → **L. Chandra Kumar (1997)**: tribunals only **supplement** HCs
# 3. Madras Bar Association (Nov 2025) → what was struck down
- **Madras Bar Association (Nov 2025):** SC struck down
  - **minimum age 50**
  - **4-year tenure** (made **5 years**)
  - **Search-cum-Selection Committee** sending **two names** (now **one name** per vacancy)
  - equating members with civil servants
  - Restored earlier framework; ordered an NTC within **four months** (12 Aug 2026)
