=== ROW 36 | Parliamentary privileges; anti-defection law (Tenth Schedule) and the role of the Speaker

## Parliamentary privileges → Articles, rules and punishments | from: S0, S1
# 1. Articles
| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 105 (Parliament) / Art. 194 (state legislatures)** | Freedom of speech; immunity for anything said or voted; other privileges as defined by law → **never codified** |
| **Art. 122 (Parliament) / Art. 212 (states)** | Courts cannot question proceedings on grounds of **procedural irregularity** |
- **Sources of privileges:** Rest on conventions (originally House of Commons practice), rules, statutes and judicial rulings
# 2. Breach of privilege — procedure
- **Privilege notice:** Any MP can allege breach or contempt by a minister, member or outsider
  - Raised under **LS Rule 222 / RS Rule 187**, usually after Question Hour
- **Procedure:** Presiding Officer grants/denies consent → lets House decide or refers to **Privileges Committee** → examines witnesses, recommends → **House decides**
- **RS committee:** **RS 10**, usually chaired by the Deputy Chairman
# 3. Terms and punishments
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Contempt vs breach** | Contempt wider → any act obstructing the House or its members in their functions |
| **Punishments** | Admonition, reprimand, imprisonment, suspension/expulsion |
- **Maharashtra privileges:** Legislative Council's Privileges Committee summoned comedian **Kunal Kamra** on a breach notice
- ⚠ **Rule 222** = LS; **Rule 187** = RS

## Anti-defection law — background, Amendments and FIRSTs | from: S2, S4
# 1. Background
- **Context:** Defections toppled governments in the **1960s-70s** ("**Aaya Ram Gaya Ram**", Haryana 1967)
- **Pre-1985:** **Y.B. Chavan Committee on Defections (1967-69)** studied the problem
- **Lapsed Bills:** Anti-defection Bills of **1973 and 1978**
- !! Not in your sources: which State first enacted its own anti-defection law before 1985. Check your CDI Block04 or standard polity text.
- **Aim:** Curb defections "induced by the lure of office" + horse-trading; ensure party discipline and stable governments
# 2. Amendment → provision
| **Amendment** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **52nd Amendment Act, 1985** (**52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985**) | Inserted the **Tenth Schedule** (APPSC-GS 2019); also amended **Arts. 101, 102, 190, 191** → vacation of seats and disqualification |
| **91st Amendment Act, 2003** | Tightened the law (table below) |
| **35th Amendment (1974)** | Used a "Tenth Schedule" for **Sikkim's association** |
| **The 36th (1975)** (36th Amendment, 1975) | Deleted that old schedule (per **IYB 2026**) |
- **FIRST:** **Party recognition:** Constitution **mentioned political parties for the first time** (first constitutional recognition)
- ⚠ **Trap:** the old Sikkim "Tenth Schedule" (**35th**, 1974) is a different schedule from the anti-defection one (**52nd**, 1985)
# 3. 91st Amendment Act, 2003 → changes
| **Change** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Split exception deleted** | Tenth Schedule, Para 3 |
| Union Council of Ministers capped at **15%** of Lok Sabha strength | **Art. 75(1A)** |
| State Council of Ministers capped at **15%** of Assembly strength | **Art. 164(1A)** (minimum **12**) [GK for minimum] |
| A disqualified defector cannot be a Minister or hold a remunerative political post until re-elected | **Arts 75(1B), 164(1B), 361B** |

## Tenth Schedule → coverage, grounds and exceptions (Paras) | from: S2, S3, S4, S7
# 1. Coverage
- **Houses covered:** **Both MPs and MLAs/MLCs** → members of **both Houses of Parliament and of State Legislatures**, so **Legislative Council** members too; operates on the **"legislature party"**
- **Members covered:** Party members, **independents** and **nominated members**; each has its own trigger
# 2. Grounds of disqualification
| **Member** | **Disqualified if** |
|---|---|
| Party member | **Voluntarily gives up membership** of the party, even without resigning (inferable from conduct), **or** **votes/abstains against the whip** without permission, and the party does **not condone it within 15 days** (i.e. not **condoned within 15 days**) |
| Independent | **Joins any party** after election |
| Nominated | Joins a party **after 6 months** of taking his seat; joining within 6 months is allowed |
- **Whip:** the whip clause is **Para 2(1)(b)**
# 3. Exceptions
| **Exception** | **Rule** | **Status** |
|---|---|---|
| **Merger** (Para 4) | At least **two-thirds** of the **legislature party** agree to merge with another party. Neither those who merge nor those who stay are disqualified | In force |
| **Split** (Para 3) | **One-third** of the legislature party breaking away | **Deleted by the 91st Amendment, 2003** |
| Presiding officers (Para 5) | **Speaker/Deputy Speaker/Chairman/Deputy Chairman** may leave (resign from) their party on election to office and rejoin after leaving office | In force |
- **Merger exception:** **Two tests** in Para 4: **original political party** must merge **and** two-thirds of legislature party must agree
- **Split exception:** Per **IYB 2026**, its deletion brought **splits within the purview of disqualification**
- ⚠ **Merger** = **two-thirds** (Para 4, in force); **Split** = **one-third** (Para 3, deleted 2003)
- ⚠ Nominated member: joining within **6 months** is allowed; after that → disqualified

## Speaker as deciding authority → Para 6, Para 7 and time limit | from: S5, S6
# 1. Deciding authority
- **Para 6:** Decided by the **Speaker** (Lok Sabha or Assembly) or **Chairman** (Rajya Sabha or Council), acting as a **tribunal**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Not** the President or Governor; Election Commission gives no opinion (UPSC 2025 PYQ)
- **Contrast:** **Other** disqualifications (Arts 103 and 192) → President/Governor decides on the **EC's opinion**
# 2. Para 7 and judicial review
- **Para 7:** Barred the courts → struck down in **Kihoto Hollohan** (see cases)
- **Judicial review:** Decision **subject to judicial review**, but only **after** the decision
  - Grounds: **mala fides, perversity, violation of natural justice or of a constitutional mandate** (constitutional violation)
# 3. Time limit
- **Time limit:** Schedule prescribes **no time limit** for the Speaker
- **Within three months:** set by **Keisham Meghachandra Singh** (Jan 2020), except in extraordinary cases (UPSC CSAT passage on the 3-month limit)

## Cases → holdings on privileges and defection | from: S0, S3, S5, S6, S7, S8
# 1. Privilege cases
| **Case** | **Holding** |
|---|---|
| **Raja Ram Pal (2007)** | Privilege decisions reviewable only for gross illegality or unconstitutionality |
| **Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024)** | 7-judge Bench → privilege gives **no immunity for bribery** to speak or vote; overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998) |
# 2. Cases on "voluntarily giving up membership"
| **Case** | **Holding** |
|---|---|
| **Ravi S. Naik v UoI (1994)** | "Voluntarily giving up membership" is **not the same as resigning**. It can be **inferred from conduct**, e.g. publicly backing a rival party [GK] |
| **G. Viswanathan v Speaker, TN (1996)** | A member **expelled** by his party **still belongs to it** for the Tenth Schedule. If he **joins another party**, he has voluntarily given up membership [GK] |
# 3. Kihoto Hollohan v Zachillhu (1992)
- **Bench:** Constitution Bench
- **Validity:** **Upheld the validity of the Tenth Schedule** (Tenth Schedule **valid**) → no violation of legislators' free speech or the basic structure
- **Para 7:** **struck down Para 7** (**Paragraph 7**)
  - Reason: not ratified by States under Art. 368(2)
  - Ruling on Para 7 was 3:2
# 4. Keisham Meghachandra Singh v Speaker, Manipur (Jan 2020)
- Speakers to decide disqualification petitions **within three months**, except in extraordinary cases
- Asked **Parliament** to consider replacing the Speaker with a **permanent independent tribunal**
# 5. Subhash Desai v Principal Secretary, Governor of Maharashtra (May 2023)
- The Shiv Sena case: **The political party, not the legislature party, appoints the whip and the leader in the House**
- **Party distinction:** The **legislature party cannot be conflated with the political party**
  - Valid merger needs two-thirds of legislature party of the original party; two-thirds of legislators cannot by themselves create a "deemed merger" of the original party
- **Applied (May 2026):** Palaniswami camp of the **AIADMK** cited the ruling to claim control of the whip under **Para 2(1)(b)**
# 6. Later rulings
| **Case** | **Holding** |
|---|---|
| **Karnataka (2019)** | SC upheld disqualification of 17 rebel MLAs; Speaker cannot bar them from contesting until end of term |
| **Telangana follow-up** | SC set a time-bound direction for the **Telangana Speaker** (2025); seven petitions rejected in Dec 2025; later gave the Speaker a **final 3 weeks** to decide the last 2 of **10 BRS MLAs** who moved to Congress, warning of **contempt** (Feb 2026) |
| **SC Bench (Jul 2026)** | **Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe** sought the Centre's reply on a plea against "deemed merger" by a legislature party under **Paragraph 4** (Para 4); said any shortcomings are **"matters for Parliament"** |
- ⚠ **Kihoto** (1992) = validity + review; **Keisham** (2020) = **three months**; **Subhash Desai** (2023) = political party appoints whip

## Working of the law → ways around it and Speaker delay | from: S8
# 1. Ways around the law
| **Route** | **How it works** |
|---|---|
| **Resort politics** | Legislators **sequestered** before a floor test to stop poaching |
| **Resignation route** | MLAs **resign** instead of defecting → cuts the majority mark without disqualification → re-contest on the new party's ticket (Examples: Karnataka 2019, MP 2020) |
| **Merger misuse** | Para 4 two-thirds rule used as a **defence for mass defection** |
- **AAP merger:** **7 of 10 AAP Rajya Sabha MPs** (incl. Raghav Chadha) claimed a "merger" with the BJP (Apr 2026)
- **TMC merger:** **20 TMC Lok Sabha MPs** (two-thirds of the party) told Speaker Om Birla they would merge with a little-known party, the **Nationalist Citizens Party of India** (NPCI); another source (Jun 2026) calls it "National People's Congress of India"
# 2. Speaker delay
- **Speaker delay:** no time limit; Speakers partisan as they **do not resign from their party**
  - Examples: AP 2014-19 (23 YSRCP MLAs, petitions undecided); Telangana 2024-26

## Committees and bodies → reform recommendations | from: S10
| **Body** | **Recommendation** |
|---|---|
| **Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990)** | Disqualify only for **voluntarily giving up membership** or for **voting against the whip on a confidence or no-confidence motion**. The **President or Governor** should decide on the **EC's advice**[GK] |
| **Law Commission, 170th Report (1999)** | **Delete the split and merger exceptions**. Treat **pre-poll alliances** as parties. Limit whips to votes that threaten the government [GK] |
| **NCRWC (2002)** | Defectors to be **barred from any public or remunerative political office** for the rest of the term, and the **defector's vote treated as invalid**[GK] |
| **Election Commission** | Decisions under the Tenth Schedule to be made by the **President or Governor on the EC's binding advice**[GK] |
| **Manish Tewari's private member's Bill (2010)** | Proposed limiting the law to confidence and money votes |
- **Newer proposals:**
  - **Fixed constitutional timelines** for Speakers to decide Tenth Schedule matters (Jul 2026)
  - **Independent tribunal** (SC, 2020)
# 1. Limiting the law to confidence and money votes
- **Core idea:** whip (and so disqualification) applies only to votes that **threaten the government's survival**
  - Such votes = **confidence and no-confidence motions and money bills**
  - Other votes → legislators free to dissent
- **Backers:** Dinesh Goswami (1990) + Law Commission (1999)
- ⚠ **170th** Report = Law Commission (1999); **NCRWC** = 2002; **Dinesh Goswami** = 1990

=== ROW 38 | Parliamentary form of government - features, merits, demerits; comparison with the presidential system

## Parliamentary government — meaning and features | from: S0
- **Basis:** India adopted the British **parliamentary system** = **cooperation and co-ordination** of legislature and executive
  - vs presidential system = **separation of powers**
- **Features:**
  - **Nominal and real executive:** President = de jure head; PM + Council of Ministers = de facto executive (Art. 74(1): ministers aid and advise; advice binding)
  - **Majority party rule:** leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha becomes PM
  - **Dual membership:** ministers must be MPs; a non-member must be elected within **six months**
  - **Leadership of the PM/CM** + **dissolution** of the lower House possible
- **Responsibility:** ministers individually responsible to the President
  - ⚠ **Trap:** the President is **not** the real head of the executive (APPSC-G2 2017 key)
> Exam angle: Assertion-Reason: "India is parliamentary because the executive is responsible to the legislature" - both true, R explains A (UPPSC 2025).

## Countries → system of government | from: S0, S1, S3
| **System** | **Origin** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| Parliamentary | **Britain** (Westminster model) | **UK, Japan, Canada, India** |
| Presidential | **USA** (origin) | **Brazil**, most of Latin America |
| Semi-presidential | — | **France, Russia, Sri Lanka** |
- **France (Fifth Republic, 1958) + Sri Lanka (1978 Constitution):** **directly elected President** with real powers + PM responsible to the legislature
- ⚠ **Westminster** = Britain; presidential origin = **USA**

## Presidential and semi-presidential systems → features | from: S1, S3
# 1. Presidential system
- **Features:**
  - President = **both Head of State and Head of Government**; single executive
  - **Directly elected**; fixed term
  - **Not accountable to the legislature**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC-G2 2025 key: correct = head of State, head of Government, directly elected; "Accountable to the legislature" is **wrong**
- **Cabinet:** members may or may not sit in the legislature (US secretaries do not)
# 2. Parliamentary vs presidential
| **Parliamentary** | **Presidential** |
|---|---|
| Nominal head + real head (PM) | One person is both heads |
| Collective executive (cabinet) | Single executive (President) |
| Fusion of powers; ministers are members of the legislature | Separation of powers |
| Executive responsible to the legislature; can be removed by no-confidence | Fixed tenure; removable only by impeachment [GK] |
| Lower House can be dissolved early | No dissolution of the legislature [GK] |
# 3. Semi-presidential system
- **Concept:** President + PM coexist, possibly from different parties
- **India vs:** India's President is indirectly elected and nominal

## India vs Britain and USA → feature comparison | from: S5, S6
# 1. Indian vs British parliamentary system
| **Feature** | **India** | **Britain** |
|---|---|---|
| Head of State | **Indirectly elected** President, 5-year term | **Hereditary** monarch |
| Supremacy | **Constitutional supremacy** - Parliament bound by a written Constitution | **Sovereignty of Parliament** - can enact or repeal any law |
| Constitution | Written | Unwritten (conventions) [GK] |
| Judicial review | Courts can strike down unconstitutional laws | Courts cannot question Acts of Parliament (CDI) |
| State structure | Federal | Unitary |
| PM | From **either House** | From the **House of Commons** |
- **Sources:** LENS (Jun 2026); CDI
- **Membership:** Britain - ministers must be MPs vs India allows six months as a non-member
- **Britain only:** legal responsibility of ministers + institutionalised **shadow cabinet**; India has neither
- **Both:** PM's role grown so much that scholars call it **"Prime Ministerial Government"**
# 2. India vs USA
| **Feature** | **India** | **USA** |
|---|---|---|
| Residuary powers | **Parliament (Union)** (IYB) | **States**[GK] |
| Division of powers | Three lists - Union, State, Concurrent (strong centre) [GK] | Enumerated federal powers; rest with states [GK] |
| Judicial review | Narrower - "procedure established by law" | Wider - "due process of law"; SC called a "third chamber" (APP) |
| Executive | Parliamentary | Presidential |
- **Common:** written Constitution, federal structure, independent judiciary with judicial review, Fundamental Rights / Bill of Rights
- ⚠ "**Procedure established by law**" = India; "**due process of law**" = USA

## Merits, demerits and the Constituent Assembly's choice | from: S2
# 1. Merits and demerits
| **System** | **Merits** | **Demerits** |
|---|---|---|
| Parliamentary | Responsible government; executive continuously accountable; executive-legislature harmony; fresh elections break deadlock | Unstable coalitions, no fixed tenure, ministers not always specialists, cabinet dictatorship when majority is large |
| Presidential | Stable, fixed-term executive | Executive-legislature deadlock; danger of a **personality cult** around a single executive |
# 2. Why the Constituent Assembly chose the parliamentary form
- **Reasons:**
  - Familiarity with the system under the **1919 and 1935 Acts**
  - India's vast size and diversity
  - Avoid **US-style executive-legislature conflict**
  - Risk of a personality cult under a single executive

|---|---|
| **Dr B.R. Ambedkar** | A democracy cannot maximise both **stability and responsibility**; India chose **responsibility** (continuous accountability) over guaranteed tenure |
