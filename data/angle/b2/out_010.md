=== ROW 39 | Governor - appointment, powers, discretionary powers, position and controversies (Article 200/201, Article 356 reports)

## Governor — appointment, term, removal and immunities (Articles 153-159, 202, 361) | from: S0, S1
# 1. Appointment and qualifications → Article
- **Art. 153:** a Governor for each state (APPSC-GS 2021 key)
  - **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956** → **one person to be Governor of 2 or more states** (**same person to be Governor of two or more states**) (APPSC-GS 2025, 2021; APPSC-GS 2025 key)
- **Art. 155:** appointed by the **President by warrant under hand and seal**; neither directly nor indirectly elected
- **Art. 157:** must be a **citizen** (**citizen of India**), at least **35 years** old (**35 or above**; completed **35 years**)
- **Art. 158:** not a member of Parliament or a state legislature (not a member of any legislature); holds no office of profit
  - Emoluments fixed by **Parliament** by law, cannot be reduced during the term
- **Art. 159:** Oath administered by the **Chief Justice of the High Court** (in his absence, the senior-most judge) [GK]
- **Conventions:** an **outsider** to the state; state **CM is consulted**
> Exam angle: minimum age of 35 (APPSC-GS 2019); "pleasure of the President" makes the office structurally dependent on the Centre (CDI).
# 2. Term and removal
- **Art. 156:** holds office **during the pleasure of the President**
- **Term:** normal term **5 years**, but continues **until his successor enters office** (Art. 159)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "quits office immediately after the term expires" is the **wrong** statement (APPSC-G1 2019)
  - Resigns to the **President**; may be **transfer**red and **reappointed**
- **Removal:** **No grounds for removal** in the Constitution (grounds **not stated**) → limits set by **B.P. Singhal (2010)** (see cases page)
- **Accountability:** not answerable to either House of Parliament, the state legislature, or the Council of Ministers (PYQ 2019)
- ⚠ Appointed by the **President**, resigns to the **President**; oath from the **Chief Justice of the High Court**
# 3. Emoluments and immunities
- **Emoluments:** **charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State** (Art. 202(3)) (some notes say Consolidated Fund of India)
  - **Common Governor:** one Governor for two states → cost shared among states as the President decides
- **Art. 361 immunities:**
  - Not answerable to any court for official acts
  - No criminal proceedings or arrest during term
  - Civil proceedings for personal acts need **2 months' notice**

## Governor and the Council of Ministers — Articles 164, 167 and the hung House | from: S4
# 1. Article 164 provisions
- **Art. 164(1):** **CM is appointed by the Governor**; other ministers on CM's advice; hold office during Governor's pleasure (APPSC-GS 2021 key: Art. 164)
- **Art. 164(2):** **collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly**
- **Art. 164(4):** non-member minister must become MLA/MLC within **six months**
- **91st Amendment Act, 2003:**
  - Council of Ministers ≤ **15%** of Assembly's total strength
  - CM + ministers ≥ **12** (Art. 164(1A))
- **Tribal Welfare Minister:** a **Minister for Tribal Welfare** (may also handle SC and backward class welfare) mandatory in **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha**
  - Bihar dropped by the 94th Amendment, 2006
- ⚠ **91st** (2003) = **15%** cap and minimum **12**; **94th** (2006) = Bihar dropped from the tribal-minister list
# 2. CM's role
- **Art. 167:** CM must **communicate** Cabinet decisions to the Governor + furnish information he calls for
  - CM = chief link between Governor and Council
- **Loss of majority:** CM must resign or be dismissed
  - Once a new CM is sworn in, the old one ceases; only one CM can hold office
# 3. Hung House — discretion flashpoint
- **Hung House (Art 164):** order of invitation
  - 1. Pre-poll alliance
  - 2. Largest party with support
  - 3. Post-poll coalition
  - 4. Post-poll alliance with outside support
  - Early floor test follows
- **Governor-state conflicts - Hung assembly, Tamil Nadu (May 2026):**
  - TVK 108 seats (10 short)
  - Governor invited **C. Joseph Vijay** on 120 letters of support (Art. 164), per Sarkaria/Punchhi order, with an early floor test; sworn in as CM

## Discretionary powers — constitutional vs situational (Article 163) | from: S3
# 1. Article 163
- **Art. 163(1):** Council of Ministers headed by the CM aids and advises the Governor, except where he must act in his discretion
- **Art. 163(2):** Governor's own decision on whether a matter is discretionary (what falls within his discretion) is **final**
  - **Governor's decision is final**; his acts cannot be questioned on that ground
- **Art. 163(3):** advice tendered by ministers **cannot be inquired into in any court**
# 2. Types of discretion

| **Constitutional (express) discretion** | **Situational discretion** |
|---|---|
| Reserving a bill for the President (Art. 200) | Appointing a CM when no party has a clear majority (**hung Assembly**) |
| Recommending President's Rule (**Art. 356**) | Dismissing a ministry that has lost majority |
| Seeking information from the CM (**Art. 167**) | Dissolving the Assembly when the ministry has lost majority |
| Administrator of adjoining UT (**Art. 239**) | - |
| Sixth Schedule royalty share (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram); special responsibilities under **Art. 371-371J** (e.g., law and order under **371A **Nagaland, **371H** Arunachal) | - |

# 3. Scope of discretion
- **vs President:** Governor's discretion is **wider** (**wider than the President's**); the Constitution gives the President no such discretion
- **Art. 239(2):** as administrator of an adjoining UT, the Governor acts **independently of his own state Council of Ministers**
  - Express discretion (UPSC)
- **Discretion is subject to judicial review** (see the Governor cases page: Rameshwar Prasad, Nabam Rebia)
- ⚠ **Hung Assembly** = situational discretion; **Art. 200** reservation, **Art. 356** report, **Art. 167** information = constitutional discretion

## Bills → Governor's and President's options (Articles 200/201) | from: S5
# 1. Article 200 - Governor's options
- **Four options:** **assent**, **withhold** assent, **return** once for reconsideration (not a **Money Bill**), or **reserve** for the President
- **Re-passed bill:** Governor **must assent**; cannot withhold again (APPCA 2026)
  - Bill may be reserved **only once**; a re-enacted bill cannot be reserved again unless materially altered
- **Mandatory reservation:** a bill that **derogates from the powers of the High Court** (endangering the **High Court's** position)
  - APPSC-G1 2024 key "Only III"
# 2. Bills reserved to get protection → Article
- **Reserved to get protection** (not a general veto):
  - Compulsory acquisition of property (**Art. 31A/31C**)
  - Concurrent-List repugnancy - bills repugnant to central law (**Art. 254(2)**)
  - Taxes on water/electricity in inter-state river projects (**Art. 288**)
  - Inter-state trade restrictions (**Art. 304(b)**) - need prior Presidential sanction
  - Money bills during a **Financial Emergency (Art. 360)**
- **Central control:** reservation = a form of **central control over states** (APPSC-GS 2025; APPSC 2025 key: reservation is **an example of central control over states**)
- **Centre's control over state legislation:** **Previous sanction of the President** before introducing bills restricting inter-state trade - **Art. 304(b)** (PYQ 2018)
# 3. Article 201 - President's options
- **Options:** assent, withhold, or (non-Money Bill) direct the Governor to return it
  - Legislature must reconsider within **6 months**; President **not bound** to assent afterwards
- **Absolute veto:** President has an **absolute veto** over reserved bills
- **Time limit:** none prescribed (Apr 2025: President withheld assent to Tamil Nadu's anti-NEET Bill)
- ⚠ Only **mandatory** reservation = bill derogating from the **High Court's** powers; **304(b)** = prior sanction, not reservation after passage

## Cases and References on the Governor → holdings | from: S0, S6, S7
# 1. Landmark cases

| **Case** | **Holding** |
|---|---|
| **Shamsher Singh (1974)**, 7 judges | Governor bound by aid and advice; acts on ministerial advice. Public criticism of Cabinet policy is an "unconstitutional faux pas" against the parliamentary system (APPCA; TH 24 Jan 2026) |
| **S.R. Bommai (1994)** | Majority to be tested **on the floor**; Art. 356 subject to judicial review (CDI) |
| **Rameshwar Prasad (2006)** | Bihar Assembly dissolution struck down; Governor's report (Art. 356 reports) reviewable; no dissolution on a subjective (unproven) fear of "horse-trading" (CDI; APPCA) |
| **Nabam Rebia (2016)** | No general discretion for the Governor, e.g. on summoning the House; summoning, proroguing and dissolving (Art. 174) and the address (Arts 175-176) are **not discretionary**; done on **aid and advice** (CDI; VIS; APPCA; TH) |
| **Perarivalan (2022)** | Governor bound by Cabinet advice on pardon/remission under **Art. 161**; reaffirmed in 2026 (LENS, Apr 2026) |

# 2. Removal — B.P. Singhal
- **B.P. Singhal v. UoI (2010)**:
  - Pleasure doctrine not unfettered
  - Reasons for removal need not be given, but removal must not be **arbitrary**
  - Not merely because the Centre's government changes; being out of tune with the Centre is not a ground
  - Limited judicial review
# 3. Assent time limits — Punjab 2023, TN April 2025, Reference Nov 2025
- **State of Punjab v. Principal Secy to Governor (2023):** Governor must act "as soon as possible" and cannot keep bills pending indefinitely + **a re-passed bill must get assent**
- **State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor of TN (8 April 2025):** read time limits into Art. 200
  - Referring 10 re-enacted bills to the President was unconstitutional
  - **1 month** if withheld on advice
  - **3 months** against advice
  - **1 month** after re-passage (re-presented bill)
  - Used **Art 142** to grant **"deemed assent"** to 10 bills (declared them assented)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes (APPCA 2026) date it to 2023 and treat deemed assent as good law; decided **Apr 2025** (**State of TN v. Governor (April 2025)**)
- **Art 143 Reference** (President Murmu) - **16th Presidential Reference** - **Presidential Reference (Art. 143) opinion of November 2025** (5-judge bench; **Five-judge opinion, Nov 2025**):
  - **no court-imposed timelines** - courts **cannot fix timelines**
  - **deemed assent rejected** - courts cannot declare **deemed assent**; "**deemed assent**" is unconstitutional
  - No indefinite inaction either: prolonged, unexplained inaction → **limited mandamus** (limited direction to decide)
  - Judicial review limited to mala fides and procedure
  - President need not seek SC advice on each reserved bill
  - Governor not bound by advice in choosing among the Art 200 options
  - Governor = "**guide, philosopher and friend**" of the government
  - Some notes still teach deemed assent
- **Longest delays:** **Kerala** and TN
> Exam angle: statements on the four options, the one mandatory reservation, and whether courts can fix timelines (current position: they cannot).
> Exam angle: the April 2025 timelines and deemed assent do not survive the Nov 2025 Reference opinion.
- ⚠ **Art 142** = deemed assent granted (Apr 2025); **Art 143** Reference = deemed assent rejected (Nov 2025)

## Article 356 — provisions, FIRSTs and practice | from: S8, S9
# 1. Provisions
- **Ground:** state's government **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution**
  - President may act on the **Governor's report** or otherwise

| **Feature** | **Rule** |
|---|---|
| Duration | **6 months** at a time, maximum **3 years** (APPSC 2019 key) [GK] |
| Beyond 1 year | Only with a **National Emergency** in force **and** an **EC** certificate that elections cannot be held (44th Amendment) [GK] |
| Assembly | Suspended or dissolved; Parliament legislates for the state (Art 357) [GK] |

- **Punjab extension:** **68th Amendment** stretched Punjab's 1987 proclamation to **5 years** (2026)
- **Central laws under PR:** reach a state only through an **Art 357(1)(a)** resolution
  - Example: Water Amendment Act, extended to Manipur (Dec 2025)
# 2. FIRSTs and frequency
- **FIRST:** **Punjab, 1951**
  - **Kerala, 1959** = first dismissal of an elected government
- **Frequency:** used **over 125 times**
  - Never used in **Telangana** or **Chhattisgarh** (APPSC 2019 key)
  - Most often in **Manipur**; 2025 was its **11th** time
- **Recent:** Manipur, from **13 Feb 2025** (APPSC 2024 key: Art 356)
  - **Revoked on 4 Feb 2026**; **Y. Khemchand Singh** became CM
- **AP instance:** Andhra State had it in **1954-55**
- ⚠ **Punjab** = first use (1951); **Kerala** = first dismissal of an elected government (1959)
# 3. Special cases
- **Former J&K:**
  - 6 months of **Governor's Rule** under **Sec 92 of the J&K Constitution**, then Art 356 (e.g., 2018)
  - After 2019: President's Rule under the Reorganisation Act until **Oct 2024**
- **Ayodhya (Dec 1992):** BJP governments of UP, MP, Rajasthan and HP removed
  - **Bommai** upheld MP, Rajasthan, HP dismissals on the ground of **secularism** (UPSC)

=== ROW 40 | Chief Minister and the State Council of Ministers; Advocate General of the State

## Chief Minister — Article 164: appointment, tenure and responsibility | from: S0, S1
# 1. Appointment — Article 164
- **Art. 164(1):** **Governor appoints the CM** (APPSC-GS 2021) - usually leader of the majority party
- **Hung Assembly:** Governor's discretion (no constitutional criteria)
  - Sarkaria (1987) + Punchhi (2010) order: **pre-poll alliance** → **single largest party with support** → **post-poll coalition** → **post-poll alliance with outside support**
  - Early **floor test** follows (May 2026)
- **Oath:** administered by the **Governor** (Art. 164(3))
- **Non-member:** can be CM/minister for **6 months** (Art. 164(4)); ceases unless elected
# 2. Tenure and responsibility
- **Confidence:** CM stays only while he has the **Assembly's confidence**
  - Loses it → Governor can ask him to resign or dismiss the government
  - Literal "pleasure" reading cautioned against in the Constituent Assembly
- **Collective responsibility:** to the **Legislative Assembly** (Art. 164(2)) (BPSC 2022)
- **Salaries:** fixed by the **state legislature** (Art. 164(5))
- **CM's powers:** term not fixed; recommends portfolios, can drop ministers, can advise dissolution of the Assembly
- ⚠ Clauses of **Art. 164**: (1) appointment, (2) collective responsibility, (3) oath, (4) **6 months** rule, (5) salaries

## CM's duties towards the Governor — Article 167 | from: S1
- **Communicate:** all Council of Ministers decisions on administration and legislative proposals to the Governor
- **Council consideration:** if Governor requires, **submit for Council consideration** any matter decided by a single minister
- **Chief link:** CM = **chief link** between Governor and Council
  - Seeking information under Art. 167 = a constitutional discretion of the Governor

=== ROW 41 | State legislature - Assembly and Legislative Council; creation and abolition of the Council (Article 169)

## Legislative Assembly — strength, term and quorum (number game) | from: S0
# 1. Strength
- **Strength:** (Art. 170) **60-500** members, directly elected (**not more than 500 and not less than 60**)
- **Exceptions below 60:**
  - **Sikkim 32** (**Art. 371F**)
  - Goa and Mizoram = **40**
  - Arunachal: minimum **30** under Art. 371H; actually has **60** (some notes wrongly list it as below 60)
- **Seats:** **AP 175**; **Telangana 119**; **Goa 40**; **UP 403**
- **Anglo-Indian nomination:** Governor's power to nominate an **Anglo-Indian** to Assemblies (Art. 333) **discontinued in 2020** by the 104th Amendment; APP's line on it is stale
# 2. Term, sessions and quorum
- **Term:** **5 years** (Art. 172)
  - May be dissolved earlier
  - Extended by Parliament **1 year at a time** during a National Emergency, not beyond **6 months** after the Emergency ends
- **Minimum age (Art. 173):** **25** for the Assembly vs **30** for the Council
- **Sessions (Art. 174):** No more than **6 months** between sittings
- **Quorum:** **10 members or 1/10th** (**10 members or one-tenth**) of the House (Art. 189(3)), whichever is greater

## Legislative Council — composition, size and tenure (number game) | from: S1
# 1. Composition

| **Share of members** | **Chosen by (electorate)** |
|---|---|
| **1/3** | Local bodies (municipalities, district boards etc.) |
| **1/12** | Registered **graduates** of 3+ years' standing |
| **1/12** | **Teachers** of 3+ years (3 years) in institutions not below **secondary school** (secondary level and above) |
| **1/3** | Elected by **MLAs**, from among non-members of the Assembly |
| **1/6** | **Nominated by the Governor**: literature, science, art, cooperative movement (co-operation), social service |

- ⚠ **1/3 – 1/12 – 1/12 – 1/3 – 1/6** = **local bodies – graduates – teachers – MLAs – Governor**
# 2. Size
- **Size (Art. 171):**
  - Maximum = **1/3** of the Assembly's total strength (**one-third of the Assembly**)
  - Minimum = **40** (**at least 40**)
  - Actual strength fixed by **Parliament** → keeps the directly elected House predominant
- **Mode of election:** Indirect, by **PR-STV**
- **Nominations:** The **1/6** nominations to Councils (Art. 171) are made on Cabinet advice
# 3. Tenure and eligibility
- **Nature:** A **permanent body** (not dissolved)
- **Term:** **6 years** per member
- **Retirement cycle:** **1/3** of members retire every **second year** (**one-third retiring every two years**)
- **Minimum age (Art. 173):** **30** for the Council

## Article 169 and States with Legislative Councils | from: S2, S3
# 1. Article 169 — creation and abolition
- **Assembly resolution:** Passed by a **majority of its total membership** + **at least 2/3 of members present and voting**
- **Parliament's role:** Then passes a law by **simple majority**
  - This law is **not deemed an amendment** under **Art. 368** (APPSC-GS 2018: Art. 169)
- **Governor's role:** The **Governor has no role** in initiating or approving the resolution
- **Limits vs strength:** Constitution fixes the Council's maximum and minimum limits; Parliament fixes its strength
- **Implication:** The Council's existence depends on the **will of the Assembly**
- ⚠ Assembly = **special majority**; Parliament = **simple majority**; Governor = **no role**
# 2. States with Councils — present list and recent changes
- **Present list (2026):** **Six states** = **Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh**
- **J&K:** Council abolished by the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Lists showing **J&K** or only four states are out of date
- **AP:** Abolished its Council in **1985**, revived in **2007** (see M1-B-35)
- **Tamil Nadu:** Abolished its Council in **1986**
- **Karnataka Council (2026):**

## Presiding officers → powers (Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Chairman) | from: S4
# 1. Election, tenure and vacating office
- **Election (Art. 178):** Elected by the Assembly from among its members
- **Tenure:** Holds office for the life of the Assembly; on dissolution stays in office until just before the new Assembly first meets
- **Vacating office (Art. 179):**
  - Ceases to be a member
  - Resigns **to the Deputy Speaker** (**Resigns to the Deputy Speaker**, APPSC-GS 2018 key); the Deputy Speaker resigns to the Speaker
  - Removed by a resolution of a **majority of all the then members**, after **14 days' notice**
- **During a removal resolution (Art. 181):**
  - Speaker **cannot preside** (**does not preside**)
  - May speak and votes in the first instance only
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "the Speaker will preside" is the statement that is **not** a power (APPSC-G1 2022 key)
# 2. Powers
- **Voting (Art. 189):** No vote in the first instance; a **casting vote** on a tie
- **Money Bill:** **Final decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill.** → certification by the Speaker (Art. 199(3))
- **House management:** Adjourns or suspends the House for lack of **quorum**; maintains order
- **Anti-defection:** Decides **Tenth Schedule** disqualification → subject to judicial review (**Kihoto Hollohan 1992**)
- **Other powers:**
  - Allows a secret sitting
  - Appoints committee chairmen
  - Chairs the Business Advisory, Rules and General Purposes Committees
# 3. Deputies and Council officers
- **Officers:**
  - Assembly = Speaker / Deputy Speaker
  - Council = Chairman / Deputy Chairman (resign to each other)
- **Deputy Speaker (Art. 178):** Acts with full powers when the Speaker's post is vacant or he is absent
- **Council:** The **Chairman** resigns to the **Deputy Chairman**

## Disqualification of state legislators — Articles 190-192 | from: S5
- **Art. 191 grounds:**
  - Office of profit
  - Unsound mind
  - Undischarged insolvent
  - Not a citizen
  - Disqualified by Parliament's law (RPA 1951)
  - Defection under the **Tenth Schedule**
- **Art. 192:** Disqualification outside the Tenth Schedule decided by the **Governor**, on the **Election Commission's opinion** (binding on him)
  - Defection cases → **Speaker/Chairman**
- **Art. 190:** No one can be a member of both Houses, or of two states' legislatures
- **Art. 190(3)(b):** MLA's resignation takes effect only on the **Speaker's/Chairman's acceptance** (2026)
- **Art. 190(4):** Seat vacated if absent **60 days** without permission
- ⚠ **Governor** (on **Election Commission's opinion**) = Art. 191 grounds; **Speaker/Chairman** = **Tenth Schedule** defection

## Assembly vs Council — bills, powers and MLAs in indirect elections | from: S6, S7
# 1. Assembly vs Council

| **Point** | **Assembly** | **Council** |
|---|---|---|
| Money Bill | Introduced **only here** (**only in the Assembly**), by a minister, on the **Governor's recommendation** (Art. 207) (APPSC-GS 2018 key: Governor) | Can only recommend; must return it within **14 days**; cannot reject or amend; its recommendations may be rejected (Art. 198) |
| Ordinary Bill (from the Assembly) | Its will prevails (Art. 197) | Can reject/hold it: delay of up to **3 months** the first time and **1 month** the second time; the bill is then deemed passed |
| Bill originating in the Council | Can reject it; **the bill ends** (**Dies**) | No second chance |
| Demands for grants | Voted **only here** | Discusses only; cannot vote on them |
| Deadlock | **No joint sitting** in states, unlike Parliament (Art. 108); the Assembly prevails | - |

- **Council's power:** **Only delaying**; much weaker than the Rajya Sabha's
- **Constitutional amendments:** Council has **no role in ratifying** them
- ⚠ Council delay: **14 days** (Money Bill); **3 months** + **1 month** (ordinary bill)
# 2. Order of proceedings
- **Order of proceedings:** (Art. 208) Set by the Rules of Procedure
  - **Governor's address** on the first day of the first session each year, followed by discussion
  - Order of a session (2026): Governor's address → general discussion → voting on demands (Assembly only)
  - Budget session: **demands for grants are voted only in the Assembly** (2026)
# 3. MLAs in indirect elections

| **Election** | **MLAs vote?** |
|---|---|
| President | **Yes** - elected MLAs (Art. 54) [GK] |
| Vice-President | **No** - only MPs (APPCA 2026) |
| Rajya Sabha | **Yes** - elected MLAs, by PR-STV (CDI) |
| Legislative Council | **Yes** - elect one-third of MLCs (Art. 171) |
