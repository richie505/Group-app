=== ROW 18 | Amendment of the Constitution - Article 368, types of majorities, procedure, limitations

## Article 368 — location, source, nature and persons | from: S0
- **Location:** **Part XX, Article 368** (sole Article of Part XX) (PYQs)
- **Wording:** Parliament "may, in exercise of its constituent power, amend **by way of addition, variation or repeal** any provision" (UPSC)
- **Amending power:** **Parliament** alone - including power to amend Fundamental Rights, by special majority
- **Source:** borrowed from **South Africa** (amendment procedure + election of Rajya Sabha members)
- **Nature:** **neither rigid nor flexible but a synthesis of both** (**neither purely rigid nor purely flexible**)
  - Partly rigid, partly flexible - unlike the very rigid US Constitution and the flexible British one

| **Person** | **Statement** |
|---|---|
| **Granville Austin** | Process "one of the most ably conceived aspects"; "appears complicated" but "is merely diverse" (UPSC) |

- ⚠ Source country of the procedure = **South Africa** (not the US, which is "very rigid")

## Amendment procedure → step-by-step rules | from: S1
| **Step** | **Rule** |
|---|---|
| Introduction / Initiation | Only by a Bill in **Either House of Parliament** only - never a State Legislature (PYQ) |
| Mover | Minister or **private member** |
| Prior Presidential sanction | **Not required** - **No prior permission of the President** / no prior presidential recommendation needed (PYQ 2024 trap) |
| Passage | **Special majority** in each House **separately** = majority of total membership + **two-thirds present and voting** |
| Deadlock | **No joint sitting** (Art. 108 does not apply) - failure in either House makes the bill lapse / Bill falls |
| Federal provisions | Also ratified by legislatures of **half the States** by simple majority |
| Assent | **Obligatory** - President **must give assent**; can neither withhold nor return the Bill; made so by the **24th Amendment, 1971** [GK] |

> Exam angle: "prior permission", "joint sitting" and "President may return" are all false for amendment bills.
> Exam angle: statement sets on "private member needs prior permission" (false) and "joint sitting possible" (false).

| **Point** | **Constitutional amendment bill (Art. 368)** | **Ordinary bill** |
|---|---|---|
| Majority | **Special majority** - majority of total membership + **two-thirds of present and voting**, in each House separately (UPSC) | Simple majority |
| President's prior sanction | Not needed (APP) | Not needed (except Arts. 3, 117 types) |
| States | Federal provisions need ratification by **half the states**, no time limit (APP) | No role |

## Types of majority → requirement → used for | from: S2, S6
| **Route** | **Requirement** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| Simple majority (outside Art. 368) | Majority of members present and voting; like ordinary law; **not deemed an amendment** under Art. 368 (not counted as amendments) | New States, alteration of areas (Arts. 2, 3); creating new states (Art. 4); Art. 2-4 (new states); Legislative Councils (Art. 169); citizenship; official language; quorum |
| Special majority (Art. 368) | Majority of **total membership** of each House + **two-thirds of members present and voting** | **Fundamental Rights** (incl. Art. 32), **DPSP**, everything / all provisions not in the other two routes |
| Special majority + State ratification | Above + ratification by **not less than one-half of the States** | Election of President, executive power of Union/States, Supreme Court/High Courts, distribution of legislative powers (Seventh Schedule), Art. 368 itself |

- **Article 32:** needs **special (two-thirds) majority** - no State ratification (APPSC-G2 2017 key; PYQ G2 2017: "two-thirds majority")
  - But SC powers under Art. 32 are part of the basic structure → cannot be destroyed
# Special majority — different meanings across Articles
| **Type** | **Requirement** | **Used for** |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 368 type | Majority of total membership + 2/3 present and voting, each House | Amendment (368); removal of SC/HC judges (124, 217), CAG (148), CEC (324), SEC (243K); approval of National Emergency (352) |
| Art. 61 type | **Two-thirds of total membership** of the House | Impeachment of the President - strongest majority |
| Art. 249/312 type | **Two-thirds of members present and voting in Rajya Sabha **only | Parliament legislating on a State List subject (249); new All-India Services (312) |

- ⚠ **61** = two-thirds of total membership (President); **249/312** = two-thirds present and voting, Rajya Sabha only; **368** = both Houses, total + two-thirds

## Criticism of the amendment procedure | from: S7
- **Criticisms:**
  - **No special body** (constitutional convention/assembly) or referendum - Parliament alone amends most provisions; no outside agency
  - **No joint sitting** - no mechanism to resolve deadlock between Houses on an amendment Bill
  - **No time limit** for State ratification
  - States **cannot initiate**; ratification needed only for a few provisions

  - Blend of rigidity and flexibility; basic-structure limit keeps judicial review over amendments
- **Criticism & debates:** 2026 vote shows special majority protects against a slim majority; critics say simple-majority routes are too easy

=== ROW 21 | Basic Structure doctrine - evolution and content; Kesavananda Bharati and after (Indira Gandhi, Minerva Mills, Waman Rao, I.R. Coelho)

## Basic structure — meaning, origin, persons and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
# 1. Meaning
- **Amending power:** Parliament may amend any part under **Art. 368** (including FRs and the Preamble)
  - **Limit:** **cannot damage or destroy the basic structure** = features that give the Constitution its identity, i.e. it **cannot alter, damage or destroy its core features**
  - PYQ 2024: "core features cannot be amended or altered by Parliament" (APPSC-GS 2024 key)
- **Definition:** Basic structure = what **cannot be amended in exercise of the power under Art. 368** (APPSC-G1 2018 key)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** it is not "the Preamble alone"
  - **Preamble:** Kesavananda held the **Preamble is part of the Constitution** and amendable, subject to the basic structure
# 2. Origin & nature
- **Country:** **Germany** (Basic Law)
- **Judge-made:** **judge-made and nowhere defined**; nowhere in the text; **not** added by any Act of Parliament
  - Propounded by SC in **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "incorporated by an Act of Parliament" = false (APPSC-G1 2024; PYQ G1 2024, statement I false)
- **Not exhaustive:** SC has **never exhaustively defined** it; list grows case by case
> Exam angle: statement sets testing "judge-made vs inserted by amendment" and "can Parliament amend FRs today" (yes, subject to basic structure).
# 3. Persons → contribution
| **Person** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Dietrich Conrad** | Jurist; argued in India (1965) that the amending power has **implied limits** - origin of the idea |
| Justice Mudholkar | Dissent in **Sajjan Singh** (1965) first spoke of "basic features" |
| CJI **S.M. Sikri** | Headed the **13-judge bench** in Kesavananda Bharati |
# 4. FIRSTs
| **FIRST** | **Case** |
|---|---|
| **First propounded** the **basic structure doctrine** | **Kesavananda Bharati** (1973) |
| First application | **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain** (1975) |
| Largest bench ever | Kesavananda Bharati - **13 judges** |

## Ninth Schedule and Arts. 31A, 31B, 31C → origin, cut-off, Coelho test | from: S2
# 1. Origin & purpose
- **Created by:** **1st Amendment (1951)** with **Art. 31B** (and 31A)
  - **2026 = 75 years** of the 1st Amendment (assent by President **Rajendra Prasad**)
  - Also added **Art. 15(4)** + grounds of public order, friendly relations, incitement to **Art. 19(2)**
- **Purpose:** protect **land-reform/zamindari-abolition** laws from challenge for violating **Fundamental Rights** (PYQ G1 2024: I and II correct)
- **Coverage:** **both Central and State laws**
- **How laws enter:** **only by Parliament through a constitutional amendment**
  - Example: **76th Amendment, 1994** placed Tamil Nadu's **69% reservation** Act
- **Size:** started with **13** Acts → now **282**
# 2. Judicial review of entries
| **Case** | **Year** | **Ninth Schedule rule** |
|---|---|---|
| Kesavananda Bharati | 1973 | Amendments (including Ninth Schedule inclusions) cannot destroy the basic structure |
| Waman Rao | 1981 | **Cut-off date 24 April 1973** - laws/entries added after it are open to challenge on basic structure |
| **I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu** | 2007 | **nine-judge bench** (9 judges); **No blanket immunity**, because **judicial review is basic**; **Rights / direct-impact test**: post-1973 laws invalid if they damage the basic structure or abrogate the essence of **Arts. 14, 19, 21** |
# 3. Articles 31A, 31C - saving laws
| **Article** | **Saves** | **Inserted by** |
|---|---|---|
| **31A** | Laws on acquisition of estates, agrarian reform etc. from Arts. 14, 19 | **1st Amendment, 1951** |
| **31C** | Laws implementing **Art. 39(b), (c)** from Arts. 14, 19 | **25th Amendment, 1971** |

- ⚠ **13** Acts at start → **282** now; cut-off **24 April 1973** = date of Kesavananda

## Doctrines → origin (Supreme Court vs Parliament) | from: S4

## Scope, supremacy debate and criticism | from: S0
# 1. Scope - HC power over central laws, amendments
- **High Courts can** declare central laws invalid: **42nd Amendment (1976)** bar removed by **43rd Amendment (1977)**
- **Constitutional amendments can** be challenged (Basic Structure)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** both "cannot" statements false (UPSC 2019)
# 2. Supremacy debate
- **Judicial review principle:** **accepts the theory of supremacy of Courts** = **supremacy of the Constitution and the courts** over Parliament (APPSC-G2 2017 key; PYQ G2 2017)
- **Basic structure as a limit:** supremacy of Constitution, not of courts or Parliament
- **Parliament not sovereign:** Indian Parliament is **not sovereign in the British sense**
  - Reasons: written supreme Constitution + FRs + judicial review + federal division + limited amending power
# 3. Criticism vs defence
| **View** | **Argument** |
|---|---|
| Criticism | No textual basis; "rewriting of the Constitution" by unelected judges; **list is open-ended** → content uncertain |
| Defence | Among the biggest contributions of the Indian judiciary to constitutionalism; checks majoritarian misuse of Art. 368 |

## Universal angles | from: S5 | universal
# 1. Current relevance
- **1st Amendment at 75:** 18 June 2026 = **75 years of the 1st Amendment** (created the Ninth Schedule); **I.R. Coelho** limit restated

- **Significance:** keeps Art. 368 limited; judicial review itself is basic → cannot be removed even by amendment
