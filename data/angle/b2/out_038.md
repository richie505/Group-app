=== ROW 146 | Consumer rights - Consumer Protection Act 2019, CCPA, e-commerce rules

## Consumer Protection Act 2019 — six rights, definitions and new features | from: S1, S7
# 1. The Act
- **Replaced:** **Replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986**
- **Six consumer rights:**
  - Right to **safety**
  - Right to be **informed**
  - Right to **choose**
  - Right to be **heard**
  - Right to **seek redressal**
  - Right to **consumer awareness**
- **Consumer rights (UPSC 2012 key):**
  - Consumers may get **food samples tested**
  - **Legal heir** of a deceased consumer may file a complaint
# 2. Definitions
| **Term** | **Meaning under CPA 2019** |
|---|---|
| **"Consumer"** | **includes online and electronic purchases**; buyer for resale/commercial use ≠ consumer |
| **"Advertisement"** | Includes electronic media, internet, websites |
# 3. New features
- Created the **Central Consumer Protection Authority** (**CCPA**)
- e-filing of complaints + hearings by videoconference
- **product liability** of manufacturers, sellers, service providers
- Penalties for adulterated and spurious goods; e-commerce rules
# 4. Filing rules
- **Place of filing:** Where complainant lives or works
- **Deemed admission:** Complaint deemed admitted if not decided within **21 days**
- !! Not in your sources: the current filing-fee slabs (older rules: nil up to ₹5 lakh; check).

## Consumer Commissions → jurisdiction, appeals and structure | from: S2
- **Structure:** **Three-tier quasi-judicial** machinery → District, State and National Commissions ("Forums" under the 1986 Act)

| **Commission** | **Original jurisdiction (2021 revision)** | **Appeal from its order** |
|---|---|---|
| District (DCDRC) | Up to **₹50 lakh**[GK] | To State Commission within **45 days**[GK] |
| State (SCDRC) | ₹50 lakh to **₹2 crore**[GK] | To National Commission within **30 days**[GK] |
| National (**NCDRC**) | Above **₹2 crore** (LENS, Aug 2026) | To **Supreme Court** within **30 days** |

- **Jurisdiction basis:** **value of the consideration paid** for goods/services, not compensation claimed
- ⚠ **Trap:** Original CPA 2019 slabs (₹1 crore / ₹10 crore) revised in 2021; older notes/APPCA Mar 2026 still quote them
- ⚠ Appeal days: District → State = **45**; State → National and National → SC = **30**
- **NCDRC:** Set up **1988**; headed by sitting/retired **SC Judge or HC Chief Justice**
  - Above **35** State and **600+** District Commissions
- **Old District Forum:** President + 2 members, **one of them a woman**; a State could set up more than one (BPSC 2022)
- **Aug 2026:** SC directed NCDRC to report on pendency and vacancies → **5.7 lakh** cases pending

## Central Consumer Protection Authority → powers and penalties | from: S4
# 1. Nature and powers
- **Status:** **Executive agency** under CPA 2019; came into existence **24 July 2020**
- **Core mandate:** Prevent and regulate **false or misleading advertisements**
- **Powers:**
  - Inquire into violations of consumer rights
  - Order **recalls** and **refunds**
  - File **class-action** complaints
  - Impose penalties
# 2. Misleading ads and endorsers
| **Provision** | **First offence** | **Repeat** |
|---|---|---|
| **Penalty (Section 21)** | Up to **₹10 lakh** | Up to **₹50 lakh** |
| **Endorser ban** | Up to **1 year** | Up to 3 years |

- **June 2022 guidelines:** Endorsements reflect genuine opinion; material connection must be **disclosed**
# 3. In the news
- Advisories against celebrity endorsements of betting apps
- CCPA fined **Meta ₹10 lakh** over walkie-talkie listings → Meta challenged in Delhi HC

## E-commerce rules and dark patterns → provisions | from: S5
# 1. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020
- **Notified by:** **Department of Consumer Affairs** under CPA 2019
- **Grievance officer:** **acknowledge a complaint within 48 hours** + **redress it within one month**
- **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023:** 13 specified dark patterns
# 2. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026
- **Dates:** Gazetted **9 Sep 2026**; effective **1 January 2027**

| **Area** | **New requirement** |
|---|---|
| Imports | Disclose the **importer and country of origin** |
| Identity | Legal name, HQ and branch addresses, customer-care and grievance-officer details |
| Grievances | Join the **NCH convergence** process; give complainants copies of their complaints |
| Search | No manipulated results; sponsored listings clearly disclosed |
| Discounts | Show reduced and prior price; prior price = **lowest in the preceding 30 days** |
| Dark patterns | Comply with the 2023 Guidelines; **annual self-audit**; display the compliance certificate |

- **Consent:** Use of consumer data needs **express, affirmative consent**; unrelated bundled fees curbed

## Core principles of India's foreign policy → features | from: S2
# 1. Anti-colonialism, anti-imperialism, anti-racism

# 2. Disarmament

## Panchsheel 1954 → five principles and milestones | from: S3
# 1. Origin and principles
- **Source document:** Preamble of the **Agreement on Trade and Intercourse between India and the Tibet Region of China, 1954**
  - Signed **29 April 1954**, Nehru-**Zhou Enlai** era
- **The five principles:**
  - Mutual **non-aggression**
  - Mutual **non-interference** in internal affairs
  - **Equality and mutual benefit**
  - **Peaceful co-existence**
- ⚠ **Trap:** **Non-alignment is NOT one of the five principles** (UPSC 1997)
# 2. Later milestones
| **Milestone** | **Year** | **What happened** |
|---|---|---|
| **Burma** | 1954 | Adopted Panchsheel in a joint declaration later in 1954 |
| **Bandung** | **1955** | Absorbed into Bandung's Ten Principles |
| **UNGA resolution** | **1957** | Recognised it as a principle of international law |
| **Belgrade** | **1961** | NAM summit made it a core principle |

- ⚠ **Don't confuse:** Nehru's **Tribal Panchsheel (1952)**, drafted with Verrier Elwin, on tribal policy

=== ROW 148 | Phases of Indian foreign policy - Nehruvian era, 1971-1991, post-Cold War, post-1998, contemporary multi-alignment

## Phases of foreign policy → label, period and features | from: S0, S1, S2, S3, S4
| **Phase** | **Label** | **Features** |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 (1946-62)** | "**Optimistic non-alignment**" | Idealism + Panchsheel + NAM + Commonwealth membership + anti-imperialism |
| **Phase 2 (1962-71)** | "**Realism and recovery**" | Defence build-up; 1965 war + Tashkent (1966); refusal to sign the **NPT (1968)** |
| Third phase (1971-1991) | "**regional assertion**" (Jaishankar) | India the leading power in South Asia |
| **Phase 4 (1991-98)** | "**Safeguarding strategic autonomy**" | After the USSR's collapse + the **1991 economic crisis** |
| Jaishankar's phase 5 (**1998-2013**) | India as a "**balancing power**" | Pokhran-II, NFU, Indo-US nuclear deal |
| **Phase 6 (2014 onwards)** | "**Energetic engagement**" | Multi-alignment |

- ⚠ Order of labels: optimistic non-alignment → realism and recovery → regional assertion → safeguarding strategic autonomy → balancing power → energetic engagement
- **1962 war with China:** Showed the limits of idealism
- **Criticism:** Taking **Kashmir to the UN** (Jan 1948)
- **Post-Cold War economic diplomacy:**
  - LPG reforms → attracting FDI and markets became a foreign-policy goal
  - Strategic ties with the US began in the "unipolar moment" (**1991-2008**)
- **Context since 2008:** Since **2008** world moving to multipolarity → India's response = **issue-based alignment**
  - US: security and technology (Quad)
  - Russia: defence and energy
  - Outreach to Africa and Latin America
  - Claim to the UNSC and the NSG

=== ROW 149 | India-Pakistan relations - Kashmir, terrorism, Indus Waters Treaty, trade and dialogue

## Kashmir → accession, UN reference and Article 370 | from: S0
# 1. Jammu and Kashmir accession - ruler, invasion, date, UN
- **Instrument of Accession:** Signed by **Maharaja Hari Singh**, **26 October 1947**, after the tribal invasion
  - Ceded only **defence, external affairs and communications**
  - Troops airlifted **27 October**
  - Led to the first Indo-Pak war
- **UN reference:** Nehru took it to the **UN** (UNSC) on 1 Jan 1948 (**January 1948**) under **Article 35** of the UN Charter
  - UN called for a plebiscite **conditional on Pakistan's withdrawal** - never happened
  - **UNSC Resolution 47 (1948)**
# 2. Article 370 and after
| **Event** | **Date** |
|---|---|
| **Article 370** operative | **17 November 1952** |
| Article 370 **abrogated** | **5 August 2019** |
| **J&K Reorganisation Act 2019** | created UTs of J&K and Ladakh |
| Supreme Court upheld abrogation | **December 2023** |
- **Pakistan's response:** Downgraded diplomatic ties + suspended bilateral trade and the Samjhauta Express + raised it at the UN
- **Gilgit-Baltistan:** India claims it as part of J&K; a political status issue, not a border dispute
- ⚠ Accession **26 Oct** vs troops airlifted **27 Oct**; UN reference under **Article 35** of the UN Charter

## Indus Waters Treaty 1960 → provisions, institutions and abeyance | from: S6, S7
# 1. Signing and allocation
- **Signed:** **Karachi, 19 September 1960** by **Nehru** + President **Ayub Khan**
- **Broker:** **World Bank** (APPSC G2 2017)

| **Rivers** | **Allotted to** | **India's rights** |
|---|---|---|
| **Eastern**: Ravi, Beas, Sutlej | **India** (unrestricted use) | Full use |
| **Western**: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab | **Pakistan** | Domestic use, agriculture and **run-of-river hydropower**; limited storage under Treaty criteria |
- **Pakistan's share:** About **80% of the total flow of 168 MAF**
  - Pakistan relies on the system for **80%** of its 16 million ha of farmland + **93%** of its water use
# 2. Institutions and dispute resolution
- **Permanent Indus Commission:** One **Commissioner** from each country
  - Indian side: Indus Wing of the Department of Water Resources

| **Article IX level** | **Handled by** |
|---|---|
| "Questions" | PIC |
| "Differences" | **Neutral Expert** |
| "Disputes" | **Court of Arbitration** |
- No hierarchy set → World Bank appointed **both** a Neutral Expert and a Court of Arbitration for **Kishanganga and Ratle**
# 3. Abeyance (2025) and after
- **Trigger:** **Pahalgam attack (22 April 2025)** → **CCS** put the Treaty **"in abeyance" from 23 April 2025**
  - Until Pakistan "credibly and irrevocably" gives up support for cross-border terrorism
  - First ever suspension (Apr 2025)
  - Remains in force (MEA, 4 Jul 2026)
  - IYB 2026 calls it "terminated"; official usage = abeyance, which the Treaty itself does not provide for
- **Effects:** India stops sharing flow data; can build storage on the western rivers
  - Chenab projects: **Kirthai-II (930 MW)** + **Sawalkote (1,856 MW)** (Jun 2026)
- **Jul 2026:** India sent Pakistan a formal notice to renegotiate the Treaty
- **Aug-Sep 2026:** Court of Arbitration at the **PCA, The Hague** ruled the IWT "remains in force"; interim measures on the **Ratle** project
  - **India rejected** the ruling: court has "no jurisdiction" (1 Sep 2026)
- ⚠ **Eastern** (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) = India; **Western** (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) = Pakistan

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal
- **Current relevance:**
  - IWT stays in abeyance; India rejected the PCA ruling (Sep 2026)
  - India seeking to renegotiate the Treaty (Jul 2026)
- **Significance:** Simla-based **bilateralism** (no third-party mediation) = India's fixed position
- **Criticism & debates:** IWT gives India about 20% of the waters; no aquifer or drought clause
- **FIRST/LAST:** 2025 abeyance = **first suspension** of the IWT since 1960
