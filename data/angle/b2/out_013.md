=== ROW 46 | Federal structure of India - features of federalism and unitary bias; Seventh Schedule (three lists), residuary powers

## Federalism — definitions, types and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
# 1. Federalism - meaning
- **Definition:** **sharing of power between the Union and State governments**, each supreme in its sphere, under a written constitution with an independent umpire (APPSC-GS 2018 answer: federalism)
- **vs Unitary:** units draw power from the Centre
- **vs Confederation:** loose league of sovereign States that keep right to secede
# 2. Unitary government - characteristics, merits and demerits
- **Characteristics:** one supreme central government; local units are its creations with delegated powers; no constitutional division of powers
- **Merits:** uniform laws and policy; quick decisions; strong crisis response
- **Demerits:** over-centralisation; local needs neglected; poorly suited to large, diverse countries
  - Hence India is "federal in structure with unitary features" (IYB 2026)
# 3. Types of federation - holding-together vs coming-together

| **Type** | **Meaning** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| **Coming-together** | independent States pool sovereignty, keep equal power | **USA**, Switzerland, Australia |
| **Holding-together** | large country divides power among units to hold diversity together; stronger Centre | **India**, Spain, Belgium |

# 4. Genesis of the Indian federation
- USA = federation by agreement of states (**integration**)
- India = unitary colonial system converted to federal → "**centripetal bias**" (CDI view: India's federation formed by "disintegration" vs US by "integration")
- **National-integration design:** framed after Partition + integration of **14 provinces and 500+ princely States** → centralisation looked indispensable (TH 17 Feb 2026)
- ⚠ **Integration** = USA (coming together); **disintegration** = India (holding together)
# 5. Federal and unitary countries - examples

| **Federal** | **Unitary** |
|---|---|
| **USA** (formed by agreement of states) | **Britain** / **UK** (Parliament holds all law-making power) |
| **Canada**, **Australia**, **Argentina** | **France**, **Italy**, Japan, China |
| Switzerland, Germany, Russia, Brazil | Sri Lanka |

- **Parliamentary form (British model):**

## Articles 1-4, 370 and 371 → Union of States and asymmetric federalism | from: S2, S10
# 1. Indestructible Union
- **No "Federation":** the word **"Federation" is not used** anywhere = **"Federation" is nowhere used** in the Constitution
- **Article 1(1) (Art. 1):** "**India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States**"
  - States and UTs specified in **First Schedule**
  - Territory = States + UTs + territories acquired
- **Integral whole:** divided into States only for administrative convenience
# 2. Destructible States
- **Article 3:** Parliament can **form new States, alter areas, boundaries or names** by **simple majority**
  - Bill needs **President's recommendation** + reference to the state legislature, whose views are **not binding**
- **Article 4 (Art. 4):** such laws amend the First and Fourth Schedules; **not** Art. 368 amendments
- **Result:** no territorial guarantee → **"an indestructible Union of destructible States"**
- **Contrast:** USA = "indestructible Union of **indestructible** states"
- ⚠ India = indestructible Union of **destructible** States; USA = indestructible Union of **indestructible** states
# 3. Articles 1-4 - name of the Union, First Schedule, Territory vs Union of India

| **Article** | **Subject** |
|---|---|
| 2 | Admission or establishment of new states (states not part of India) |

- **Art. 2 (number game):** admit or establish **new states** not part of India (Sikkim)
- **Union of India:** = the states only (members of the federal system); Territory is the wider term
# 4. Asymmetric federalism
- **Art. 370 (J&K):** separate constitution + flag; State concurrence for Union laws; no Financial Emergency or DPSPs
  - Made **inoperative in August 2019**; upheld by Supreme Court **December 2023**
- **Arts. 371 to 371J:** special provisions for
  - Maharashtra and Gujarat
  - North-Eastern States (Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Mizoram and others)
  - Sikkim, Goa, Karnataka, **Andhra Pradesh (Art. 371D)**
- **Fifth Schedule:** administration of Scheduled Areas

## Seventh Schedule and residuary powers — lists, entry counts, models | from: S6, S8
# 1. Seventh Schedule - Article 246
- **Mechanism:** operates through **Article 246**
- **Precedence:** **Union > Concurrent > State**

| **List** | **Original** | **Now** | **Scope** |
|---|---|---|---|
| I - Union | **97** | **98** | National importance, uniform law |
| II - State | **66** | **59** | Local/regional importance |
| III - Concurrent | **47** | **52** | Both legislate; Union law prevails |

- **Entry counts:** numbering still ends at 97/66/47 because entries were inserted (e.g. **92A** by 6th CAA, 1956) or omitted
  - Some notes give 100/61/52
  - **6th Amendment (1956):** added **Entry 92A** (inter-state sales tax) to the Union List
# 2. Residuary powers - Article 248
- **Legal basis:** **Article 248** (Part XI, Chapter I) + **Entry 97, List I**
  - **Parliament alone** legislates on matters not in State or Concurrent List
  - Includes **residuary taxes** (gift tax, wealth tax, expenditure tax)
- **GST change:** Art. 248 now reads "subject to Article 246A" - amended by **101st CAA, 2016**
# 3. Residuary power - models

| **Model** | **Residue with** |
|---|---|
| **Canada** | Centre (India's model) |
| **USA and Australia** | the states |
| GoI Act 1935 | the **Governor-General** |

- ⚠ Entry **97** = residuary entry of List I; also the original Union List count **97**
> Exam angle: PYQ 2025 - "Art. 248 = residuary" and "Parliament has exclusive power over items in no list" → **both correct**.

## Emergency provisions → switching from federal to unitary mode | from: S9
# 1. Mode switch
- **Unique feature:** change of mode **needs no constitutional amendment**
- **Part XVIII (Arts. 352-360):** federation becomes **unitary without a formal amendment**

| **Situation** | **Unitary effect** |
|---|---|
| **National Emergency (Art. 352)** - war, external aggression, **armed rebellion** | See effects table below |
| **Art. 249** - Rajya Sabha resolution (**2/3 of members present and voting**) in national interest | Parliament legislates on a State List subject (resolution valid 1 year, renewable) |
| **Art. 356** - failure of constitutional machinery | President's Rule; Parliament/President exercise the State's powers |
| **Financial Emergency (Art. 360)** | See Financial Emergency below |

# 2. National Emergency - effects and safeguards (44th Amendment)

| **Effect of National Emergency (Art. 352)** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| Executive | Union's executive power extends to directing **any State** on how it exercises its executive power |
| Legislative | Parliament can legislate on **State List** matters (**Art. 250**); such laws lapse **6 months** after the emergency ends |
| Lok Sabha term | Can be extended **1 year at a time**; State Assembly terms can also be extended |
| Finance | President can **modify Centre-State distribution of revenues**, i.e. **reduce or cancel** transfers to States (subject to Parliament's approval) |
| Fundamental Rights | Art. 19 suspended only in an **external** emergency (Art. 358); other rights suspended by order under **Art. 359**, **except Arts. 20 and 21** |

- **Ground:** "internal disturbance" replaced by **"armed rebellion"**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "internal disturbance" is no longer a ground (APPSC-GS 2018)
- **Proclamation:** only on **written advice of the Union Cabinet**
- **Parliament approval:** within **1 month**
- **Special sitting:** notice by **1/10th of Lok Sabha** members → sitting within **14 days** to consider disapproval
- **Rights:** Arts **20-21** made non-suspendable
- **Judicial review:** **38th Amendment** barred it → **Minerva Mills** reopened it
# 3. Financial Emergency (Art. 360)
- **Centre's powers:**
  - Direct States to follow canons of financial propriety (Centre directs financial propriety)
  - **Reduce salaries and allowances** of State employees
  - Require State **money bills to be reserved** for the President
  - Reduce salaries of Union employees and **SC/HC judges**
> Exam angle: the President can **reduce or cancel** fund transfers, but "**increase** the transfer" is the wrong statement (APPSC-G2 2017). Modifying the distribution of finances and giving directions to States are correct consequences (APPSC-G2 2023).
# 4. Emergency types compared - Arts 352, 356, 360

| **Type** | **Article** | **Grounds** | **Approval** | **Instances** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| National | **352** | **War, external aggression, armed rebellion** ("internal disturbance" replaced by the **44th Amendment**, 1978) | Within **1 month**, **special majority**, renewable every 6 months | **1962, 1971, 1975** |
| State (President's Rule) | **356** | Failure of constitutional machinery in a State; Art 365 | Within **2 months**, simple majority; maximum 3 years; beyond 1 year only with a national emergency plus **ECI certification** (Art. 356(5)) | Used many times since 1951 |
| Financial | **360** | Threat to the financial stability or credit of India | Within 2 months, simple majority | **Never declared** |

- ⚠ Approval: **352** = **1 month**; **356** and **360** = **2 months**
> Exam angle: "Art. 311 - Financial Emergency" is the mismatch; Financial Emergency is **Art. 360**.

=== ROW 47 | Centre-State legislative relations - Articles 245-255, doctrine of repugnancy, parliamentary legislation on state list

## Articles 245-255 → number game and territorial extent | from: S0, S1
# 1. Part XI Chapter I at a glance
- **Location:** **Part XI** (Relations between the Union and the States, Art. 245-263) (APPSC-GS 2023)

| **Article** | **Content** |
|---|---|
| **245** | **Territorial extent** - Parliament for whole or part of India, incl. **extra-territorial** laws; States only for own territory |
| **246** / 246A | **Subject-matter** division via the three lists (Seventh Schedule) / GST |
| 247 | Parliament may set up **additional courts** for Union laws |
| 248 | **Residuary** powers |
| 249 / 250 | State List - national interest / Emergency |
| 251 | Inconsistency with laws under 249/250 |
| **252** | Law for **two or more States by consent**; others may adopt |
| **253** | Laws to implement **international agreements** |
| **254** | **Repugnancy** - Union law prevails; State law with **President's assent** prevails in that State |
| 255 | Recommendations and previous sanctions = **matters of procedure only** |

- **Art. 255:** Act not invalid for want of a required recommendation or previous sanction if the Governor/President later **assents**
# 2. Article 245 - territorial extent of laws
- **Parliament:** Whole or any part of India; alone has **extra-territorial legislation**
  - Art. 245(2): no Parliament law invalid for extra-territorial operation
  - **Not a limit:** Extra-territorial operation of its laws (permitted by Art. 245(2))
- **State legislature:** Own state only; extra-territorial reach only with sufficient **territorial nexus**
- **Exceptions (special areas):**
  - UTs A&N and Lakshadweep: **President's regulations** for **Andaman & Nicobar** and **Lakshadweep** have force of Parliament's Acts
  - **Governor** can bar/modify Parliament's Acts for **Scheduled Areas**
  - Governor of Assam for Assam tribal areas
- ⚠ **Extra-territorial** laws = Parliament only; State needs **territorial nexus**

## Seventh Schedule, Article 246 and residuary power — lists, entries, precedence | from: S2, S3
# 1. Article 246 scheme
- **Seventh Schedule (Art. 246):** Enumerates subjects for **Union (List I), State (List II), Concurrent (List III)**
- **Source:** Borrowed from **Canada** + a third **Concurrent List** (Government of India Act 1935 tradition)

| **Clause** | **Who legislates** |
|---|---|
| **246(1)** | Parliament exclusive on List I |
| **246(2)** | Both on List III |
| **246(3)** | State on List II, subject to (1) and (2) |
| **Art. 246(4)** | Parliament may legislate on any matter for areas not in a state (UTs); for UTs Parliament covers all three Lists |
| **Art. 246A (101st CAA)** | Both make GST laws; only Parliament for inter-state supply (IGST) |

# 2. Entry counts

| **List** | **Originally** | **Now (CDI)** |
|---|---|---|
| Union | **97** | **98** |
| State | **66** | **59** |
| Concurrent | **47** | **52** |

- **Entry counts:** Some notes (UPSC) give now **100 / 61 / 52**
  - ⚠ **Safe exam fact:** original 97/66/47
- **Precedence (order of predominance):** **Union List > Concurrent List > State List**
# 3. Residuary legislative power - Article 248
- **Basis:** **Art. 248** + **Entry 97 of the Union List** → Parliament has **exclusive** power over any matter not in the State or Concurrent List
  - Covers residuary **taxation** too; residual powers vest in **Parliament** (APPSC-G2 2025: both statements correct)
- **Borrowed from:** **Canada** (federation with a strong Centre)
  - Canada model vs USA/Australia (residue with states)

## Parliament legislating on the State List — five special circumstances and limits | from: S4
# 1. Special circumstances - overview

| **Situation** (when Parliament can make law on a State List matter) | **Article** | **Key condition** / effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Rajya Sabha resolution** (national interest) | **Art. 249** | Rajya Sabha resolution by **2/3 of members present and voting** in the national interest; valid **up to 1 year**, renewable |
| **National Emergency** | **Art. 250** | While a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation; laws become inoperative **6 months after** the emergency ends |
| **States' request** (request of two or more states) | **Art. 252** | Resolutions by the legislatures of **2 or more states**; only **Parliament** can amend/repeal; other states may adopt it by resolution |
| **International agreements** | **Art. 253** | To implement treaties, agreements, conventions - for whole or any part of India |
| **President's Rule** in a state | **Art. 356** | Parliament (or President by delegation) makes laws for the state; **President's Rule laws:** stay in force after PR ends, until the state legislature repeals, alters or re-enacts them |

- **Count:** CDI counts these as **five special circumstances**
  - APP's heading says "4" (lists four plus PR) but its table lists all five
  - The five = **Art. 249, 250, 252, 253** and **356**
> Exam angle: PYQ 2023 - during an Emergency, State List laws are made by **Parliament**; PYQ 2018 - international agreements = **Art. 253**.
> Exam angle: statement sets asking which situations are valid ("request of two or more states" is valid; a request by one state is not).
# 2. Limits on Parliament's legislative competence
- **State List:** Cannot legislate except under **Arts. 249, 250, 252, 253, 356**
- **Art. 301-303:** Cannot prefer or discriminate between states in trade laws
  - Parliament cannot discriminate between states except in scarcity
- **Inter-state trade restrictions (state laws):** Reasonable restrictions in public interest allowed, but bill needs **President's previous sanction** (Part XIII, Arts. 301-305)

## Articles 249-253 → conditions, duration and effect | from: S5, S6
# 1. Article 249 - resolution and duration
- **Majority:** Supported by **two-thirds of members present and voting** (APPSC-GS 2025)
- **Duration:** In force **not exceeding 1 year** (**up to one year**)
  - Renewable any number of times, **1 year at a time** (APP MCQ answer: one year)
- **Lapse:** Laws made under it **cease 6 months after the resolution lapses**
# 2. Effect on state power - Articles 250 and 251
- **State legislature:** Power not suspended; can still legislate on the same matter
- **Emergency:** During a Proclamation of Emergency, **Parliament** (not any minister) can legislate on State List matters for all or part of India (APPSC-GS 2023)
  - State's power not suspended, but Parliament's law prevails on conflict
- **Art. 251:** State law conflicting with a Parliamentary law made under Art. 249/250 → the Parliamentary (Union) law prevails
  - State law inoperative only while the Union law lasts
# 3. Article 252 - consent of states
- **Procedure:** Legislatures of **two or more states** pass resolutions → Parliament legislates for those states
- **Adoption:** Any other state can adopt the law later by a resolution of its own
- **Amendment/repeal:** Only **Parliament**; the requesting states lose that power
# 4. Article 253 - international agreements
- **Art. 253 (treaties):** Parliament can make laws for the whole or any part of India to implement treaties, international agreements, conventions and decisions of international bodies, even on State List subjects (APPSC-GS 2018)
  - Covers environment treaties and similar
  - **No state consent is needed**
- ⚠ **252** = needs consent of **two or more states**; **253** = **no state consent** needed

## Doctrines → meaning and cases (repugnancy, pith and substance, colourable legislation) | from: S7, S8
# 1. Repugnancy - Article 254
- **Art. 254(1):** Central law vs state law on a **Concurrent List** subject → **Central (Union) law prevails**
  - State law void to the extent of repugnancy ("doctrine of repugnancy")
- **254(2):** State law **reserved and assented by the President** prevails **in that state**
  - Governor may reserve a bill under 254(2) as an exception to aid-and-advice
- **Proviso:** Parliament can later **override it with a fresh law** (amend or repeal that state law) → states "practically powerless"
# 2. Interpretation doctrines
- **Pith and substance (Doctrine of Pith and Substance):** Courts look at the true object of a law, not its incidental encroachment, to decide its List
  - APP MCQ: doctrine used for Union-State List encroachment
- **Colourable legislation:** "Cannot do indirectly what cannot be done directly" - **K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v State of Orissa (1953)**

| **Doctrine** | **Meaning** | **Case** |
|---|---|---|
| **Pith and substance** | True nature decides the list; incidental encroachment ignored | **State of Bombay v. F.N. Balsara (1951)** - Bombay Prohibition Act upheld |
| **Colourable legislation** | "What cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly" | **K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v. State of Orissa (1953)** |
| Harmonious construction | Entries read together so both operate [GK] | - |
| Repugnancy (Art. 254) | Union law prevails in Concurrent field | - |

- ⚠ **Balsara (1951)** = pith and substance; **Gajapati Narayan Deo (1953)** = colourable legislation
