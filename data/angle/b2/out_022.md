## Grow More Food Inquiry, CDP and NES — features and organisation | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. Grow More Food Inquiry Committee (1952)
- **Chairman:** **V.T. Krishnamachari** (APPSC-G2 2008 key)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Distractors - Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao
- **Finding:** Found campaign too narrow → integrated village-development approach
- **Recommended:** Nationwide **extension organisation** → CDP and **NES (1953)**
# 2. Community Development Programme (1952) — launch
- **Date:** **2 October 1952** (Gandhi Jayanti), during **First Five Year Plan (1951-56)**
- **Title:** "Mother of social development programmes"
- **Pilot:** Began with **55 community projects**
- **Unit:** The **block**
# 3. CDP — objectives and components
- **Objective:** Raise **standard of living of the rural population** through **public participation** in village development
  - APPSC 2018 key: "development of village through public participation"
  - Put the DPSP into practice → step towards **Directive Principles (Part IV)**, esp. Art. 40
- **Components:** Agriculture, irrigation, communications, health, housing, **social education**
- **Social education** (adult literacy, community centres, youth and women's clubs): part of **Community Development Programme** (APPSC 2019)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** NOT part of NAEP, IRDP or National Literacy Mission (APPSC 2019 key)
- **Planners' expectation:** Village panchayat + trained CDP staff → push land reforms, organise voluntary labour, set up cooperatives
- **AP angle:** **Andhra State Plan (1953, capital Kurnool)** under First Plan; CDP part of it
> Exam angle: year asked directly (1952, not 1950/1957/1959); "aim = public participation" is the keyed option.
# 4. National Extension Service (1953)
- **Launch:** NES **1953**, extension arm complementing and supporting CDP (BPSC 2022 key: 1953)
- **Relation:** Lighter, extension-staff version → spread CDP pattern (block, BDO, VLW) nationwide; intensive CD blocks came later

# Nyaya Panchayats — nature and committee views
- **Nature:** Judicial panchayats set up by **some states** to try **petty civil and criminal cases** (1960s variations)
- **Committee views:**
  - Ashok Mehta (1978): **separate from development panchayats**, presided by **qualified judge**
  - L.M. Singhvi (1986): nyaya panchayats for **clusters of villages**
- **Examples:** UP (UP Panchayat Raj Act 1947); Bihar's Gram Kachahari
- ⚠ **Trap:** **Not** part of the three-tier PR structure

## Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) → appointment, findings, recommendations | from: S3, S4, S5
# 1. Appointment and report
- **Weakness of CDP and NES:** Seen as imposed by government; failed to draw people in → little people's participation → Balwant Rai Mehta review
- **Appointed by:** **Government of India**, **January 1957** (high-level committee)
- **Chairman:** **Balwantrai G. Mehta**
- **Terms:** Examine **Community Development Programme (1952)** + **National Extension Service (1953)**; suggest improvements
- **Report:** **November 1957** (APPSC-G2 key: 1957)
# 2. Findings
- **Main finding:** **Local people's participation** / public participation is indispensable for development
- **Remedy:** **Democratic decentralisation** → transfer power and resources to elected 3-tier bodies (later Panchayati Raj)
  - "Democratic decentralisation" scheme "later came to be known as Panchayati Raj"
# 3. Recommendations
- **Structure:** **Three-tier** system - Gram Panchayat (village), **Panchayat Samiti (block)**, **Zila Parishad (district)**
  - "Organically linked" through **indirect elections** (UPSC)
- **Roles:**
  - **District Collector as chairman** of the **Zila Parishad**
  - Panchayat Samiti: block = basic unit of planning and development
- **Functions:** Planning and development entrusted to these bodies
  - Genuine transfer of power and responsibility + adequate resources
  - System for further devolution later

| Tier | Level | Election | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gram Panchayat | village | **direct** | local body |
| Panchayat Samiti | block | **indirect** | **executive body** |
| Zila Parishad | district | **indirect** | **advisory, coordinating, supervisory** |

# 4. Non-recommendations
- **Did NOT recommend:** two-tier system; direct election to Zila Parishad; Zila Parishad as executive body (APPSC 2019 key: "c and d only")
> Exam angle (APPSC 2019): "two-tier" and "direct elections to both village panchayat and Zila Parishad" are false. "All planning and development activities entrusted to these bodies" and "Collector as ZP chairman" are true. Keyed answer: c and d. Nyaya Panchayat is NOT one of the three tiers (UPPSC 2024).

## Ashok Mehta Committee (1977-78) → appointment, two tiers, recommendations | from: S8, S9
# 1. Appointment and report
- **Appointed by:** **Janata government**, PM **Morarji Desai** (APPSC-G2 2008 key), **December 1977** (1977-78)
- **Report:** **August 1978**, **132 recommendations**
# 2. Two tiers
- **Structure:** **Two-tier** in place of three tiers
  - APPSC-G2 2023 and 2025 key: "two tier system with **four years** of term"

| Tier | Unit |
|---|---|
| Zila Parishad | district, the **executive body**, responsible for district planning |
| Mandal Panchayat | group of villages, population **15,000-20,000** |

- **Unit:** **District** = first point of decentralisation under popular supervision below the state
- **Lower tier:** **Mandal Panchayat**; development functions and staff → Zila Parishad
> Exam angle: APPSC (2023, 2025) asked Ashok Mehta = "**two-tier system with four years of term**".
> Exam angle (UPPSC 2023, key "only (1)"): "three-tier replaced by two-tier" is correct. The second statement, about the block, is the trap: Ashok Mehta made the **district**, not the block, the unit.
# 3. Other recommendations
- **Constitutional recognition:** For PRIs - sanctity, stature, continued functioning (UPSC)
- **Political parties:** **Official participation of political parties** at all levels of panchayat elections
- **Elections:** Run by state's **Chief Electoral Officer** with Chief Election Commissioner
  - Superseded body → elections within **6 months**
- **Taxation:** **Compulsory powers of taxation** → PRIs raise own money
- **Social audit:** **Regular social audit** by district-level agency + committee of legislators
- **Other points:** **Minister for Panchayati Raj** in state cabinet; **SC/ST reservation by population**

## Ashok Mehta Committee → fate and legacy; BRM vs Ashok Mehta | from: S10
- **Fate:** **Janata government fell** before completing term → Centre took **no action**
- **APPSC-G2 2017 key:** **Sixth Five Year Plan** - "could not become an official plan of India" - gave PRIs new lease of life
  - Janata government's **1978-83** plan, dropped after **1980**
  - Matches Ashok Mehta report of **1978**
- **Legacy:** Post-1977 panchayats on its lines = "**Second Generation Panchayats**"
- **West Bengal:** Adopted its recommendations; strengthened PRIs (UPSC notes); **Karnataka** also followed
- **Andhra Pradesh:** NTR government replaced Panchayat Samithis with **Mandal Praja Parishads** (Mandal Praja Parishads Act, **1986**)
  - Mandal-level tier in the Ashok Mehta mould

| Point | Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) | Ashok Mehta (1978) |
|---|---|---|
| Tiers | 3 | 2 |
| Unit of planning | block (Samiti executive) | district (ZP executive) |
| Elections | direct at village; indirect above | party participation at all levels |
| Collector | ZP chairman | reduced development role |
| Constitutional status | not proposed | recommended |

- ⚠ **3 – block – Collector chairman** = **Balwant Rai Mehta**; **2 – district – parties** = **Ashok Mehta**

## L.M. Singhvi, Thungon and Gadgil Committees → constitutional status | from: S13, S14
# 1. L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986)
- **Appointed by:** **Rajiv Gandhi government**, **1986** → concept paper on reviving PRIs
- **Title:** "**Revitalisation of Panchayati Raj Institutions for Democracy and Development**" (APPSC 2018, G2 2008)
- **Chairman:** **L.M. Singhvi**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Sarkaria, Rajamannar and G.V.K. Rao options are distractors (APPSC-G2 2008)
- **Recommendations:**
  - **Constitutional recognition** of PRIs through a **new chapter** in the Constitution
  - Constitutional provisions for **regular, free and fair elections**
  - Reorganise villages to make gram panchayats viable; more money for village panchayats
  - **Gram Sabha** = "embodiment of direct democracy"
  - **Nyaya panchayats** for clusters of villages
  - **Judicial tribunals** in each state for PR election and dissolution disputes
- **Key:** Singhvi = committee that "first suggested constitutional status" (APPSC 2018)
  - Older notes: Ashok Mehta also recommended it
# 2. P.K. Thungon (1988)
- **Body:** Sub-committee of Parliament's Consultative Committee
- **Recommendations:**
  - Constitutional recognition; 3 tiers; ZP as pivot of planning
  - Fixed **5-year** term; supersession at most **6 months**
  - List of PR subjects in the Constitution (later the **Eleventh Schedule**)
  - Reservation for SC/ST (by population) and women
  - State Finance Commission; **Collector as ZP CEO**
# 3. Gadgil (1988)
- **Body:** Set up by Congress party - **Congress Party** Committee on Policy and Programmes
- **Recommendations:**
  - Constitutional status; three tiers; direct elections at all levels
  - SC/ST/women reservation
  - PRIs to levy taxes
  - **State Finance Commission** + **State Election Commission**
- **Impact:** "Fed into draft amendment proposals"; model for the **64th Amendment Bill**

## Bills and Amendments → year, government, fate (1989 to 1992) | from: S15
# 1. 64th Amendment Bill (1989)
- **Moved by:** **Rajiv Gandhi** government, Lok Sabha, **July 1989** → constitutional status to panchayats
- **Basis:** Singhvi, Thungon, Gadgil recommendations - constitutional status, regular elections, reservation
- **Fate:** **Passed by the Lok Sabha** → **defeated in the Rajya Sabha** (October 1989); no special majority → **lapsed**
- **Opposition criticism:** Feared it would **centralise** power and bypass States - Centre dealing directly with local bodies over states' heads
  - **Basis:** "Local Government" = **State List** subject
- **Nagarpalika Bill:** Rajiv Gandhi govt; constitutional status + structural reform for **municipalities**
- **Key:** 64th Bill = **legislative origin of the 73rd Amendment Act, 1992** (BPSC 2020 key)
# 2. National Front government's bill (1990)
- **V.P. Singh (National Front) govt:** Fresh bills in **September 1990**, incl. revised Nagarpalika Bill
- **Fate:** Never passed; lapsed when government fell - **lapsed when the Lok Sabha was dissolved**
# 3. Narasimha Rao government's bills (1991-92)
- **Introduction:** **P.V. Narasimha Rao** govt dropped controversial parts → modified Panchayat and Municipality bills, **September 1991**
  - Singhvi, Thungon, Gadgil thinking fed into it
- **Passage:** Referred to **Joint Parliamentary Committee** → passed by Parliament **December 1992**

| Act | In force | Adds |
|---|---|---|
| 73rd Amendment, 1992 | **24 April 1993** | Part IX (Art. 243-243O), **Eleventh Schedule** (29 items) |

- **National Panchayati Raj Day:** 24 April (Apr 2026)
- ⚠ 64th Bill: **passed Lok Sabha**, **defeated Rajya Sabha** (1989); 1990 bill: **Lok Sabha dissolved**

## Universal angles | from: S19 | universal
# 1. Significance: PR evolution and impact
- **Evolution:** Development schemes of CDP → self-government; Art. 40 (DPSP) → justiciable Part IX
- **Origin chain:** CDP → NES → Mehta Committee → NDC acceptance (Jan 1958) → Rajasthan and AP (1959) (memory chain)
- **Third tier:** Laid the groundwork for a "third tier of government"
- **Ashok Mehta-type committee:** First committee to seek constitutional recognition + party-based elections for PRIs; shaped the **1978-83** revival phase
- **Blueprint of 73rd Amendment:** Singhvi + Thungon + Gadgil → constitutional status, SEC, SFC, 5-year term, reservation
- **1989 bill → 1992 Acts:** Made PRIs **justiciable** + put a **constitutional obligation** on states
- **Women:** Over **49.7%** of elected panchayat representatives; **19 States/UTs** reserve 50% of seats (23 Apr 2026)
- **17th National Panchayati Raj Day:** Held on **24 April 2026**
# 2. FIRST/LAST: firsts in PR
- **First to adopt PR:** Rajasthan (Nagaur, 2 Oct 1959)
- **Whole state:** AP first to adopt PR across the whole state
- **Constitutional status:** 64th Bill = first attempt
- **CDP:** India's first major post-Independence rural development programme
# 3. AP angle: adoption and tiers
- **Adoption:** AP second to adopt Panchayati Raj (1959)
  - Implemented the three-tier Mehta model on **1 November 1959** under CM N. Sanjiva Reddy's government
  - First elections to Zilla Parishads, Samithis and Panchayats saw village and caste clashes
- **Intermediate tier:** **Mandal Parishad**, not a block Panchayat Samiti
  - **Mandal Praja Parishads** for its 588 mandals (May 2026)
- **Firka Development Scheme:** Madras Presidency, 1946; covered present-day Andhra areas
- **NIRDPR:** National rural development institute, headquartered in **Hyderabad**
# 4. Current relevance: elections and amendments
- **AP local body elections:**

# 5. Comparative note: Collector, states, JPC
- **Collector's role:**
  - BRM: Collector-chaired ZP
  - ARC, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao: smaller role for the Collector; G.V.K. Rao wanted a DDC
  - Hanumantha Rao (1984) + Dantwala: Collector kept as main coordinator / central to district planning
- **West Bengal:** Panchayat Act **1973**; first panchayat elections **June 1978**; 2026 amendment curbs pradhans' financial powers (29 Jul 2026)
- **JPC route:** Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 (simultaneous elections) still with a **Joint Parliamentary Committee** (25 Aug 2026) = same route as 1991 PR bills
