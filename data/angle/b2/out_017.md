=== ROW 63 | Elections - delimitation, model code of conduct, EVM-VVPAT, NOTA, election petitions

## Elections — definitions, types and FIRSTs | from: S3, S4, S5, S10, S13, S14, S17
# 1. Electoral systems
| **System** | **Used for** |
|---|---|
| **FPTP (territorial, single-member)** | **Lok Sabha, Assemblies** |
| **PR (single transferable vote)** | **Rajya Sabha, Legislative Councils, President, Vice-President** (VP) |
- **FPTP winner:** candidate with the **most votes (plurality)**, not a majority
- **Why PR rejected for Lok Sabha:**
  - Hard for voters
  - **Fragmentation and unstable** governments
  - Expensive; allows no by-elections; weakens voter-MP link
- **Security deposit:** forfeited if a candidate gets **less than one-sixth** of valid votes
# 2. Types of elections
| **Type** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **General** | All seats, normal term end |
| **Mid-term** | After premature dissolution |
| **By-election** | Single vacancy, within **6 months**, Sec. 151A RPA - exceptions under Commencement of the election process |
# 3. Key terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Delimitation** | Fixing the **number of seats and boundaries** of Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies |
| **Floating constituency** | No fixed geographical boundary; example: **Sangha (Sikkim)** seat - only registered Buddhist monks vote and contest |
- **Delimitation principle:** **"one person, one vote, one value"** / **one citizen-one vote-one value** (UPSC; APPSC-G1 2024)
  - Art. 81(2) uniformity of representation
- **Floating constituency PYQ:** APPSC-G2 2023, 2025: both statements correct
# 4. FIRSTs
| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| **First general election** | **1951-52**; the first four (1951-52, 1957, 1962, 1967) were **simultaneous** with Assembly polls |
| **MCC first drafted / used** | **Kerala** government, **1960**; first used in the **1960 Kerala** Assembly election |
| **Indelible ink first used** | **1962** general election |
| **NOTA first Lok Sabha use** | **2014** → **1.1%** of votes (over 6 million) |
- ⚠ **Trap:** "first used in 1960 in Mysore" is the **wrong** statement (APPSC 2019) - **1960** = MCC in Kerala; ink = **1962**

## Constitutional Articles on elections → provisions | from: S0, S2, S5
# 1. Part XV, Articles 324-329
| **Article** | **Subject** |
|---|---|
| **324** | ECI - superintendence, direction, control of elections |
| **325** | **One general electoral roll**; no exclusion on religion, race, caste, sex [GK] |
| **326** | Adult suffrage |
| **327 / 328** | Parliament / State legislature may make election laws [GK] |
| **329** | **Bar on courts**: delimitation laws cannot be questioned; elections challenged only by **election petition**[GK] |
- **Local polls:** State Election Commissions (**Arts. 243K, 243ZA**) run panchayat and municipal polls, not the ECI
# 2. Article 326 - right to vote
- **Art. 326:** universal adult suffrage - every citizen aged **18+** may vote unless disqualified
  - Grounds: non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, corrupt/illegal practice
- **Voting age cut:** **61st Amendment Act, 1988** → **21 to 18**, in force 1989 (some notes say 1987)
- **Nature:** **Statutory right, not a Fundamental Right** (RPA 1950 Section 19; RPA 1951) - a **statutory right** (under the RPA)
# 3. Articles on reserved seats
| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 330** (Lok Sabha) + **Art. 332** (Assemblies) | SC/ST seats in proportion to population; **No separate electorate** - all voters of the constituency vote |
| **Art. 331** | President nominated **2** Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha |
| **Art. 333** | Governor 1 Anglo-Indian per Assembly |
| **Art. 334** | Originally **10 years** (till 1960), repeatedly extended |
| Art. **334A** | Women - one-third of seats (**106th Amendment (2023)**) |
- ⚠ **330 / 332** = SC/ST seats; **331 / 333** = Anglo-Indians (ended)

## RPA sections → content | from: S1, S6, S9, S15
# 1. RPA 1950 vs RPA 1951
| **RPA 1950** | **RPA 1951** |
|---|---|
| Allocation of seats, delimitation [GK] | Conduct of elections, notifications [GK] |
| **Electoral rolls**; **Sec. 19** voter eligibility; **Sec. 21** roll revision (basis of **SIR**) | **Sec. 8** disqualifications; **29A** party registration; **77-78** expense accounts |
| Chief Electoral Officers, EROs [GK] | Corrupt practices, **election petitions**[GK] |
# 2. Sections → content
| **Section** | **Content** |
|---|---|
| **Sec. 20A, RPA 1950** (2010) | **Overseas electors:** NRIs enrol at their passport address |
| **Sec. 14** | **Lok Sabha:** **President** issues the notification on the ECI's recommendation |
| **Sec. 15** | **Assembly:** **Governor** issues it on the ECI's recommendation |
| **Section 126A RPA 1951** | No exit poll conducted/published from the **start of polling in the first phase** until **half an hour after the close of the last phase** |
| Sec. 126 | **Opinion polls:** barred in the **48 hours** before the close of poll |
- **ECI role:** fixes the schedule and nomination dates
- **Bypolls:** within **6 months** of a vacancy
  - Exceptions: remaining term **under one year**, or ECI (consulting the Centre) certifies difficulty
- **Exit poll:** poll of voters taken **immediately after they leave** the polling station; not the same as an opinion poll
  - **Key:** APPSC 2018 - a, c, d correct
- ⚠ **Sec. 14** = President (Lok Sabha); **Sec. 15** = Governor (Assembly)
# 3. Electoral rolls
- **Four qualifying dates:** **1 January, 1 April, 1 July, 1 October** (Election Laws (Amendment) Act 2021)
- **Revisions:**
  - **Summary revision** = annual
  - **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** = rebuilds the roll; SC allowed the ECI's **Bihar SIR** to proceed
  - **SIR (Special Intensive Revision):** Bihar SIR removed about **70 lakh names**; turnout that followed was highest since Independence (CEC, 17 Aug 2026)

## Model Code of Conduct — status, timeline and Part VII | from: S10
# 1. Legal status
- **Nature:** ECI guidelines for parties, candidates and the ruling government; **not statutory** / **Not a statutory law**
- **Source of authority:** ECI's **plenary power under Art. 324** (**Article 324**)
  - SC in **Mohinder Singh Gill v CEC (1978)**: Art. 324 = "**reservoir of power**"
# 2. Timeline
| **Year** | **Milestone** |
|---|---|
| **1960** | First drafted by the **Kerala** government; first used in the **1960 Kerala** Assembly election |
| **1968** | Formalised by the ECI |
| 1974 | Revised |
| **1979** | **Part VII** on the "**party in power**" added |
| **1991** | Enforced with rigour (strict enforcement) by CEC **T.N. Seshan** |
# 3. Duration
- **Comes into force:** **from the announcement of the schedule** (**announcement of the election schedule**) - **Harbans Singh Jalal v UoI (1997)**, P&H HC
  - Example: from **15 March 2026** for the Assam, Kerala, TN, West Bengal and Puducherry polls
- **Lifted:** once results are declared
  - ECI lifted it in Assam, Kerala, TN, WB, Puducherry on **7 May 2026**, except the Falta re-poll seat (reported 8 May 2026)
# 4. Part VII and sanctions
- **Part VII curbs (party in power):**
  - Ministers may not combine **official visits with electioneering** / campaigning
  - No use of official machinery/transport
  - No new schemes or grants once polls are announced
- **Sanctions:** censure up to **suspension/withdrawal of recognition** under **Para 16A** of the Symbols Order

# 2. Contesting from more than one constituency - Section 33(7)
- **Sec. 33(7) RPA:** contest from **at most two** constituencies (since the **1996** amendment)

- **Before 1996:** no cap - **Devi Lal contested three Lok Sabha seats in 1991** (UPSC 2021 key: statement correct)
- **Reforms:**
  - **One candidate, one seat** (Law Commission 255th report)
  - Vacating candidate pays for the bypoll (ECI proposal)
# 3. Other changes made by the RP (Amendment) Act, 1996
- **Nominations:**
  - Cap of **two constituencies** (above)
  - **Higher security deposits**
  - More proposers for independents/unrecognised parties
- **Countermanding:** only on death of a **recognised-party** candidate
- **Poll day:**
  - **Paid holiday** (Sec. 135B)
  - **Liquor ban** 48 hours before poll (Sec. 135C)
  - Bypolls within **6 months** (Sec. 151A)
- **Disqualification:** offences under the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971**
- ⚠ **Sec. 70** = vacate within **14 days**; **Sec. 81** = petition within **45 days**; **Sec. 78** = accounts within **30 days**

## Criminalisation of politics — disclosure, data, reforms | from: S3
# 1. Disclosure
- **Affidavits (2002):** candidates file details of criminal cases, assets, liabilities, education

## Money power — expenditure limits, donations, electoral bonds | from: S4, S5
# 1. Candidate expenditure limits
| **Category** | **Lok Sabha** | **Assembly** |
|---|---|---|
| **Larger States/UTs** | **Rs 95 lakh** (₹95 lakh; earlier Rs 70 lakh) | **Rs 40 lakh** (₹40 lakh; earlier Rs 28 lakh) |
| **Smaller States/UTs** | **Rs 75 lakh** (₹75 lakh; earlier Rs 54 lakh) | **Rs 28 lakh** (₹28 lakh; earlier Rs 20 lakh) |
- **Legal basis:** **Rule 90, Conduct of Election Rules 1961**
- **Current values:** raised by **Conduct of Elections (Amendment) Rules, 2022** under the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961
  - Older keys: Rs 40 lakh (APPSC-G1 2012 era), Rs 70 lakh (CGPSC key)
- **Scope:** limits apply to **candidates, not parties**; travel of **20 star campaigners** excluded (Sep 2026)
  - **Loophole:** Sec. 77 caps **candidate** spending, not **parties'** own election spending
- **Accounts:**
  - RPA **Sec. 77**: keep expense accounts
  - **Sec. 78**: file within **30 days** with the District Election Officer (DEO)
  - Failure → ECI can **disqualify** the candidate
# 2. Party donations - cash limits and evasion
- **Tax exemption conditions:**
  - **No cash donation above Rs 2,000**
  - Records of donations above **Rs 20,000**
  - Now **Sec. 12, Income Tax Act 2025** (11 Sep 2026); earlier Sec. 13A, IT Act 1961
- **Evasion:** large gifts split below **Rs 20,000**; money diverted to **surrogate digital ads and paid news**
- ⚠ **Rs 2,000** = cash cap; **Rs 20,000** = record threshold
# 3. Electoral bonds - features
- **Scheme:** notified **2018** (via Finance Act 2017 amendments)
- **Seller:** only **SBI**; bearer bonds
- **Denominations:** **₹1,000 to ₹1 crore**; valid **15 days**
- **Encashment:** only registered parties with at least **1%** (**1% of the vote**) in the last election
- **Anonymity:** donor identity hidden from public - older notes call it a "transparency" gain
# 4. ADR v Union of India, 15 Feb 2024 (Association for Democratic Reforms v Union of India)
- **Electoral bonds:** struck down by SC (**ADR v UoI, Feb 2024**)
- **Bench:** **five-judge Constitution Bench** (5-judge), unanimous
- **Verdict:** scheme **struck down as unconstitutional** / **struck the scheme down as unconstitutional**
  - Violated voters' **right to information under Art. 19(1)(a)**
  - Struck down removal of the corporate donation cap from the Companies Act
  - Ordered **SBI** to give donor data/details to the ECI
- **Context:** ADR - **60% (₹19,083 cr)** of party funds in 2004-23 from **undisclosed sources**

## EVM, VVPAT, NOTA and RVM — FIRSTs and features | from: S7, S8, S9, S10
# 1. FIRSTs
| **Milestone** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| First EVM use (pilot) | **70-Parur Assembly constituency, Kerala, 1982** (IYB); **70-Parur, Kerala (1982)** |
| Wider trial | 16 Assembly seats in MP, Rajasthan and Delhi, 1998 [GK] |
| First whole-State Assembly election on EVMs | **Goa, 1999**[GK]; **Goa Assembly election, 1999**[GK] |
| First nationwide use | **Lok Sabha General Election 2004**, every polling station (**2004** Lok Sabha election = universal use) |
| VVPAT first use | By-election in **51-Noksen Assembly constituency, Nagaland, 2013**, after **Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961** were amended |
| NOTA button added | **27 Sept 2013**, PUCL v Union of India; symbol (ballot paper with black cross) designed by **NID Ahmedabad** |
| NOTA first used | **2013 Assembly elections** (Chhattisgarh, Mizoram, Rajasthan, MP, Delhi) |
| NOTA first Lok Sabha use | **2014**; NOTA got **1.1%** (over 60 lakh votes) |
- **PYQ:** the Goa 1999 statement is true (APPSC-G2 2025 key)
- ⚠ **Trap:** Gujarat was **not** the first State to use EVMs in an Assembly election; EVMs **not** first used pan-India before 2004
> Exam angle: "first" statements. Parur 1982 = first use; Goa 1999 = first whole State; 2004 = all-India.
# 2. EVM - manufacturers and capacity
- **Manufacturers (two PSUs):**
  - **Bharat Electronics Ltd (BEL)**, Bengaluru
  - **Electronics Corporation of India Ltd (ECIL)**, Hyderabad
- **Capacity:**
  - M3 EVM records up to **2,000 votes**
  - One control unit takes up to **24 ballot units = 384 candidates incl. NOTA**
  - M2: 4 BUs, 64 candidates
- **Colour photos:** EVMs to show **colour photos** of candidates
- **SC stance:** has repeatedly **upheld EVMs**; in ADR v ECI (2024) refused a **return to paper ballots**
- **ECI admission:** EVMs "are still a subject of frequent public questioning" (2 Sep 2026)
# 3. VVPAT - working and verification
- **Working:** **prints a slip** with candidate's serial number, name and symbol
  - Voter sees it for about **7 seconds** → **instant feedback**
  - Slip drops into a sealed box
- **Generations:** **M1, M2 and M3** (current); VVPATs made by BEL and ECIL
- **ADR v ECI (April 2024) - detail:** candidates placed **2nd and 3rd** (runners-up) can seek a check of **5% of EVMs' burnt memory** per segment within **7 days** of result
- **New ECI rules:**
  - **VVPAT slips must be counted wherever EVM data do not match Form 17C**
  - **Penultimate EVM/VVPAT round** counted only after **postal ballots** are finished
# 4. Remote voting machine and totalisers
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Remote EVM (RVM)** | ECI prototype for domestic migrants; up to **72 constituencies** from one remote polling station; demonstrated to parties in **Jan 2023** |
| **Totaliser** | Pools count of several control units → **booth-wise voting patterns stay hidden**; recommended by **Law Commission 255th Report (2015)** |
- **SC notice (1 Sep 2026):** SC **sought the Centre's response** on totalisers as a "fall-back option"
  - **Totalisers:** count several EVMs together to hide booth-wise trends; SC sought the Centre's response (3 Sep 2026)
- **ECI objection:**
  - No legal framework in the RPA or Conduct of Election Rules
  - Would make booth- and machine-wise checks (EVM, VVPAT, **Form 17C**) harder
