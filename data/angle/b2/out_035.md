# 1. 86th Amendment (2002)
- **86th Amendment (2002):** did three things
  - inserted **Art. 21A** (free and compulsory education for **6-14 years** as a Fundamental Right)
  - rewrote **Art. 45** (early childhood care up to 6)
  - added **Art. 51A(k)**
- **Unni Krishnan follow-up:** Constitution Amendment Bill (July 1997) → enacted as **86th Amendment (Dec 2002)**
- ⚠ **21A** = Fundamental Right **6-14**; **45** = DPSP, care **till 6**; **51A(k)** = parent's duty **6-14**; **24** = below **14** in factories/mines
- ⚠ **39(f)** reworded by **42nd** (1976); **21A, 45, 51A(k)** all by **86th** (2002)

## RTE Act 2009 → sections, dates and numbers | from: S3, S4
# 1. Basics
- **Full name:** **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009**
- **Constitutional basis:** gives effect to **Article 21A**
- **In force:** **1 April 2010**
> Exam angle: statement pairs - "6-14 years" and "enacted under Art. 21A" are both correct (APPSC answer: both).

# 2. Sections → provision
| **Section** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Section 3** | Coverage: age **6-14 years**; free and compulsory education in a **neighbourhood school** till **elementary education (Classes 1-8)** (Class I-VIII) |
| **Section 12(1)(c)** | Unaided private schools (excluding **minority institutions**) reserve **25%** of entry-level seats for EWS/weaker sections and disadvantaged groups; State reimburses per-child expenditure |
| **Section 21** | **School Management Committees** in every government/aided school; three-fourths members = parents/guardians |
| **Sec. 38** | Rules; **SC (Jan 2026):** directed rules under Sec. 38 + NCPCR monitoring; quota works in only **16 States/UTs** (details in Universal angles) |

# 3. Teachers and numbers
- **Pupil-teacher ratio:** **30:1** primary / **35:1** upper primary
- **Teacher qualification:** set by academic authority notified by Centre = **NCTE** (National Council for Teacher Education), not a State Council; TET per NCTE guidelines
  - Act amended in **2012 and 2017** on teacher qualifications

# 4. No-detention → amendments
| **Stage** | **Rule** |
|---|---|
| Original Act | no detention till Class 8 |
| **RTE (Amendment) Act 2019** | ended automatic promotion - regular exams in **Classes 5 and 8**; **detention at certain stages (Classes 5 and 8) with safeguards** - children failing re-exam can be held back |
| Further amended | **2022** |

- ⚠ **30:1** = primary; **35:1** = upper primary
- ⚠ Teacher norms = **NCTE** (Centre), not a State council

## RTE Act features — included vs not included | from: S5
| **Included** | **Not included / wrong statements** |
|---|---|
| Free and compulsory education for all children 6-14 | Centre bearing **sole** financial responsibility (funds are **shared** by Centre and States) |
| Duty of appropriate Govt and local authority to establish neighbourhood schools | Pre-school (below 6) and secondary/higher education |
| Duty of every parent/guardian to send the child to school (links Art. 51A(k)) | Coverage of minority schools under the 25% quota |
| 25% EWS seats in unaided private schools, reimbursement by State | Teacher norms set by a State council |

- **Also in the Act:**
  - Ban on screening/capitation fee
  - Ban on physical punishment and mental harassment
  - Ban on private tuition by teachers
  - Out-of-school child admitted to age-appropriate class
> Exam angle: APPSC 2017 asked the odd one out - "Central government has sole responsibility for funds" is NOT a feature.

## Child abuse and child labour — forms, causes and effects | from: S0, S8
# 1. Forms of child abuse
| **Form** | **Meaning / examples** |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Acts of commission (beating, burning) or omission (medical or nutritional neglect) |
| **Sexual (CSA)** | Using a child for sexual stimulation |
| **Emotional/non-physical** | Verbal battering, educational neglect, fostering delinquency |
| **Social** | Infanticide, **child marriage**, child prostitution, beggary |

- **Online abuse / CSAM:**
  - Over **4.5 lakh** cases reported in India in **2023**; dealt with under **IT Act 2000**
  - **IT Rules 2021:** Large platforms must detect CSAM automatically
- **Child labour as abuse:** India has the largest number of child labourers in the world; debt bondage runs across generations
- **Sectors of child labour:** Brick kilns, pesticide-heavy farm work, toxic chemicals, tea stalls, begging

# 2. Causes → consequences of abuse
| **Causes** | **Consequences** |
|---|---|
| Poverty, family breakdown, parental alcoholism, conflict between parents | Loss of self-esteem, dependency, trauma |
| Lack of affectionate relationships; unsafe workplaces and institutions | Deviant behaviour (drug abuse, stealing), delinquency |
| Gaps in institutional care | School dropout, interpersonal problems, **revictimisation** |

# 3. Causes of child labour
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Poverty** | Forces children to support families |
| **Cheap labour** | Employers get more work for less pay |
| **Other causes** | Family disorganisation, lack of awareness, kidnapping, weak enforcement |
| **Debt bondage** | Drives child and bonded labour in farms, brick kilns, domestic work; outlawed by **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976** |

## POCSO Act 2012 → sections, punishments and amendments | from: S5
# 1. Full name, enforcement, age definition
- **Full name:** **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012**
- **Drafted by:** **Ministry of Women and Child Development** (**MWCD**)
- **In force:** **14 November 2012**
- **Child = below 18 years:** **gender-neutral** for child and offender; consent below 18 **legally irrelevant** ("statutory rape")
- **Separate law:** IPC replaced by **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023** from **1 July 2024**; POCSO remains a separate special law

# 2. Offences and procedure
- **Offences:** **penetrative** and **non-penetrative** sexual assault, their **aggravated** forms, **sexual harassment**, **child pornography**
  - Aggravated = committed by person in trust/authority (police, security forces, public servant)
| **Section / rule** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Mandatory reporting (Section 19)** | anyone (incl. a child) who knows of offence → report to Special Juvenile Police Unit or local police; failure: up to **6 months** and/or fine |
| **Burden of proof on the accused** | **Presumption against the accused** (**Sec. 29-30**) |
| **False complaints** | punishable (Sec. 22), except good-faith ones |

- **Child-friendly procedure:**
  - Statement at child's residence or place of choice, preferably by a **woman police officer (at least SI)**, **not in uniform**
  - No child kept at police station at night; no contact with accused
- **Special Courts:** **in-camera** trial; investigation in **2 months**; trial within **one year** (**1 year**); compensation + rehabilitation
- **Monitoring:** **NCPCR and SCPCRs**

# 3. Amendments
| **Year** | **Change** |
|---|---|
| **Ordinance of 21 April 2018** | (date asked by APPSC) harsher punishment for rape of young girls |
| **2019 Amendment** | Penetrative sexual assault: minimum **10 years** (extendable to life); aggravated: minimum **20 years**; up to **life or death penalty** (**Death penalty** for aggravated penetrative sexual assault) |
| **2019 Amendment** | Child pornography defined; storing without deleting/reporting = fine at least **Rs 5,000**; repeat **Rs 10,000** |
| **2019 Amendment** | Punishment for giving chemicals to hasten sexual maturity |
| **POCSO Rules 2020** | replaced the 2012 Rules |

- ⚠ **10** = penetrative minimum; **20** = aggravated minimum (2019)
- ⚠ Investigation **2 months**; trial **1 year**; non-reporting up to **6 months**
