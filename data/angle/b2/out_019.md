## CAG — appointment, term, removal, salary and independence | from: S1
# 1. Appointment and oath
- **Art. 148:** Appointed by the **President** by **warrant under his hand and seal**
- **Oath:** Before the President, form in the **Third Schedule**
# 2. Term and removal
- **Term:** **6 years or age 65**, whichever earlier (APPSC 2022 key)
  - Longest fixed term among President, VP, MP (APPSC-G2 2017)
- **Removal:** Same grounds and manner as a **Supreme Court judge** (**like a Supreme Court judge**)
  - Address by both Houses with **special majority** for proved misbehaviour or incapacity
  - Does **not** hold office at the President's pleasure
- **Resignation:** To the **President**
- **Post-retirement bar:** Not eligible for further office under GOI or any State
# 3. Salary and independence
- **Salary:** **Salary equal to an SC judge**; fixed by Parliament (**Second Schedule**)
  - Salary, **leave**, pension and retirement age cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment
- **Leave:** Granted by the **President**
- **Expenses:** Salaries, allowances, pensions of the office and office expenses **charged** on the Consolidated Fund of India (CFI) - not votable
- **Parliament:** **No minister** represents the CAG in Parliament
# 4. Term compared with other constitutional posts
| **Post** | **Term** |
|---|---|
| **CAG** | **6 years or 65** |
| CEC | 6 years or 65 [GK] |
| UPSC member | 6 years or 65 |
| President / Vice-President | 5 years |
| Lok Sabha member | 5 years (unless dissolved) |

> Exam angle: "whose term is longest - President, LS member, CAG, VP?" → **CAG**.

## CAG duties and types of audit — Art. 149, limits | from: S2, S3
# 1. What he audits (Art. 149)
- **Audits:**
  - Receipts and expenditure of the **Consolidated Fund** of India, each State and each UT with a legislature
  - **Contingency Funds** and **Public Accounts**
  - Trading/manufacturing/profit-loss accounts of departments
  - Bodies **substantially financed** by government
  - Govt companies
- **Local bodies:** Audited when requested by the President/Governor; Sixth Schedule councils also covered
- **Limit:** **Cannot audit secret service expenditure** - must accept the competent authority's certificate
# 2. Auditor, not Comptroller
- **Only an Auditor-General, not a Comptroller:** He is only the **Auditor General**
  - **No control over issue of money** / **no control over the release/issue of money** from the Consolidated Fund
  - In Britain the C&AG does control issue
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "CAG controls the entire financial system of the country" (some notes) = the **wrong** statement (APPSC-G2 2017)
> Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2017 wrong statement - "CAG controls the entire financial system of the country".
# 3. Types of audit
- **Types:**
  - Obligatory = **legal** + **regulatory** audit (compliance) + financial attest
  - Discretionary = **propriety audit** (wisdom, economy of expenditure) + **performance audit**
| **Type** | **Nature** | **Tests** |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal and regulatory (compliance)** | Obligatory | Money legally available and spent as rules require |
| **Financial attest** | Obligatory | Accounts true and per reporting framework |
| **Propriety** | Discretionary | Wisdom, faithfulness, economy; flags extravagance |
| **Performance** | Discretionary | **Economy, efficiency, effectiveness** of schemes |

- **CAG+1:** Social audits feeding CAG performance audits; MGNREGA social audit standards **2016**
# 4. Audit of PSUs

| Private auditors appointed on CAG's advice, CAG supplementary audit | **Central Warehousing Corp., IFCI** |
| Wholly private audit | **LIC, RBI** |

## Attorney General — Article 76, appointment, term, removal, duties | from: S0
# 1. Status and appointment
- **Attorney General:** The **Attorney General** is the **highest / first law officer** of India + chief legal adviser to GOI (APPSC 2018, 2019)
- **Art. 76:** Appointed by the **President**; only the AG is created by the Constitution
- **Qualification:** Must be **qualified to be a Supreme Court judge**
  - Indian citizen + HC judge for **5 years**, or HC advocate for **10 years**, or **eminent jurist** in the President's opinion
- **AG and Cabinet:** AG is **not a member of the Union Cabinet**; Law Minister handles legal matters at government level
# 2. Term and removal
- **Term:** **Term not fixed** by the Constitution
- **Removal:** Procedure/grounds **not specified** → holds office **during the pleasure of the President**; resigns to the President
- **Convention:** Resigns when the Council of Ministers resigns or is replaced (appointed on its advice)
- **Remuneration:** **Remuneration determined by the President** (not charged on the Consolidated Fund)
# 3. Duties
- **Duties:**
  - Advise GoI on legal matters referred by the President
  - Appear for GoI in all **Supreme Court** cases concerning it
  - Represent GoI in **Art. 143** references
  - Appear in any High Court when required
- **References:** Made to the AG through the **Ministry of Law**; AG consulted only on legal matters of real importance
> Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 - "qualified to be an SC judge" and "advises GoI" are correct; a "fixed term" statement is wrong (LENS Oct 2025 practice question also tests "fixed five-year term" - false).
> Exam angle: "Who is the highest law officer?" → AG (not CJI, not Law Minister).

## Advocate General — Article 165, duties, Art. 177 | from: S3
# 1. Advocate General - Article 165
- **Art. 165:** Appointed by the **Governor**; must be **qualified to be a High Court judge**
- **Counterpart:** The **Advocate General (Art. 165)** = the **first law officer of a state**, corresponding to the **Attorney General (Art. 76)**
- **Tenure:** Holds office **during the Governor's pleasure**; changes with the state government by convention
- **Remuneration:** **Remuneration determined by the Governor** (**Remuneration is fixed by the Governor.**)
- **Order of precedence:** **No. 25**, below the Additional Solicitor General
- **Assisted by:** Additional Advocates General
# 2. Duties and rights
- **Duties:** Advises the State Government on legal matters + performs duties assigned by the Governor
- **Art. 177:** Right to **speak and take part** in either House of the state legislature and its committees; **no right to vote**
  - Right of audience in any court in the state
- **Art. 194(4):** Enjoys the privileges of a member of the state legislature
- **AP:** Advocate General of AP appointed by the **Governor of AP** under **Art. 165**

## Functions and powers — NCSC, NCST, NCBC | from: S2
# 1. Functions of NCSC / NCST
- **Core functions:**
  - Investigate and monitor **safeguards**
  - Inquire into **specific complaints** of deprivation of rights
  - Advise on **socio-economic planning** (planning of socio-economic development) + evaluate progress; recommend measures
- **Reports to the President:** **Annually** (or when it deems fit)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Not the Prime Minister** (APPSC 2022)
  - President lays them before Parliament; state-related reports go to the **Governor**
- **Mandatory consultation:** Union and every State Government must **consult** the Commission on all major policy matters
  - SCs = **Art. 338(9)**; STs = **Art. 338A(9)**
- **NCBC:** Same pattern - safeguards, complaints, advise on development of **socially and educationally backward classes**, annual report to the President
- **NCST extra work:** **Minor forest produce ownership**, mineral and water rights, rehabilitation of displaced tribals (CDX p.69)
# 2. Powers of commissions - civil court powers, investigation, reports to whom (NCSC, NCST, NCBC)
- **Powers of a civil court** while investigating/inquiring (**Art 338(8)**, **338A**, **338B**):
  - Summon and examine on oath
  - Require documents
  - **Receive evidence on affidavits**
  - **Requisition public records** (**requisition any public record** from any court or office)
  - Issue commissions for examining witnesses
  - APPSC-G1 2022: all of these
- **NCSC basis:** Acts "under the powers conferred by Article 338" (27 Apr 2026)
- **De-reservation:** Proposals must first be examined by NCSC/NCST/NCBC; in **2026** NCSC began a full review (13 Aug 2026)
- **Other bodies:** NHRC and NCM also have civil-court powers in inquiries
  - APPCA (Jun 2026) says NCM has "no automatic civil-court powers"; the NCM Act does give them
