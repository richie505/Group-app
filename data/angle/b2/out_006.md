=== ROW 23 | Doctrines of constitutional interpretation - harmonious construction, colourable legislation, pith and substance, territorial nexus

## Doctrines → meaning and field | from: S0, S1, S3
| **Doctrine** | **Meaning** | **Field** |
|---|---|---|
| **Parens patriae** | State as guardian of those who cannot protect themselves [GK] | Welfare / rights cases |
| **Pith and substance** | Judge a law by its **true nature**; incidental trespass is fine | Union-State lists |
| **Colourable legislation** | **Cannot do indirectly what cannot be done directly** ("fraud on the Constitution") | Legislative competence |
| **Territorial nexus** | State law valid outside the State if there is a **sufficient nexus** | Extra-territorial operation |
| **Harmonious construction** | Read conflicting provisions so **both operate** | FR vs DPSP; provision vs provision |
| **Incidental and ancillary** | Power over an entry covers matters incidental to it | - |
| **Constitutional morality** | Fidelity to constitutional values over popular morality | Phrase: Ambedkar's, after Grote |
| **Transformative constitutionalism** | Constitution as a tool to change an unequal society | - |

- **Reconciling Central & Provincial laws:** **pith and substance** (PYQ G2 2017)
- **True character:** read from the whole Act, its object and its effect
- **Colourable legislation:** about **competence, not motive**
- **Entries:** get the **widest meaning**

## Judicial and executive doctrines → constitutional anchor | from: S1, S2
| **Doctrine** | **Anchor** [GK] |
|---|---|
| **Prospective overruling** | First applied in **Golaknath (1967)** |
| **Stare decisis** | **Art. 141** - SC's law binds all courts |
| **Pleasure** | **Art. 310**, with Art. 311 safeguards for civil servants |
| **Separation of powers** | Functional, not rigid (Ram Jawaya Kapur, 1955); basic structure (Kesavananda) |

# 1. Repugnancy - Article 254
- **Art. 254:** Concurrent List → Parliament's law **prevails** over a repugnant State law
  - **Art. 254(2):** State law with **President's assent** prevails in that State unless Parliament later overrides it
- **Order:** **Union > Concurrent > State**; residuary power with Parliament (**Art. 248**)
# 2. Residuary powers compared with USA, Canada, Australia

| **Federation** | **Residuary powers with** |
|---|---|
| India | Centre (Parliament), Art. 248 |

=== ROW 24 | Structure of the Indian government - separation of powers and checks and balances

## Separation of powers — definitions, thinkers and degree in India | from: S0, S1
- **Constitutional government:** = **limited government** → **limited by the terms of the Constitution** (UPSC 2020, 2021 keys)
- **Constitutionalism:** powers within constitutional limits + rule of law + checks and balances + judicial review + protection of FRs
- **Best safeguard of liberty:** in a liberal democracy = **separation of powers** (UPSC 2021 key)

| **Person** | **Statement / contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Montesquieu** | Division of powers = most effective way to protect liberty; **"The Spirit of Laws" (1748)**; drew on the British constitution |
| Ambedkar | Preferred **responsibility over stability** |

| **Feature** | **Parliamentary (India, UK)** | **Presidential (USA)** |
|---|---|---|
| Principle | **Fusion** - cooperation and coordination | **Separation of powers** |
| Executive | Drawn from and accountable to legislature | President independent of Congress |
| Heads | Nominal (President) and real (Council of Ministers) | Single real executive |
| Checks | Questions, confidence/no-confidence votes | Checks and balances among three organs |

- **Why parliamentary system:** familiarity (1919/1935 Acts) + diversity + avoid American-style executive-legislature conflict
- **Degree in India:** **no rigid separation**; separation **implicit**, not absolute; overlapping functions → checks and balances
  - **Art. 50 (DPSP):** State shall **separate the judiciary from the executive** in the public services of the State
  - **Legislature-executive fusion:** parliamentary system; ministers must be legislators
  - **Judiciary insulated:** Arts. 50, 121, 211, 235 (control of subordinate courts)

## Legislative and executive checks → devices and Articles | from: S2, S3
# 1. Legislature over executive
- **Subordination:** in India the **executive is subordinate to the legislature** (BPSC 2001 key)
  - Council of Ministers stays only while it has **confidence of the Lok Sabha**
- **Collective responsibility:**
  - Union → Lok Sabha (**Art. 75(3)**)
  - States → Assembly (**Art. 164(2)**)

| **Device** | **Purpose** |
|---|---|
| Question Hour, Zero Hour, discussions | Day-to-day watch over administration (IYB 2026) |
| Adjournment, calling-attention, censure motions | Draw attention to lapses (IYB) |
| **No-confidence motion** | Removes the government - Lok Sabha only [GK] |
| Budget, cut motions, PAC and Estimates Committees | Financial control (IYB) |
| **Impeachment (Art. 61)** | Removal of the President |

# 2. Executive over legislature

| **Power** | **Union** | **State** |
|---|---|---|
| Summon/prorogue; max **6-month** gap between sessions | **Art. 85** (President) [GK] | **Art. 174** (Governor) [GK]; 6-month rule (APP) |
| Dissolve lower House | Lok Sabha - Art. 85(2) [GK] | Assembly (APP) |
| Address / messages | **Arts. 86, 87** (VIS 2026) | **Arts. 175, 176** (VIS) |
| Ordinance | **Art. 123** | **Art. 213** (LENS Jun 2026) |
| Assent / veto | Art. 111 [GK] | Art. 200; reserve for President (Art. 201) (UPSC) |

- ⚠ Union - State pairs: **85 - 174**, **86, 87 - 175, 176**, **123 - 213**, **111 - 200**, **75(3) - 164(2)**

## Judicial checks → Articles | from: S4
- **Judicial review:** of laws and executive action (Arts. **13, 32, 226**); laws declared **ultra vires** are void
- **Bar on discussing judges:**
  - **Art. 121:** no discussion in **Parliament** on conduct of an SC/HC judge in discharge of duties, **except on a motion for removal**
  - **Art. 211:** no discussion in a **State Legislature** on conduct of an SC/HC judge
- **Reverse bar (Arts. 122, 212):** courts cannot inquire into validity of proceedings of Parliament/State Legislature on grounds of irregular procedure
- **Art. 142 "complete justice":** fills gaps (Vishaka guidelines before the **POSH Act, 2013**)
  - Risk: overreach; scope undefined
- ⚠ **121 / 211** = legislature cannot discuss judges; **122 / 212** = courts cannot question legislative procedure

=== ROW 25 | President of India - election, qualifications, term, impeachment; executive, legislative, financial, judicial powers

## Election of the President — electoral college, value of vote, disputes | from: S0
# 1. Composition (Art 54)

| **Votes in President's election (Arts. 54, 55)** | **Does NOT vote** |
|---|---|
| **Elected** members of **both Houses** of Parliament (Elected MPs: LS + RS) | **Nominated** members of Parliament (Nominated MPs of both Houses) and Assemblies |
| **Elected** members of **State Legislative Assemblies** | All members of **Legislative Councils** (elected and nominated) |
| Elected members (MLAs) of **Delhi and Puducherry** Assemblies (**70th Amendment, 1992**) | Other UTs without Assemblies |

- **Who votes:** elected members of **both Houses of Parliament** + elected members of the **Legislative Assemblies of the States**, incl. **Delhi and Puducherry**
- **Who does not:** nominated members excluded; a CM who sits in the **Legislative Council cannot vote** (APPSC-GS 2025 key)
- **APPSC-G2 2023 key:** A (elected MPs), C (elected MLAs of States), E (Delhi/Puducherry)
# 2. Voting system & value of vote
- **System (Art 55(3)):** **proportional representation by single transferable vote**, **secret ballot** → borrowed from **Ireland**
- **Principle (Art 55(1)):** uniformity among States + **parity between States and Union** (States as a whole)
  - MLA vote = (State population / elected MLAs) x 1/1000
  - MP vote = total value of all MLAs' votes / total elected MPs
- **Population base:** **1971 Census**, frozen (till the first Census after 2026 - 84th Amdt)
# 3. Disputes (Art. 71)
- **Forum:** decided **only by the Supreme Court**
- **Acts done:** before the election is voided remain valid
- **Bar:** election cannot be challenged on a **vacancy in the electoral college** (Art. 71(4))

## Qualifications, term, oath, resignation and emoluments | from: S1
# 1. Qualifications & conditions
- **Art 58:**
  - Citizen; **35 years**
  - Qualified for election to the **Lok Sabha**
  - **No office of profit** under the Union, a State or any local/other authority
- **Art. 59:** not a member of any legislature; official residence rent-free
# 2. Term, oath, resignation
- **Term 5 years** (Art. 56)
- **Re-election:** **eligible for re-election** (Art. 57) → no limit on terms
- **Oath (Art 60):** by the **Chief Justice of India** (**CJI**) (in absence, senior-most SC judge)
  - Swears to "preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law"
- **Resignation:** in writing to the **Vice-President** → informs the Speaker of the Lok Sabha
# 3. Emoluments
- **Fixed by:** Parliament by law; listed in the **Second Schedule**
- **Protection:** **emoluments fixed by Parliament and not diminished during term**
- !! Not in your sources: the current monthly emolument figure - add from the President's Emoluments and Pension Act as last amended.

## Impeachment and vacancy — Articles 61, 62, 65 | from: S2, S3
# 1. Impeachment (Art. 61)
- **Ground:** **"violation of the Constitution"** only; procedure **Art. 61** (APPSC-GS 2022 key)
  - Word "impeachment" used only for the President → judges and the VP are "removed"
- **Procedure:**
  - Charges in **either House**: resolution signed by **one-fourth** of its members after **14 days'** notice
  - Passed by **two-thirds of the total membership** of the initiating House
  - Other House investigates (President may appear or be represented); must also pass by 2/3 of total membership → President stands impeached
- **Participation:** **Nominated** MPs take part; **elected MLAs do not**
- **Record:** **No President has ever been impeached**
- ⚠ Election: **elected MLAs vote, nominated MPs do not**; impeachment: **nominated MPs take part, elected MLAs do not**
> Exam angle: "Removal of the President is under Article ___" - answer **61** (APPSC 2022).
# 2. Causes & election time limit (Art 62)
- **Causes:** expiry of term, death, resignation, impeachment/removal, disqualification, election declared void
- **Expiry of term:** election completed **before** the term ends (Art. 62(1))
  - Outgoing President **continues until successor enters office** (Art. 56)
- **Death, resignation, removal, disqualification, void election:** election **within 6 months**
- **New President:** gets a **full 5-year term**
- **Contrast:** VP vacancy (Art 68) has **no** 6-month deadline
# 3. Acting President (Art 65)
- **Vice-President acts:** **VP acts** as President; while acting **stops functioning as Rajya Sabha Chairman** (Deputy Chairman presides)
- **Both vacant:** **Chief Justice of India** (**CJI**) → then senior-most SC judge (President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969)

## Powers of the President — executive, financial, judicial, legislative; appointments | from: S4, S5
- **Executive:**
  - All action in his name
  - Appoints PM, ministers, SC/HC judges, Governors, **AG, CAG**, UPSC, Finance Commission
  - Administers **UTs** through administrators; **regulations for UTs** under **Art. 240** → Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, DNH&DD; Puducherry when Assembly suspended
- **Financial:** budget laid; money bills/demands for grants on his recommendation; **Contingency Fund** advances
- **Judicial:** pardons (Art. 72); consults SC under **Art 143** (advice not binding)
- **Diplomatic/Military:** treaties negotiated in his name; **Supreme Commander** of the armed forces
- **Emergency:** Arts. 352, 356, 360
- **Legislative:**
  - President is **part of Parliament** (Art. 79) → no Bill becomes law without assent
  - **Sessions:** summon/prorogue both Houses; **dissolve Lok Sabha**; summon **joint sitting** (presided by Speaker)
  - **Address:** under **Arts. 86, 87** (first session after general election + **first session of each year**); send messages
  - **Other:** nominates **12 to RS**; prior recommendation for Art. 3 and money bills; ordinances (Art. 123)

| **Appointment** | **Basis** |
|---|---|
| **CJI and SC/HC judges** | Arts. 124(2), 217(1): after consultation with CJI; **collegium** (CJI + 4 senior-most, Third Judges Case 1998) (LENSD 3 Aug 2026) |
| CAG (148), Governors (155), Attorney General (76) | On the advice of the PM / Council of Ministers (Art. 74) [GK] |
| **CEC and ECs** | Art. 324; selection committee under the 2023 Act [GK] |
| Finance Commission, UPSC, commissions for SC/ST/BC | President (UPSC) |

- **APPSC-G2 2025 key:** CJI, SC/HC judges, AG and Governors → **all** appointed on the **PM's recommendation** (formal Art. 74 view)

## Position of the President — Article 74 and amendments | from: S6
- **Nature:** President = **nominal/de jure** executive; PM = **real/de facto** executive
  - Council of Ministers headed by PM "to aid and advise the President"
- **Art 74(1):** Council of Ministers aids and advises
  - **42nd Amendment (1976):** advice **binding**
  - **44th Amendment (1978):** may send back **once for reconsideration** → then must act on advice
- **Art 74(2):** advice tendered cannot be inquired into by any court
- ⚠ **42nd** = advice binding; **44th** = one reconsideration, then binding

## Pardoning power — terms, Article 72 vs 161 and cases | from: S7
# 1. Article 72 scope
- **Art 72 scope:** pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions, or suspend, remit or commute sentence of any person convicted of an offence where:
  - (a) sentence by a **court-martial**
  - (b) offence against a **Union law**
  - (c) **death sentence**
- **Limits:** limited to Art 72(1)(a)-(c) and Union executive matters; **amnesty** outside Art 72
# 2. Terms → meaning

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **pardon** | Removes sentence and conviction |
| **commutation** | Substitutes a **lighter form** of punishment (e.g. death to rigorous imprisonment) |
| **remission** | **Reduces the period** of sentence without changing its character (shorter period, same character) |
| **respite** | Lesser sentence for a special fact (pregnancy, disability) |
| **reprieve** | Temporary stay (esp. of death sentence) |

- ⚠ **Commutation** = lighter **form**; **remission** = shorter **period**, same character
# 3. Article 72 vs Article 161 (Governor)

| **Point** | **President (Art. 72)** | **Governor (Art. 161)** |
|---|---|---|
| Offences covered | Against **Union laws** (**Union** law) | Against **State laws** (**state** law) |
| **Death sentence** | Can **pardon** | Cannot pardon; can suspend, remit or commute (UPSC) |
| **Court-martial** | Yes - has power | **No** - no power (UPSC) |
| Forms | Pardon, commutation, remission, respite, reprieve | Same, except as above |

# 4. Nature, judicial review and cases
- **Judicial review:** **limited** → only for mala fide, arbitrary or irrelevant grounds

| **Case** | **Holding** |
|---|---|
| **Maru Ram (1980)** | Exercised on **Council of Ministers' advice** |
| **Kehar Singh (1989)** | President can examine evidence afresh; no right of oral hearing |
| **Epuru Sudhakar v. Govt of AP (2006)** | Limited judicial review |
| **Madras HC** Full Bench (**Apr 2026**) | Governor bound by Cabinet advice on **remission** |

## Veto types → meaning and the President's veto (Article 111) | from: S8
# 1. Veto types - which the President holds (Article 111)

| **Veto** | **Meaning** | **Applies to** | **Indian President?** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Absolute** | Withholds assent | Private members' Bills; Government Bills when the Cabinet resigns before assent | - |
| **Suspensive** | Returns for reconsideration once; if re-passed (with or without amendment) he **must** assent | **Ordinary Bills** only - **not Money Bills** | **Yes** - not for money bills |
| **Pocket** | Keeps the Bill pending - no time limit in the Constitution | Ordinary Bills | - |
| **Qualified** | Overridable only by a higher (e.g. 2/3) majority - US practice | **Not available** to the Indian President | **No** |

- **Money Bill:** assent or withhold only
- **Constitutional Amendment Bill:** **must assent** (24th Amdt)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "suspensive veto on Money Bills" = false (APPSC-G1 2024; key: Only III valid)

=== ROW 26 | Veto powers and the ordinance-making power (Article 123); pardoning power (Article 72)

## Veto by type of bill and pocket veto | from: S0, S1
| **Bill** | **Union legislation** | **State legislation** |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary | Ratify, reject or return | Ratify, reject or return |
| Money | Ratify or reject; **cannot return** (usually assents - introduced with his prior recommendation) | Ratify or reject; cannot return |
| Constitutional Amendment | **Must assent** (24th Amendment, 1971) [GK] - cannot reject or return | Not applicable |

- **Return for reconsideration:** **one-time** power; Financial Bills (I) and (II) can be returned
# 1. Pocket veto
- **Meaning:** neither assents, rejects nor returns
  - Possible because Art 111 prescribes **no time limit** (unlike US 10 days)
- **Only Union instance:** **Zail Singh (1986)** on the **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill**
- **Governor:** does **not** have a pocket veto → SC (2025): indefinite inaction under Art 200 is justiciable
- ⚠ **Money Bill** = cannot return; **Constitutional Amendment Bill** = must assent; **Ordinary Bill** = return once

## Ordinance (Article 123) — conditions, force, origin and life | from: S2, S3
# 1. Conditions & force
- **Conditions:** Parliament (either House) **not in session** + President **satisfied** immediate action needed; on ministerial advice
- **Force:** of an Act; can be **retrospective**, amend or repeal an Act
  - Void if beyond Parliament's competence or violating Fundamental Rights
  - Scope co-extensive with Parliament's power; **cannot amend the Constitution**
- **Origin:** relic of the **GoI Act, 1935**
- **Governor's parallel power:** **Art. 213**
# 2. Life, lapse
- **Lapse:** **6 weeks after reassembly** (**six weeks after Parliament reassembles**, from the later date)
  - Earlier if **both Houses disapprove**
  - President may **withdraw at any time** (**withdrawn**)
- **Maximum life:** **6 months + 6 weeks**

## Amendments and cases on ordinance satisfaction and re-promulgation | from: S3
- **Satisfaction:** **38th Amendment (1975)** (**38th Amdt (1975)**) made satisfaction under Arts 123, 213, 352 **non-justiciable** (made final)
  - **44th (1978)** reversed it
  - IYB 2026 p.699 prints "would be called in question" - read "would not"

| **Case** | **Holding** |
|---|---|
| **D.C. Wadhwa (1987)** | Repeated **re-promulgation** without placing before the legislature is a fraud on the Constitution [GK] |
| **Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar (2017)** | **7-judge** bench: re-promulgation unconstitutional (a fraud on the Constitution); **laying before the legislature is mandatory**; satisfaction is open to judicial review (justiciable) [GK] |

- ⚠ **38th** = satisfaction non-justiciable; **44th** = reversed; **Krishna Kumar Singh (2017)** = justiciable

=== ROW 27 | Vice-President; Prime Minister and the Union Council of Ministers - Articles 74, 75, 77, 78

## Vice-President — office, qualifications and election (Arts 63, 64, 66) | from: S0
# 1. Office & qualifications
- **Art. 63:** there shall be a Vice-President
- **Art. 64:** **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; not a member of RS; holds no other office of profit
  - Only a **casting vote** on a tie
- **Ex officio:** also **President of ICWA** (Sep 2026)
- **Qualifications:** citizen, **35 years**, eligible for election to the **Rajya Sabha**
# 2. Election (Art. 66)
- **Electoral college:** **members of both Houses** (elected **and nominated**) by **PR by single transferable vote** (PR-STV), secret ballot
  - **State legislatures have no role** → **State MLAs are NOT** in it; nominated MPs ARE
  - Size = all MPs, **788** (543 LS + 245 RS) when full
- **Conduct:** by ECI under the Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952
> Exam angle: "Who elects the VP" (both Houses only) and "Where can the removal resolution first be moved" (Rajya Sabha).

## Vice-President vs President — oath, term, removal, acting, disputes (Arts 65, 67-71) | from: S1
# 1. Oath and term
- **Oath:** by the **President** (Art 69)
- **Term:** **Term 5 years** from entering office; continues till successor takes charge (Art 67(c)); re-eligible any number of times
# 2. Resignation, removal, vacancy - compared with the President

| **Point** | **President** | **Vice-President** |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified to be member of | Lok Sabha | **Rajya Sabha** |
| Resignation to | Vice-President | **President** (Art 67(a)), effective immediately |
| Removal | Impeachment, Art 61, either House, 2/3 | **Art 67(b)**: resolution **only in RS**, by **majority of all its then members (effective majority)**; LS agrees by simple majority; **14 days' notice**; no grounds stated |
| Vacancy election | Within 6 months (Art 62) | "As soon as possible", **no deadline** (Art 68) |

# 3. Acting President (Art. 65)
- **When:** on vacancy (death, resignation, removal) or President's absence/illness
- **While acting as President (Art 65):** ceases to be RS Chairman → **Deputy Chairman** presides
  - Draws the **President's salary**, not the Chairman's
  - Until new President elected **within six months** of the vacancy
# 4. Election disputes - Article 71
- **Forum:** decided by the **Supreme Court** under **Art 71**[GK]
- **History:** **39th Amendment (1975)** removed SC's power over disputes on elections of President, VP, PM and Speaker → **44th Amendment (1978)** restored it
- **Other matters:** regulated by Parliament (Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952)
- **Void election:** **acts done before** SC's decision remain valid

## Prime Minister and ministers — appointment, membership, tenure (Art 75) | from: S3, S4
# 1. Appointment and qualifications
- **Appointment (Art 75(1)):** By the President → normally (by convention) leader of the **Lok Sabha majority**
  - Other ministers = appointed on the PM's advice
- **Membership:** MP of **either House** (PM **may belong to either House**), or become one within **6 months** → a non-member can be PM for six months
- **Oath:** Administered by the President
# 2. Non-member ministers (Art. 75(5))
- **Art. 75(5):** any person may be a minister; a non-MP must become a member of either House within **six months** (**six consecutive months**), else ceases to be a minister
  - Applies to the PM too
- **S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001):** non-member **cannot be re-appointed** repeatedly without getting elected (UPSC notes spell it "S.R. Chauhan")
# 3. Tenure and status
- **No fixed tenure:** Holds office during the President's pleasure → in practice while he commands a **Lok Sabha** majority
- **Ministers' tenure (Art. 75(2)):** Hold office **during the pleasure of the President**
  - President removes a minister only **on the PM's advice** (UPSC)
- **Example:** MoS **Ravneet Singh Bittu**'s resignation accepted "under clause (2) of Article 75" (**25 Jul 2026**)
- **Executive status:** PM = **de facto** head of government vs President = **de jure** head of State

## Articles 74, 77, 78 → content | from: S6
| **Article** | **Content** |
|---|---|
| 74(1) | CoM headed by PM to **aid and advise** the President; advice binding after one reconsideration |
| 74(2) | Advice **not inquired into** by any court |
| 75 | Appointment, pleasure, collective responsibility, oath, 6-month rule, salaries |
| 77(1) | All executive action **expressed in the President's name** (taken **in the President's name**) |
| 77(2) | Authentication of orders as per rules |
| 77(3) | President makes **Rules for convenient transaction** and **allocation of business** → **Allocation of Business Rules, 1961** and **Transaction of Business Rules, 1961** (AoB and ToB Rules, 1961) |
| 78 | PM's duty to **communicate** to the President **all decisions** of the CoM on administration and legislation, and legislative proposals; furnish information called for; if the President requires, submit for CoM consideration any matter decided by a minister alone |

- ⚠ **Trap:** President does **not** acquire discretionary powers during a National Emergency → advice stays binding (PYQ)
> Exam angle: "All work of the GoI is done in the name of the President" - Art 77.

## Council of Ministers — size, categories and non-constitutional posts | from: S5, S7
# 1. Union - 91st Amendment
- **91st Amendment Act, 2003:** total ministers **including the PM** ≤ **15%** of **total strength of the Lok Sabha** (**Art. 75(1A)**) (APPSC G2 2025)
  - At 543 seats → cap **81**
- **Defection:** member disqualified for **defection** cannot be minister until re-elected (Art 75(1B))
# 2. Size of the State Council of Ministers - limits, amendment
- **Art. 164(1A):** total incl. CM **not exceed 15%** of the Assembly's strength; **not be less than 12** (APPSC-G2 2017)
- **Example:** West Bengal Assembly of **294** → max **44** ministers
# 3. Categories of ministers
- **Ranks / composition:** The Council "comprises" four ranks - **Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (Independent Charge), Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers**

| **Category** | **Role** |
|---|---|
| Cabinet Ministers | Head important ministries; attend Cabinet meetings |
| Ministers of State (independent charge) | Hold a ministry/department on their own |
| Ministers of State | Attached to Cabinet Ministers |
| Deputy Ministers | Assist Cabinet Ministers/MoS |

- **Classification:** **Constitution does not classify ministers** into ranks → ranking by convention, decided by the PM's choice
# 4. Non-constitutional posts

| **Post** | **Status and facts** |
|---|---|
| **Deputy PM** | **Not mentioned in the Constitution** (not a constitutional post); takes oath as an ordinary minister; first Deputy PM: **Sardar Patel**; rank **5A** in the Table of Precedence (after President 1, VP 2, PM 3) (2026) |
| **Leader of the House (LS)** | Not a constitutional office; = PM if an LS member, else a minister he names (e.g. Pranab Mukherjee under Manmohan Singh); first: Nehru |
| **PMO** | Not mentioned in the Constitution → extra-constitutional staff agency of the PM |
