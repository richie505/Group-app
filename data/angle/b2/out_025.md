=== ROW 85 | Public policy - meaning, types, policy cycle (formulation, implementation, evaluation), policy instruments

## Public policy — meaning, Indian bases and Lowi types | from: S0, S1
# 1. Meaning
- **Working definition:** Systematic course of action adopted by the state to address public issues, achieve development goals and regulate behaviour → bridge between political intent and administrative action
  - **Public policy:** Government actions, laws and rules to address specific problems
# 2. Indian bases of policy
- Preamble + **DPSPs**
- Judicial interpretation (e.g. Right to Food under Art. 21)
- International commitments (SDGs, Paris Agreement)
- NITI Aayog agendas, Budget, Economic Survey
# 3. Types of policy — Lowi classification

| **Lowi type **[GK] | **Meaning** | **Indian example **[GK] |
|---|---|---|
| Distributive | Benefits spread to specific groups; costs borne by all | Farm input subsidies, PM-KISAN |
| Redistributive | Transfers resources from one group to another | Land reforms, progressive income tax |
| Regulatory | Limits individual or firm behaviour | Licensing, environmental and food-safety standards |
| Constitutive | Creates or reorganises institutions and rules | Setting up NITI Aayog, delimitation |

- ⚠ **Distributive** = costs borne by all (PM-KISAN); **Redistributive** = one group to another (land reforms)
- ⚠ **Constitutive** = creates institutions (NITI Aayog, delimitation); **Regulatory** = limits behaviour (licensing)

## Policy cycle — stages from agenda setting to termination | from: S2
- **Cycle:** agenda-setting → formulation → adoption → implementation → evaluation

| **Stage** | **What happens** |
|---|---|
| **Agenda setting** | Problem identified; media, advocacy and expert analysis push it onto the agenda |
| **Formulation** | Research, stakeholder consultation, **cost-benefit analysis** and impact assessment of options |
| **Adoption** | Legislature/executive debates, amends and formally adopts |
| **Implementation** | Planning, resource allocation, coordination; may need new regulations or programmes |
| **Monitoring and evaluation** | Data and metrics test whether goals are met; feedback leads to adjustment |
| **Termination or revision** | Ineffective policies ended or revised; **policy learning** feeds back to agenda setting |

- **Cross-cutting:** Communication + transparency run through every stage to build trust
- **Example:** **Draft National Policy for Women 2016**, based on **Pam Rajput Committee**
- ⚠ **Cost-benefit analysis** = formulation stage; **policy learning** = termination/revision stage

## Policy instruments → Indian illustration | from: S4
| **Instrument** | **Indian illustration** |
|---|---|
| Regulation | Licensing, standards, mandates, e.g. Delhi EV policy: only e-three-wheelers and N1 trucks registered from **1 Jan 2027**, e-two-wheelers from **1 Apr 2028** (TH, 30 Jun 2026) |
| Taxes and subsidies | Tax rates, subsidies, **DBT**-based subsidy reform, **PLI** incentives, public borrowing (CDI Fiscal Policy) |
| Direct provision | Government delivers the service itself (schools, PHCs) |
| Information | Proactive disclosure (RTI Act Sec. 4), public campaigns |
| Nudges | Behavioural design; concept from Thaler and Sunstein [GK] |

- **Hood's "NATO" tools:** Nodality, Authority, Treasure, Organisation
- ⚠ Delhi EV: e-three-wheelers/N1 trucks **1 Jan 2027**; e-two-wheelers **1 Apr 2028**

## Implementation — approaches, causes of failure and remedies | from: S5
- **Top-down:** Policy set at the centre, carried out faithfully
  - Pressman and Wildavsky, "Implementation" (1973): chains of clearances derail programmes
- **Bottom-up:** Front-line officials shape real outcomes
  - **"Street-level bureaucracy"** → **Michael Lipsky** (1980)
  - "Backward mapping" → Richard Elmore
- **Causes of failure:**
  - Weak inter-departmental coordination + resource shortfalls + poor targeting
  - Political change + capacity gaps
  - Bypassing of citizens by parastatals and SPVs
- **Remedies:** Phased roll-out with milestones + real-time monitoring + public outcome tracking
  - Swarna Andhra Vision 2047: multi-phase execution + performance-evaluation framework
  - AP's **Action Taken Reports** against **seven sector white papers** from **22 Jun 2026**

## Social policy → terms, welfare vs development and exam keys | from: S7
| **Term **[GK] | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Social policy | The State's overall framework for social well-being |
| Social justice | Removing social and economic inequalities (Art. 38, 46) |
| Social welfare | Services for weaker and vulnerable sections |
| Social defence | Rehabilitation in deviant or vulnerable situations (drugs, beggary, juveniles) |

- **Social welfare services:** **Targeted** at the vulnerable → disabled persons, destitute children, widows
  - ⚠ **Key:** **General health services for all are not** a welfare service (APPSC-GS 2019)
- **Social development programmes:** Mid-Day Meal, SSA, Rural Health Mission
  - ⚠ **Key:** **Look East Policy is not** one (UPPSC 2015)
- **Welfare vs development:** **Welfare** = short-term, consumption-oriented vs **development** = long-term, production-oriented

=== ROW 86 | Actors in policy-making - executive, legislature, judiciary, bureaucracy, media, civil society, think tanks

## Executive — Cabinet, PMO, Cabinet Secretariat, NITI Aayog | from: S0
- **Cabinet + Cabinet Committees:** Take key decisions
  - Criticisms: delays, bureaucratic sway, overlapping mandates, political considerations, secrecy
- **Cabinet Secretariat:** Headed by Cabinet Secretary; directly under PM; runs Transaction of Business Rules
- **PMO:** PM's policy office

- ⚠ **NITI Aayog** = **2015**, non-constitutional and non-statutory; **Planning Commission** = **1950**

## Legislature — law-making, controls and options against the Court | from: S1, S2
# 1. Law-making
- **Law-making:** Parliament = chief law-making body, but **Cabinet** decides substance + timing of bills; bureaucracy drafts
  - Private members' bills rarely pass
# 2. Controls on the executive
- **Controls on executive:** **Question Hour** (most effective), Zero Hour, half-an-hour discussions, adjournment motions, financial control, ratification of laws
  - Without Rajya Sabha majority governments must concede (e.g. Prevention of Terrorism Bill 2002 rejected there)
- **Committee scrutiny falling:** Bills referred dropped from **71%** (15th Lok Sabha) → **16%** (17th Lok Sabha) (30 Jul 2026)
- ⚠ **71%** = 15th Lok Sabha; **16%** = 17th Lok Sabha
# 3. Options against a Supreme Court decision

| **Option against an SC decision** | **Limit** |
|---|---|
| Amend the law to remove the **basis** of the judgment (validating law) | Cannot simply overrule a verdict [GK] |
| **Constitutional amendment** (Art. 368) | Must respect the **basic structure** (Kesavananda Bharati) |
| Review or curative petition by the Government | Before the same Court [GK] |

## Judiciary — PIL origin, activism and its critics | from: S3
- **PIL origin:** Began **1979** with a case filed on behalf of prisoners, not by them
  - Courts then acted on newspaper reports and postcards
  - Basis: **Art. 32, 226** + **Art. 142** ("complete justice")
- **Criticism:** **Upendra Baxi's** typology of fears → ideological, epistemic, management, legitimation, democratic
  - Police-reform orders ignored → legitimation risk
  - Overreach → policy paralysis + backlog
- **Current:** An MP called SC's orders on NCERT Class 8 chapter on "corruption in judiciary" "**judicial overreach**" (14 Mar 2026)
- ⚠ PIL **1979** = filed on behalf of prisoners, not by them

=== ROW 87 | Governance and good governance - meaning, dimensions, World Bank/UNDP indicators

## Governance — definitions by body and FIRSTs | from: S0
| **Body** | **Definition** |
|---|---|
| **World Bank** | "The manner in which power is exercised in the management of a country's economic and social resources for development"; stresses accountability, transparency, rule of law |
| **UNDP (1997)** | Governance = exercise of economic, political and administrative authority to manage a country's affairs at all levels; good governance is participatory, transparent, accountable and responsive |
| UNESCAP | Stresses efficiency, effectiveness, equity, sustainability |

- **Origin of "good governance":** **World Bank** first brought it into development discourse (APPSC-GS 2018) → **1989** report on Sub-Saharan Africa
- **Government vs governance:** Governance broader → involves market + civil society, not just the state
- **Democracy and development:** Democracy **encourages citizen participation and accountability** (APPSC-GS 2025)
- ⚠ **World Bank 1989** = first "good governance"; **UNDP 1997** = governance definition

## Good governance — UN/UNDP attributes, four pillars and tools | from: S1, S2
# 1. The 8 attributes
- **The 8 attributes:** Consensus oriented; participatory; rule of law; effective and efficient; Accountable; transparent; responsive; equitable and inclusive
  - Most critical three: transparency, accountability, responsiveness (UNDP)
# 2. Four pillars
- **Ethos:** service
- **Ethics:** honesty, integrity
- **Equity:** empathy for weaker sections
- **Efficiency:** speedy delivery using ICT
# 3. Tools
- **Tools for good governance:** People's participation (most foundational), RTI, Sevottam, Citizen Charters, e-governance
> Exam angle: "which is NOT a feature" - look for centralisation, secrecy, discretion or majoritarian rule as the odd one out.

## Citizens' Charter — principles, elements and adoption in India | from: S0
# 1. Purpose and principles
- **Six principles:** Quality, choice, standards, value, accountability, transparency
- **Purpose:** Written commitment on service standards = **time-bound service delivery and grievance redress** (APPSC 2025 key)
- **Basic elements:**
  - Clear **service standards**
  - **Information about services**
  - **Grievance redress mechanism**
  - **Contact details** of responsible officers
- **What it is not:** A legally enforceable right; charters in India are **non-justiciable**
  - 2nd ARC recommended **statutory status**
# 2. Adoption in India
- **Adopted:** **1997**
- **Coordination:** **DARPG** coordinates formulation; nodal officers named in every ministry
- **Website:** **goicharters.nic.in**
- **Citizens Charter Bill 2011:** Time-bound delivery + grievance redress; lapsed with dissolution of Lok Sabha in **2014**
> Exam angle: "main purpose of a Citizen's Charter" - time-bound service delivery and grievance redress.
- ⚠ UK **1991** → India **1997**; Bill **2011** lapsed **2014**

## Social audit, Lokpal and ombudsman — mechanisms compared | from: S5, S6
# 1. Social audit
- **MGNREGA mandate:** **MGNREGA 2005 (Section 17)**; Gram Sabha audits all works
  - Social audit = accountability tool of MGNREGA (APPSC 2018)
- **AP society:** **AP Society for Social Audit, Accountability and Transparency (SSAAT)**, set up **2009** as an independent society
- **2nd ARC:** Institutionalise social audits in all major welfare schemes
# 2. Grievance vs corruption vs maladministration

| **Mechanism** | **Deals with** | **Nature** |
|---|---|---|
| CPGRAMS / state portals | Service-delivery grievances | Executive, time-bound disposal |
| Lokpal / Lokayukta | **Corruption** allegations against public functionaries | **Statutory** (Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013) |
| Classic ombudsman | Maladministration and citizen complaints | Independent officer of the legislature |

- ⚠ **Lokpal** = corruption (statutory, **2013** Act); **CPGRAMS** = service grievances (executive)
