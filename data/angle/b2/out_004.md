=== ROW 14 | Right to Constitutional Remedies - Article 32; the five writs and their scope

## Article 32 — clauses, scope, status and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
# 1. Clauses of Article 32
- **32(1):** right to move the **Supreme Court** (**Supreme Court directly**) by appropriate proceedings to enforce Part III rights - itself a **Fundamental Right** (APPSC GS 2025)
- **32(2):** SC may issue directions, orders or **writs** incl. the five named
- **32(3):** Parliament may empower **any other court** to issue writs - no such law made so far → only SC and HCs issue writs
- **32(4):** right not suspended except as the Constitution provides
- **Scope:** only Fundamental Rights (not statutory or other constitutional rights); jurisdiction **original but not exclusive**
  - **SC writs:** only for FRs, not statutory rights (UPSC)
# 2. Status and significance
- **Basic Structure:** part of the **basic structure** - cannot be abridged even by amendment (UPSC)
- **Rationale:** a right without a remedy is worthless
# 3. FIRSTs and numbers
| **FIRST / NUMBER** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| Number of writs | **Five** writs |
| Origin | **English law** - "prerogative writs" of the King, the "fountain of justice" (UPSC) |
| Before 1950 | only the **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** HCs could issue writs; Art. 226 now empowers all HCs |

## Writs → literal meaning, purpose and scope | from: S1
| **Writ** | **Literal meaning** | **Purpose** | **Lies against** | **Does not lie against** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | "**to have the body of**" | Produce a detained person before court; release if detention illegal | **Public authorities and private individuals** | Lawful detention, contempt of court/legislature, detention outside jurisdiction [GK] |
| **Mandamus** | "**we command**" | Command a public official/body to perform a **public legal duty** it has failed or refused to perform | Public officials, bodies, public corporations, inferior courts, tribunals, govt - **not private persons** | **Private individuals/bodies**; **President, Governors**[GK]; discretionary duty or legislative functions, contractual obligation, non-statutory instructions [GK]; Chief Justice of a HC acting judicially [GK] |
| **Prohibition** | "To **forbid**" | Higher court stops a lower court/tribunal from **exceeding jurisdiction** | Only **judicial and quasi-judicial** bodies | Administrative authorities, legislative bodies, private persons [GK] |
| **Certiorari** | "**to be certified**" / to be informed | Higher court transfers a case from, or **quashes an order** of, a lower court/authority/tribunal | Judicial, quasi-judicial and (now, since **1991**) certain administrative bodies affecting rights | Legislative bodies, private persons [GK] |
| **Quo Warranto** | "**by what authority**" (warrant) | Tests/inquires into legality of a person's claim to a **public office** | Holder of a **substantive public office** created by statute/Constitution | Ministerial or private office [GK] |

- **Match (PYQ G2 2025):**
  - Quo warranto = by what authority
  - Mandamus = we command
  - Habeas corpus = to have the body
  - Certiorari = to be certified
- **Habeas corpus:**
  - Producing a person before court: **habeas corpus** (APPSC 2021)
  - Only writ against private persons
  - Writ safeguarding **personal liberty** - the "**bulwark of individual liberty** against arbitrary detention"; the greatest safeguard of liberty (UPSC)
- **Quo warranto:** may be sought by **any interested person**, not only the aggrieved
- **Art. 361:** President and Governors not answerable to any court for their official acts

## Writ limits — Mandamus; Prohibition vs Certiorari | from: S2, S3
# 1. Mandamus limits
- **Mandamus and law/policy:**
  - **Not issued to the Government to make a law**
  - **Cannot lie in the absence of a policy**; once a policy is laid down its terms can be enforced
  - PYQ A-R G2 2025: both true, R explains A
- **Mandamus not against:** a **private individual/body**; the **President or Governors**; a non-statutory departmental instruction (see the writs table)
# 2. Prohibition vs Certiorari
| **Point** | **Prohibition** | **Certiorari** |
|---|---|---|
| Stage | While proceedings are **pending** | After an order is **passed** (also to transfer a pending case) |
| Nature | **Preventive** only; directs inactivity | **Preventive and curative**; **Curative** when issued **after** the order/decision |
| Effect | Stops the lower body from continuing | Quashes the order already passed |

- **Common features:** both only against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies (traditionally); both check excess of jurisdiction
- ⚠ **Prohibition** = pending, preventive only; **Certiorari** = after order, preventive and curative

## Article 32 vs Article 226 — SC and HC writ jurisdiction | from: S4
| **Point** | **Art. 32 (SC)** | **Art. 226 (HC)** |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Itself a **Fundamental Right** | Constitutional (discretionary) remedy |
| Rights enforced / scope | **Only FRs** (**Fundamental Rights**) | FRs **and "any other purpose"** (legal rights) - **wider** |
| Territory | Whole of India | Own territory; beyond it if cause of action arises within (**15th Amendment, 1963**) |
| Suspension | Can be suspended under **Art. 359** | Not suspendable as such |
| Writs | **Five** | **Five** |

- **Jurisdiction:** **original but not exclusive** - **concurrent** with HCs for FRs; aggrieved person may choose either
- **Guardian of Fundamental Rights = the Judiciary** (Supreme Court as "protector and guarantor") (APPSC 2021)
  - **Shared role:** HCs share the role under Art. 226
- **Res judicata:** HC dismisses a writ **on merits** → same plea cannot go to the SC under Art. 32 (fresh Art. 32 petition on same facts barred), only in appeal; dismissed not on merits → SC can be moved (UPSC)

## Article 37 — non-justiciability and the FR–DPSP balance | from: S1
# 1. Article 37 - non-justiciability
- **Art. 37:** Part IV "**shall not be enforceable by any court**" / "**shall not be enforced by any court**"
  - But principles are "**fundamental in the governance of the country**"
  - **Duty of the State** to apply them in making laws
- **Nature of obligation:** FRs = mostly **negative** obligations on the State vs DPSPs = **positive** obligations
- **A-R (APPSC G2 2025):**
  - A: DPSPs **not independently enforceable** = true
  - R: they act as a **guidepost for legislation** = true
  - ⚠ **Trap:** R does **not** explain A - non-enforceability flows from **Art. 37**'s express bar, not from their guiding role
# 2. Courts and the FR-DPSP balance
- **Relationship:** FRs and DPSPs are **complementary**
- **Basic Structure:** balance between Part III and Part IV (**Minerva Mills, 1980**)
- **Interpretive aids:** courts use **harmonious construction**; many DPSPs read into **Art. 21** (UPSC)

=== ROW 16 | Conflict and harmony between Fundamental Rights and DPSP - Champakam, Golaknath, Kesavananda, Minerva Mills

## FR vs DPSP — differences, legal status and doctrine | from: S0, S1
# 1. Differences
| **Point** | **Fundamental Rights** | **DPSP** |
|---|---|---|
| Part | III (Arts 12-35) | IV (Arts 36-51) |
| Justiciability | **Justiciable** | **Non-justiciable** (Art. 37) |
| Nature | Mostly **negative** - restrain the State | **Positive** - direct the State |
| Aim | **Political democracy**; individual welfare [GK] | **Social and economic democracy**; community welfare |
| Obligation | Laws violating them are **void** (Art. 13) | Courts cannot strike a law for violating them |
| Source | USA | Ireland |

- **Main difference:** DPSPs are **not enforceable** in courts, FRs are (UPSC 2009)
- **Legal status:** FRs are **superior**, but DPSPs are "fundamental in governance" (UPSC)
# 2. Harmonious construction - doctrine
- **Approach:** read Part III and Part IV **together** so both operate
- **Relationship:** FRs and DPSPs **complementary, not competing** - "FRs are means, DPSPs are the ends"; an **integrated scheme** (UPSC)
- **Cases:** Kerala Education Bill (1958), **Minerva Mills (1980)**
- **Justice Chandrachud:** the two together = **conscience of the Constitution** (UPSC)
# 3. DPSPs as interpretive aids
- **Art. 45 → Art. 21A:** Right to Education (86th Amendment, 2002)
- **Art. 48A + Art. 21:** right to a healthy environment
- **Validity test:** DPSP law abridging FRs tested against the **basic structure** + **golden triangle (Arts. 14, 19, 21)**

  - **42nd (1976):** extended 31C to give precedence to **all DPSPs** over FRs (Arts 14, 19); also claimed unlimited amending power under **Art. 368** (UPSC) → **Minerva Mills** struck it down
  - ⚠ **Trap (RPSC 2013):** "the **44th** Amendment gave all DPSPs primacy over 14 and 19" is **false** - the **44th** did **not** give DPSPs precedence; the attempt was the 42nd; struck down (RPSC 2013: statement I false)
- **After Minerva Mills:** 31C restored to **39(b)-(c)** (UPSC); also held an Emergency proclamation is subject to judicial review
- **Property Owners Association (2024):** original Art. 31C, as upheld in Kesavananda, **continues in force**
- **Scope today:** only narrow DPSP primacy - for 39(b)-(c) laws

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - FR-DPSP conflict shaped the **basic structure** doctrine and limits of Parliament's amending power
  - **Minerva Mills** made FR-DPSP balance part of the Basic Structure - the settled position
- **Criticism & debates:** judicial vs parliamentary supremacy; whether 31C cover for 39(b)-(c) laws weakens Art. 14
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - First conflict case = **Champakam Dorairajan (1951)** → led to the **First Amendment**
  - First amendment responding to a court = **1st Amendment (1951)**
  - **I.R. Coelho (2007)** = last word on the Ninth Schedule
- **Comparative note:** FRs from the **USA**, DPSPs from **Ireland**

## Enforceability — cases, laws and Verma Committee | from: S5, S6
# 1. Non-justiciable nature
- **Status:** **non-justiciable** like DPSPs - no direct constitutional/court enforcement, no legal sanction or penalty in the Constitution
- **Enforcement route:** **Parliament may enforce them by law**; courts use them to interpret laws
- **Reasonableness:** courts may look to FDs when judging the **reasonableness** of a law restricting an FR
# 2. Cases
| **Case** | **Year** | **Held** |
|---|---|---|
| **Bijoe Emmanuel** | (1986) | **Limit:** duties under 51A **cannot override FRs** in Part III; respect for national symbols needs only respectful conduct, not compelled participation (24 Aug 2026) |
| **AIIMS Students' Union** | **2002** | **Correlative:** rights and duties correlative - FDs as important as FRs |

- **Counterpart of rights:** freedom of speech (**Art. 19(1)(a)**) ↔ duty to uphold **sovereignty and integrity** (**51A(c)**)
- **Aim:** a **code of conduct** for citizens, civic virtue and patriotism
- **Exercise of rights:** with restraint and respect for others' rights
# 3. Enforcing laws and Verma Committee
- **Justice J.S. Verma Committee (1999):** on operationalising Fundamental Duties; found existing laws already give effect to some

| **Law** | **Duty served** |
|---|---|
| Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, **1971** | 51A(a) - Flag, Anthem, Constitution |
| Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 [GK] | 51A(e) - harmony, no caste/religion offences |
| Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 [GK] | 51A(c) - sovereignty and integrity |
| Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 [GK] | 51A(g) - environment |

- **Imputations prejudicial to national integration:** now **Sec. 197, BNS 2023** (earlier IPC Sec. 153B)
- ⚠ **Swaran Singh** = 1976, suggested duties; **J.S. Verma** = 1999, operationalising duties

## Criticism — vague, not exhaustive, placement | from: S7
