=== ROW 3 | Salient features of the Indian Constitution - lengthiest, blend of rigidity and flexibility, quasi-federal etc.

## Lengthiest Constitution — counts, Parts and FIRSTs | from: S0
# 1. Written, single document — who said
- **Written:** Like the US Constitution; British Constitution unwritten; contained in a **single document**
- **"Elephantine size":** **H.V. Kamath** (PYQ key, APPSC 2018)
# 2. Number game — original vs now
| **Item** | **Original (26 Nov 1949)** | **Now** |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | **395** | about **470** (CDI; older notes say 448) - numbering still ends at 395, new ones are lettered (e.g. 21A, 51A) |
| Parts | **22** | **25** |
| Schedules | **8** | **12** |

- **Original:** Preamble + **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules**
- **Now:** About **470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules** (2026)
  - APP gives about 448 Articles
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Old UPSC notes say 9 original Schedules and 397 Articles - wrong; original had **8**
> Exam angle: "How many Articles in the original Constitution?" → 395.
# 3. Parts added and repealed → amendment
| **Part** | **Change** | **Amendment** |
|---|---|---|
| **IVA** | Added - Fundamental Duties | 42nd, 1976 |
| **XIVA** | Added - Tribunals | 42nd, 1976 |
| **IXA** | Added - Municipalities | 74th, 1992 |
| **IXB** | Added - Co-operative Societies | 97th, 2011 |
| **Part VII repealed** | Repealed | 7th Amendment 1956 |

- **22 Parts → 25:** 22 + 4 - 1 = **25**
# 4. Reasons for bulk (causes → length)
- **Reasons for its length:**
  - India's size, diversity and complexity
  - Heavy borrowing from / influence of the **1935 Act** (**Government of India Act 1935**)
  - **A single Constitution for the Centre and the states** (**one constitution for both Centre and States**)
  - Dominance of **legal luminaries** (lawyers) in the Assembly
  - Administrative detail normally left to ordinary law

## Terms → meaning: constitutionalism, supremacy, democracy, republic | from: S1, S2
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Constitutionalism** | Government **limited by a written, supreme constitution**; power by rules, not the ruler's will (absolute rule) |
| **Supreme law** | Constitution is the **supreme law**; Union and States derive powers from it; inconsistent laws void (judicial review, Art. 13) |
| **Supreme authority** | **Supreme authority is the Constitution**, not Parliament → Parliament = "a creature of the Constitution" |
| **Representative (indirect) democracy** | People elect representatives; a **parliamentary executive** governs; direct-democracy devices (referendum, initiative, recall) not part of the national system |
| **Sovereign democratic republic** | Elected head of state; ultimate authority with the **people** ("We, the people") |
| **Ultimate source of authority** | **"We, the People of India"** (Preamble) |

# 1. Constitutionalism in India
- **In India:** **Parliament is a creature of the Constitution**, not sovereign in the British sense
  - Limits: written constitution, **Fundamental Rights (Art. 13)**, **judicial review**, federal division of powers, **basic structure** limit on amendment (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973)
- **Basic-structure elements:** Supremacy of the Constitution, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, limited amending power

## Parliamentary form — features and comparisons with the USA and Britain | from: S3
# 1. Features and reasons
- **Model:** **British** model, not the American presidential one
- **Features:**
  - Majority-party rule
  - **Collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 74(1), Art. 75)
  - Ministers are members of the legislature
  - Leadership of the PM or CM
  - Dissolution of the lower House
- **Why the framers chose it:**
  - Already familiar under the 1919 and 1935 Acts
  - India's size and diversity
# 2. India vs USA
| **Parliamentary (India)** | **Presidential (USA)** |
|---|---|
| Cooperation and coordination between legislature and executive | **Separation of powers** |

# 3. India vs Britain
- **Contrast with Britain:** British pattern but with basic differences

| **India** | **Britain** |
|---|---|
| Parliament **not sovereign**: bound by a written Constitution, FRs, judicial review, federal division, basic structure | **Sovereignty of Parliament** |

> Exam angle: APPSC GS 2019 keyed "India's head of state is elected; Britain rests on parliamentary sovereignty; Indian Parliament is not sovereign" as correct, and "Britain's head is elected" as wrong.

## Other salient features → provisions, Articles and amendments | from: S4
# 1. Judiciary
- **Synthesis of parliamentary sovereignty (Britain) and judicial supremacy (USA):** Courts can strike down laws
  - Judicial review narrower than USA - Art. 21 uses "procedure established by law"
- **Integrated and independent judiciary:** **Single integrated judiciary** for Union and States enforces both Central and state laws
  - Independence: tenure, salary protection, no discussion of judges' conduct, bar on post-retirement practice, contempt powers
# 2. Rights, duties and franchise
| **Feature** | **Provision / number** |
|---|---|
| **Fundamental Rights** | Six categories; property moved to **Art. 300A**; all suspendable in a National Emergency **except Arts. 20 and 21** |
| **DPSPs** | Socialist, Gandhian, liberal-intellectual; non-justiciable |
| **Fundamental Duties** | 11 (42nd Amendment + one by the 86th) |
| **Universal adult franchise** | Voting age cut from 21 to **18** (61st Amendment, 1988) |

# 3. Bodies, emergency and tiers
- **Independent bodies:** **ECI, CAG, UPSC, SPSC** (SPSCs)
- **Emergency provisions:** Part XVIII, **Arts. 352-360**
  - **National, State (President's Rule), Financial**
  - Federal structure turns unitary without formal amendment
- **Three-tier government:** 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) added **local government** (Panchayats, Municipalities; Part IX, IXA) to the original dual polity

- **IYB 2026 description:** "a **union of states**, a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic** with a parliamentary system of government"

- **NOT features:**

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - Features form the base of the **basic structure** list: supremacy of the Constitution, federalism, secularism, parliamentary system, judicial review, independence of judiciary
  - Explain why India is "quasi-federal" and why Parliament is powerful but not supreme

## Preamble text — order of words, dates, original vs absent words | from: S1
# 1. Source of authority
- **"We, the People of India":** Constitution derives its **authority from the people**
  - Opens the Preamble; enacting clause - the people "adopt, enact and give to ourselves" the Constitution
# 2. Present text
- **Order of words:** **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic**
- **Ends with:** "IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949**, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION."
  - Date in the Preamble = **26 November 1949**: date of adoption, not commencement (26 Jan 1950)
# 3. Original vs absent words
- **Original 1949 text:** "SOVEREIGN DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC" + "unity of the Nation"
  - **At commencement (26 Jan 1950):** India described as a **"Sovereign Democratic Republic"**
  - Words **absent in 1949**: Socialist, Secular, Integrity
- **Not in the Preamble at all (absent from the Preamble):** Federal/federation, Fundamental Duties, "welfare state", "adult franchise"
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Liberty "of **action**" and equality "of **rights**" are NOT in it

## Keywords and objectives → meaning | from: S2, S3
# 1. Keywords
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Sovereign** | Internally and externally free / independent; no external control; own foreign policy; free to frame own policies |
| **Socialist** | **Democratic socialism**: a **mixed economy** - public and private sectors co-exist; "a blend of Marxism and Gandhism, leaning towards **Gandhian socialism**" (**leaning to Gandhian socialism**) |
| **Secular** | Equal respect for all religions; **no state religion**. Before 1976 the word appeared only in **Art. 25(2)(a)**; linked to Arts. 25-28 |
| **Democratic** | Supreme power with the people; government responsible to the people; representative parliamentary democracy covering political, social and economic democracy |
| **Republic** | **Elected head of state**; no hereditary office; public offices open to all |

- **PYQ:** "'democratic' is a mix of British Fabian Socialism and American Populism" marked **not valid** (answer: None) (APPSC G1 2024)
# 2. Objectives → kinds named in the text
| **Objective** | **Kinds named in the text** |
|---|---|
| **JUSTICE** | social, economic and political |
| **LIBERTY** | of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship |
| **EQUALITY** | of status and of opportunity |
| **FRATERNITY** | assuring dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation |

# 3. Objectives → meaning
- **Justice:**
  - Social: equal treatment without social distinction
  - Economic: no discrimination on economic grounds + removal of glaring inequality
  - Political: equal political rights and access to office
- **Liberty:** **Absence of restraints** on individual activity **plus opportunities** to develop one's personality
- **Equality:**
  - Three dimensions: **civic, political, economic**
  - Made concrete by Arts. **15, 15(2), 16, 17**
- **Fraternity:** Sense of brotherhood, promoted by **single citizenship**

## Cases on the Preamble → year and holding; legal effect | from: S4, S6
# 1. Is the Preamble part of the Constitution
| **Case** | **Year** | **Holding** |
|---|---|---|
| **Berubari Union** (President's reference) | **1960** | Preamble is **NOT** part of the Constitution. It is a key to the framers' minds and can guide interpretation where an article is ambiguous |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | **1973** | Overruled Berubari: the Preamble **IS part** of the Constitution; it **can be amended under Art. 368**, but not its **basic structure** |
| **LIC of India** | **1995** | Preamble is an **integral part** of the Constitution [GK] |

> Exam angle: APPSC GS 2025 asked "Preamble is an integral part - which case?" The answer is **Kesavananda Bharati**. Golaknath, Minerva Mills and Gopalan are the distractors.
# 2. Amendability and secularism as basic structure
| **Case** | **Year** | **Holding** |
|---|---|---|
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | **1973** | Preamble **can be amended under Art. 368**, but its **basic features cannot be destroyed** - Parliament **cannot alter its basic features** |
| **Waman Rao** | **1981** | Basic-structure review applies to amendments made after **24 Apr 1973** |
| **S.R. Bommai** | **1994** | Reaffirmed secularism as basic structure → a state government acting against it could be dismissed |
| Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India | **25 Nov 2024** | SC **upheld 'Socialist' and 'Secular'** in the Preamble; rejected challenges to the 42nd Amendment insertions |

- **Pre-1976 position:** **Secularism** was part of the basic structure **even before** the word was inserted - **secularism was part of the basic structure even before 1976**
# 3. Legal effect
- **Enactment sequence:** Preamble **enacted after the rest of the Constitution** had been adopted, so that it matched the text
- **Legal effect:**
  - **Not a source of power** to the legislature + **not a limit** on powers expressly granted (**neither a source of power nor a limit on power**)
  - **Non-justiciable:** no court can enforce it
  - Used to **interpret** ambiguous provisions (**aid to interpretation**)

## 42nd Amendment, 1976 → words added and other changes | from: S5
- **Only amendment of the Preamble:** **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** (42nd Constitutional Amendment Act; amended **only once**)
  - **Amendments so far:** **One** only
- **Words added:** **Socialist, Secular and Integrity**
  - **Socialist**
  - **Secular**
  - **Integrity** - "unity **and integrity** of the Nation"
  - ⚠ **Trap:** UPSC notes say "1976 Second Amendment" - wrong; it is the **42nd**
- **Context:** Enacted during the **Emergency (1975-77)** under **Indira Gandhi**
- **Other changes:**
  - **Fundamental Duties**
  - New DPSPs
  - Curbs on **judicial review**
  - Freeze on delimitation of parliamentary seats (Jul 2025)
- **"Mini-Constitution":** Due to sweeping changes
  - **Minerva Mills (1980)** struck down clauses giving Parliament unlimited amending power and excluding judicial review
> Exam angle: APPSC asked "Secular added by?" in G2 2023 and G2 2025, and "Socialist and Secular added by?" in GS 2025. The answer is always the **42nd**. Distractors are the 44th, 43rd, 45th, 52nd and 86th.

| **6** | Migrants **from Pakistan** to India |
| **7** | Migrants **to Pakistan** after **1 Mar 1947** who returned on a resettlement permit (6 months' residence) |
| 8 | Persons of Indian origin residing abroad, registered by an Indian diplomatic or consular representative |
| **9** | No one is a citizen if he **voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship** - no longer a citizen |
| **10** | **Continuance** of citizenship / rights, subject to laws of Parliament |
| **11** | **Parliament's power** to regulate acquisition, termination and all other matters of citizenship by law |

- **Scope of Part II:** Only citizens **at commencement** (**on 26 Jan 1950**); the rest is left to Parliament (Art. 11)
- ⚠ **Art. 6** = **from** Pakistan; **Art. 7** = **to** Pakistan
# 2. Citizen-only rights and offices
- **Citizen-only rights:** Arts. **15, 16, 19, 29, 30**; vote and contest
- **Citizen-only offices:** President, VP, judges, Governor, AG
  - A naturalised citizen can also become **President**
# 3. Competent authority to regulate citizenship
| **Authority** | **Role** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 11** | **Parliament** alone legislates on and regulates citizenship |
| **Rules** | **Union Home Ministry** frames them (Citizenship Rules 2009, amended 2026) |
| **Election Commission** | Checks citizenship only for electoral rolls, under **Art. 324** |

## Single vs dual citizenship — features and other countries | from: S1
- **Single citizenship:** Allegiance only to the Union; no separate state citizenship
  - Source: **Britain** (UPSC borrowed-features list); **Canada** also has it
  - **USA and Switzerland** have **double citizenship** (national + state)
- **Dual citizenship:** **India does not allow dual citizenship** (Art. 9) - A and R both true, R explains A (PYQ 2023)
- **Domicile:** Only one **domicile** - domicile of India; no state domicile in the constitutional sense
- **Not proof of citizenship:** Aadhaar, voter ID, ration card (MEA, Jun 2026)

## Citizenship Act 1955 → Sections: acquisition and loss | from: S2, S3
# 1. Acquisition - five modes (Sections 3-7)
- **Five modes** (PYQ 2023): **Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory**

| **Acquisition - 5 modes** | **Section** | **Key condition** |
|---|---|---|
| **Birth** | 3 [GK] | Born in India **26 Jan 1950 to before 1 July 1987** (26 Jan 1950 - 30 Jun 1987): citizen whatever the parents' nationality; from **1 July 1987** (1 Jul 1987 - 2 Dec 2004): at least one parent an Indian citizen; from **3 Dec 2004**: both parents citizens, or one parent Indian **and** the other not an illegal migrant |
| **Descent** | 4 [GK] | Born **outside India** (abroad); father or mother (either parent) an Indian citizen at the birth; birth registered at an Indian consulate |
| **Registration** | 5 [GK] | PIOs and spouses of citizens after the prescribed residence, **usually 7 years** (older notes say 6 months); current rule = 7 years; spouses and minor children of citizens, etc. |
| **Naturalisation** | 6 [GK] | Ordinary residence of **12 years** (11 of the preceding 14 years + the last 12 months); some notes: normally **11 years**; conditions waivable for **distinguished service** to science, art, literature, world peace etc.; **oath of allegiance** |
| **Incorporation of territory** | 7 [GK] | Govt of India (Centre) specifies / notifies who become citizens when a territory joins India |

- **Excluded from birth:** Children of **foreign diplomats** and **enemy aliens**
- **Marriage:** Not a separate mode (a registration category)
- **Other Sections:** 7A - registration of **OCI cardholders** [GK]; 14A - National Register of Indian Citizens and national identity card (TH, 7 Jan 2026)
# 2. Loss - three modes (Sections 8-10)
| **Loss - 3 modes** | **Section** | **How** |
|---|---|---|
| **Renunciation** | Sec. 8 [GK] | By declaration; minor children lose it too but may resume within **1** year of majority |
| **Termination** | Sec. 9 [GK] | Automatic on voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship |
| **Deprivation** | Sec. 10 [GK] | By the **Central Government** (Centre), only for citizens by **naturalisation, registration or Art. 5(c)** - fraud, disloyalty, aiding / helping an enemy in war, etc. |

# 1. Provisions
- **Timeline:**

  - Rules notified / effective: **11 Mar 2024**

## Terms → meaning: NRI, PIO, OCI, Commonwealth citizen; OCI rights | from: S6
# 1. Terms
| **Term / category** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **NRI** | An **Indian citizen** ordinarily residing outside India (abroad); holds an Indian passport |
| **PIO** | A **foreign citizen** of Indian origin whose ancestors were Indian nationals; the PIO card was **merged into OCI in 2015** |
| **OCI** | A foreign national of Indian origin **registered as an Overseas Citizen of India** cardholder under the Act (Sec. 7A) |
| Commonwealth citizen | A citizen of a country that is a **member of the Commonwealth of Nations** |

> Exam angle: NRI = citizen abroad; PIO and OCI = foreign citizens.
# 2. OCI cardholder
- **Eligible:** Foreign citizens of Indian origin whose ancestors were Indian nationals + their spouses and children
- **Barred:**
  - Those who are or were (or whose parents/grandparents were) citizens of **Pakistan or Bangladesh**
  - Foreign military personnel
- **Benefit:** **Lifelong visa** - **lifelong, visa-free** entry; OCI card alone is enough to enter India (since **2016**)
- **Not available:** **Voting, contesting elections, constitutional offices, public employment** as a rule (cannot vote or hold certain public posts); OCI holders are **not citizens**
- **Update 2026:** Citizenship (Amendment) Rules 2026
  - **e-OCI cards** + central OCI database
  - Online application and renunciation (ociservices.gov.in)
  - A minor cannot hold another country's passport alongside an Indian one
