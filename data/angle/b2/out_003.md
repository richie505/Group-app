=== ROW 11 | Articles 20, 21, 21A and 22 - protection in conviction, life and liberty, education, preventive detention

## Articles 20 and 22(1)-(3) — clauses → protections and who gets them | from: S0, S2, S5
# 1. Article 20 - three protections
| **Clause** | **Protection** | **Scope** |
|---|---|---|
| **20(1)** | **No ex post facto criminal law** - no conviction except for a law in force at the time; no heavier penalty | Only criminal laws, not retrospective **civil or tax** laws; bars conviction/greater penalty, not trial |
| **20(2)** | **No double jeopardy** - not **prosecuted and punished** twice for the same offence | Only prosecution and punishment by a court/judicial tribunal; not **Departmental** proceedings |
| **20(3)** | **No self-incrimination** - an accused cannot be compelled to be a witness against himself | Only criminal proceedings; covers oral and documentary testimony, not **Physical evidence** like fingerprints/specimens (Kathi Kalu Oghad 1961) |

- **Selvi v State of Karnataka (2010):** **narco-analysis, polygraph and brain-mapping** without consent violate **20(3)**
# 2. Who gets them
| **Article** | **Available to** |
|---|---|
| Art. 20 | **citizens, foreigners and companies** (legal persons); non-suspendable in emergency |
| Art. 21 | **citizens and non-citizens** |

- **Art. 20 does not cover:** **Preventive detention**
# 3. Article 22(1)-(2) - rights of arrested persons
- **Rights (ordinary/punitive arrest, Art. 22(1)-(2)):**
  - Be **informed of grounds** of arrest (told the **grounds**)
  - **Consult and be defended** by a **lawyer** of choice
  - Produced before the **nearest magistrate within 24 hours** (excluding journey time)
- **Art. 22(3) exclusions:** **not available** to an **enemy alien** (**enemy aliens**) or a person held under a **preventive detention** law (**preventive detainees**)
- **Not covered:** arrest under court orders, civil arrest, deportation of aliens

## Article 21 — doctrines: procedure established by law vs due process | from: S1
| **A.K. Gopalan (1950)** | **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** |
|---|---|
| "Procedure established by law" = any procedure **enacted by the legislature** (from the **Japanese** Constitution) | Procedure must be **just, fair and reasonable** - not arbitrary |
| Arts. 19, 21, 22 **separate** | Arts. **14, 19, 21 linked** - the golden triangle |
| Art. 21 protects only against the **executive** | Also protects against **arbitrary legislation** - in effect American **due process** |

| **Term** | **Source / meaning** |
|---|---|
| **"Procedure established by law"** | from the **Japanese Constitution**; more definite than American "due process of law" |
| Due process | USA |

- Comparative note: "Procedure established by law" - Japan vs "due process" - USA
- **A.K. Gopalan (1950):** no due process in India; courts could not examine reasonableness (UPSC)
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978):** overruled Gopalan; procedure must be **"fair, just and reasonable"** → Art. 21 also protects against **legislative** action; in effect introduced American due process (UPSC)
- ⚠ **Gopalan = separate Articles, executive only; Maneka = golden triangle 14-19-21, legislation too**

## Preventive detention — Article 22(4)-(7) safeguards, law-making power, laws → year | from: S6
# 1. Term and safeguards
- **Preventive vs punitive:** on suspicion of future acts, without trial vs after conviction
- **Art. 22(4):** no detention beyond **3 months** unless an **Advisory Board** (HC judges or persons qualified to be) reports sufficient cause
- **Art. 22(5):** detainee told the grounds (grounds communicated) + **earliest opportunity to represent** (earliest chance of **representation**)
- **Art. 22(7):** Parliament may fix the maximum period
- **44th Amendment 1978:** cut the period to **2 months**, but the clause was **never notified** (**not yet notified**) → **3 months** still applies (16 Jun 2026; IYB records the 2-month text)
- **Public order:** SC - preventive detention only for **'public order'**, not routine **'law and order'** (Jun 2026)
# 2. Law-making power

|---|---|
| **Parliament** alone | defence, foreign affairs, security of India |
| **Parliament and states** | public order, security of a state, supplies |

## Article 25 — text, terms → meaning, State powers and Explanations | from: S4
# 1. Article 25(1) - text
- **Art. 25(1):** "all persons are equally entitled to **freedom of conscience** and the right freely to **profess, practise and propagate** religion"
  - Subject to **public order, morality and health** and to the other provisions of Part III (other FR provisions)
> Exam angle: quoted text of 25(1) → identify the Article (asked 2018, 2023).
# 2. Terms → meaning
| **Component** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Conscience** | Inner freedom to mould one's relation with God/creatures as one desires; includes the freedom to follow no religion |
| **Profess** | Declaring beliefs openly - verbally or by visible symbols (turban, crucifix, kirpan) |
| **Practise** | Worship, rituals, ceremonies integral to the religion |
| **Propagate** | Spreading one's beliefs to others - does **NOT** include a right to **convert** another person (it would impinge on the other's freedom of conscience) (UPSC) |

# 3. Article 25(2) - State powers
- **25(2)(a):** **regulate or restrict economic, financial, political or other secular activity** associated with religious practice
- **25(2)(b):** provide for **social welfare and reform** or **throw open Hindu religious institutions of a public character** to all classes and sections of Hindus (UPSC)
# 4. Explanations and limits
- **Explanation I:** wearing and carrying **kirpans** = part of the profession of the **Sikh** religion
- **Explanation II:** in Art. 25(2)(b), "Hindus" includes persons professing the **Sikh, Jaina or Buddhist** religion (**Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists**) (UPSC)
  - Hindu religious institutions construed accordingly
- **Limits:**
  - Public order, morality, health
  - Art. 25 additionally subject to other Part III rights
  - State can intervene to root out **social evils**
  - **No forcible conversion**
- ⚠ **Explanation I = kirpan (Sikh); Explanation II = "Hindus" includes Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists**

## Secularism — meaning, Articles, Indian vs Western model, basic structure | from: S8
# 1. Meaning and Indian model
- **Constitutional meaning:** **equal respect for all religions** + no discrimination on grounds of religion (APPSC 2019); **no state religion** (UPPSC 2005)
- **Indian model:** **Sarva Dharma Sambhava** = state neutrality + **positive intervention** for reform
- **Roots:** Ashoka's Dhamma (RE 7, RE 12), **Nehru Report (1928)**, **Karachi Resolution (1931)**
- **Constituent Assembly:** rejected adding **'God'** to the Preamble (1949)
# 2. Components and provisions
- **Components:** freedom of religion, equality of all religions, state neutrality, no religious tax, minority rights
  - ⚠ **Trap:** NOT a component = "not applicable to irreligious persons" (APPSC-G1 2018); freedom of conscience covers non-believers too
- **Related Articles:** Arts. 14, 15, 16, **25** (freedom of conscience; profess, practise, propagate), **26** (denominations manage their religious affairs), **27** (no tax to promote a particular religion), 29, 30, **325** (no exclusion from electoral rolls on religion)
# 3. Indian vs Western model; theocratic state
| **Indian (positive) secularism** | **Western (negative) secularism** |
|---|---|
| Principled distance and equal respect | Strict separation, mutual exclusion |
| State may reform religious practice (Art. 25(2)), e.g. temple entry | Religion purely private; state keeps away from religious affairs |
| Aid allowed (e.g. Amarnath Yatra, Haj support); may aid minority institutions | No state aid to religious institutions |

- **Theocratic state:** official religion guides law and state policy
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Religious state" is **not** a feature of the Indian Constitution (APPSC 2018)
# 4. Secularism and the basic structure - cases
| **Case** | **Holding** |
|---|---|
| **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** | set out the **basic structure** doctrine; secularism counted a **basic feature** (UPSC) |
| **S.R. Bommai (1994)** | Secularism part of the **basic structure**, inherent **even before 1976**; State government acting against secularism can be dismissed (Art. 356) |
| Sabarimala case, 2026 | **Secularism is part of the Basic Structure** (reaffirmed) |

## Minorities — meaning, types, Articles and notified minorities | from: S0
# 1. Meaning - "minority" undefined

## Right to property — Amendments → year → change | from: S2
| **Stage** | **Position** |
|---|---|
| 1950 | Fundamental Right under **Art. 19(1)(f)** (acquire, hold, dispose of property) and **Art. 31** (no deprivation save by authority of law; acquisition only for public purpose with compensation) |
| **1st Amendment, 1951** | **Arts. 31A, 31B** and **Ninth Schedule** inserted to shield land reform laws |

## Article 300A and LARR Act 2013 — provisions | from: S2
# 1. Article 300A
- **Text:** "No person shall be deprived of his property save by **authority of law**"
- **Available to:** **all persons** (citizens, non-citizens, companies)
- **Protection:** against executive action, not legislation
- **Enforcement:** via Art. 226/ordinary suit, not Art. 32
  - ⚠ **Trap:** PYQ G1 2019 (State cannot dispossess except by law) - key given as "Article 300"; correct provision is **Art. 300A**
# 2. LARR Act, 2013
- **LARR Act, 2013:** replaced Land Acquisition Act, 1894
- **Key provisions:**
  - SIA mandatory
  - Consent = **70%** (PPP) / **80%** (private)
  - Compensation up to **4x** market value (rural), **2x** (urban)
  - Unused land returned after **5 years** (May 2026)

## Fundamental vs constitutional/legal right — classification | from: S3
| **Right** | **Status** | **Where** |
|---|---|---|
| Vote | Constitutional/statutory, **not FR** | **Art. 326** (adult suffrage, 18+); **Sec. 62, RPA 1951**; registration Sec. 19, RPA 1950 |
| Information | FR (part of **Art. 19(1)(a)**) [GK] | RTI Act, 2005 gives statutory machinery [GK] |
| Work | DPSP | **Art. 41**[GK] |
| Education (6-14) | FR | **Art. 21A** (+ Art. 45 DPSP for under-6, Art. 51A(k) duty) [GK] |

- **Rule:** Every FR is a constitutional right, but not every constitutional right is an FR
- **Voting paradox:**
  - Voting is statutory, yet right to know candidate details, NOTA, ballot secrecy recognised under **Art. 19(1)(a)**
  - Opposition sought FR status for the right to vote (7 Jul 2026)
- ⚠ **Property = Art. 300A legal right; Vote = Art. 326 constitutional right; neither is an FR**
