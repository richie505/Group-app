## Features of Part III → justiciable, qualified, negative vs positive | from: S1
# 1. Justiciable and qualified
- **Justiciable:** Aggrieved person can move the **SC directly (Art. 32)**; HCs under Art. 226 enforce FRs + other legal rights
- **Not absolute:** FRs are **qualified, not absolute**
  - Restrictions imposed **by law**, only on grounds the Constitution names
  - **Courts judge reasonableness** (judicial review, Art. 13, 32, 226)
  - Art. 19 lists **11** restriction grounds in all (**2026**)
- ⚠ **Trap:** "FRs are absolute" is **wrong** (PYQ 2019); APPSC GS 2019: "FRs are absolute" and "all FRs are suspended in an emergency" are both **false** (answer: Neither)
# 2. Nature of rights → Articles

| **Feature** | **Articles** |
|---|---|
| **Negative** rights (limits on the State) | Arts. **14, 15(1), 16(2), 18(1), 20, 22(1), 28(1)** |
| **Positive** rights: confer privileges | e.g. Arts. 21A, 29, 30 |
| **Absolute limits on legislative power** | Arts. **15, 17, 18, 20, 24** |
| **Against whom:** mostly against the **State** action; some also bind private persons | Arts. **15(2), 17, 23, 24** |

- **Model:** **US Bill of Rights**
- **Natural and legal:** Both **natural and legal**; protect individuals and minorities (PYQ 2023: both correct)

|---|---|

## Articles 12 and 13 → meaning of State and law | from: S3, S4
# 1. Article 12 - "State"
- **"State":**
  - Government and Parliament of India
  - Government and legislature of each state
  - **All local authorities**
  - **Other authorities** in India or under the control of the Government of India
- **Private bodies:** Writs do not ordinarily lie against them, unless the State acts in collusion
# 2. Article 13 - clauses

| **Clause** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **13(1)** | Pre-constitution laws (in force **before the commencement of the Constitution**) inconsistent with FRs are **void** to that extent |
| **13(2)** | State shall not make a law abridging FRs; post-constitution laws inconsistent are void to that extent |
| **13(3)** | "Law" includes **ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage** |

- ⚠ **Trap:** APP wrongly says "before Independence"
- **Significance:** Base of **judicial review**; makes FRs supreme over ordinary law (UPSC p.364)

## Cases and amendments on Articles 12 and 13 → holdings | from: S3, S4

| **Case / amendment** | **Article** | **Holding / test** |
|---|---|---|
| **Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal (1967)** | 12 | Statutory bodies are "other authorities" |
| **R.D. Shetty v International Airport Authority (1979)** | 12 | Laid down the tests for an **instrumentality or agency of the State** |
| **Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib (1981)** | 12 | A society running a Regional Engineering College is "State"; test is **how it was created and controlled**, not the form; **instrumentality/agency** tests - govt share capital, financial aid, **deep and pervasive control**, monopoly, public function |
| **Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002)** | 12 | Body is 'State' if financially, functionally and administratively **dominated** by govt |
| **Golaknath (1967)** | 13 | FRs have a **transcendental** position - Parliament **cannot** abridge them |
| **24th Amendment (1971)** | 13 | Added **Art. 13(4)**: Art. 13 does not apply to Art. 368 amendments |

- ⚠ **1967** twice: **Rajasthan SEB** (Art. 12) and **Golaknath** (Art. 13)

## Doctrines → meaning and case | from: S5
# 1. Doctrines of FR interpretation (eclipse, severability, waiver)

| **Doctrine** | **Meaning / core idea** | **Case** |
|---|---|---|
| **Severability** | Law void **only to the extent of inconsistency**; only the **inconsistent part** is void (invalid part struck) - valid part / rest survives if separable; "Void only to the extent of inconsistency, rest valid" = **severability** (PYQ 2018; APPSC GS 2018) | **A.K. Gopalan (1950)**; **R.M.D.C. (1957)** - R.M.D.C. v. Union of India (1957); some notes cite Motor General Traders v State of AP (1984) |
| **Eclipse** | Inconsistent **pre-constitutional** law (law violating FRs) is dormant, not dead; revives when the conflict is removed; mainly for pre-constitution laws - **Ambica Mills (1974)** extended it to post-constitution laws against non-citizens | **Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v State of MP (1955)** - revived after the 1st Amendment; **State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills (1974)** - post-Constitution law stays valid for non-citizens (some notes give Ambica Mills as the eclipse case) |
| **Waiver** | A person **cannot waive a Fundamental Right**; only statutory or contractual rights can be given up | Basheshar Nath v CIT (1959) |
| **Prospective overruling** | Ruling operates only for the future | **Golaknath (1967)** |
| **Progressive interpretation** | Constitution read as a living document for present needs | Maneka Gandhi (1978) widened Art. 21; e.g. **trauma care** under Art. 21, SaveLIFE Foundation v. UoI (2026) |
| **Absolute liability** | Hazardous industry liable without exceptions of strict liability | **M.C. Mehta (1987) - Oleum gas leak** |
| Territorial nexus | State law valid outside State if sufficient connection | State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C. (1957) |

- ⚠ **Severability** = only the inconsistent part void; **Eclipse** = dormant, revives; **Waiver** = an FR cannot be given up
# 2. Federal interpretation doctrines
- **Federal doctrines:** Pith and substance (variant: **aspect doctrine**), colourable legislation, territorial nexus, incidental and ancillary powers, repugnancy
> Exam angle: doctrine-to-case matching; pith and substance is the doctrine for Union-State List encroachment.

## Articles 33-35 and 359 → limiting and suspending FRs | from: S6
# 1. Limiting FRs
- **Method:** Only by a **constitutional amendment**, not ordinary law; subject to the **basic structure** (Kesavananda Bharati 1973)
- **Other limits:** Arts. 31A-31C

| **Article** | **Content / provision** |
|---|---|
| **33** | **Parliament only** may restrict FRs of **armed forces, paramilitary, police, intelligence** bodies/staff, their telecom staff (even non-combatants like barbers, cooks) |
| **34** | Restrictions on FRs while **martial law** is in force; Parliament may **indemnify** officials (pass an **indemnity** Act); "martial law" is **not defined** |
| **35** | Only **Parliament alone** (not states) legislates to enforce certain FRs - 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34 - and punishments for Part III offences - for uniformity |
| **359** | President suspends **enforcement** of named FRs by order; **never Art. 20 and 21** (44th Amendment, 1978) |

# 2. Suspension of Article 32 - emergency, Article 359
- **Art. 359:** During a **National Emergency**, the **President** may by order suspend the right to move any court for enforcement of specified FRs
  - Exception: **except Arts 20 and 21**

=== ROW 9 | Right to Equality - Articles 14-18; reasonable classification, reservation jurisprudence

## Articles 14-18 → content and type of equality | from: S0, S1
# 1. Articles 14-18 → content

| **Article** | **Content** |
|---|---|
| **14** | "The State shall not deny to **any person** equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India" - **Equality before law** and **equal protection of the laws** (APPSC 2025: "Right to Equality" → Art. 14) |
| 15 | No discrimination **only** on **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth**; 15(2) covers shops, restaurants, hotels, wells, tanks, bathing ghats - binds **private persons too** |
| 16 | Equality of opportunity in **public employment**; 16(2) adds **descent and residence** as barred grounds; 16(3) Parliament may prescribe **residence** requirement |
| 17 | "Untouchability" abolished; its practice forbidden; enforcing any disability is an **offence** |
| 18 | Titles abolished, except **military and academic** distinctions |

- **Art. 14 vs 15:** Art. 14 applies to all persons; Art. 15 to **citizens only** (PYQ 2018: "Art. 15 is available only to citizens" is correct)
- **Right to equality:** Guaranteed by Art. **14** (PYQ 2025)
# 2. Types of equality → Article

| **Type** | **Articles** |
|---|---|
| **Social** | 15, 17, 18 (PYQ 2017: Arts. 17-18 = social equality) |
| Economic | 39(b), (c), (d) - DPSP [GK] |
| Political | **325, 326** - no exclusion from rolls, adult suffrage [GK] |

- **Equality link:** Art. 17 secures **social equality**; Art. 18 secures social equality by ending artificial distinctions
- **Source:** Equality secured by the **Constitution**, enforced by courts
- **Preamble:** Promises "**equality of status and of opportunity**"
- **Ambedkar:** Liberty, equality, fraternity = an **inseparable trinity**

## Article 14 terms → meaning (rule of law, equal protection, classification) | from: S2
# 1. Equality before law vs equal protection

| **Equality before law** | **Equal protection of laws** |
|---|---|
| **British** origin / concept (**Dicey's rule of law**) | **American** origin / concept (US 14th Amendment [GK]) |
| **Negative** concept - no special privilege; no one above law; all equally subject to ordinary law | **Positive** concept - like treated alike, equal treatment in equal circumstances; permits / allows reasonable classification |

- ⚠ **British = negative**; **American = positive**
# 2. Rule of law
- **Rule of law (A.V. Dicey):**
  - (i) No arbitrary power
  - (ii) Equality before law
  - (iii) Constitution as the result of rights declared by courts
  - **India accepts only (i) and (ii)** - here rights flow from the Constitution
- **Status:** Rule of law is a **basic feature**
- **Exceptions:**
  - **Art. 361:** President and Governors not answerable to courts; no criminal case during term; civil case only after **2** months' notice
  - Foreign diplomats; UN agencies
# 3. Classification
- **Classification:** Art. 14 forbids **class legislation**, not **reasonable classification**
  - **Twin test:** **Intelligible differentia** + **rational nexus** with the object
  - Reiterated **Apr 2026**: State **cannot treat pensioners differently from serving employees on DA** (Kerala/KSRTC)

## Cases on equality and reservation → ruling | from: S2, S4
# 1. Article 14 - arbitrariness
- **E.P. Royappa v. State of TN (1974):** "**Equality and arbitrariness are sworn enemies**" / "**equality is antithetic to arbitrariness**"
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978):** Any arbitrary action violates Art. 14; unguided discretionary power struck down
# 2. Reservation cases → issue decided

| **Case** | **Ruling / issue decided** |
|---|---|
| **Champakam Dorairajan** (1951) | Communal G.O. struck down under Art. 29(2); DPSPs subordinate to FRs → **1st Amendment** (Art. 15(4)) |
| **M.R. Balaji v State of Mysore (1962)** (**M.R. Balaji v Mysore (1962)**; also dated 1963; some notes date it 1960) | Caste cannot be the sole test; reservation must be reasonable, generally **below 50%** |
| **Indra Sawhney / Mandal (1992)** (**Indra Sawhney (16 Nov 1992)**) | 9-judge bench, **6:3**: **27% OBC quota upheld** with **creamy layer** excluded; **50% ceiling** / **50% cap** save exceptional / extraordinary cases; **no reservation in promotions**; ordered a permanent body - NCBC |
| **M. Nagaraj (2006)** | 5 judges upheld 77th, 81st, 85th Amendments with conditions: state must show **quantifiable data** - **backwardness, inadequate representation (quantifiable data), efficiency** (Art. 335) |
| **Ashoka Kumar Thakur (2008)** | Upheld the 93rd Amendment and the 2006 Act for State/aided institutions; **27% OBC quota in central institutions**; creamy layer excluded |

| **Maratha case (Jaishri Patil, 2021)** / **Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil (2021)** | **Maratha** quota struck down for crossing **50%** without exceptional circumstances; read the 102nd Amendment as making the Central list the only SEBC list - taking away States' power to identify SEBCs |

- ⚠ **Nagaraj** needed SC/ST backwardness data; **Jarnail Singh** said no such data needed

=== ROW 10 | Right to Freedom - Article 19 (six freedoms) and reasonable restrictions

## Article 19(1) → six freedoms (number game) and who gets them | from: S0, S1
# 1. Clauses → freedom

| **Clause** | **Freedom** |
|---|---|
| 19(1)(a) | **Speech and expression** |
| 19(1)(b) | Assemble **peaceably and without arms** |
| **19(1)(c)** | Form **associations or unions or co-operative societies** - "co-operative societies" added by the **97th Amendment, 2011** (also Art. 43B, Part IXB) |
| 19(1)(d) | **Move** freely throughout India |
| 19(1)(e) | **Reside and settle** in any part of India |
| 19(1)(f) | Acquire, hold and dispose of property - **deleted by the 44th Amendment, 1978** |
| 19(1)(g) | Any **profession, occupation, trade or business** |

- **Number:** **Seven** freedoms originally → **six** now
  - "Which Article gives six freedoms?" → **Art. 19** (PYQ 2018; APPSC 2018)
  - Right to Freedom group = **Arts. 19-22**
- **Read in (not written):**
  - **Freedom of the press** (PYQ 2019: part of Art. 19)
  - Right to protest
  - **No fundamental right to strike** (see assembly)
- **Travel abroad:** Falls under **Art. 21**, not 19(1)(d)
- **PYQ 2018 matching:** (a) speech, (b) assembly, (c) associations, (d) movement
- **In force:** Freedom of expression from **26 Jan 1950** (APPSC 2018)
- ⚠ **(c)** co-operative societies added by the **97th**; **(f)** property deleted by the **44th**
# 2. Availability
- **Who:** Only **citizens**; **not foreigners or companies** / legal persons (shareholders who are citizens may sue)
- **Against whom:** **State action only**; private violation → ordinary legal remedy
- **Detained persons:** A person **imprisoned or detained** keeps the freedom of **expression** (e.g. writing and publishing books) though movement is lost - **retains freedom of expression** despite curtailed movement (PYQ G1 2018; APPSC G1 2018 key)

## Reasonable restrictions → clause, grounds and amendments | from: S2, S6
# 1. Clause → grounds

| **Clause** | **Freedom** | **Grounds** |
|---|---|---|
| 19(2) | Speech | **Sovereignty and integrity**, security of State, **friendly relations with foreign States**, **public order**, decency or morality, **contempt of court**, **defamation**, **incitement to an offence** |
| 19(3) | Assembly | Sovereignty and integrity; public order |
| 19(4) | Association | Sovereignty and integrity; public order; morality |
| 19(5) | Movement, residence | General public interest; **protection of Scheduled Tribes** |
| 19(6) | Profession, trade | General public interest; **professional or technical qualifications**; **State trade or monopoly** |

- **Count:** 11 reasonable-restriction heads across Art. 19 (2026)
- **Not a ground under 19(2):** **Sedition** (**Sedition is not a ground** in 19(2) - APP PYQ), "general public interest", economic grounds
# 2. Amendments → grounds added

| **Amendment** | **Ground added** |
|---|---|
| **1st (1951)** / **1st Amendment 1951** | **Public order, friendly relations with foreign States, incitement to an offence** + State-trade clause in 19(6) - 19(6) shielded nationalisation (LENSD, 19 Jun 2026); Act's **75**th year in Jun **2026** |
| **16th (1963)** / **16th Amendment 1963** | **Sovereignty and integrity of India** - in 19(2), (3), (4) (IYB 2026) |

# 3. Test of reasonableness
- **Test:** (test of reasonableness)
  - Court decides - nature of the right, purpose, extent and urgency of the evil, proportionality (V.G. Row, **1952**)
  - Restrictions must not eclipse the right itself
# 4. Movement and residence - 19(5)
- **19(1)(d):** Move anywhere in India (internal movement)
- **19(1)(e):** Reside and settle anywhere
- **Restrictions (19(5)):**
  - **General public interest**
  - To **protect Scheduled Tribes'** culture, land and property - e.g. limiting outsiders' entry and settlement in tribal/Inner Line areas
# 5. Profession, trade - 19(6) State monopoly and qualification limits
- **Art. 19(6):** State may
  - Prescribe **professional or technical qualifications**
  - Carry on any trade **to the exclusion of citizens** (nationalisation / State monopoly)
  - Inserted by the **1st Amendment**
- **Limit:** No right to carry on **dangerous or immoral** trades (e.g., trafficking)

## Implied rights under 19(1)(a) → case | from: S3

| **Implied right** | **Case** |
|---|---|
| **Freedom of press** and circulation | **Romesh Thappar (1950)**; Sakal Papers (1962) [GK]; Indian Express Newspapers (1985) [GK] - freedom of press is part of **Art. 19** (APPSC 2019) |
| **Hoisting the national flag** | **Naveen Jindal (2004)**[GK] - flying the national flag = expression under 19(1)(a) |
| **Right to silence** (not singing the anthem) | Bijoe Emmanuel (1986) [GK] |
| **Right to information / right to know** | **State of UP v. Raj Narain (1975)**[GK]; voters' right to know candidates, **NOTA** and ballot secrecy - under 19(1)(a) (LENS Jul 2026) |
| **Commercial speech** (advertisements) | Tata Press (1995) [GK] |
| Speech over the **internet** | Anuradha Bhasin (2020) [GK] |
| Telecast; no govt monopoly on airwaves | Cricket Association of Bengal (1995) [GK] |
| Against phone tapping | **PUCL, 1997** - safeguards |

- **Medium:** Art. 19 protects speech "**irrespective of the medium**" / **whatever the medium** (PYQ 2019; APPSC 2019)
- **Films:** Can be pre-censored unlike the press - reach larger audiences
- **Other implied rights:**
  - Against pre-censorship
  - To demonstrate or picket (but **not** to strike)

## Cases on speech and press → ruling | from: S4

| **Case** | **Ruling** |
|---|---|
| **Romesh Thappar v State of Madras (1950)** | Ban on the journal "Cross Roads" struck down, because "public order" was not then a ground → **1st Amendment 1951**[GK] |
| **Kedar Nath Singh (1962)** | Upheld **sedition (s.124A IPC)** only for **incitement to violence/public disorder**[GK] |
| **Bennett Coleman v UoI (1972)** | Newsprint policy fixing pages violated **press freedom**[GK] |

## Assembly, association and strikes → scope and status | from: S5
# 1. Assembly and protest
- **Scope:** **Peaceful and unarmed**; covers **meetings, demonstrations, processions** on public land
- **Excludes:** **Violent, disorderly** or **armed** assembly
- **Right to protest:** Not written but read from 19(1)(a), 19(1)(b) and 21; restricted under 19(2)-(3)
  - **Peaceful protest** is everybody's right, but must not occupy public space or disturb citizens (SC, **22 May 2026**)
# 2. Strikes, bandhs, gherao → status

| **Action** | **Status** | **Case** |
|---|---|---|
| Strike | **Right to strike is not a Fundamental Right** - only a legal right | All India Bank Employees' Assn (**1962**) |
| Demonstration vs strike | Demonstration protected, strike not | **Kameshwar Prasad (1962)** |
| Strike by government employees | Government employees have no right to strike | **T.K. Rangarajan, 2003** |
| Bandh | **Bandhs are illegal:** curb others' freedoms | **CPI(M) v. Bharat Kumar**, **1997-98** |
| **Gherao** | Not protected | - |

# 3. Association
- **FCRA:** Centre defends curbs under **Art. 19(4)**
  - **INSAF (2020):** Barred only associations in active politics from foreign funds
