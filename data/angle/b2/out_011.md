## Appointment, qualifications and tenure — Articles 124 to 128 | from: S2, S5, S6
# 1. Appointment (Art. 124(2))
- **Appointing authority:** The **President appoints** judges after consulting the CJI; for other judges consulting the CJI is compulsory
- **CJI:** **Appointed by the President** (**CJI is appointed by the President**, APPSC-GS 2025 key)
  - Convention upheld by the Court: **senior-most judge** becomes CJI
  - **Senior-most as CJI:** Convention broken in **1973 (A.N. Ray)** and **1977 (M.H. Beg)**
  - Second Judges Case said only the senior-most should be CJI
# 2. Qualifications (Art. 124(3))
- **Eligibility:** A **citizen** of India, and either:
  - **5 years** as an HC judge
  - **10 years** as an HC advocate
  - A **distinguished jurist** in the President's opinion → never used in **76 years** (Justice Bhuyan, 31 Aug 2026)
# 3. Acting, ad hoc and retired judges
| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **126** | **Acting CJI**, appointed by the President when the office is vacant or the CJI is unable (APPCA) |
| **127** | **Ad hoc judges** (no quorum, when a quorum is lacking): the CJI appoints a qualified **High Court judge** (an **HC judge** qualified for the SC), with the President's **previous consent** and after consulting the HC Chief Justice (APP, APPCA) |
| **128** | **Retired judges**: the CJI may request a retired SC judge (or a qualified retired HC judge; a **retired SC, Federal Court or HC judge**) to sit, with the **President's previous consent** and the **judge's consent** (APP; APPSC-G2 2025 key "Both i and ii") |
- **Art. 128 status:** Full powers of an SC judge but **not deemed a judge** of the court (**not deemed regular judges**)
- **Additional judges:** **No additional judges in the SC**; additional and acting judges only in **High Courts** (Art. 224)
- ⚠ **Trap:** **126** = acting CJI (President); **127** = ad hoc (CJI, HC judge); **128** = retired judges (both consents)
# 4. Retirement and resignation
- **Retirement:** At **65** (**65 years**)
  - Age determined by the authority and manner **Parliament** provides by law (**Art. 124(2A)**, 15th Amendment 1963)
- **Resignation:** Addressed **to the President**

## Jurisdictions → Articles — original, writ, appellate, advisory | from: S8, S9
# 1. Jurisdiction map
| **Jurisdiction** | **Article** |
|---|---|
| **Original** (federal disputes) | **131** |
| **Writ** (enforcing Fundamental Rights) | **32** (IYB) |
| **Appellate** - constitutional / civil / criminal | **132 / 133 / 134**[GK] |
| Special Leave to Appeal | **136**[GK] |
| **Advisory** | **143** (APPCA) |
| **Review** of its own judgments | **137** (APPCA) |
| Election of President / Vice-President | **71**: original, exclusive and final (APP) |
# 2. Original jurisdiction (Art. 131)
- **Nature:** **Exclusive** jurisdiction over federal disputes:
  - **Centre vs one or more states** (**Centre and one or more states**)
  - **Centre and some states vs other states**
  - **State vs state** (**Between two or more states**)
- **Excluded:**
  - Disputes under pre-Constitution treaties or agreements
  - **Inter-state river waters (Art. 262)**
  - **Finance Commission** matters (Art. 280)
  - Adjustment of expenses between Centre and states (**Art. 290**)
  - **Election disputes** of Parliament and state legislatures → go to High Courts (APPSC-G1 2022 key: "II and III" excluded)
- **Art. 32 writ jurisdiction:** Also original but **not exclusive** (HCs have Art. 226)
- **President and Vice-President elections:** Disputes = original, exclusive and final
# 3. Appellate, advisory and other powers (Arts 129 to 145)
| **Article** | **Jurisdiction / power** |
|---|---|
| 132 / 133 / 134 | Appeals: **constitutional** / **civil** / **criminal** cases from HCs |
| **136** | **Special Leave Petition**: discretionary appeal from any court or tribunal except military ones |
| 137 | Review of its own judgments |
| **141** | Law declared by it binding on all courts |
| **142** | Orders for **complete justice** |
| **143** | **Advisory**: President's references; the advice is not binding |
| **129** | **Court of record**, with power to punish **contempt** of itself |
| **145** | Rules; at least **5 judges** for constitutional questions |
- **Art. 142 examples:**
  - Declared **10 TN bills assented** (Apr 2025)
  - Declared a **Right to Safe Travel** under Art. 21 + nationwide safety directives (27 May 2026)
  - HCs have no equivalent power (**Anil Kumar Jain v. Maya Jain**)
- **Art. 143:** The **16th Presidential Reference** (Nov 2025) answered questions on bill timelines (see B-37)
- ⚠ **Trap:** **131** = original (exclusive); **32** = writ (not exclusive, HCs Art. 226); **136** = SLP; **143** = advisory (not binding)

## Supreme Court of India vs US Supreme Court | from: S12
| **Point** | **India** | **USA** |
|---|---|---|
| Advisory jurisdiction | **Yes** (Art. 143) | No [GK] |
| Appellate scope | Very wide: constitutional, civil, criminal, special leave (Art. 136) | Mainly constitutional/federal questions [GK] |
| Review standard | **"Procedure established by law"** - narrower review | **"Due process of law"** - substantive and procedural review; called a "third chamber" (APP) |
| Writ jurisdiction for FRs | Art. 32, itself a Fundamental Right | No equivalent [GK] |
- **Synthesis:** India blends American judicial supremacy + British parliamentary sovereignty
