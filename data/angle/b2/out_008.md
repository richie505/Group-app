=== ROW 31 | Presiding officers - Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Chairman; Secretariat; sessions, adjournment, prorogation, dissolution

## Presiding officers → election, resignation and removal | from: S0, S2, S3
| **Office** | **Chosen how** | **Resigns to** | **Removal** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speaker (LS) | LS elects Speaker and Deputy Speaker from its members (**Art 93**) | **Resigns to the Deputy Speaker** (**Art 94**) | Resolution of a **majority of all the then members** (effective majority) after **14 days'** notice (Art 94) |
| Deputy Speaker (LS) | **Election:** By the LS after the Speaker | **Resigns to the Speaker** | - |
| Chairman (RS) | **Vice-President** is **ex officio Chairman** (Art 64, 89) | - | Only when removed as VP |
| Deputy Chairman (RS) | Art 89(2), 90; elected from RS members | To the **Chairman** | Effective majority with **14 days'** notice |
- **Speaker vacates office** on ceasing to be a member (Art 94)
- **While a removal resolution is pending:**
  - Speaker (**Art 96**) → **cannot preside**, but can speak, take part and **vote in the first instance** (not on a tie)
  - Chairman → cannot preside + **cannot vote**, but may speak
- **Salary (Speaker):** Charged on the Consolidated Fund (**Art 97**)
- **On dissolution:**
  - **Continuity:** Speaker continues till immediately before the **first meeting of the new LS**
  - **Speaker Pro Tem:** Senior-most member, sworn in by the President → presides and conducts the new Speaker's election
- ⚠ Speaker → resigns to Deputy Speaker; Deputy Speaker → to Speaker; Deputy Chairman → to Chairman
- ⚠ Removal pending: Speaker **can vote** (first instance); Chairman **cannot vote**

## Speaker → powers and Articles | from: S0, S1
| **Power** | **Article / basis** |
|---|---|
| **Casting vote:** Only in case of a tie | **Art 100(1)** |
| **Money Bill certification:** Final say on whether a bill is a **Money Bill** | Art 110(3) |
| **Joint sitting:** Presides over a **joint sitting** | Art 108; Art 118(4) |
| **Anti-defection:** Decides cases under **Tenth Schedule** | Subject to judicial review (**Kihoto Hollohan, 1992**) |
| **Leader of the Opposition:** Recognises the **Leader of the Opposition** | Party needs at least **one-tenth** of the House's strength (Speaker's direction); LoP statutory since the **1977 Act** |
| **Other powers:** Rulings protected | **Art 122** |
| Appoints committee chairmen | - |

## Deputy Speaker, Chairman and panels → roles and status | from: S2, S3
# 1. Deputy Speaker
- **Presiding role:** When the Speaker is absent or office vacant; presides over a joint sitting if Speaker absent
- **Status:** Not subordinate to the Speaker; becomes **chairman of any committee** he is appointed to
- **Convention:** From the **Opposition**
# 2. Chairman of Rajya Sabha
- **Vote:** Casting vote only
- **Acting President:** Draws the President's salary; **Deputy Chairman** presides (Art 91)
# 3. Panels
- **Panel of Chairpersons:** Nominated by the Speaker (up to **10**)
  - Member presides in absence of both, but not when Speaker's office is vacant
- **RS equivalent:** **Panel of Vice-Chairpersons** nominated by the Chairman

## Secretariat and sessions → Articles | from: S4, S5
| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **85** | President summons each House; gap between two sessions **not more than 6 months** |
| **86** | President may address either House or both together at any time, require attendance, and send **messages** |
| **87** | **Special address** at the start of the **first session after each general election** and the **first session of each year** |
| **98** | Each House has a **separate secretarial staff** |
| **100(3)** | Quorum |
| 174 | States: same six-month rule |
# 1. Sessions and the six-month rule
- **Art 85:** Gap counted from the **last sitting of one session to the first sitting of the next** (APPSC G2 2017)
  - So at least **two sessions a year**
- **Three sessions (by convention):**
  - **Budget** (Feb-May, longest)
  - **Monsoon** (Jul-Sep)
  - **Winter** (Nov-Dec)
- **Quorum:** **One-tenth** of total members incl. the presiding officer → **Art 100(3)**
  - Some notes list it as Art 100(1); **100(1)** actually carries the casting vote
- **States (Art 174):** State Assemblies averaged only **24 sittings** in **2025** (Odisha **43**, Nagaland **7**) (PRS, 4 Jun 2026)
# 2. President's address
- **Purpose:** Outlines government policy; debated on a **Motion of Thanks**
# 3. Secretariat - Art 98
- Posts **common to both Houses** may be created
- Recruitment: Parliament may regulate by law; until then rules by the President after consulting Speaker/Chairman
- **Head:** **Secretary-General** of each House
  - Appointed by the presiding officer; rank = Cabinet Secretary
  - Independent of the executive
- ⚠ Quorum = **100(3)**; casting vote = **100(1)**

=== ROW 32 | Parliamentary devices - Question Hour, Zero Hour, motions (adjournment, no-confidence, censure, calling attention)

## Devices of parliamentary control — list and FIRSTs | from: S0, S2, S3, S4
- **Tools:**
  - Question Hour, Zero Hour, half-an-hour discussion, short-duration discussion
  - Calling attention motion, adjournment motion, censure motion, no-confidence motion
  - Committee system: Government Assurances, Petitions, Subordinate Legislation, PAC, Estimates
  - Budget approval, Motion of Thanks
- **States:** Same devices + estimates/public accounts committees
| **FIRST / Indian innovation** | **Year** |
|---|---|
| Short-duration discussion | **1953** |
| Calling attention motion | **1954** |
| Zero Hour (**Indian innovation**, not borrowed from any other country) | **1962** |
- ⚠ **1953 – 1954 – 1962** = short-duration – calling attention – Zero Hour

## Question Hour and Zero Hour → timing, types, notice | from: S1, S2
# 1. Question Hour
- **Timing:** **First hour** of every sitting in Lok Sabha [GK]; ministers answer searching questions; maximum attendance of members (APP)
- **Significance:** Most effective vigil on the executive
| **Type** | **Answer** | **Supplementaries** | **Paper colour** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starred (asterisk) | **Oral** | Allowed | Green |
| Unstarred | **Written** | Not allowed | White |
| Short-notice | Oral | Allowed [GK] | Light pink |
| To a private member (on his bill/resolution) | Oral/written | - | Yellow |
- **Notice:**
  - Short-notice question = **less than 10 days**; on a matter of urgent public importance; oral answer [GK]
  - Ordinary questions = at least **15 clear days'**
- ⚠ Starred = oral + green; unstarred = written + white
# 2. Zero Hour
- **Rules status:** **Not mentioned in the Rules of Procedure**; informal
- **Origin:** In use since **1962**
- **Timing (LS):** Starts **immediately after Question Hour**; lasts until regular business is taken up; no fixed duration in rules
- **Timing (RS):** Urgent matters raised in the **first hour** (Zero Hour precedes Question Hour)
- **Notice:** **Without the prior notice** a question needs; members raise any important matter, only intimating the Presiding Officer the same day, who decides whom to allow [GK]
- **Reply:** Ministers **not bound to reply**
- **Follow-up:** Chair's directions / assurances → Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs sends extract to ministry concerned for action
> Exam angle: "time after Question Hour when members raise matters without prior notice" = Zero Hour (APPSC-GS 2025; G2 2023, 2025).

## Rules of Procedure → devices | from: S3, S4, S5
| **Rule** | **Device** |
|---|---|
| **Rule 377 (LS) / Special Mention Rule 180A-E (RS)** | Matters not raisable under any other device; members raise matters that are not points of order ("special mention"); Rajya Sabha equivalent is Special Mentions [GK] |
| **Rule 193 (LS)** | Short-duration discussion, no voting |
| **Rule 184 (LS)** | Discussion followed by vote |
| **Rule 198** (LS) | No-confidence motion |
| **Rule 222 (LS)**, **Rule 187 (RS)** | Privilege motion (LENSD, May 2026) |
- **Short-duration discussion:** Max **2 hours**; Indian innovation (1953)
- **Half-an-hour discussion:** Matter sufficiently debated needing factual elucidation; no formal motion or vote
- ⚠ **193** = no vote; **184** = vote

## Motions → House and key requirement | from: S4, S5
| **Motion** | **House** | **Key requirement** |
|---|---|---|
| Adjournment | **Lok Sabha only** | **50 members**' support; sets aside House business for a **definite matter of urgent public importance**; element of censure; debate at least **2 h 30 min** |
| Calling attention | Both [GK] | Draws a minister's attention to a matter of urgent public importance; seeks authoritative statement from minister; Indian innovation since **1954**; in the Rules of Procedure |
| Privilege | Both | Breach by minister/member; **Rule 222 (LS)**, **Rule 187 (RS)** (LENSD, May 2026) |
| Cut motions | Lok Sabha [GK] | On demands for grants |
- **Cut motions (on demands for grants):**
  - **Policy cut** = reduce demand to Re 1 → disapproval of policy
  - **Economy cut** = reduce by a specified amount
  - **Token cut** = reduce by **Rs 100** → a specific grievance
- ⚠ Adjournment and no-confidence → both **Lok Sabha only**, both need **50 members**

## No-confidence vs censure → basis, Speaker's role, instances | from: S5
# 1. Basis and procedure
- **Constitutional status:** **Not mentioned in the Constitution**; flows from **Article 75(3)** → Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**
- **Rule:** Moved under **Rule 198** of Lok Sabha rules
- **House:** **Only in Lok Sabha**
- **Support:** **50 members** for leave
- **Reasons:** **No reasons need be stated**
- **Effect:** If passed, whole Council of Ministers must resign → enforces **collective responsibility**
- **Speaker's role:** Decides admissibility — whether the motion is in order; admits or rejects (APPSC-GS 2025 key)
  - ⚠ **Key:** Speaker has **discretion to admit or reject** the motion (APPSC-GS 2025)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Speaker does not resign or dissolve the House
- **Status:** **Most powerful weapon** of executive accountability (APPSC-G2 2025 key)
- **Contrast:** **Question Hour** = most effective method of **day-to-day** vigil on the executive
# 2. Censure vs no-confidence
| **Feature** | **Censure motion** | **No-confidence motion (Rule 198 LS)** |
|---|---|---|
| Reasons | **Must** state reasons | Not required |
| Against | Council of Ministers, a minister or a group [GK] | Entire Council of Ministers; **only in Lok Sabha**; needs **50 members** [GK] |
| Effect if passed | Not bound to resign; seeks disapproval of a policy [GK] | Council of Ministers **must resign** |
> "reasons must be stated" = censure motion.
# 3. Instances
| **Instance** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **First** | Against Nehru (**1963**, J.B. Kripalani) |
| **Andhra State** | Gouthu Latchanna's motion (Nov 1954); **69 for, 68 against** → Prakasam ministry fell by one vote after 13 months |
- President's Rule **16 Nov 1954 - 27 Mar 1955**

## Resolutions and the Whip → features | from: S6, S8
# 1. Resolutions
- **Nature:** Self-contained proposal, **always a substantive motion**
  - Mover cannot withdraw without leave of the House
  - Must be put to vote
  - Same subject barred for **one year**
- **Types:**
  - **Private member's:** Taken up on **alternate Fridays**, afternoon
  - **Government:** Moved by a minister (e.g., approving treaties)
  - **Statutory:** Moved under a constitutional/statutory provision
- **AP Legislature:** Uses same devices; e.g., AP Legislative Council adjourned amid Question Hour/short-duration discussion row on the Tirumala laddu issue (Feb 2026)
# 2. Office of Whip
- **Basis:** **Not mentioned in the Constitution, the Rules of the House or any statute** (parliamentary statute); rests on **conventions** of parliamentary government
  - ⚠ **Key:** Only the convention statement valid (APPSC-G1 2024)
- **Function:** Each party appoints a whip/chief whip → ensure attendance and voting per party line
- **Penalty:** Defying the whip on a vote can attract **Tenth Schedule** disqualification
- **Coordination:** Minister of Parliamentary Affairs keeps close contact with chief whips of parties in both Houses

=== ROW 33 | Legislative procedure - ordinary, money, financial and constitutional amendment bills; joint sitting

## Bills → types: government vs private member's | from: S0
| **Government bill** | **Private member's bill** |
|---|---|
| Introduced by a minister | Any MP who is **not a minister** |
| **7 days'** notice | **1 month's** notice (**one month's notice**) |
| Drafted by department with Law Ministry | Drafted by the member |
| Rejection signals loss of confidence | Defeat has no bearing on / does not affect the government |
- **LS committee:** **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions**
  - Classifies them, allots time, **examines constitutional amendment bills** by private members before introduction
  - RS has none → its **Business Advisory Committee** (BAC) allots time
- **Record:** Only **14** private members' bills have become law, the last in **1970**

## Ordinary bill → stages and President's assent (Art. 111) | from: S1
# 1. Introduction and readings
- **Introduction:** In **either House** (government bill: by a minister, 7 days' notice; private member: 1 month)
- **Three readings** in each House (APPSC-GS 2018):
| **Stage** | **What happens** |
|---|---|
| First reading | Leave to introduce; introduction; title and objectives; published in the **Gazette** |
| Second reading | General discussion → **committee stage** (select/joint committee) → **consideration/report stage**: **clause-by-clause** voting on amendments |
| Third reading | Bill accepted or rejected as a whole; no amendments of substance [GK] |
# 2. Second House
- **Transmission:** Same stages in second House; may pass, amend, reject, or keep it for **6 months**
- **Deadlock:** → joint sitting
# 3. President's assent - Art. 111
- **Options:** **Give assent, withhold assent, return for reconsideration** (non-money bills only)
- **Re-passage:** If re-passed with or without amendment → President **must assent**
- **Pocket veto:** No time limit fixed → "pocket veto" possible

## Money Bill → Article 110 provisions, procedure and cases | from: S2
# 1. Contents - Art. 110(1)
- **Scope:** Deals **only** with Art. 110(1) matters
  - Tax: imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, regulation
  - Borrowing by GoI
  - Custody of Consolidated/Contingency Fund + money in or out of them
  - Appropriation from CFI; declaring expenditure **charged**
  - Receipt/custody/audit of CFI or Public Account money; incidental matters
- **Not money bills:** Fines/penalties, fees for licences or services, taxes by local bodies for local purposes
# 2. Procedure
| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 110(3)** | **Speaker certifies** it; decision is **final** |
| **Art. 109** | Certificate endorsed when sent to the RS; Rajya Sabha must return within **14 days** with or without recommendations |
| **Art. 111** | Certificate endorsed when sent to the President; President may assent or withhold but **cannot return** for reconsideration |
- **Introduction:** **Only in the Lok Sabha**, only by a **minister**, only on the **President's recommendation** (APPSC-GS 2019)
- **Rajya Sabha:** LS may accept or reject its recommendations; if not returned → deemed passed
  - **No joint sitting**
- **President:** In practice assents, since introduced on his recommendation (some notes say he "cannot withhold")
# 3. Aadhaar cases
| **Case** | **Year** | **Holding** |
|---|---|---|
| **K.S. Puttaswamy** | **2018** | Upheld Aadhaar Act passed as a Money Bill |
| **Rojer Mathew** | **2019** | Money-Bill question referred to a larger (7-judge) bench |

## Money Bill vs Financial Bills (I, II) vs Finance Bill | from: S3
| **Feature** | **Money Bill (Art. 110)** | **Financial Bill I (Art. 117(1))** | **Financial Bill II (Art. 117(3))** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art. 110 matters | Exclusively | Partially, plus other matters | Not at all - only expenditure from the CFI |
| Speaker's certificate | Required | Not required | Not required |
| House of introduction | Only LS | Only LS | Either House |
| Who introduces | Minister only | Minister only | Minister or private member |
| President's recommendation | At introduction | At introduction | At **consideration** stage only |
| Rajya Sabha | Recommend only | Amend or reject | Amend or reject |
| Joint sitting | Not allowed | Allowed | Allowed |
| President returns it? | Cannot | Can | Can |
- **FB-I amendments:**
  - Amendments **reducing/abolishing** a tax need no recommendation
  - Amendments **increasing** a tax need the President's recommendation
- **Finance Bill:** Gives effect to the Budget's tax proposals; usually certified a Money Bill
- **Relation:** Every money bill is a financial bill vs not every financial bill a money bill
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes reverse the definitions and say all financial bills start in LS only → FB-II can start in either House

## Articles → House of introduction and President's prior recommendation | from: S4
- **Only in the Lok Sabha (cannot originate in RS):** **Money Bills** and **Financial Bills (I)**
| **Bill** | **Article** | **President's prior recommendation** |
|---|---|---|
| Bills to form new states or alter boundaries/names | **Art. 3** | Required (either House) |
| Money bills and FB-I | **Art. 117(1)** | Required |
| FB-II / bills involving expenditure from the CFI | **Art. 117(3)** | Before consideration |
| Bills affecting taxation in which states are interested | **Art. 274** | Required |
- **State level:** **No bill restricting inter-state trade** can be introduced in a state legislature without the President's previous sanction

## Joint sitting → Article 108 provisions and instances | from: S5
# 1. Grounds (deadlock)
- After one House passes a bill, the other:
  - **Rejects** it
  - Houses **finally disagree on amendments**
  - Does not pass it for **more than six months** (**6 months**); periods of prorogation/adjournment over **4 consecutive days** excluded
# 2. Procedure
- **Summoning:** **Summoned by the President**, who "notifies his intention" → neither House proceeds with the bill
  - ⚠ **Key:** If President does not summon it, the bill comes to an end (APPSC-G2 2017)
- **Presiding:** **Presided by the Speaker** → Deputy Speaker → **Deputy Chairman of RS** → a member chosen by those present
  - ⚠ **Trap:** The **VP/Chairman does not preside**
- **Decision:** **Simple majority of members present and voting** of both Houses together
- **Quorum:** One-tenth of total members
- **Excluded:** **Money bills** and **constitutional amendment bills** (Constitution Amendment Bills)
  - Financial bills (I) and (II) can go to a joint sitting
# 3. Held only three times
| **Bill** | **Year** |
|---|---|
| **Dowry Prohibition Bill** [GK] | **1961** |
| **Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill** [GK] | **1978** |
| **Prevention of Terrorism Bill** (POTA) [GK] | **2002** |
- ⚠ **1961 – 1978 – 2002** = Dowry – Banking Service Commission – POTA

## Lapsing of bills — dissolution, prorogation, adjournment | from: S6
| **Lapses** | **Does not lapse** |
|---|---|
| Bill pending in LS (originated there or sent by RS) | Bill pending in RS **not yet passed by LS** |
| Bill **passed by LS, pending in RS** | Bill for which President has **notified a joint sitting before dissolution** |
| Bill passed by RS, pending in LS | Bill passed by both Houses, pending **assent** / returned by the President for reconsideration [GK] |
- **Rajya Sabha:** Never dissolved
- **Joint sitting:** Cannot be summoned for a bill that has already lapsed
> Exam angle: "passed by LS but not yet taken up in RS does not lapse" is **false** (APPSC-G1 2024 statement).
| **Event** | **Bills** | **Motions / notices** |
|---|---|---|
| Adjournment / adjournment sine die | **Do not lapse** - taken up next session (PYQ APPSC-GS 2025) | Continue [GK] |
| Prorogation | **Do not lapse**[GK] | Pending notices lapse, except notices for introducing bills [GK] |
| Dissolution of Lok Sabha | See table above [GK] | All pending before the LS lapse [GK] |

=== ROW 34 | Budget in Parliament - stages, cut motions, appropriation and finance bills, Consolidated/Contingency/Public Account

## Budget → Article 112, terms and FIRSTs | from: S0
# 1. Constitutional basis
- **Budget = Annual Financial Statement:** Under **Article 112** (APPSC-GS 2018, 2023)
  - Estimated receipts and expenditure for financial year **1 April - 31 March**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** The word **"budget" is not used** in the Constitution
- **Laying:** President causes it to be laid before **both Houses**
- **Presentation:** In Lok Sabha by the **Finance Minister** (Union Budget 2023-24: Nirmala Sitharaman - APPSC-GS 2023)
- **Structure:** Covers three funds (Consolidated, Contingency, Public Account); two parts → **Revenue Budget** + **Capital Budget**
# 2. Terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Budget Circular** | Starts the process (September) |
| **Halwa ceremony** | Marks printing (North Block) |
| **bougette** | French "little bag" → etymology of "budget" |
# 3. Preparation and FIRST
- **Prepared by:** **Budget Division, Department of Economic Affairs**, Ministry of Finance
- **First Union budget:** **26 Nov 1947** (APPSC-GS 2018), by R.K. Shanmukham Chetty

## Funds → Articles 266-267 | from: S1
| **Fund** | **Union** | **States** | **What goes in / key rule** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consolidated Fund of India** | **266(1)** | Art. 266(1) | All revenues, all loans raised (T-bills, internal/external), loan repayments; withdrawal only by **appropriation law** (Art. 114(3)) |
| **Public Account of India** | **266(2)** | - | PF, small savings, deposits, remittances; govt acts as **banker/trustee**; no appropriation needed |
| **Contingency Fund of India** | **267** (**Art. 267(1)**) | Art. 267(2) | Unforeseen expenditure pending approval; advances by executive, recouped later [GK]; **Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950**; at **President's** disposal; held by the **Finance Secretary (DEA)** |
- **Contingency Fund corpus:**
  - **₹30,000 crore** from FY 2021-22
  - Earlier: ₹500 crore from 2005, raised from ₹50 crore (some notes say ₹5 crore)
- **Public Account:** Art. **266(2)**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes put it under 266(1)
- **State** Contingency Fund: Established by the **state legislature** by law (Art. 267(2)); at the Governor's disposal
- **Audit:** CAG audits all three funds of Union and states

## Cut motions → terms | from: S2
| **Cut motion** | **Amount** | **Purpose** |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy cut** | Demand reduced to **Re 1** | Disapproval of the policy behind the demand [GK] |
| **Economy cut** | Reduced by a specified amount | Economy in expenditure [GK] |

## Articles → Appropriation Bill and grants (114-116) | from: S3, S4
# 1. Appropriation Bill - Art. 114
- **Coverage:** **Grants voted by Lok Sabha + charged expenditure**, not exceeding the amount in the AFS
- **Art. 114(3):** No money withdrawn from the CFI except under appropriation made by law
- **Amendment bar:** No amendment may **vary the amount or alter the destination** of a voted grant, or vary charged expenditure
- **Repeal:** Carries an **automatic repeal clause**
  - ⚠ **Key:** Statements I and IV valid (APPSC-G1 2024)
# 2. Grants - Art. 115 and 116
| **Grant** | **Article** | **Purpose** |
|---|---|---|
| **Vote on account** | **116** | Advance for part of the year (about **two months**, one-sixth) till Appropriation Act; 3-5 months in election years |
| **Vote of credit** | 116 | Unexpected demand of indefinite magnitude/character [GK] |
| **Exceptional grant** | 116 | Not part of the current service of any year [GK] |
| **Supplementary grant** | **115** | Sanctioned amount insufficient |
| Additional grant | 115 | New service not in the budget [GK] |
| **Excess grant** | 115 | Spent more than granted; needs **PAC** approval first [GK] |
- **Vote on account:** Since the 1 Feb budget (2017), usually needed only in **interim-budget** years
- **Token grant (re-appropriation):** Must also be voted by LS
- ⚠ **115** = supplementary, additional, excess; **116** = vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional
