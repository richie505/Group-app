=== ROW 49 | Andhra Pradesh - climate, rainfall regions, cyclone vulnerability, drought-prone areas

## Climate — seasons and extremes → place | from: S0
# 1. Climate and seasons
- **Climate:** Hot and humid; summer **March-June** (APPSC 2025 key; APPSC-GS 2025)

| **Season** | **Period** |
|---|---|
| Summer | March to mid-June |
| SW monsoon | Mid-June to September |
| Retreating monsoon | October-December |
| Winter | January-February |

- **Winter (January-February):** Mostly dry (13-30° C); occasional light rain from retreating systems + western disturbances
- **Mango showers (April-May):** Pre-monsoon showers that help mangoes ripen
# 2. Extremes → place
| **Extreme** | **Place** | **Fact** |
|---|---|---|
| **Hottest place** | **Rentachintala (Palnadu)** | Often above **47° C** |
| Other hot zones | Kurnool, Kadapa, Nandyal, Vijayawada | - |
| **Heat waves** | Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur | Growing; peak **May-June** |
| **Coldest place** | **Lambasingi (ASR)** | The **"Kashmir of AP"**, down to about **2° C**; South Indian place with snow-like frost (APPSC-GS 2019) |

- **IMD:** Heat Action Plans in **23** heatwave-prone regions (2026)

## Rainfall — monsoon shares and data | from: S1
# 1. Seasonal shares
| **Monsoon** | **Share and facts** |
|---|---|
| **SW monsoon (June-September)** | **~2/3 (65-70%)** of AP's rainfall; onset 2nd week of June; **July** = wettest month |
| **Retreating SW / NE monsoon (October-December)** | **~1/3**; withdraws October-November; Bay of Bengal cyclones bring this season's rain to southern states; main rainy season for **Rayalaseema** + southern coastal districts (Nellore, Tirupati, Prakasam) |

- **India vs Coromandel:** Most of India gets rain in the SW monsoon; **Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu** (Köppen **As**) gets most from the **NE monsoon**
  - **AP:** In between, about **one-third** of rain in October-December
# 2. Normal and annual rainfall
- **Normal rainfall (2024-25):** **SWM 574.7 mm + NEM 285.3 mm = ~860 mm** a year
  - Older State average: **~966 mm**

| **Monsoon (APSES 2024-25)** | **Normal (mm)** | **Actual 2024-25 (mm)** | **Deviation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total (Jun-Dec) | 860.0 | **960.3** | **+11.7%** |

- **2023-24 (deficit):** Total 718.2 mm (**-16.5%**)
- **2024:** SWM **681.6 mm (+18.6%)** (17 districts excess); NEM **278.7 mm (-2.3%)** (15 districts deficient)
# 3. 2026: a deficient (El Nino) year
- Deficit 27.3% on 23 Jun; 11 of 28 districts deficient
- SW monsoon deficit **51.2% (1 Jun-25 Aug)**; **all 28 districts deficient**
- **10 of 28** districts "large deficient" in August (18 Sep 2026)
- Causes: **El Nino** subsidence + all seven low-pressure systems at the **head of the Bay** (CDCA Aug 2026)
# 4. IMD report
- **Title:** "Observed Rainfall Variability and Changes over Andhra Pradesh State"
- **Variability:** **Quite high** (APPSC 2025 key)
- **Lowest annual rainfall:** **South-western region (Rayalaseema)** (APPSC-GS 2023)

## Regions → hazards; drought-prone Rayalaseema | from: S2, S3
# 1. Region → main hazards
| **Region** | **Main hazards** |
|---|---|
| Coastal Andhra (Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Nellore) | cyclones, storm surge, floods, coastal erosion |
| Rayalaseema, southern Prakasam | chronic **drought** (NE monsoon failure), groundwater depletion, heat |
| Inland plains (Palnadu, Kurnool, Anantapur) | rising **heat waves** |

  - **Floods in the Godavari-Krishna deltas**
  - **Drought in semi-arid Rayalaseema**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Regions do not share identical hazards
- **Seasons:** River floods in SW monsoon (Jul-Sep); most cyclones post-monsoon/NE monsoon (Oct-Dec)

- **2004 tsunami:** AP coast hit, but **Tamil Nadu** worst-affected (APPSC 2025 key)

# 2. Rain-shadow Rayalaseema — causes
- **Rain shadow:** **Rayalaseema** lies in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats + Karnataka/Telangana highlands → little SW-monsoon rain
  - Other notes: rain-shadow of the Western and Eastern Ghats → low, erratic rainfall (Jan 2026)
  - **Anantapur** = lowest-rainfall district
- **Chronic droughts:** Rayalaseema + southern Prakasam when NE monsoon fails; groundwater depletion adds
- **Rural distress:** Worst in drought-prone Rayalaseema + rain-fed Prakasam, Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa

## Cyclone mitigation — measures and lessons | from: S6
# 1. Measures
- **Measures:** IMD early warning, **cyclone shelters**, evacuation plans
- **NDMA Cyclone Guidelines 2008:**
  - Structural: **multipurpose cyclone shelters**, raised platforms, cattle shelters, all-weather evacuation roads, storm-surge barriers
  - Nature-based: **mangroves, coastal bio-shields**, saline embankments, sand-dune restoration
- **Mangroves and coastal shelter belts:** Buffer surges; part of APSDMA's hazard-wise measures
- **Roof design:** Hipped roofs + firm roof-to-wall anchoring resist high winds better than gable roofs
- !! Not in your sources: recent AP coastal mock drills and the number of cyclone shelters.
# 2. NCRMP and forecasting
- **NCRMP (National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project):** With IMD forecasting + shelters, credited with cutting deaths
  - Phase I (World Bank-aided): **AP and Odisha**

| **IMD track-forecast error (2013-2022)** | **Change** |
|---|---|
| 24 h | **93 to 74 km** |
| 72 h | **201 to 153 km** |

# 3. Preparedness lessons
- **Hudhud lesson:** APSDMA **linked IMD alerts quickly to district administrations** → low deaths
  - = **Proactive risk governance: early warning + preparedness** (APPSC 2025 key)
- **Falling deaths:** Phailin, Fani → accurate warnings + timely evacuation

## Tiger, biosphere and elephant reserves | from: S3, S4
# 1. Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve
- **Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam (NSTR) Tiger Reserve:** In the **Nallamala** hills of the Eastern Ghats; river **Krishna**; spans AP + Telangana
  - Declared **1983** (CDCA Jul 2026)
  - Managed on a core-buffer model under **NTCA** (WPA 1972); Srisailam circle
- **Area:** About **3,728 sq km**; **India's largest tiger reserve** by area; **largest in India by core/critical tiger habitat** (APPSC 2024 key; CDCA Jul 2026)
  - Hence "largest tiger habitat is in AP" (UPPSC 2011)
- **Core areas:** **Nandyal, Prakasam, Palnadu**
  - Gundla Brahmeswaram (extended core) in Nandyal and Prakasam; core about **3,325 sq km** in total
- **Tigers:** **63-67** (All India Tiger Estimation 2022)
- **Chenchus:** **600 Chenchus** as protection watchers and guides (2025-26)
- **Count:** **one** tiger reserve (2024-25 and 2025-26) - follow the survey
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes list Sri Venkateswara and Papikondalu tiger reserves and call NSTR a BR; the survey supports neither
# 2. Seshachalam Biosphere Reserve
- **Seshachalam Biosphere Reserve:** AP's only BR; notified **2010**; **4,755.997 sq km**
  - Spread over the **Tirupati and Kurnool circles**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes call Sri Venkateswara the "1st Biosphere Reserve"; the BR's name is **Seshachalam**
- **Survey count:** **1 tiger reserve and 1 biosphere reserve**
# 3. Elephants
- **1 Elephant Reserve:** Koundinya Sanctuary + **Rayala Elephant Reserve** (Anantapuramu circle)
  - Kaundinya = AP's only Asian elephant habitat
- **Palamaner:** Elephants moved into Palamaner/Kaundinya in the **1980s** (APPSC 2024 key)
  - Cause: **drought in the Hosur-Dharmapuri forests and Bannerghatta**
  - Also described as **drought in the Hosur-Dharmapuri reserve forest and Bannerghatta**

## Forest regions and mangroves → species and type | from: S5, S6
# 1. Region → forest type
| **Region** | **Where** | **Type / signature species** |
|---|---|---|
| Nallamala hills | Nandyal-Prakasam-Palnadu | Largest forest in AP; dry deciduous; NSTR |
| Seshachalam hills | Tirupati-Annamayya | Dry deciduous; **Red Sanders** (Pterocarpus santalinus); Seshachalam BR |
| Palakonda hills | Kadapa-Annamayya | Dry deciduous; Red Sanders belt |
| Krishna-Godavari mouths | Kakinada, Krishna, Bapatla | Tidal / mangrove ("Koringa forests") |

- **Red Sanders:** **Endemic** to the southern Eastern Ghats of AP - the **Seshachalam, Palakonda and Veligonda** ranges (SV NP/WLS landscape)
- **Rainfall-based types:**
  - Moist/tidal belt at **125-200 cm** (north coast, Agency tracts, delta mouths)
  - **Dry deciduous at 75-100 cm** (Kadapa, Kurnool)
  - Thorn forests in the lowest-rainfall tracts
- **Forest-bearing hills:** North **Simhachalam**; West **Balaghat**; South **Palakonda and Nallamala**
# 2. Mangroves
- **Location:** **Godavari and Krishna estuaries**
  - **Coringa WLS** (Kakinada, 235.70 sq km)
  - **Krishna WLS** (Bapatla/Krishna, 194.81 sq km)
- **Afforestation:** Large-scale, from the **6th Five-Year Plan**
- **Rank claim:** State surveys (2024-25; 2025-26 p.84) call AP's mangroves the "**second-largest mangrove ecosystem in India**"
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **ISFR 2023 places Gujarat second**; use ISFR for national ranking questions

## Universal angles | from: S10 | universal
- **Current relevance:**
  - A tiger wandering around Papikonda returned to East Godavari without attacking anyone (CDCA Jul 2026)
  - 2025-26 analysis: notified forest revised to 37,421.11 sq km; 48 community eco-tourism centres; ZAAP manages Vizag and Tirupati zoos
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Smallest NP: Rajiv Gandhi (2.30 sq km)
  - Smallest WLS/sanctuary: Nelapattu (4.58 sq km)
  - Largest NP: Papikonda (1,012.90 sq km) (Annexure 5.26)
  - Largest PA: NSTR/Rajiv Gandhi WLS
  - Koundinya = AP's only elephant sanctuary
- **Comparative note:** Chilika is not a tiger reserve
- **Significance:** India's largest tiger reserve + an endemic-tree belt (Red Sanders) under a special protection area
- **Criticism & debates:** Survey's "second-largest mangrove" claim conflicts with ISFR 2023; sanctuary districts differ across notes after the district reorganisation
