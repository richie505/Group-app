## Floods — meaning, causes, types and committees | from: S2
# 1. Meaning, causes and types
- **Flood:** Water exceeds channel capacity and spills onto normally dry land; slow-onset, region- and season-predictable
- **Causes (APPSC 2025 key):** **heavy/prolonged rainfall, poor drainage, deforestation, dam failure**
  - Others: storm surges/cyclonic surge, snow/ice melt, low infiltration, siltation from soil erosion (UPSC)
- **Types:** Riverine, flash, coastal, urban (UPSC)

| **Structural** | **Non-structural** |
|---|---|
| Channel and drainage improvement, diversion | Flood-proofing, flood fighting, flood insurance |

# 2. Flood places

- **Assam:** **800 sq km** of upstream Nagaland forest cleared in 2013-23 → faster runoff

## Cloudbursts, flash floods and GLOFs → places and events | from: S3
# 1. Cloudbursts
- **Definition (IMD):** Extreme rain of about **100 mm per hour** over a small area (~20-30 sq km)
- **HKH "monsoon paradox":** Seasonal rain declining/erratic while short, intense cloudbursts rise
- **GLOF (glacial lake outburst flood):** Glacier retreat enlarges proglacial lakes that burst
- **IMD:** Gives flash-flood guidance for India + neighbours (Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal)
# 2. Event → place

| Event | Place | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Cloudburst, June 2013 | **Kedarnath (Uttarakhand)** | - |

  - Central Himalaya glacier area down **>20%** in three decades

## Droughts — types, triggers, effects and drought-prone regions | from: S5
# 1. Types

| **Type** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Meteorological** | Rainfall well below normal (the long-period average) over a period |
| **Hydrological** | Fall in surface and groundwater (rivers, reservoirs, streams, aquifers) after prolonged deficiency |
| **Agricultural** | Soil-moisture deficit causing crop failure |
| **Socio-economic** | Water/food shortage hitting livelihoods and markets |
| Ecological | Ecosystem productivity collapses; environmental distress |

# 2. Causes and triggers
- **Climatic triggers:** **irregular or delayed monsoon**/erratic rainfall, monsoon failure, monsoon breaks, high temperature
- **El Nino:** **6 of 10** El Nino years depressed Indian rain
- **Heat:** High heat + evaporation speed up soil-moisture loss
- **Normal SW-monsoon rainfall:** **868.6 mm** (1971-2020 base)
# 3. Long-term consequences
- **Effects:** **groundwater depletion**/falling water tables, **land degradation** and desertification, **rural (distress) migration**, rural indebtedness, lower farm output, ecosystem damage
# 4. Drought-prone regions
- **Drought-prone regions:** Western Rajasthan, Kutch-Saurashtra, Marathwada-Vidarbha, interior Karnataka, **Rayalaseema** (AP)
- **Rayalaseema (AP):** Highly drought-prone
  - Monsoon failure (some notes stress the North-East monsoon), crop loss, distress migration
  - Deep borewells → severe groundwater depletion

## Tsunamis — meaning, behaviour, risk zones and the 2004 event | from: S6
# 1. Meaning and causes
- **Meaning:** Japanese **"tsu" = harbour + "nami" = wave** ("harbour wave")
- **Definition:** Series of waves from **sudden vertical displacement of the ocean floor**
- **Causes:**
  - Mainly **undersea earthquakes** (tectonic, chiefly at subduction zones) (UPSC)
  - Volcanic eruptions, **submarine landslides**, asteroid/meteorite impacts
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Not a "tidal wave" (tides come from gravitational pull)
# 2. Wave behaviour

| **Deep ocean** | **Near the coast** |
|---|---|
| Very long wavelength; height under 1 m; speed ~700-800 km/h (up to **800 km/h**); low height, barely noticed [GK] | Wave slows in shallow water, height rises sharply (shoaling); massive inundation |

- **Warning signs:** Sudden **receding of sea water**, rapid sea-level rise, earthquake shaking
# 3. Source ocean and risk zones
- **Origin:** Mostly the **Indian Ocean** (APPSC 2025 key)
  - **Sumatra-Andaman (Sunda) subduction zone** / Sunda-Andaman
  - Makran zone threatens the west coast

| **Tsunami risk zone** | **States/UTs** |
|---|---|
| Very high | **Andaman & Nicobar** (near the Sunda subduction zone) |
| High | Tamil Nadu, **Andhra Pradesh**, Puducherry, Odisha |
| Moderate | Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat |

# 4. 26 Dec 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami
- **Trigger:** Sumatra-Andaman earthquake, Mw ~9.1
- **Worst-hit state:** **Tamil Nadu** (Nagapattinam worst district/coast) (APPSC 2025 key)
- **Also hit:** A&N Islands, Kerala, AP, Puducherry
- ⚠ Very high risk = **A&N**; 2004 worst-hit = **Tamil Nadu** (Nagapattinam)

## Storm surges, cyclone-prone coasts and coastline data | from: S7
# 1. Storm surges and cyclone-prone areas
- **Storm surge:** Abnormal sea-level rise pushed by cyclone winds + low pressure; with sea-level rise → coastal flooding, permanent inundation of low-lying land
- **Coverage:** Cyclones affect **13 coastal states/UTs** and **84 coastal districts**
- **Most cyclone-prone state:** **Andhra Pradesh** (APPSC 2025 key)
- **East vs west:** East coast gets far more cyclones than the west
- **AP coast:** **62+ cyclonic events** in four decades
- **Mangroves:** With coastal shelter belts, anchor sediment + **buffer storm surges**; with dune stabilisation and seawalls → coastal defence

=== ROW 28 | Disaster management framework - hazard vs disaster, DM Act 2005, NDMA, early warning systems

## Terms → meaning (hazard, vulnerability, disaster, risk) and hazard types | from: S0

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hazard** | Potentially damaging event |
| **Vulnerability** | Exposure + inability to cope |
| **Disaster** | Hazard meeting a vulnerable population → **Hazard x Vulnerability = Disaster**; **Sec. 2(d)** of the DM Act: Disaster = catastrophe causing substantial loss beyond the community's coping capacity |
| **Risk** | Likelihood of loss; rises with hazard, exposure, vulnerability; falls with coping capacity |

- Earthquake in an empty desert = hazard, not a disaster
- **Community vulnerability factors:** **kutcha housing**, dependence on farm labour, thin health infrastructure, low crop-insurance cover, weak early warning

| **Type** | **Examples** |
|---|---|
| Geological | Earthquakes, landslides, volcanoes, tsunamis |
| Hydro-meteorological | Cyclones, floods, droughts, cloudbursts, heat waves |
| Biological | Epidemics, pest attacks |

## DM Act 2005 → bodies, sections and heads | from: S2, S3
# 1. Background and objective
- **Background:** High-powered committee **1999**; National Committee after the **Gujarat earthquake**
- **Enacted:** **Dec 2005** (23 Dec 2005)
- **Core shift:** **from relief-centric response to proactive prevention, mitigation and preparedness**
- **Nodal ministry:** **Ministry of Home Affairs** (**MHA**) - overall coordination
- **Structure:** **three-tier structure**: NDMA-SDMA-DDMA
# 2. Bodies and key sections

| **Body** | **DM Act section / basis / year** | **Head / seat** | **Role** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NDMA** | Sec 3 (**s.3**) | **Prime Minister**; New Delhi | Apex; lays down policies, approves National Plan, guidelines, aid to other countries, NIDM policy |
| National Executive Committee (NEC) | Sec 8 (s.8) | **Union Home Secretary** | - |
| **SDMA** | Sec 14 (**s.14**) | **Chief Minister**; State Executive Committee under the **Chief Secretary** | - |
| **DDMA** | Sec 25 (**s.25**) | **Collector/District Magistrate** (**Collector/DM**); elected local-body representative / head is **co-chair** (**co-chairperson**) | - |
| **NIDM** | Sec 42 (**s.42**); began as NCDM (1995), renamed NIDM | New Delhi; **Southern Campus at Kondapalli (AP)** | Training, research, capacity building |
| **NDRF** (force) | Sec. 44 (**s.44**); raised 2006 | **Director General**; drawn from paramilitary forces; under NDMA superintendence | Specialist response force |
| NDRF Academy | - | **Nagpur** | - |
| NPDRR | Multi-stakeholder platform | Union Home Minister | - |

- **DDMA co-chair:** Detail most often tested - only tier where an elected representative sits with the bureaucratic head
- ⚠ **s.3** NDMA - **s.14** SDMA - **s.25** DDMA - **s.42** NIDM - **s.44** NDRF
# 3. NDRF — the specialist response force
- **Composition:** Specialist force drawn from the **central paramilitary forces** - search and rescue teams, engineers, dog squads
- **Superintendence:** NDMA
- **Gap:** SDRFs not yet set up in all states → strain on NDRF during simultaneous disasters
  - Suggested fix: mandatory SDRFs + joint NDRF-SDRF training
- **AP presence:** NDRF 10th Battalion + an NIDM southern campus near **Vijayawada/Gannavaram**, AP

=== ROW 29 | Marine resources - fisheries, minerals, polymetallic nodules, EEZ, blue economy

## UNCLOS maritime zones, EEZ and Acts | from: S0
- **UNCLOS:** Adopted **1982**, in force **1994**
  - Set up **International Seabed Authority (ISA)** - regulates seabed mining beyond national jurisdiction

| **Zone (from baseline)** | **Limit** | **Coastal-state rights** |
|---|---|---|
| **Territorial sea** | **12 nm** | Full **sovereignty** (innocent passage for others) |
| Contiguous zone | **24 nm** [GK] | Enforce customs, fiscal, immigration, sanitary laws [GK] |
| **EEZ** | **200 nm** | **Sovereign rights** to explore, exploit, conserve all living and non-living resources |
| Continental shelf | Edge of margin, extended shelf up to 350 nm [GK] | Sovereign rights over seabed and subsoil resources |
| High seas | Beyond national jurisdiction | Freedom of navigation, overflight, research |

- **EEZ scope:** Excludes the territorial sea + continental shelf beyond 200 nm
- **India's EEZ:** **~2.37 million sq km** (~24 lakh sq km); world's **18th largest**
  - Andaman-Nicobar + Lakshadweep islands add much of it
  - Older/some notes say 2.02 or 1.6 million sq km
- **Indian law:** **Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, EEZ and Other Maritime Zones Act, 1976**
- ⚠ **12 nm** = sovereignty; **200 nm** EEZ = sovereign rights only
