=== ROW 20 | Elements and factors of climate; world climatic classification (Koeppen)

## Climate — elements, definitions and terms | from: S0, S3
- **Elements:** Temperature, pressure, winds, humidity, precipitation, cloudiness
- **IMD normal (LPA):** SW monsoon (Jun-Sep) rainfall **868.6 mm**, from the **1971-2020** base period

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Weather** | State of the atmosphere at a place and time |
| **Climate** | Average weather over a long period (WMO standard **30 years**) |
| **Climograph** | A station's monthly temperature (line) + rainfall (bars) on one chart – used to read the climate type |
| **Biome** | Large natural community of flora and fauna over a major habitat, shaped by climate; contains many habitats (UPSC) |
| **Anthropogenic biomes (anthromes)** | Ecological units shaped by sustained human use (croplands, villages, cities) |
| **Tree line / timber line** | Altitude/latitude beyond which trees cannot grow – upper limit of tree growth on mountains or in high latitudes (APPSC GS 2019) → above it lies alpine tundra |

- ⚠ **Weather** = one place and time; **Climate** = **30-year** average

## Controls of climate → how each operates, Indian examples | from: S1
# 1. Controls of climate

| Control | How it operates | Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| **Latitude** | Angle of the sun's rays and insolation – insolation falls poleward (UPSC Geo p.42) | India's mainland lies **8 deg N-37 deg N**; areas south of the Tropic of Cancer are tropical |
| **Altitude** | Temperature falls with height (normal lapse rate ~**6.5°C per 1,000 m**; about 6.5 degC per km [GK]) | Shimla far cooler than Amritsar at a similar latitude |
| **Distance from the sea** | Coasts equable/maritime; interiors extreme/continental (IYB p.19) | **Islands (A&N):** Equable, humid; monsoon arrives there first |
| **Mountain barriers / relief** | Himalayas block cold Central Asian winds and trap the monsoon (IYB p.19); **Relief:** Windward slopes wet, leeward rain-shadow dry | **Himalayas** keep north India **3-8 deg C** warmer in winter than places on the same latitude |
| **Ocean currents** | Cold (cool) currents cool and dry coasts – make tropical west coasts arid; warm currents make east coasts warm and rainy (APP Oceanography) | - |
| **Winds and pressure belts** | Monsoons, westerlies, western disturbances, cyclones (UPSC Geo p.42) | - |

- ⚠ **Trap:** **Distance from a river is not a control of climate** (APPSC GS 2025)
> Exam angle: "Distance from river" is **not** a control of climate (APPSC GS 2025).

## Classifications → scholars, basis and codes (Koeppen, Thornthwaite) | from: S2
# 1. Scholar → basis

| Scholar | Basis |
|---|---|
| **Wladimir Koeppen** | Empirical scheme based on mean **temperature and precipitation**, linked to vegetation; variables used: **temperature, precipitation and natural vegetation** |
| **Thornthwaite (1931, revised 1948)** | Based on **precipitation effectiveness** + **thermal efficiency** (potential evapotranspiration) |

# 2. Koeppen groups → criterion

| **Letter** | **Group** | **Criterion** |
|---|---|---|
| A | Tropical humid | Coolest (coldest) month above 18 degC |
| B | Dry | Evaporation exceeds precipitation (BS = steppe, BW = desert) |
| C | Warm temperate | Coldest month between -3 and 18 degC |
| D | Cold snow-forest | Coldest month below -3 degC |
| E | Polar | Warmest month below 10 degC (ET = tundra, EF = ice cap) |
| H | Highland | Cold due to altitude (altitude-controlled) |

- **Second letters:**
  - **f** = no dry season
  - **m** = monsoon
  - **w** = dry winter
  - **s** = dry summer

## World climatic regions → location, climate and vegetation | from: S3, S4
# 1. Climatic regions

| **Region / biome** | **Location / representative area** | **Climate / key feature** | **Vegetation / key traits** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equatorial (selvas) / tropical rainforest | Equatorial belt (0-10 deg) - Amazon, Congo, Indonesia, SE Asia | Hot, wet all year; rain almost daily (afternoon convectional rain); rain 50-260 inches | Dense evergreen hardwoods (mahogany, ebony); dense multi-layered canopy, epiphytes, lianas; richest diversity - over half of all known species |
| Tropical monsoon (tropical deciduous) | India, SE Asia, N Australia | Seasonal wind reversal; summer rain; 700-2,000 mm rain | Teak, sal; trees shed leaves in the dry season (UPSC Env p.26) |
| Savanna (tropical wet-dry; Sudan type) | Sudan belt, East Africa, Llanos, Campos | Wet summer, dry winter | Tall grass with scattered trees |
| Hot desert (tropical, mid-latitude) | Sahara, Thar, Kalahari | Very low rainfall - under 25 cm; high diurnal range | Xerophytes |
| Mediterranean | 30-45 deg, western margins | Winter rain | Evergreen shrubs, olives, citrus |
| **China type** (sub-tropical) | Central/S China, **SE USA**, S Brazil, E Australia | Warm humid east margin; summer rain | - |
| Temperate deciduous | Mid-latitudes between tropics and polar regions (APPSC G2 2025); **NW Europe** (British type), British Columbia, S Chile, New Zealand; E USA, E China | Westerlies; rain all year; mild | Oak, beech, maple, birch shed leaves in autumn |
| **Taiga / Boreal** | Northern North America, Europe and Asia (Eurasia); northern hemisphere only | Long cold winters | Evergreen **conifers** - spruce, fir, pine; acidic **podzol** soils; lowest productivity of all forests |
| Tundra | Arctic coasts / fringe of N America and Eurasia; **alpine tundra** above the tree line in Himalayas, Andes, Alps | **Permafrost** | No trees; mosses, lichens; reindeer, arctic fox, musk-ox |

- **Table basis:** Largely textbook; notes confirm sub-tropical (China-type) biome = eastern China and SE USA, British-type deciduous biome = NW Europe
# 2. Hot deserts
- **Location:** **15-30 deg N and S** on the **western margins** of continents
- **Causes:** subtropical high + offshore trade winds + cold currents
- **Examples:** Sahara, Kalahari, Namib, Atacama, Arabian, Thar, Great Australian
# 3. Taiga
- **Taiga is the largest land biome**
- **Wide** (very wide, not narrow) winter-summer temperature range; moderate precipitation
- ⚠ **Trap:** "Narrow range" was the incorrect statement (APPSC G1 2023, 2024)
# 4. Mediterranean climate — features and causes
- **Seasonal pattern:** **Hot dry summers, mild wet winters** – rain comes in **winter**
  - Winter: rain from westerly depressions (cyclonic rain)
  - Summer: hot, dry and sunny under the subtropical high (drought)
- **Reason:** Seasonal shift of pressure belts
- **Location:** **30-45 deg N/S on the western margins** of continents
- **Economy:** Citrus fruit, grapes and wine ("orchard lands")

| **Mediterranean basin** | Rome, Athens |
| **California** | Los Angeles, San Francisco |
| **Central Chile** | Santiago |

- ⚠ **Hot deserts** = **15-30 deg** western margins; **Mediterranean** = **30-45 deg** western margins; **China type** = east margin

## Terms → meaning (depth mapping, zones, minor relief) | from: S1, S4
# 1. Depth mapping

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Bathymetry** | Measuring ocean depth + study and mapping of the sea floor (APPSC GS 2018) |

## Ocean bottom relief → depth and features | from: S2

| **Division** | **Depth / extent** | **Form / characteristic** | **Key feature** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Continental shelf** | To about 200 m | Gently sloping, shallowest submerged edge / extension of the continent | Richest fishing grounds, oil and gas; "submerged prolongation of land" |

## Ocean deposits → types and make-up | from: S6
- **Terrigenous deposits:** From the land (gravel, sand, silt, mud); laid (settle) **mainly on the continental shelves** and slopes (APPSC G1 2024, statement I correct)
  - Coarseness **decreases** away from shore: coarse near coast → fine mud farther out (get **finer** away from the shore)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Coarseness increases seaward" was the wrong statement (APPSC G1 2024)
- **Pelagic deposits:** Deep-sea oozes (calcareous, siliceous) + **red clay**; cover the deep-sea plains and deeps

| **Pelagic type** | **Make-up** |
|---|---|
| Calcareous ooze (globigerina, pteropod) | Lime shells of tiny organisms [GK] |
| Siliceous ooze (diatom, radiolarian) | Silica skeletons; colder or deeper water [GK] |
| **Red clay** | Fine inorganic clay and volcanic dust; the most widespread deep-sea deposit, largest in the deep Pacific [GK] |

## Ocean temperature and salinity — distribution, factors and ranking | from: S7, S8
# 1. Temperature — horizontal distribution
- **Latitude:** Falls from the equator to the poles; enclosed tropical seas are warmest
- **Hemispheres:** Northern hemisphere oceans warmer than southern (more land)
- **Tropical zone:** **Western** side **warmer** than **eastern**
  - West: warm currents along continents' east coasts; trade winds pile warm water westward
  - East: **cooler** – cold currents + upwelling on continents' west coasts
- **Temperate zone:** Westerlies make **eastern sections warmer** (reversed; e.g. North Atlantic Drift)
  - Both statements correct (UPSC 2021)
- **Annual range:** Smaller in the vast **Pacific** than in the **Atlantic**
- ⚠ Tropics: **west warmer**; temperate: **east warmer**
# 2. Temperature — vertical distribution and heat
- **Layers:** Warm surface layer (to ~500 m; 20-25 degC in the tropics [GK]) → **thermocline** (temperature drops fast with depth, ~500-1,000 m) → cold deep layer near 0-4 deg C
- **Polar seas:** Only the single cold layer
- **Mid-latitudes:** Warm layer forms only in summer
- **Heat uptake:** Oceans take up **>90%** of excess heat
- **Marine heatwave:** SST 3-4 deg C above average for at least 5 days
# 3. Salinity — definition and factors
- **Definition:** Grams of dissolved salt per 1,000 g of water (per mille / ppt)
  - Marine water = **~35 ppt**; brackish water = **5-35 ppt**
- **Raised by:** **Evaporation** + freezing
- **Lowered by:** **Precipitation, river inflow** + ice melt
- **Redistributed by:** Currents and winds
- **Pattern:** Highest in subtropics (~20-30 deg); lower at the rainy equator
# 4. Water bodies → salinity ranking

| **Water body** | **Salinity (per mille)** | **Why / note** |
|---|---|---|
| **Lake Van** (Turkey) | **330** | Closed soda lake - the world's largest alkaline lake |
| **Dead Sea** | **238** | Closed basin, intense evaporation |
| **Great Salt Lake** (Utah) | **220** | Closed basin |
| Red Sea | ~41 (among the saltiest seas) | High evaporation, little river inflow |
| Mediterranean | ~37-39 | - |
| Black Sea | **17-18 ppt** | About half the ocean average |
| Baltic Sea | Lowest (under ~10) - among the least saline | Many rivers, low evaporation, cold |

> Exam angle: the APPSC G2 2023 key is **Lake Van > Dead Sea > Great Salt Lake** (NCERT figures).

## Salinity — definition, salts and factors | from: S1, S2
# 1. Definition and source
- **Definition:** Total dissolved salts in sea water, in **parts per thousand (per mille, ppt)** or PSU
- **Ocean average:** About **35 per mille** (35 g per kg)
- **Source of salts:**
  - Weathered rock material carried by rivers
  - Volcanic/hydrothermal emissions on the sea floor
# 2. Factor → effect on salinity

| **Factor** | **Effect on salinity** |
|---|---|
| Evaporation | Raises it [GK] |
| Precipitation | Lowers it [GK] |
| River inflow | Lowers it - e.g. Black Sea fed by the Danube, Dnieper, Dniester (LENSD Jul 2026) |
| Ice melting / freezing | Melting lowers, freezing raises [GK] |
| Currents and winds | Redistribute saline water [GK] |

- **Effect:** Salinity **reduces evaporation** (APPSC GS 2018)
# 3. Dissolved salts → share

| **Salt** | **Approx. share of dissolved salts** |
|---|---|
| **Sodium chloride** | ~77.8% [GK] |
| Magnesium chloride | ~10.9% [GK] |
| Magnesium sulphate | ~4.7% [GK] |
| Calcium sulphate | ~3.6% [GK] |
| Potassium sulphate | ~2.5% [GK] |

- **By ions:** **Chloride** + **sodium** dominate, followed by sulphate and magnesium

## Seas and regions → salinity (horizontal distribution) | from: S3
- **Highest:** **Subtropics** (high evaporation, low rain)
- **Lower:** **Equator** (heavy rain) and near the **poles** (melting ice, low evaporation)

| Sea | Salinity | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Red Sea**, Persian Gulf, Mediterranean | High | Hot, dry areas |

## Density and clines → terms | from: S4
- **Factors:** Density rises as **temperature falls** + as **salinity rises**; pressure (depth) also raises it
- **Effect:** Denser water sinks, lighter water rises → drives currents
  - Cold polar water sinks and creeps towards the equator

**Clines → gradient each denotes**

| **Cline** | **Rapid change in** |
|---|---|
| **Thermocline** | Temperature [GK] |
| **Halocline** | Salinity [GK] |
| **Pycnocline** | Density [GK] |
| **Chemocline** | Chemistry (e.g. oxygen/hydrogen sulphide) - typical of meromictic basins like the Black Sea, where upper and lower layers rarely mix (LENSD Jul 2026) |

- **Role:** Each cline acts as a barrier to vertical mixing of water
- ⚠ **Halocline** = salinity; **Pycnocline** = density
