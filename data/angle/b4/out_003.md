=== ROW 13 | Major landforms - glacial, arid/aeolian, karst and coastal landforms

## Landform → geomorphic agent; weathering and mass wasting types | from: S0, S10
# 1. Landform → agent (quick revision)
| **Landform** | **Agent** |
|---|---|
| Delta, V-valley, gorge, canyon, levee, peneplain | Running water (river) |
| Cirque, horn, U-valley, hanging valley, moraine, till, esker | Glacier |

# 2. Weathering type → process
| **Type** | **Process** |
|---|---|
| Physical | Exfoliation, frost wedging, block disintegration - no chemical change [GK] |
| Chemical | Solution, carbonation, oxidation, hydration, hydrolysis [GK] |
| Biological | Roots, burrowing animals, humans [GK] |
- **AP soils from weathering:** Granite/gneiss → **red soils**; basalt → **black soils**
- **Mass wasting:** Soil creep, slump, landslide, mudflow, rockfall under gravity

## Fluvial landforms → river stage; delta type → condition | from: S1
# 1. Stage → landforms
| **Stage / course** | **Landforms** |
|---|---|
| Youth (upper course) | V-shaped valley, **gorge**, **canyon**, **waterfall**, potholes, rapids |
| Mature (middle course) | Meanders, flood plains, alluvial fans at the foot of hills |
| Old (lower course) | Oxbow lakes, natural levees, braided channels and riverine islands, deltas |
# 2. Landform terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Canyon** | Upper-course feature (APPSC 2022); forms in horizontally bedded sedimentary rocks; step-like sides, wider at the top |
| **Gorges** | Hard rocks; almost equal width at top and bottom |
| **Meander** | **Outer bend** = erosion, concave cliff-slope vs **inner bend** = deposition, convex slip-off slope |
| **Oxbow lake** | Detached meander loop with stagnant water |
| **River terraces** | Cut by **vertical erosion** of a stream into its own floodplain; paired = same level on both banks; unpaired = one bank, slow uplift |
| **Braided channel** | Brahmaputra; sandbars called **chars** |
| **Estuary** | River mouth freely open to the sea (an ecotone); west-flowing Narmada and Tapi form estuaries, not deltas |
# 3. Delta type → condition → example
| **Delta type** | **Condition** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Arcuate (most common) | Sediment denser than sea water | Nile, Sundarbans (Ganga) |
| Cuspate | Sediment density = sea water | Tiber (Italy) |
| Bird-foot | Sediment lighter than sea water, carried far | Mississippi |
- ⚠ Denser → arcuate; equal → cuspate; lighter → bird-foot

| **Term** | **Meaning / region** |

## Glacial landforms → meaning | from: S3
| **Landform** | **Key point / feature** |
|---|---|
| Cirque | Armchair-shaped, scooped-out hollow at the valley head |
| Arete | Knife-edge ridge between two cirques |
| Horn | Pointed peak where cirques cut back from several sides (Matterhorn) |
| U-shaped valley | Glacier deepens and widens a river valley; ice moves faster in the centre |
| Hanging valley | Tributary valley left high above the deepened main valley; stream enters as a **waterfall**; agent = glacier (UKPSC 2024) |
| Moraine / till | Unsorted glacial debris / mound - lateral (edges), terminal (snout), medial, ground moraines |

| Fjord | Glacially carved valley drowned by the sea (Norway, Greenland, New Zealand) |
- **Glacial deposits:** Till, moraine and boulder clay = **glacial activity** (APPSC 2017)
- **Drumlin fields:** Give "**basket of eggs**" topography (some notes attach the term to boulder deposits)
- **Also:** Kettle holes; outwash plain and **fjord** (drowned glacial valley) as above
- ⚠ **Moraine** = unsorted; **esker** = **stratified**

## Aeolian (arid) landforms → meaning | from: S4

| **Landform** | **Key point** |
|---|---|

- **Other forms:**
  - **Mesa** (wide flat-topped table hill) → **butte** (smaller remnant)
  - **Inselberg:** Isolated residual hill, e.g. Uluru

## Karst terms → meaning; speleothems; longest caves | from: S5
- **Karst:** Landscape carved by groundwater dissolving **limestone/dolomite**; named after the Karst region on the Adriatic coast (Slovenia)
- **Sequence:** Sinkhole (no surface drainage) → **doline** (sinkholes combined) → **uvala** (dolines combined) → **polje** (large depression from cave collapse)
| **Feature** | **Description** |
|---|---|
| Sinkhole | Small, deep depression with no surface outlet; water drains underground |
| Doline | Larger depression formed by joining sinkholes; clay can seal it into a doline lake |
| Uvala | Several dolines merged |
| Polje | Large depression from collapse of an underground cave |
| Sinking creek / bogas | Stream lost through cracks; open-topped ones are bogas |
| Blind valley | Valley ending abruptly where its stream vanishes underground into a swallow hole [GK] |
- **Cave deposits - speleothems (calcium carbonate):**
  - **Stalactites** hang from the cave roof (APPSC 2022)
  - **Stalagmites** rise from the floor
  - Meet / joined → **pillars/columns**
- **Longest caves:** India = **Krem Liat Prah** (Meghalaya); world = **Mammoth Cave** (USA)

## Coastal erosion — agents, processes, landforms, defences | from: S8
- **Agents:** **Waves** (main agent), tides and currents; also wind
- **Processes:** **Hydraulic action, abrasion (corrasion), attrition**, solution
- **Erosional sequence:** Sea cliff + **wave-cut platform** → sea cave → **arch** → **stack** → stump
- **East coast erodes more:** Soft, low-lying alluvial sand and silt + no rocky cliffs + flat wide plains let the sea move inland
- **Human causes:** Harbours, beach sand mining, offshore dredging, dams cutting sediment supply
| **Defence** | **Methods** |
|---|---|
| Soft | Beach nourishment, dune stabilisation, mangroves |
| Hard | Seawalls, groynes, offshore breakwaters |

=== ROW 14 | Atmosphere - structure (layers) and composition

## Composition of the atmosphere — gas → % by volume; variable constituents | from: S0
# 1. Permanent gases - data
| **Gas** | **% by volume** |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N2) | **78.08** (~**78%**: APPSC-GS 2025) |
| Oxygen (O2) | **20.95** |
| Argon (Ar) | **0.93** |
| Carbon dioxide (CO2) | about **0.04** (older notes: 0.036) |
| Neon | 0.002 |
| Krypton | 0.001 |
| Helium | 0.0005 |
| Xenon, hydrogen | traces |
- **Descending order:** **Nitrogen > Oxygen > Argon > CO2** (**N2 > O2 > Ar > CO2**) (APPSC-G1 2023)
- ⚠ Third place = **Argon**, not CO2
- **CO2:**
  - Transparent to incoming sunlight; absorbs outgoing terrestrial radiation (greenhouse gas)
  - Rose **3.5 ppm** to **423.9 ppm** in 2024 - largest rise since 1957; **152%** of pre-industrial (WMO Greenhouse Gas Bulletin 2025, Oct 2025)
- **Height limits:** CO2 and water vapour only up to about **90 km**; oxygen negligible by **120 km**
# 2. Variable constituents
- **Water vapour:**
  - Up to **4%** in warm, wet tropics; under 1% in deserts and polar air
  - Decreases with height and from equator to poles
  - Absorbs insolation + keeps in earth's heat (blanket effect)
  - Most abundant greenhouse gas; decides air stability
- **Dust:**
  - Sea salt, soil, smoke, soot, ash, pollen, meteor dust
  - Concentrated in lower layers; more in **subtropical and temperate** belts (dry winds) than equatorial and polar
  - Act as **hygroscopic nuclei** for clouds / condensation

## Layers of the atmosphere → height, temperature trend, phenomena | from: S1, S2, S8
# 1. Division by composition
**Homosphere vs heterosphere (Group-II table)**
| **Region** | **Height** | **Layers** | **Composition** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homosphere | Surface (0) to ~80-90 km [GK] | Troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere [GK] | Gases evenly mixed; uniform composition / mix of gases [GK] |
| Heterosphere | Above ~80-90 km (above ~90 km) [GK] | Thermosphere and exosphere [GK] | Gases in layers by molecular weight: nitrogen (N2), atomic oxygen (O), helium (He), hydrogen (H) (upward) [GK] |
# 2. Division by temperature
- **Basis of division:** **How temperature changes with height**; composition gives the other division (homosphere/heterosphere)
- **Mass:** **99%** of atmosphere's mass within **32 km** of the surface
| **Layer** | **Height** | **Temperature with height** | **Key features** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Troposphere | Avg **13 km** (8 km poles, 18 km equator) | Falls, about **1°C per 165 m** (6.5°C/km) | All weather; water vapour, dust, clouds |
| Stratosphere | Tropopause to ~**50 km** | **Rises** (inversion) | **Ozone layer**; calm, stable air - high-altitude balloons; jet aircraft cruise near its base [GK] |
| Mesosphere | ~50 to ~**80 km** | Falls to about **-100°C** at 80 km (coldest layer) | Meteors burn up; top = mesopause |
| Thermosphere (with ionosphere) | Above ~80 km / 80-85 km (to ~600 km) | Rises rapidly | **Ionosphere** - **ionosphere reflects radio waves**; auroras; ISS orbits at ~400 km |
| Exosphere | Outermost (~600-10,000 km) | - | Extremely thin / rarefied; merges into outer space |
- **Troposphere height:** Older notes give 0-10 km; the pole-equator figures above are the ones asked
- **Temperature trend:**
  - Falls in the **troposphere**
  - → Rises in the **stratosphere** (ozone absorbs UV: an inversion)
  - → Falls in the **mesosphere**
  - → Rises in the **thermosphere** (absorption of high-energy radiation)
- **Reversal (inversion) layers:** Stratosphere and thermosphere
> Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 - meteorites burn up in the **mesosphere** (not thermosphere/ionosphere).
# 3. Phenomena → layer (quick revision)
| **Phenomenon** | **Layer** |
|---|---|
| Weather, clouds, rain | Troposphere |
| Ozone layer | **Stratosphere** (APPSC 2025) |
| Meteors burn up | **Mesosphere** (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| Aurora, airglow | Thermosphere (ionosphere) |
| Radio-wave reflection | Ionosphere |
| Jet aircraft | Lower stratosphere, free of clouds and turbulence: the standard exam answer [GK] (some notes put commercial aircraft in the troposphere) |

## Boundaries → height — tropopause, stratopause, mesopause; troposphere thickness | from: S3
# 1. Tropopause
- **Position:** Boundary between troposphere and stratosphere
- **Height:** ~**8 km** at the poles, ~**18 km** at the equator; higher in summer than in winter
- **Temperature:** About -80 °C over the equator vs -45 °C over the poles
- **Role:** Caps the weather and vertical mixing of the troposphere
- ⚠ Tropopause higher **and** colder over the equator (-80 °C) than over the poles (-45 °C)
# 2. Other boundaries (upper limits)
- **Upper limits:** Tropopause, stratopause (~50 km), **mesopause** (~80 km)
# 3. Troposphere thickness and lapse rate
- **Thickness:** Thickest at the **equator** (strong heating drives **convection currents** that lift heat higher); thinnest at the poles
- **Normal lapse rate:** Falls **1 °C per 165 m** (= **6.5 °C per km**)

## Ionosphere — layer → height → scientist name; radio communication | from: S4
- **Extent:** About **60 km to over 1,000 km**; within the thermosphere, about **80-400 km**
- **Formation:** Solar **UV and X-rays** strip electrons from atoms
- **Effects:**
  - Charged particles → **auroras and airglow**
  - Electrically charged ions **reflect radio waves** back to Earth → long-distance communication
- **Daily cycle:** Expands by day, shrinks at night
- **Disruption:** Solar flares and geomagnetic storms disrupt GNSS signals (9 Jul 2026)
| **Layer** | **Height** | **Role / note** |
|---|---|---|
| D | ~60-90 km | Absorbs medium/long (low-frequency) waves; fades at night |
| E (Kennelly-Heaviside) | ~90-150 km | Reflects medium-frequency radio waves |
| F (Appleton; F1, F2 by day) | ~150 km upward (~150-400 km) | Main reflector of short-wave radio; key for long-distance radio |
- ⚠ **E** = Kennelly-Heaviside; **F** = Appleton

## Ozone layer — location, formation cycle, role, unit | from: S5
- **Location:** Stratosphere, **10-50 km** (about **90%** of ozone lies here)
  - Densest around **15-35 km** (some notes: ~20-30 km); normal peak **25-30 km**
- **Formation - ozone-oxygen (Chapman) cycle:**
  - O2 + UV → O + O; O + O2 → O3
  - O3 absorbs UV and splits back - a natural balance
- **Role:** **Earth's shield** - absorbs harmful **UV-B and UV-C** (APPSC 2025 / APPSC-GS 2025)
  - UV-B causes skin cancer, cataract, immune suppression, lower crop yield
- **Unit:** **Dobson Unit (DU)**
- **Ground-level (tropospheric) ozone:** "Bad" secondary **pollutant**; part of photochemical smog; formed from NOx + VOCs in sunlight
- ⚠ Stratospheric ozone = shield; ground-level ozone = pollutant

## Ozone depletion — ODS → use; halocarbons and the ozone hole | from: S6
- **Primary cause:** **Halocarbons** (APPSC 2023 / APPSC-GS 2023) such as **CFCs, HCFCs** → release **chlorine and bromine** atoms that destroy ozone faster than it forms
| **ODS** | **Use** |
|---|---|
| CFCs | Refrigeration, ACs, aerosols, foam blowing |
| HCFCs | Transitional CFC substitute |
| Methyl bromide | Agricultural fumigant |
| Carbon tetrachloride, methyl chloroform | Solvents |
- **Ozone hole:**
  - Seasonal thinning over **Antarctica**, **Aug-Nov** (southern spring); helped by polar stratospheric clouds
  - Came into focus / reported in **1985** (older notes give 1983)
- **Halocarbons - ozone depletion and warming:**
  - **CFCs:** Destroy **stratospheric ozone** + potent GHGs; GWP 1,000-23,000; about **1,000 times** more heat-absorbent than CO2 (older notes); make up **15%** of the enhanced effect
  - **HFCs:** Replaced CFCs; do not harm ozone, but high GWP

=== ROW 15 | Insolation and heat budget; horizontal and vertical distribution of temperature, inversion

## Insolation — factors and latitudinal distribution; hottest latitudes | from: S0
- **Factors:**
  - **Angle of the sun's rays** (vertical rays heat a smaller area)
  - **Length of day**
  - **Transparency of the atmosphere** (clouds, dust, vapour)
  - **Earth-sun distance**: perihelion ~3 Jan, 147 million km; aphelion ~4 Jul, 152 million km
  - **Land-sea distribution**, altitude, slope/aspect of land
- ⚠ **Perihelion** = ~3 Jan, 147 million km; **aphelion** = ~4 Jul, 152 million km
- **Distribution:**
  - Maximum reaches **subtropical deserts** (least cloud), not the equator
  - Equator receives the most annual insolation; poles the least (but long summer days)
  - Falls towards the poles; higher over continents than oceans at the same latitude
- **Hottest latitudes:** **Sub-tropical deserts (about 20-30° N)**, not the equator → clear skies + dry air

## Heating processes → mechanism; vertical distribution and lapse rate | from: S1, S7
# 1. Process → mechanism
| **Process** | **Mechanism** |
|---|---|
| Insolation (solar radiation) | **Direct** heating by incoming short waves |
| Conduction | Heating of the **lower layers** of air in contact with the warm ground; works best in solids (particle-to-particle contact) |
| Convection | **Vertical** transfer by rising warm air - heated air becomes less dense and rises, cooler air sinks (confined to the troposphere) |
| Advection | **Horizontal** transfer of heat by wind / moving air (e.g. the Loo of north India) |
| Terrestrial radiation / radiation | Transfer by electromagnetic waves, needs no medium; long-wave heat given off by the earth = the main source heating the atmosphere |
> Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2023 matched Advection - horizontal, Convection - vertical, Insolation - direct, Conduction - lower layers.
# 2. Vertical distribution - why temperature falls with height
- **Heated from below:**
  - Sun emits **short-wave** radiation; earth re-emits **long-wave infra-red** (8,000-25,000 nm), absorbed by CO2 and water vapour
  - So air heated **from below**, not directly by the sun
- **Higher air:** Thinner + less water vapour and dust → absorbs less heat
- **Result:** Temperature falls with height (normal lapse rate **6.5 °C/km**)

## Albedo — surface → albedo data; clouds; ice-albedo feedback | from: S2
# 1. Albedo and clouds
- **Definition:** Share of incoming radiation a surface reflects
- **Ranking:** **Fresh snow** (highest, APPSC-G1 2019) > sand > grass > forest > water
| **Surface** | **Albedo (approx.) **[GK] |
|---|---|
| Fresh snow | 80-90% |
| Sand (dry) | 20-40% |
| Grass | 20-25% |
| Crop land | 15-25% |
| Water (sun high) | below 10% |
- **Clouds:**
  - **High, thin clouds trap heat** (warming)
  - **Low, thick clouds reflect sunlight** (cooling)
- ⚠ High thin = warming; low thick = cooling
# 2. Ice-albedo feedback
- **Mechanism:** Melting ice exposes dark ocean → more absorption → more warming
- **Black carbon:** On snow, cuts albedo
- **Arctic warming:** At least **2°C**; warms **3-4 times** faster than the global average (Apr 2025; older notes say about twice)

## Earth's heat budget — data (NCERT units) | from: S3
# 1. Insolation budget
- **Planetary albedo:** About **35%** = **35 units of 100**
| **Of 100 units of insolation (top of the atmosphere)** | **Units **[GK] |
|---|---|
| Reflected to space (clouds 27, snow/ice 2, scattering 6) | 35 |
| Absorbed by atmosphere | 14 |
| Absorbed by earth's surface | 51 |
- **Surface:** All 51 absorbed units returned as terrestrial radiation, so earth neither warms nor cools overall
# 2. Terrestrial radiation budget
- **Earth radiates 51 units:**
  - **17** straight to space
  - **34** absorbed by the atmosphere (6 direct, 9 convection/turbulence, 19 latent heat of condensation)
  - Atmosphere sends **48** (14 + 34) to space
# 3. Latitudinal balance
- **Latitudinal balance:** Surplus in the tropics, deficit towards the poles; winds and currents carry heat **poleward**

## Greenhouse effect — gases → share and source; GWP; warming data | from: S4
# 1. Greenhouse effect
- **Short vs long wave:** Atmosphere **transparent to short-wave** sunlight but **opaque to long-wave** (infra-red) terrestrial radiation; absorbs and re-radiates part back
  - Heated mainly **from below, by terrestrial radiation**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Heated more by incoming solar radiation" is **wrong**; greenhouse gases absorb long waves: **right** (UPSC 2024)
- **Greenhouse gases:** CO2, CH4, water vapour, N2O trap the sun's heat and raise Earth's temperature (APPSC 2025 / APPSC-GS 2025)
- **Without atmosphere:** Surface well below freezing (about -18 °C instead of +15 °C average); trapped heat keeps the average temperature up (UPSC 2025: all three statements correct)
# 2. Gas → share → source
| **Gas** | **Share of greenhouse effect** | **Main source** |
|---|---|---|
| CO2 | ~60% | Fossil fuels, deforestation, cement |
| Methane ("marsh gas") | ~20% | Paddy, wetlands, livestock, landfills |
| CFCs | ~15% | Refrigeration, aerosols |
| N2O | ~5% | Fertilisers, burning coal/biomass |
- **Water vapour:** Most abundant greenhouse gas (a feedback)
- **GWP (100 yr):** CO2 **1**; methane **28-36**; N2O **265-298** (older table: 21, 310)
# 3. Warming data
- **Global land temperature:** Up **1.59 °C** since pre-industrial (UNEP Emissions Gap 2025)
- **India's annual mean:** Up **0.7 °C** since 1900 (some notes say India has crossed 2 °C)
  - Slower because of poleward heat transfer, Arctic amplification and aerosol masking

## Horizontal distribution and range of temperature — isotherms, thermal equator, continentality | from: S5, S6
# 1. Isotherms and thermal equator
- **Isotherms:** Join places of equal temperature; run roughly east-west, parallel to latitudes
| **Month** | **Isotherms over land** | **Isotherms over oceans** |
|---|---|---|
| **January** (NH winter) | Bend **equatorward over land** | **Poleward over oceans** - oceans warmer than land (UPSC 2025: both statements correct, II explains I) |
| **July** | Pattern reverses, poleward over land | Pattern reverses |
- Land heats and cools faster than water
- **Southern Hemisphere:** Isotherms more regular (mostly ocean)
- **Thermal equator:** Line of highest mean temperature; shifts with the sun's apparent movement
  - Lies mostly **north of the equator** (more land in the NH)
- **India, winter:** **20°C isotherm** runs from the mouth of the Tapti (Tapi) to the Mahanadi delta; temperature rises from north to south
# 2. Range of temperature
- **By latitude:** **Seasonal differences are maximum in high latitudes** (APPSC-G1 2023); largest annual range in continental interiors of high latitudes (Siberia)
  - Annual range minimum at the **equator** (equatorial belt = smallest annual range)
- **Interior vs coast:**
  - Continental interiors = large annual and daily ranges (**continentality**) - hot summers, cold winters (northern interiors of India)
  - Coasts = **equable/maritime**, small range, moderated by the sea
- **Clear vs cloudy nights:** **Clear nights** lose more heat by radiation (cold nights, frost) vs **cloudy nights** stay warmer (clouds trap outgoing radiation and send heat back)
- **India:** Winter diurnal range highest in the north-west = **14-18°C**
- **AP:**
  - Coast = low diurnal range; Rayalaseema = high
  - Visakhapatnam & Kakinada get sea-breeze relief while Palnadu bakes

## Temperature inversion — conditions, types, effects | from: S8
- **Definition:** Normal lapse reverses: temperature **rises** with height
- **Conditions:** **long winter nights, clear skies, calm dry air, snow cover** (**clear skies, calm and dry air**)
| **Type** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| **Ground/radiation inversion** | Surface; night cooling |
| **Valley inversion** (**valley (air-drainage) inversion**) | Cold air drains downslope into valleys → frost pockets on valley floors, slopes warmer → orchards/plantations on upper slopes |
| **Frontal/advection inversion** | - |
| Permanent | In **polar areas**; stratosphere = permanent inversion layer |
- **Effects:** **Fog, frost, smog**; pollutants trapped in the planetary boundary layer
  - North Indian winter mornings: inversion + heavy moisture + **fog**
