=== ROW 4 | Seasons - inclination of the axis, solstices and equinoxes, distribution of insolation

## Causes of seasons — key reason, terms and traps | from: S0
- **Key reason:** **Axis inclined 23½° from the perpendicular (66½° to the orbital plane)** (APPSC 2018)
- **Other factors:** **Parallelism of the axis** (always points to the Pole Star) + **revolution** + spherical shape → changing Sun angle and day length
- **Hemispheres:** Tilted **toward** the Sun = summer; other = winter; South is the exact opposite of the North
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Perihelion** | Earth **nearest the Sun (~3 Jan)** during NH winter |
| **Aphelion** | **farthest (~4 July)** in NH summer |
- ⚠ **Trap:** Distance does **not** cause seasons; **tilt** does

## Insolation — factors and distribution | from: S2
- **Factors:**
  - **angle of incidence** (vertical rays heat a smaller area)
  - **duration of day**
  - **transparency of the atmosphere**
  - land configuration
- **Distribution:** Falls from the tropics to the poles
  - Maximum over **subtropical deserts** (cloudless skies), not the Equator
- ⚠ Maximum insolation = **subtropical deserts**, not the Equator

## Seasons by hemisphere and latitude zone → features | from: S1
- **Longest day:** NH **21 June**; SH **22 December** (shortest day the reverse)
- **Australia:** **Christmas in Australia falls in summer**
- **Maximum contrast:** Seasonal differences **maximum in high latitudes** (APPSC-G1 2023) - huge swings in day length and insolation
| **Latitude zone** | **Seasonal feature** |
|---|---|
| Low latitudes | Small temperature contrast; seasons marked by rain (wet/dry) |
| Subtropics | Hot summers, mild winters |
| Mid-latitudes | Four distinct seasons |

## Places in India → overhead Sun, day length, hot season | from: S3
- **Overhead Sun:** **twice a year** only between the Tropics ("zero-shadow days")
  - Places north of **23½°N** (Delhi, Srinagar) never get an overhead Sun
- **21 June:** No noon shadow on the Tropic of Cancer
  - North of the Tropic: noon shadows shortest in June, longest in December
- **June day length (increases northward):** **Srinagar > Delhi > Hyderabad > Chennai > Kanyakumari**
- **AP (12°41'N - 19°07'N):** Between the Equator and the Tropic of Cancer → overhead Sun twice a year + high temperatures year-round
- **India - hot weather season (March-May):** Sun's northward march brings the **hot weather season (March-May)**
  - ~50°C at **Ganganagar** (1935)
  - Heatwaves reach AP, Telangana, Odisha
  - ~10% of annual rain is pre-monsoon ("coffee blossom" showers in South India)

## Universal angles | from: S4 | universal
- **Current relevance:** International Day of Yoga, 21 June 2026 (theme "Yoga for Healthy Ageing") marks the summer solstice
- **AP angle - AP's seasons:**
  - Summer (Mar-May)
  - SW monsoon (Jun-Sep, ~2/3 of rain)
  - Winter (Oct-Feb; **Lambasingi** coldest, ~2°C)

  - IMD's four seasons: winter (Jan-Feb), pre-monsoon (Mar-May), SW monsoon, post-monsoon
  - Traditional six ritus (Vasanta to Shishira)

## Seismic waves — types, terms and shadow zone | from: S1
| **Wave** | **Behaviour** |
|---|---|
| **P-waves** | fastest; pass through **solids, liquids and gases**; reach the inner core at **11.23 km/s** |
| **S-waves** | slower; **solids only**; **S-waves vanish in the outer core → outer core is liquid** |
| **Surface waves** | arrive last, do the most damage |
- **Boundaries:** wave speed changes with density → waves **reflect and refract** at layer boundaries → how discontinuities were found
- **Shadow zone:** belt **105°-145°** from the epicentre gets no direct P or S waves
  - P-waves not recorded between about **105° and 145°**
  - **S-waves are absent everywhere beyond 105°** → S-wave shadow much larger; proves outer core is liquid
- **Each earthquake has its own shadow zone:** set by where its epicentre is
  - (CGPSC 2020: Statement I true)
- ⚠ P = solids + liquids + gases; S = **solids only**

## Layers — thickness, density and state (data) | from: S2, S6, S7
| **Layer** | **Depth / extent / thickness** | **Density** | **State / share / notes** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crust | Oceanic ~5 km, continental ~30 km, up to ~70 km under mountains; mean ~20 km in your notes | Upper 2.8, lower 3.0 | Solid; continental crust granitic (SIAL), oceanic basaltic (SIMA) |
| Mantle | Moho to **2,900 km** | Mean **4.6** | Solid (partly plastic above); **83% of volume, 68% of mass**; silicates rich in iron and magnesium |
| Outer core | 2,900-5,150 km | ~10-12 | **Liquid** |
| Inner core | 5,150-**6,371 km** (centre) | **13.3-13.6** | **Solid** |
- **Crust note:** some notes give mean crustal thickness ~20 km and call the crust mostly basalt - true only of oceanic crust; continental crust is granitic
- **Mantle zones:**
  - Old scheme: upper mantle (Moho to 1,000 km), lower mantle (1,000-2,900 km)
  - **IUGG** scheme: Moho-200 km, 200-700 km, 700-2,900 km
- **Core share:** **16% of Earth's volume**, **32% of its mass**
- **Density gradient:** **2.8-3.0 (crust)** → **4.6 (mantle)** → **11-13.6 (core)** (inner core 13+)
  - Earth's average ~**5.5 g/cm³**
- ⚠ Mantle = **83%** volume, **68%** mass; core = **16%** volume, **32%** mass

## Lithosphere and asthenosphere — terms → meaning | from: S4
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Lithosphere** | crust + rigid uppermost mantle, about **100 km** thick; broken into **tectonic plates** that move over the **semi-fluid asthenosphere** |

## Core and magnetic field — features | from: S6
- **Boundaries:** begins at **~2,900 km**; splits at **5,150 km**
- **Outer core (2,900-5,150 km): liquid (molten)**
  - Proof: **loss of S-waves**
- **Inner core (5,150-6,371 km): solid**
  - Density **13.3-13.6**; P-waves cross at 11.23 km/s

- **Role of iron:** accounts for Earth's magnetism and rigidity
- **Magnetic field - origin:** **dynamo action of convecting molten iron in the outer core** - churning molten iron runs the **geodynamo**; iron in NIFE indicates the interior's magnetism
- **Axis inclination:** magnetic axis inclined **~11.5° to the geographic axis**
- ⚠ Outer core **liquid**, inner core **solid** (pressure)

## Rock cycle — processes linking the three types | from: S6
- **Cycle:** **Magma → (cooling) igneous → (weathering, erosion by wind and water, deposition, lithification) sedimentary → (heat, pressure) metamorphic → (melting) magma.**
  - Surface → weathering, erosion, transportation, deposition → **sedimentary** (burial, compaction, cementation)
  - Deeper burial, heat and pressure → **metamorphic**
  - Melting → **igneous** → uplift back to surface
- **Key rule:** any type can turn into any other; exposed metamorphic rock can weather into sediment
- **Mantle link:** rock material comes up in eruptions; subducted crust melts back into the mantle
- **Carbon cycling:** CO2 in rain → carbonic acid → weathers rock → stored as bicarbonate/limestone

=== ROW 7 | Earth movements - continental drift, sea-floor spreading, plate tectonics and plate boundaries

## Theories → proponents, year and mechanism | from: S0, S2, S3, S4
| **Theory** | **Proponent / year** | **Mechanism** |
|---|---|---|
| Continental drift | **Alfred Wegener** (1912) (APPSC 2018) | Driving forces: **pole-fleeing force** (from Earth's rotation) + **tidal force** (Sun and Moon) |
| Convection current theory | **Arthur Holmes** (1930s) | Radioactive decay heat in mantle → **convection currents**; rising limbs split crust (ridges); sinking limbs drag it down (trenches) → carry plates |
| Sea-floor spreading | **Harry Hess (1961)** (early 1960s) | Magma rises at **mid-oceanic ridges** → new crust spreads sideways → destroyed at **trenches** |
| Palaeomagnetic stripes | Vine and Matthews, 1963 | **symmetrical bands of normal and reversed polarity** on either side of ridge |
| Plate tectonics | **1967**, **McKenzie and Parker**, independently **Morgan** | Lithosphere broken into rigid plates floating on semi-fluid **asthenosphere**; driving force: **mantle convection** (Arthur Holmes) |
| Jig-saw computer fit | Bullard, 1964 | - |
- **Wegener's weakness:** forces judged **too weak** → theory not accepted
- **Holmes's significance:** gave drift the driving force Wegener lacked
- ⚠ **1912** Wegener → **1930s** Holmes → **1961** Hess → **1967** plate tectonics

## Plate boundaries → motion and examples | from: S5
| **Boundary** | **Motion** | **Landforms / examples** |
|---|---|---|
| **Divergent** (constructive) | Plates move apart; new crust forms | **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**; rift valleys; Red Sea / East African Rift (Afar triple junction) - Nubian, Somalian, Arabian plates diverging |
| **Convergent** (destructive) | Collision or subduction | **Himalayas** (India-Eurasia) - continent-continent (Indian vs Eurasian); Andes - ocean-continent (Nazca under South American) [GK]; Mariana Trench - ocean-ocean subduction [GK]; trenches, island arcs and the Andes [GK] |

  - Divergent: stretching
  - Convergent: subduction friction (Pacific Ring of Fire)
  - Transform: sudden slip

## India in Gondwanaland and the Indian plate — drift, collision, new findings | from: S1, S6
- **India's parent:** **India is part of Gondwana Land** (APPSC 2018); Indian craton once part of Pangaea
- **Peninsular plateau:** old **Gondwana shield**; part of the African landmass until the **Cretaceous**
  - **Owen Fracture** now separates African and Indian plates
- **Timeline:**
  - **Gondwanaland broke up ~225 million years ago**; Indian craton slid towards Eurasian craton
  - Himalayas rose ~65 million years ago
- **Direction & rate:** **northward**; moves **north at ~5 cm/year** into Eurasian plate
- **Palaeomagnetic evidence:** magnetic latitudes in Indian rocks show India drifting from **south of the equator northward** (far south before the Cretaceous)
- **Deccan Traps:** erupted as India crossed near the equator over the **Reunion hotspot**
- **Collision timing:** **~4-5 crore (40-50 million) years ago**; older notes say 50-55 million
- **Process:** Indian plate **subducted** under Eurasia, **closed the Tethys**; sediments folded and **thrust** up → Himalayas, **still rising**
- **New findings:**
  - India **underthrust** Asia **45-20 million years ago**, lifting **Tibetan Plateau**
  - Pushed deeper under western Tibet → west more rugged
- **Delamination:** denser lower layer **peeling off and sinking into the mantle** at ~100 km beneath Tibet, seen with **94 seismic stations** in southern Tibet (seismic imaging, helium isotopes)
- **Ladakh Magmatic Arc:** between Indus and Shyok suture zones; formed by northward subduction of **Neo-Tethys** plate
  - 130-million-year history laid the base for the Himalayas
- ⚠ **Owen Fracture** = African/Indian plates; **Reunion hotspot** = Deccan Traps

## Folding and fold mountains — terms and examples | from: S7, S8
- **Cause:** **Compression** bends layers into folds
  - **anticline** = up-arch; **syncline** = down-trough
- **Sequence with rising pressure:** symmetrical → asymmetrical → **overfold** (overturned) → recumbent → **nappe** (recumbent fold broken by a thrust, pushed far forward)
- **Siwaliks:** simple anticline-syncline folds of the **"Jura" type** (Alps)
| **Young fold (Tertiary / Alpine orogeny)** | **Old fold (worn down)** |
|---|---|
| **Himalayas** (Asia) | **Aravallis** (India - one of the oldest) |
| **Alps** (Europe) | **Appalachians** (North America) [GK] |
| **Andes** (South America) [GK] | **Urals** (Russia; Europe-Asia divide) [GK] |
| **Rockies** (North America) [GK] | - |
- **Kunlun:** rims northern edge of Tibetan Plateau
- **Himalayas:**
  - Formed from **Tethys Sea sediments** in the **Tertiary** period
  - Uplift phases (plate theory): **Eocene, Miocene and Pliocene**
  - Began **~70 million years ago**; **still going on**
  - **Siwaliks** = youngest range

## Faults → force, movement and places | from: S9
- **Fault:** a **fault** = fracture between rock blocks letting them move - suddenly (earthquake) or slowly (creep)
| **Fault** | **Force** | **Movement / example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | Tension | Hanging wall moves **down**; divergent zones - Rio Grande Rift, East African Rift |
| **Reverse** | Compression | Hanging wall moves **up** |
| **Thrust** | Compression | Low-angle reverse fault; the Himalayan **MCT, MBT, HFF** |
| **Strike-slip** | Shear | Horizontal - blocks slide past horizontally; **San Andreas**; Sagaing (Myanmar) |
- **Indo-Gangetic plain:** bounded by **Himalayan Frontal Fault** (north) + **normal fault** (south)
- **Block mountains:** uplifted blocks = **horsts**, sunken = **grabens**
  - Examples: **Vosges** and **Rhine Valley** (APPSC 2022: horsts = block mountains)
  - India: Satpura = block mountain; **Narmada and Tapi** flow in rift valleys
- **East African Rift:** a **series of lakes**, mostly **freshwater**; **Lake Malawi at its southern end**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Lake Victoria is not a rift lake** - lies between the two branches (APPSC G1 2024: I, II, III)
- **Red Sea:** also a rift

## Diastrophism — definition and types | from: S0
- **Definition:** all processes that move, raise/elevate or build up the crust
| **Orogenic** | **Epeirogenic** |
|---|---|
| **Mountain building** by severe folding in long narrow belts | **Continent building**: uplift or warping of large continental areas |
| **Horizontal (tangential)** compressive/tensional forces | **Vertical (radial)** forces (UKPSC 2024) |
- **Included:** orogeny, epeirogeny, earthquakes, plate tectonics/movements
- ⚠ **Trap:** "heterogenic" NOT included (MPPSC 2023)
- **Peninsular block:** escaped folding but had **vertical movements** (transgression, regression) → epeirogenic effect

## Scholars → mountain-building and isostasy theories | from: S1
| **Scholar** | **Theory** |
|---|---|
| **Kober's** | Geosynclinal theory: long, shallow, sinking sea troughs (geosynclines) collect sediments between rigid forelands (kratogens) → forelands squeeze sediments into mountains |
| **Airy** | Crust of **uniform density**, different thickness; mountains have deep "roots" [GK] |
| **Pratt** | Crust of **different density**, uniform depth of compensation; higher blocks are lighter [GK] |
- **Geosyncline stages:** lithogenesis (sedimentation) → orogenesis (folding, mountain building) → gliptogenesis (gradual rise, denudation)
- **Indian example:** **Tethys geosyncline** uplifted into the Himalaya (Tertiary / Aryan era)
- **Isostasy:** balance of crustal blocks "floating" on denser material below
- ⚠ **Airy** = same density, different thickness; **Pratt** = different density, same depth
