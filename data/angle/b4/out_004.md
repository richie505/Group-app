## Pressure terms → meaning; instruments → what they measure | from: S0

## Wind controls — Coriolis force (Ferrel's law), friction, geostrophic wind | from: S1
# 1. Coriolis force
- **Cause:** **Rotation of the Earth**
- **Deflection:** **Right in the northern hemisphere (NH)**, **left in the southern hemisphere (SH)** (APPSC-G1 2019) = **Ferrel's law**
- **Magnitude:** Zero at the **equator**, maximum at the **poles**; directly proportional to wind speed
- **Consequence:** Cyclones form only beyond **5°** latitude
# 2. Friction & geostrophic wind
- **Friction:** Slows surface winds (stronger over land); negligible in the upper air
- **Wind modifiers:** **Coriolis force** (direction) + **friction** (speed)
- **Geostrophic wind:** Upper-air wind blowing **parallel to isobars** when pressure gradient force is balanced by Coriolis force (no friction)

## Pressure belts → latitude and other name; ITCZ and seasonal shift | from: S2, S3
# 1. Belts & names
| **Belt** | **Latitude** | **Other name / nature; other names / weather** |
|---|---|---|
| Equatorial low | 0-5° N and S (both sides of equator) | **Doldrums**, **ITCZ** - calm, rising air, trade winds converge |
| Subtropical (sub-tropical) high | **30-35° N and S** | **Horse latitudes** - calm, weak winds, sinking dry air, hot deserts |
| Subpolar (sub-polar) low | ~60-65° N and S | Westerlies meet polar easterlies; storms |
| Polar high | Poles (90° N and S) | Cold, dense, sinking air |
- **Horse latitudes:** **30-35° N/S** belt of weak winds (APPSC 2019; APPSC-GS 2019)
- **Doldrums:** Extend on both sides of the **equator**; belt of calm, not strong winds (APPSC-G1 2019, 2024)
# 2. PYQ trap
- **APPSC-G1 2024:**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Doldrums are strong winds" is WRONG (they are calm)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "NH trade winds blow from the north-west" is WRONG (they are **north-easterly**)
  - Answer = **neither**
# 3. ITCZ and seasonal shift
- **Definition:** **Inter Tropical Convergence Zone** = low-pressure zone near the **equator** where trade winds converge (APPSC 2019; APPSC-GS 2019)
- **Seasonal shift of belts:** About 5-10° **north in the NH summer** and **south in winter** → follows the overhead sun (apparent movement)
- **NH summer:** ITCZ shifts north over the Indian plains (monsoon trough)
  - ITCZ shift over India drives the monsoon (UPSC)
- **Winter:** ITCZ migrates **south of India**
  - + high-pressure cells over Tibet and Siberia → **north-east monsoon**
  - Southward shift of the doldrums → heavy rain in southern states (bimodal rainfall)

## Jet streams → position and role; discoverer; polar vortex | from: S6
# 1. Jet streams
- **Nature:** Narrow, high-velocity **upper-level westerly/easterly winds at 10-12 km**
- **Influence:** Regulate monsoon onset and withdrawal
- **Discovery (FIRST):**
  - First identified by **Wasaburo Ooishi** (Japan, 1920s)
  - Widely known during World War II → noticed by high-flying bomber pilots
| **Jet** | **Position / season** | **Key facts / role in Indian monsoon** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet (STJ)** | South of the Himalayas in winter; shifts **north of the Himalayas** in summer | Carries **western disturbances**; its withdrawal lets the monsoon "burst"; returns in October to end it |
| **Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)** | Summer only, **5-20°N**, June to early October (South China Sea to East Africa) | Comes with the SW monsoon; creates upper-air anticyclone and surface low over Indian plains; strengthens SW monsoon |
| **Somali (Findlater) Jet** | Low-level, off the East African coast | With the Somali current, intensifies / strengthens monsoon winds |
- ⚠ **STJ** = westerly, winter, western disturbances; **TEJ** = easterly, summer, strengthens SW monsoon
# 2. Polar vortex
- **Definition:** Year-round **low-pressure, cold-air** reservoir around the poles, held in by a circular jet; strongest in winter
- **Layers:**
  - Tropospheric part: up to **15 km**
  - Stratospheric part: **15-50 km** (seasonal)
- **Role:** Anticlockwise flow **keeps cold air locked near the poles**; **strong vortex keeps the cold air up north** and warm air to the south (APPSC 2018; APPSC-GS 2018)
- **Breakdown:** Rossby waves / **sudden stratospheric warming** weaken it → winds reverse, vortex splits → cold air spills into mid-latitudes (cold spells)

- **Monsoon:** Arabic "mausim" (season) = seasonal wind reversal

|---|---|---|
| **Halley's theory** | **1686** | Monsoon = giant land-sea breeze (land-sea breeze on a large scale) |
| **Modern monsoon theory** | **1957** | Built on the **northward shift of the ITCZ** + jet streams + Tibet plateau (Tibet heat island / heating) + ENSO |

=== ROW 17 | Humidity, evaporation, condensation, clouds and forms of precipitation

## Humidity terms → meaning; latent heat; AP humidity data | from: S0
# 1. Measures of humidity
| **Measure** | **Definition **[GK] |
|---|---|
| Absolute humidity | Actual water vapour per unit volume of air (g/m3) |
| Specific humidity | Grams of water vapour per kg (unit weight) of air (g/kg); unaffected by volume changes |
| Relative humidity | Actual vapour as a % of what the air can hold at that temperature |
| **Dew point** | Temperature at which air becomes saturated and condensation begins |
| **Latent heat** | Taken in during evaporation, released on condensation → powers thunderstorms and cyclones |
- ⚠ **Absolute** = per volume (g/m3); **specific** = per kg (g/kg)
- **Relative humidity:** RH **falls when temperature rises**, rises on cooling (same vapour) → warm air holds more vapour
  - Higher over oceans, lower over continents
- **Saturation:** RH = **100%** = **saturation**
# 2. Distribution
- **Water vapour:** Up to **4%** by volume in warm, wet (humid) tropics; under 1% in deserts and polar areas
  - Falls with latitude and with altitude
# 3. AP
- **Coastal Andhra:** RH **70-90%** year-round (high heat index)
- **Rayalaseema:** Often **below 30%** in peak summer

## Hydrological cycle — processes; hydrometeorology; India water budget data | from: S1
# 1. Processes
- **In the cycle:**
  - **Evaporation** + **transpiration** (together **evapotranspiration**)

  - **Infiltration/percolation**, **surface runoff**, groundwater flow
- **Not in the cycle (distractors):** Photosynthesis, combustion, respiration → biological/other processes
# 2. Hydrometeorology
- **Definition:** Study of transfer of water and energy between the land surface and the lower atmosphere → rainfall, evaporation, floods, droughts
# 3. India's water budget (2026) - data

## Forms of condensation and precipitation → condition | from: S2
| **Form** | **Condition / note** |
|---|---|
| Dew | Moisture condenses on cool surfaces, dew point above 0°C |
| Frost | Dew point at or below 0 °C; forms as ice crystals (Lambasingi, "Kashmir of AP", sees frost at ~2 °C) |
| Fog | Cloud at ground level; air cooled below dew point; often under inversion; visibility under 1 km |
| Mist | Like fog but thinner, wetter (more moisture) and better visibility - over 1 km |
| Smog | Fog + smoke/pollutants; **London** smog: sulphurous, cool and humid; **Los Angeles** smog: photochemical, needs sunlight, forms **PAN**, carries ground-level ozone (UPSC) |
| Rain | Drops over 0.5 mm |
| Drizzle | Fine drops under 0.5 mm, from stratus |
| Snow | Condensation below 0 °C as ice crystals |
| Sleet | Rain freezing into ice pellets as it falls through a cold layer / cold air |
| Hail | Layered ice balls from **cumulonimbus** with strong updrafts |
- ⚠ **Fog** = visibility under 1 km; **mist** = over 1 km
- ⚠ **London** smog = sulphurous; **Los Angeles** smog = photochemical
- **Ideal for dew/frost:** **Clear sky, calm air, high RH, cold long nights**
- **Prevented by:** **Cloudy skies, wind, low humidity** (**cloudy or windy nights**)
- **Coloured rain:** Dust or spores mixed with rain
  - Sirocco dust over Italy ("blood rain")
  - Kerala's red rain of **2001**

## Clouds → appearance and height; optical phenomena → cause | from: S3, S4
# 1. Cloud → family → appearance → weather
| **Family (base height) / level** | **Cloud** | **Appearance / weather** | **Weather** |
|---|---|---|---|
| High (above ~6 km; 8,000-12,000 m; ice crystals) | **Cirrus** | Thin, feathery, **silky** | Fair weather; no rain |

# 2. Optical phenomena → cause
| **Phenomenon** | **Cause** |
|---|---|
| **Halo** (sun/moon) | Ring of light from sunlight **refracting through millions of hexagonal ice crystals** in thin high (**cirrostratus**) cloud; typically a ring of about **22°** radius (APPSC-G1 2024 key: statements I and II) |
| Rainbow | Refraction, dispersion and internal reflection of sunlight in raindrops; seen with the sun behind the observer [GK] |

- ⚠ **Halo** = refraction through ice crystals; **corona** = diffraction by water droplets

=== ROW 18 | Air masses and fronts - classification and associated weather

## Air masses — meaning, Bergeron code → source region | from: S0
- **Air mass:** Very large body of air with nearly uniform temperature and humidity horizontally
  - Acquired by staying over a homogeneous **source region** (hot desert, warm ocean, snow-covered plain)
- **Classification (Bergeron):**
  - First letter = moisture → **c** continental = dry; **m** maritime = moist
  - Second letter = thermal source → **T** tropical, **P** polar, **A** arctic, **E** equatorial
| **Code** | **Source region** | **Character** |
|---|---|---|
| mT | Tropical/subtropical oceans | Warm, humid |
| cT | Subtropical hot deserts (Sahara, Arabia) | Hot, dry |
| mP | High-latitude oceans | Cool, moist |
| cP | Snow-covered high-latitude continents (Siberia, Canada) | Cold, dry, stable |
| cA | Arctic basin, Greenland ice | Very cold, very dry |
| mE | Equatorial oceans | Hot, very humid |
- ⚠ **c** = dry (continental), **m** = moist (maritime) - first letter is moisture, not temperature
- **Polar anticyclones:** Cold, dense cP/cA air → cold-core highs, strongest near the surface
  - **Siberian High, Canadian High, Antarctic High**
  - Weather: clear skies, fog, frost, sharp night cooling (Jul 2025)

## Air mass modification → weather; stability | from: S1
- **Modification:** Air mass changes as it leaves its source → heated or cooled from below; gains or loses moisture
| **Situation** | **Result** | **Weather** |
|---|---|---|
| Cold air moves over warmer surface | Heated from below, unstable | Convection, cumulus, showers [GK] |
| Warm air moves over colder surface | Cooled from below, stable | Fog, stratus, drizzle [GK] |
- **Hygroscopic nuclei:** Dust and salt particles → nuclei for condensation
- **Stability:** Water vapour (up to **4%** by volume in the humid tropics) controls stability/instability of air
- **Arctic amplification:** Meandering jet stream carries warm air masses into the Arctic → **Arctic amplification** (UPSC Env p.220)
## Fronts → weather; Bjerknes polar front theory | from: S2, S3
# 1. Front types → weather
- **Front:** Sloping boundary zone between two air masses of different temperature and density
  - Cold air always forms a wedge under the warm air
| **Front** | **What happens** | **Slope** | **Weather** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | Cold air advances and lifts warm air sharply | Steep | Cumulonimbus, heavy short showers, thunder, squalls, sudden temperature fall [GK] |
| Warm | Warm air rides up over retreating cold air | Gentle | Wide belt of layered cloud, prolonged light-moderate rain ahead of the front [GK] |
| Stationary | Neither air mass advances; winds blow parallel to the front | - | Cloud and rain can persist for days [GK] |
| Occluded | Faster cold front overtakes the warm front; warm air lifted off the ground | - | Mixed cloud and rain; last stage of a temperate cyclone [GK] |
- ⚠ **Cold** front = steep, short heavy showers; **warm** front = gentle, prolonged light rain
- **Arcus (shelf/roll) clouds:** Form along the leading edge of thunderstorm outflows or cold fronts → mark the warm/cool air boundary (Jul 2025)
- **Polar anticyclones + polar fronts:** Form cold fronts → storms and temperature drops
# 2. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Frontogenesis** | Formation or sharpening of a front where contrasting air masses converge |
| **Frontolysis** | Weakening and dissolution of a front as the temperature contrast is lost |
# 3. Cloud and rain sequence at passage of a front
| **Stage** | **Clouds** | **Weather** |
|---|---|---|
| Approaching warm front | **Cirrus → cirrostratus → altostratus → nimbostratus** | Pressure falls; steady, widespread rain [GK] |
| Warm sector | Clearing, some stratus | Warmer, humid, rain eases [GK] |
| Cold front passes | **Cumulonimbus** | Heavy showers, thunder, wind shift; then cold, clear air [GK] |
# 4. Proponents → polar front theory
- **Proponents:** **Vilhelm and Jacob Bjerknes** (Bergen School, Norway), around the end of World War I
- **Core idea:** Temperate (wave) cyclones form along the **polar front** → cold polar air meets warm westerly air
- **Life cycle:** Wave on the front → warm and cold fronts → occlusion → dissipation
- **India link:** Western disturbances reach north-west India at the **occluded-front stage**
- ⚠ **Bjerknes** = polar front theory; **Bergeron** = air-mass classification

=== ROW 19 | Atmospheric disturbances - tropical cyclones, temperate cyclones, anticyclones, thunderstorms, tornadoes

## Tropical cyclones — meaning, formation conditions, limits of occurrence | from: S0
# 1. Nature & formation conditions
- **Definition:** Low-pressure centre with spiralling high-speed winds
- **Origin:** Only over **warm oceans** (not rivers or lakes - APPSC-GS 2025; PYQ 2025); draw energy from them
  - Lose energy on landfall → cut off from moisture (p.20)
- **Favourable conditions (2026):**
  - **SST above 26.5 °C** (some notes: **above 27 degC**) over a large sea surface
  - High humidity in lower and middle troposphere
  - **Low vertical wind shear** (small variation in vertical wind speed)
  - Enough **Coriolis force**
  - Pre-existing weak low / low-level cyclonic circulation
  - **Upper-level divergence**
# 2. Limits of occurrence
- **Belt:** Between **30 deg N and 30 deg S** (APPSC 2022 key); roughly **5-30° N and S**, western parts of tropical oceans
- **Do not form:**
  - Within about **5 deg of the equator** (Coriolis negligible)
  - **South Atlantic** and **south-east Pacific** (cold waters, strong shear)

## Cyclone and anticyclone structure — parts → feature; rotation and steering | from: S1, S5
# 1. Tropical cyclone parts
| **Part** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | Calm, clear, sinking air; lowest pressure (20-50 km across) [GK] |
| **Eyewall** | Ring of tallest, towering cumulonimbus clouds around the eye - **strongest winds and heaviest rain**[GK] |
| Rain bands | Spiral bands of cloud and rain outside the eyewall [GK] |
- ⚠ **Eye** = calm, lowest pressure; **eyewall** = strongest winds
# 2. Rotation - cyclone vs anticyclone
| **System** | **NH** | **SH** | **Pressure and winds** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cyclone | **Anticlockwise in the northern hemisphere** (**anticlockwise in the NH**) | Clockwise in the southern (clockwise in the SH) | Low at centre; Coriolis deflection (Nov 2025) |
| Anticyclone | **Clockwise in the NH** | Anticlockwise in the SH | High pressure at the centre; **outward, sinking (diverging) winds** |
- **Steering:** Mid/upper-troposphere winds
  - Trade winds push them west onto **eastern coasts**
  - Westerlies recurve mature storms north-east
# 3. Anticyclone weather
- **Weather:** **Clear, dry, calm** (settled) weather
- **Cold-core polar highs:** Siberian, Canadian, Antarctic → fog and frost (Jul 2025)
- **Heat dome:** Stalled warm high traps hot air (27 May 2026)

## Temperate (extra-tropical) vs tropical cyclones — features | from: S4
- **Origin:** **Mid and high latitudes** along the polar front
- **Fronts:** Have a **clear frontal system** (warm and cold fronts)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "They do not have a clear frontal system" = the incorrect statement (APPSC 2024; APPSC-G1 2024)
- **Area:** Cover a **larger area** than tropical cyclones
- **Origin surface:** Can **originate over land and sea** (PYQ 2024); tropical ones only over the sea
- **Movement:** **West to east** with the westerlies
- **Weather:** Winds weaker, but rain spread over a wide area and longer period
- **India's example:** **Western disturbances** from the Mediterranean
  - Carried by the sub-tropical westerly jet; reach NW India in winter at the occluded stage (UPSC Geo p.43; 28 Aug 2026)
| **Feature** | **Tropical** | **Temperate** |
|---|---|---|
| Latitude | 5-30° | **35-65°** (mid and high latitudes) [GK] |
| Origin | Only over warm seas | **Land and sea** |
| Fronts | None | **Clear frontal system** (warm, cold, occluded) |
| Size | Smaller, very intense | **Larger area**, gentler winds |
| Movement | East to west (trade winds) | West to east (westerlies) |
| Life | Days; die on land | Longer; cross continents |
- **Comparison rows:** Textbook

## North Indian Ocean cyclones — Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea, season, India's exposure data | from: S8
# 1. Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea
- **Bay of Bengal:** Produces **75-80%** of cyclones affecting India
  - Reasons: warmer (higher) SSTs + enclosed, funnel shape
  - Remnants of Pacific typhoons also regenerate there
- **Arabian Sea:** Fewer cyclones but becoming more/very intense (Tauktae, Biparjoy)
- **El Nino effect:** INCOIS (Hyderabad) study → **El Nino may reduce** post-monsoon Bay of Bengal cyclones (7 Jul 2026)
| **INCOIS (El Nino vs La Nina, 1995-2019)** | **Years** | **Bay of Bengal post-monsoon cyclones** |
|---|---|---|
| El Nino | **9** | Only **15** |
| La Nina | **11** | **33** |
# 2. Seasonality
- **North Indian Ocean season:** **Bimodal** (p.20)
  - Main peak = **post-monsoon (Oct-Dec)**
  - Secondary = **pre-monsoon (Apr-May)**
  - In the monsoon: storms at onset (June) and withdrawal (Sept)
# 3. India's exposure - data
- **Share:** About **10%** of the world's tropical cyclones (APPSC-GS 2023, on the old **7,516.6 km** coastline)
- **Coastline remeasured 2023-24:** **11,098.81 km** (**+47.7%**, finer **1:250,000** GIS scale)
- **Significance:** India's coastline **~8,000 km**; nearly **5,900 km** of the coast directly exposed to cyclones
- ⚠ **7,516.6 km** = old coastline; **11,098.81 km** = remeasured 2023-24
