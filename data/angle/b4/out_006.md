=== ROW 24 | Ocean movements - waves, tides (types and causes), ocean currents of the world

## Waves → terms and meaning | from: S0
# 1. Nature and parts
- **Nature:** Waves carry **energy, not water**; water particles move in small **circles**; **wind** supplies the energy; deep water barely affected

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Crest** | Top |
| **Trough** | Bottom |
| Wave **height** | Crest to trough |
| **Wavelength** | Crest to crest |
| **Period** | Time between crests |
| Steep waves | Young, local wind |
| **Swells** | Slow, steady, long-period waves from distant storms |
| **Breakers** | Waves slow by friction near shore; **break when depth < half the wavelength** |
| **Kallakkadal (Kerala swell surge)** | Long-period swells (30-300 s) from southern Indian Ocean storms ~**10,000 km** away; most frequent pre-monsoon |
| **Rip currents** | Narrow, fast channels flowing **away from shore** – shoreward water escapes through a narrow path |

- **Largest waves:** Open ocean
# 2. Wind waves vs tsunami

|  | **Wind waves** | **Tsunami ("harbour wave", Japanese)** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Wind on the surface | Sudden vertical displacement of the sea floor (quake, landslide, volcano) |
| Wavelength | Short | Extremely long |
| Speed | Slow | Up to **800 km/h** in deep ocean (CDI) |

- ⚠ Waves carry **energy, not water**; **Wavelength** = crest to crest, **height** = crest to trough

## Tides — causes, types and timing | from: S1
# 1. Causes and mechanism
- **Tide:** Periodic rise and fall of sea level **once or twice a day**
  - Gravitational pull of **moon** (greater) + **sun** (lesser); **centrifugal force** counters gravity
- **Two tidal bulges:**
  - Side facing the moon = gravitational attraction = **direct tide**
  - Opposite side = centrifugal force = **indirect tide**
- **Tide-generating force:** = gravitational attraction − centrifugal force; horizontal components matter more than vertical
- **Bulge height:** Higher over **wide continental shelves**, lower at mid-ocean islands
  - Channelled into bays/estuaries → **tidal currents**
- **Surges (storm surges):** Caused by winds + pressure changes – weather-driven, irregular, unlike tides
- ⚠ **Direct tide** = gravity (moon side); **indirect tide** = centrifugal force (opposite side)
# 2. Types and timing

| **Type** | **Condition / when** | **Range** |
|---|---|---|
| **Semi-diurnal** | Two high + two low tides a day (most common) | - |
| **Diurnal** | One high + one low tide a day | - |
| **Mixed** | Tides of unequal height | - |
| **Spring tide** | Sun, moon and earth in a line (**new moon and full moon**) | Highest range: highest high, lowest low |
| **Neap tide** | Sun and moon at right angles (first/third quarter, ~7 days after spring) | Lowest range |

- **Recurrence:** High tides about every **12 h 25 min**
- **Perigean tides:** Moon closest → unusually high
- ⚠ **Spring** = in a line (new/full moon); **Neap** = right angles

## Ocean currents — definition, drivers and gyres | from: S2, S3
# 1. Definition and drivers
- **Definition:** Steady volume of water moving along a set path
- **Drivers:** **Solar heating, wind, gravity, the Coriolis force** (right in N hemisphere, left in S) + **density differences**

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Planetary winds** | Wind friction pushes surface water; trade winds → equatorial currents westward; westerlies → drifts eastward |
| **Solar heating** | Water expands near equator → slight slope; **gravity** pulls water down the gradient |
| **Density differences** | Saltier, colder water denser → sinks; lighter water rises → deep (thermohaline) circulation; cold water sinks at poles, moves slowly to equator |

- **Equatorial Counter Current:** Flows **west to east** between North and South Equatorial Currents
  - Water piled on western side by trade winds flows back east through the calm **doldrums** belt
- **Rule of thumb:**
  - **Warm currents:** From equator towards poles along the surface; usually along **east coasts** of continents (low-mid latitudes)
  - **Cold currents:** From poles towards equator; usually along **west coasts**
# 2. Gyres
- **Gyres:** Large circular current systems in every ocean basin, formed by winds, Coriolis force and land barriers
  - **Clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere**, **anticlockwise in the Southern**

| **Ocean** | **Gyre components** [GK] |
|---|---|
| North Atlantic | N. Equatorial - Gulf Stream - N. Atlantic Drift - Canary |
| South Atlantic | S. Equatorial - Brazil - West Wind Drift - Benguela |
| North Pacific | N. Equatorial - Kuroshio - N. Pacific Drift - California |
| South Pacific | S. Equatorial - East Australian - West Wind Drift - Humboldt |

# 3. Sargasso Sea
- **Sargasso Sea:** In the **North Atlantic Ocean**; calm centre of the North Atlantic gyre
  - Only sea bounded by **currents, not land** ("sea without shores")
  - Boundaries: Gulf Stream (W), North Atlantic Drift (N), Canary (E), North Equatorial Current (S)
  - Calm, warm, salty; floating **Sargassum** seaweed

## Indian Ocean currents — seasonal reversal, WICC, South Indian Ocean gyre | from: S5
# 1. North Indian Ocean - monsoon reversal
- **Unique feature:** Currents **reverse direction with the monsoon winds** (unlike steady gyres of Atlantic and Pacific)
  - Summer: **SW Monsoon Current** flows **clockwise**
  - Winter: **NE Monsoon Current** flows **anticlockwise**

| **Season** | **Arabian Sea / Bay of Bengal circulation** | **West India Coastal Current (WICC)** | **East India Coastal Current (EICC)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| SW monsoon (summer) | Clockwise; SW Monsoon Current flows eastward [GK] | Flows **southward** (equatorward) [GK] | Flows northward (Feb-Sep, strongest pre-monsoon) [GK] |
| NE monsoon (winter) | Anticlockwise; NE Monsoon Current flows westward [GK] | Flows **northward, Nov-Feb** (poleward) | Flows southward (Oct-Dec) [GK] |

- **West India Coastal Current (WICC):** Flows **northward (poleward) in November-February**; turns southward during the SW monsoon (APPSC GS 2018 key)
- **East India Coastal Current:** Also reverses seasonally
- **Findlater Current (Arabian Sea):** Ocean current tied to the **Somali Jet**; reverses with monsoon winds
  - SW monsoon: **coastal upwelling off the Horn of Africa** (good fishing)
  - Upwelling zone small → limited effect on Indian monsoon (unlike the IOD)
# 2. South Indian Ocean
- **Gyre:** Permanent normal anticlockwise gyre = South Equatorial → Agulhas → West Wind Drift → West Australian
  - **Agulhas** (warm) along SE Africa; West Australian (cold) along Australia

## Effects of currents → places (climate, deserts, fishing grounds, fog) | from: S6
- **Climate (warm):** **North Atlantic Drift** (Gulf Stream extension) keeps **NW Europe** (UK, Norway) coasts mild + ports ice-free in winter (e.g. **Murmansk**)
- **Climate (cold):** Cold currents along west coasts chill/dry coastal lands → **deserts**; can bring cold weather to coasts (UPSC p.161)

| Place | Current(s) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Atacama | Humboldt | Desert |
| Namib/Kalahari | Benguela | Desert |
| Sonoran | California | Desert |
| **Grand Banks, Newfoundland** | Gulf Stream + Labrador | Fishing ground; fog |
| Off **Japan** | Kuroshio + Oyashio | Fishing ground |
| Peru | Humboldt | Upwelling - fisheries |
| Somalia | Findlater | Upwelling - fisheries |

- **Fishing grounds:** Where warm + cold currents meet, plankton flourishes
- **Upwelling:** Cold, nutrient-rich water (Peru/Humboldt, Somalia/Findlater) supports fisheries
- **Fog:** Forms where warm and cold currents meet (e.g. Newfoundland)
- **Climate change link:** Disrupted ocean circulation alters global temperature distribution (a natural cause)

## ENSO — terms, persons and indices | from: S7
# 1. El Nino and La Nina
- **El Nino:** Warm current flows south along the **Peru** coast, replacing the cold Peruvian current
  - Recurrence: about every **3-7 years**; 2026 notes say **2-7 years**
  - Name = "Christ child"
- **La Nina:** Cold phase; **trade winds strengthen**; central-eastern Pacific cooler than normal
- **Mechanism:** Weaker easterlies → warm western-Pacific water slides east → trades weaken further (**Bjerknes feedback**) → Walker circulation disrupted → sinking air over Indian Ocean = high pressure

|  | **El Nino** | **La Nina** |
|---|---|---|
| Trade winds | Weaken or reverse | Stronger than usual |
| Eastern Pacific (Peru) | Warmer; upwelling suppressed, **fewer phytoplankton** | Cooler; strong upwelling |
| Indian monsoon | Delayed, weakened (drought risk) | Usually good rain |

# 2. Definitions and data
- **IMD definition:** Central equatorial Pacific at least **0.5 deg C** warmer than average for five consecutive overlapping three-month periods
- **Monsoon link:** **6 of 10** El Nino years brought deficient Indian rainfall
- **Last El Nino:** **2023-24**
- **Super El Nino:** SST anomaly above **+2.0 deg C** in the central Pacific
> Exam angle: "El Nino and La Nina are storms" is the false statement (RPSC 2024). They are climate patterns.
# 3. Southern Oscillation and SOI
- **Southern Oscillation:** Atmospheric pressure **seesaw between the Pacific and Indian Oceans**
  - **Tahiti** (Pacific) vs **Port Darwin** (Australia)
  - High pressure over equatorial South Pacific ↔ low pressure over South Indian Ocean, and vice versa
  - Described by **Sir Gilbert Walker** in **1920** (**1920s**)
- **ENSO components:** **El Nino/La Nina = oceanic component; Southern Oscillation = atmospheric counterpart**
- **SOI:** = pressure difference **Tahiti minus Darwin**
  - Sustained **negative SOI = El Nino**
  - **Positive SOI = La Nina**
- ⚠ **Negative SOI** = El Nino; **Positive SOI** = La Nina; **Walker** = Southern Oscillation

=== ROW 25 | Ocean-atmosphere interaction - El Nino, La Nina, ENSO, Indian Ocean Dipole, Walker circulation

## El Nino — definition, types and terms | from: S0, S1
# 1. Definition and mechanism
- **Definition:** Abnormal warming of the **central and eastern equatorial Pacific**
  - Unusual **warm current flows southwards along the Peru (west South American) coast**, replacing the cold **Peruvian (Humboldt)** current
  - Ocean: **Pacific Ocean**
- **Name:** "The little boy / Christ child" – appears around **Christmas**
- **Recurrence:** Every **3-7 years**; some notes say **2-7 years**
- **Mechanism:** Easterly **trade winds weaken or reverse** → warm water piled in western Pacific shifts east → warming weakens winds further (**Bjerknes feedback**)
# 2. Types

| Type | Maximum warming |
|---|---|
| **Normal (canonical) El Nino** | Maximum warming in the **eastern Pacific** near Peru |
| **El Nino Modoki ("similar but different", Japanese)** | Maximum warming in the **central Pacific**; cooler waters on both eastern and western sides (tripole) |

- ⚠ **Canonical** = eastern Pacific; **Modoki** = central Pacific

## El Nino and La Nina → impacts and contrast | from: S0, S2
# 1. El Nino impacts
- **Off Peru:** Upwelling of cold nutrient-rich water stops → **fewer phytoplankton**, fish catch collapses
- **India:** Rising air shifts east, air descends over Indian Ocean → **high pressure there, delayed/weak Indian monsoon**
  - Drought; lower summer-crop (rice, sugarcane) yields; food inflation; harsher heat
- **Record:** **6 of 10** El Nino years brought depressed Indian rainfall; last global El Nino = **2023-24**
> Exam angle: El Nino is a climate pattern, **not a storm**; statement sets on Peru warm current, 3-7-year recurrence and meaning of the name.
# 2. La Nina conditions
- **Conditions:** Cold phase of ENSO; **trade winds strengthen**
  - Warm water pushed further west towards Asia
  - **Cold water upwells in the eastern Pacific** (~half-degree cooling)
# 3. El Nino vs La Nina

| **Feature** | **El Nino** | **La Nina** |
|---|---|---|
| Central/east Pacific SST | Warmer | Cooler |
| Trade winds | Weak/reversed | **Stronger than usual** |
| Indian monsoon | Weak, below normal | Normal to above normal |
| North India winter | - | **Colder winters, frequent cold waves** |
| Bay of Bengal | Fewer post-monsoon cyclones (INCOIS study) | More floods and severe cyclones |
| Australia/Indonesia | Drought [GK] | Heavy rain, floods [GK] |

- **2025:** **2025 was the warmest La Nina year on record** – notable as La Nina normally cools global temperature

## Walker circulation and ENSO monitoring | from: S3, S4
# 1. Walker circulation
- **Normal:** East-west (**zonal**) circulation over the tropical Pacific
  - Rising air over warm **western Pacific/Indonesia**
  - Sinking over cool **eastern Pacific**
  - Surface **easterly trade winds** close the loop
- **El Nino:** **El Nino weakens and shifts the Walker Circulation eastward** → less convection over India
- **La Nina:** **La Nina strengthens** it (linked to unusual cold over East, Central and South India)
- ⚠ El Nino **weakens**, La Nina **strengthens** the Walker circulation
# 2. Monitoring
- **Nino 3.4 region:** 5°N-5°S, 120°W-170°W in the central equatorial Pacific
- **Satellites:** NASA's **Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich** tracks Kelvin waves – early El Nino signal

## IOD and MJO — discoverers; IOD phases and monsoon link | from: S5, S6
# 1. Discoverer → phenomenon

| Phenomenon | Discovered / identified | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Indian Ocean Dipole** | **Discovered 1999** | SST difference between **western pole (Arabian Sea)** and **eastern pole (eastern Indian Ocean south of Indonesia)** |
| **Madden-Julian Oscillation** | **Madden and Julian, 1971-72** | **Eastward-moving** pulse (belt) of clouds, convection and rain; starts as a warm pool in the tropical **Indian Ocean** |

# 2. Indian Ocean Dipole
- **Measure:** **Dipole Mode Index (DMI)**
  - Late May 2026 DMI value **-0.34 °C** = neutral
- **Cycle:** Develops **April-May, peaks in October**

| **Phase** | **SST pattern** | **Monsoon effect** |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive IOD** | Western Indian Ocean warmer; winds blow **east to west** (Bay of Bengal to Arabian Sea) | **More rain** over India - can offset El Nino |
| **Negative IOD** | Western (Arabian Sea) cooler, eastern warmer | **Reduced** rainfall over India |

- **1997:** Strong positive IOD saved India from drought despite a Super El Nino
- **Relation to ENSO:** IOD runs independently of ENSO (separate phenomenon)
  - Some notes link positive IOD with La Nina and negative with El Nino
- ⚠ **Positive IOD** = more rain (offsets El Nino)

## Madden-Julian Oscillation → features and monsoon effect | from: S6
- **Periodicity:** Every **30-60 days**
- **Convection height:** Up to **10 km**
- **Path:** Fades in the eastern Pacific but circles the globe aloft
- **Monsoon effect:** Shifts heavy-rain zone eastward → active/break spells
- ⚠ **MJO** = **30-60 days**, eastward; **IOD** = 1999

## Universal angles | from: S7 | universal
- **Current relevance (IMD forecast, April 2026):**
  - **Below-normal monsoon, 92% of LPA (87 cm)** due to a developing El Nino
  - First "below normal" April warning in **11 years**
- **Current relevance (INCOIS, Hyderabad, 2026):** ENSO governs the number of **post-monsoon (Oct-Dec) Bay of Bengal cyclones**; El Nino may reduce them
- **AP angle:**
  - Fewer/more Bay of Bengal cyclones + weak monsoon years hit coastal AP and rain-fed Rayalaseema farming
  - NIOT OMNI buoys off Nellore support IOD monitoring
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Walker (1920s) first described the Southern Oscillation
  - IOD identified in 1999
  - Last El Nino 2023-24

## Plankton and upwelling zones → places | from: S2, S3
# 1. Plankton
- **Primary producers:** **Phytoplankton** (diatoms, dinoflagellates, cyanobacteria) = main **primary producers** of the ocean
- **Oxygen:** Oceans, mainly phytoplankton, produce **about half (50%+) of Earth's oxygen** - more than all rainforests combined
# 2. Upwelling zones
- **Upwelling:** Cold, nutrient-rich subsurface water rises along coasts where winds push surface water offshore → plankton boom → rich fisheries

| **Upwelling zone** | **Associated current / season** |
|---|---|

- **El Nino effect:** Suppresses Peru upwelling → fewer phytoplankton, fishery collapse

## Coral reefs — composition, conditions, types and features | from: S4
# 1. Composition and symbiosis
- **Builders:** Skeletons of colonial marine invertebrates = tiny **coral polyps**
- **Chemical component:** **calcium carbonate** (aragonite) skeleton
- **Symbiosis:** **mutualistic symbiosis** (**mutualism**) with **zooxanthellae** (single-celled algae)
  - Algae: photosynthesise → supply food/energy and colour
  - Coral: gives shelter and nutrients
# 2. Growth conditions
- **Temperature:** Warm water (**above ~20 deg C**; about **20-21 °C** minimum, optimum 23-25 °C)
- **Water:** **clear, shallow, sunlit** (usually < ~50 m), sediment-free, normal salinity
- **Location:** Mostly within the tropics, roughly 30 deg N-30 deg S; avoid muddy river mouths
# 3. Types

| **Type** | **Feature** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Fringing reef** | Grows close to the shore, shallow lagoon or none | Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kutch |
| **Barrier reef** | Parallel to the coast, separated by a wide, deep lagoon | **Great Barrier Reef** (Australia) - Queensland, Coral Sea |
| **Atoll** | Ring-shaped reef enclosing a lagoon, no central island | **Lakshadweep** (India's only true atolls) |

# 4. Epithet, roles and services
- **Epithet:** **"Rainforests of the Sea"**
  - Cover **< 1% of the ocean floor** yet support **> 25% of all marine species**
  - With tropical rainforests: highest species richness
- **Roles/services:**
  - **Break wave power during storms, preventing coastal erosion**; protection from storm surges
  - Nurseries/spawning grounds; fisheries; tourism; biodiversity
> Exam angle: APPSC 2024 paired "rainforests of the sea" with "breaks wave power" - both true but the second does not explain the first.

## Coral bleaching — meaning, triggers and events (data) | from: S6
# 1. Meaning
- **Definition:** Corals turn **white after expelling zooxanthellae** (polyps **expel symbiotic zooxanthellae**) and lose pigments (APPSC-G2 2017); stressed corals expel their symbiotic algae
- **Status:** Bleached coral is stressed, not necessarily dead; prone to starvation/disease
# 2. Triggers
- **Primary cause:** **rise in sea surface temperature** / rising SST and **marine heatwaves** (now the **strongest global driver** of coral loss)
- **Other triggers:**
  - Pollution and runoff, high UV, disease, storms/cyclones
  - **Extremely low tides**, ocean acidification
  - Crown-of-thorns starfish, coral mining, tourism
# 3. Global bleaching events

| **Global bleaching event** | **Year** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **1998** | Strong El Nino |
| 2nd | **2010** | - |
| 3rd | **2014-2017** | 68.2% of reef areas hit |
| 4th | 2023 - **ended ~mid-2025** | Fastest, most extensive: **84%** of reef areas in **83** countries (NOAA) |

- **El Nino link:** Since 1998 every strong **El Nino** has coincided with a global bleaching event
- **Recovery:** Large-scale bleaching takes **more than 15 years**
- **Frequency (GCRMN 2026):** Since 2010 major disturbances recur every **4-6 years** vs once a decade earlier
# 4. Indian events
- **Feb 2025:** Bleaching in Gulf of Mannar, Palk Bay, Lakshadweep, **Andaman & Nicobar**, Gulf of Kachchh
- **Andaman mass bleaching:** Linked to about a **2°C rise** in Indian Ocean temperature (UPPSC 2024 key)

## Ocean acidification and algal blooms → terms and data | from: S7
# 1. Ocean acidification
- **Definition:** Falling ocean pH (seawater is alkaline, **pH ~8.1**) from CO2 uptake
- **Epithets:** **"evil twin of global warming"** / "the other CO2 problem"
- **Chemistry:** CO2 + water → **carbonic acid** → bicarbonate + hydronium ions; carbonate ions fall
  - Result: **corals, plankton and shellfish struggle to build calcium carbonate shells** (calcifying organisms) – weakens calcifiers
- **Data:**
  - Oceans absorb about **30-40%** of anthropogenic CO2 (some notes say >25%)
  - Acidity up **30%** since the Industrial Revolution; may rise **150%** by 2100
- **Worst areas:** Cold temperate seas (higher CO2 solubility) + melting Arctic
# 2. Algal blooms and red tides

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Harmful algal blooms** | Excess nitrogen and phosphorus (farm runoff, sewage) → eutrophication → bloom dies and decomposes → oxygen stripped → **dead zones**; blooms block sunlight for corals and seagrass |
| **Red tide** | Toxic bloom (mostly dinoflagellates); discolours water, kills fish, contaminates shellfish |

- **Dead zone examples:** **Gulf of Mexico** (Mississippi runoff); **Chesapeake Bay**
- **INCOIS:** Issues **algal bloom and coral bleaching alerts**
