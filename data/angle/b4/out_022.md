=== ROW 85 | Industrial location - factors and theories (Weber); world industrial regions

## Theorists → location theories | from: S0, S1
| **Theorist** | **Idea** |
|---|---|
| **Alfred Weber (1909)** | **Least-cost theory** - firm locates where total **transport + labour + agglomeration** costs are least |
| **Edgar Hoover** | Transport-cost structure, terminal costs; ports and break-of-bulk points favoured [GK] |
| **Walter Isard** | **Substitution principle** - trade-off between transport and other costs [GK] |
| **August Losch** | Maximum-profit / market-area (demand) approach [GK] |
| **P. Sargant Florence** | **Location Quotient** - a region's share of an industry vs its national share [GK] |
- ⚠ **Weber** = least cost; **Losch** = maximum profit / market area; **Isard** = substitution; **Florence** = Location Quotient

## Location terms → meaning | from: S0, S2
# 1. Weber's terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Locational triangle** | Two raw-material sources + one market; least-transport-cost point lies inside it |
| **Material Index (MI)** | **MI = weight of localised raw materials / weight of finished product** |
| **Isodapanes** | Lines of equal extra transport cost; labour or agglomeration savings pull a firm off the least-transport point |
- **MI > 1** → locate at raw material
- **MI < 1** → at market
# 2. Material types
| **Material type** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Ubiquities** | Available everywhere | Water, air [GK] |
| **Pure localised** | Lose no weight | Cotton [GK] |
| **Gross (weight-losing)** | Lose weight in processing | Iron ore, coal, sugarcane [GK] |
- **Cotton textiles:** **Non-weight-losing** → not bound to raw-material sites
# 3. Footloose industries and agglomeration economies
- **Footloose industries:** Not tied to a raw material or a fixed location
  - Light, high-value, low transport cost, low pollution
  - e.g. **diamond cutting**, electronics, **software**, watch-making
  - PYQ (APPSC-GS 2019 key): **Diamond**
- **Agglomeration economies:** Cost savings when firms cluster
  - Shared infrastructure, specialised suppliers, skilled labour pool
  - e.g. **Mumbai-Pune, Chennai-Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR** clusters

=== ROW 86 | India - industrial regions and industrial belts; factors of localisation

## Factors of location and industry orientation | from: S0
# 1. Factors
- **Factors:** **Raw material** (cuts transport cost), **power**, **market**, **labour** (skilled and cheap), **transport** (roads, railheads, ports), water, **government policy** (tax breaks, subsidies, SEZs), clustering, environment
- **Indian examples:**
  - Minerals (Chotanagpur steel, coal)
  - **Ports**: east-coast ports Chennai, Vizag, Paradip, Tuticorin serve mineral + petroleum hinterlands
  - Power; cheap labour (West Bengal); market; government policy; clustering
- **Steel belt:** On the **Chota Nagpur Plateau** (Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal) - raw materials are weight-losing
# 2. Orientation of industries
| **Orientation** | **Located near** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| Raw-material (weight-losing, perishable) | - | iron & steel (~4 t input per t), sugar (cane), cement (limestone) |
| Power | - | aluminium (14,000-15,000 kWh/t) |
| **Market based** (weight-gaining, fragile) | Consumers | Bakery, soft drinks [GK] |
| **Footloose** (market / footloose) | Anywhere with skills and links | cotton textiles (cotton is a "pure" material), software, electronics |
# 3. Footloose industries
- **Footloose industries:** Light inputs, negligible transport cost; depend on internet, logistics, skilled people
  - e.g. **diamond polishing, electronics assembly, software**
  - Clusters: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Noida
- **PYQs:**
  - Footloose = **software / diamond** (APPSC 2019)
  - Raw material not the main factor for **electronics** (UPPSC 2015)
# 4. Weber's least-cost theory
- **Alfred Weber (1909):** Plant locates where total transport cost is lowest, then adjusts for labour + agglomeration
- **MI > 1:** Weight-losing (e.g. iron & steel, sugar) → near raw material
- **MI < 1:** Or ubiquitous inputs (water) → near market
- **Pure raw material:** No weight loss (e.g. cotton) → free to locate anywhere between source and market
