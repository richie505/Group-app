=== ROW 10 | Earthquakes - causes, seismic waves, measurement, distribution, effects

## Earthquake terms → meaning — focus, epicentre, depth, seismic doublet | from: S0, S1
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Focus / hypocentre** | Point inside Earth where rupture starts and energy is released; shallow or deep |
| **Epicentre** | Point on surface directly above focus; strongest shaking |
| **Seismic doublet** | Two independent quakes of **similar magnitude**, in quick succession, close together |
| Aftershock sequence | Tapers off (vs doublet) |
- **Depth:** typical crustal quakes 10-29 km
  - Rare **continental mantle earthquakes** start **80+ km below the Moho** vs ordinary crustal quakes at 10-29 km; Stanford made first global map
- **Seismic doublet example:** **Venezuela (M 7.1 and 7.5)** on Caribbean-South American **strike-slip** boundary; toll **589 dead**

## Causes of earthquakes — type → cause → example | from: S1
| **Type** | **Cause** | **Indian / other examples** |
|---|---|---|
| **Tectonic** | Stress on a **fault** builds along plate boundaries until it exceeds rock strength and the rock ruptures → seismic waves; at divergent (Mid-Atlantic Ridge), convergent (Himalaya, Ring of Fire) or transform (San Andreas) boundaries | Himalayas; **Bhuj 2001** (Kutch) [GK]; San Andreas (transform) |
| **Volcanic** | Rising magma fractures rock; volcanic structures collapse | Ring of Fire volcanoes (UPSC) |
| **Reservoir-induced** | Weight of water when large reservoirs are filled | **Koyna 1967** (Maharashtra) [GK] = India's classic case |
| **Intraplate** | Old hidden faults within a **stable continental region** (stable shields) | **Latur (Killari) 1993**, Koyna, **Jabalpur** (CDI) |
| **Induced (other)** | Mining, **fracking**; usually low magnitude | - |
- **Latur 1993**: far from any plate boundary → quakes strike **stable areas along hidden faults** (APPSC 2025)
- ⚠ **Koyna** = reservoir-induced classic; **Latur** = intraplate on hidden faults

## Seismic waves → arrival, motion, medium; shadow zone | from: S2
| **Wave** | **Arrival** | **Nature / particle motion** | **Medium** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P (primary)** | **1st** (fastest, arrives first) | Longitudinal - push-pull; compressional: **back and forth along** the direction of travel | Solid, liquid, gas |
| **S (secondary)** | **2nd** (slower) | Transverse - side to side; shear: **perpendicular** to the direction of travel | **Solids only** - their loss in the outer core shows it is molten |
| **Surface (L)** (Love, Rayleigh) | **Last** (slowest) | Move along the surface; **Love**: side to side; **Rayleigh**: rolling, like ocean waves | Along the surface; **cause most damage** |
- **Shadow zone:** No S-waves beyond about **105°** from the epicentre → proves the **outer core is liquid**
- ⚠ **Love** = side to side; **Rayleigh** = rolling

## Magnitude vs intensity — scales → persons and instruments → use | from: S3
# 1. Magnitude vs intensity
|  | **Magnitude** | **Intensity** |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | **Energy released at the focus** | **Observed effects** (damage) on people, buildings and ground **at a place** |
| Nature | Quantitative; one value per quake (APPSC 2025) | Qualitative; **varies from place to place**, falling with distance from the epicentre |
| Scale | **Richter scale** (Charles F. Richter, **1935**); now **Moment Magnitude (Mw)** | **Modified Mercalli (MMI)** and **MSK** scales, both **I to XII**; MMI runs from **I (not felt) to XII (total destruction)** |
# 2. Scales → features
- **Richter scale:** **logarithmic**; measures wave amplitude
  - Each **+1** = **10x amplitude** (10 times) + about **31x energy** (**31 times**)
- **Mw:** based on **rupture area** of the fault + size of seismic waves; has largely replaced Richter for strong quakes
# 3. Instruments → use
| **Instrument** | **Use** |
|---|---|
| **Seismograph** | Records and measures earthquakes (APPSC 2025) |
| Seismometer | The sensor of a seismograph; broadband seismometers in India's national network |
| Seismoscope | Only shows that a tremor occurred and its time |
| Tiltmeter | Ground tilt at faults and volcanoes |
| Creepmeter | Slow slip (creep) along a fault |
> Exam angle: "Richter scale measures intensity" is wrong - it measures **magnitude**; earthquakes still cannot be predicted accurately (APPSC 2022).
> Exam angle: magnitude = energy at the focus; intensity = effect at a place (APPSC 2025).

=== ROW 11 | Weathering, mass wasting and soil formation; denudation and the cycle of erosion

## Denudation and weathering — process → what happens; weathering landforms | from: S0
# 1. Denudation
- **Sequence:** Denudation = **weathering → mass wasting/erosion → transportation → deposition**
- **Agents:** Water (most powerful), wind, ice, gravity
# 2. Weathering process → what happens
| **Process** | **What happens / example** |
|---|---|
| Physical (mechanical) | Rock breaks without chemical change - frost wedging, heating and cooling (exfoliation), salt crystal growth [GK] |
| Solution | Soluble minerals (rock salt, gypsum) dissolve [GK] |
| Carbonation | CO2 in rain forms **carbonic acid**, which dissolves limestone; carbon stored as bicarbonate (LENS Jul 2025) |
| Hydration | Minerals absorb water and swell, e.g. anhydrite to gypsum [GK] |
| Hydrolysis | Water reacts with silicate minerals - feldspar to clay; muscovite's potassium replaced by hydronium to form **vermiculite** (APPSC 2024) |
| Oxidation | Iron minerals rust - red, yellow soils [GK] |
| Biological | Roots, burrowing animals, lichens and micro-organisms break and decay rock [GK] |
- **Fastest:** In hot, wet climates
- **Freeze-thaw:** Breaks rocks in the high Himalaya
- ⚠ **Hydration** = swelling (anhydrite → gypsum); **hydrolysis** = reaction (feldspar → clay, muscovite → **vermiculite**)
# 3. Weathering landforms → meaning
| **Landform** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Exfoliation domes** | Onion-like peeling of granite |
| **Tors** | Granite block piles |
| **Block disintegration** | Along joints from temperature change |

## Mass wasting — type → nature; conditions and regions | from: S1
- **Definition:** Downslope movement of earth material under **gravity** = landslide (APPSC 2019)
| **Type** | **Nature** |
|---|---|
| Rockfall | Free fall of rocks from steep cliffs |
| Slide | Coherent mass slips along a surface |
| Slump | Rotational slip along a curved surface in weak, saturated soils |
| Debris flow / mudflow | Fast water-soil-rock mix, after heavy rain or snowmelt |
| Creep | Very slow movement by wetting-drying and heating-cooling |
- **Conditions:** Slopes above **30 degrees**, weak/fractured rock, bare slopes
- **Triggers:** Rainfall (SW monsoon) main trigger in India → earthquakes next
- **Most at risk:** Himalaya and North-East
- ⚠ **Slump** = rotational, curved surface; **creep** = very slow

## Geomorphologists → cycle of erosion; peneplain, monadnock, inselberg | from: S2
# 1. Proponent → theory
| **Person** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **W.M. Davis (1899)** | Landscape = structure + process + stage; stages **youth → maturity → old age**; end = low, almost flat **peneplain** + resistant residual hills = **monadnocks** |
| **W. Penck (Walther Penck)** | Landform depends on ratio of uplift to erosion, not time-stages; uplift and erosion act together; slopes retreat parallel |
- ⚠ **Davis** = time-stages; **Penck** = uplift vs erosion, parallel slope retreat
# 2. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **peneplain** | Low, almost flat end-surface of the Davis cycle |
| **monadnocks** | Resistant residual hills on a peneplain |
| **Inselbergs** | Residual island-hills of arid/semi-arid regions; formed by desert erosion (wind + sheet-wash) |
- **Indian example:** Peninsular plateau = old **peneplain**, rejuvenated in places

- **River deposits:** Silt of the Godavari, Krishna, Pennar
- **Climate contrast:** Coastal rain vs Rayalaseema aridity

## Soil texture data and soils → climate region; in-situ vs transported | from: S4, S5
# 1. Soil texture - particle size and water holding
| **Particle** | **Size** |
|---|---|
| Sand | 2-0.05 mm |
| Silt | 0.05-0.002 mm |
| Clay | Below 0.002 mm |
- **Water-holding capacity:** **Clay > silt > sand**
  - Clay and silt get saturated (landslide-prone); sand drains well
  - Clay binds organic carbon; sandy soils lose it by leaching (Nov 2025)
- **Rainforest soils:** Poor despite lush forest - heavy rain **leaches** nutrients + dead matter decays fast
# 2. Zonal soils → climate / vegetation
| **Soil** | **Climate / vegetation** |
|---|---|
| **Podzol** | Cold taiga (boreal conifer forests); thin, acidic, leached, humus-poor |
| **Laterite** | Hot, heavy-rain tropics; intense leaching of silica and lime leaves iron and aluminium oxides; acidic |
| **Terra rossa** | Mediterranean, over limestone; red [GK] |
| **Chernozem** | Temperate grasslands (steppes, prairies); black, humus-rich [GK] |
| **Oxisols** | Equatorial-tropical land surfaces stable for very long periods (APPSC 2019) |
- ⚠ **Podzol** = cold taiga; **Chernozem** = temperate grassland; **Laterite** = hot heavy-rain tropics
# 3. In-situ vs transported
| **Type** | **Formation / region** |
|---|---|
| **Residual / in-situ (sedentary)** | Form where the rock weathered; mainly Peninsular India; APPSC 2018: in-situ = **sedentary soil** |
| **Transported / drifted (ex-situ)** | Moved by rivers, wind, ice; mainly North India; alluvium covers **45.6%** of India |

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Current relevance:**
  - FAO's **Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026** (75 countries): soil biodiversity loss ranked **4th** of seven threats, after erosion, loss of soil organic carbon and nutrient mismanagement (3 Sep 2026)
  - Steep, glacier-fed Himalayan catchments release CO2 through pyrite oxidation (13 Sep 2026)
- **AP angle - soil shares (Jul 2026):**
  - Red soil ~60% of cultivated land (Rayalaseema); older notes say red 66%
  - Black (regur) ~25%
  - Deltaic alluvium ~6%; older notes say alluvium 5%
  - Hill (skeletal) ~5%
  - Laterite ~1%
- **Significance:**
  - Water = most powerful agent of soil erosion (sheet, rill, gully - Chambal ravines)
  - Wind erosion dominates the Thar

=== ROW 12 | Major landforms - fluvial landforms and river profiles

## River course → work and features; profile terms → meaning | from: S0
# 1. Course (stage) → dominant work → features
| **Course (stage)** | **Dominant work** | **Typical features** |
|---|---|---|
| Upper (youth) | Vertical erosion, steep gradient | V-shaped valleys, **gorges, canyons**, waterfalls, rapids, potholes |
| Middle (maturity) | Lateral erosion + transport | Wider valleys, meanders, floodplains begin |
| Lower (old age) | Deposition, gentle gradient | Broad floodplains, levees, oxbow lakes, braided channels, deltas/estuaries |
- **APPSC 2022:** A **canyon** forms in the upper course (floodplain, alluvial fan, delta do not)
# 2. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Graded profile** | Smooth concave long profile; erosion = deposition |
| **Base level** | Lowest level a river can erode to; **Sea level** = ultimate base level; lakes and dams = local base levels |
| **Stream capture (river piracy)** | Headward-eroding stream breaks into a neighbour's basin and diverts its upper waters |
| **Drainage divide** | Ridge separating basins; sharp in young relief (Himalaya), faint on old plateaus |

## Erosional landforms → formation; gorge vs canyon; ravines → region | from: S1, S2
# 1. Landform → formation
| **Landform** | **Formation** |
|---|---|
| **V-shaped valley** | Rills → gullies → valleys by vertical erosion |
| **Waterfall** | River drops over a resistant rock band or fault step |
| **Potholes** | Rock fragments whirled by eddies drill circular holes in the bed (pothole-drilling); large one below a waterfall = plunge pool |
| **Incised (entrenched) meanders** | Cut deep into hard rock when uplift rejuvenates the river |
| **Gully / badland** | Gully = water-worn channel of semi-arid areas; overland flow → rills → rills merge and deepen → gullies; dense gully network = **badland** topography |
# 2. Gorge vs canyon (Grand Canyon)
| **Gorge** | **Canyon** |
|---|---|
| Deep valley with very steep to straight sides | Steep, **step-like** side slopes |
| Almost the same width at top and bottom | **Wider at the top** than at the bottom |
| Forms in **hard rocks** | Forms in **horizontally bedded sedimentary rocks** |
- **Relation:** Both are forms of the V-shaped valley; canyon = **variant of a gorge** (not the reverse)
  - ⚠ **Trap (APPSC 2018):** "Gorges and canyons are V-shaped valleys" correct; "gorges are extended forms of canyons" WRONG
# 3. Places → erosional landform
| **Place** | **Region** | **Feature** |
|---|---|---|
| **Grand Canyon** | USA | Cut by the **Colorado River** |
| **Dihang gorge** | Arunachal (Brahmaputra entry) | Brahmaputra enters India here; called "Grand Canyon of the Tsangpo" (Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon, near Namcha Barwa) |
| **Chambal valley ravines** | Central India | India's classic badland (APPSC 2022: Chambal basin) |
| **Chos of Hoshiarpur** | Punjab | Gullied badland |
> Exam angle: badland region picked from options (Chambal vs Rann of Kutch, Godavari delta, western coastal plain).

## Depositional landforms → formation; meanders and terraces | from: S3
| **Landform** | **How it forms** |
|---|---|
| Alluvial fan | River leaving steep hills drops its coarse load at the break of slope, spreading it as a fan/cone [GK] |
| Natural levee | Raised banks on both sides of the channel built by sand deposited at the edges; a natural barrier between channel and floodplain |
| Floodplain | Flat land beside the channel built by fine alluvium dropped when the river overflows [GK] |
| Braided channel | Sand deposits in the channel split it into many parallel threads (riverine islands/bars) |
| Delta | Final depositional form at the mouth where the river meets the sea |
| Oxbow lake | Sand deposited on the concave side steepens a meander loop until it is cut off; the stagnant water left is the oxbow |
- **Meander:** Outer bend eroded (cliff, concave) vs inner slip-off side built up (convex)
- **River terraces:**
  - Mark old valley-floor/floodplain levels; formed when a stream cuts **vertically into its own floodplain**
  - Paired = same height on both banks; unpaired = one bank only (areas of slow uplift)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Made by down-cutting, so NCERT lists them among **erosional** landforms → APPSC 2023 key on "depositional activity of Himalayan rivers" = **braided channels, alluvial fans, natural levees** (terraces excluded)

## Delta types → river; estuaries; largest delta | from: S4
| **Type** | **Shape** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Arcuate (fan) | Most common, arc-shaped | **Nile**, Ganga (Sundarbans) |
| Bird's foot | Long branching distributaries | **Mississippi** |
| Cuspate | Tooth/cusp-shaped | **Tiber** (Italy) |
| Estuarine | No delta; drowned funnel-shaped mouth | Narmada, Tapi [GK] |
- ⚠ **Nile** = arcuate; **Mississippi** = bird's foot; **Tiber** = cuspate
- **Conditions:** Slow, steady flow so silt settles (not all rivers form deltas) + heavy sediment load + shallow sea + weak tides and currents
- **West-flowing Deccan rivers:** Form estuaries, not deltas - mainly **steep gradient** (hard rock, short courses)
- **Estuary:** Partly enclosed river mouth with free connection to the sea (an ecotone); most formed in the **Holocene** sea-level rise
- **Largest delta:** **Ganga-Brahmaputra** (Sundarbans: world's largest mangrove forest)

## Drainage patterns → rock and place | from: S5
| **Pattern** | **Form / rock / relief** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Dendritic | Uniform rock; tree-like | Rivers of the northern plains |
| **Trellis** | Folded rocks; tributaries parallel, meeting (joining) at right angles | Appalachians; old folded belts; old folded Himalaya; Singhbhum |
| Radial | Streams flow outward from a central high | **Amarkantak** (Narmada, Son, Mahanadi) |
| Centripetal | Streams drain into a central depression | **Loktak** lake, Manipur |
| **Rectangular** | Jointed/faulted rock; right-angle bends along joints and faults in hard rock | Joint-controlled plateaus; jointed peninsular rocks |
| Annular | Eroded dome with rings of hard and soft rock | Structural domes |
- ⚠ **Amarkantak** = radial (outward); **Loktak** = centripetal (inward)

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Current relevance:** Sinking deltas (Apr 2026)
  - Seven major deltas (Ganga-Brahmaputra, Nile, Mekong, Yangtze, Amazon, Irrawaddy, Mississippi) = about **57%** of world's sinking delta area
  - Deltas cover 1% of land but hold ~6% of people + 10 of 34 megacities
  - Main causes: groundwater extraction + reduced silt
- **AP angle:** Coastal plain
  - ~950 km long, ~60 km wide, up to 150 m
  - Built by **Godavari** (delta = **Konaseema**) and **Krishna** deltas
  - Minor deltas: **Vamsadhara** (Srikakulam) and **Penna** (Nellore)
- **Kolleru:** Freshwater lake between the two big deltas; Kolleru WLS **308.55 sq km** (2024-25 annexure; older notes say 250 sq km)
- **Pulicat:** Saltwater lagoon on the AP-TN border, well south of the Penna mouth near Nellore (some notes place it at the Penna mouth)
