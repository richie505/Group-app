=== ROW 0 | The universe and the solar system - origin theories, the Sun, planets, asteroids, comets, meteors

## Universe and solar system — definitions, ages, units and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1, S3, S5, S10
# 1. Definitions and ages
- **Cosmology:** Study of the origin, evolution and structure of the universe
- **Standard Model of cosmology:** Big Bang + dark matter + dark energy + CMB
- **Age of the universe:** **13.8 billion years** (APPSC 2019)
- **Age of Earth and solar system:** ~**4.6 billion years**
- **Most abundant element:** **hydrogen**, then helium (universe and the Sun)
  - Sun = **~74% H and ~24% He**
- **Present view:** All planets formed from the **same spinning protoplanetary disc** as the Sun
  - Hot inner disc → only metals and rocky minerals solid (Mercury to Mars)
  - Cool outer disc → water, ammonia and methane ices (giant planets)
# 2. Distance units
- **Units:** **AU** = mean Earth-Sun distance ~**149.6 million km** < **light year** (distance light travels in a year) ~**9.46 x 10^12 km** (~9.46 trillion km) < **parsec** = **3.26 light years**
- ⚠ Order of size: **AU < light year < parsec**
# 3. Types of planets and dwarf planets
| **Basis** | **Types** |
|---|---|
| Inner planets | Rocky/terrestrial |
| Outer planets | Gas giants = Jupiter, Saturn; Ice giants = Uranus, Neptune |
| Dwarf planets - asteroid belt | **Ceres** |
| Dwarf planets - Kuiper belt | **Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, Eris** |
- **IAU definition (2006):** **IAU** adopted a new definition of "planet" in **2006**
  - Planet must orbit the Sun + be nearly round + have **cleared its orbital neighbourhood** (orbital path)
  - **Pluto** fails the last test → **dwarf planet (2006)**
- **Trigger for change:** **Eris**
- **Pluto:** Largest Kuiper Belt object (39 AU); found 1930 by Clyde Tombaugh
# 4. FIRSTs and LASTs

- ⚠ **Aryabhata** = astronomer (476-550 CE) and also the name of India's first satellite

## Stars and galaxies — life cycle and terms → meaning | from: S1
- **Milky Way:** Our (barred) spiral galaxy
  - Spiral = rotating disc + central bulge + halo with dark matter
  - Indians found spiral galaxy **Alaknanda** with **JWST**
# 1. Life cycle of stars
- **Low/medium mass:** **nebula** (gas-dust cloud) → protostar → main sequence → red giant → planetary nebula → **white dwarf**
- **Massive:** Supergiant → **supernova** → **neutron star** or **black hole**
- **Lifespan:** **Giants live short, dwarfs live long**
- **White-dwarf ceiling:** **Chandrasekhar limit ~1.44 solar masses**
# 2. Terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Cepheids** | Pulsating stars; period-luminosity used to measure distances |
| **Pulsar** | Fast-spinning neutron star emitting beams |
| **Black hole** | Not even light escapes |
| **Magnitude** | Lower number = brighter star (logarithmic scale); absolute magnitude = brightness at a standard 10 parsecs |
| **Twinkling** | Stars twinkle, planets do not |
- **Colour = surface temperature:** blue (hottest) → white → **yellow (Sun)** → orange → red (coolest)
- **Polaris:** Ursa Minor, ~323 light years; within 1° of the north celestial pole
- **Future pole star:** Axis wobble will make **Vega** the pole star in ~12,000 years
- ⚠ Blue = hottest, red = coolest; lower magnitude = brighter

## The Sun — size, layers and solar activity | from: S2
- **Mass:** **over 99% of the solar system's mass**
- **Diameter:** ~**109 x** Earth's
- **Distance:** Mean Earth-Sun distance = **1 AU (~150 million km)**; light takes ~**8 min 20 s** to reach Earth
- **Future:** Becomes a **red giant** after exhausting its hydrogen
| **Layer** | **Key fact** |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium (H → He); about **15 million °C**[GK] |
| Radiative and convective zones | Between the core and the photosphere |
| **Photosphere** | Visible surface; about **5,500-6,000°C**[GK]; supergranule cells ~30,000 km, ~24 h life |
| **Chromosphere** | Reddish lower atmosphere, seen at eclipses |
| **Corona** | Outer atmosphere, a faint halo seen in a total eclipse; **over a million °C** - hotter than the surface [GK] |
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Sunspots** | Cooler, darker magnetic patches/regions of intense magnetic activity; markers of the **~11-year solar cycle** (minimum to maximum) |
| **Solar flares** | Sudden bursts of radiation from magnetic reconnection; classed **A, B, C, M, X** (X strongest) |
| **CMEs** | Plasma clouds from the corona at 250-3,000 km/s; reach Earth in 1-3 days |
- **Sunspot cycle:** Matched by plasma currents in the 35,000-km-deep **Near-Surface Shear Layer**
  - Sun's magnetic poles **reverse at each peak**
- **Effects on Earth:** **geomagnetic storms, auroras**; damage to satellites, GPS, aviation, radio, communication, navigation and **power grids**
- ⚠ **Corona** (outer) is hotter than the **photosphere** (surface)

## Planets → superlatives and features | from: S3, S4
- **Order:** Mercury → Venus → **Earth (between Venus and Mars)** → Mars | asteroid belt | Jupiter → Saturn → Uranus → Neptune
- **Revolution:** All 8 planets revolve **counter-clockwise** (seen from above the Sun's north pole)
- **Period of revolution:** **Increases with distance** from the Sun
| **Superlative** | **Planet** |
|---|---|
| Nearest to Sun; smallest; shortest year (**88 days**) | **Mercury** |
| Largest; most massive (**~318 x Earth's mass**); fastest spin (~10 h day) | **Jupiter** |
| Least dense - density **less than water** | **Saturn** |
| Densest planet | **Earth** |
| Hottest (average **464°C**, thick CO2 atmosphere) | **Venus** (Mercury only 167°C) |
| Earth's twin; brightest planet; Earth's nearest neighbour; "morning/evening star" | **Venus** |
| Coldest average (**-200°C**); farthest since 2006; longest year (~165 years) | **Neptune** (Uranus -195°C) |
| Largest day-night temperature gap (>1000°F; slow spin, scant air) | **Mercury** |
| Retrograde (east-to-west) rotation | **Venus and Uranus** |
| Rotates on its side (highly tilted axis) | **Uranus** |
| Stays closest to Earth the longest (small orbit keeps it near every planet on average) | **Mercury** (APPSC 2019) |
| Red Planet (rusty surface, polar ice caps, thin air) | **Mars** |
| No natural satellite | **Mercury and Venus have none** (APPSC 2019: Venus) |
- **Average temperatures:** Venus 464°C > Mercury 167°C > Earth 15°C > Mars -65°C > Jupiter -110°C > Saturn -140°C > Uranus -195°C > Neptune -200°C > Pluto -225°C
  - **Lowest single temperature:** Uranus, about -224°C
- **Features:**
  - **Mars:** **Mars has polar ice caps**; thin atmosphere; signs of subsurface water/ice
  - **Jupiter, Saturn:** Mostly **hydrogen and helium**
  - **Uranus/Neptune:** Methane tints them blue-green
> Exam angle: "no natural satellite", "retrograde rotation" and "least dense" are the most repeated one-liners.
- ⚠ Hottest = **Venus** (not Mercury, the nearest); coldest average = **Neptune** but lowest single reading = **Uranus**

## Earth's unique features — habitability and evolution of the spheres | from: S7
- **Goldilocks (habitable) zone:** Zone around a star where temperature is **"just right"** for liquid water - neither too hot nor too cold
  - **Earth lies in the Sun's habitable zone** (APPSC 2019: both statements right/true)
- **Only known planet with life:** Right distance from the Sun + liquid water + protective atmosphere
- **Earth-like planets:**
  - **K2-18b:** ~124 light years, Leo; 5.2 x wider, ~9 x more massive than Earth; shows a **possible biomarker molecule**
  - **27 possible circumbinary (twin-sun) planets** found with TESS data; first found in 2005
| **Sphere** | **How it evolved** |
|---|---|
| **Lithosphere** | Heavy iron sank → core; lighter material → crust (differentiation) |
| **Atmosphere (three stages)** | Early hydrogen-helium lost to the solar wind → **Degassing** of the interior (water vapour, nitrogen, CO2) → **Photosynthesis** added oxygen |
| **Hydrosphere** | Water vapour from degassing condensed → rain → filled ocean basins |
- **Magnetic field:** Generated by the **geodynamo** (churning liquid-iron outer core); shields from solar wind
  - 2026 study: some **pole reversals lasted up to ~70,000 years**, not the assumed ~10,000

=== ROW 2 | Motions of the earth - rotation and revolution; effects (day and night, Coriolis force, tides)

## Rotation — direction, period, speed and data | from: S0
- **Direction:** **west to east** (anticlockwise seen from above the North Pole)
  - Exceptions: only **Venus and Uranus** rotate the other way
- **Period:** **23 h 56 m 4 s** (one rotation)
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Sidereal day** | Relative to a star = **23 h 56 m 4 s** |
| **Solar day** | Sun to Sun = **24 h** - ~4 min longer because Earth also moves along its orbit |
- **Speed:** ~**1,670 km/h at the Equator** → falls with latitude → **zero at the poles**
- **Why not felt:** Uniform motion (no acceleration) + atmosphere rotates with Earth
- **Climate change is slowing rotation:** Ice melt shifts mass toward the equator → raises moment of inertia → **slowing rotation**
  - Days longer by ~**1.33 ms per century** (about **1.33 milliseconds per century**)
- **UPSC 2025:** Tested that a **shift in Earth's rotation and axis** is under way
- ⚠ Sidereal day (**23 h 56 m 4 s**) is shorter than the solar day (**24 h**)

## Persons and laws → evidence of the motions | from: S0, S2
| **Person / law** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| Aryabhata | First proposed that Earth rotates |
| **Foucault pendulum (Paris, 1851)** | Plane of swing slowly turns → proves rotation |
| **Ferrel's law** | Winds and currents deflected **to the right in the Northern Hemisphere, to the left in the Southern** |
- **Other evidences of rotation:** Coriolis deflection of winds/currents; equatorial bulge; apparent east-to-west motion of stars and Sun

## Day and night — terms → meaning | from: S1
- **Day and night:** Caused by **rotation**
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Circle of illumination / terminator** | Line dividing day from night; also called **"grey line" / "twilight zone"** (APPSC 2019); passes through both poles only at the equinoxes |
| **Midnight sun** | Beyond the **Arctic/Antarctic Circles** Sun stays up 24 h in summer; e.g., northern Norway ("Land of the Midnight Sun") |
| **Twilight** | Diffused light before sunrise/after sunset |
- **Unequal lengths:** **tilted axis** (23½°) + revolution
- **Equator:** ~12 h all year; variation grows with latitude
- **Poles:** ~6 months day + 6 months night
- **Twilight:**
  - **Shortest at the Equator:** Sun rises/sets vertically
  - **Longest at high latitudes:** Oblique path; weeks-long near the poles

## Effects of rotation — Coriolis force, polar flattening and tides | from: S2, S3
# 1. Coriolis force
- **Cause:** **Earth's rotation** (APPSC-G1 2019; BPSC 2022)
- **Deflection:** Winds and currents deflected **to the right in the Northern Hemisphere, to the left in the Southern** = **Ferrel's law**
- **Variation:** **Absent at the Equator, maximum at the poles; increases with wind velocity** (UPSC 2024: both correct)
  - Proportional to 2Ωv sin(latitude)
- **Effects:**
  - Cyclones need enough Coriolis force → rarely form **within 5° of the Equator**
  - Cyclones spin **anticlockwise in NH**
  - Ocean gyres and current deflection
# 2. Polar flattening and tides
- **Polar flattening**
- **Tides:** Each place passes through two tidal bulges daily → **two high and two low tides** (~12 h 25 min apart)
| **Tide** | **Position** |
|---|---|
| Spring tides | Sun-Earth-Moon in line (new and full moon) |
| Neap tides | Sun and Moon at right angles (~7 days later) |
- **Spring and neap tides:** Each occurs twice a month
  - Perihelion/aphelion slightly alter tidal ranges
- ⚠ Coriolis: **zero at the Equator**, maximum at poles (opposite of rotation speed)

=== ROW 3 | Concept of time - local and standard time, GMT/UTC, Indian Standard Time, time zones, International Date Line

## Time — units and terms → meaning (GMT, UTC, DST) | from: S0, S2
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **GMT** | Mean solar time at **Greenwich** (Prime Meridian) |
| **UTC** | World time standard kept by **atomic (caesium) clocks**; adjusted with leap seconds |
| Daylight saving time | Clocks advanced ~**1 hour in summer** in mid/high-latitude countries to use evening daylight; set back in autumn |
| **Natural units** | **day** (rotation), **month** (Moon's revolution), **year** (Earth's revolution) |
| **Conventional units** | Second, minute, hour, week |
- **Prime Meridian (0°):** Runs through the **Royal Observatory, Greenwich**; adopted at the **International Meridian Conference, 1884**

## Local time — rules and worked examples | from: S1
| **Rule** | **Value** |
|---|---|
| Earth turns 360° in 24 h | **1° = 4 minutes**; **15° = 1 hour**[GK] |
| 1 minute of arc (1') | **4 seconds** of time [GK] |
| Place to the **east** | Time is **ahead** - add [GK] |
| Place to the **west** | Time is **behind** - subtract [GK] |
- **Direction:** **East is ahead (add time), West is behind (subtract)**
- **Longitude difference:**
  - Same-side longitudes → subtract
  - Opposite sides (one E, one W) → **add** the degrees
- **Steps:** Longitude difference x 4 min = time difference → add for east, subtract for west
- **Message/flight problems:** Local arrival time = departure local time + journey time +/- longitude time difference (add when flying east)
- **Worked PYQ (APPSC-G1 2019):** A = 60°W at 10 a.m.; B = 90°E
  - Difference 150° x 4 = 600 min = **10 h**; B is east → **8 p.m.**
- **Finding longitude:** Local time 2 h behind Greenwich → **30°W**

## International Date Line — course, places and crossing rule | from: S3
- **IDL:** Imaginary line from North Pole to South Pole roughly along the **180° meridian**
  - Through the **middle of the Pacific Ocean** (APPSC 2021) and the **Bering Strait**
- **Zigzags:** **zigzags to avoid land** / keep island groups on one date
  - Around the **Bering Strait** (Russia-Alaska), **Siberia (Chukotka), Aleutians, Kiribati, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa** (the **Fiji-Tonga-Kiribati** islands)
  - Samoa moved to the Asian side of the line in 2011
| **Place** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Big Diomede (Russia) and Little Diomede (USA)** | Different dates, in the Bering Strait |
| Kiribati's Line Islands (UTC+14) | First to see a new day |
| **Crossing** | **Rule** |
|---|---|
| **Westward (America → Asia)** | **gain/skip ahead a day** (add a day) - a day is skipped |
| **Eastward (Asia → America)** | **lose/repeat a day** - date goes back one day |
- ⚠ Going **west** → skip a day; going **east** → repeat a day

## Indian Standard Time — meridian, reference town, states crossed | from: S4, S6
- **IST meridian:** **82°30'E (82.5°E)** (APPSC 2019)
- **Offset:** **IST = GMT + 5 h 30 min** (APPSC 2019) (82.5 x 4 min = 330 min)
  - E.g., weather balloons launched at 0000 and 1200 UTC = **5.30 a.m. and 5.30 p.m. IST**
- **Reference town:** **Mirzapur** (Uttar Pradesh)
- **States crossed (5 states):** **Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh** (UP, MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **not West Bengal** (APPSC 2021)

- **Local time gap (Arunachal vs Gujarat):**
  - **Longitudinal span:** **68°7'E - 97°25'E ≈ 29°**

## Revolution — solstices, equinoxes and day length (data) | from: S8

## Axial tilt and inclination — terms, consequences and changes | from: S9
- **Tilt:** **23½° from the perpendicular** to the orbital plane = **66½° to the plane**
  - ⚠ **23½°** from the perpendicular = **66½°** to the orbital plane (same tilt, two references)
- **Consequences:** Seasons; unequal day length; positions of tropics (23½°) and polar circles (66½°)
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Precession** | Axis **wobbles (precession)** → pole star changes; celestial pole pointed to **Vega ~14,000 years ago**; will again in ~12,000 years |
| **Milankovitch cycles** | Orbital and axial cycles drive long-period climate change |
- **Observed change:** Melting polar and glacier ice shifts mass away from the axis → **slows rotation**
