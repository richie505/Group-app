## Language families → branches, languages and areas | from: S0
# 1. Language families and shares
| **Family** | **Share of speakers** | **Branches and member languages** | **Main areas** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indo-European (Indo-Aryan)** | Largest: **~73%**; about **78%** in 2011 | Indo-Aryan: Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia, Assamese, **Sindhi, Kachchhi** (Kachchi); **Dardic**: Kashmiri | North, west, east India; Dardic in J&K; largest by area and speakers |
| **Dravidian** | About **~20%**; about **19.6%** in 2011 | South: Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam; South-Central: **Telugu**; Central/North: tribal languages (Kui/Kuvi, Gondi, Kurukh) | South India; Central Dravidian in AP, MP, Odisha, Maharashtra; North Dravidian in Bihar, Odisha, WB |
| **Austric (Austro-Asiatic)** | About **1.1-1.4%** (**~1.38%**) | **Munda**: Santhali, Mundari, Ho, Savara; **Mon-Khmer**: **Khasi**, Nicobarese | Jharkhand-Odisha-WB tribal belt, Meghalaya, Nicobar Islands |

| Andamanese / Semito-Hamitic | Tiny | Andaman tribes / Arabic | - |

- **Share figures:** 73/20/1.38/0.85 are older census figures

- **Telugu:** **South-Central Dravidian** (an older table puts AP under "Central Dravidian")

- **Khasi:** Austro-Asiatic (Mon-Khmer) → evidence Meghalaya plateau was once part of peninsular India
- ⚠ **Khasi** = Austro-Asiatic (Mon-Khmer); **Garo** = Sino-Tibetan (Bodo group)

## Eighth Schedule — amendments → languages added | from: S4
# 1. Eighth Schedule languages - included, and the amendments that added them
| **Year / Amendment** | **Languages added** | **Total** |
|---|---|---|
| 1950 (original) | Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu | 14 |
| **21st** Amendment, **1967** | **Sindhi** (15th scheduled language) | 15 |
| **71st** Amendment, **1992** | **Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali** | 18 |
| **92nd** Amendment, **2003** | **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** | **22** |

- **Constitutional link:** **Arts. 344(1) and 351**
- **Number:** **14 originally, 22 now**, added by three amendments
- **96th Amendment (2011):** Changed "Oriya" → "**Odia**"
- ⚠ **21 – 71 – 92** = **Sindhi – Konkani/Manipuri/Nepali – Bodo/Dogri/Maithili/Santhali**
- **Coverage:** 22 cover mother tongues of **96.71%** of population; **99 languages** remain outside (Jul 2025)
  - Earlier: 18 languages spoken by 91% (before 2003)
- **Santhali (92nd Amdt):** **Austroasiatic**; President released digital Constitution editions in Santhali + first-time editions in Kokborok and Kashmiri (2026)
- **Benefits of inclusion:**
  - Interpretation in Parliament
  - Sahitya Akademi recognition
  - Authoritative translation of Constitution under **Art. 394A**
  - Option in UPSC Indian-language paper
  - Central development funds
# 2. Not included / pending demands
- **English:** **English has never been in the Schedule**
- **Others pending:** **Bhojpuri**, **Kokborok**, **Khasi and Garo**, **Gor Boli / Lambadi** (Indo-Aryan; Banjaras; no script of its own)
- **PYQ:** **Bhojpuri is NOT in the Eighth Schedule**, while Nepali, Dogri and Bodo are (UPPSC 2025)
> Exam angle: "which language is NOT in the Eighth Schedule" (Bhojpuri, English) and amendment-to-language matching.

=== ROW 69 | Migration - types, streams, causes and consequences; internal migration in India

## Migration — definitions and types | from: S0
# 1. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Census migrant** | Current place differs from **place of last residence** or **place of birth**; two methods: **place of birth** → lifetime migrants; **place of last residence** (2026) |
| **PLFS / NSO: Usual Place of Residence** | Place lived in at least **6 months** |
| **Refugee** | Forced to leave; cannot return for fear of persecution (race, religion, nationality, social/political group) |
| **Net migration** | In-migration − out-migration |

# 2. Types of migration
| **Basis** | **Types** |
|---|---|
| Administrative boundary | **Internal** (within the country: between states, districts, towns, villages) / **International** (legal immigrants, illegal immigrants, refugees) |
| Time | Temporary or **seasonal** (e.g. harvest labour) / permanent; also return migration (going back once the cause is over) |
| Distance | Long-distance / short-distance |
| Number | Individual / mass |
| Cause | Economic, social, political, religious |
| Will | **Voluntary** (weighing push and pull factors) / **forced** (persecution, development-induced displacement, exploitation) |

## Migration streams → where they dominate | from: S1
| **Stream** | **Where it dominates** |
|---|---|
| **Rural to rural** | **Largest stream in India** (2011); contributed most in 2011 (CGPSC 2018 PYQ); **female-dominated because of marriage**; historically dominant between agricultural areas (low- → higher-potential farm areas) |
| Rural to urban | Typical of developing countries; dominated by **males** for **employment**, especially between states; drives urbanisation |
| Urban to urban | Typical of developed countries (USA, Japan) |
| Urban to rural | Smallest; occasional, only in special circumstances (return, retirement) |

- **Intra-state:** Women dominate, rural-to-rural
- **Inter-state:** Men dominate, rural-to-urban (NCERT pattern)
- **UPPSC 2017 PYQ:**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Assertion "rural-urban is the largest share" = **false**
  - Reason "rural-rural is mainly female (marriage)" = **true**
- **JPSC 2023:** Inter-state female migrants by last residence (2011) → answer key begins with **Urban-to-Urban**

## Causes of migration — push and pull factors, by gender | from: S2
**Push (origin) vs Pull (destination)**
| **Push (area of origin)** | **Pull (area of destination)** |
|---|---|
| Low farm productivity, low farm income, monsoon failure, drought, famine, rural unemployment | Jobs, higher wages |
| Poverty, poor rural infrastructure | Education, health care, public facilities |
| Conflict, persecution, political instability, disasters | Better living standards, security, amenities, transport access |

- **Sources:** UPSC Society; ES
- **By gender (PLFS 2020-21):**
  - **Women migrate mainly for marriage**: female rate **47.9%** → about **86.8%** of female migrants moved for **marriage**
  - **Men mainly migrate for employment** / **men for work**: male rate **10.7%** → about **22.8%** for **employment** (Jun 2026)
- ⚠ Women → **marriage**; men → **employment**
- **Youth:** About **26%** of population aged 10-24 → job search drives rural-urban migration
- **Rural-to-urban causes (BPSC 2024 PYQ):** All of these:
  - High labour demand in cities
  - **Unbalanced rural-urban development**
  - Few rural jobs
- **Other factors:**
  - Age: adults most mobile
  - Information networks: Keralites, Sikhs
  - Environment: drought in the Sahel

## Consequences of migration | from: S5
| **Dimension** | **Consequences** |
|---|---|
| Economic | Remittances; urban labour supply, informal workforce; labour for construction, textiles and hospitality; skill transfer ("social remittances"); drives the cities' share of GDP |
| Demographic | Population grows at destination, falls at source; source left with ageing + more women; distorted age-sex structure at origin and destination; urban growth; uneven density/population change across constituencies → matters for delimitation (Delimitation Bill 2026, TH 20 Apr 2026) |
| Social | Cultural mixing and inter-cultural dialogue; "sons of the soil" hostility (e.g. against "Bihari bhaiya"); slums, "dual city"/"double city", ghettoisation; family ties kept even in nuclear households (M.S. Gore) |
| Environmental | Overcrowding, slums, pressure on urban infrastructure, water, housing and sanitation in cities |

- **Fertility link:** Workers move from high-fertility to ageing low-fertility states
- **Covid-19 exposure:** COVID-19 lockdown → job insecurity, about **122 million job losses**, wage exploitation, no social security

## Acts, codes, schemes and compacts on migrants | from: S6, S7, S8
# 1. Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act 1979
- **Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act, 1979** / **Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RECS) Act, 1979** / Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service: Regulates recruitment, wages, conditions
  - Registration: establishments with **5 or more** inter-state migrants; contractors needed licences
  - **Displacement allowance** (50% of monthly wages) + journey allowance
  - Licences online on Shram Suvidha portal
  - Weak enforcement = known gap
# 2. OSH Code 2020
- **Now subsumed in the OSH Code:** **OSH Code, 2020** / **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code** (merges 13 laws)
  - One of the **4 Labour Codes** (consolidated 29 laws), **into force in November 2025**
  - Covers **self-migrating workers**
  - Annual **lump-sum travel allowance**
  - Portability of **PDS** and social security
# 3. Schemes → year and features
| **Scheme** | **Year** | **Features** |
|---|---|---|
| **e-Shram portal** | **2021** | "One-Stop-Solution" for unorganised workers; **31.78 crore** registered by **July 2026** (**over 31.7 crore** mid-2026); linked to 15 central schemes; gig and platform worker module; available in **22 languages** via BHASHINI |
| **One Nation One Ration Card** | - | NFSA ration portability → migrants draw rations anywhere; working in **all 36 States/UTs**; 99.9% Aadhaar seeding; problems: biometric failures, data mismatches between states |
