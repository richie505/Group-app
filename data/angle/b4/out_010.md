=== ROW 38 | India - climate: mechanism of the monsoon, theories, seasons and their characteristics

## Monsoon — meaning, wind type and features | from: S0

- **Etymology:** Arabic **"Mausim"** (season); some notes say Urdu 'mausin' or Malay 'monsin'
- **Wind type:** **Seasonal/periodic (secondary) winds** that reverse direction with the season - not primary (planetary) winds
  - Sea → land in summer; land → sea in winter
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "primary winds" statement I is false (PYQ G2 2023)
- **Latitudinal limit:** Tropics between **20°N and 20°S** (PYQ G2 2023/2024: II and III correct)
- **Distribution control:** Mainly **relief**
- **Nature:**
  - Not steady; **unequally distributed**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "equally distributed" is NOT a feature (PYQ 2022)
  - Marked by **breaks** and uncertainty/variability
  - Sudden onset = **"burst"** (first week of June)

## Theories and persons → core idea | from: S0, S1

| **Theory / driver** | **Core idea** |
|---|---|
| **Thermal / differential heating** (classical; Sir Edmund **Halley, 1686**) | Land heats faster than sea, so summer low over land (drawing moist sea winds) and high over sea; reversed in winter [GK] |
| **Dynamic / ITCZ shift** | Northward shift of the **ITCZ** in summer: it moves over the Ganga plain as the **monsoon trough**, pulling SE trades across the equator as SW winds [GK]; its N-S swings set rainfall (UPSC CSAT) |
| **Jet streams** | The **subtropical westerly jet** (**STJ**) shifts north of the Himalayas in summer → onset; it returns south → withdrawal (APP). The **TEJ** (South China Sea to East Africa) coincides with the SW monsoon |
| **Tibetan plateau heating** | Plateau (~5,000 m) acts as a **heat island**: intense summer heating → low pressure, an upper-air anticyclone and a stronger **Tropical Easterly Jet**; **permanent high** in the S Indian Ocean (UPSC p.39) |
| **ENSO / IOD / MJO** | **El Nino weakens**, La Nina strengthens/aids. A **positive IOD** can offset El Nino. An unfavourable **MJO** phase stalls the monsoon (LENS Jul 2026) |

| **Person** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Al Masudi** (10th c.) | Noted the seasonal winds |
| Sir Edmund **Halley** | Thermal theory (**1686**) |

- **Modern (complex) explanation:** Dated **1957** in some notes
- **Significance:** Without them north India would be desert
- ⚠ **STJ** north of Himalayas → onset; STJ back south → withdrawal

## Terms → meaning | from: S1, S3, S4, S6, S8

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Mascarene High** | Permanent high-pressure cell in the south Indian Ocean (near **Madagascar**) → drives **cross-equatorial flow**; listed among the SW monsoon's formation factors |
| **Somali jet** | Low-level jet along the East African coast; with the **Somalia current** it intensifies monsoon winds |
| **Nautapa** | Traditional **nine-day** peak of summer heat in north India, just before the monsoon arrives |
| **Dust aerosols** | Heat the mid-troposphere over the **Indo-Gangetic plain** → help pull the monsoon inland |
| **Breaks** | Trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills, so the plains go dry while the hills get heavy rain [GK] |
| **LPA** | **868.6 mm** (1971-2020) |
| **October heat** | Clear skies + hot, humid weather of the retreating monsoon |
| **Ocean Mean Temperature (OMT)** | Average temperature of upper Indian Ocean down to the **26 deg C isotherm**; measured **Jan-Mar**; predicts monsoon rain better than **SST** |

## SW monsoon — onset dates and the two branches | from: S4

| **Stage** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Onset** | Bay of Bengal islands ~a week earlier: A&N Islands ~**25 May**, then **Kerala ~1 June**, the first state (APP; IYB). IMD declares onset after 10 May when **60% of 14** stations get 2.5 mm for two days and westerlies reach ~600 hPa (TH, 3 Jun 2026); it uses rainfall over **5-day pentads** |
| **Advance** | Bhopal ~15 June, Delhi ~25 June; the country is covered in about a month (APP) |
| **Full coverage** | IMD's revised normal date **8 July**; some notes say "by end of July", others "by end of June" |
| **Duration** | **<75 days** in W Rajasthan to **>120 days** in SW peninsula (IYB 2026) |
| **Withdrawal** | Begins from the NW in October and ends by **end-November** (APP) |

- **SW monsoon share:** About **75%** of annual rain

| **Branch** | **Strikes / covers** | **Missed / weak** |
|---|---|---|
| **Arabian Sea branch** | Western Ghats windward and Malabar coast first; heaviest rain at **Agumbe, Hulikal** (Karnataka, Ghats crest); moves north along the west coast and on through Narmada-Tapi to central India [GK] | Interior peninsula (rain-shadow east of the Ghats); **Rajasthan** (winds parallel to the Aravallis) |
| **Bay of Bengal branch** | Myanmar coast and the NE hills at right angles (**Meghalaya - Mawsynram**), then deflected west by the Himalayas along the **Ganga plain** | Rain falls E → W; the NW stays dry |

> Exam angle: both statements are correct: "Kerala receives the SW monsoon first" and "the Bay branch soaks Meghalaya" (APPSC GS 2024). Monsoons lie mainly within **20 deg N-20 deg S** and relief governs SW-monsoon rain (APPSC G2 2024 key).

## Retreating monsoon, NE monsoon and western disturbances → region | from: S6

| **Phenomenon / system** | **Origin** | **Effect / receives** |
|---|---|---|
| **Retreating monsoon** (**Oct-Nov**) | Sun moves south; NW low vanishes. **Westerly jet returns** south of the Himalayas; **tropical easterly jet disappears** | Clear skies + "**October heat**"; **Bay of Bengal cyclones** → heavy rain in the south (bimodal) |
| **NE monsoon** (Oct-Dec) | Retreating winds pick up Bay of Bengal moisture | **Coromandel coast (Tamil Nadu)**, **Rayalaseema, Nellore, Prakasam** |
| **Tamil Nadu coast in SW monsoon** | - | Stays relatively dry - rain-shadow and winds parallel to the coast; little SW-monsoon rain → depends on the NE monsoon (Coromandel = Koeppen As climate) |
| **Western disturbances** | **Mediterranean** temperate cyclones, via Iran-Afghanistan-Pakistan | **NW India**: **10-15 cm** winter rain, vital for **rabi**; **snow** in J&K, HP, Uttarakhand hills |

- **Western disturbances:** Fewer of them → reduced winter rain and snow in north India

=== ROW 39 | India - rainfall distribution, variability, El Nino linkage, drought and flood-prone areas

## Terms → meaning: variability, ENSO, drought types | from: S1, S2, S3

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **CV formula** | **CV = (standard deviation / mean) x 100** |
| **El Nino** | Central/east equatorial Pacific at least **0.5°C** warmer for **five consecutive overlapping 3-month periods** |
| **La Nina** | Generally strengthens the monsoon; colder winters; more Bay cyclones |
| **IOD** | Positive IOD can offset El Nino |
| **Meteorological** drought | Rain deficit |
| **Agricultural** drought | Soil moisture |
| **Hydrological** drought | River/groundwater |
| **Flash drought** | Sudden heat + no rain |

- ⚠ El Nino weakens, La Nina strengthens; positive IOD offsets El Nino

## Regions → rainfall gradient, variability, drought and floods | from: S0, S1, S3, S4

- **Monsoon duration:** **< 75 days** in western Rajasthan vs **> 120 days** in the south-west peninsula
- **Rainfall gradient:**
  - Peninsula: decreases **west to east** (rain-shadow)
  - Northern plains: decreases **east to west** (Kolkata > Patna > Prayagraj > Delhi > Bikaner)

| **Variability** | **Regions** |
|---|---|
| **Low CV (< 25%)** | West coast, Western Ghats, NE, eastern peninsula |
| **High CV (> 50%)** | Western Rajasthan, Kutch, Ladakh |

- **Dates:** Large inter-annual variability in onset and withdrawal
- **Chronic drought regions:**
  - **Western India - Rajasthan, Gujarat** (PYQ 2025)
  - Rain-shadow Deccan
  - AP: **Rayalaseema** and south **Prakasam** (NE monsoon failure + groundwater depletion)
- **Flood worst-hit:**
  - **Brahmaputra** valley ("sorrow of Assam")
  - **Kosi** and Ganga plains (Bihar, UP)
  - Coastal deltas (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Odisha, WB) hit by Andaman Sea depressions
- **Himalayan rivers:** Swell in monsoon → frequent floods; **cloudburst** flash floods rising in the Himalaya and NE
