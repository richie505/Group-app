## Himalayas — origin, ranges, divisions and glaciers | from: S1

- **Young fold mountains:** **2,414 km** from **Nanga Parbat to Namche Barwa**
  - Area about **5 lakh sq km**; **200-500 km** wide
  - Rose from Tethys sediments as the Indian plate moved north (Eocene-Pliocene); uplift still going on
- **Structural zones:** Indus-Tsangpo Suture, **Main Central Thrust**, **Main Boundary Thrust**, **Himalayan Frontal Fault**

| **Range (north → south)** | **Key features** |
|---|---|
| **Trans-Himalaya** | Karakoram, Ladakh and Zaskar ranges, north of the Himadri [GK] |
| **Himadri** (Greater) | Loftiest and oldest; average **>6,000 m**; granite core, permanently snow-bound [GK] |
| **Himachal** (Lesser) | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat (Nepal) [GK]; valleys of **Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu**, Lahul-Spiti; hill stations Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Darjeeling |
| **Shiwaliks** (Outer) | **Youngest** and narrowest (**15-50 km**), average ~1,300 m; folded river deposits; longitudinal valleys called **duns** |

## Northern Plains and Indian Desert — regions and features | from: S2, S3

- **Northern Plains:** **6,52,000 sq km**; **2,400 km** long (W-E); **70-500 km** wide
  - **Northern edge:** **Himalayan Frontal Fault**
  - **Gradient:** land drops only **~200 m** over the 1,600 km from Delhi to the Bay
- **Punjab plain:** formed by Indus tributaries, doabs
- **Ganga plain:** about **3,75,000 sq km**; alluvium **2,000-3,000 m** thick
  - 5 divisions: **Ganga-Yamuna Doab** (upper >200 m, middle 100-200 m, lower <100 m), Rohilkhand, Avadh, Bihar, Bengal plains
- **Brahmaputra plain:** Assam valley; river enters Bangladesh
- **Kosi** = "Sorrow of Bihar"
- **Molassis basin:** **Mizoram** in the Purvachal hills; soft unconsolidated deposits (NCERT)
- **Thar location:** west of the Aravallis; one of the mainland's four regions = "Thar - the desert region"
- **IYB division:**
  - **Great Desert:** Rann of Kutch edge beyond the Luni, northward
  - **Little Desert:** Luni to Jaisalmer-Jodhpur
  - Rocky limestone belt lies between them
- **Order of features (APPSC-GS 2025 key):** **semi-arid/arid weather → sand dunes → thorny bushes → seasonal streams in rain**
- **Features:** **barchan** and **seif** dunes, salt lakes, desertification spreading north
- **Inland drainage:** **Luni**, Machhu, Rupen, Saraswati, Banas, Ghaggar → drain into salt lakes or lost in sand
  - **Luni:** only significant river; seasonal; ends in the **Rann of Kutch**
  - **Sambhar:** saline lake
- **Rann of Kutch origin:** Kutch was an **island** amid seas and lagoons → aridity turned lowland into a **salt plain**
  - **Great Rann** (north) vs **Little Rann** (south)
- **Rann of Kutch (Gujarat):** seasonal salt marsh; Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary; rich in solar energy

> Exam angle: the APPSC GS 2025 key orders desert features as arid climate → dunes → thorny bushes → seasonal streams.

## Peninsular Plateau — sub-divisions and the Deccan Traps | from: S4

- Gondwana **shield** (Precambrian): triangular block of igneous and metamorphic rocks
- Tilts **east** → most rivers flow east

| **Sub-division** | **Location / rock** | **Facts** |
|---|---|---|
| Central Highlands | North of Narmada; Aravalli, Malwa, Vindhyas | Avg **800-1,200 m**, slope SW to NE; Aravalli = oldest; Vindhyas an escarpment (APP) |
| Malwa plateau | MP; Deccan Trap basalt | Vindhyas on its south (APP; CDI) |
| Deccan plateau | South of Narmada; (~7 lakh sq km) horizontal lava sheets; **Deccan Trap = basalt** (APPSC-GS 2025) | Inverted triangle, stable, oldest rocks (IYB 2026) |
| Chhotanagpur | Jharkhand (Ranchi plateau) | Iron, coal, manganese; Hundru Falls (APP) |

- **Satpura:** highest peak **Dhupgarh** (see peaks table)
- **Amarkantak:** in the Maikala range
- **Deccan Traps:**
  - Fissure eruptions of **basaltic lava** from **end-Cretaceous to early Eocene**
  - Area: once about **10 lakh sq km** → now about **5 lakh sq km** (Kuchchh, Saurashtra, Maharashtra, Malwa, N Karnataka)
  - Thickness: **3,000 m** in the west → 150 m at the east
  - Weathered into **regur** (black cotton soil)

## Ganga system — course, tributaries by bank, confluence order | from: S3

- **Headstreams:** **Bhagirathi** + **Alaknanda** → join at **Devprayag** (called Ganga from here)
- **Lower course:** swings south at **Farakka barrage** (WB)
  - Left arm: **Bhagirathi-Hooghly**
  - Right arm: **Padma** into Bangladesh → joins Brahmaputra and Meghna → enters the Bay of Bengal as the **Meghna**
- **Navigation:** navigable up to Prayagraj

| **Bank** | **Tributaries (source)** |
|---|---|
| Left (Himalayan) | **Ramganga** (joins at Farrukhabad), Gomti, **Ghaghara**, **Gandak**, **Kosi**, **Mahananda** (easternmost) |
| Right (Peninsular) | **Yamuna** (Yamunotri; joins at Prayagraj), Tons, **Son**, Punpun, Damodar |

- **Yamuna:** right bank: **Chambal** (joins in Etawah), **Betwa** (joins at Hamirpur), Sindh, Ken; left bank: Hindon
- **Son:** joins near **Maner, Patna** (1 Sep 2026)
- **Gandak:** = **Narayani** in Nepal; enters India at **Valmikinagar**; **Sadanira** of the Shatapatha Brahmana (28 Aug 2026)
- **Order of confluences below Prayagraj:** Tons (R) → Gomti (L) → Ghaghara (L) → Son (R) → Gandak (L) → Kosi (L) → Mahananda (L)

> Exam angle: "south-based tributary" = Betwa/Son/Yamuna group; Burhi Dihing and Dhansiri are Brahmaputra tributaries, not the Ganga's (PYQ 2024: neither statement correct).

=== ROW 37 | India - Peninsular drainage: east-flowing and west-flowing rivers, river basins, watershed

## River types — Himalayan vs peninsular; east- vs west-flowing | from: S0, S1

| **Feature** | **Himalayan rivers** | **Peninsular rivers** |
|---|---|---|
| Regime / flow | **Perennial** (rain + snowmelt) | **Seasonal**, rain-fed (monsoon-fed) |
| Valley | Deep **antecedent gorges** | Broad, shallow, graded valleys; older |
| Course | Meandering, shifting | Fixed, straight courses (hard rock) |
| Mouth | Large deltas | Deltas (east) / estuaries (west) |
| Use | Irrigation and navigation | Hydropower; delta irrigation (east-flowing) |

- **Antecedent rivers:** Rise in the **trans-Himalaya**; kept their course by cutting **gorges** as the ranges rose
  - Examples: **Indus**, **Sutlej**, **Brahmaputra**

| **Feature** | **East-flowing (Bay of Bengal)** | **West-flowing (Arabian Sea)** |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, **Pennar, Palar**, Vamsadhara, Nagavali | **Narmada, Tapi**, **Sabarmati, Mahi**, **Periyar**, Mandovi, Sharavati |
| Mouth | Large **deltas** | **Estuaries, not deltas** → natural harbours on the west coast |
| Use | Irrigation | **Hydropower** |

- ⚠ East-flowing = deltas + irrigation; west-flowing = estuaries + hydropower

## West-flowing and rift-valley rivers → features | from: S1

- **Course:** **Narmada** and **Tapi** flow west through **rift valleys**
  - Narmada: between the **Vindhyas** and **Satpuras**
  - Tapi: between the **Satpura** and **Ajanta** ranges
- **Rarity:** Tapi = one of only **three** peninsular rivers running east to west
- **Tapi mouth:** at **Suvali near Surat**
- **Count:** About **600** small rivers drain the west coast
- **Nature:** Short and swift; suit hydropower
- **Aravalli-born rivers:** (VIS 2026)
  - West: Luni, Sabarmati
  - East: Banas
  - North: Sahibi
