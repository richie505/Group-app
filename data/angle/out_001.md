=== ROW 112 | Ethnicity - concept, ethnic identity and ethnic movements in India

## Ethnicity — definitions, types and data | from: S0, S3
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Ethnic group** | People identifying with each other on common **language/dialect, history, society, culture or nation** |
| **Markers** | Language, religion, race, region, culture |
| **National minority** | Non-dominant citizen group with own ethnic, religious or linguistic identity; term from European minority-rights law |
| **Intra-ethnic** conflict | Within a group |
| **Inter-ethnic** conflict | Between groups |

- **Approaches:**
  - **Consensus**: groups coexist with separate identities
  - **Conflict**: groups as interest groups competing unequally (South Africa, Sri Lankan Tamils)
- **India's diversity:**
  - **19,500+** languages and dialects returned
  - **121 languages and 270 mother tongues** (Census 2011, Jul 2025)
  - **22** scheduled languages
- ⚠ **121** languages vs **270** mother tongues vs **22** scheduled languages vs **19,500+** returned

## M.N. Srinivas → concepts and statements | from: S4, S5, S7

- ⚠ "Brahminisation" = Srinivas's earlier name for Sanskritisation

## Terms → meaning | from: S2, S3, S4, S7
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|

=== ROW 114 | Social stratification and mobility in India

## Stratification and caste — definitions, bases and features | from: S0, S2
- **Stratification:** Ranking of groups in a hierarchy of unequal wealth, prestige and power
- **Bases tested:** **Caste, class, gender, age, race, ethnicity**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Language is NOT a basis** of social stratification (APPSC-GS 2019)
- **Characteristics (Melvin Tumin):** Social (not biological), ancient, universal, diverse in form, consequential (decides life chances)
- **Origins:** Harappan social differentiation (citadel vs lower town), no rigid caste; **Varna system crystallised in the Later Vedic period**
- **Varna vs Jati:** Varna = theoretical, Vedic, four-fold; Jati = real, innumerable local groups
  - Untouchables kept outside Varna scheme
  - Ranking principle = ritual **purity and pollution**
- **Classification:** **Vertical** (hierarchical ranking by birth) + **horizontal** (division into many separate groups)
- **Non-Hindus:** Muslims - **Ashraf, Ajlaf, Arzal**
> Exam angle: "which is NOT a basis" type questions; Green's definition; Varna vs Jati.

## Sociologists → theories and statements | from: S1, S2, S3, S5
| **Approach** | **Theorist** | **Core idea** |
|---|---|---|
| Functionalist | **Kingsley Davis and Wilbert Moore** (1945) [GK] | Stratification is necessary: higher rewards draw scarce talent into functionally important positions |
| Critique of functionalism | **Melvin Tumin**[GK] | Stratification wastes talent and passes inequality on to the next generation |
| Conflict (Marxian) | **Karl Marx** | Class depends on ownership or non-ownership of the **means of production** (APP) |
| Multi-dimensional | **Max Weber** | Three separate dimensions: **class, status, party (power)**[GK]; class position and "market situation" decide **life chances** (APP) |
| Ideological (caste) | **Louis Dumont** (Homo Hierarchicus) [GK] | Caste read through the religious ideology of **purity and pollution** (CDX) |

| **Person** | **Statement / contribution** |
|---|---|
| **A.W. Green** | Caste = "a system of stratification in which mobility up and down the status ladder, at least ideally, may not occur" (one APP note prints "A.R. Green") |
| **Andre Beteille** | Caste, class and power separate and can diverge; land ownership and political authority moved away from old ritual hierarchy; fieldwork in **Sripuram** village, Thanjavur (Tamil Nadu) |
| **Beteille** | Urban marriage alliances increasingly decided by **class (education, occupation, wealth) alongside caste** |
| Daniel Thorner | Agrarian classes: Maliks, Kisans, Mazdoors |
| **Dominant caste** (M.N. Srinivas) | Numerical strength + sizeable arable land + fairly high ritual rank |
| **Pitirim Sorokin** | First set out vertical and horizontal mobility |
| Kulke | **Kshatriyaisation** |

- ⚠ Davis-Moore = functionalist; **Tumin** = its critic; Weber = class, status, party

## Caste vs class — comparison and change | from: S2, S3
| **Feature** | **Caste** | **Class** |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | **Birth and hereditary status** | Achievement - **education, wealth, occupation and business ownership** |
| System | **Closed** | **Open** |
| Mobility | Restricted | Allowed |
| Inequality | **Cumulative** | **Dispersed** |

- **Source:** CDX Indian Society Pt 1 p.22
- **Caste link:** Agrarian hierarchy broadly **matches the caste hierarchy**; upper castes own land
- **Recent change:**
  - Inter-caste conflicts now about **power, not ritual status**
  - **Jajmani system has weakened**
  - Caste dominance rests on secular factors
- ⚠ Caste = closed, cumulative inequality; class = open, dispersed inequality

## Data — middle class, OBC and caste survey | from: S4, S7
- **Emergence:** **English education** (Macaulay's Minute, **1835**) → English-educated middle class that led the freedom struggle
- **Middle-class definitions (no single one):**
  - **World Bank**: $10-50 per capita per day (PPP)
  - **NCAER**: ₹2-10 lakh a year (2001-02 prices)
  - **PRICE**: ₹5-30 lakh a year
- **Size:** **43.2 crore (31%)** in 2021 → projected **61% by 2047**
  - OECD: world's **largest middle class by 2035**, overtaking China
- **Occupational base:** Salaried professionals, white-collar and service jobs, business
- **OBC:** Communities not on SC/ST lists
  - Mandal Commission: **over 52%** of population
  - **11 indicators** = 4 social + 4 economic + 3 educational
- **Telangana caste survey:** Backward classes not one block
  - Ranked **242 castes**
  - **135 castes (67% of the population)** above State average on backwardness index
  - SC/ST households concentrated in rural areas
- ⚠ Mandal **11** indicators = **4 + 4 + 3** (social, economic, educational)

## Mobility routes — education, reservation, migration, Dalit and OBC | from: S4, S5, S6, S7
- **ES 2025-26 (Ch. 13) definition:** **Inter-generational and intra-generational** change in status
  - Key determinants: health, education, technology, institutions
  - Low mobility entrenches historical inequality
- **Education:** Better education → better jobs + higher social standing; emerging class system allows upward mobility
- **Middle-class route:** Trusts **professional qualifications**; 2026 NEET paper leak shook that trust
- **Reservation:**
  - Southern backward classes (Self-Respect Movement of **Periyar**) demanded reservation in government jobs
  - Led to **group mobility**
  - Broadened the **urban middle class** with backward classes and Dalits
- **Occupational mobility:** **Ezhavas** of Kerala moved from toddy tapping to tailoring and cottage industries
- **Migration and urbanisation:** City anonymity + caste-blind public spaces weaken caste identity; but migrants carry caste networks and prejudices into cities
- **Dalit change - three avenues:** State policy, reform movements, Sanskritisation/Westernisation
- **Contemporary demands:** Dominant peasant castes (**Marathas, Gujjars, Patels**) seek OBC status for "greater life chances"

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
- **Current relevance:** Gen Z "horizontal" category of young Brahmin, Dalit and OBC voters sharing job and exam worries cuts across vertical caste loyalties
- **Significance:** ES 2025-26: poverty fell from **21.9% (2011-12) to 2.3% (2023-24)**
- **AP angle:** Kakatiya society stratified but relatively open; martial castes (**Velamas, Kammas, Reddys**) formed military and local administration
- **Criticism & debates:** Kshatriyaisation claims (Marathas, Jats, Yadavs) bring mobility but reproduce the hierarchy

=== ROW 115 | Rural, urban and tribal society in India - features and interlinkages

## Rural, urban and tribal — definitions, elements and features | from: S0, S2, S5, S6
- **Elements of rural community:** **Territory, we-feeling (community sentiment), relative self-sufficiency**, small size, homogeneity
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Cultural diversity is NOT an element** (UPPSC 2021)
- **Features of urban life:** **Competition, impersonal relationships, loss of humanistic values**, anonymity, weak social control
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Informal ties are NOT a feature** (JPSC 2021)
- **Urbanism vs urbanisation:** Urbanism = social, cultural, behavioural side of urban life; urbanisation = process of population shifting to urban areas (UPSC)
- **Migration:** Change of residence between areas for a considerable period
- **Tribal features:** Territorial community, common name, dialect, claim of common ancestor, **animism/totemism**, hereditary chieftainship, **clan system**, **youth dormitories**, simple economy
- **Imperial Gazetteer:** Families with common name, dialect and territory, originally endogamous
> Exam angle: "Which is NOT an element/feature"; village → scholar matching.

=== ROW 116 | Casteism - meaning, causes, manifestations and remedial measures

## Casteism — definitions and caste vs casteism | from: S0
- **Casteism:** The evil form of the caste system
  - Discrimination and prejudice against people because of their caste
  - Power, money and prestige concentrate in a few castes

| **Caste (institution)** | **Casteism (attitude and practice)** |
|---|---|
| Hereditary, endogamous group with a traditional occupation and shared identity | Partisan loyalty to one's own caste against others |
| Social structure (Ghurye's features) | Use of caste for political, economic or social gain |
| May exist without hostility | Produces discrimination, conflict and vote-bank politics |

- **G.S. Ghurye's features** of caste:
  - Segmental division; hierarchy
  - Restrictions on feeding and social intercourse
  - Civil and religious disabilities and privileges
  - Restricted choice of occupation
  - **Endogamy** (exception: hypergamy/**anuloma**)
- ⚠ Caste = institution (may exist without hostility); casteism = partisan attitude and practice

## Causes and manifestations of casteism | from: S1, S2
# 1. Causes
| **Cause** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Endogamy** | Marrying only within the caste keeps boundaries and "purity" intact; only about **5%** of Indian marriages are inter-caste (Aug 2025) |
| **Family and socialisation** | Families reproduce caste through customs, rituals and marriage norms; children absorb it early |
| **Political mobilisation** | Caste-based vote banks and caste politics have increased |
| **Economic competition and exploitation** | Dominant castes use ritual status to monopolise land and resources → lower castes kept dependent; upper castes own land |
| **Limits of urban anonymity** | Migrants carry caste prejudice into cities; **Caste networks** decide informal jobs; caste associations + caste stickers on vehicles persist (UPSC) |

# 2. Manifestations
- **Political:** **Caste-based voting** + caste associations acting as pressure groups
- **Honour killings paradox:** States with empowered Dalits (**TN, Telangana, Maharashtra, Kerala**) show both more inter-caste unions and more honour killings (Aug 2025)
- **Housing and jobs:**
  - Upper-caste landlords refuse tenants
  - Workplace discrimination
  - Caste-based matrimonial sites
- **Campus discrimination:** see UGC Regulations 2026 on the Acts page

## Caste and elections — national and Andhra Pradesh | from: S3
# 1. National picture
- **Origin:** Casteist element in electoral politics goes back to **Minto-Morley Reforms, 1909** (separate electorates)
- **Caste arithmetic:** Parties court caste leaders at every level
  - Tamil Nadu: **Gounders, Mukkulathors and Vanniyars** used caste solidarity to enter the legislature (15 Apr 2026)
- **Caste-based parties:** Samajwadi Party + RJD grew out of backward-caste mobilisation
- **Caste census:** Caste enumeration returns in **Census 2027**
  - 2022-23 **Bihar caste survey** cited as a model (9 Jul 2026)
# 2. Andhra Pradesh
- **Justice Party era:** Mobilised **Reddys, Kammas, Kapus and Velamas** against Brahmin dominance
  - Won the **Communal G.O. (1921)**
  - Laid ground for their dominance in post-independence Andhra politics
- **Karamchedu** massacre: Dalits broke from Congress and Communists → formed independent **Dalit Mahasabhas** (Katti Padmarao, Manda Krishna Madiga)
- ⚠ **Minto-Morley 1909** = origin of caste in electoral politics; **Communal G.O.** = **1921** (Justice Party)
