=== ROW 75 | Balance of Payments - structure, current and capital account, convertibility, forex reserves

## Balance of Payments — definitions, structure, identities and terms | from: S0, S1
# 1. Definition & structure
- **BoP:** Systematic record of all economic transactions of residents with the rest of the world in a period, on **double-entry** bookkeeping
  - Has a **current account and a capital account** (PYQ 2025; APPSC-G2 2025 key)
- **IMF vs RBI:**
  - IMF's **BPM6** = three accounts (current, capital, financial)
  - RBI combines the last two as **"Capital Account"**

| **Current account** | **Capital (and financial) account** |
|---|---|
| Merchandise exports and imports (visible trade; trade balance) | **FDI**, **FPI/FII**, ADRs/GDRs |
| **Invisibles**: services - software, travel, transport, banking, insurance | **ECB** (external commercial borrowings), external assistance |
| Invisibles: primary income - interest, dividends, profits, wages | **NRI deposits** (e.g., FCNR(B)), banking capital |
| Secondary income: **remittances**, gifts, grants | Short-term trade credit; reserve changes (change in forex reserves) |

- **Invisibles:** Services + primary income + transfers
  - Services = **non-factor** (shipping, banking, tourism, software) + **factor income**
# 2. Recording rules
- **Remittances:** Current Account → Invisibles → Private Transfers
- **Capital account entries:**
  - Purchase of foreign assets = **debit**
  - Sale of Indian assets to foreigners = **credit**
# 3. Identities & formulas
- **Identity:** Current Account + Capital Account + Financial Account + Net Errors & Omissions = **0**
  - BoP always balances in accounting terms; surplus in one account = deficit in another
- **Current account balance:** (exports - imports of goods and services) + net primary income + net secondary income
- **Balance of current account:** Exports minus imports of goods **and services** + net unilateral transfers (APPSC-GS 2019 key)
- **Balance of Trade (BoT):** Merchandise exports - merchandise imports only
  - BoT = part of the current account; BoP = the whole
- ⚠ BoT = goods only; current account balance = goods **and services** + transfers
# 4. Financing & overall balance → terms

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Financing a CAD** | A **CAD** must be financed by a capital account surplus (net inflow) or by drawing down reserves; monetary authority = **ultimate financier** of any BoP deficit |
| **Autonomous** transactions | Happen for their own sake |
| **Accommodating** transactions | Fill the gap (reserve changes, official borrowing) |
| **Overall BoP** | Positive → adds to reserves; negative → draws them down; reserves run short → **BoP crisis** |

=== ROW 76 | Exchange rate systems and rupee movement; currency internationalisation

## Terms → meaning: quotes, NEER/REER, devaluation vs depreciation | from: S0, S1, S4
# 1. Exchange rate terms

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Exchange rate | Price of one currency in terms of another |
| Direct quote | Units of **domestic** currency per one unit of foreign currency (e.g. ₹95 = $1; e.g., **Rs 96 = $1**) [GK] |
| Indirect quote | Units of **foreign** currency per one unit of domestic currency [GK] |
| Spot / forward rate | Rate for immediate delivery (settlement within **two days**) / rate agreed now for delivery at a future date [GK] |
| Arbitrage | Buying currency cheap in one market and selling it dearer in another at the same time (PYQ key) |
| Hot currency | Temporary name for any hard currency in the forex market (hard = highest global faith; soft = easily available) |
| Import cover | Months of imports that reserves can pay for |
| **Hedging** | Covering exchange-rate risk (e.g., via forwards) |

- **Quotes:** ⚠ **Trap:** The PYQ's "false" statement swapped the direct and indirect quote definitions
> Exam angle: "which statement is false" - the swapped definition of a direct quote (foreign units per one domestic unit) is the false one.
- **Market participants:** Commercial banks, authorised dealers/brokers, monetary authorities
# 2. NEER and REER
- **NEER:** Trade-weighted average of the rupee against a basket of major trading-partner currencies
  - **Base 2015-16 = 100**
  - **A rise = rupee appreciation** → imports cheaper, exports less competitive
- **REER:** **NEER adjusted for relative inflation** (inflation differential): **REER = NEER x (domestic prices / foreign prices)**
  - **High REER = overvalued rupee**, low = undervalued; the better indicator of competitiveness
- **RBI indices:** **40-currency** and **6-currency** trade-weighted indices
# 3. Devaluation vs depreciation, appreciation vs revaluation

| **Term** | **Regime** | **What happens** | **Done by** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depreciation | Flexible/floating | Exchange rate rises - more rupees per dollar (market raises Rs per $, e.g., Rs 80 → Rs 96) | Market demand-supply |
| Appreciation | Flexible/floating | Exchange rate falls - fewer rupees per dollar (market lowers Rs per $) | Market demand-supply |
| Devaluation | Fixed | Official lowering of the currency's value against a reference currency/basket - government raises the exchange rate (Rs per $), rupee cheaper | Government/monetary authority |
| Revaluation | Fixed | Official raising of the currency's value - government lowers the rate, rupee dearer | Government/monetary authority |

- **Reading a quote:**
  - ₹94 → ₹96 per $ = rupee **depreciated**
  - ₹96 → ₹94 = rupee **appreciated**
- ⚠ Depreciation/appreciation = market (floating); devaluation/revaluation = government (fixed)

## Exchange rate regimes → types | from: S2
- **Exchange rate regime:** System by which a country manages its currency; two broad types - **fixed** and **floating**

| **Regime** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Fixed/pegged** | Value fixed to a currency, basket or gold; government fixes the rate and RBI buys/sells to hold it |
| **Floating / flexible** | Market-determined (demand and supply) |
| **Managed float** | Hybrid - market-set with central-bank intervention; India's system; direct (buy/sell forex) or indirect (interest rates) action |

- **Post-Bretton Woods classes:**
  - Fixed: currency unions, currency boards (e.g. **Argentina 1991-Jan 2002**), pegs
  - Intermediate: bands, crawling pegs
  - Flexible: managed and independent float
- **Bretton Woods (1944):** Fixed rates on the dollar

## Changes → effects of devaluation, determinants, impossible trinity | from: S4, S5
# 1. Effects of devaluation/depreciation
- **Expenditure-switching policy:** Devaluation makes home goods cheaper for foreigners + imports costlier at home → spending switches to home-produced goods
  - Recognised BoP correction methods: devaluation, exchange depreciation, exchange control, monetary/fiscal measures, import curbs
- **Trade effect:** Devaluation/depreciation makes **exports cheaper** (in foreign currency) + imports dearer → trade balance tends to improve
  - It does **not** raise export prices
- **Limits:**
  - Imported inflation
  - **Inelasticity trap** - inelastic oil demand → falling rupee widens the **CAD**
  - Costlier **ECB** repayment
- **Net impact (ES 2025-26):** Weaker currency helps the trade balance more than it hurts the financial channel (capital outflows) → **net impact positive**
  - A falling rupee (vs a weak one) makes buyers delay orders
> Exam angle: A-R pair "Devaluation improves the balance of trade (A true) because it raises export prices (R false)".
- **A-R key:** Devaluation **improves the balance of trade** because it **lowers** export prices abroad and raises import prices
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Reason "devaluation increases price of exports" is false (APPSC-G2 2025: A true, R false)
# 2. Determinants of the exchange rate
- **Determinants:** Inflation differential (**PPP** theory for the long run), fiscal deficit, **CAD**, forex/import cover, short-term debt, interest rates, capital flows
- **Long-run determinants:** **PPP**, savings-investment balance, current account
  - ES 2025-26: long-term anchor = savings-investment balance + current account (**East Asian** lesson: manufacturing exports)
  - Higher imports ≠ depreciation if backed by net investment inflows
# 3. Impossible trinity and capital flows
- **Impossible Trinity (Mundell-Fleming trilemma):** Cannot at once have:
  - Fixed exchange rate + free capital flows + independent monetary policy
  - RBI's dilemma with volatile **FPI** flows

| **Capital flow** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Large inflows | Appreciation (**Dutch disease**) |
| Sudden outflows | Crashes (**1991**, **2013** **Taper Tantrum**) |

## IMF reserve assets and swap arrangements → nature, basket, year | from: S9, S10
# 1. SDR — nature & form
- **SDR:** **Interest-bearing** international reserve asset created by the IMF
  - Supplements members' official reserves; exchangeable for freely usable currencies
- **Form:** **Book-keeping/accounting entries** (APPSC-G2 2025 key; PYQ 2025)
  - Called **"paper gold"**; originally **35 SDR = 1 ounce** of gold
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Where options are only paper currency/gold/silver, the key chose **paper currency** (PYQ 2025)
- **Creation:** **1969** under the IMF's **First Amendment** (some notes date it to **1967**, when it was agreed)
# 2. SDR basket & allocation
- **Basket (since 1 Oct 2016):** **US dollar, euro, Chinese renminbi, Japanese yen, pound sterling**
  - Older notes list only four currencies, without the renminbi
- **Largest general allocation:** About **$650 bn (SDR 456 bn) in August 2021**
# 3. Reserve tranche
- **Reserve (gold) tranche:** Part of a member's **IMF quota** paid in reserve assets (historically gold); drawable any time without conditions
# 4. Currency swap arrangements
- **SAARC Currency Swap Framework:**
  - Instituted by RBI in **2012** for short-term BoP liquidity support to SAARC members
  - **2024-2027** window: dedicated **₹250 billion** INR window + separate **$2 billion** USD-euro facility
  - Bilateral agreements with RBI; **3-month** tenor, rollable twice
  - **Maldives** drew **$400 million** (**Oct 2024**) + the first **₹30 billion** INR drawdown (**Apr 2026**)
- **India-Japan Bilateral Swap Arrangement:** Renewed w.e.f. **28 Feb 2026**
  - Two-way; local currencies against US dollars

## Rupee internationalisation → framework, terms, symbol, bonds | from: S11
# 1. INR trade settlement
- **INR trade settlement framework:** RBI, **July 2022**; built into **Foreign Trade Policy 2023**
  - Invoicing + settlement of exports/imports in rupees
  - Aims to **encourage use of the Indian currency** in cross-border trade; cut dollar dependence and exchange risk
- **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVA):** Foreign banks keep rupee accounts in Indian banks → INR settlement (e.g. **Russia**, **UAE**, **Tanzania**)
  - Since **2025**: surplus SRVA balances can be invested in corporate bonds and **CPs**
  - **Vostro** = "yours" (foreign bank's account with a domestic bank) vs **nostro** = "ours"
- **UAE:** Rupee-dirham settlement operational; rupee accepted at **Dubai** airports from **July 2023**
# 2. Symbol & bonds
- **Rupee symbol ₹:** Adopted **15 July 2010**; designed by **D. Udaya Kumar**
- **Masala bonds:** Rupee-denominated bonds issued abroad; investor bears the currency risk
  - Named by **IFC**; first issued **2014**
  - **Kerala** first state to issue (**2019**)
  - First listed on the **Luxembourg Stock Exchange**
