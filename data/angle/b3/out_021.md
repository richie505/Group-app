=== ROW 82 | Monetary policy - objectives, instruments (CRR, SLR, repo, reverse repo, MSF, OMO, LAF), transmission

## Monetary policy — authority, objectives and framework phases | from: S0
# 1. Monetary policy basics
- **Authority:** Formulated and implemented by the **RBI** under the **RBI Act, 1934** (APPSC-GS 2023)
- **Objectives:** **Price stability** (paramount), growth, financial stability, external balance, development goals
# 2. Phases and key changes

| **Phase** | **Framework** | **Landmarks** |
|---|---|---|
| 1985-1998 | Monetary targeting (M3) with feedback | Chakravarty Committee 1985; Vaghul Committee 1987 (money market) |
| 1998-2016 | Multiple indicator approach (growth, inflation, exchange rate, credit) | LAF 2000; MSS 2004; Base Rate 2010; MSF 2011; MCLR 2016 |
| 2016 onwards | Flexible inflation targeting | Urjit Patel Committee 2014; MPFA 2015; RBI Act amended 2016; SDF 2022 |

- **Key post-1991 change:** **Deregulation of interest rates** (APPSC-GS 2023)
- **Multiple indicator approach dropped:** Opaque → markets could not predict RBI's moves when indicators conflicted
# 3. Persons
- **Seven Ages:** Governor **Shaktikanta Das** traced the phases in "**Seven Ages of India's Monetary Policy**", **24 Jan 2020**, **St. Stephen's College** (APPSC-GS 2024 statement)

## Instruments — quantitative vs qualitative, main instrument | from: S2

| **Basis** | **Quantitative (general)** | **Qualitative (selective)** |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Volume and cost of total credit | Direction of credit to sectors or borrowers |
| Other name | Traditional methods of control | Selective credit control |
| Tools | Bank rate, repo, reverse repo, OMO, CRR, SLR (**variable reserve ratio**) | **Margin requirements**, **moral suasion**, credit rationing, **direct action**, selective credit control, consumer credit regulation |

- **Main instrument:** The **policy repo rate**
  - Single operating policy rate under **FIT** + most-watched signal
  - MPC sets it to hit the inflation target; liquidity kept so money-market rates stay near it
- **OMOs:** Main tool for **durable** liquidity (e.g. ₹1 lakh crore of purchases in Dec 2025)
- **Margin requirement:** Gap between the value of the security and the loan
  - Higher margin = smaller loan against the same security → cuts credit against it
- ⚠ **Moral suasion**, **margin requirements**, **direct action** = qualitative; **CRR, SLR, OMO** = quantitative

## Terms → meaning: policy rates, LAF corridor, stance | from: S3
# 1. Policy rates

| **Instrument** | **Meaning** | **Liquidity effect when raised** | **Rate now** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Repo rate** (policy repo rate) | RBI lends overnight to banks against approved collateral (G-secs); the **policy rate** | Tightens | **5.25%** (MPC, 5 Aug 2026) |
| **SDF** (rate) | Banks park surplus / overnight deposits with RBI **without collateral**; **floor** of the LAF corridor | Absorbs more | **5.00%** |
| **MSF** (rate) | Penal overnight borrowing by dipping into SLR portfolio up to **2%** of NDTL; **ceiling** of the corridor, repo + **25 bps** | Tightens | **5.50%** |
| Bank rate | Rediscount rate / longer-term lending, rediscounting of bills with **no collateral**; aligned with MSF; penal rate for CRR/SLR shortfalls (penal rates linked to it) | Tightens | **5.50%** |
| Fixed-rate reverse repo | RBI absorbs funds against G-secs; used at RBI's discretion since SDF | Absorbs more | **3.35%** (May 2026) |
| **CRR** | Share of NDTL kept as **cash with RBI**; earns no interest | Reduces lendable funds | **3%** of NDTL |
| **SLR** | Share of NDTL held by banks themselves in liquid assets (cash, gold, approved G-secs) | Reduces lendable funds | **18%** |
| **OMO** | Outright purchase or sale of G-secs, done through banks and not directly with the public | Sale absorbs; purchase injects | - |

- **Rate sources:** Rates as of 6 Aug 2026 (MPC unanimous; neutral stance); CRR/SLR/reverse repo as of May 2026
- **Repo cuts:** **125 bps since Feb 2025** (6.50% → 5.25%)
# 2. MSF and the corridor
- **MSF design:**
  - Began **May 2011** at repo + **100 bps**, borrowing up to **1%** of NDTL (older notes)
  - Now **25 bps above repo** (5.25% vs 5.50%)
> Exam angle: order of the corridor - SDF (floor) < repo < MSF = bank rate (ceiling).
- ⚠ MSF borrowing limit: **1%** of NDTL (2011, older notes) vs **2%** now; spread **100 bps** then vs **25 bps** now
# 3. Policy stance terms

| **Stance** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Accommodative | Supports growth, ample liquidity, rate cuts likely |
| Neutral | Rates can move either way, data-dependent |
| Calibrated tightening | No cuts; hikes measured, not at every meeting |
| Hawkish | Worried about inflation; tightens (raises rates, drains liquidity) |
| Dovish | Worried about growth; bias towards easing [GK] |

- **Stance path:** Neutral → **accommodative** (Apr 2025) → **neutral** (**June 2025**) → neutral retained through Aug 2026

## CRR and SLR — features, legal basis, floor and ceiling | from: S4
- **SLR form of holding:** Held by banks themselves (not deposited with RBI) as **cash, gold, unencumbered G-secs/T-bills/SDLs and approved securities** (UPSC)
  - **MSS** securities count towards SLR
- **SLR statutory range:** **0% to 40%** of NDTL
  - Earlier **25% floor** removed by **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2007**
- **SLR trend:** **38.5%** of NDTL in **1991-92** → cut gradually on the **Narasimham** Committee's advice → **18%** now
- **SLR shortfall penalty:** **Bank rate + 3%** a year → **bank rate + 5%** if shortfall continues

| **Point** | **CRR** | **SLR** |
|---|---|---|
| Form | **Cash** balance with RBI | Cash, **gold**, unencumbered **approved securities** held by the bank itself |
| Law | **Sec 42, RBI Act**[GK] | **Sec 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949**[GK] |
| Limits | No floor or ceiling since 2006 [GK] | Ceiling **40%**; RBI range 0-40% |
| Interest | None | Earns interest on G-secs |
| Current level | **3% of NDTL** (**100 bps** cut released about **₹2.5 lakh crore** by Dec 2025, ES 2025-26) | **18%** (UPSC notes; your current lane has no newer value) |

- **Trap:** "Required reserve ratio" = **CRR** (APPSC 2023)
- ⚠ **Sec 42 RBI Act** = CRR; **Sec 24 BR Act** = SLR

## Tool → effect: money supply, demand, transmission channels, bond yields | from: S5, S9, S11
# 1. Dear money vs cheap money

| **Tool** | **To control inflation (dear money, contractionary)** | **To boost growth (cheap money, expansionary)** |
|---|---|---|
| CRR | Raise | Cut |
| SLR | Raise | Cut |
| Repo, reverse repo, bank rate | Raise | Cut |
| OMO | Sell G-secs | Buy G-secs |

- **Repo transmission chain:** Repo up → money-market rates up → costlier credit → lower consumption and investment → lower aggregate demand → inflation cools
- **CRR/SLR cut:** Raises lendable funds and the **money multiplier**
  - M3/M0 = **6.21** on **31 Dec 2025**; **6.0** adjusted for SDF
- **Raising CRR:** Less money in circulation + **lower demand** (APPSC-G1 2022: statements I and II)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Does NOT raise purchasing power or banks' lending capacity
# 2. Transmission channels

| **Channel** | **How a rate cut works** |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | Cheaper loans raise consumption and **investment** |
| Credit | Banks have more funds and lend more |
| Exchange rate | Lower rates can weaken the rupee, helping exports [GK] |
| Asset price | Bond and equity prices rise, creating a wealth effect [GK] |
| Expectations | A credible target anchors inflation expectations |

- **Effect of rate cut:** **Increases investment expenditure** (UPSC 2014)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Does not raise savings or tax collection
- **Mechanism:** Repo change → money-market rates → aggregate demand → inflation
# 3. What moves G-sec yields
- **Drivers of G-sec yields:** **US Federal Reserve's actions** + **RBI's actions** + **inflation and short-term rates** → all three (UPSC 2021)
- **OMO purchases:** **₹1 lakh crore** (Dec 2025) meant to **stabilise bond yields**
- **Sovereign spread:** Spread of Indian yields over US bonds narrowed → lower borrowing costs
  - **S&P** upgraded India from **BBB- to BBB** (2025-26)

## Liquidity management and sterilisation — LAF, SDF, VRR/VRRR, I-CRR, MSS | from: S6, S7
# 1. Short-term windows

| **Tool** | **Meaning / feature** |
|---|---|
| **LAF** | Repo + reverse repo/SDF windows → main short-term liquidity tool; primary window for injecting and absorbing day-to-day liquidity (UPSC) |
| **SDF** | Proposed by RBI in **2015**; enabled by **Budget 2018-19** (RBI Act amendment); began **April 2022**; **absorbs surplus liquidity without collateral** → RBI need not hold G-secs to drain funds (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| **VRR / VRRR** | Auction-based (competitive) LAF tools; last accepted rate = cut-off; VRR injects liquidity; VRRR absorbs it for longer maturities than the overnight SDF → keeps short rates near repo |
| **Fine-tuning operations** | Short VRR/VRRR auctions **to keep liquidity in line with the monetary policy stance**; **49** in **FY2024** (FY24) (APPSC-G2 2025) |

# 2. Durable liquidity and reserve tools
- **I-CRR:** Temporary **10%** incremental CRR announced **10 Aug 2023** → impounded about **₹1.1 lakh crore** (APPSC-G2 2025: both statements correct)
- **OMO:** Outright purchase/sale of G-secs for durable liquidity
  - FY26 injections: **₹2.39 lakh crore** OMO purchases (Apr-May 2025)
- **LTRO (2020):** **1-3 year** funds at the repo rate
- **USD/INR buy-sell swap:** e.g. **$5 bn**, **3-year**, Dec 2025 → injects rupee liquidity without changing the size of RBI's balance sheet
# 3. Sterilisation
- **Sterilisation:** Forex inflows swell rupee liquidity → RBI sells G-secs (**OMO/MSS**) or runs **VRRRs** to offset
  - Sep 2026 case: **FCNR(B)** inflows of **$127.23 bn**
- **MSS (April 2004):** For **sterilisation**; sells securities to mop up surplus liquidity
  - RBI issues T-bills/securities (**MSBs**) to absorb rupees created by forex purchases
  - Proceeds sit in a separate MSS account the Government cannot spend
# 4. Liquidity conditions - surplus vs deficit
- **Surplus vs deficit:** **Surplus liquidity** speeds transmission + pulls market rates below repo vs **deficit liquidity** weakens it
- **Dec 2025 tightness:** Currency leakage, tax outflows, reserve needs → RBI injected **₹1 lakh crore** via OMO + **$5 bn swap**
- **2026 stance:** RBI to keep "sufficient liquidity" for transmission (Governor, Feb 2026)
  - By **3 Sep 2026**: surplus about **₹10.32 lakh crore** → had pushed the call rate below repo; RBI draining via VRRR
  - **4 Sep 2026:** RBI absorbed about **₹6.02 lakh crore** through two **3-day** VRRRs

## Lending rate benchmarks — who sets which rate, committees and directions | from: S8
# 1. Who sets which rate

| **Fixed by RBI/MPC** | **Set by each bank** | **Market-determined** |
|---|---|---|
| Repo (MPC), SDF, MSF, bank rate, reverse repo, CRR, SLR | PLR/BPLR (old), Base Rate (1 Jul 2010), **MCLR** (1 Apr 2016), spread over EBLR | **Call money rate** (WACR), TREPS, T-bill and G-sec yields |

- ⚠ **Trap:** **SLR** and **CRR** are set by RBI, not by banks; MCLR = each bank's **internal** benchmark (each bank computes its own)
# 2. Benchmark evolution

| **Benchmark** | **From** | **Key point** |
|---|---|---|
| PLR | 1994 [GK] | Rate for the best borrowers, set by each bank |
| BPLR | 2003 [GK] | Opaque; banks lent below it |
| **Base rate** | **1 July 2010** | Minimum lending rate, based on the average cost of funds → slow transmission |
| **MCLR** | **1 April 2016** | Based on the **marginal cost of funds** (marginal cost of deposits) + cost of CRR/SLR carry + operating cost + **tenor premium**; an internal benchmark |
| **EBLR** | **1 October 2019** | All new floating-rate **personal/retail and MSME** (MSE) loans are linked to an **external benchmark**: the repo rate, the **3-month** or **6-month** T-bill yield, or an **FBIL**-published rate; loans linked directly to repo → fastest route |

- **Purpose of MCLR** (UPSC 2016: both statements correct):
  - More **transparency** in how banks fix rates
  - **Faster transmission** of policy rates to borrowers
- **MCLR in practice:** **HDFC Bank** **raised MCLR by up to 10 bps** on **8 Jun 2026** → 1-year MCLR **8.40%**
# 3. Committee and directions

| **Committee / direction** | **Year** | **Provision** |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal Study Group on the working of the MCLR system** - chaired by **Janak Raj** | **2017** | Finding: MCLR transmission weak; recommendation: move to an **external benchmark**; outcome: **EBLR mandate from 1 Oct 2019** |
| **Draft RBI (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026** | Proposed effect from **1 April 2027** | Covers banks, RRBs, co-operative banks, AIFIs, NBFCs/HFCs; floating-rate personal and MSME loans stay tied to an external benchmark; interest on the daily reducing balance |

## Issues — challenges after 1991, monetary vs fiscal policy, obstacles to transmission | from: S1, S10
# 1. Challenges after 1991

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Exchange-rate volatility** from capital flows | **Main challenge** (APPSC-GS 2023) |
| **Open capital account** | Inflows/outflows move liquidity and the rupee → RBI sterilises (**MSS, OMO, VRRR**), e.g. **FCNR(B)** inflows of **2026** |
| **External shocks** | Oil prices, global rate cycles, wars (West Asia conflict of 2026) |
| **Fiscal dominance** | Large Government borrowing; automatic monetisation through **ad hoc T-bills** ended in **1997** → replaced by **Ways and Means Advances** |
| **Other** | Weak transmission |

- ⚠ **Trap:** Forex reserves were NOT a problem; inflation was not low; revenue was not in surplus
# 2. Monetary vs fiscal policy - complementary roles against inflation

| **Basis** | **Fiscal policy** | **Monetary policy** |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | Government (Finance Ministry, Parliament) | RBI / MPC |
| Tools | Spending, taxes, subsidies, borrowing | Repo, OMO, CRR/SLR, LAF |
| Effect on demand | Direct | Indirect, through interest rates |
| Flexibility | Rigid, slow; legislative lags (needs legislative approval) | Quick, fast (bi-monthly meetings) |

- **Limit:** Monetary tools are demand-side tools only
  - **Food inflation:** Mostly supply-driven → rate hikes cannot add onions or tomatoes to the market
  - Supply-driven food/fuel inflation needs fiscal steps too: buffer-stock releases, duty cuts and farm reform
# 3. Obstacles to transmission

| **Obstacle** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Administered small-savings rates** | Hold deposit rates up → banks cannot cut lending rates |
| **NPAs** | Weak balance sheets (though GNPA now at a multi-decadal low of **2.2%**, Sep 2025) |
| **Dependence on deposits** | Banks reprice only new deposits |
| **Heavy Government borrowing** | Keeps yields high (crowding out) |
| **Informal credit** | Large unorganised money market outside RBI's reach; people prefer cash |
| **Structural rigidities** | In banking (2026); liquidity mismatches |

## Universal angles | from: S13 | universal
- **Current relevance:**
  - ES 2025-26: **100 bps repo cut to 5.25%**; **100 bps CRR cut to 3%**; OMO purchases **₹2.39 lakh crore** (Apr-May 2025) + **₹1 lakh crore** (Dec 2025)
  - Liquidity then swung to a record surplus by Sep 2026
  - Aug 2026 MPC minutes point to a possible hike
  - Draft Interest Rate Directions 2026 (in force from **1 Apr 2027**): "improve transmission" + prevent excessive pricing for small borrowers (Aug 2026)
- **Significance:** Corridor + **SDF** → collateral-free way to absorb large surpluses
- **Comparative note:** MSF = repo + 25 bps; SDF = repo - 25 bps → LAF corridor **50 bps** wide (**5.00-5.50%**)
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - Weak pass-through to deposit rates; large firms shifting to non-bank credit
  - Repo = demand-side tool → cannot fix food or fuel supply shocks; RBI has little reach over food inflation
  - Weak transmission from rigidities in banking (Sep 2026)

=== ROW 83 | Monetary Policy Committee and the flexible inflation targeting framework

## Framework — objectives and phases from monetary targeting (1985) to FIT (2016) | from: S0, S2
# 1. Objectives
- **Primary objective / mandate:** **Price stability**
  - Growth second, "keeping in mind" the target (RBI Act)
  - Financial stability also a goal
- **Not an objective:**
  - Maximising Government revenue
  - Regulating the stock market or mutual funds (**SEBI**)
  - Fixing the rupee-dollar rate
# 2. Phases of the framework
- **Group-II table:**

| **Phase** | **Approach** |
|---|---|
| 1985-1998 | Monetary targeting (Chakravarty) [GK] |
| **1998-2014** | **Multiple-indicator approach**: inflation, growth, exchange rate, credit, etc. [GK for the start year] |
| 2014-2016 | Glide path: Urjit Patel Committee, then the 2015 Agreement |
| **2016 onwards** | **Flexible inflation targeting** with the MPC |

- **Multiple-indicator approach:** Criticised as **opaque** → markets could not predict RBI when indicators pointed different ways
- **Challenges after 1991:** **Exchange-rate volatility** (APPSC 2023 key) + open capital account + external shocks + fiscal dominance

## Committees → recommendation | from: S0

| **Committee** | **Year** | **Contribution / recommendation** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sukhamoy Chakravarty** Committee | 1985 | Organised money market (UPSC); monetary targeting (money supply growth as the anchor) with feedback [GK] |
| **N. Vaghul** | 1987 | Blueprint for the money market; led to Commercial Paper (1990) (UPSC; APP) |
| **S.S. Tarapore** | 1997; 2nd report 2006 | Roadmap to capital account convertibility (UPSC; APP) |
| **Urjit Patel** Committee (Expert Committee to Revise and Strengthen the Monetary Policy Framework; set up by RBI) | **2014** | Drop the multiple-indicator approach for **FIT**; **CPI as nominal anchor** (CPI-C); **4% ±2%** target; a committee (MPC) to decide rates (CDI) |

- **Recent committee:** Committee to strengthen the monetary policy framework headed by **Urjit Patel** (APPSC-G2 2017 key)
- ⚠ **1985** Chakravarty – **1987** Vaghul – **1997** Tarapore – **2014** Urjit Patel

## Acts, agreements and Sections → provision | from: S1, S3, S4, S5
# 1. Agreement and Act

| **Act / agreement** | **Year** | **Provision** |
|---|---|---|
| **Monetary Policy Framework Agreement** | Signed by **Government of India and RBI in 2015** | Formally adopted flexible inflation targeting; Government sets the target; RBI keeps CPI inflation at **4% +/- 2%**; RBI **answerable to the Government** when it misses |
| **RBI (Amendment) Act, 2016** | 2016 | Inserted **Sections 45Z to 45ZO** into the RBI Act, 1934; APPSC-GS 2022 key: amended in **2016** |

> Exam angle: "In which year was the RBI Act amended for FIT?" The answer is **2016**.
# 2. Sections

| **Section** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Section 45ZA** | **Central Government, in consultation with RBI**, sets the target **once every five years**; notified in the Official Gazette |
| **Section 45ZB** | MPC constituted as a statutory body |
| **Section 45ZN** | Failure = average CPI **above 6% or below 2% for three consecutive quarters**; RBI reports to the Government (the Centre) the **reasons, remedial action and time to return** to target (time needed) |

- ⚠ **Trap:** Target is **45ZA, not 45ZB**; many books wrongly print 45ZB
- **45ZN wording:** The Act says **quarters**
  - **Conflict:** Some notes say "3 consecutive months"; others (2026) say **quarters** → **quarters** is correct
- **First invoked:** **2022**, after CPI stayed above 6% from **January 2022**

## Monetary Policy Committee — basis, composition, voting, quorum | from: S5
# 1. Statutory basis and role
- **Basis:** Statutory body constituted under **Section 45ZB** of the RBI Act, created by the **2016** amendment
- **First meeting:** **October 2016**
- **Role:** **MPC fixes the policy repo rate** needed to reach the inflation target; decisions bind the RBI (APPSC-G2 2017; APPSC-GS 2019 keys)
  - Before this: Governor decided alone
# 2. Composition and procedure

| **Member** | **How** |
|---|---|
| RBI Governor | Chairperson (ex officio); **casting vote** on a tie |
| Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy | Ex officio |
| One RBI officer | Nominated by the RBI Central Board |
| Three external members | Appointed by the **Central Government**, **4-year** term |

- **Members:** **6 members** = 3 from RBI + 3 from Government
- **Voting:** One vote each; decided by **majority vote** (UPSC)
  - **Governor has a casting vote** in a tie
- **Meetings:** **At least 4 times a year** (UPSC); in practice bi-monthly (six times)
- **Quorum:** **4**, including the Governor or, in his absence, the Deputy Governor
- **Reappointment:** External members not eligible
- ⚠ **6** members – quorum **4** – meets at least **4** times – external term **4** years
