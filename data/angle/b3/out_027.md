=== ROW 104 | Deficit financing - meaning, methods and consequences

## Deficit financing — meaning, purpose and plan phases | from: S0, S1
# 1. Meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Indian usage** | Meeting budget gap by **borrowing from the RBI** → govt "buys money"; RBI creates new money (APPSC 2019 answer: RBI) |
| **Western usage** | Any financing of a budget deficit, mainly borrowing from the **public/market** |
| **Fiscal deficit (Union Budget)** | = budgetary deficit + net increase in internal and external borrowings |

- **Effect:** **Increase in money supply** (UPPSC 2016)
- ⚠ Indian = borrowing from **RBI**; Western = borrowing from the **public/market**
# 2. Purpose in Indian planning
- **Purpose:** Raising resources for **economic development** (APPSC 2019) → **"pragmatic tool"** to finance **Five Year Plans**
- **Why needed after 1947:**
  - Low tax base + poor compliance
  - Weak **privately owned banking** sector
  - Low **domestic savings** rate
# 3. Phases
- Post-independence to late **1960s**: limited use to fund plans
- **1970s** onwards: heavy reliance (oil shocks + welfare spending widened deficits) → debt and inflation worries

## Acts and agreements controlling deficit financing | from: S2
| **Agreement / Act** | **Year** | **Provision** |
|---|---|---|
| RBI-Government agreement | **September 1994** | Began phasing out **ad hoc T-bills** |
| RBI-Government agreement | **March 1997** | Ended them → **WMA** from **1 April 1997** |
| **FRBM Act, 2003** (APPSC 2019) | 2003 | RBI **not to subscribe to primary issues** of central government securities from **1 April 2006** → bars RBI from financing the Centre in normal times |
| **2018 amendment** | 2018 | Permits it only on **escape-clause** grounds → monetised borrowing only under **escape clause** |

- **Escape-clause cap:** Deviation capped at **0.5% of GDP** a year
- **Liquidity tool now:** **OMOs** → sale of G-Secs absorbs liquidity; purchase injects it

## Consequences — mode of financing → effect | from: S3
# 1. Effects
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Inflation** | RBI financing raises money supply + demand without more goods → **demand-pull inflation**; hurts the poor most |
| **Crowding out** | Heavy market borrowing absorbs loanable funds → raises interest rates → squeezes private investment |
| **Debt trap** | Borrowing just to pay interest → **committed liability** → little room for health, education, infrastructure |
| **Rating downgrades** | Raise borrowing cost for government + Indian firms |
# 2. Inflationary ranking
| **Mode of financing** | **Inflationary effect** |
|---|---|
| Creation of new money | **Most inflationary** (UPSC 2021) |
| Borrowing from banks | High |
| Borrowing from the public | Lower (uses existing savings) |
| Repayment of public debt | Not inflationary (withdraws money) |

=== ROW 105 | Fiscal policy - objectives, instruments; fiscal, revenue, primary and effective revenue deficits

## Fiscal policy — definition and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
# 1. Definition
- **Fiscal policy:** Govt's policy on its **revenue and expenditure** (APPSC G2 2008)
  - Covers level of purchases, transfers, tax structure, borrowing
  - Influences **aggregate demand**, allocation, distribution
- **Etymology:** Latin **fiscus** = "basket"/public purse (some notes say Greek)
- **Made by:** **Ministry of Finance**, with legislative backing
- **Union Budget:** Prepared by **Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)** = "Annual Financial Statement" under **Art. 112**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** The word "Budget" does not appear in the Constitution
# 2. FIRSTs
| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| First theory linking fiscal policy to economic performance | **J.M. Keynes**, General Theory, **1936** → basis of active demand management |
| Long-Term Fiscal Policy | **1985**, Finance Minister **V.P. Singh** |

## Persons → contributions to fiscal policy | from: S1, S2
| **Person** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **J.M. Keynes** | General Theory (1936); active demand management |
| **V.P. Singh** | Presented Long-Term Fiscal Policy (1985) as Finance Minister |
| **Richard (R.A.) Musgrave** | Three budget functions; term **fiscal federalism** popularised (**1959**) |
| **N.K. Singh** | Committee **2016**, report **2017**; debt anchor **60%** = Centre **40%** + States **20%** |
# 1. Musgrave's three budget functions
| **Function** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Allocation** | Provide **public goods** and allocate resources, the **primary purpose** of a budget (APPSC GS 2023) |
| **Distribution/redistribution** | Transfers + taxes change disposable income towards a fair outcome; reduce inequality through **progressive taxation** (APPSC GS 2019) and welfare spending |
| **Stabilisation** | Expand or reduce demand to smooth the cycle: control inflation and unemployment |

## Fiscal rules → year → target (FRBM to debt anchor) | from: S1
| **Rule** | **Target** |
|---|---|
| **FRBM Act 2003** | FD **3%**, RD zero; amended **2018** |
| **FRBM (2018) ceilings** | **FD 3% by Mar 2021**; general govt debt **60%**; **Centre 40%** by **Mar 2025** |
| **FY 2021-22 commitment** | FD **below 4.5% by 2025-26** → met at **4.4%** |
| **Debt-anchor rule from 2026-27** | **Debt-GDP ratio replaces fiscal deficit as the anchor**; FD calibrated each year to keep **debt falling** |

- Central debt **55.6% (BE 2026-27)**, down from **56.1% (RE 2025-26)**
- Target **50 ± 1% by March 2031**

## Objectives of fiscal policy — what is and is not an objective | from: S2
- **Objectives (UPSC):**
  - **Full employment**, **price stability**, **economic growth**
  - **Equitable distribution** of income and wealth (reducing inequality)
  - Resource mobilisation + balanced regional development
- **Progressive taxation evidence:**
  - Direct taxes = **58.8%** of total taxes in **FY25**
  - ITRs: **6.9 crore** (**FY22**) → **9.2 crore** (**FY25**)
- **Not fiscal-policy objectives:** **Directing interest rates**, managing liquidity and money supply → belong to **monetary policy**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC GS 2023 distractor

## Instruments of fiscal policy — fiscal vs monetary policy | from: S3
- **Tools:** **Taxation**, **public expenditure** (capex as **"pump priming"**), **public borrowing/debt**, the **budget**, subsidies/**DBT**, **PLI**
- **Public borrowing:**
  - Internal = bonds, T-bills, small savings
  - External = multilateral, sovereign bonds
  - **Finances the fiscal deficit**
- **Public expenditure:** About **28% of GDP** (ES 2016-17; APPSC G2 2017)

| **Policy** | **Authority** | **Tools** | **Speed** | **Impact** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fiscal** | Govt (MoF) | **Taxation, public expenditure, public debt/borrowing, subsidies**, DBT, PLI | Slow (budget cycle) | Direct, targeted |
| Monetary | RBI / MPC | Repo, CRR, SLR, OMOs, LAF | Fast (bi-monthly MPC) | Indirect, via credit |
| Trade | Ministry of Commerce | Tariffs, quotas, export incentives, MEP [GK] | - | - |

## Deficits → formula and meaning | from: S4
| **Deficit** | **Formula** | **What it shows** |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue deficit (RD)** | **Revenue expenditure - revenue receipts** | Borrowing for day-to-day consumption (**dissaving**) |
| **Effective revenue deficit (ERD)** | RD - **grants for creation of capital assets** (Rangarajan Committee concept) | "True" consumption deficit; grants to States that build assets (e.g., MGNREGA roads/ponds) are not counted as consumption |
| **Fiscal deficit (FD)** | **Total expenditure - total receipts excluding borrowings** = RD + capital expenditure - non-debt capital receipts = borrowings and other liabilities | Govt's **total borrowing requirement** from all sources |
| **Gross fiscal deficit** / gross vs net FD | Same as FD (gross of the Centre's net lending); net FD = gross FD minus net lending of the Centre | Net FD removes lending that comes back |
| **Primary deficit (PD)** | **FD - interest payments** | Current year's imbalance excluding the burden of past debt |
| **Budget deficit** (old concept) | Total expenditure - total receipts (revenue + capital, including borrowings) = revenue account deficit + capital account deficit; FD = budget deficit + borrowing | Used when ad hoc T-bills financed the gap |
| **Monetised deficit** | Part of the deficit financed by **RBI** (borrowing from RBI / increase in RBI credit to government / printing fresh currency = "high-powered money") | Inflationary; FRBM disallows it under normal conditions |

- **ERD:** Introduced in **Union Budget 2011-12**; written into FRBM by **2012 amendment**
- **Budget deficit:** Stopped being headline measure after **ad hoc Treasury Bills** abolished (**1997**)
> Exam angle: APPSC 2019 - budget deficit + borrowings and other liabilities = **fiscal deficit**; APPSC 2023 - RD = revenue expenditure - **revenue receipts**.
> Exam angle: definitions asked directly ("total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowing" = fiscal deficit; RD = revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts).

## Relationship between deficits — which is larger, zero deficits, worked example | from: S5
# 1. Which is larger
- **FD vs PD:** **PD = FD - interest payments** → with positive interest, **FD > PD** always
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "PD always higher than FD" is **false** (APPSC 2023)
- **FD vs RD:** **FD > RD** when capex exceeds non-debt capital receipts
- **ERD vs RD:** **ERD <= RD**, because grants for capital assets are deducted
- **2026-27 BE:** **FD 4.3% > RD 1.5% > PD 0.7%** of GDP
# 2. Zero and negative deficits
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Zero primary deficit** | FD = interest payments → borrowing only to pay interest on past loans → **debt-trap** signal |
| **Debt trap** | Borrowing to make interest payments |
| **Zero revenue deficit** | = **balanced revenue budget**; large RD share in FD → borrowing funds consumption |
| **Primary surplus (negative PD)** | Non-interest spending fully covered by **non-debt receipts** |
# 3. Worked example (₹)
- **Inputs:**
  - Revenue receipts **3,00,000**; capital receipts **1,60,000** (loan recoveries and other **10,000** + borrowings **1,50,000**)
  - Revenue expenditure **3,50,000**; capex **1,10,000**; interest **1,20,000**
- **Results:**
  - RD = 3,50,000 - 3,00,000 = **50,000**
  - FD = 4,60,000 - 3,10,000 = **1,50,000** (= borrowings)
  - PD = 1,50,000 - 1,20,000 = **30,000**
- **As % of GDP:** FD **4** on GDP **400** = **1% of GDP**
> Exam angle: give a budget table, ask FD/PD/RD; remember FD always equals borrowings and other liabilities.

## Financing the fiscal deficit — RBI, ad hoc T-bills, WMA, market borrowings | from: S6
# 1. Deficit financing - meaning and effects
- **Deficit financing (Indian usage):** Covering the gap by drawing down **cash balances**, **borrowing from RBI** or issuing **new currency**
- **Effects:**
  - RBI money creation → money supply ↑ → **inflation**; money-printing (RBI financing) = **most inflationary route**
  - Borrowing from public → absorbs liquidity (can slow private activity)
  - External borrowing → **currency risk**
# 2. Borrowing from RBI - terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Ad hoc Treasury Bills** | Automatic monetisation of Centre's deficit by RBI; **Abolished from 1 April 1997** (**1994** RBI-Government agreement); replaced by **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** |
| **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** | Temporary RBI advances to **Centre/States** to bridge short mismatches in receipts and payments (day-to-day cash) |
| **Overdraft** | Use of WMA beyond the limit |

- **FRBM:** RBI barred from subscribing to **primary issues** of Government securities (from **2006-07**) except under **escape clause** → monetised deficit disallowed only "under normal conditions"
# 3. Sources of financing the fiscal deficit
- **Market borrowings** (dated G-Secs, T-bills, bonds); loans from domestic and foreign sources
- **Small savings / NSSF**, provident funds
- **External** loans
- Net home borrowing also includes indirect borrowing from banks via **SLR**
- **Budget 2026-27:** **Borrowings and other liabilities = 24 paise of every rupee** of receipts (largest single item)

## Terms → meaning: deficit quality, twin deficit, fiscal stance, multiplier | from: S7, S8
# 1. Quality of deficit and twin deficit
- **High RD/FD ratio:** Borrowing funds consumption, not investment
- **2026-27 BE:** RD **1.5** / FD **4.3** = roughly **one-third** of borrowing still goes to revenue spending (derived from Budget 2026-27)
- **Twin deficit:** Fiscal deficit + current account deficit together
  - Higher government dissaving → lower national savings → wider savings-investment gap met by foreign capital
- **CAD financing sources:**
  - **FDI** (most desirable); **FPI** (volatile)
  - ECBs, NRI deposits, forex reserve drawdown, IMF/World Bank loans
# 2. Fiscal stance
| **Stance** | **Action** | **Use / risk** |
|---|---|---|
| Expansionary | Higher spending / lower taxes | Recessions; risk of inflation |
| Contractionary | Lower spending / higher taxes | Overheating; risk to growth and jobs |
| Neutral | Balanced effect on demand | Stable equilibrium |

- **Recession response:** **More spending on public projects** (UPSC PYQ)
- **Counter-cyclical (ideal):** Leans against the cycle - deficits in recession, consolidation in booms (e.g. **Atma Nirbhar Bharat, 2020**)
- **Pro-cyclical:** Cutting in slumps, spending in booms → deepens volatility
- **Related terms:** **Fiscal drag**, **pump priming**, **fiscal neutrality**
# 3. Crowding out, crowding in and multiplier
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Crowding out** | Heavy **govt borrowing** absorbs loanable funds → **pushes up interest rates** → **private investment falls** (UPSC 2026 key) |
| **Crowding in** | Public capex builds infrastructure → draws private investment |
| **Fiscal multiplier** | = change in GDP / change in govt spending (or taxes) |
| **Capex multiplier** | Estimated **2.5-3** in India → larger for **capital expenditure** than revenue spending or transfers |

=== ROW 106 | FRBM Act - targets, escape clause, N.K. Singh Committee; fiscal consolidation path

## FRBM Act, 2003 — origin, FIRSTs, objectives and original targets | from: S0, S8
# 1. Origin and FIRSTs
| **FIRST / origin** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| **Introduced by** | **Yashwant Sinha** |
| **Enacted** | **2003** for **fiscal discipline** (APPSC 2019) |
| **Rules** | FRBM Rules notified **2004**, in force from **5 July 2004** |
| First Budget in India | **1860** (**James Wilson**) |
| First Budget of free India | **1947** (**R.K. Shanmugham Chetty**) |
# 2. Objectives
- **Inter-generational equity**; long-run macroeconomic stability
- Elimination of the **revenue deficit**; reduce FD
- Institutionalise fiscal discipline; improve macro management
- Transparency; balanced budget over time
# 3. Original targets
| **Original target** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| Fiscal deficit | **Fiscal deficit 3% of GDP by 31 March 2008** (original deadline) |
| Revenue deficit | **Eliminate** - **Revenue deficit eliminated by 31 March 2009** (deadline pushed to March 2009) |
| Annual reduction (Rules) | **Annual reduction:** FD by **0.3%** and RD by **0.5%** of GDP each year |

- **Tax-GDP ratio:** Not a legal target; one of the four indicators projected in the **Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement**

## FRBM Act — mandated statements; what it prescribes and does not | from: S1
# 1. Statements
| **Statement (laid under Section 3 of the Act)** | **Content** |
|---|---|
| **Macro-Economic Framework Statement** | GDP growth prospects, inflation, external balance (CAD, capital flows), savings-investment, monetary conditions, assumptions and risks |
| **Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement** | **3-year rolling targets** (**Three-year rolling targets**) for 4 indicators: **RD, FD, tax revenue** (tax-GDP), **total outstanding liabilities** (as % of GDP) |
| **Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement** | Tax, expenditure, borrowing and debt strategy for the year; how current policy fits FRBM |
| **Medium-Term Expenditure Framework Statement** | 3-year expenditure projections / ceilings (added by the **2012** amendment); laid after the Budget |

- **Mandate:** All four are statements the Act mandates to be laid in **Parliament** (2026)
- **Rolling targets:** MTFPS targets **not given since 2021-22** - three-year rolling targets **suspended since 2021-22** for post-pandemic flexibility
- **Budget 2026-27:** Laid the **MEFS** and a combined **MTFPS cum FPSS**
# 2. Prescribes vs does not
- **Prescribes:**
  - Deficit limits + statements laid with the Budget
  - Transparency disclosures (borrowings, contingent liabilities)
  - Cap on guarantees: additional guarantees against the Consolidated Fund capped at **0.5% of GDP** a year
- **Does NOT:**
  - Provide **penal provisions** for missing targets (APPSC-G2 2017)
  - Make the Centre answerable for **States' failures**/targets (APPSC-G2 2017)
  - Apply to States → States have their own Acts
> Exam angle: "FRBM does NOT provide penal provisions" - the favourite trap.

# 3. N.K. Singh Committee — recommendations

|---|---|

## FRBM amendments and suspension episodes → year → change | from: S4
| **Year** | **Change** |
|---|---|
| 2004 | FRBM Rules notified; annual reduction targets; RD elimination deadline moved to 2009 |
| 2008-09 | Targets suspended after the **global financial crisis** (for stimulus) |
| 2012 | **Effective revenue deficit** (**ERD**) concept/target and **Medium-Term Expenditure Framework Statement** added |
| 2015 | Target dates deferred; targets re-set (FD 3% by 2017-18) |
| **2018** | **Debt anchor** (60/40: general govt 60%, Centre 40% by 2024-25, i.e. by 31 March 2025); **FD 3% by 31 March 2021**; RD/ERD targets dropped; escape clause with **0.5%** cap; buoyancy clause |
| 2020-21 | Escape clause used; COVID deviations; no amendment, to keep flexibility |

- **Status:** "FRBM Act, 2003 (amended 2018)" (2026)
- **2008 global financial crisis:** Targets suspended for stimulus
- **COVID 2020-21:** FD **9.2% of GDP**; targets recalibrated with a gradual return path
> Exam angle: BPSC 2023 - FD reached **9.2%** in FY21 (true; statement 1 correct).

## Escape clause and buoyancy clause — conditions and deviation limit | from: S5
- **Grounds (escape clause triggers):**
  - **National security, war, national calamity** (security threats, disasters)
  - Collapse of agriculture
  - **Structural reforms** with unanticipated fiscal effects
  - Severe economic slowdown / sharp fall in real output growth (**3 percentage points** below the average of the previous four quarters) (**2018 amendment**)
- **Deviation limit:** **Not more than 0.5% of GDP** in a year
- **Accountability:** Government must justify the deviation + give a path back to the target, placed before **Parliament**
- **Buoyancy clause:** If real output growth rises sharply → FD must fall by **at least 0.25% of GDP**
- ⚠ Escape clause = deviation up to **0.5%**; buoyancy clause = FD cut of at least **0.25%**

## Fiscal consolidation path — data and the new debt rule | from: S6
| **Year** | **Fiscal deficit (% of GDP)** |
|---|---|
| 2020-21 | **9.2%** (pandemic peak) |
| 2024-25 | 4.8% |
| 2025-26 | **4.4%** (target "below 4.5%" set in FY 2021-22 - met) |
| 2026-27 BE | **4.3%** |
# 1. Past targets & deviation
- **2018 amendment targets:** FD **3% by 31 March 2021**; general government debt **60%** + Central debt **40%** of GDP **by 31 March 2025**
- **Deviation acknowledged:** From the Act's **3% FD** and **40% Central debt** targets (FRBM deviation statement)
- **Halving commitment:** Halve 2020-21 FD (9.2%) in five years → met: **4.4% in 2025-26** (not a legislative target)
# 2. New fiscal rule (from 2026-27)
- **New anchor / debt rule:** FD set each year to keep **Central debt falling to 50 ± 1% of GDP by 31 March 2031** (**55.6%** in BE 2026-27); FD **4.3% (2026-27 BE)**
- **Shift:** Debt, not FD, is now the primary target; **50%** is above FRBM's **40%**
# 3. Views & recommendations
- **ES 2025-26:** Argued for delaying strict FRBM-type targets to keep flexibility in a volatile geopolitical environment; warned **States' finances are worsening** (30 Jan 2026)
- **16th FC:** Centre's FD to **3.5% of GDP by 2030-31**; States **3% of GSDP**

# 2. AP FRBM Act
- **Name:** **Andhra Pradesh Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2005** (**AP FRBM Act, 2005**; some notes say **2009**)
- **Targets:** Eliminate the revenue deficit; fiscal deficit at **3% of GSDP**
- **Amendments:** From time to time to match Finance Commission borrowing limits
- **Effect:** FRBM limits restrain AP's open-market borrowing
- **AP BE 2026-27:** FD **3.84%**, RD **1.11%** of GSDP → above (remains above) the **3%** FRBM norm (15 Feb 2026)
- **Slippage:** FD **5.05%** vs mandated **4%** ceiling; borrowings **₹81,071 crore** (7 Mar 2026)

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
# 1. Significance
- **Significance:** FRBM made fiscal discipline a legal duty + created the Budget's disclosure statements
# 2. Current relevance
- **Current relevance:** Budget 2026-27 (1 Feb 2026) switched to a debt-GDP rule (**50 ± 1% by 2031**); rolling FD targets still suspended
- **FY27 FD:** **26.8%** of target by July (1 Sep 2026)
- **Expert view:** Want a published debt and FD glide path with a nominal GDP assumption (5 Feb 2026)
# 3. Criticism & debates
- **Criticism & debates:** Frequent escape-clause use + target deferrals; FRBM targets missed repeatedly
- **Design gaps:** No penalty clause; no independent fiscal council yet
- **New rule:** Tolerates higher debt than FRBM's **40%**
# 4. AP angle
- **AP angle:** AP FD **3.84%** (BE 2026-27) remains above the 3% norm (see State laws page)
