## Harrod-Domar model — terms and equation | from: S1
- **Equation:** **g = s / k** → growth rises with more saving, falls with a higher capital-output ratio
- **k:** Extra capital needed for one extra unit of output → measures productivity of capital
- **Bottleneck:** **limited aggregate savings**
- **Multiplier:** More investment raises output and employment
- **Used in:** First Plan (1951-56)
> The answer is **Harrod-Domar** (APPSC 2022, 2024 and 2025).

## Mahalanobis model — strategy, sectors, omissions, outcome | from: S2
# 1. Strategy and sectors
- **Feldman-Mahalanobis:** Second Plan model; bottleneck = **scarcity of capital goods** (vs Harrod-Domar's limited savings)
- **Strategy:** **Unbalanced growth strategy**; priority to the **capital goods sector** (steel, machinery)
  - Idea: building machines at home → later self-sufficiency in consumer goods
- **Four-sector version:**
  - (1) capital goods
  - (2) factory consumer goods
  - (3) household/small-scale consumer goods
  - (4) services such as health and education
- ⚠ Bottleneck: **Harrod-Domar** = limited savings; **Mahalanobis** = capital goods; **wage-goods** = food
# 2. Omissions and criticisms
| **Included / emphasised** | **Excluded / neglected** |
|---|---|
| **Import substitution**, self-reliance | **Foreign trade** - **Assumed a closed economy** (no foreign trade) |
| Dominant **public sector** ("socialistic pattern of society", IPR 1956) | Consumer goods left to small/cottage sector |
| Raise investment from **7% to 11%** of national income | Exports / export promotion |
- **Not included:** **neglected agriculture**
- **Criticisms:** Shortages of food and capital, severe food shortages, **high inflation** by the late **1950s**
# 3. Outcome - Second Plan
- **Growth:** Target **4.5%**, achieved **4.21%**
  - Some notes say target **7.5%**, achieved **4.1%**
- **Legacy:**
  - Steel plants: **Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela**
  - Chittaranjan locomotives, Perambur coaches, Atomic Energy Commission
- **Goal adopted:** **Socialistic pattern of society** (Industrial Policy **1956**)

## Alternatives to Mahalanobis — wage-goods model and the Janata strategy | from: S4, S5
# 1. Wage-goods model (Vakil-Brahmananda)
- **Core idea:** Real constraint = supply of **wage goods** (food and essential consumer goods), not capital goods
  - More wage goods → disguised unemployed put to productive work; stresses employment
- **Priority:** **Agriculture and wage goods** vs Mahalanobis's heavy-industry priority
# 2. Janata government alternative
- **Janata Party:** Came to power in **1977**, during the Fifth Plan
  - **Ended the Fifth Plan a year early (1978)**
  - Adopted a **Rolling Plan (1978-80)**
- **Emphasis:** **Agriculture and rural development, employment, small and cottage industries**, minimum needs → rather than heavy industry

## NITI Aayog vs Planning Commission — comparison | from: S7
| **Aspect** | **Planning Commission (1950)** | **NITI Aayog (2015)** |
|---|---|---|
| Set up | **15 March 1950**, Cabinet resolution; first chairman **Nehru** | 1 January 2015, Cabinet resolution |
| Money / funds | Allocated plan funds and grants to ministries and States | **No fund-allocation power** (**no power to allocate funds**); advisory |
| Approach / planning | Top-down, one-size-fits-all Five-Year Plans | **Bottom-up**, cooperative federalism; Vision-Strategy-Action Agenda |
| States' role | Limited to the **National Development Council** (NDC) and annual plan meetings/talks | Every CM sits on the **Governing Council**; gives States a direct role |
| Members | Up to **8 full-time**; no part-time members | Full-time + up to 2 part-time members |
| Top official / secretary | Deputy Chairman (Cabinet rank) and Member-Secretary through usual process | Vice-Chairperson and **CEO** appointed by the PM |
- **Plan approval (old):** Planning Commission plans went to the **NDC** for approval
  - NDC members: PC members, CMs, UT administrators, Union Ministers
- **Reference:** UPSC notes p.566

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
- **Significance:** top-down Planning Commission → collaborative partnership; state rankings drive competitive federalism
- **Current relevance:**
  - Reconstituted **April 2026**: Ashok Kumar Lahiri as VC
  - Membership moved away from economists → science and health members
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - Recommendations only advisory; States' role advisory
  - Critics point to central dominance; some states skip meetings
  - No financial / fund-allocation powers → influence depends on persuasion; political coordination, not fiscal redistribution
- **Comparative note:** Zonal Councils = **statutory** bodies under States Reorganisation Act, 1956
  - Chaired by Union Home Minister; NITI representative attends as adviser
