=== ROW 28 | Population policy of India - NPP 2000, family welfare programme, ageing and migration issues

## Population policy — milestones and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
- **First country:** India = **first country in the world** to launch a national family planning programme, in **1952** (APPSC-GS key)

| **Phase** | **Approach** |
|---|---|
| 1950s (1st-2nd Plans) | Clinic-based approach |
| 1960s | Extension-education approach, targets |
| **1976** | **First National Population Policy** (first comprehensive policy, 16 April; marriage age raised to 21/18); 42nd Amendment put population control in the **Concurrent List**; coercive sterilisation during the 1976-77 Emergency |
| 1977 | Renamed the **Family Welfare Programme** after the Emergency's forced sterilisations |
| **1996**-97 | **Target-free approach** (1996), then Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) from 1997 |
| 2000 | **NPP 2000**: voluntary, informed choice, target-free; declared **15 February 2000** |

- **Phases/shift:** (APPSC-GS 2019 key)
- ⚠ **1952** = first programme; **1976** = first policy; **1977** = renamed Family Welfare; **2000** = NPP

## NPP 2000 — objectives, targets, bodies and shortcomings | from: S1
# 1. Basics
- **Approach:** Target-free, voluntary, informed choice; promotes the **two-child norm**
- **Delivery:** Government + industry + NGOs
# 2. Objectives and stabilisation target
| **Objective** | **Content / target** |
|---|---|
| Immediate | Meet the **unmet need for contraception**, health infrastructure and personnel, integrated reproductive and child health services |
| Medium-term | Bring TFR to **replacement level (2.1) by 2010** |
| Long-term | **Stable population by 2045** (UKPSC 2024 key; some notes say 2046) |

- **Stabilisation year:** **2045**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes say **2046** - WRONG; the policy says **2045** (PYQ keys)
- !! Not in your sources: the revised stabilisation year projected after 2000.
# 3. Population bodies and reports
| **Body / report** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **National Commission on Population** | Set up **2000** under NPP 2000; **chaired by the Prime Minister**; members include Chief Ministers + Union ministers |
| **State of World Population** report | Published by **UNFPA** |
| **NFHS** | MoHFW, through **IIPS Mumbai** |

# 4. Shortcomings
- **Narrow view:** Family-planning-centred; narrow focus on sterilisation
- **Legacy:** Emergency-era coercion left distrust
- **TFR 2.1:** Reached only around NFHS-5 (2019-21), not by 2010

=== ROW 29 | Poverty - concepts (absolute, relative, chronic), poverty line debates

## Types of poverty → meaning | from: S0
| **Type** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Absolute | Lack of minimum resources for survival; set by a fixed consumption/calorie norm (India's focus) |
| Relative | Income well below the society's median; compares fractile groups; persists in the USA and UK |
| Subjective | A person's own sense of their relative position (UPSC) |
| Chronic | Poverty that lasts over long periods or generations [GK] |
| Transient / cyclical | Temporary poverty from shocks, seasons or cycles [GK] |
| Culture of poverty | **Oscar Lewis**[GK]: poverty passed on as a way of life |

- **Absolute poverty definition:** **World Summit for Social Development, Copenhagen, 1995**
- **Absolute poverty (APSES 2024-25):** Measured by World Bank line
- **Relative poverty:** Widely used in developed economies
- ⚠ **Absolute** = India's focus; **relative** = developed economies

## Poverty measures and laws → formula (HCR, PGI, FGT, Engel) | from: S1
| **Measure** | **Captures** | **Formula** |
|---|---|---|
| Head Count Ratio | **Incidence**: share of population below the line | HC = Nq / N |
| Poverty Gap Index | **Depth**: average shortfall from the line | (1/n) Σ (Z − yi)/Z |
| Squared Poverty Gap | **Severity**: more weight to the poorest | (1/n) Σ [(Z − yi)/Z]² |

- **Family:** **Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT)**
- **HCR vs PGI:** HCR unchanged if poor get poorer; PGI changes
- **Engel's Law:** As family income rises, share spent on food falls
- ⚠ **Incidence – depth – severity** = **HCR – PGI – squared gap**

## Poverty line in India — basis, calorie norm, Tendulkar vs Rangarajan | from: S2, S3
# 1. Basis
- **Basis:** **consumption expenditure** (MPCE from NSS / HCES), not income
  - Poor = consumption below the line (APPSC-GS 2024 key: statements A and B correct)
- **Calorie norm (ICMR, Alagh):** **2,400 kcal rural** / **2,100 kcal urban** per capita per day
- **Poverty Line Basket (PLB):** Socially accepted minimum basket of basic needs bought in the market
  - Lines differ across time + countries as basic needs vary by region and over time (UPPSC 2025 key: A and R both true, R explains A)
# 2. Tendulkar and Rangarajan lines (2011-12)
| **Line (2011-12)** | **Rural** | **Urban** | **All-India poverty** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tendulkar | **₹816/month (about ₹27/day)**[GK] | **₹1,000/month (about ₹33/day)**[GK] | **21.9%** (rural 25.7, urban 13.7) |
| Rangarajan | **₹972/month (₹32/day)** | **₹1,407/month (₹47/day)** | **29.5%** |

- **Tendulkar (2011-12):** **₹816 rural / ₹1,000 urban per month**; criticised as too low
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes (2024-25) list ₹972/₹1,407 as Tendulkar's and ₹1,407/₹2,100 as Rangarajan's - a mix-up
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes list ₹816/₹1,000 under "2010-11"; these are 2011-12 figures
- **Rangarajan rural line:** **₹32 a day** (APPSC-GS 2025 key)
- **Recall method:** **MMRP** (7-day recall for some food items) → 10-12% higher consumption, lower poverty; first used by Rangarajan

## Committees → method (calorie norms, recall, price updating) | from: S1

|---|---|

## Terms → meaning (Lorenz curve, Gini, Kuznets and EKC) | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. Lorenz curve
- **Axes:**
  - X-axis = cumulative % of population (poorest first)
  - Y-axis = cumulative % of income
- **Line of equality:** **45° diagonal** = perfect equality; further the curve bows away → greater inequality
- **Limitation:** Visual only; gives **no single number** (Gini gives that)
# 2. Gini coefficient
- **Derivation:** From the **Lorenz curve** = area between curve and equality line ÷ whole area under that line
- **Range:** **0 (perfect equality) to 1 (one person has everything)**
  - **Gini index** = same value × **100**
  - **Higher value means more inequality** (JPSC 2023 key: statements I and III correct)
# 3. Kuznets curve and Environmental Kuznets curve
- **Kuznets curve:** As a developing economy grows, inequality first **rises** then **falls**
  - **inverted-U** between inequality and per capita income
- **Environmental Kuznets curve:** Borrowed from the **Kuznets Curve**; plots **environmental degradation/pollution** (Y-axis) vs per capita income
  - Shape: also **inverted-U** - degradation rises in early growth, peaks, then falls as income rises
  - Message: "the solution to pollution is economic growth"

## Types of unemployment → where predominant | from: S0

## Disguised, frictional, structural and cyclical → features | from: S1, S2, S3
# 1. Disguised unemployment
- **Definition:** More workers engaged than actually required; **marginal productivity of labour is zero or negative**
- **Nature:** Appear employed (e.g. all members on a family plot) but add no productive value; common among family members in **agriculture**
- **Identification:** Withdrawing some workers leaves **total output unchanged** (MPL = 0)
- **Type:** Hidden (not open) unemployment; **PLFS** headline rates do not capture it
# 2. Frictional vs structural
| **Feature** | **Frictional (search)** | **Structural** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Time lag while changing/searching jobs | Mismatch of skills and jobs; change in technology or demand pattern |
| Duration | Short-term, temporary | Long-run |
| Nature | Normal, exists even at full employment | Deep-rooted; needs reskilling/structural reform |
| Example | Worker quits to look for a better job | Labour-intensive economy turning capital-intensive |

- **Technological unemployment:** Job loss due to changes in technology; sub-type of **structural** unemployment
  - Drivers: **Automation in manufacturing and AI in services** may reduce demand for low-skilled, routine jobs
# 3. Cyclical unemployment
- **Cause:** **Fall in aggregate demand** for goods and services during **recession/depression** → firms lay off workers to cut costs
- **Business-cycle phase:** **Contraction/trough (depression)**; falls when recovery/boom begins
> Exam angle: "unemployment caused by depression in an economy" = **Cyclical** (APPSC 2019).

## Other classifications → meaning | from: S4, S5
# 1. Paired classifications
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Able and willing to work but no job at all |
| **Hidden/disguised** | Appears employed, contributes nothing |
| **Voluntary** | Unwilling at prevailing wage (high benefits, high marginal tax, waiting for better job) |

| **Invisible underemployment** | Full-time work but very low income or work below one's ability (e.g. **MBA** working as a driver) |

# 2. Other types
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Casual** | Day-to-day contract work |
| **Chronic** | Long-term persisting |
| **Long-term** | Unemployed > **12 months** |
| **Classical/real-wage** | Wages too high |
| **Unemployment trap** | Benefits discourage work |
| **Full employment** | Everyone willing at prevailing wage finds work; frictional + structural still exist |

## Economists → laws and statements on unemployment | from: S3, S5, S7
| **Economist** | **Statement / contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Keynes** | Unemployment in developed economies = **deficiency in effective demand** (cyclical/demand-deficient) |
| **Milton Friedman** | Associated with the **natural rate of unemployment** |
| **A.W. Phillips (1958)** | **Phillips curve**: **inverse relationship between unemployment and inflation** - lower unemployment ↔ higher inflation |
| **Friedman-Phelps** | Long-run Phillips curve vertical at the natural rate |
| **Arthur Okun** | **Okun's law**: **1 percentage point** rise in unemployment above natural rate → roughly **2%** fall in output below potential GDP |

- **Phillips curve breakdown:** In **stagflation** (high inflation + high unemployment)
- **Okun's implication:** Inverse link between growth and unemployment
- ⚠ **Phillips** = unemployment vs inflation; **Okun** = unemployment vs output

## Nature of India's unemployment — predominant type and causes | from: S6
# 1. Predominant type
- **Core nature:** **Indian unemployment is basically structural** - labour demand < supply; long-run
  - Due to rapid population growth + labour immobility + shift to capital-intensive production
- **Other features:** High **seasonal** + **disguised** unemployment (large agricultural workforce) + underemployment
  - Differs from demand-deficient (**Keynesian**) unemployment of developed countries
# 2. Causes
- **India-specific structural causes:**
  - Neglected structural transformation of secondary sector
  - **Jobless growth** - growth driven by capital-intensive sectors (IT, finance), not labour-intensive manufacturing
  - Less focus on **MSMEs**
  - Stringent labour laws; delays in paperwork; slow infrastructure
  - Import-oriented rather than export-led economy
- **Other causes:**
  - Skill-curriculum mismatch
  - ~**90%** workforce informal
  - Low female **LFPR**
> Exam angle: odd-one-out questions list causes (population growth, slow industrialisation, faulty education) and slip in a non-cause such as "high growth of labour-intensive manufacturing".
