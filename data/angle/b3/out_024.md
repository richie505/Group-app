=== ROW 93 | Inflation - types, causes, measurement (CPI, WPI, core inflation), effects and control measures

## Inflation — definitions and types | from: S0
| **Type** | **Meaning / cause** |
|---|---|
| **Headline** | Inflation of the **whole CPI basket** incl. food and fuel - the "raw", volatile figure; what consumers feel |
| **Core** | CPI **excluding food and fuel** (and electricity) - shows the underlying, demand-side trend |
| **Food** | Rise in food prices - monsoon, supply shocks; measured by the **Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI)**, with **10 sub-groups** (cereals, meat and fish, egg, milk, oils, fruits, vegetables, pulses, sugar, spices) |
| **Demand-pull** | Excess demand (aggregate demand > aggregate supply): expansionary/fiscal stimulus, public spending, loose money, higher purchasing power (UPSC 2021) |
| **Cost-push** | Rising input costs (wages, raw materials, fuel, rupee fall) → supply falls, prices go up; e.g. West Asia oil shock 2026 |
| Bottleneck | Structural shortages: poor farm productivity, storage, distribution |
| Export-led | Strong export demand drains domestic supply and pushes prices up |
| **Stagflation** | High inflation + stagnant growth/unemployment |
| **Skewflation** | Inflation (prices rise) in a few items (e.g. food) while overall inflation is low / others stay stable |
| **Disinflation** | Fall in the **rate** of inflation, still positive (e.g. 3% to 2%) |
| **Deflation** | Fall in the general price level (negative inflation) |
| Reflation | Inflation returning after deflation → a sign of recovery |
- **By speed:** Creeping (below 3%), **walking/trotting (3-10%)** (around 3-10% a year), galloping, hyperinflation
- ⚠ **Disinflation** = rate falls but stays positive; **Deflation** = prices fall

## Causes of inflation in India — factor → effect | from: S1
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Supply shocks (dominant)** | Erratic monsoon + **El Niño** (vegetables); crude oil prices; **rupee depreciation**; **West Asia conflict** pushed up fuel and input costs in 2026 |
| **Demand side** | Public expenditure, loose monetary policy, higher incomes |
| **Other factors** | **MSP hikes**, **hoarding**, inflation **expectations** |
- **Stagflation factors:** Inflation from **higher government spending** + **fall in industrial production and slow growth**
  - ⚠ **Key:** **a and b only** (APPSC-G2 2025)

## Effects of inflation and deflation — gainers and losers | from: S2
# 1. Effects of inflation
- **Purchasing power:** Inflation **lowers the purchasing power of money** (APPSC 2025) → a rising GNP/GDP deflator **decreases purchasing power** (APPSC G2 2025)
| **Group** | **Who** |
|---|---|
| **Gainers** | **Debtors** (money repaid at lower real value), producers, holders of real assets |
| **Losers** | **Creditors**, **fixed-income groups** (pensioners, salaried people without DA), savers |
- Rule: **Debtors gain and creditors lose**
- **Costs:** **Menu costs** of changing prices (repricing), shoe-leather costs, uncertainty that hurts investment
- **Regressive:** Food is a large share of poor households' spending; food & beverages = **~37%** of the CPI basket
# 2. Effects of deflation
- **Purchasing power:** Rises
- **Deflationary spiral:** Postponed buying → lower profits → **higher unemployment** → heavier **real debt** burden

## Economists → curves: the Phillips curve | from: S3
| **Person** | **Statement / contribution** |
|---|---|
| **A.W. Phillips (1958)** | **Inverse relation** between unemployment and (wage) inflation → lower unemployment comes with higher inflation |
| Friedman | **Long run:** Vertical, no lasting trade-off |
- **India's curve is flat:**
  - IIP-CPI data (2012-2026) show little trade-off
  - **92% informal workforce** (~92% of workers) → weak wage bargaining; wages do not rise with output
- **Stagflation risk:** Rate hikes cut output and jobs without lowering supply-driven inflation much

=== ROW 94 | Indian tax system - direct and indirect taxes, tax buoyancy, tax-GDP ratio, progressivity

## Terms → meaning: tax, fee, cess, surcharge | from: S0
| **Levy** | **Key feature / meaning** |
|---|---|
| Tax | Compulsory contribution (pecuniary burden) imposed under authority of law to finance the government; **no quid pro quo** (**no direct quid pro quo**) (APPSC 2023) |
| Fee | Payment for a specific service (**quid pro quo**) |
| Cess | "Tax on tax" for a **specific purpose**; ends when purpose met; **not shared with States** (kept wholly by the Centre) |
| Surcharge | Additional (extra) charge on tax payable, % of tax; usually permanent; no surcharge if no tax is due; **not shared with States** (Art. 271) |
> Exam angle: "pecuniary burden imposed by legislative authority to support government" = **Tax** (APPSC 2023).
- **Example cess:** **Clean Energy Cess** on coal (Finance Act 2010) → renamed **Clean Environment Cess** (Budget 2016-17)
- **Federal grievance:** Cesses and surcharges are **outside the divisible pool** shared with States

## Articles and Amendments — constitutional basis of taxation | from: S1
| **Article / entry** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Article 265** | No tax levied or collected except by **authority of law** |
| Taxing entries | Union List 15, State List 20, Concurrent List 3 |
| Residuary taxing power | Parliament (gift, wealth, expenditure taxes) |
| **80th Amendment, 2000** | Union taxes shared with States on FC advice (effective 1 April 1996) |

## Direct vs indirect taxes — classification and taxable event | from: S2
| **Basis** | **Direct tax** | **Indirect tax** |
|---|---|---|
| Burden | Impact + incidence fall on the **same person**; cannot be shifted | Collected by an intermediary (seller) → passed on to consumer through price |
| Administered by | **CBDT** (Central Boards of Revenue Act, **1963**) | **CBIC** |
| Price effect | Generally non-inflationary | Inflationary |
- **Impact vs incidence:** **Impact** = on whom tax is first imposed; **incidence** = who finally bears it; shifting moves burden forward/backward
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes reverse these terms

## Types — progressive, proportional, regressive taxes | from: S3
| **Type** | **Meaning** | **Indian example** |
|---|---|---|
| Progressive | Rate rises with income ("ability to pay") | Income tax |
| Proportional | Same (uniform) rate at all incomes | Sales tax (flat rate) |
| Regressive | Poor pay higher share of income (higher burden on low incomes) | Excise, customs, GST on essentials |
| Degressive | Progressive up to a point, then flat | Capped slab structures |
- **India's system:** Progressive direct taxes + regressive indirect taxes
  - Historically regressive-leaning (indirect-heavy) → becoming more progressive as direct share rises

## Data — tax-GDP ratio: Centre, States, combined, peers | from: S6
# 1. Concept
- **Tax-to-GDP:** Total tax revenue / nominal GDP → yardstick of **fiscal capacity**
  - Low ratio = large informal sector or high evasion
- **World Bank benchmark:** **15%** = tipping point for sustainable growth
- **Falling ratio:** Revenue growing slower than GDP (buoyancy < 1) → weaker fiscal space, more borrowing
# 2. India's numbers
| **Measure** | **Value** |
|---|---|
| **Centre (gross tax revenue)** | **9.9% in 2019-20** (APPSC 2021, ES 2021-22); **~11.5% of GDP** post-pandemic (FY22-FY25; 2025-26 Survey); 11.2% in FY24; some notes quote ~11.7% (2024) |
| **Combined (Centre + States)** | **~19.6%** (Bank of Baroda report) |
| **States** | Own tax revenue ≈ **7% of GSDP** |
| **Peers** | India's average tax-GDP (2001-22) **16.36%** → lowest among emerging and developing economies; below OECD average |
| **Evasion** | ~**4.3%** of tax revenue lost yearly |

## Terms and persons → tax buoyancy, elasticity, Laffer curve, base widening | from: S7, S8
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Buoyancy** | % change in tax revenue / % change in GDP, **including** discretionary changes (rates, base); above 1 = revenue grows faster than GDP |
| **Elasticity** | Same response **excluding** discretionary changes |
| **Laffer curve** | Inverted-U; revenue zero at 0% and 100% rates |
| **Base widening** | More taxpayers, fewer exemptions preferred to rate hikes → Chelliah approach (lower rates, broader base) |
| **Deadweight costs** | Removed by a **simpler tax system** → productivity gains + fewer distortions in resource allocation (APPSC-G1 2024 A-R) |
- **Laffer curve:** Popularised by **Arthur Laffer (1974)**; term coined by Jude Wanniski
- ⚠ **Buoyancy** includes discretionary changes; **elasticity** excludes them
- **Recent buoyancy (2026-27 BE):**
  - Centre's gross tax buoyancy **0.8 in 2026-27 (BE)**
  - Direct taxes **1.1** vs indirect **0.3** (GST not keeping pace with GDP)
- **Fiscal consolidation:** ES 2025-26 credits buoyant revenues → deficit 4.4% of GDP in FY26
- **Direct tax cut:** **Cut in direct taxes raises disposable income** (APPSC 2018) → higher consumption, savings, investment
  - e.g. income up to **₹12 lakh** tax-free under the new regime
  - Caution: personal income tax revenue growth in 2025-26 only **0.037%** after rate rationalisation
