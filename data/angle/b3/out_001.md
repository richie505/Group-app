=== ROW 5 | Occupational structure of India - trends and inter-state variation

## Occupational structure — definitions, terms and FIRSTs | from: S0, S2, S4, S7
# 1. Definitions and theory
- **Meaning:** distribution of workforce across primary, secondary, tertiary activities; shifts away from farming as an economy develops
- **Clark-Fisher thesis:** As income rises, labour shifts primary → secondary → tertiary
- **India's structural retardation:** occupational structure changed far more slowly than output; services grew **without a manufacturing-led stage**
# 2. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Unorganised sector** | **small and scattered units largely outside government control** (GS 2025); low pay, no job security, few social benefits |
| Unorganised sector (**NCEUS**) | unincorporated private enterprises owned by individuals/households, proprietary or partnership basis, **fewer than 10 workers** |
| **Organised manufacturing** | registered factories employing **10+ workers (with power) / 20+ (without power)** + registered companies; the rest = unincorporated/household (4 Sep 2026) |
| Unorganised workers (**Code on Social Security 2020**) | home-based, self-employed or wage workers in the unorganised sector (2025-26 glossary) |
| Informal worker (NCEUS) | Unorganised-sector workers + organised-sector workers without employment/social security benefits [GK] |
| **Informalisation of formal enterprises** | informal employment exists **inside the formal sector** too - contract and outsourced hiring |
| **"Formal informality"** | new EPFO jobs often short-term/contractual (2022-23) |
| **Jobless growth** | GDP growth not matched by formal, quality jobs |
| **Premature deindustrialisation** | services-led growth; manufacturing never absorbed farm labour |
| **Feminisation of agriculture** | female workers **55%** agricultural labourers, **24%** cultivators, yet women hold only **12.8%** of operational holdings |
# 3. FIRSTs
| **FIRST / LAST** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **2011 first** | labourers (**30.0%** of workers) outnumbered cultivators (**24.7%**) for the first time |
| Census **2011** | **15th** since **1872** (**7th** after Independence) |
| Next Census in **2027** | first app-based digital census |
- ⚠ Census 2011 = **15th** overall but **7th** after Independence

## Terms → meaning: Hindu rate of growth and ICOR | from: S1
# 1. Hindu rate of growth
- **Variable:** Annual growth of **real GDP** ≈ **3.5%** (some notes: **3.52%** for **1950-1980**)
  - Per capita GDP grew only **1.37%** a year → growth barely above population growth
- **Period:** **1950s to 1980**; broken in the **1980s** (**5.1-5.5%**)
- **Interpretation:** Label satirically invoked cultural fatalism; analysts blame inefficient state planning
- **2026 echo:** "**Bharat rate of growth**" of **8%+** through AI-led "Reforms 3.0" (30 Jun 2026)
# 2. Capital-output ratio (ICOR) - low growth despite high savings
- **ICOR = change in capital / change in output**: extra investment needed for one more unit of GDP
  - **Lower ICOR = more efficient investment** (Jan 2026)
- **Harrod-Domar model (First Plan):** Growth depends on **savings rate** + **capital-output ratio** (g = s/v)
- **Second Plan:** Raised investment from **7% to 11%** of national income
  - Heavy, capital-intensive industry with long gestation → high ICOR → growth stayed low
- **Now:** GFCF ~**30%** of GDP (2025-26); net household financial savings down to ~**5.3%** of GDP (Jan 2026)

## Policies, plans and the licence regime, 1944-1991 | from: S0

- **Functions of PC:** Assess material, capital and human resources; formulate the Five Year Plans; fix priorities and stages and allocate resources; determine resources needed; appraise progress; plan grants to States; perspective planning
- **Attached office:** UIDAI (**Jan 2009**)
- **Replacement:** PC dissolved in **2014** → **NITI Aayog** on **1 January 2015** by government resolution
  - "Bottom-up", cooperative federalism, think tank not fund allocator
> Exam angle: "body set up after Independence for a common plan" = Planning Commission (APPSC-GS 2007).
- ⚠ PC **15 March 1950**; NDC **1952**; NITI Aayog **1 January 2015**

## Objectives of planning — chief vs subsidiary | from: S1
- **Four long-term objectives:** **Growth, Modernisation, Self-reliance, Social justice (equity)** + employment and removal of poverty
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "literature" is the odd one out (BPSC 2022)
- **Principal goal:** **Growth** (APPSC 2024 key); an important goal (APPSC-GS 2024); others subsidiary/complementary
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Curbing private enterprise, a commune system or "no pollution" were not
- **APPSC-G2 2017 key:** "**to achieve social equality**" is not a chief objective
  - Chief objectives: reducing inequality of income and wealth, higher growth, removal of poverty
- **Planning Commission's declared aim:** Rapid rise in the **standard of living** by efficient use of resources, raising production and **employment opportunities for all**
- **Socialistic pattern of society:** Avadi Congress **1955** → adopted by the **Second Plan (1956-61)** and IPR **1956**

## Plans → thrust, strategy and programmes | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. First Plan launch
- **Launch:** **First Plan** on **1 April 1951**; Five-Year Plans ran **1951-2017**
- **Focus:** Large foodgrain imports + inflation → **highest priority to agriculture, including irrigation and power**
- **Growth:** **3.6%** achieved vs **2.1%** target
# 2. Plan → main thrust
| **Plan** | **Main thrust** |
|---|---|
| Second (1956-61) | Rapid industrialisation, socialistic pattern (Mahalanobis) |
| Third (1961-66) | Self-reliance, food self-sufficiency, import substitution |
| Fifth (1974-79) | Removal of poverty and self-reliance; Minimum Needs Programme [GK] |
| Sixth (1980-85) | Poverty alleviation; MNP |
| Seventh (1985-90) | Growth, modernisation, self-reliance, social justice |
| Ninth (1997-2002) | Seven Basic Minimum Services |
# 3. Strategy shifts - trickle-down to direct attack on poverty
| **Phase** | **Approach** | **Programmes** |
|---|---|---|
| First to Third Plans | **Trickle-down**: growth will reach the poor | Community Development Programme (1952) |
| Fourth Plan onward (1969-) | Target-group approach | SFDA, MFAL, **Drought Prone Area Programme **[GK] |
| Fifth Plan (1974-79) | **Direct attack on poverty**; consumption of those below poverty line | **Minimum Needs Programme**, 20-Point Programme [GK] |
| Sixth Plan (1980-85) | **Removal of poverty foremost** | **IRDP**, NREP, RLEGP [GK] |
- **Fourth Plan:** Particular emphasis on the **less privileged and weaker sections**
- **Fifth Plan:** Self-reliance + raising consumption of the poor

## Plan financing — domestic savings, deficit financing, foreign aid | from: S3
- **Sources:**
  - **Domestic savings** (budgetary resources, public sector surpluses, market borrowing)
  - **Deficit financing** (borrowing from RBI / money creation)

## Types of planning — terms → meaning | from: S4, S5
# 1. Imperative vs indicative
| **Feature** | **Imperative (directive)** | **Indicative** |
|---|---|---|
| Market mechanism | **Entirely replaced** by a command hierarchy | Plan **improves the working of** the market |
| Ownership | State / collective | Mixed |
| Targets | Binding quotas, allocation, even prices | Guidelines, goals and signals for the private sector |
| Examples | **USSR (1928, Stalin)**, China | Mixed economies; France [GK]; India after 1991 (Eighth Plan) [GK] |
- **India's shift:** To **indicative planning** from the **Eighth Plan (1992-97)** after the **1991** reforms
# 2. Other planning dichotomies
| **Pair** | **Distinction** |
|---|---|
| Centralised vs decentralised | One central authority decides, top-down (Planning Commission era) vs decisions by lower governments or private units within broad policy (UPSC notes); bottom-up: **District Planning Committee** (Art. 243ZD) and **Metropolitan Planning Committee** (Art. 243ZE) [GK for articles]; Kerala People's Plan Campaign |
| By direction vs by inducement | Commands and controls vs incentives through prices, taxes, subsidies [GK] |
| Financial vs physical | Allocation in money terms vs in real terms - materials, manpower, output targets [GK] |
| Democratic vs totalitarian | People's participation and consent vs imposed by the state [GK] |
- **India:** Democratic planning
  - Early on: imperative in the public sector
  - After **1991**: indicative and by inducement
# 3. Perspective planning
- **Meaning:** Long-term planning over **15-20 years** or more; five-year and annual plans carved out of it
- **India:**
  - Perspective planning = a Planning Commission function
  - Long-term **perspective plan (1985-2000)** accompanied the Seventh Plan
  - Lack of perspective = standard criticism of Indian planning
- **Now:**
  - **Viksit Bharat @2047**
  - GSDP **Rs 308.11 lakh cr by 2047-48**; ten "Padi Sutralu"
  - DMF **five-year perspective plans** from baseline surveys (2026)

## Rolling plan — Myrdal, Janata government, 1978-80 | from: S6
- **Origin:** Suggested by **Gunnar Myrdal** for developing countries; introduced by the **Janata Party** government, which ended the Fifth Plan a year early
- **Period:** **April 1978 - March 1980** (APPSC-GS 2025: **1978-80**)
  - Janata **Sixth Plan (1978-83)** = "the rolling plan" (UKPSC 2021)
  - Novel idea of the draft Sixth Plan (APPSC-G1 2012)
- **Features:**
  - Plan revised **every year** and extended by one year
  - Three plans run together: **annual plan** (with the budget) + **five-year plan** rolled forward yearly + **perspective plan** of **10-20 years**
  - Flexible targets instead of fixed five-year targets
- **Outlay:** **Rs 12,177 crore**; stressed employment against the Nehru model
- **End:** Fall of the Janata government → **1979-80** an annual plan → Congress **Sixth Plan from 1 April 1980**
- **Criticism:** Yearly revision weakened commitment to targets
- ⚠ Janata Sixth Plan **1978-83** (rolling) vs Congress Sixth Plan from **1 April 1980**
