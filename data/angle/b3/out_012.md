## NABARD — origin, merged bodies, roles and funds | from: S1
# 1. Origin and ownership
- **Set up:** 1982, in the **Sixth Plan**; HQ **Mumbai**; initial corpus **Rs 100 crore**
- **Basis:** recommended by **CRAFICARD**; created under the **NABARD Act, 1981**; established **12 July 1982**
- **Bodies merged:**
  - RBI's **Agricultural Credit Department (ACD)**
  - **Rural Planning and Credit Cell (RPCC)**
  - **Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation (ARDC)**
- **Ownership:** **RBI sold its stake in 2019** → fully owned by Government of India
- ⚠ Act **1981**; established **12 July 1982**
# 2. Roles
- **Status:** **Apex development finance institution** for agriculture and rural credit; coordinates rural financing system (APPSC 2024 key); premier institution for agricultural credit (APPSC 2019 key)
- **Primary role:** **Refinance institution** (APPSC 2025 key) - refinance and development
  - Refinances StCBs, RRBs, land development banks, SCBs, NBFC-MFIs
  - Does not lend to farmers directly
- **Other roles:** Supervises RRBs + cooperative banks; runs **SHG-Bank Linkage Programme**
# 3. Lending and funds

| **Fund** | **Year / size** | **Purpose** |
|---|---|---|
| **RIDF** | **₹42,714 crore** | Direct lending to States, funded from banks' **PSL shortfall** deposits; rural roads, bridges, irrigation |
| **LTIF** (under PMKSY) | Rs 1,441 crore | Irrigation |
| Micro Irrigation Fund | - | Micro-irrigation |
| **Warehouse Infrastructure Fund** | 2013-14, ₹5,000 crore | Warehousing |
| **Food Processing Fund** | 2014-15, ₹2,000 crore | Food processing |

- **Refinance (2024-25):**
  - Direct refinance to cooperatives ₹37,217 crore
  - Total refinance **₹3,25,497.81 crore**
- **Potential Linked Credit Plans:** District plans roll up into **State Focus Paper**
  - AP 2026-27 plan **Rs 5.11 lakh crore** (+~20%); agriculture **Rs 2.55 lakh crore**; crop loans Rs 1.66 lakh crore

## Kisan Credit Card and interest subvention — features and limits | from: S2, S3
# 1. KCC basics
- **Launch:** **August 1998**; **NABARD** model scheme on **R.V. Gupta Committee** recommendation
- **Issuers:** Commercial banks, RRBs, cooperative banks, small finance banks, PACS
  - ⚠ Not payment banks or local area banks
- **Purposes:** Crop cultivation, post-harvest + marketing loans, household consumption, farm-asset maintenance, investment credit for allied activities
- **Allied extension:** **Extended to animal husbandry and fisheries** - KCC-MISS interest subvention for **animal husbandry and fisheries from 2019**
# 2. KCC limits and data
- **Collateral-free:** Up to **₹2 lakh** (Rs 2 lakh) (₹3 lakh with hypothecation tie-up)
- **Flexi-KCC:** ₹10,000-50,000 for marginal farmers
- **Scale (31 March 2025):** **7.72 crore** operative KCCs; outstanding short-term credit **₹10.20 lakh crore** (Rs 10.20 lakh crore) (some notes cite 7.81 crore, Jun 2026)
- **RBI revised KCC Directions (19 June 2026):** For loans from **1 January 2027** (1 Jan 2027)
  - Tenure up to **6 years** (earlier 5)
  - Short crops 12 months; long crops up to 18 months
- **AP Pashu KCC (Apr 2026):** Up to **Rs 1.60 lakh** (₹1.60 lakh) without collateral at 7%; 4% on prompt repayment (3% subvention on timely repayment)
# 3. Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS)
- **MISS (Central Sector; RBI + NABARD):**
  - Short-term KCC crop loans at **7%**
  - **1.5%** subvention to lenders
  - **3% prompt repayment incentive** → effective **4%**
- **Limit:** **₹5 lakh** (Aug 2026); earlier crop loans up to **Rs 3 lakh at 7%**
- **Post-harvest:** 7% loans for up to **6 months** against WDRA negotiable warehouse receipts (small and marginal farmers)
- **Reach:** Subsidy since inception **₹1.87 lakh crore** (Rs 1.87 lakh crore); ~**5.9 crore** beneficiaries (Feb 2025)
- **Kisan Rin Portal (Sept 2023):** Integrates KCC + subvention claims
  - Flagged **₹1,080.88 crore** (Rs 1,080.88 crore) duplicate/excess claims out of Rs 37,506.53 crore (2025-26)
- !! Not in your sources: whether the Rs 3 lakh MISS ceiling has been raised in a recent Budget.
- ⚠ **7%** loan − **3%** prompt repayment = **4%** effective; lender gets **1.5%**

## e-NAM — launch, State reforms, advantages and coverage | from: S5, S6
# 1. Basics
- **Launch:** **April 2016**; completed **10 years** in April 2026
- **Nature:** Pan-India electronic trading portal networking existing APMC mandis into a **unified national market** - virtual market backed by physical APMC mandis; motto "One Nation, One Market"
- **Lead implementing agency:** **Small Farmers Agribusiness Consortium (SFAC)**
- **Funding:** Central Sector Scheme via **Agri-Tech Infrastructure Fund (ATIF)**; now under Integrated Scheme for Agricultural Marketing (ISAM-eNAM)
- **Grant:** One-time up to **₹75 lakh per mandi** for equipment + infrastructure
# 2. State reforms and advantages
- **Reforms required of states:** **Allow electronic trading** - **e-trading/e-auction** (APPSC 2016 answer)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** regulating prices was a distractor
  - **Single licence** valid across the state; **single-point levy** of market fee
- **Advantages:** Pan-India portal, nationwide market access, transparent price discovery (UPSC 2017: both correct)
  - More buyers, less information asymmetry, quality assaying
  - Direct payment to farmer's account
- **Modules:** FPO trading, farm-gate trading, **warehouse trading with e-NWRs**, AI assaying; inter-State trade
> Exam angle: "which reform is required of states" and statement pairs on e-NAM's advantages.
# 3. Coverage
- **National (Mar 2026; Jun 2026 notes):** **1,656 mandis** across **23 states and 4 UTs**; 1.80 crore farmers, 2.72 lakh traders; **13.22 crore MT** worth **₹4.82 lakh crore**
  - Earlier (2026): **1,522 mandis**, 1.79 crore farmers, 2.67 lakh traders; 247 commodities (Dec 2025)

| **AP e-NAM indicator** | **APSES 2024-25 (Dec 2024)** | **AP SES 2025-26 (CDI)** |
|---|---|---|
| Markets integrated | **33** | **33** |
| Farmers / traders / commission agents registered | 1,452 / 69 / 9 | **183 / 104 / 25** |
| FPOs registered | 52 | **39** |
| Quantity / value traded | 9.01 lakh MT / ₹5,954.96 cr | **9.88 lakh MT / ₹5,054.86 cr** |

- **AP commodities:** dry chillies, cotton, turmeric, lemons, onions, tomatoes, groundnut, castor, ajwain
- **AP weakness (2024-25):**
  - Online payments only **Rs 26.88 lakh**
  - Just **11 mandis** in inter-mandi trade (Rs 13.98 crore; about **0.23%** of value)
  - Works for price discovery, not yet for payment or inter-state trade

## Operation Greens and Agriculture Infrastructure Fund → features | from: S11, S12
# 1. Operation Greens
- **Announced:** **Union Budget 2018-19** (APPSC 2018 key)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** distractors were Operation Flood, Mission Indradhanush and SAMPADA
- **Crops:** **Tomato, Onion, Potato (TOP)** – grown seasonally and regionally but eaten all year
- **Model:** **"on the lines of Operation Flood"**
- **Aim:** Curb erratic prices via FPOs, agri-logistics, processing, professional management
- **Implementing ministry:** **Ministry of Food Processing Industries**
- **TOP to TOTAL:**
  - 2020 (Atmanirbhar Bharat package): extended to all fruits and vegetables
  - Budget 2021-22: extended to **22 perishable crops**
- **Subsidy pattern:**
  - Short-term interventions = **50%** subsidy on transport + storage
  - Long-term value-chain projects = 50% grant-in-aid; **70%** for FPOs
# 2. Agriculture Infrastructure Fund
- **Size:** **₹1 lakh crore** medium- and long-term debt-financing facility for post-harvest infrastructure + community farming assets; launched 2020
- **Interest subvention:** **3%** on loans up to **₹2 crore**
- **Credit guarantee:** Through **CGTMSE**
- **Eligible projects:** Warehouses, cold stores, processing units, sorting and grading units, custom hiring centres, supply chains
  - Largest sanctioned category = custom hiring centres (**32,560**)
- **Mar 2026:** **₹84,681 crore** sanctioned for **1,71,267 projects**, mobilising **₹1.34 lakh crore**
- **Aug 2025 (earlier):** ₹70,665 crore for 1,20,769 projects
- **AP angle:**
  - **Farm-gate infrastructure:** Funded at Rythu Bharosa Kendra level (now **Rythu Seva Kendralu**)
  - **PACS:** Conversion into Multi-Purpose Facility Centres

## MSP and CACP — concept, recommending and approving bodies | from: S1
# 1. Concept and authority
- **Definition:** Floor price announced before each **Rabi/Kharif** sowing season
  - Protects farmers from price crashes / distress sales in bumper years
  - Market price < MSP → government agencies buy the stock offered
- **Authority:** **Recommended by the CACP; approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Old APPSC key (APPSC G2 2017) credits CACP with "determining" MSP, since CACP recommends
- **Legal status:** **Administrative decision, not a legal right**; no statute backs it
> Exam angle: "recommending vs approving body" (CACP vs CCEA) is the favourite trap.
# 2. CACP

| **Item** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | Set up **1965** as **Agricultural Prices Commission**; renamed **CACP in 1985** |
| **Status** | **Attached office of the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare**, not a statutory body |
| **Composition** | Chairman + Member Secretary + one official member + two non-official members |
| **Also recommends** | **FRP** for sugarcane |

- **Factors considered:**
  - **cost of production**
  - **demand and supply**
  - Domestic and international prices / price trends
  - **inter-crop price parity**
  - Terms of trade between agriculture and non-agriculture
  - **minimum 50% margin** over cost
  - Likely effect on consumers
- ⚠ APC **1965** → CACP **1985**

## Terms → meaning — cost concepts and sugarcane prices | from: S2, S4
# 1. Cost concepts

| **Cost concept** | **What it covers** |
|---|---|
| **A2** | Paid-out costs: seeds, fertilisers, chemicals, hired labour, fuel, irrigation |
| **A2+FL** | A2 + imputed value of **family labour** |
| **C2** | A2+FL + rent on owned land + interest on fixed capital (comprehensive cost) |

- **Current policy:** MSP ≥ **1.5 times the cost of production** = **minimum 50% margin over A2+FL**, not C2
> Exam angle: "MSP = 1.5 × C2" is the trap; the actual formula is 1.5 × A2+FL.
- **Swaminathan Commission (NCF, 2004-06):** Wanted **C2 + 50%** (National Commission on Farmers, **2004**)
  - Government applies 50% over **A2+FL**
  - SKM: paddy at C2+50% should be **₹3,243/q** (**₹3,243/quintal**) for 2026-27 vs ₹2,441 fixed
# 2. Sugarcane prices

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **FRP** | Legally mandated minimum price **sugar mills** must pay cane farmers/growers; fixed by **CCEA** on **CACP** recommendation; linked to mill's **sugar recovery %** (recovery rate) |
| **Statutory Minimum Price (SMP)** | Replaced by FRP from **2009-10** |
| **State Advised Price (SAP)** | Higher price some States (e.g., UP, Punjab, Haryana) announce, usually above FRP |

- **FRP legal basis:** **Sugarcane (Control) Order, 1966** under **Essential Commodities Act, 1955**
- **Latest hike:** **₹10/q** (**₹10/quintal**) for sugar season **2026-27** (Oct-Sept)
- **PYQ (CGPSC 2021):** FRP relates to **sugarcane**
- ⚠ SMP → FRP from **2009-10**; SAP is set by States, above FRP

## PM-AASHA — components, outlay and performance | from: S7
# 1. Basics
- **Full form:** **Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay SanraksHan Abhiyan** (Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan)
- **Launch:** **September 2018**, Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare
- **Purpose:** Make MSP reach farmers, incl. small and marginal farmers
- **Beneficiaries:** **farmers** – a **farmer**-welfare scheme (PYQ; APPSC 2018; CGPSC 2024)
- **Outlay:** **₹35,000 crore** for extension to **2025-26** (15th FC cycle) (APPSC-GS 2023 key)
# 2. Components

| **Component** | **How it works / what it does** |
|---|---|
| **PSS** (Price Support Scheme) | Physical procurement at MSP of **pulses, oilseeds, copra** by **NAFED and NCCF** at a state's request. Only registered farmers with valid land records qualify. From 2024-25, procurement is capped at **25%** of production. |
| **PDPS** (Price Deficiency Payment Scheme) | No physical purchase/procurement: the farmer is paid **MSP minus market price** (the gap) in cash, directly. Mainly for oilseeds. |
| **PPSS** (Private Procurement & Stockist Scheme, pilot) | 2018 pilot in which private stockists/agencies procured at MSP for oilseeds (original 2018 component) |
| **MIS** (Market Intervention Scheme) | Perishables not under MSP (onion, tomato, apple, chillies, grapes, garlic). Triggered by a **≥10% price fall or ≥10% production rise** (**10% or greater price fall or production rise**). Centre-state losses shared **50:50 (75:25 for NE)** - **75:25** for the NE States. |

- **2026 components:** **PSS, PDPS and MIS**; PPSS belongs to the original 2018 design
- ⚠ **PSS** = physical buying; **PDPS** = cash gap, no buying; **MIS** = perishables
# 3. Performance and pulses mission
- **PSS 2024-25 (as on 17 Aug 2025):** **61.87 LMT** oilseeds, pulses, copra procured; MSP value **>₹39,000 crore**; **26.56 lakh farmers** benefited
- **Pulses mission:** **100% procurement of tur, urad and masoor at MSP for 4 years** (**four years**) by NAFED/NCCF under PSS of **PM-AASHA**

## Criticism and the legal-guarantee debate | from: S10
# 1. Bias in coverage

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Crop bias** | MSP covers 22 crops but procurement mostly **paddy and wheat** → MSP for pulses, oilseeds, coarse grains largely symbolic |
| **Regional bias** | Concentrated in **Punjab, Haryana, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Chhattisgarh**; eastern + NE farmers gain little |
| **Shanta Kumar HLC (2015)** | Only about **6% of farmers** sell to procurement agencies at MSP |

# 2. Legal guarantee demand
- **Legal status:** Administrative decision, **not a legal right**; farmer groups demand legal guarantee
- **Farmer unions:** Law binding procurement at MSP
- **Economists' view:** Legal MSP could distort trade, encourage/entrench monoculture, breach **WTO** norms → prefer PDPS-type deficiency payments
  - Alternatives: wider procurement, price-deficiency payments (PM-AASHA), direct income support (PM-KISAN), decentralised procurement, digital procurement via e-NAM
- **Supreme Court:** Issued notice to Centre on plea for MSP ≥ weighted average cost of production (14 Apr 2026)
- **ES 2025-26:** Use procurement-efficiency savings to fund **voluntary crop diversification** away from water-guzzling rice and wheat
