=== ROW 0 | Basic characteristics of the Indian economy; features of a developing economy

## Types of economies → defining feature | from: S0, S1
| **Type** | **Core feature** | **Example / proponent** |
|---|---|---|
| Capitalist (capitalism) | Private ownership, profit motive, market forces and prices; economic inequality is a feature | USA [GK] |
| Socialist | Collective ownership, equitable distribution, state welfare | Socialist elements in Sweden, Norway |
| Controlled / command (planned) economy | Central plan replaces the market; government decides what, how and for whom to produce | Former USSR, North Korea |
| Gandhian | Decentralised, self-contained villages, cottage industries | **Gandhian Plan 1944 - S.N. Agarwal** |
| Mixed | Public and private sectors side by side | India, UK, Germany |
| Traditional | Custom and tradition decide production | - |

| **Label** | **Defining feature** |
|---|---|
| Mixed | **Private and public sectors go side by side**; the State regulates the private sector through **taxes, subsidy and credit**; planning treats both sectors as complementary (IYB) |
| Developing | **Low per capita income**; **low capital formation** - possible only with a **high savings rate**; high public investment in infrastructure is a precondition for private-sector growth |
| Underdeveloped | Low PCI, low capital formation, predominance of agriculture, disguised unemployment, high dependency |
| Dual | A modern industrial/urban sector coexists with a traditional agrarian, subsistence sector [GK] |
| Labour-surplus | Surplus labour in agriculture whose marginal productivity is near zero - the Lewis model setting [GK] |

> Exam angle: statements matching a feature (regulation through taxes/credit, coexistence of sectors) to the correct label.
# 1. India as a mixed economy
- **Mixed economy:** co-existence of **private and public sectors** (APPSC key)
- **India = mixed:** pragmatic middle path between liberal-capitalist, Soviet and Gandhian models
- **Aim:** growth by democratic means + a social order based on justice
- **Largest share of national income:** **private sector** (APPSC-G2 2008)
- **True statements (UPPSC 2024):**
  - India is an **underdeveloped** economy and a **mixed** economy
  - Father of Economics = **Adam Smith**, not Keynes

## India at Independence → features and causes of underdevelopment | from: S2
# 1. Features at Independence
- **Low per capita income:** first National Income Committee (**1951**)
  - 1948-49 national income = **Rs 8,710 crore**; per capita = **Rs 225**
- **Agriculture predominance:** main livelihood on the eve of Independence; **72%** of the workforce in **1951**
- **Weak industrial base:** basic/capital goods only about **one-fourth** of industrial production at Independence
- **Low capital formation:** savings rate too low to finance investment
  - Thin social overhead capital (transport, irrigation, energy, education, health)
- **Labour:** **Disguised unemployment** in farming + a **high dependency ratio**
# 2. Causes of underdevelopment
- **Most important cause:** **co-existence of under-utilised manpower and under-utilised natural resources** (APPSC key)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** NOT high density, lack of capital or low per capita income taken alone
- **Not a main cause:** **working efficiency of the population** (APPSC-G2 2017)
  - Real causes listed there: rapid population growth + high dependency ratio, low human development, high incidence of poverty

| **Cause** | **What the notes record** |
|---|---|
| Colonial legacy | Deindustrialisation: exporter of textiles turned into a raw-material supplier and captive market; world GDP share fell (Angus Maddison) |
| Capital deficiency | Capital formation needs a high savings rate; savings were low |
| Population pressure | High dependency ratio; surplus workers crowd into farming |
| Low productivity | Disguised unemployment in agriculture |
| Weak human capital | Literacy **below 16%**, life expectancy **32 years** in 1947 |

## Issues and debates → mismatch and service-led growth | from: S1, S2
- **The mismatch** (the employment-output paradox; NITI Aayog, 2025-26): services produce over half of output but employ only about a third of workers
  - **Reason:** high-end services (IT, finance) need high skills → most new jobs in low-value trade, hospitality, transport, personal services
- **Historical trend:** GDP shares moved out of agriculture much faster than employment; services' employment share rose the most
- **Origin of the service-led model:** boom began in the mid-1980s; sped up with the 1990s reforms
- **Sustainability debate:** EAC-PM Chairman - service-led growth cannot last without a strong manufacturing base (Nov 2025)

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing linkages | Creates linkage demand for logistics, IT and finance |
| Manufacturing jobs | Employs about **72 million** (PLFS 2023-24) |
| Manufacturing stability | Steadies GDP cycles |

- **Counter-view:** services = "high-growth, low-volatility anchor", growing about **7-8%** a year (2025-26)
