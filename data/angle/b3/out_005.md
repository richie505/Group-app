=== ROW 23 | Methods of measuring national income - product, income and expenditure methods; difficulties

## Economists → definitions of national income | from: S0
| **Economist** | **Key idea / definition's core** |
|---|---|
| **Marshall** | Labour and capital acting on natural resources produce a net aggregate of commodities (material and immaterial) and services each year: the "national dividend" |
| **Pigou** | The part of the community's income, including income from abroad, **that can be measured in money** |
| **Fisher** | Based on **consumption**: only services received by the ultimate consumers |
| **Kuznets** | Net output of commodities and services flowing to ultimate consumers from the productive system, or into **net additions to capital stock** |

- ⚠ **Marshall** = "national dividend"; **Pigou** = measured in money; **Fisher** = consumption
- !! Not in your sources: the Samuelson definition the guide lists.

## Terms → meaning (circular flow, value added, double counting) | from: S1, S3
# 1. Circular flow of income
- **Real flow:** Factor services: household → firm; goods and services: firm → household
- **Money flow:** Factor payments: firm → household; consumption spending: household → firm

| **Leakages (withdrawals)** | **Injections** |
|---|---|
| **Saving** (S) | **Investment** (I) |
| Taxes (T) | Government spending (G) |
| **Imports** (M) | **Exports** (X) |

- **Equilibrium:** **S + T + M = I + G + X** = total leakages equal total injections
- **Open-economy identity:** **Y = C + I + G + (X - M)**
# 2. Double counting and value added
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Value added** | **Value added = value of output - intermediate consumption.** |
| **GVA vs NVA** | **GVA includes depreciation. NVA = GVA - depreciation.** |
| **Inventories** | Change in inventories = production - sales; part of output |
| **Double deflation (2022-23 series)** | **Real GVA = Real output - Real intermediate consumption**; output and input prices deflated separately |

- **Solution to double counting:** Count only **final goods**, or add **value added** at each stage
  - Example: cotton farmer → weaver → tailor; counting all three sales counts cotton three times

## Methods → what is summed (product, income, expenditure) | from: S2
| **Method** | **Also called** | **What is summed / how it works** |
|---|---|---|
| **Production / Product** | Value added method; product / output method; net product / final product method | Sum of **value added** by every producing unit/firm (output - intermediate inputs) = total **final product** = GDP; avoids **double counting** (cotton farmer, weaver, tailor are not counted separately from the shirt) |
| **Income** | Factor income / factor payment / distributed share method | Compensation of employees + operating surplus (rent, interest, profit) + mixed income |
| **Expenditure** | Consumption-and-investment method (final expenditure); consumption / outlay method | **C + I + G + (X - M)** - final spending on output |

> Exam angle: **"Cost and Accounting method" is NOT a method** of measuring national income (APPSC-G2 2025).
# 1. Income method
- **Economic Survey formula:** **GDP = Compensation of employees + Consumption of fixed capital + (Taxes on production - subsidies on production) + Gross operating surplus**
# 2. Expenditure components
- **Private consumption:** **Private consumption is the largest share (about 59% of GDP)** → India less exposed to global swings
  - 11th Plan (**2007-12**) average = **59%** (APPSC-GS 2019)
- **Government spending:** Next
- **Business investment:** **Business investment is the most volatile** component
- **Net exports:** Last; the trade effect
- **Current data (Q1 2026-27):**
  - GFCF rose to **34.3% of GDP** (nominal) from **31.4%**
  - Real GFCF grew **11.9%**; private consumption **7.1%** (**1** and **17** Sep **2026**)

## Methods → sectors in India | from: S5
- **India's approach:** **India uses a combination of the production and income methods** (= **production method and the income method**)
  - Expenditure method = cross-check of demand-side components

| **Method** | **Sectors** |
|---|---|
| **Production (value added) method** | Agriculture and allied activities, mining, manufacturing |
| **Income method** | Services such as trade, transport, banking, public administration (textbook split) |
| **Expenditure method (commodity flow)** | Used in part for construction; expenditure data (PFCE) serves as a check |

- **New 2022-23 series:** Aligns **Supply and Use Tables** with national accounts → cuts discrepancies between production- and expenditure-side GDP (2026)

## Inclusion and exclusion test — what enters national income | from: S4
| **Item** | **In NI?** | **Reason** |
|---|---|---|
| Wages and salaries, rent, dividends, corporate profits, interest (on productive loans) | **Yes** | Factor payments |
| **Imputed rent** of owner-occupied houses [GK] | Yes | - |
| **Self-consumed farm output**[GK] | Yes | - |
| Change in inventories | Yes | - |
| Production for self-use (fixed capital) [GK] | Yes | - |
| **Transfer payments** (pensions, scholarships, gifts) | No | - |
| **Transfer earnings** | **No** | Not payments for factor services (G2 2025 A-R: both true, R explains A) |
| **Interest receipts from public debt** / **Interest on public debt** | **No** | Treated as transfer (APPSC-G1 2022, 2023) |
| **Second-hand sales** | No | Only the broker's commission counts [GK] |
| **Purely financial transactions** (shares, bonds) [GK] | No | - |
| **Tax revenue** as such | No | A transfer to government, not a factor income/payment [GK] |
| Housewives' unpaid services [GK] | No | - |

- ⚠ **Imputed rent** = in; **interest on public debt** = out

## Difficulties in measuring national income in India | from: S6
# 1. Structural difficulties
- **Non-monetised economy:** Large **non-monetised/subsistence** output + **self-consumption** (farm output kept for home use)
- **Informal sector:** Big **unorganised/informal** sector without accounts
- **Records:** Illiteracy and missing records
- **Double counting:** Risk exists; **no occupational specialisation** (a farmer may also be a weaver)
- **Black money / parallel economy**
- **Valuation:** Problems in valuing depreciation and inventories
- **Population:** Large population growth complicates per capita comparisons
# 2. Current issues (IMF review, Dec 2025)
- **IMF flags:**
  - Persistent **gaps between production- and expenditure-side GDP** → **partial capture of informal activity**
  - No **seasonally adjusted** quarterly data
  - Delays in GFCF by institutional sector
- **Fix:** New series uses **Supply and Use Tables** to reduce the discrepancy
- **Data limits:** Household-sector GVA **extrapolated using indicators** after benchmark year; private corporate segment depends on **MCA database** (Mar 2026)

## State income — AP terms, formulae and compilation | from: S7
# 1. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning / formula** |
|---|---|
| **Income originating** | Value produced **within** the State's boundaries (= GSDP) |
| **Income accruing** | **Income accruing = State income + income of the State's residents earned in other States/abroad.** |
| **GSDP formula** | **GVA (basic prices, industry-wise) + product taxes - product subsidies = GSDP (market prices)** |
| **NSDP** | **NSDP = GSDP - depreciation**; AP's per capita income based on NSDP |
| **Production taxes** | Stamp duties + property taxes |
| **Production subsidies** | Apprentice + interest subsidies |

- **Base year:** **2011-12** (APSES 2024-25)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes say AP uses **2019-20** as base - not supported by the Survey
# 2. Compilation
- **Agency:** **Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES), AP** compiles GSDP; at State level DESs compile State Domestic Product
- **Sectors:** GSDP computed across **9 sectors** (APPSC-G2 2017) = old CSO classification
  - Agriculture-forestry-fishing; mining; manufacturing; electricity-gas-water; construction; trade-hotels; transport-storage-communication; finance-real estate; community and social services
- **Approach:** States use the **production (income-originating) approach**
  - Manufacturing GVA used to be allocated to States by their ASI shares → now **GST data** also available (Mar 2026)
- **MoSPI uniform guideline (May 2026):** **Uniform guideline for compiling GSVA** (base **2022-23**)
  - Integrate administrative records, sector databases and surveys
  - Better capture of services and the unincorporated segment
  - Consistency with national accounts

=== ROW 27 | Demographic transition theory and India's position; demographic dividend and its window

## Demographic transition — stages and India's position | from: S0
| **Stage** | **Birth rate** | **Death rate** | **Result** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High | High | Stable, pre-industrial |
| 2 | High | Falling | Population explosion |
| 3 | Falling | Low | Growth slows |
| 4 | Low | Low | Stable, low growth (**AP is here**) |

- **India's phases:**
  - Stagnant **1901-21** (1921 = "great divide")
  - Steady 1921-51
  - **Explosion 1951-81**
  - Declining growth after 1981
- **Current stage:** Late Stage 3 moving into Stage 4
- **TFR:** Below replacement for the **fifth year running** (SRS 2024)
- ⚠ **India** = late Stage 3 into Stage 4; **AP** = Stage 4

## Terms → meaning (CBR, CDR, MMR, dependency ratio, youth) | from: S1, S2, S3
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **CBR/CDR** | Births/deaths per **1,000 mid-year population** |
| **MMR** | Maternal deaths per **1,00,000 live births** (some notes say per 1,000) |
| **Dependency ratio** | (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64) |
| Young-age ratio | 0-14 |
| Old-age ratio | 65+ |
| **Youth (National Youth Policy 2014)** | **15-29** |
| "Demographic nightmare" | Dividend without health, education, skills and jobs |

- **Dependency ratio significance:** Lower ratio signals a dividend (UPSC)
