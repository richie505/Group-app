## IPR 1948 → mixed economy and categories | from: S0
- **Status:** **First industrial policy of independent India**
- **Announced:** **6 April 1948** by Industry Minister **Dr. Shyama Prasad Mukherjee**
- **Economic system:** Foundation of a **mixed economy** - defined roles for both public + private sectors; reserved key sectors (arms, atomic energy, railways) for the State (APPSC-GS 2019, 2023 key)

| **Category (IPR 1948)** | **Industries** | **Arrangement** |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusive Central monopoly | Arms & ammunition, atomic energy, railways | State only |
| Mixed sector (6) | Coal, mineral oils, iron & steel, aircraft, shipbuilding, telephone/telegraph apparatus | New units by State; existing private units allowed for **10 years**, then review |
| Centrally regulated (18) | Salt, automobiles, heavy chemicals, fertilizers, cement, sugar, paper, textiles etc. | Private, under Central regulation |
| Rest (residual) | All others | Open to private and co-operative enterprise (private sector) |

- **Joint sector:** (Centre/State + private partnership) introduced later by **Industrial Policy of 1970**; 1948 created the "mixed sector" category
  - ⚠ Sources differ: the 1970 licensing policy (after the Dutt Committee) and the 1973 Industrial Policy Statement are both cited for the joint sector. These notes follow 1970 here.

## IPR 1956 → goal, schedules and provisions | from: S1
# 1. Background
- **IPR 1956 announced:** **30 April 1956**; reviewed IPR-1948 after the First Plan
- **Goal:** **"Socialist pattern of society"** (Avadi session, 1955)
- **Plan basis:** **Second Five Year Plan** (**Mahalanobis model** - heavy and basic industries)
> Exam angle: "Which resolution formed the basis of the Second Plan?" - IPR 1956.
- **Epithets:** **"Economic Constitution of India"**, **"Bible of State Capitalism"**
# 2. Schedules
- **Three schedules** set up:

| **Schedule** | **Content** | **Role of State** |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **17** industries of basic/strategic importance (defence, heavy industry, minerals, transport & communication, power) | Exclusive State responsibility; arms, atomic energy, railways, air transport were government monopolies |
| **B** | **12** industries (other minerals, road & sea transport, machine tools, ferro-alloys, basic chemicals, aluminium) | Progressively State-owned; State takes the lead, private sector may supplement |
| **C** | All remaining industries (rest) | Left to private initiative (private sector), under licensing |

- ⚠ Schedule **A = 17**, **B = 12**, **C** = rest
# 3. Major provisions & licensing
- **Major provisions:** **Licensing, expansion of the public sector, emphasis on small-scale industries** (APPSC-G2 2023: all three correct)
  - i.e. **licensing**, **expansion of public sector**, **small-scale/cottage industries**; also balanced regional development, labour welfare
- **Licensing basis:** **Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951** → 1956 regime grew into the **"Licence-Permit Raj"**

## Policy statements 1973, 1977, 1980 → features | from: S2, S3, S4
# 1. 1967-1973
- **Item reservation for SSI:**
  - Began **1967**
  - Continued after 1991
  - Last items dereserved in **2015**
- **1973 Statement:** Identified a **'core sector'** list; tightened MRTP
# 2. Industrial Policy Statement, 1977
- **Context:** Janata Government; largely drafted by **George Fernandes**
  - Shift from heavy industry → decentralisation + rural industrialisation
- **Emphasis:** **Small-scale sector** (identified/defined **'tiny' units**), **small-scale, tiny and cottage** industries, khadi and handlooms, employment
- **Curbs on large houses:** Via **MRTP and FERA** → **IBM and Coca-Cola** exited India
- **Key institution:** **District Industries Centres (DICs)** - one-roof support (credit, raw material, machinery, marketing) for small + cottage industries in every district
# 3. Industrial Policy Statement, 1980
- **Government:** Congress government
- **Basis:** **IPR 1956** (1956 Resolution) with modifications
- **Focus areas:**
  - **Technology:** **technological upgradation** + modernisation
  - **Exports:** **export orientation** (first strong emphasis)
  - **'Economic federalism':** tax holidays + cheap credit / incentives for **backward districts**
  - **Large vs small units:** Removed the "artificial barrier" between large and small units
- **Relaxations:**
  - **MRTP relaxed:** MRTP firms could expand capacity up to **25%** without fresh approval
  - **SSI:** Investment limits raised
  - **Excess capacity:** **regularised excess capacity**
- ⚠ **1977** = tiny units, DICs, IBM/Coca-Cola exit; **1980** = export orientation, economic federalism

## NIP 1991 → background and delicensing | from: S5
# 1. Background
- **Announced:** **24 July 1991** (PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Manmohan Singh**) after balance-of-payments crisis
- **Structure:** Two parts - large/medium industry + SSI
- **1991 reform:** Ended the **licence-permit-quota raj** - licensing, expansion limits, price controls, import-export restrictions went
# 2. Licensing
- **Licensing:** Abolished except **18 industries** → later reduced to **5**
  - Delicensed units file only an **Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM)**

| **Stage** | **Industries under compulsory licensing** |
|---|---|
| July 1991 | **18** |
| 1993 | 3 delicensed - automobiles, white goods, leather |
| Dec 1996 / Jul 1997 | Electronic goods; then 5 more delicensed |
| Budget 1998-99 | Coal, petroleum delicensed - **6** left |
| Now | **5** (CDI) |

- **The 6 (older list):**
  - Alcohol; tobacco (cigars/cigarettes); hazardous chemicals
  - Electronic aerospace & defence equipment; industrial explosives; drugs & pharmaceuticals
- **Now (5):** Alcohol, cigarettes/tobacco, electronic aerospace & defence equipment, industrial explosives, hazardous chemicals
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Older notes still say **6** (post-1998-99 list included drugs & pharmaceuticals, since delicensed)

- **1991 PSU measures:**
  - **20% disinvestment** in selected PSUs; disinvestment begun 1991-92
  - Greater autonomy through **MoUs**
  - Chronically sick PSUs referred to **BIFR** (set up under SICA 1985) for revival or closure
- **2016:** SICA repealed + BIFR dissolved; cases → Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code
- ⚠ Reserved: **17 → 8 → 3 → 2**; licensing: **18 → 6 → 5**

## NIP 1991 → MRTP, foreign investment and finance | from: S7
# 1. MRTP
- **MRTP:** Asset threshold for MRTP firms scrapped → no prior approval for expansion/mergers
  - Act later replaced by **Competition Act 2002**; many SSI items de-reserved
# 2. Foreign investment and technology
- **Foreign equity:** **Automatic approval for up to 51% foreign equity** in a list of **high-priority**, high-investment, high-technology industries (e.g. capital goods, entertainment electronics, food processing)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Not all industries (APPSC-G1 2012)
  - Also for export trading companies
- **Technology:** **Automatic approval for foreign technology agreements** in high-priority industries; foreign brand names/trademarks permitted
- **FIPB:** **Foreign Investment Promotion Board (FIPB)** set up to speed approvals
- **SSI equity:** Up to **24%** equity in SSI units by others
# 3. Finance and labour
- **Mandatory convertibility clause:** (FIs/lenders converting term loans to equity) relaxed for term loans
  - ⚠ **Key:** applies only to term loans of **less than 10 years** - learn the key's wording (APPSC-G2 2025 key, as per key)
- **National Renewal Fund (1992):** Safety net for workers hit by restructuring - VRS compensation, retraining, redeployment
# 4. Present position (2026)
- **FDI:** Up to **100%** under automatic route in most sectors; negative-list approach
- **FIPB abolished June 2017:** Approvals via FIF portal → integrated with **National Single Window System** since Aug 2022
- ⚠ 1991 = **51%** automatic foreign equity (priority list); SSI = **24%**; now **100%** automatic in most sectors

=== ROW 64 | Industrial sickness - meaning, causes, consequences and remedial measures

## Sick unit → definitions (SICA 1985 and RBI) | from: S0
# 1. SICA 1985: definition
- **General sense:** Unit **fails to generate a surplus on a continuing basis**; depends on repeated infusion of funds
- **SICA 1985 - original test:**
  - Industrial company registered for **at least 7 years**
  - **Accumulated losses equal or exceed its net worth** at end of a financial year
  - **Cash losses** in that year + the preceding year
- **1993 amendment:** Age cut to **5 years**; cash-loss test dropped (older notes give only the 7-year version)
# 2. RBI definition
- **RBI (micro and small units):** sick if
  - Any borrowal account stays **NPA for more than 3 months**, or
  - Net worth eroded by **50%** through accumulated losses in previous year
- **Replacement:** RBI's **Framework for Revival and Rehabilitation of MSMEs (2016)**
  - Early identification of incipient stress (SMA-0/1/2 categories)
  - Committees for Stressed MSMEs
- ⚠ SICA: **7 years** (original) → **5 years** (1993); RBI: NPA **> 3 months** or net worth eroded **50%**

## Causes and consequences of sickness → factors and effects | from: S1, S2
# 1. Causes
| **Internal (within the firm)** | **External (outside the firm)** |
|---|---|
| Management: poor planning, diversion of funds, faulty project appraisal | Demand: recession, sudden demand contraction, aggressive competition |
| Finance: excess debt, poor working-capital management | Credit: delayed and costly credit |
| Production: **obsolete technology**, poor quality control, low labour productivity | Infrastructure: chronic **power shortages**, transport bottlenecks |
| - | Policy: sudden changes in taxes, export-import policy, environmental rules |

- **MSMEs:** **Delayed payments** by large buyers + government departments = major trigger of sickness
# 2. Consequences and sectoral spread
- **Concentration:** **Textiles (cotton mills), jute, sugar and engineering**

| **Effect** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| **NPAs** | Bank loans turn into **NPAs** → weakens lenders |
| **Unemployment** | Why the State took over sick private units (esp. **textiles and engineering**) to protect jobs |
| Impact on PSUs | Public sector absorbed these sick private textile + engineering units → lowered PSU returns |
| **Locked capital** | Plant, land, funds idle in unviable units; revenue lost to exchequer |

## Institutions → year and functions (IRBI, BIFR, AAIFR) | from: S3
# 1. Before SICA
- **IRBI:** **Industrial Reconstruction Bank of India (IRBI)**, set up **1971** - rehabilitated sick units
# 2. BIFR set-up and functions
- **BIFR:** **Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR)** created by SICA; set up **January 1987** (functioning from 1987)
- **Appeals:** **Appellate Authority for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (AAIFR)**
- **Core job:** **revival of sick industries**
  - Early detection of sickness
  - Revival of viable units
  - Closure/winding-up of unviable ones
- **Coverage:** Both **public and private** companies with industrial undertakings
> Exam angle: "BIFR deals with?" - revival of sick industries (not stock markets, loan recovery or SSI promotion).
- ⚠ **SICA** = 1985 Act; **BIFR** under it set up **January 1987**

## Schemes and funds → purpose (NRF, margin money, RBI measures) | from: S3, S4, S6
# 1. National Renewal Fund, 1992
- **Set up:** **1992** (after 1991 reforms) as a **social safety net for industrial restructuring**
- **Purpose:**
  - **Help workers displaced by retrenchment during restructuring/modernisation** - VRS compensation, **retraining**, redeployment
  - Support restructuring + modernisation of industries
- **Beneficiaries:** Workers of sick/restructured units; organised-sector workers, mainly in **public sector undertakings**
- **End:** Wound up in 2000
# 2. Other measures
- **Liberalized Margin Money Scheme (June 1987):** Reduced sickness in small-scale sector
- **RBI measures:**
  - 2016 MSME revival framework
  - **One-time restructuring** of stressed MSME loans without downgrade (2019, extended 2020-21)
- **Present framework:**
  - Companies = **IBC 2016** (NCLT)
  - Delayed payments = **MSME Samadhaan**

## Acts → BIFR failure and the IBC successor | from: S5
- **Why it failed:**
  - Procedural delays
  - Legal loopholes let promoters stall debt recovery (a SICA reference froze recovery)
  - Ineffective revival mechanism
- **Abolition:** BIFR ceased on **30 November 2016**; from **1 December 2016** BIFR + AAIFR dissolved
- **Successor:** Pending cases → **NCLT/NCLAT** under **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016**

| **Old regime** | **Successor** |
|---|---|
| SICA 1985 | IBC 2016 (time-bound resolution, "maximisation of asset value") |
| BIFR | National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) - adjudicating authority |
| AAIFR | National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) |
| Debtor-in-possession | Creditor-in-control; regulator IBBI; licensed Insolvency Professionals |

- ⚠ BIFR → **NCLT**; AAIFR → **NCLAT**

## MRTP Act, 1969 → purpose, thresholds and replacement | from: S1
# 1. Purpose & origin
- **Purpose:** **Control private monopoly** + concentration of economic power; curb monopolistic, restrictive and unfair trade practices (APPSC-GS 2019 key)
- **Recommended by:** **Dutt Committee** (Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee)
- **Timeline:** Enacted **1969**, effective **1970**
# 2. MRTP firm thresholds
| **Year** | **Threshold** |
|---|---|
| **Original** | Assets **₹20 crore+** or **33%** market share |
| **1984** | Share cut to **25%** |
| **1985** | Asset limit raised to **₹100 crore** |
| **NIP 1991** | **Scrapped the MRTP asset threshold** (Rs 100 crore since 1985) → large firms no longer needed prior approval to expand, merge or take over |

# 3. Replacement
- **Replaced by Competition Act, 2002:** MRTP Commission → **CCI** (APPSC-GS 2018 key: 2002)
  - Some notes say "Competition Act, 2001"
- ⚠ MRTP market share **33% → 25%** (1984); assets **₹20 crore → ₹100 crore** (1985)

## LPG terms → meaning (disinvestment, privatisation, monetisation) | from: S3, S10
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Disinvestment** | Sale of government equity in PSEs; government may keep majority and control - **reduction of government share in public sector**, the term for **reducing government share** (APPSC-GS 2019) |
| **Privatisation** | Transfer of **ownership and management control** to private sector (strategic sale) |
| **Strategic sale** | Block of shares (usually 50%+) + **transfer of management control** to a strategic partner |
| **Liberalisation** | Removal of licences, quotas and controls (not a sale); State acts as facilitator |
| **Globalisation** | Integration with world economy - tariff cuts, FDI, FERA to FEMA |
| **Monetisation** | Leases **brownfield, de-risked** public assets (roads, railways, power lines, pipelines) to private operators for a fixed period; **Ownership stays with government**; assets return |

| **Type** | **Government stake / control** |
|---|---|
| Minority disinvestment | Keeps more than 51% and control |
| Majority disinvestment | Control transferred, minority stake kept |
| Complete privatisation | 100% sold - ownership and control to buyer - most comprehensive mode |

- ⚠ Monetisation leases assets vs Disinvestment = sells equity permanently

## Disinvestment → rationale, DIPAM, phases and criticism | from: S5, S8
# 1. Rationale and DIPAM
- **Rationale:** Poor returns of many PSUs; fiscal deficit; efficiency; market listing
- **Department of Disinvestment:** Set up **1999** (set up Dec 1999); some notes date it to 2000
  - Later **DIPAM** under Ministry of Finance (renamed 2016)
- **DIPAM** (Ministry of Finance): Manages Central Government equity + disinvestment
  - Modes: strategic sale, **OFS**, IPO/FPO, CPSE ETF
# 2. Phases
| **Phase** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| 1991-98 | minority sales (₹17,557 cr) |
| 1999-2004 | first strategic sales |
| 2014 onwards | ~**70%** of all proceeds since 1991 |

# 3. Criticism
- **"Selling the family silver":** Proceeds used to bridge fiscal deficit rather than build assets
- **Other concerns:**
  - **Undervaluation** of PSU assets
  - Loss of dividend income
  - National-security concerns in oil, defence, transport

## PSE restructuring → institutions and New PSE Policy 2021 | from: S9
# 1. Restructuring institutions

# 2. New PSE Policy 2021 (Atmanirbhar Bharat)
- **4 strategic sectors:**
  - (i) Atomic energy, space, defence
  - (ii) Transport & telecom
  - (iii) Power, petroleum, coal, minerals
  - (iv) Banking, insurance, financial services
- **Rule:** "Bare minimum" 1-4 CPSEs each; non-strategic → privatise, merge or close
# 3. Recent proposals
- **16th FC:** Close **308** inactive State PSUs; privatise DISCOMs
- **Economic Survey 2025-26 proposal:** Let government stake in listed CPSEs fall to **26%** while retaining "Government company" status
- ⚠ NCLT = **s.408**; NCLAT = **s.410**

## Universal angles | from: S12 | universal
- **Current relevance:** Policy shifted from selling PSUs → earning from them (dividends)
  - Disinvestment receipts falling since 2022-23; dividends rising
  - No separate disinvestment heading in Budget
  - **NMP 2.0** launched (1 Mar 2026)
- **Criticism & debates:** Proceeds fill fiscal deficit - "selling family silver"; union resistance; risk of private monopolies
- **Comparative note:**
  - Singapore's **Temasek model** = government as passive shareholder
  - Disinvestment sells equity vs monetisation leases assets vs strategic sale transfers control
- **AP angle:** Post-1991 APIIC turned facilitator; PPP ports Krishnapatnam + Gangavaram
