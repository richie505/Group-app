## Banking — definitions, types and FIRSTs | from: S0, S3
# 1. FIRSTs
| **First** | **Fact** |
|---|---|

# 2. Organised vs unorganised
| **Sector** | **Who** |
|---|---|
| **Unorganised** | Moneylenders + indigenous bankers |
| **Organised** | RBI, commercial banks (public, private, foreign, RRBs, SFBs, payments banks, local area banks), co-operative banks, DFIs (**NABARD**, **Exim**) |
# 3. Types of banks
- **Five ownership groups:** SBI, nationalised banks, RRBs, foreign banks, private banks
  - Also SFBs, payments banks, local area banks
  - **SFBs** exist for financial inclusion
- **Scheduled banks:** Listed in the **Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934**
  - Requirement: paid-up capital and reserves of at least **₹5 lakh**
  - Privilege: may borrow from RBI at bank rate/repo/MSF
- **Co-operative banks:** Registered under State Co-operative Societies Acts + regulated by RBI (**BR Act**); one member one vote
- **SBI group:** Associate banks + **Bharatiya Mahila Bank** merged into SBI on **1 April 2017**
- **Foreign banks:** Operate as branches or as wholly owned subsidiaries
- **Banks vs NBFCs:** NBFCs cannot take demand deposits or issue cheques; deposits not **DICGC**-insured
  - !! DICGC: formed in 1978 by merging the DIC (1962) and CGCI [GK].

## Basel Accords — bodies, years and FIRSTs | from: S3, S13
# 1. Framework
- **Framer:** **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** under the **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)** (UPSC)
  - BIS est. **1930** → world's oldest international financial organisation
- **Coverage:** Capital risk, market risk, operational risk
| **Accord** | **Year** | **Focus / core content** |
|---|---|---|
| Basel I | **1988** | Credit risk only; five risk buckets (0, 10, 20, 50, 100%); minimum capital standard 8% of risk-weighted assets |
| Basel II | **2004** | Three pillars: minimum capital, supervisory review, market discipline |
| Basel III | Post-2008 crisis (reply to 2007-09 crisis); in India from **1 Jan 2013** | More/better capital (CET1), capital buffers, **leverage ratio**, **LCR**, **NSFR**, counterparty credit risk |
- **Basel III three pillars** (as your notes phrase them): absorb shocks; improve risk management and governance; improve transparency and disclosure
# 2. Basel Accords vs Basel Convention
|  | **Basel Accords** | **Basel Convention** |
|---|---|---|
| Body | BCBS / BIS, Basel (Switzerland) | UNEP-administered treaty [GK] |
| Subject | Bank capital, liquidity and risk | Transboundary movement of **hazardous wastes** and their disposal (adopted **1989**, in force 1992) [GK] |

## Data — capital adequacy norms, RBI vs BCBS | from: S3
- **India:** RBI introduced CRAR in **1992** as part of reforms, following BIS standards; set at **9%** under Basel I (**1999**)
| **Norm** | **BCBS minimum** | **RBI norm** |
|---|---|---|
| Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) | 4.5% | **5.5%** |
| Tier 1 | 6% | 7% |
| Minimum / total CRAR | 8% | **9%** (UPSC) |
| Capital conservation buffer | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| CRAR + CCB | 10.5% | **11.5%** |
| Countercyclical buffer | 0-2.5% | Framework since **2015**, **not activated** (20 May 2026) |
| Leverage ratio | 3% | 4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for others |
| LCR / NSFR | 100% each | 100% each |
- **RBI vs BCBS:** RBI norm **9%** stricter than BCBS's **8%**
  - Some notes list "Tier I 4.5%" and "total CRAR 9.5%" → **4.5%** is the BCBS **CET1** floor
- **Actual bank figure:** **HDFC Bank** CAR **19.6%** vs regulatory norm **11.9%** (19 Jul 2026)
- **Oct 2025 proposal:** RBI proposed (draft) lower **differentiated risk weights** for MSMEs and real estate under Basel III → lower minimum capital needs

## Terms → meaning: capital, buffers and liquidity ratios | from: S3, S4, S5
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **CAR/CRAR** | Capital / risk-weighted assets |
| **Tier 1** | Pure equity (paid-up capital, reserves, undistributed profits) |
| **Tier 2** | Equity-debt mix (e.g. subordinated debt) |
| **Capital conservation buffer (CCB) 2.5%** | Buffer of 2.5% |
| **Counter-cyclical buffer (CCyB) 0-2.5%** | Checks excess credit growth; introduced in India by RBI in **2015**; main trigger = **credit-to-GDP gap** |
| **Leverage ratio** | **Tier-1 capital / total (non-risk-weighted) exposure** |
| **LCR >= 100%** | Stock of high-quality liquid assets (**HQLA**) to survive a **30-day** stress |
| **NSFR** | Stable funding over a **1-year** horizon (2026) |
| **AT-1 bonds** | **Perpetual** debt with a call option |
- **Leverage ratio:** Caps lending relative to own capital → floor that stops balance sheets growing on thin capital (APPSC-GS 2019)
  - Came with Basel III, not Basel II
- **HQLA levels:** Level 1 HQLA = cash, G-secs, central-bank reserves; Level 2 = highly rated corporate bonds
  - RBI deferred revised LCR norms to **31 Mar 2026**
> Exam angle: match each ratio to its purpose (LCR = 30 days, NSFR = 1 year, CCyB = credit boom); RBI vs BCBS minimums.

=== ROW 86 | Non-performing assets - causes, twin balance sheet problem, recognition and provisioning

## NPA — definitions, classification and types | from: S0, S1
# 1. Definition
- **NPA:** Loan/advance whose **interest or principal instalment stays overdue for more than 90 days** (APPSC-G1 2022 key)
  - UPPSC 2009: a loan on which interest or principal is not recovered
- **Farm loans:**
  - Short-duration crops = overdue for **2 crop seasons**
  - Long-duration crops = overdue for **1 crop season**
# 2. Classification - SMA, standard, sub-standard, doubtful, loss
| **Class / category** | **Criterion / meaning** |
|---|---|
| Standard | Regular; no overdue beyond 90 days |
| SMA-0 / SMA-1 / SMA-2 | Special Mention Accounts, overdue 1-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 days: early warning, not yet NPA |
| Sub-standard | NPA for **12 months or less** (up to **12 months**) |
| Doubtful | NPA for **more than 12 months** |
| Loss | Loss identified by the bank, auditors or RBI but not yet written off |
- **Special Mention Accounts:** **1-90 days** overdue; stressed but still standard → **SMA-0**, **SMA-1**, **SMA-2**
  - **Day bands:** **0-30 / 31-60 / 61-90** days
  - **Sequence:** Standard → SMA-0/1/2 → NPA (sub-standard → doubtful → loss)
- ⚠ SMA = up to **90 days**, still standard; NPA = beyond **90 days**; doubtful = NPA **more than 12 months**

## Terms → meaning: NPA and stress measures | from: S0, S1, S2, S6
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Gross NPA** | All NPAs |
| **Net NPA = Gross NPA - provisions** | (2026) |
| **Stressed assets** | **NPAs + restructured loans + written-off assets** |
| **Interest coverage ratio** | Operating earnings (**EBIT**) / interest due |
| **Wilful defaulter** | Can pay but does not, or diverts funds |
| **Evergreening** | Fresh loans to repay old ones → default stays hidden; the **AQR** ended it |
| **PCR** | Provisions held / gross NPAs |
| **Income recognition** | Interest on an NPA **not booked as income until actually received** |
| **IRACP** | Framework = Income Recognition, Asset Classification, Provisioning (May 2026) |
- **Interest coverage ratio:** Shows a borrower's **present** and **emerging** risk when a bank lends (UPSC 2020 key: statements 1 and 2)
  - ICR below **1** → earnings do not cover interest

## Twin balance sheet problem — Economic Survey 2016-17 | from: S3
- **Meaning:** **Over-leveraged corporates** + **capital-starved, NPA-laden banks** at the same time
  - Firms cannot invest → banks cannot lend → private capex stalls
- **Framed in:** **Economic Survey 2016-17** (CEA **Arvind Subramanian**), chapter on the **"festering"** twin balance sheet problem
- **Mechanism:** Loan losses eat bank capital → banks freeze new loans rather than recall old ones → private investment falls (worked example)

## Causes of NPAs — factor → effect | from: S2
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Aggressive lending (2010-15)** | To infrastructure, steel, power without strict appraisal; stalled projects |
| **Global shocks** | **2008** crisis + weak exports hurt borrowers' cash flows |
| **Wilful defaults and fraud** | Promoters diverted funds to shell companies |
| **Evergreening** | Default stays hidden |
- **Vicious cycle:** Aggressive lending + shocks → NPAs → provisioning → capital erosion → weak credit

## Asset Quality Review (2015) and the 4R response | from: S4
- **Conducted by:** **RBI in 2015** (10 Feb 2026)
- **Purpose:** Uncover hidden NPAs + make banks recognise them
- **Outcome:**
  - Disclosed/reported NPAs jumped sharply, peaking in **2018**
  - Problem became transparent → exposed the **twin balance sheet problem**
  - Led on to the **IBC** and recapitalisation
- **Government response:** **4R strategy** (Recognise, Resolve, Recapitalise, Reform) with **IBC**, recapitalisation and **NARCL**

## Rules → new NPA Master Directions (from 1 April 2027) and ECL | from: S7
# 1. New NPA Master Directions
- **Borrower-level tagging:** One loan of a borrower is an NPA → all that borrower's loans at the bank become NPAs
- **Overdue test:** **90-day** test stays
- **Upgrade:** Only after all arrears on every facility are paid
- **Identification:** Banks must identify NPAs through **automated systems** (28 Apr 2026; May 2026)
# 2. Expected Credit Loss
- **ECL shift:** RBI moving from **incurred-loss** model to a 3-stage **Expected Credit Loss (ECL)** model
| **ECL stage** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 (low risk) | **12-month** expected loss |
| Stage 2 (significant rise in risk) | Lifetime loss |
| Stage 3 (credit-impaired, over **90 days**) | Full lifetime provision |
- Loss allowances use the **effective interest rate (EIR)** method
