=== ROW 102 | Public expenditure - revenue and capital, developmental and non-developmental, trends; subsidies

## Revenue vs capital expenditure — definitions and classifying items | from: S0
# 1. Definitions and classification test
| **Revenue expenditure (no asset, no liability reduction)** | **Capital expenditure (creates asset / reduces liability)** |
|---|---|
| Does not create assets; running the government | Creates physical/financial assets |
| Recurring | Mostly non-recurring |
| Salaries, pensions, **interest payments** | Roads, airports, irrigation, buildings |
| **Subsidies** (food, fertiliser, PDS) | Purchase of defence equipment (capital outlay) |
| **Grants to State governments** (even if they create assets for States) | **Loans and advances to States**, PSUs, foreign governments |
| Maintenance of existing assets | **Repayment of loans** (reduces liability) |

- **Test:** Expense that changes Govt's **assets or liabilities** = capital; all else = revenue
- **Thumb rule:** **Revenue = running the government; Capital = creating assets**
> Exam angle: food and fertiliser subsidies are **revenue expenditure** (APPSC-G2 2008).
- ⚠ Grants to States = **revenue** even if they create assets; loans to States = **capital**
# 2. AP composition
- **AP order:** Revenue Expenditure > Capital Outlay > Loans and Advances
- **AP share:** Revenue expenditure ~**83%** of **2026-27 BE** total
- **AP revenue spending:** Rice subsidy, power subsidy, welfare pensions, housing

## Economists → laws of public expenditure growth | from: S1

|---|---|
| **Wagner's law (Adolph Wagner)** | Public expenditure rises **faster than national income** as economy industrialises → **law of increasing state activity** |

- **Ranking:** States' share of taxes **22%** > **interest 20%** > central sector schemes **17%** > defence **11%** > CSS **8%** > FC and other transfers **7%** = other **7%** > major subsidies **6%** > pensions **2%**
- **Interest payments:** **₹14,03,972 crore**
  - **26%** of total expenditure
  - **40% of revenue receipts** (**37%** in 2013-14; **42%** in 2020-21)
- **Committed expenditure** (salaries, pensions, interest) = **65.3% of revenue receipts**
# 3. Capital expenditure push
- **Capex 2026-27:** **₹12,21,821 crore (+11.5%)**
  - = Capital outlay **₹9,43,042 crore** + loans **₹2,78,780 crore**
  - Growth: **28%** in **Apr-Nov 2025**; **23.7%** in **Q1 2026-27**
- **Effective capital expenditure:** Capex + **grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets**
  - BE 2026-27 = **₹17.15 lakh crore (4.4% of GDP)**; of which grants **₹4.93 lakh crore**
  - Effective capex / fiscal deficit = **101.1%**
- **SASCI:** **50-year interest-free loans** to States for capex; **₹1.85 lakh crore** in 2026-27

## Subsidies — size, explicit vs hidden, NBS, Oil Pool Account | from: S4

=== ROW 103 | Public debt - internal and external, debt-GDP ratio, sustainability, state debt and off-budget borrowing

## Public debt — meaning and types of the Centre's liabilities | from: S0
- **Public Debt (Art. 292):** Borrowings contracted against the **Consolidated Fund of India**
- **Other liabilities:** Sit in the **Public Account** (government acts as **banker/trustee**)

| **Head** | **Items** |
|---|---|
| Internal debt - marketable | **Market loans (dated securities / G-Secs)**, **Treasury Bills** (91, 182, 364-day), Cash Management Bills |
| Internal debt - non-marketable | 14-day Intermediate T-Bills (to States, select central banks), **special securities to NSSF**, securities to international financial institutions (IMF, World Bank, ADB), compensation bonds, saving bonds |
| Other liabilities (Public Account) | **Small savings**, post-office deposits, **provident funds**, **reserve funds and deposits** |
| External debt (Centre's own) | Loans from foreign governments and multilateral institutions (excludes State borrowings) |

> Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2017 - component of internal debt = **market loans** (small savings, PF, reserve funds are "other liabilities"; foreign loans are external).

## Central government debt and market borrowings — data | from: S1
# 1. Composition and size
| **Measure** | **Value** |
|---|---|
| **G-Secs** | Over **90%** of Central Government debt |
| **Internal debt** | **96.7%** of public debt |
| **External liabilities** | Only **3.3%** of Centre's total liabilities (**₹7,11,182 crore**, BE 2026-27) |
# 2. Market borrowings
- **Biggest source:** **Market borrowings** = biggest source of **capital receipts** (APPSC-G2 2008)
  - "Borrowings and other liabilities" = **24 paise of every rupee** in 2026-27 → single largest receipt head
- **2026-27 dated securities:**
  - Gross market borrowing **₹17.20 lakh crore**; net **₹11.73 lakh crore**
  - Weighted average maturity **19.03 years**; weighted average yield **6.65%**
  - **Switches and buybacks** to ease redemptions
- **Total borrowing requirement 2026-27:** **₹16,95,768 crore**
- **Household savings → Govt borrowing (UPSC 2022, both true):**
  - Via bank deposits (**SLR** holdings), **small savings/NSSF**, insurance/PF
  - **Dated securities** issued at market-related rates in auctions = large component of internal debt

## External debt — data, composition and publisher | from: S2
# 1. Size
- **India's external debt:** **USD 718.2 billion** at end-Dec 2024 = **18.4% of GDP**
- **Other estimate:** External debt-GDP **20.4%**; forex reserves cover **90%** of it (**RBI Annual Report**, Jun 2026)
- **Ranking:** **21st** in world; **11th** in **G20**
# 2. Composition
- **Non-sovereign dominance:** **Most external debt is non-sovereign** (owed by companies/banks)
  - Government's external debt ≈ **2.6% of GDP**; under **5%** of its total debt
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Not all external debt is in **US dollars** (UPSC 2019: both statements wrong)
- **Largest component historically:** **Commercial borrowings** (UPPSC 2017 key)
- **Sovereign borrowing:** Mostly **multilateral, concessional, project-linked**
  - **ADB is the largest multilateral lender (32.8%)** → then **World Bank (IBRD/IDA)**, **NDB**, **AIIB**
# 3. Publisher
| **Report** | **Publisher** |
|---|---|
| **"India's External Debt: A Status Report"** | Published by **Ministry of Finance (DEA)**; **RBI** compiles quarterly data |

## Debt-GDP targets and sustainability terms | from: S3, S4
# 1. Debt-GDP ratio — values and targets
- **New anchor:** **Budget 2026-27** made the **debt-GDP ratio the primary target** instead of fiscal deficit

| **Measure** | **Value / target** |
|---|---|
| Centre, BE 2026-27 | **55.6%** = outstanding liabilities **₹2,14,82,050 crore** (RE 2025-26: 56.1%; peak **61%** in 2020-21) |
| Centre, new medium-term target | **50 ± 1% by 31 March 2031** |
| General government (FC-16 projection) | **77.3% (2026-27) → 73.1% (2030-31)** |
| FRBM Act as amended 2018 | General govt **60%**, Centre **40%** by 2024-25 |
| N.K. Singh Committee (2017) | **60%** by 2023 = Centre **40%** + States **20%** |

- **Data basis:** BUD; 11 Mar 2026; **p.128**
# 2. Sustainability terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Debt trap** | Borrowing to pay interest on past debt; **Zero primary deficit** → fresh borrowing only covers interest |
| **Interest-growth differential (r - g)** | g (nominal GDP growth) > r (effective interest rate) → debt-GDP can fall even with small primary deficit; r > g → **primary surplus** needed |
| **States' key ratios** | **Debt-GSDP** + **interest payments / revenue receipts (IP/RR)** |
# 3. 2026-27 data
- Effective interest rate on Central debt **7.12%** (rising for **three years**) vs nominal GDP growth **10%**
- **Interest / revenue receipts about 40%**
- **Low debt risks:** Floating-rate debt only **4.1%** (end-**FY24**); external debt small, long-term, concessional

## State borrowing — Art. 293 limits, highest debt-GSDP States, AP | from: S5
# 1. Constitutional limits
- **Art. 293:**
  - States borrow only **within India** on security of the State Consolidated Fund
  - State owing the Centre needs **Centre's consent** to borrow
- **States:** Issue **SDLs (State Development Loans)**
- **External loans:** Since **2005-06** only through the Centre → **back-to-back** for general category States
- **16th FC:** States' fiscal deficit capped at **3% of GSDP** for **2026-31**
# 2. Recent trends
- **Borrowing:** States borrowed **₹5.23 trillion** up to **Oct 2025** (**62%** of FY26 plan)
- **Fiscal deficit:** Combined States' FD **3.2% of GDP** in **FY25**; **18 States** above 3% in **FY24**

| **State** | **Debt / outstanding liabilities (% of GSDP)** |
|---|---|
| **Punjab** | **42.9%** (2023-24) |
| West Bengal | 38.3% (2023-24); 39% (2025-26 BE) |
| Rajasthan | 37.9% (2023-24) |
| Kerala | 35.5% (2025-26 BE) |
| Andhra Pradesh | 34.6% (2023-24) |

- **Data basis:** **FC-16** inter-State comparison; **RBI Study of State Budgets 2025**
- ⚠ Highest debt-GSDP = **Punjab** (not Kerala or WB)
# 3. AP liabilities
- **Total debt (2025-26 RE):** **₹6,35,177 crore = 36.04% of GSDP** (**35.73%** in 2024-25)
  - Market borrowing **₹4,83,093 crore** (largest component)
  - Interest **₹37,233 crore**
- **Earlier figures (2024-25 RE):** **₹5,64,488 crore**, **35.15%** (Survey RE); accounts-based figure **₹5,68,623 crore**
- **Composition (2023-24 RE):** Market loans **75%** > others **12%** > central loans **6%** ≈ PF **6%** > small savings **1%**

## Off-budget borrowing — FCI and NSSF, guarantees, AP corporations | from: S6
# 1. Concept
- **Off-budget borrowing:** Loans raised by govt-owned entities (**PSUs, SPVs, corporations**) but **serviced from the budget** → not in the deficit
- **Centre's classic case:** **FCI borrowed from the NSSF** to cover unpaid food subsidy
  - **Budget 2021-22** ended this → food subsidy fully on-budget
- **Guarantees:** = **contingent liabilities**; **FRBM** limits new Central guarantees to **0.5% of GDP** a year
# 2. AP
| **Item** | **Figure** |
|---|---|
| **Off-budget debt** | **₹1.28 lakh crore (March 2023) = 9.7% of GSDP**; **₹15,499 crore** spent in **2022-23** servicing it (**CAG**); **₹1,40,470 crore** as of **31 March 2024** (**Principal AG**, 20 Feb 2026) |
| **Guarantees** | **₹1,77,328 crore (10% of GSDP)** |
| **Total liabilities incl. PSUs** | **₹9,74,556 crore** (**White Paper, 12 Jun 2024**) |
# 3. 16th FC
- **Recommendations:** Stop off-budget borrowing; bring existing debt on-budget; widen definitions of deficit and debt
- **Union response:** **Deferred** accepting this
