## Functions of RBI — and what RBI does not do | from: S3
# 1. Functions

| **Function** | **Legal basis / content** |
|---|---|
| Monetary authority | Frames, runs and implements monetary policy; price stability primary, growth secondary; six-member statutory **MPC** (RBI Act) sets the repo rate |
| Currency authority / bank of issue | **Sec 22, RBI Act**: **Sole right to issue notes** except the Re 1 (₹1) note; exchanges and destroys unfit notes |
| Banker, agent and adviser to Government | **Sec 20** (Centre); **Sec. 21/21A** (State Governments, by agreement): payments, receipts, public debt management (Public Debt Office), Ways and Means Advances, overdrafts, G-sec operations |
| Banker's bank / lender of last resort | Keeps SCBs' accounts, holds and controls bank reserves, lends to banks in distress (severe liquidity trouble) |
| Custodian of forex | Manages the forex reserves and market under **FEMA 1999**; represents India at the IMF and World Bank |
| Regulator and supervisor | Banks and NBFCs (licences, capital adequacy), AIFIs; HFCs since 2019 (moved over from NHB); payment systems under the **PSS Act, 2007** |
| Developmental | Helped set up IDBI, NABARD, SIDBI, EXIM, NHB (ownership later moved to Government) |

- **Public Debt Office:** Functions under RBI
  - Public Debt Management Cell created to ease the debt-vs-monetary-policy conflict of interest
# 2. What RBI does not do
- **Not done by RBI:** Accept deposits from or lend to the general public; mint coins; issue the Re 1 note
- **Not regulated by RBI:** Securities market (**SEBI**); insurance (IRDAI); pensions (PFRDA)

## Currency management — issuer, reserve systems, denominations, note features | from: S4, S5
# 1. Issuing authority

| **Item** | **Issuer** | **Legal tender** |
|---|---|---|
| **Notes** | RBI (signed by the Governor) | Every RBI note = unlimited legal tender |
| **Re 1 note and coins** | **Government of India** (Re 1 note signed by the Finance Secretary) | Coins = limited legal tender |

- **Coins:** Minted by Government of India; RBI only distributes them as Government's agent
  - Can be issued up to **₹1,000** (Coinage Act)
# 2. Reserve backing and printing limit

| **System** | **Rule** |
|---|---|
| **Proportional Reserve System** (until **1956**) | 2/5 of backing in gold or sterling, with gold at least ₹40 crore |
| **Minimum Reserve System** | Introduced in 1956; since 1957 RBI must hold at least **₹200 crore** of gold and foreign exchange, of which **₹115 crore** in gold |

- ⚠ Notes say 1956 or 1957: system came in 1956, the ₹200 crore floor in 1957
- **Printing limit:** Beyond that floor, RBI can issue currency without limit
- **Backing:** Notes = RBI liabilities, backed by gold, foreign securities and G-secs held in the **Issue Department**
# 3. Denominations and demonetisation
- **Highest denomination (RBI Act):** **₹10,000** (Sec. 24)
  - ₹10,000 note: introduced 1938 → demonetised 1946 → reintroduced 1954 → demonetised 1978
- **Demonetisation power:** **Sec. 26(2)** of the RBI Act
- **Episodes:**
  - **1946**
  - **1978:** High Denomination Bank Notes (Demonetisation) Act - ₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000
  - **8 Nov 2016:** ₹500, ₹1,000; SC upheld by **4:1** in 2023
- **₹2,000 note:** Withdrawn from circulation **May 2023** but remains legal tender
# 4. Note features
- **Languages:** **17 languages** on notes
  - Front: Hindi and English
  - Back: **language panel of 15** scheduled languages
- **MANI** (Mobile Aided Note Identifier): App for visually impaired users to identify denominations (RBI, 2020)

## Money — functions and kinds | from: S0, S2
# 1. Functions of money

| **Class** | **Functions** |
|---|---|
| Primary | Medium of exchange; measure of value (unit of account) |
| Secondary | Store of value; standard of deferred payments; transfer of value |
| Contingent | Basis of credit; distribution of national income; gives liquidity [GK] |

- **Not a function:** **Production of goods is NOT a function of money** (APPSC 2025)
# 2. Kinds of money - legal tender
- RBI notes = unlimited legal tender anywhere in India
- Coins = limited legal tender
- **Digital Rupee** = legal tender under RBI Act (amended by Finance Act 2022)
- Plastic money (debit/credit cards) = not legal tender
# 3. Paired kinds

| **Pair** | **Difference** |
|---|---|
| **Hard vs soft money** | Hard = backed by gold/credible assets; soft = paper backed by government bonds |
| **Bank money vs cash** | Bank money = secondary money; cash = high-powered money |

## Terms → meaning | from: S2, S6

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Seigniorage** | Difference between a currency's face value and its cost of production; the central bank's profit from issuing currency |
| Legal tender | Money that a creditor **is legally bound to accept** in settlement of claims (UPSC 2018) |
| Fiat money | Notes and coins with no intrinsic value; money by government order |
| Near money | Highly liquid assets that cannot directly pay for things: time deposits, T-bills, bills of exchange, bonds near redemption, savings certificates |
| Plastic money | Debit and credit cards [GK] |
| **Token coin** | Rupee coin face value above metal value |
| **Velocity** | Average number of times a currency unit changes hands in a period |

- **Seigniorage:** Face value − cost of producing currency = issuer's profit (central bank/government)
  - A ₹500 note costs about ₹4 to print; a source of RBI surplus
> Exam angle: "Seigniorage refers to" - profit earned by the central bank/government by issuing currency.
- **Velocity:** Example: a **₹10** note used twice = **₹20** of transactions
  - Higher among low-income spenders and in booms

## Monetary aggregates — M0 (high-powered money) to M4, NM0-NM3, L1-L3 | from: S3, S4
# 1. High-powered money (M0)
- **Names:** **Reserve money, monetary base, base money, central bank money**, **high-powered money** (M0)
- **Role:** Basis of credit creation; most important money indicator for RBI
- **Uses (components) side:** currency held by the public + **cash reserves of banks** (cash in hand + deposits with RBI) + other deposits with RBI
- **Formula (PYQ):** **H = currency held by the public + cash reserves of commercial banks (with themselves and with RBI) + other deposits with RBI** (APPSC-G2 2025 answer)
- **Currency in circulation:** Notes + rupee coins + small coins = currency with the public + cash in hand with banks
- **'Other' deposits with RBI:** Quasi-government and financial institutions (incl. primary dealers), foreign central banks and governments, international agencies such as the IMF
# 2. M1 to M4 (Second Working Group, 1977)

| **Aggregate** | **Components** | **Type** |
|---|---|---|
| M0 (Reserve money) | Currency in circulation + Bankers' deposits with RBI + 'Other' deposits with RBI | Monetary base |
| **M1** | Currency with the public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI | **Narrow money**, most liquid |
| M2 | M1 + post office **savings bank** deposits | Narrow |
| **M3** | M1 + **time deposits** with the banking system | **Broad money** ("aggregate monetary resources"; main aggregate RBI tracks) |
| M4 | M3 + all post office (savings bank) deposits (excluding **NSCs**) | Broad, least liquid |

- **Origin:** **Second Working Group on Money Supply (1977)**
- **Liquidity order:** **M1 > M2 > M3 > M4** → M1 most liquid, M4 least liquid
- ⚠ M2 = **M1** + PO savings; M3 = **M1** (not M2) + time deposits
- **Sources of broad money:** Net bank credit to Government + bank credit to commercial sector + net foreign exchange assets of the banking sector + Government's currency liabilities to the public − net non-monetary liabilities of the banking sector
> Exam angle: "which is the broadest/most liquid", and matching aggregates with components.
# 3. New monetary and liquidity aggregates (1998)
- **Origin:** RBI's **Working Group on Money Supply: Analytics and Methodology of Compilation (1998)**, chaired by **Y. V. Reddy** (the third working group)
- **New monetary aggregates:** **NM0, NM1, NM2, NM3**
  - Residency-based; separate short-term from long-term deposits
- **Liquidity aggregates L1, L2, L3:**
  - Add post office deposits, term money/CDs of FIs, public deposits of NBFCs

## Money multiplier and credit creation — formula, policy effects | from: S5
# 1. Concept and formula
- **Money multiplier:** Broad money / reserve money (**M3/M0**)
- **Formula:** m = (1 + cdr) / (cdr + rdr)
  - **cdr** = currency-deposit ratio (preference for cash)
  - **rdr** = reserve-deposit ratio (CRR, SLR-driven)
- **Credit creation:** Banks keep reserves (**CRR, SLR**) and lend the rest → each loan creates a new deposit

| **Money creator** | **Creates** |
|---|---|
| **RBI** | Reserve money (notes) |
| **Government** | Coins and the ₹1 note |
| **Commercial banks** | Deposit money by lending beyond reserves they must hold (CRR/SLR) |

# 2. Policy effects

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Higher CRR, higher SLR or higher cdr** | **Lower multiplier** |
| Stronger banking habit (UPSC 2019) | Raises the multiplier |
| **Cash withdrawal** | Raises cdr + reduces bank reserves → lower multiplier, money supply contracts |
| RBI buying G-secs from the public (**OMO purchase**); Government borrowing from RBI (monetisation) | **Raise money supply** - injects reserve money |
| RBI selling G-secs (UPSC 2012) | **Reduce money supply** |
| **Weak banking habits** | High cash preference limits India's credit multiplier |

- Example: 10% reserves → ₹100 of base can expand deposits towards ₹1,000

## Economists → theories of demand for money | from: S6
# 1. Determinants of money demand
- **Basic driver:** Value of transactions sets money demand
- **Interest rate link:** At **higher interest rates, money demanded falls**
- **Fixed money supply:**
  - Rise in money demand → **raises the interest rate**
  - Fall in money demand → lowers it
# 2. Theories

| **Theory** | **Economist** |
|---|---|
| Quantity theory, **MV = PT** | Irving Fisher [GK] |
| Cash-balance approach | Marshall, Pigou (Cambridge) [GK] |
| **Liquidity preference** (transactions, precautionary, speculative motives) | **J.M. Keynes**[GK] |
| Inventory-theoretic transactions demand | **Baumol** (1952), Tobin (1956) [GK] |
| Modern quantity theory | Milton Friedman [GK] |

- **Quantity theory:**
  - **Fisher: MV = PT**
  - **Cambridge cash-balance: M = kPY** (k = 1/V)
- **Keynes' liquidity preference:**
  - Motives = transactions + precautionary + speculative (some notes name only transaction and speculative motives)
  - Speculative demand varies inversely with the interest rate
- **Baumol-Tobin:** Transaction demand for cash is also interest-sensitive
  - Holding cash has an opportunity cost (**square-root rule**)
- ⚠ **MV = PT** = Fisher; **M = kPY** = Cambridge (Marshall, Pigou)
