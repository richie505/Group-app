## Agenda 2030 — origin, adoption, nature | from: S0
# 1. Origin
- **Origin:**
  - Conceptualised at the **UN Conference on Sustainable Development (Rio+20), Rio de Janeiro, 2012**
  - Outcome document "**The Future We Want**" called for global goals to follow the MDGs
  - Colombia and Guatemala first proposed the SDG idea there
# 2. Adoption
- **Structure:** **17 SDGs** with **169 targets**, for **2015-2030**
- **Adopted by:** **UN General Assembly, 70th Session, 2015**
- **Document:** "**Transforming our World: the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development**"
# 3. Nature
- **Universal:** Applies to all nations (developed, developing, least developed)
- **Guiding motto:** "**no one is left behind**"
- **Human-rights basis:** **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** (1948) + international human-rights treaties
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Paris Agreement, Magna Carta and NAFTA are distractors
- ⚠ Conceived at **Rio+20 (2012)**; adopted at **UNGA 70th session (2015)**

## Terms → meaning (green economy to greenwashing) | from: S0, S2, S3, S5
# 1. Green economy and green technology
- **Green economy:**
  - Reduces environmental risks and ecological scarcity
  - **Decouples** growth from resource use and pollution
  - Fosters **green industries and green jobs** (the PYQ answer)
  - Central theme of **Rio+20, 2012**
- **Green jobs:** Work in farming, industry, services or administration that preserves or restores environmental quality
- **Green technology:** Pollutes less, uses resources sustainably, recycles more of its waste
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Incorrect** statement = "it is not expensive for developing countries"; high cost is its main barrier (PYQ key)
# 2. Circular economy
- **Definition:** Economic system built on **reuse, recycling and waste minimisation**
  - PYQ answer: "promotes the **reuse, recycling and repair** of materials to reduce waste"
  - ⚠ **Trap:** does **not** focus exclusively on carbon or fossil fuels

| **Linear model** | **Circular model** |
|---|---|
| Take, make, dispose | Reduce, reuse, repair, recycle; keep materials in use [GK] |
| Waste is the end point | Waste becomes a resource, which decouples growth from resource use (CDI) |

# 3. Other terms

| **ESG** | Three pillars: **Environmental, Social, Governance** |

=== ROW 83 | International treaties on chemicals and waste - Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm POPs, Minamata; Montreal Protocol and Kigali Amendment

## Ozone layer and ozone hole — terms, location, FIRSTs, UV effects | from: S0
# 1. Ozone layer
- **Location:** **Stratosphere (about 10-50 km)**; shields Earth from harmful **UV-B** radiation (some notes say 10-40 km)
- **Unit:** **Dobson Unit (DU)**; "ozone hole" = total ozone below **220 DU**
# 2. Ozone hole
- **Location:** First detected over **Antarctica**, mid-1980s
  - First reported **1985** by British Antarctic Survey scientists (some notes say discovered 1983)
- **Season:** Seasonal thinning in **Southern Hemisphere spring, Aug-Nov**
- **Polar stratospheric clouds:** Form in extreme cold of polar vortex → surfaces convert stable chlorine into active forms → destroy ozone when sunlight returns
# 3. UV radiation effects - target-wise
- **UV-B effects:** DNA damage in plants and animals

| **Target** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Human health | **Skin cancer**, **eye cataracts**, blindness, suppression of the human immune system; 1% ozone loss raises harmful UV ~2% |
| Crops | Reduced growth/photosynthesis in rice, wheat, corn, soybean; 1% UV-B rise may cut food output ~1% |
| Phytoplankton/marine | Damage to plankton and fish, shrimp, crab larvae; food-chain disruption |
| Materials | Degrades wood, plastic, rubber, fabrics |

## Ozone-depleting substances → use | from: S1
- **Main cause (APPSC 2025, twice):** **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** → release chlorine in the stratosphere, which breaks down ozone
  - ⚠ **Trap:** CO2, methane and N2 are the usual wrong options
- **Mechanism:** Chlorine and bromine atoms released by UV **catalytically** destroy ozone molecules

| **Substance** | **ODS?** | **Main uses/sources** |
|---|---|---|
| CFCs | Yes (main culprit) | Refrigerators, ACs, aerosol propellants, foam blowing, solvents |
| Halons (contain bromine) | Yes; **about 100 times more potent** than CFCs | Portable fire extinguishers |
| HCFCs | Yes, weaker (interim/transitional CFC substitute) | Refrigeration, foam, solvents, aerosols |
| Carbon tetrachloride, methyl chloroform | Yes (release ozone-destroying chlorine) | Solvents, chemical feedstock |
| Methyl bromide | Yes | Agricultural fumigant, quarantine treatment (pest control) |
| HFCs | **No** (non-ODS) but high global warming potential | ODS substitutes in ACs/refrigeration |

- **CFCs as greenhouse gases:** ~15% of greenhouse effect
  - APPSC-G2 2025 assertion-reason: "CFCs are GHGs and increase UV" + "CFCs are man-made (fridges, ACs, aerosols)" → A and R both true, but R is not the explanation (R only says where CFCs are used)
- ⚠ **HFCs** = not ODS but high GWP → Kigali; **halons** = bromine, fire extinguishers
> Exam angle: "which gas causes ozone depletion", ODS matched to its use, assertion-reason on CFCs.

## Vienna Convention and Montreal Protocol — provisions, results, World Ozone Day | from: S2
# 1. Vienna Convention (1985)
- **Vienna Convention (1985):** Framework treaty → cooperation on research, ozone monitoring, protective measures
# 2. Montreal Protocol (1987)
- **Nature:** Action treaty adopted under Vienna; legally binding
- **Adoption:** **16 September 1987**, signed at **Montreal, Canada**; in force **1 January 1989**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** a convention table says "Helsinki, Finland" - wrong
- **Aim:** Phase out production and consumption of **ozone-depleting substances**
- **Coverage:** About **100 man-made chemicals** → CFCs, halons, CTC, methyl chloroform, HCFCs, methyl bromide
- **FIRST:** First and only UN treaty to be **universally ratified** (ratified by every UN member); **198 Parties** (older notes say 197)
  - Called the most successful environmental treaty in history
- **Results:**
  - About **99% of ODS phased out** (as of 2024)
  - Ozone back to 1980 levels by **2040** for most of the world, **2045** over the Arctic, **2066** over the Antarctic
  - Avoids an estimated **0.5-1°C** of warming by 2050
- **Multilateral Fund:** Set up **1991**; technical + financial help to developing countries; India argued for it from 1989 and secured it in the 1990 negotiations
- ⚠ Recovery **2040 - 2045 - 2066** = world - Arctic - Antarctic
# 3. World Ozone Day
- **Date:** **16 September** (day the Montreal Protocol was signed, 1987)
- **Proclaimed:** UN General Assembly, **1994**
- **2026:** Marked **10 years since the Kigali Amendment was adopted**
  - !! Not in your sources: the official 2026 theme wording.

## Kigali Amendment, 2016 — HFC phase-down schedule | from: S3
# 1. Basics
- **Parent treaty:** Amends the **Montreal Protocol**
- **Adopted:** **2016 in Kigali, Rwanda**
- **In force:** **1 January 2019**
- **Nature:** Legally binding; phases down **hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)**
- **HFCs:**
  - Replaced ODS in **ACs and refrigerators**
  - Do not deplete ozone (not ODS) but very **high global warming potential** (potent GHGs)
# 2. Targets and India
- **Global target:** Cut HFC use by **80-85% by the late 2040s**
  - Full implementation avoids up to **0.5°C of warming by 2100**

| **Group** | **Baseline** | **Freeze** | **Final cut** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developed countries | 2011-13 | Reductions start 2019 | **85% by 2036** (2026) |
| Developing Group 1 (incl. China) | 2020-22 | 2024 | 80% by 2045 |
| **India's group (Group 2, with Gulf states, Iran, Iraq, Pakistan)** | **2024-26** | **2028** | **85% by 2047** |

- **India:** **Ratified in 2021**
  - Intermediate steps: 10% by 2032 → 20% by 2037 → 30% by 2042
- ⚠ India: baseline **2024-26**, freeze **2028**, **85% by 2047**
# 3. Updates
- **Update:** MoEFCC told all Central and State authorities to stop granting environmental clearances for **new or additional HFC production after 31 December 2027**
- **MOP37** (Montreal Protocol): Focused on implementing Kigali

## Basel Convention, 1989 — provisions, Ban Amendment | from: S5
- **Adopted:** **Basel, Switzerland, 1989**
- **Core objective:**
  - Controls the **transboundary movement of hazardous wastes and their disposal**
  - Reduces hazardous waste generation + requires environmentally sound management
- **Problem addressed (APPSC-G2 2025):** **Physical transfer of hazardous waste across borders for reprocessing or storage** (mostly dumping from rich → poorer countries)
- **India:**
  - Ratified **June 1992**
  - Implemented via **Prior Informed Consent** mechanism under the **Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016**
- **Recent changes:** **Plastic waste amendment** now operative; e-waste provisions strengthened
- **Ban Amendment:**
  - Bans exports of hazardous waste from OECD/EU countries + Liechtenstein to other countries
  - In force **5 December 2019**
> Exam angle: match Basel to hazardous waste, Montreal to the ozone layer and Kyoto to greenhouse gases. "Biodiversity" is the usual wrong option for Basel.

## Rotterdam and Stockholm Conventions — PIC, POPs, annexes | from: S6, S7
# 1. Rotterdam Convention
- **Core mechanism:** **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** for certain hazardous chemicals + **pesticides** in international trade
  - Importing country must agree before a listed chemical is exported to it
- **Timeline:** In force **2004**; India **acceded in 2005**
- **India's Designated National Authorities:** Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilisers + Ministry of Agriculture
  - Official Contact Points: MoEF&CC
- **Listed chemicals (Annex III):** Pesticides + industrial chemicals banned or severely restricted by Parties
- **BRS link:** Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm share one joint secretariat under UNEP
  - India: coordinated by MoEFCC's **Hazardous Substances Management Division**
# 2. Stockholm Convention - basics
- **Timeline:** Adopted **2001**; in force **2004**
- **Scope:** **Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)** = toxic chemicals that last long, bioaccumulate in living tissue, travel long distances
- **POPs Review Committee (POPRC):** Assesses candidate chemicals
- ⚠ Rotterdam and Stockholm both in force **2004**
# 3. Stockholm - chemicals listed
- **Initial list:** **12 POPs** (the "dirty dozen")
  - Key: **DDT, PCBs, dioxins and furans**
  - Others: aldrin, chlordane, dieldrin, endrin, heptachlor, hexachlorobenzene, mirex, toxaphene
- **Current count:** **34 chemicals** (some notes still say 23; others "over 30")

| **Annex** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| A | Elimination | aldrin, dieldrin, UV-328 |
| B | Restriction | DDT (limited use for malaria control) |
| C | Unintentional by-products | dioxins, furans |

- **2025 additions to Annex A:**
  - **Chlorpyrifos**: neurotoxic insecticide
  - **Long-chain PFCAs**: "forever chemicals" in cookware (PFAS family)
  - **Medium-chain chlorinated paraffins**: used in plastics
# 4. Stockholm - India
- **Ratified:** **2006**
- **2020:** Ratified **7 more POPs** incl. chlordecone, hexabromobiphenyl, pentachlorobenzene, hexabromocyclododecane, hexachlorobutadiene

## Minamata Convention — mercury, sources, origin of name | from: S8
- **Timeline:** Adopted **2013**; in force **2017**
- **Core objective:** Protect health + environment from man-made emissions of **mercury** and mercury compounds, across the whole mercury lifecycle
- **Membership:** 140+ Parties
- **Major emission sources:**
  - **Coal-fired power plants**
  - **Artisanal and small-scale gold mining**
  - Industrial processes + waste
- **Latest decision:** Phase out **dental amalgam by 2034**
- **Name:** From **Minamata, Japan**
  - Mercury in factory wastewater poisoned people eating local fish (1950s) → **Minamata disease**
