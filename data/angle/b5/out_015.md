=== ROW 60 | Hydropower and pumped storage; large dams and their trade-offs

## Hydropower — energy conversion, types and classification | from: S0, S1
# 1. Energy conversion
- **Stages:**
  - Stored water = **potential energy**
  - Falling water → **kinetic energy**
  - Spins the turbine → **mechanical energy**
  - Generator → **electrical energy** by electromagnetic induction
- **Net transformation:** **Potential → Electrical** (APPSC 2023; APPSC-GS 2023 key: potential to electrical)
# 2. Types of hydro plants
- **Run-of-the-river:** no large dam/reservoir; uses the natural flow without much altering the watercourse

| **Type** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| Run-of-river | Little or no storage; uses natural flow [GK] |
| Storage (reservoir) | Dam stores water, regulates output [GK] |
| **Pumped storage** | Surplus power pumps water uphill; released at peak - a "**grid battery**", fastest large-scale storage (CDI) |

# 3. Classification by capacity
- **Classification:** **Small hydro = up to 25 MW (MNRE)**; large hydro **>25 MW (Ministry of Power)** (2026)
- **Transfer history:** plants up to **3 MW** → MNRE in **1989**; up to **25 MW** in **November 1999**
- ⚠ **Small hydro (≤25 MW) = MNRE**; **large hydro (>25 MW) = Ministry of Power** - yet large hydro is now counted as renewable (March 2019)

## Large dams — sustainability and trade-offs | from: S3
# 1. Sustainability
- **Strengths:** clean (near-zero operating emissions), renewable water cycle, flexible
- **Benefits:** flood control, irrigation, drinking water, flexible power for grid stability
- **Verdict:** **hydro is the best source for sustainable development** (UPPSC 2012) - **hydro-electricity** cheap and clean once built (UPSC)
- **Weakness:** drought/snowpack variability
# 2. Trade-offs

| **Trade-off** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| Displacement | Bhakra Nangal, Sardar Sarovar, Tehri displaced millions; **18.5 million** affected by projects 1950-1990; over 3,300 dams built |
| Seismicity | **Seismic risk in the Himalayan region** |
| Sedimentation | Reduces reservoir capacity over time |
| Ecology | Alters river ecosystems, blocks fish migration |
| Gestation | **10-20 years** for major projects |

## Pumped storage — principle, grid role and projects outside AP | from: S6
# 1. Principle and grid role
- **Principle:** surplus hours (high solar/wind) → water **pumped from a lower to an upper reservoir**; peak demand → released to generate power
- **Nature:** a "**giant water battery**" - the fastest-responding large-scale storage
- **Grid role:** lets solar and wind stand in for coal at peak hours - needed for the 500 GW and 60% (2035) goals
- **Exam context:** UPSC 2024 - pumped-storage hydropower discussed under **long-duration energy storage**
- **Storage need (2035-36):** capacity doubling to 1,121 GW → **174 GW / 888 GWh** storage incl.

=== ROW 61 | Nuclear energy - three-stage programme, types of reactors, PHWR/FBR/AHWR, thorium strategy

## Nuclear fuels — fissile vs fertile isotopes, enrichment, non-fuels | from: S0

| **Isotope** | **Type** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|
| **U-235** | Fissile | Only **0.72%** of natural uranium; fuel of reactors and weapons |
| **U-238** | Fertile | **99.28%** of natural uranium; converted to Pu-239 in reactors |
| **Pu-239** | Fissile | Not natural; made from U-238 by neutron absorption; needs a reprocessing plant |
| **Th-232** | Fertile | Converted to **U-233** in FBR blankets |
| **U-233** | Fissile | Fuel of Stage III and of KAMINI |

# 1. Uranium isotopes and enrichment
- **Natural uranium:** **U-238 99.28%**, **U-235 0.72%**, U-234 0.0057%
- **Enrichment:** raising the share of **U-235** (the fissile isotope) by isotope separation - not U-238
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC-G1 2022 - "Neither I nor II" (both statements false)
- **Enrichment levels:**
  - LWRs = **low-enriched uranium (3-5% U-235)**
  - Weapons-grade = about 90% U-235
- **Plutonium:** not natural - bred from U-238
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC 2007 keyed "U-238" as the better fuel; U-238 is fertile - fissile Pu-239 is the fuel
# 2. Non-fuels and hydrogen isotopes
- **Not nuclear fuels:** moderators, coolants and absorbers - graphite, heavy water, sodium, boron, cadmium, zirconium
- **Deuterium:** 1 proton + 1 neutron (heavy hydrogen, stable)
- **Tritium:**
  - 1 proton + 2 neutrons; made by bombarding **lithium** with neutrons
  - Half-life **12.3 years**; beta emitter; fusion fuel

## Terms → meaning — fission, fusion, bombs and radioactivity | from: S1, S2, S3

| **Point** | **Fission** | **Fusion** |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Heavy nucleus **splits** (U-235 + neutron → barium + krypton + 2-3 neutrons) | Two light nuclei **join** |
| Condition | Neutron hit; critical mass for chain reaction | Extremely high temperature and pressure [GK] |
| Energy | Large; chain reaction | Larger per unit mass [GK] |
| Where / examples | Nuclear power reactors, **atom bomb** | **Sun and stars**, **hydrogen bomb** [GK] |
| Power use | All present-day nuclear electricity | Still at R&D stage |

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Chain reaction** | Released neutrons split more U-235 nuclei → heat + radiation |
| **Critical mass** | Minimum mass of fissile material that sustains a (self-sustaining) chain reaction |
| **Criticality** | Self-sustaining chain reaction |
| **Xenon-135** | A "nuclear poison" absorbing neutrons |
| **Half-life** | Time for half the nuclei to decay |

> Exam angle: "Breaking apart of a nucleus is called fusion" is the **incorrect** statement (APPSC 2019) - that is fission.
# 1. Atomic vs hydrogen bomb
- **Atom vs hydrogen bomb:** **Atom bomb** = uncontrolled **fission**; **hydrogen bomb** = **fusion**, triggered by a fission bomb
- **Bombs used:**
  - **Hiroshima** (Little Boy) = **U-235**
  - **Nagasaki** (Fat Man) = **Pu-239**
# 2. Radioactivity
- **Radioactivity:** discovered by **Henri Becquerel (1896)**; emissions **alpha, beta, gamma**

| **Radiation** | **Nature** | **Penetration** |
|---|---|---|
| Alpha | Helium-4 nucleus (2p + 2n) | Stopped by paper/skin |
| Beta | Fast electrons/positrons | Few mm of aluminium |
| Gamma | Electromagnetic waves [GK] | Highest; needs lead/concrete [GK] |

- **Natural decay series:**
  - Uranium: U-238 → Pb-206
  - Thorium: Th-232 → Pb-208
  - Actinium: U-235 → Pb-207
  - Neptunium series = artificial
- ⚠ **Hiroshima = U-235**; **Nagasaki = Pu-239**

## Reactor components and reactor types | from: S3
# 1. Reactor components

| **Part** | **Function** | **Substances** |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel rods | Pellets of fissile material | - |
| **Moderator** | Slows neutrons so U-235 can fission | Heavy water, light water, graphite [GK] |
| **Coolant** | Carries heat to make steam | Heavy water (PHWR), light water (LWR), **liquid sodium** (FBR) |
| **Control rods** | Absorb neutrons, control the rate | Boron, cadmium [GK] |
| Steam generator / turbine | Heat → steam → electricity | - |

- **Control rods & moderator:** without control rods the chain reaction runs away (meltdown risk); without a moderator a thermal reactor stops
# 2. Reactor types

| **Reactor** | **Fuel** | **Moderator** | **Coolant** |
|---|---|---|---|
| BWR | Enriched uranium | Light water | Light water (boils in core) [GK] |
| PWR / VVER | Low-enriched uranium | Light water | Pressurised light water |
| AHWR (Stage III) | Th-232 + U-233/Pu-239 | Heavy water | Boiling light water |
| SMR | Varies | Varies | Up to 300 MWe per unit |

- **Power reactor types:** Tarapur = BWR; Kudankulam = VVER
- **LWRs:** >85% of world capacity; light water as coolant/moderator; India now prioritises LWRs for fast capacity addition

## Homi Bhabha and the three-stage programme — PHWR → FBR → AHWR | from: S4, S8
# 1. Homi Bhabha
- **Title:** **"Father of Atomic Energy / Indian nuclear programme"** (UKPSC 2007)
- **Three-stage plan:** conceived by **Homi J. Bhabha**, designed in the **1950s** to get round scarce uranium using abundant thorium
- **Aim:** **self-reliance through a closed fuel cycle**
# 2. The three stages

| **Stage** | **Reactor** | **Fuel** | **Output** |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **PHWR** (heavy water / D2O moderator + coolant) | **Natural uranium** | Power / electricity + **Pu-239** from spent fuel |
| II | **FBR** (e.g. PFBR; liquid sodium, no moderator) | **MOX: Pu-239 + U-238** | Power; breeds more Pu-239 than it burns; **Th-232 blanket makes U-233** |
| III | **AHWR** / thorium reactors (thorium breeders) | **Th-232 + U-233** | Power + U-233; large-scale thorium use |

# 3. Advanced Heavy Water Reactor (Stage III)
- **Advanced Heavy Water Reactor:** **300 MW**, **thorium-based** (Th-232 + U-233); conceptual prototype designed at BARC
- **Role:** India's flagship thorium reactor; core of **Stage III**
- **Bridge:** **bridge from Stage I to Stage III** - advances thorium use **without passing through Stage II**
- **Design:** heavy-water moderated, **boiling light-water coolant**
- **Purpose:** large-scale **thorium** use
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "AHWR envisages large-scale use of plutonium-bearing spent fuel" is FALSE (APPSC-G2 2025)
# 4. Emerging shortcut - ANEEL fuel
- **ANEEL fuel:** thorium + **HALEU** (US firm Clean Core Thorium Energy)
  - Finished high-burnup tests at Idaho National Laboratory
  - Works in existing PHWRs; could hasten Stage III (May 2026)
- **BARC's cautions on HALEU-thorium:**
  - Shutdown rods become **~26% less effective**
  - Stabilisation takes **7-10 years**
- ⚠ **Stage I PHWR = natural uranium**; **Stage II FBR = MOX**; **Stage III AHWR = Th-232 + U-233**

## Fast breeder reactors — breeding principle, PFBR Kalpakkam, FBTR | from: S6
# 1. Breeding principle
- **Breeding:** core surrounded by a **U-238 blanket**; **fast neutrons** turn it into **Pu-239** → **more fuel than consumed** (APPSC-G1 2022: statements I and II correct)
- **Moderator & coolant:** **No moderator** (fast neutrons); **liquid sodium coolant**
- **Fuel:** **MOX (U-Pu oxide)** = Pu-239 + U-238, from reprocessed Stage-I spent fuel
- **Fuel use:** FBR ~**10%**+ vs ~**1%** in PHWR; burn-up ~8,000 → ~1,00,000 (~100,000) units
# 2. PFBR, Kalpakkam
- **PFBR, Kalpakkam (TN): 500 MWe (1,250 MWt)**, liquid-**sodium**-cooled, **pool type**
- **Design & build:** **Designed by IGCAR**; built and commissioned by **BHAVINI** (2003, DAE) (APPSC-G1 2024 answer: BHAVINI)

| **Milestone** | **Date** |
|---|---|
| Work began | **2004** |
| Core loading (PM present) | **4 March 2024** |
| First criticality | **April 2026** (TH, 8 Apr 2026) |

- **Achieved criticality (April 2026):** PM announced **first criticality on 6 April 2026** (earlier: criticality "expected March 2026")
  - **AERB** must clear low-power tests before commercial operation
- **Significance:** India = **2nd country after Russia** to operate a commercial-scale FBR
- **Cost & delays (Parliamentary Standing Committee):**
  - Final cost **₹8,181 crore** - more than double the sanction
  - Criticality **16+ years late**
  - Fast-reactor fuel cycle facility due **2029**
- **Next:** pre-project approval for **2 x 500 MWe FBR 1&2** at Kalpakkam after a year's performance review
  - Some notes also list a planned FBR-600 series
# 3. FBTR
- **FBTR (Fast Breeder Test Reactor, IGCAR):** runs on a unique **uranium-plutonium mixed carbide** fuel
- **IGCAR 2005 milestone:** **reprocessing FBTR's mixed carbide fuel** (UPSC 2006)
- ⚠ **PFBR designed by IGCAR**, **built by BHAVINI** - the exam answer is **BHAVINI**

## Fusion research — ITER, Indian tokamaks, ignition | from: S9
# 1. ITER
- **ITER:** International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor, **Cadarache, southern France**
- **Design:** a **tokamak** fusion (not fission) device; **world's largest magnetic confinement** experiment (APPSC 2018)
- **Members:** **EU, India, China, Japan, South Korea, Russia, USA**
- **India's role:** active partner; joined **2005**; supplies the **cryostat**; ~9% in-kind share
- **Aim:** demonstrate fusion as a large-scale, carbon-free power source; clean, abundant fuel
  - Designed for 500 MW output from 50 MW input (about 10 times more power out than in)
  - All four statements correct in APPSC-G1 2022
# 2. Indian fusion research
- **Institute for Plasma Research, Gandhinagar:** tokamaks **Aditya** (first Indian tokamak) and **SST-1** (Steady State Superconducting Tokamak)
# 3. Ignition and critique
- **First fusion ignition / net energy gain (Dec 2022):** **NIF** - **National Ignition Facility, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, USA**; achieved by laser
- **Cost critique:** experts say fusion cost models are too optimistic - "experience rate" ~2% (Apr 2026)
- ⚠ **ITER = magnetic confinement (tokamak), France**; **NIF ignition = laser, USA**
