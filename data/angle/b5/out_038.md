=== ROW 119 | Health financing in India - public expenditure, out-of-pocket spending and insurance

## Public health expenditure — National Health Accounts data | from: S0
| **National Health Accounts (10th edition, 2022-23)** | **2013-14** | **2022-23** |
|---|---|---|
| Govt Health Expenditure (GHE), % of GDP | 1.15% | **1.43%** (1.48% on new GDP base) |
| GHE share of Total Health Expenditure | 28.6% | **43.7%** |
| OOPE share of THE | 64.2% | **43.4%** |
| Social security expenditure share of THE | 6% | 9.9% |
| Per-capita GHE | ₹1,042 | **₹2,786** |

- **Government vs household share (NHA 2022-23, as of Jun 2026):** Government share of THE **43.7%** vs **28.6%** in **2013-14**; household OOPE = most of the rest (**43.4%**)
- **NHA preparation:** By **NHATS under NHSRC** (MoHFW)
  - Uses **System of Health Accounts (SHA) 2011** + NHA Guidelines 2016
  - First published for 2013-14 (29 May 2026)
- **COVID peak:** GHE = **1.84% of GDP (2021-22)**
- **PYQ context:** Centre + States spent about **1.3% of GDP** on health in 2019-20 (Economic Survey 2021-22)
> Exam angle: statement sets on NHA (SHA 2011 base; GHE highest during COVID; OOPE fell, not rose, during COVID - 39.4% of THE).

## Targets and grants — NHP 2017 target, 15th Finance Commission | from: S0, S1
- **NHP 2017 target:** GHE **2.5% of GDP by 2025** - missed (30 Jan 2026)
  - Centre's share to be 40% of public spending; Centre now ~0.29% of GDP
- **15th Finance Commission total grant:** **₹70,051 crore** health grants through local governments (**2021-26**)
- **Components:**
  - Diagnostic infrastructure for sub-centres/PHCs
  - Block public health units
  - Urban health and wellness centres
  - Buildings for building-less SCs/PHCs/CHCs

## Out-of-pocket expenditure — data and issues | from: S2
- **OOPE trend:** **64.2% → 43.4%** of THE (2013-14 to 2022-23)
  - Still about **50% of current health expenditure** (NHA 2022-23)
  - Older notes: 62.6% → ~47-48%; 67% in 2017
- **NSS 80th round (health, Jan-Dec 2025):** (30 Apr 2026)
  - Insurance covers **47.4%** rural and **44.3%** urban households
  - Hospitalisation OOP rose sharply, esp. private hospitals
- **Childbirth OOP:** **₹37,630** private vs ₹2,299 public (over 16x) (4 Sep 2026)
- **Uncovered costs:** Outpatient care, diagnostics, medicines = **over 50%** of OOPE
  - Chronic NCDs keep households paying: diabetes 11.4%, hypertension 35.5% of adults

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **"Missing middle"** | **40 crore+** Indians lack financial protection (parliamentary panel, 4 Sep 2026); NITI Aayog first flagged the term |

## National Health Mission — structure, ASHA, components, funding | from: S1
- **NRHM launched 2005** (APPSC 2023 PYQ): Accessible, affordable, quality care for rural India
- **Sub-missions:** NHM now = **NRHM + NUHM** (NUHM **2013**)
- **ASHA = Accredited Social Health Activist:** Village-level community health worker linking households to the health system
- **Backbone of rural care:** ASHAs + ANMs
- **AP:** **42,060 ASHAs** (2026)

| **NHM component** | **What to remember** |
|---|---|
| Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) | Cash assistance to pregnant women for **institutional delivery**; 100% centrally sponsored; AP: 1,76,966 beneficiaries (Apr-Dec 2024, APSES) |
| Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK) | Free delivery and newborn care at public facilities - "zero expense" (with PMSMA, SUMAN) |
| Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK) | Adolescents **10-19 years**: Adolescent Friendly Health Clinics, Weekly Iron Folic Acid Supplementation, peer educators, menstrual hygiene |
| Anaemia Mukt Bharat | Launched 2018: IFA supplementation, deworming, testing and treatment, fortified foods, behaviour change - the **6x6x6** strategy [GK] |
| Kilkari / Mobile Academy | **72** free weekly audio messages from the 2nd trimester until the child turns one (27 states/UTs); Mobile Academy is an audio course for ASHAs |
| Free Drugs Service Initiative | Free generic medicines at public facilities; DVDMS manages drug supply |

- **Funding pattern:**
  - Centre:State = **60:40**
  - NE and Himalayan states = **90:10**

## Immunisation — Mission Indradhanush, U-WIN, age → vaccine | from: S2
- **Mission Indradhanush (launched Dec 2014):** Catch-up drive under the **Universal Immunisation Programme**
  - Reaches zero-dose and partly immunised children
  - Target = **90% full immunisation**
- **U-WIN:** Digitises the maternal and child vaccination record - digital registry of routine immunisation for pregnant women and children [GK]

| **Age** | **Vaccines under UIP** [GK] |
|---|---|
| Birth | BCG, OPV-0, Hepatitis B birth dose |
| 6, 10, 14 weeks | OPV 1-3, Pentavalent 1-3, Rotavirus; fIPV; **PCV** (6 and 14 weeks) |
| **9-12 months** | **MR-1** (correct PYQ pair), JE-1 (endemic districts), PCV booster, Vitamin A |
| 16-24 months | MR-2, DPT booster-1, OPV booster, JE-2 |
| 5-6 years | DPT booster-2 |
| 10 and 16 years | Td (tetanus and adult diphtheria) |

- **PYQ wrong pairs:**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Td is NOT at 5-6 years
  - ⚠ **Trap:** OPV-1 is NOT at 16-24 months
  - ⚠ **Trap:** DPT booster-2 is NOT at 6 weeks

## Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission — components, ABHA data, platforms → purpose | from: S4
- **ABDM launched:** **September 2021**

- **Four registries:**

## HIV/AIDS — 95-95-95 targets and burden data | from: S4
- **95-95-95 (UNAIDS, adopted by India):**
  - 95% of people with HIV know their status
  - 95% of them on treatment
  - 95% of those treated have suppressed viral load
- **AP:** Minister called the national target **95-95-99** (99% viral suppression)
  - AP stands at **88% / 87% / 96.2%**; target Dec 2026
  - **Suraksha Dashboard** launched (13 Aug 2026)
- **India burden:** **over 2.5 million** living with HIV
  - Highest burden: **Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka**
  - Goal: end AIDS as a public health threat by **2030**
- **Dominant strain in India:** **HIV-1 subtype C**
