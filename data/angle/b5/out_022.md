## National Green Tribunal — establishment, procedure, benches | from: S4, S5
# 1. Establishment and nature
- **Basis:** Set up in **2010** under **NGT Act, 2010**; **statutory** (not constitutional) body
- **Dates:** Act passed **2 June 2010**; Tribunal set up **18 Oct 2010** (**18 October 2010**); started functioning **July 2011**
- **Purpose:** **Effective and expeditious disposal** of cases on environmental protection and conservation of forests and natural resources; enforcing environmental legal rights; **relief and compensation** for damage
  - i.e., to **handle environmental protection and pollution-control cases** (APPSC-G2 2025 answer)
  - Gives effect to right to healthy environment under **Art. 21**
# 2. Procedure and composition
- **Procedure:** Not bound by **CPC 1908**; guided by **principles of natural justice**; powers of a civil court
- **Disposal time:** Within **6 months**
- **Appeal:** To **Supreme Court** within **90 days**

| **Bench type** | **Location (IYB 2026)** |
|---|---|
| **Principal Bench** | **New Delhi** |
| Zonal Benches | **Pune, Kolkata, Bhopal, Chennai** |
| Circuit Benches | **Shimla, Shillong, Jodhpur** |

- **Composition:** **Chairperson** + **Judicial and Expert Members** (each 10 to 20)
  - Chairperson: retired SC judge; Act allows a judge of the SC or Chief Justice of a High Court
  - Current: **Justice Prakash Shrivastava** (8 Sep 2026)
- **NGT vs CPCB:** NGT **adjudicates** disputes and awards compensation; CPCB = **regulator** that sets standards, monitors, advises the Centre
- **Cases in 2026:** Dhanauri wetland, Ganga pollution, Saparnika river (Jun 2026)
- ⚠ Act **2 June 2010** → set up **18 Oct 2010** → functioning **July 2011**; disposal **6 months**; appeal **90 days**

## CITES, 1973 — provisions and appendices | from: S2
# 1. Basics
- **Full name:** **Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora**
- **Other name:** **Washington Convention** (APPSC G2 2025 key)
- **Adoption:** **3 March 1973, Washington D.C.**; in force **1 July 1975**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes give "1963" - wrong; **1973** is correct
- **Scope:** Regulates international trade in wild fauna and flora; about **35,000 species** → bans commercial trade for ~3%, regulates the other 97%
- **Membership:** **184 Parties**
- **Secretariat:** **Geneva**, administered by **UNEP** (**UNEP in Geneva**)
- **World Wildlife Day = 3 March:** Day CITES was signed (proclaimed by UNGA in 2013)
# 2. Appendices I, II, III - criteria and trade
| **Appendix** | **Listing criterion** | **Trade** |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Threatened with extinction (tiger, snow leopard, rhino) | Commercial trade **prohibited**[GK]; only exceptional non-commercial (e.g., research) with import and export permits |
| **II** | Not yet threatened [GK]; not necessarily threatened, but could be without control (corals, many sharks and rays) | Trade **controlled by permits**[GK]; allowed with **export permit** |
| **III** | Listed by one Party seeking others' help to control trade [GK]; protected in at least one country that asks others for help (walrus - Canada) | Allowed with permits/certificates of origin |

- **Moving species:** Between Appendices only by **COP vote**

## Cartagena and Nagoya Protocols — provisions | from: S9, S10
# 1. Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety
- **Subject:** **Biosafety** - regulates **safe transfer, handling and use of Living Modified Organisms (LMOs)** from modern biotechnology [GK] (PYQ 2018 key)
- **Timeline:** Adopted **2000**; in force **2003** [GK]
- **Parties:** **173 parties** incl. India
- **Name:** After **Cartagena, Colombia**
- **Advance Informed Agreement (AIA):**
  - Importing country is informed + consents before the first transboundary movement of LMOs
  - Shipments carry documentation identifying the LMO
- **Biosafety Clearing-House (BCH):** Online platform for sharing LMO information and decisions
- **India:**
  - Nodal ministry: **MoEF&CC**
  - GM approvals: **Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)**
# 2. Nagoya Protocol on ABS - subject and nature
- **Full name:** Nagoya Protocol on **Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization (ABS)**
- **Nature:** **Legally binding** supplementary agreement to the **CBD**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** it is NOT voluntary (PYQ)
- **Utilisation of genetic resources:** = research and development on their genetic and/or **biochemical** composition (APPSC 2025 key)
- **Coverage:** Plant, animal and microbial genetic resources + associated traditional knowledge, **excluding human genetic material**
- **Pillars:**
  - Prior informed consent
  - **Mutually Agreed Terms** for monetary/non-monetary benefits
  - Compliance against illegal acquisition
# 3. Nagoya - timeline
- **Adopted:** **COP10, Nagoya, Japan (2010)**
- **In force:** **2014** (LENS, Mar 2026; APPCA)
- **Parties:** **137 parties** incl. India
- **India ratified:** **2012**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes say 2014 - that is the entry-into-force year
# 4. Nagoya - India's law and report
- **Implementation:** **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** (amended 2023) + **BD Rules 2024**
- **NBA regulations:** **ABS/BD Regulations in April 2025**, incl. rules on **Digital Sequence Information**
- **1st National Report on Nagoya:** **3,556** Certificates of Compliance (**>60%** of global total)
- ⚠ Cartagena = **biosafety/LMOs** (2000 → 2003, 173 parties); Nagoya = **ABS** (2010 → 2014, 137 parties)

=== ROW 80 | Sustainable development - meaning, nature, scope and components

## Sustainable development — scope and pillars | from: S0, S1
# 1. Scope - includes and excludes
- **Includes:** Meeting present needs (development not rejected) + equity within and across generations + inclusion ("leave no one behind")
- **Excludes:** Growth that runs down natural capital; treating environment and development as opposites
- **National Environment Policy 2006:** Conservation and livelihood security are **linked, not opposed**
- **Assertion-reason logic:** Present generation **may use** resources, but not in a way that **compromises** future generations' ability to meet their needs
# 2. Three pillars
- **Three interlinked pillars:**
  - **Economic development**
  - **Social equity**
  - **Environmental protection**
  - Alternate phrasing (2024-25): economic growth, social inclusion, environmental protection
- **Idea:** Balance all three, not maximise any one
- **Triple bottom line:** **People, Planet, Profit** = business version of the three pillars
- ⚠ **Trap (odd-one-out):** Political, military or cultural dimensions are **not** among the three classic pillars

## Principles → meaning | from: S2
| **Principle** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Intergenerational equity** | The present must not deplete resources at the cost of future generations |
| **Precautionary principle** | Lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone cost-effective preventive measures |
| **Polluter pays** | The polluter bears the cost of preventing and controlling pollution |
| **CBDR** | Shared but differentiated duties; developed nations carry more |
| **Carrying capacity** | Human use must stay within what ecosystems can support (the finite carrying capacity of Earth) |

- **Endorsement:** Principles endorsed at **Rio 1992**; underpin international environmental law
- ⚠ **Trap (odd-one-out as a "goal"):** **Maximising economic growth at any cost**
