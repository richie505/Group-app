=== ROW 86 | Loss of biodiversity - causes, invasive alien species, IUCN Red List categories

## Extinction — definitions, types and traits | from: S0
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Extinction** | Death of the last individual of a species, globally or locally |
| **Co-extinction** | Loss of one species drags down dependants, e.g. parasites of an extinct host fish; a plant + its obligate pollinator |
| Background extinction | Slow natural loss at a low steady rate [GK] |
| Mass extinction | Large share of species lost in a short geological time; **five** in Earth history [GK] |
| Sixth extinction | Present human-driven episode; rates **tens to hundreds of times** the background rate (IPBES); **>100 times** in last 300 years (APP) |
- **Sixth mass extinction:** Present, human-driven wave of extinctions
  - Fifth = end-Cretaceous event, about 66 million years ago → wiped out dinosaurs
- **High-risk traits:** Large body size, small population, low reproductive rate, narrow/specialised niche, endemism
- ⚠ IPBES = **tens to hundreds of times**; APP = **>100 times** in last 300 years

## Causes of biodiversity loss — frameworks and PYQ answers | from: S1
# Frameworks
| **Framework** | **Components** |
|---|---|
| **Evil Quartet (NCERT-style)** | **habitat loss and fragmentation**; **over-exploitation**; **invasive alien species** (alien species invasion); **co-extinctions** |
| **HIPPO (popularised by E.O. Wilson)** | **H**abitat destruction / loss; **I**nvasive species; **P**ollution; human **P**opulation; **O**ver-harvesting / Overexploitation |
- **Newer lists:** add **climate change** + **disease**
- ⚠ Evil Quartet has **co-extinctions**; HIPPO has **pollution** + **population**
# Most important cause
- **Primary cause:** **The most important cause is habitat loss.** = **Habitat destruction is the primary and major cause** of biodiversity loss (IPBES)
- **Largest drivers (global + India):** Urbanisation, infrastructure, mining, agricultural expansion
- **In India:** Habitat loss (urbanisation, infrastructure, mining, farming) → then climate change, invasives, pollution, overexploitation
# PYQ facts
- **Causes of biodiversity loss:** **modern agriculture**
- **Causes of extinction:** **loss of habitat + loss of genetic variation**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** NOT "loss of appetite"
- **Threats:** Global warming, fragmentation, alien invasion
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **promotion of vegetarianism is not** a threat

## Drivers → effects: deforestation, fragmentation, climate change and consequences | from: S1, S2, S5
# Deforestation
- **Effect:** Destroys natural habitats → drives species to extinction (APPSC 2025; PYQ 2025 key)
- **Primary driver:** **agricultural expansion and urban development** (PYQ 2022 key)
- **Other drivers:** Mines, dams, harbours, industries, settlements
- **Rainforest loss:** Tropical rainforest cover fell from **14% to 6%** of land surface
- **Amazon ("lungs of the planet"):** Cleared for soybean + cattle pasture
# Fragmentation and edge effects
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Fragmentation** | Large habitats broken into small patches → hits migratory animals + wide-ranging / large-territory mammals and birds hardest |
| **Edge effect** | Patch boundaries get more light, wind, heat, predators, weeds → interior species decline as fragments shrink |
| Warming | **coral bleaching**, ocean acidification, shifts in ecosystem boundaries, disrupted migration |
| Rising temperatures | Shift species ranges + raise extinction risk in hotspots (5 Jun 2026) |
| Warming Arctic | Opening to alien plants (12 Feb 2026) |

| **Disturbs ecological balance** | **Restores ecological balance** |
|---|---|
| Clear-felling, shifting cultivation, mining, quarrying | Afforestation, eco-restoration of degraded land |
| Draining wetlands, mangrove-to-shrimp-farm conversion | Protected areas, wildlife corridors, eco-bridges |
| Urban sprawl on coasts and wetlands | Removal of invasives with native restocking |

# Consequences of biodiversity loss
- **Ecological impact:** Disrupts species interactions, nutrient cycling + **ecosystem services** (pollination, water, soil)
  - Once lost, biodiversity cannot be recreated
- **Social impact:** **Food security** falls for resource-dependent communities; cultural + ethical loss
- **Zoonotic spillover:** Emerging diseases via habitat disruption + wildlife trade
  - Answer = **One Health** approach

## Invasive alien species — definition and scale | from: S3
- **Definition:** Non-native species that spreads without control + causes measurable harm = **"arrive, survive and thrive"**
- **Three tests:** Non-native origin, uncontrolled spread, measurable harm
- **Scale:**
  - About **1 in 10** Red List species threatened by invasives
  - Contributed to about **40%** of known animal extinctions since the 17th century
  - India: estimated **$127.3 billion** (**US$127.3 billion**) damage over 60 years (2026; Jun 2026)

## IUCN Red List categories → full forms and criteria | from: S8
- **Order of extinction risk:** **EX (Extinct)** → **EW (Extinct in the Wild)** → **CR (Critically Endangered)** → **EN (Endangered)** → **VU (Vulnerable)** → **NT (Near Threatened)** → **LC (Least Concern)**
  - Also: **DD (Data Deficient)** + **NE (Not Evaluated)**
- **"Threatened" means CR + EN + VU.**
- **CR = Critically Endangered** (APPSC-G2 2025, asked twice)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Not "critically extinct" (APPSC 2023 and 2025)

| **Code** | **Full form** | **Group** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|---|---|
| EX | Extinct | - | No reasonable doubt that the last individual has died, after exhaustive surveys in known/expected habitat |
| EW | Extinct in the Wild | - | Survives only in captivity or cultivation, outside its natural range |
| CR | Critically Endangered | Threatened | Extremely high risk of extinction in the wild |
| EN | Endangered | Threatened | Very high risk of extinction in the wild |
| VU | Vulnerable | Threatened | High risk of extinction in the wild |
| NT | Near Threatened | Lower risk | Close to qualifying as threatened |
| LC | Least Concern | Lower risk | Widespread and abundant |
| DD | Data Deficient | Not enough data | - |
| NE | Not Evaluated | Not assessed | - |

# Criteria (A-E)
| **Criterion** | **Basis** |
|---|---|
| A | **Declining population** |
| B | **Geographic range** (size, fragmentation) |
| C | **Small population size** |
| D | **Very small population or restricted distribution** |
| E | **Quantitative analysis** of extinction risk (Population Viability Analysis) |

## Red List / Red Data Book — name, coverage, colour pages, latest figures | from: S9
# Name and coverage
- **Name:** IUCN's list of endangered species = **Red List** (APPSC 2025); endangered species listed in the **red** data book
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Brown, White and Black lists are distractors
  - Also called Red Data List / Red Book; known as **"Barometer of Life"**
- **Coverage:** Lists **threatened plant and animal species**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Not endemic species of hotspots, not protected sites (UPSC)
- **Purpose:** Measure **extinction risk** → guide conservation priorities and funding
- **Started:** **1964**
- **IUCN Green Status (2021):** Measures a species' **recovery** vs Red List measures **risk**
# Colour-coded pages
- **pink pages** = critically endangered species
- **green pages** = species once endangered but now recovered

| **Page colour** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Black | Extinct |
| Red | Endangered |
| Amber | Vulnerable |
| White | Rare |
| Green | Formerly threatened, now out of danger |
| Grey | Status insufficiently known |

# Latest figures and India
- **Latest version 2026-1 (July 2026):**
  - **175,909 species** assessed
  - **49,505 (~28%) threatened**
  - Earlier: 44,000+ threatened in the 2024 list
- **India's Red List Roadmap:** Assess **70,000 species by 2027**, led by the **NBA** (2026)
- **Indian fauna:** **7,076 species** (Indian animal species) in IUCN Red List categories
