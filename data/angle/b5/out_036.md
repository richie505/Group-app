=== ROW 111 | Genetic engineering - recombinant DNA, GM crops (Bt cotton, Bt brinjal, GM mustard), gene editing/CRISPR

## Genetic engineering — rDNA steps, tools and FIRSTs | from: S0, S5, S6
| **Step** | **Action** |
|---|---|
| 1 | Isolation of DNA |
| 2 | Cutting DNA at specific sites by **restriction endonucleases** |
| 3 | Isolation and amplification (PCR) of desired fragment |
| 4 | Formation of rDNA (joined by **DNA ligase**) |
| 5 | Insertion into **host cell** (commonly E. coli [GK]) |
| 6 | Extracting the foreign gene product |
| 7 | **Downstream processing** |

- **Vectors:** **Plasmids** (carry antibiotic-resistance genes for selection), BACs, YACs
- **Plants:** **Agrobacterium tumefaciens** ("nature's genetic engineer")
  - Gene put in disarmed **Ti plasmid**; **T-DNA** carries it into the plant nucleus

| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| **FIRST GMO** | **1973** |
| **FIRST human hormone by rDNA** | **Insulin** (UKPSC 2024); human insulin marketed 1982 (Humulin) |
| First commercially sold GM food | **Flavr Savr tomato** - Calgene, USA, **1994**; delayed ripening / longer shelf life |
| First transgenic cow | **Rosie (1997)** - milk with human alpha-lactalbumin |

## Bt technology → gene source, mode of action, versions | from: S1
- **Gene source:** Soil bacterium **Bacillus thuringiensis**
  - During sporulation forms crystal proteins (**delta-endotoxins**) toxic to insects at low doses
- **Mode of action:** Inactive protoxin → activated in the **alkaline gut** of larva → binds midgut cells → creates pores → insect dies

| **Version** | **Gene** [GK] |
|---|---|
| Bollgard I (2002) | **Cry1Ac** |
| Bollgard II (2006) | **Cry1Ac + Cry2Ab** |

- **Target trait:** **Insect resistance**
- **Soil persistence:** Feared, but experiments show Bt proteins **degrade in soil**

## Bt cotton and stress-tolerant crops → outcomes and problems | from: S3, S4
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Yield | **190 kg/ha (2002) → 480 kg/ha (2023)** |
| Pesticide use | Cut **40-50%** |
| Region | Benefits varied by region |

- **Problems:**
  - **Pest resistance:** **Pink bollworm** developed resistance to (overcame) Bt toxins; resistance build-up = listed environmental risk
  - Non-adoption of refuge/recommended practices
  - Rise of sucking/secondary pests (whitefly, mealybug)
  - High input costs + seed dependency
- **Claimed benefits - less pesticide:** Bt cotton cut (see above); herbicide-tolerant and **drought-tolerant** crops
- **Stress tolerance without transgenes:** Genome-edited **Pusa Rice DST1** - drought + salinity; **10-30%** higher yield under stress (1 Sep 2026)
- **Mutation breeding (non-GMO):** **71 Trombay varieties** with climate resilience

## Other GM foods and transgenic animals → trait and gene | from: S5, S6
| **Organism** | **Trait / gene** |
|---|---|
| **Golden Rice** | Produces **beta-carotene** (precursor of **Vitamin A**) → targets Vitamin A deficiency/blindness (APPSC 2025); genes: **phytoene synthase from daffodil** (maize in Golden Rice 2) + **crtI from the bacterium Erwinia uredovora** |
| **Edible vaccines** | Transgenic potato/banana expressing cholera or hepatitis B antigens |
| **GM mosquitoes** | Engineered to suppress populations or block malaria/dengue/Zika transmission; ecological + ethical concerns |
| **GM fish** | Fast-growing transgenic salmon (AquAdvantage) - risk of escapees out-competing wild stock |

- **Biofortification vs GM:** **Biofortification** raises nutrients, usually by **conventional breeding**; GM inserts foreign genes
  - **Examples:** **Dhanshakti** iron-rich pearl millet (ICRISAT); high-lysine maize
  - **ICAR releases:** **524 varieties** in the year, incl. **34 biofortified** + **79 stress-tolerant**
- **Gene drives:** "Super-Mendelian" inheritance spreads a trait through a wild population (population collapse or malaria-resistant mosquitoes); CRISPR-Cas9 system copies itself onto the partner chromosome
  - **Over 90%** of offspring inherit it (normal 50%)
  - Two types: **population suppression** (sterile females) vs **population modification** (parasite cannot develop)
  - Unlike pesticides, **no simple recall** (14 Jul 2026)
  - Tanzania-led **"Transmission Zero"** project (20 Apr 2026)

## Gene editing — CRISPR discoverers, SDN categories, new tools | from: S7
- **Origin:** Natural **bacterial/archaeal defence** against viruses
- **Components:** **Cas9 = molecular scissors**; **guide RNA = navigator**
- **Discoverers:** **Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier** demonstrated it in **2012** → **Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2020**
- **Advantage:** Simpler, cheaper, more accurate than **ZFNs and TALENs**; can target many genes at once

| **Category** | **Nature** | **Status in India** |
|---|---|---|
| SDN-1 | Small change, no template, no foreign DNA | **Exempt** from GM rules (2022 guidelines) |
| SDN-2 | Small change using a template, no foreign DNA | **Exempt** (2022) |
| SDN-3 | Inserts foreign DNA | Regulated as **GM**[GK] |

- **Personalised medicine:** Built on genetic make-up = **CRISPR** (UKPSC 2025)

| **Tool / body** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| **TnpB (ICAR)** | Indigenous, **smaller alternative to CRISPR-Cas9**, easier delivery |
| **CoE for CRISPR Innovation and Translation (CoE-CIT)** | To be set up in **Bengaluru** |
| **Base editing** | Converts one DNA base into another **without double-strand breaks** |
| **ContactSeek** | AI tool using **AlphaFold3** to cut off-target edits (27 Jul 2026) |

## Environmental risks → effects | from: S9
- **Gene flow:** Transgenes move by pollen (**cross-pollination**) into non-GM crops and **wild relatives** = primary environmental concern with GM crops (PYQ)

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Gene flow | Invasive **"superweeds"** (e.g. herbicide-tolerance moving into weedy relatives) |
| Gene flow | Raised **weediness**; altered native gene pools |
| Gene flow | Can push endangered varieties of the same genus towards extinction |
| Non-target harm | Bt toxins or engineered traits may harm **beneficial insects (pollinators, natural enemies)**, **soil nematodes/microbes** or aquatic organisms |

- **Highest risk:** Crop's **centre of origin/diversity** (many wild relatives)
  - India = centre of diversity of **brinjal** → key argument against Bt brinjal

## Health and biosafety risks → cases | from: S10

| **Horizontal gene transfer** | Genes moving between unrelated organisms; antibiotic-resistance genes can pass from soil/environment bacteria to human pathogens |
| **Antibiotic-resistance marker genes** | Used in GM construction → fears of spreading resistance ("antibiotic resistance" listed among health risks) |

## Crop diversity, GURT and safeguards — issues and Acts | from: S11, S12
- **Genetic homogenisation:** Uniform engineered varieties over large areas → less agro-biodiversity, more vulnerability to new pathogens and climate shocks
- **Monoculture:** Dominance of a few patented seed firms displaces **landraces** and farmer varieties
- **PYQ:** **Genetic modification** = technology flagged for its impact on **biodiversity**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** NOT solar panels, wind turbines or rainwater harvesting
- **Safeguards:**
  - **National Genebank** - 4,70,513 accessions (2026)
  - PPV&FR Act protects farmers' varieties, landraces, wild relatives
  - National Gene Fund for benefit sharing

| **Type** | **Mechanism** | **Status** |
|---|---|---|
| **V-GURT (Terminator)** | Plant produces **sterile seeds** - next generation cannot form viable seed | Not commercialised anywhere; **banned in India under the PPV&FR Act (2001)** (CDI) |
| **T-GURT (Traitor)** | Desired trait switched on/off by a **proprietary chemical** | Research stage (CDI) |

- **Farmer concern (PYQ):** Seeds give plants **incapable of forming viable seeds**
  - Ends **seed saving/replanting** → forces annual purchase
  - Seed sovereignty + corporate dependency

=== ROW 112 | Regulation and ethics of biotechnology - GEAC, biosafety, bioethics, biopiracy, IPR and the ethical/social/legal debate

## GEAC — ministry, Act, chair, role | from: S0
- **Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee:** **Apex/statutory body** that **regulates GMOs in India** (APPSC-G2 2023)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** NOT FSSAI, CPCB or ICAR (PYQ)
- **Legal basis:** Constituted in **MoEFCC** under the **Rules, 1989** made under the **Environment (Protection) Act 1986**
- **Role:**
  - Approves **field trials and commercial release** of GM crops
  - Approves **large-scale use, environmental release and commercial cultivation** of GM organisms
  - Approves **large-scale use of hazardous microorganisms** and recombinants
- **Chair:** **Special/Additional Secretary, MoEFCC**; co-chair from **DBT**
- **Renaming:** **Genetic Engineering Approval Committee** → "**Appraisal**" Committee in **2010**

## Committees under Rules 1989 → level and role; field-trial process | from: S1, S2
| **Committee** | **Level / ministry** | **Role** |
|---|---|---|
| RDAC | DBT | **Advisory**; reviews developments, recommends safety regulations |
| **IBSC** | Each institution; first tier | Institutional biosafety; approves lab-level work; now clears genome-edited crops (Dec 2025) |
| **RCGM** | **DBT** (Ministry of S&T) | Monitors safety of ongoing research; advises / recommends to GEAC |
| **GEAC** | **MoEFCC** | Apex; approves environmental release and commercial use |
| **SBCC** | State | Monitoring; powers to inspect, investigate, punish violations (**punitive action**) |
| **DLC** | District | Monitoring at field level |

- **Six-tier system:** IBSC → RCGM → GEAC → SBCC → DLC → expert committees; plus **rDNA Guidelines 2017**
- **Wider framework:** **Biological Diversity Act 2002** + **National Biosafety Framework**
- **Field-trial process:** Committees' safety assessments → **confined open-plot trials** at agricultural universities/ICAR plots (several seasons, locations) → commercial clearance only if superior to non-GM varieties without ecological harm
- **Trial levels:** **Biosafety Research Level (BRL-I, BRL-II)** trials
- **State-level control:** Field trials need the **State's NOC** - several States refuse them

## GM debate — concerns and reasons for resistance | from: S6
- **Primary environmental concern:** **Cross-pollination/gene flow to non-GM crops** (APPSC-G2 2023)
- **Biodiversity:** Genetic modification = technology raising **biodiversity concerns** (APPSC-GS 2023)
- **Other risks:**
  - Environment: **insect resistance build-up**, harm to **non-target organisms**, weediness in wild relatives, genetic homogenisation
  - Health: toxicity, **allergenicity**, antibiotic resistance
- **Reasons for resistance:**
  - Safety, environmental release, **monoculture**, loss of diversity
  - Control by big seed companies holding patents
  - Herbicide-tolerance fears (DMH-11 carries the bar gene)
- **Delays:** Overlapping mandates (DBT, GEAC, CDSCO, FSSAI, NBA) → **7-10 years** for GM approval vs 3-5 globally
  - **No biosafety liability law**

## IPR in biotech — Patents Act sections, PPVFR Act, cases, GURT | from: S11, S12
- **Section 3(j):** Plants, animals, seeds and **essentially biological processes** are **not patentable**; **microbial processes** can be
  - UPSC 2019: biological process to create a seed **cannot** be patented; plant varieties **not eligible** for patents (only statement 3 correct)
  - IPAB existed then; abolished **2021**
- **Section 3(d):** No patent for a new form of a known substance unless it shows **enhanced efficacy** → blocks evergreening

| **Case / law** | **Point** |
|---|---|
| **Novartis v Union of India (2013)**[GK] | SC upheld rejection of **Glivec (imatinib)** patent under **Sec. 3(d)** |
| **PPVFR Act 2001** | Protects plant varieties and **farmers' rights** (UPPSC 2021) |
| **PepsiCo potato case (2019)**[GK] | PepsiCo sued Gujarat farmers over its **FL-2027 (FC5)** variety; suits withdrawn; PPVFR Authority revoked its registration (2021) |

- **Farmers' rights:** May **save, use, sow, resow, exchange, share, sell** farm produce incl. seed - but **not branded seed**
- **GURT (Genetic Use Restriction Technology):** **Terminator seeds** yield **sterile seeds** → farmers must buy fresh seed each season
  - **Legal bar:** PPVFR Act bars registration of varieties carrying GURT
  - **Farmers' objections:** **Seed dependency** on companies, costs, loss of seed-saving

## Concerns and regulation — nanotoxicity, types, guidelines | from: S9

  - Unclear **food-chain** entry
- **Types of nanoparticles:**
  - **Natural:** volcanic ash, sea spray, smoke
  - **Engineered:** made deliberately (nano-silver, CNTs, TiO2)
