=== ROW 92 | Climate change - greenhouse effect, greenhouse gases, radiative forcing, evidence and observed impacts

## Greenhouse effect → mechanism, natural vs enhanced | from: S0
# 1. Mechanism
- **Incoming:** Atmosphere **transparent to incoming solar** (**short-wave solar radiation**)
- **Outgoing:** **Opaque to outgoing terrestrial (long-wave infrared) radiation**
- **Re-emission:** Surface re-emits **infrared at 8,000-25,000 nm**
  - CO2 + water vapour absorb strongly at **14,000-25,000 nm**
  - GHGs re-radiate in all directions, partly back to the surface
- **Global warming (APPSC GS 2021):** Heat from sunlight re-radiated by the earth absorbed by CO2 and other gases
# 2. Natural vs enhanced effect

| **Natural greenhouse effect** | **Enhanced (anthropogenic) greenhouse effect** |
|---|---|
| Occurs without human intervention (no human role); keeps Earth habitable - without it Earth would be frozen | Caused by human activities that raise GHG concentrations |
| GHG levels stable for thousands of years (millennia) | Fossil fuels, deforestation, industry, agriculture, waste |
| Essential for life | Leads to global warming and climate change |

- **Without greenhouse effect:** Earth's mean surface temperature about **-18°C** (-18 C) instead of about **+15°C** (~15 C)
- **APPSC-GS 2025:**
  - Greenhouse effect = rise in Earth's temperature due to concentration of gases
  - **Primary cause of global warming** = **increase in GHGs** (APPSC 2025 key)
- ⚠ Transparent to **short-wave** in; opaque to **long-wave infrared** out

## Greenhouse gases → contribution, GWP, lifetime, source | from: S1
# 1. Largest contributor and contribution order
- **Enhanced greenhouse effect:** **CO2** contributes most (APPSC 2025 key)
- **Main anthropogenic driver:** **CO2**
  - ~0.04% of air (423.9 ppm, 2024); older tables say 0.036% / 356 ppm
- **Natural contributor:** **Water vapour** is the biggest, followed by CO2, CH4, O3 and CFCs
  - **Water vapour is the most important GHG**; humans raise it only **indirectly**, by warming the air (APPSC GS 2023)
- **Share of enhanced effect (textbook):** **CO2 60% > CH4 20% > CFCs 15% > N2O 5%** (CO2 **~60%**)
# 2. Gas → GWP, lifetime, source
- **Global Warming Potential (GWP):** Heat a gas absorbs over a period (usually **100 years**) relative to the same mass of CO2
- **Older values:** CH4 21, N2O 310 (1990s / old IPCC figures) - superseded

| **Gas** | **Natural / man-made** | **GWP (100 yr)** | **Atmospheric lifetime** | **Main anthropogenic source** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CO2 | - | **1** (reference) | 100-1,000 years | fossil fuels, deforestation, cement |
| Methane (CH4, "marsh gas") | - | **28-36** | **~12 years** | rice paddies/paddy fields, livestock, wetlands, landfills, fossil fuels |
| Nitrous oxide (N2O) | Both | **265-298** | **~114 years** | fertilisers, biomass and coal burning, industry, combustion |
| CFCs / HFCs | - | 1,000-23,000 | 50-500 years | coolants/refrigeration, foams, aerosol sprays |
| SF6 | Synthetic | **23,900** (older table; ~23,900) | long-lived | dielectric fluid in switchgear |
| Water vapour (H2O) | Natural | variable | ~9 days | natural |

- **Perfluoromethane:** Comes from **aluminium production**
# 3. Distractors and exam traps
- **Not GHGs:** **N2, O2 and Ar are not greenhouse gases** (APPSC 2025) - **nitrogen (78.08%), oxygen (20.95%), argon (0.93%)**; the bulk of air does not absorb IR
- **Carbon monoxide:** Only an indirect GHG
- ⚠ **Trap:** Deforestation **increases** CO2 by destroying sinks
> Exam angle: "Deforestation is decreasing the amount of CO2" is the **incorrect** statement, because deforestation raises CO2 (APPSC 2025; APPSC-G2 2025).
- ⚠ Natural effect: **water vapour** biggest; enhanced effect: **CO2** biggest

## Climate science terms → meaning (forcing, sensitivity, feedbacks, tipping, records) | from: S2, S5
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Radiative forcing** | change in Earth's energy balance (W/m²) caused by a factor such as a GHG |
| **Climate sensitivity** | Warming after a **doubling of CO2** over pre-industrial levels |
| **Albedo feedback** | Melting ice lowers **albedo** → more heat absorbed |
| **Water vapour feedback** | Warmer air holds more **water vapour** → traps more heat |
| **Methane hydrates** | Methane locked in ice lattices in permafrost + seabed sediments; warming releases them = positive feedback |
| **Tipping points** | Thresholds where small changes trigger **self-perpetuating shifts that cannot be reversed** on human timescales; non-linear |
| **Keeling curve** | Continuous CO2 record at **Mauna Loa, Hawaii**, since **1958**, by Charles David Keeling |
| **Ice cores** | Trapped air bubbles give past CO2 and temperature |

- **Tipping example - Amazon:** Beyond about **2°C**, parts of the **Amazon** could turn into savanna → carbon source (UNSG Scientific Advisory Board Brief #12)
- ⚠ **Keeling curve** = **Mauna Loa** (**1958**); **climate sensitivity** = doubling of CO2

=== ROW 93 | Ocean acidification - causes, effects on marine ecosystems and coral reefs

## Ocean acidification → cause, chemistry and terms | from: S0
# 1. Cause and names
- **Primary cause:** **Dissolution of atmospheric CO2** in seawater (APPSC-GS 2024)
  - Called "**evil twin of global warming**", "the other CO2 problem"
- **Uptake:** Oceans absorb about **one-third** of anthropogenic CO2 (also cited 30-40%)
# 2. Chemistry
- CO2 + H2O → **carbonic acid** → releases H+ + bicarbonate
- Extra H+ binds carbonate ions → less carbonate for shells
# 3. Data - pH change
- **pH change:** Surface pH **8.2 (pre-industrial) → ~8.1** today = roughly **30% rise in acidity**; ocean still alkaline
# 4. Worst areas
- **Worst areas:** **Cold/temperate and Arctic waters** (higher CO2 solubility; melting sea ice exposes water)
  - Eutrophic bloom collapse also raises CO2 → worsens acidification
- ⚠ pH **8.2 → ~8.1** yet acidity up ~**30%**; ocean is still **alkaline**

## Organisms and ocean stressors → effect | from: S1, S2
# 1. Organism → effect of acidification

| **Organism** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Corals | Slower skeleton growth, more bleaching and erosion |
| Shellfish (oysters, clams, molluscs) | Weaker shells, poorer larval survival |
| Pteropods ("sea butterflies") | Shell dissolution; key food for fish [GK for common name] |
| Calcareous phytoplankton | Growth and survival reduced (UPSC 2012) |
| Fisheries | Food-web disruption hits fish stocks and livelihoods |

# 2. Related stressors → meaning and effect

| **Term** | **Meaning / effect** |
|---|---|
| **Marine heatwave (MHW)** | SST 3-4°C above average for at least **5 days**; number doubled since 1982 |
| **Deoxygenation** | Nutrient runoff → algal blooms → decay consumes oxygen → hypoxic **dead zones** |

- **MHW and cyclones:** Cyclones over MHWs: up to **20%** stronger winds + **12%** more rain
- **Coral impact:** Heatwaves now the **strongest global driver of coral-cover decline** (GCRMN 2026)
- ⚠ Pteropods = "**sea butterflies**"

## Heat waves, wet-bulb temperature and health → terms and hotspots | from: S1, S2
# 1. Terms

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|

- **Urban waterlogging (e.g. Mumbai):** Spreads **leptospirosis, typhoid and hepatitis A**
- ⚠ Heat wave = **≥45°C**; survivability = wet-bulb **~35°C**; first Heat Action Plan = **Ahmedabad** (**2013**)

## Tipping points and 1.5 C vs 2 C → elements and projections | from: S11
# 1. Tipping points
- **Tipping point:** Threshold where small changes trigger large, **self-perpetuating, irreversible** shifts; behaviour **non-linear**
- **UNSG Scientific Advisory Board Brief #12 (London Climate Action Week, Jun 2026):**
  - Warming ~1.3 C
  - **Corals:** **1.2 C** threshold for large-scale warm-water reef collapse already crossed; recovery from mass bleaching takes >15 years
  - Beyond ~2 C parts of the **Amazon** may become savanna → carbon store to source

| **Tipping element** | **Status/note** |
|---|---|
