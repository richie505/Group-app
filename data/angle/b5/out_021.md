=== ROW 75 | Environmental issues and concerns - land degradation, desertification, deforestation, soil erosion

## Land degradation — terms → meaning | from: S1, S2, S4, S8
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Desertification** | Spread of desert-like conditions into non-arid areas through climate change, human activity, land degradation |
| Desertification (UNCCD sense) | Land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas from climatic variation and human activity - not the spread of existing deserts alone |
| **"Tragedy of the Commons"** | Over-use / overgrazing of a shared pasture lowers land quality (APPSC 2018); concept of **Garrett Hardin, 1968** |
| **Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN)** | No net loss of land health; target year **2030** (SDG 15.3) |
| **Soil Organic Carbon** | Indian soils reportedly fell from **1% to 0.3%** over 70 years |
| **Intensive agriculture** | Modern agriculture; effects: soil degradation, pollution, ecological imbalance - **all of these** (APPSC 2018) |
| **Urban heat island (UHI)** | Cities markedly hotter than surroundings |
| **Cool roofs** | High-reflectance (white/reflective) roof coatings → cut heat absorption + indoor cooling load |
| **'ridge to valley'** | Approach of watershed development under PMKSY Watershed Development Component |
- ⚠ Desertification is land degradation in drylands - **not** the spread of existing deserts alone
- ⚠ Tragedy of the Commons = **Garrett Hardin, 1968** (APPSC 2018)

## Causes → effects of degradation | from: S0, S1, S2, S8
# 1. Deforestation
- **Drivers:** Clearing for agriculture, urbanisation, industry, mining
- **Impacts:**
  - Loss of vegetative cover, lower soil moisture retention
  - Faster erosion, falling fertility, desertification
  - Loss of CO2 sink adds to greenhouse effect
- **Highest loss:** **Tropical forests** (Amazon, Congo basin, SE Asia)
# 2. Desertification and erosion
- **Main cause in semi-arid regions:** **Overgrazing and deforestation** (APPSC 2025)
  - Others: improper irrigation, excess chemicals, erratic rainfall, droughts, population pressure

| **Process** | **How it degrades land** |
|---|---|
| Overgrazing | Strips vegetation cover |
| Deforestation | Exposes soil to wind and water erosion |
| Water erosion | Loss of topsoil; sedimentation of rivers and reservoirs |
| Wind erosion | Topsoil blown away in dry areas (e.g. Anantapur) |
| Salinisation/waterlogging | Poor drainage and over-irrigation (AP delta regions) |

# 3. Agriculture-driven degradation
- **Effects of modern agriculture (intensive agriculture):** Soil degradation, pollution, ecological imbalance - **all of these** (APPSC 2018)
- **Chemicals:** Synthetic fertilisers and pesticides contaminate soil and water
  - Excess **urea** has skewed the **NPK ratio** in Punjab and Haryana (CSE)
- **Over-irrigation + poor drainage:** → waterlogging and salinity; by **2050, 50%** of world arable land could be affected

| **Resource depleted** | **Typical cause** |
|---|---|
| Groundwater | Deep bore tubewells for irrigation |
| Topsoil | Brick production (topsoil mining) |
| Biodiversity habitats | Expansion of agriculture |
| Land for buildings | Urban construction on water bodies |

- **Note:** Pairings above are the natural (logical) matches of the APPSC-GS 2025 question (no answer key given/published)
# 4. Urban heat islands
- **UHI causes:**
  - **Concrete/asphalt** (low albedo, heat storage)
  - **Loss of vegetation**
  - **Waste heat** from ACs and vehicles (ACs worsen UHI)
  - Dense built canyons

## Water Act 1974 and Water Cess Act 1977 — provisions | from: S1, S10
# 1. Water Act 1974
- **Status:** First pollution law
- **Purpose:** Prevent and control water pollution; maintain/restore water quality
- **Basis:** Passed under **Article 252** (on resolution of states) → applies in states that adopt it
- **Boards:** Created **Central Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution (1974)** → renamed **CPCB** by **1988 amendment**; + State Boards
- **Section 33A:** Directions and penalties
- **Rules:** Water Rules 1975, Cess Rules 1978
# 2. Water Cess Act 1977
- **Cess:** On water consumed by specified industries and local authorities → funds the Boards
- **Rebate:** **25% rebate** for installing treatment plants
- **Proceeds:** **Consolidated Fund of India**
- **Size:** 17 sections
- **Fate:** Subsumed/repealed with GST in 2017
# 3. Water Act vs Air Act
| **Feature** | **Water Act 1974** | **Air Act 1981** |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Maintain and restore **wholesomeness of water** | Prevent, control and abate air pollution |
| Structure | **8 chapters, 64 sections** | 7 chapters, 54 sections |
| Boards | Created CPCB and SPCBs | Same boards given air functions |
| Special power | Standards for discharge of effluents | Declare **air pollution control areas**; emission standards for industry and automobiles |

- ⚠ Water Act = **Article 252**; EPA = **Article 253**

## Air Act 1981 — provisions | from: S2
- **Dates:** Enacted **29 March 1981**; in force **16 May 1981**; modelled on the Water Act
- **Structure:** **7 chapters, 54 sections**; amended **1987**
- **Purpose:** **Prevent, control and abate air pollution** - i.e., maintain air quality standards (APPSC-G2 2025); set up Central and State Boards
- **Boards:** CPCB (already existing under Water Act) got air functions; SPCBs enforce
- **Noise:** Air pollutant definition **includes noise** - added by **1987 amendment**
- **Air pollution control areas:** Declared by State Government (Chapter 4, Sections 19-31)
- **Section 31A:** Power to give directions; PCBs can levy compensation
- ⚠ Water Act **8 chapters, 64 sections**; Air Act **7 chapters, 54 sections**; noise added **1987**

## Environment (Protection) Act 1986 — background, powers, authorities | from: S3
# 1. Background and nature
- **Trigger:** Enacted after the **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (Dec 1984)**
- **Constitutional basis:** **Article 253** (**Art. 253**) → implement Stockholm Conference (1972) decisions
- **Umbrella legislation:** Framework to coordinate central and state authorities set up under the Water Act 1974 and Air Act 1981
- **Purpose (PYQ):** **Framework for protecting and improving the environment** (to **protect and improve the environment**) + control pollution (APPSC 2025 answer)
- **Primary authority:** Central Government
# 2. Powers of the Centre
- Lay down emission/discharge **standards**
- Regulate **location of industries**
- Manage **hazardous wastes**
- Protect public health
- Issue directions including closure (Section 5)
# 3. Authorities under Sec 3(3)
- **Central Ground Water Authority** (1997)
- **National Ganga River Basin Authority (2009)**
- **Standards:** MoEF&CC with CPCB, published in the **Gazette**; e.g. lead in paints capped at **90 ppm**

## Consent mechanism — CTE/CTO and industry colour categories | from: S7

=== ROW 77 | Environmental Impact Assessment - process, EIA notification, public hearing, criticism

## EIA — origin, FIRSTs and amendment chronology | from: S0
- **Global origin:** **National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), USA, 1969**
- **India:**
  - Began **1976-77** - Planning Commission asked **DST** to examine **river-valley projects**
  - Till 1994, EC only an administrative decision
  - Some notes (2026) date first use to **1978** (river valley projects), then large public-sector projects needing **Public Investment Board** clearance
  - Codified in **1994**

| **Year** | **Amendment (CDI)** |
|---|---|
| 2009 | Expanded list of projects |
| 2011 | River valley projects in specific contexts |
| 2012 / 2018 | Mining; mining and infrastructure |
| 2013 | Thermal power |
| 2015 | Expansion and modernisation |
| 2020 | Draft notification (post-facto, shorter consultation) |
| 2021 | Procedural clarifications |

- ⚠ Sources differ on India's start: **1976-77** (DST, river-valley) vs **1978** (2026 notes)

## Terms → meaning (EIA and related tools) | from: S1, S2, S6
| **Tool / term** | **Scope / meaning** |
|---|---|
| **EIA** | Impacts of a specific **project before it starts** (APPSC 2025 answer; PYQ) |
| **LCA** (Life-Cycle Assessment) | Impacts of a **product/process across its life** - whole life cycle, cradle to grave (raw material to disposal) (APPSC 2025 answer; PYQ) |
| **SEA** (Strategic Environmental Assessment) | Impacts of **policies, plans and programmes** - upstream of projects |
| **Cumulative Impact Assessment** | Combined effect of several projects in one area/basin |
| **Environmental audit** | Compliance check of an **operating** unit, after the fact |
| **Cleaner production** | Preventive strategy - cut waste and pollution **at source** (process change, input substitution) vs end-of-pipe treatment |
| **Green chemistry** | Design products/processes that reduce or eliminate hazardous substances |
| **PARIVESH** | Pro-Active and Responsive facilitation by Interactive, Virtuous and Environmental Single-window Hub |

- **UPSC way-forward:** **Sector-wide EIAs** (i.e., strategic/cumulative) needed (p.169)
- **Green chemistry:** **12 principles** (Anastas and Warner, 1998)
  - e.g., prevent waste, atom economy, safer solvents, renewable feedstocks, design for degradation
- APPCB's mandate includes encouraging **cleaner production technologies**
- **Link to assessment:** Both shrink impacts at design stage → EIA/LCA find fewer residual impacts to mitigate
  - EIA requires **assessment of alternatives** in location and **process technology** (p.167)
- ⚠ EIA = **project**; LCA = **product** life cycle; SEA = **policies/plans**; audit = **after** the fact

## EIA Notifications 1994, 2006 and Draft 2020 — provisions | from: S4, S7
# 1. EIA Notification 1994
- **Notified:** **1994** (27 Jan 1994, under EPA)
- **Scope:**
  - **Schedule I** projects need MoEF clearance
  - Industrial projects above **Rs 500 million**; fragile-area projects
  - **Two-stage** (site + final) clearance for pithead thermal and valley projects
- **Public hearing:** Mandatory - added by the 1997 amendment
# 2. EIA Notification 2006
- **Notified:** **2006** (**14 Sep 2006** / **14 September 2006**) under EPA 1986; replaced the **EIA Notification 1994**
- **Shift:** Focus from **investment to impact potential** (investment size → **impact potential**)
- **EC compulsory:** **39 project categories**
- **Safeguards:** Air, water, land, biodiversity, wildlife habitat

| **Category** | **Appraisal** | **Clearance by** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Expert Appraisal Committee (**EAC**) | **MoEFCC** | No screening; always full EIA |
| **B1** | State Expert Appraisal Committee (**SEAC**) | **SEIAA** | EIA required (needs EIA) |
| **B2** | State Expert Appraisal Committee (SEAC) | **SEIAA** | No EIA |

- ⚠ **A** → EAC → MoEFCC; **B1/B2** → SEAC → SEIAA; only **B2** has no EIA
# 3. Draft EIA Notification 2020 - contested provisions
- **Status:** Wide protest; widely debated, **not been finalised** (Aug 2026)

## Cases on post-facto clearance → holding | from: S8
# 1. Cases
| **Case** | **Issue / holding** |
|---|---|
| **Common Cause v UoI (2017)** | Mining in Odisha without or beyond EC; mineral extracted illegally to be paid for as compensation (100% of its value) [GK] |
| **Alembic Pharmaceuticals v Rohit Prajapati (2020)** | Ex post facto EC held contrary to the EIA regime; 2002 MoEF circular allowing it quashed; compensation imposed instead of closure [GK] |
| **Noble M. Paikada v UoI (2024)** | Validity/legality of the **2020 exemption from EC** (**2020 notification exempting** extraction of ordinary earth for linear projects) - APPSC 2024 answer (PYQ); SC held the blanket exemption arbitrary [GK] |
| **Vanashakti v UoI (May 2025)** | Retrospective ECs a "**gross illegality**" and "anathema" to environmental law; struck down 2017 Notification / 2021 OM (LENS, Nov 2025) |
| **Vanashakti recall (Nov 2025)** | **2:1** majority **recalled** the May 2025 ruling; relied on **D Swamy (2021)** and **Pahwa Plastics (2022)**, which allowed post-facto EC in exceptional cases; ~**₹20,000 crore** of projects faced demolition (LENS, Nov 2025) |
| **S.P. Muthuraman case (2025)** | Also held retrospective ECs illegal |

# 2. Final Vanashakti verdict, 29 July 2026
- **Bench:** Led by **CJI Surya Kant** (LENSD 31 Jul 2026; TH 21 Aug 2026)
- **Holding:**
  - Nullified/quashed the **2021 Office Memorandum** (retrospective EC on paying penalties)
  - Ruling **prospective** - earlier ECs under the 2017 Notification and 2021 OM survive (31 Jul 2026)
  - Centre may issue **time-limited notifications under EPA 1986**
  - Ex post facto EC must meet Arts **14 and 21** (test of proportionality); SC keeps power under **Article 142**
  - Fresh violators cannot seek regularisation under the 2017 Notification or 2021 SOP (21 Aug 2026)
- **Criticism:** Critics call the OM-vs-notification distinction a "fig-leaf"
- ⚠ Vanashakti: **May 2025** struck down → **Nov 2025** recalled (2:1) → **29 July 2026** final, prospective
> Exam angle: "primary issue in case X" - match case to the exemption or post-facto question it decided.
> Exam angle: prior EC is mandatory under EIA 2006; post-facto approval only as a narrow exception.
