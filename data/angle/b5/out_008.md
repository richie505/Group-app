=== ROW 38 | Navigation satellites - IRNSS/NavIC constellation and GAGAN; applications and limitations

## Satellite navigation — GPS basics, NavIC full form, constellation and services | from: S0, S1
# 1. GPS and atomic clocks
- **GPS = Global Positioning System:** US Department of Defense programme
  - Started **1973**; first satellite **1978**
  - **24** satellites in **6** orbits at **20,200 km**, each circling twice a day
  - Signals: L1 (1,575.42 MHz) + L2
  - Now about **30** satellites (Mar 2026)
- **Atomic clocks:** **1 microsecond** clock error = ~**300 m** position error
# 2. NavIC
- **Full form:** **Navigation with Indian Constellation** (CGPSC 2021); formerly **IRNSS**
- **Status:** Fully operational **2018**
- **Genesis:** US denied GPS data in the **1999 Kargil War**
- **Constellation:** **7 satellites - 3 GEO + 4 inclined GSO** (at **29°** to the equator)
- **Coverage:** India + **1,500 km** beyond its borders
- **Bands:** **L5 and S**; newer **NVS** satellites add civilian **L1** signal (compatible with other GNSS) → eases phone adoption

| **Service** | **Users** | **Accuracy** |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard Positioning Service (SPS)** | All civilians | Better than **20 m**; timing better than **40 ns** (LENSD) |
| **Restricted Service (RS)** | Authorised/military users | Encrypted |

- **Accuracy:** Some notes claim better than 10 m over India and 20 m in the extended area; others give "better than 20 m" as the design figure
- ⚠ **GPS** = 24 satellites, 6 orbits, 20,200 km; **NavIC** = 7 satellites (3 GEO + 4 GSO), regional

## IRNSS and NVS satellites → launch and clock failures | from: S2
# 1. Launches
- **IRNSS-1A to 1G:** The 7 + replacements
- **Total:** **11** navigation satellites launched since **2013** (Mar 2026)
- **Launch site:** All from **Sriharikota**; IRNSS-1 series on **PSLV**
# 2. NVS series

| **Satellite** | **Vehicle / date** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|
| **NVS-01 (second generation)** | **May 2023, GSLV-F12** | First with **indigenous rubidium atomic clock** |
| **NVS-02** | **GSLV-F15, Jan 2025** (100th Sriharikota launch) | Valve glitch → wrong orbit; salvaged for alternate use |

# 3. Clock issues
- **First generation:** **Rubidium clocks from Swiss firm SpectraTime** kept failing → ISRO switched to **indigenous clocks (SAC)** in **2018**
- **IRNSS-1F failure (13 Mar 2026):** Last clock failed
  - Launched Mar 2016; 10-year life ended 10 Mar 2026
  - Only **3** PNT-capable satellites left (**IRNSS-1B, 1I, NVS-01**) → below the **4** needed
  - 1F continues one-way messaging (15 and 20 Mar 2026)
- **IRNSS-1D:** Decommissioned to 600 km above GEO (ISSAR 2025; 9 Apr 2026)
- ⚠ Clocks: **SpectraTime** (Swiss, failed) → **SAC** indigenous (2018); NVS-01 = first indigenous rubidium clock

## GAGAN and other SBAS → developer and region | from: S4
- **Full form:** **GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation**
- **Developers:** **ISRO and the Airports Authority of India (AAI)**
- **SBAS concept:** Broadcasts real-time corrections for orbit, clock and ionospheric delay → improves GPS **accuracy, integrity and availability** (UPSC 2025 statement I correct)
- **Payloads:** On geostationary **GSAT-8, GSAT-10, GSAT-15**
- **Coverage:** Serves the Indian **Flight Information Region**
- **Ground segment:**

  - Master Control Centre (INMCC)
  - Uplink stations (INLUS)
- **Aviation use:** Precision approach and landing for civil aviation
  - DGCA certified GAGAN for aviation approach guidance in **2015**
- **Nodal authority:** **Satellite Navigation Programme Office** under ISRO → for NavIC and GAGAN

=== ROW 39 | Earth observation - IRS series, Cartosat, Resourcesat, Oceansat, RISAT, EOS series and applications

## Remote sensing — definition, components, sensor types | from: S0
- **Definition:** Information about the Earth **without physical contact**, from **reflected or emitted electromagnetic radiation**
- **Components of a remote sensing system:**
  - **Energy source/illumination** → radiation through the atmosphere → **interaction with target** → recording by sensor → **transmission, reception and processing** → interpretation → application
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Interaction of energy with minimization/minimisation" is NOT a component (APPSC 2022)

| **Sensor type** | **Energy source** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| **Passive** | Natural sunlight (Sun's reflected light) or the target's emitted heat | IRS, Resourcesat, Oceansat, cameras (visible cameras), multispectral/hyperspectral imagers, infrared radiometers |
| **Active** | Own signal: sensor sends its own pulse and reads the echo | **RISAT radar (SAR)**, **laser/LiDAR** (APPSC 2023: laser is active) |

| **Imaging type** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| **Optical (visible/NIR)** | Reflected light → needs daylight + clear sky; blocked by cloud |
| **SAR (microwave)** | Pierces clouds and darkness → sees at night |
| **Hyperspectral** | Hundreds of narrow bands → material/chemical identification (EOS-N1) |

## Resolution types and remote-sensing techniques → what they map | from: S1
- **Four resolution types:** **spatial, spectral, temporal, radiometric**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "**chemical**" resolution does not exist (APPSC 2025)

| **Technique** | **Maps** |
|---|---|
| **InSAR** | Ground deformation - subsidence, earthquake and volcano uplift [GK] |
| **LiDAR** | Laser ranging - elevation, 3D terrain, forest canopy height [GK] |
| **NDVI** | Vegetation health from red vs near-infrared reflectance [GK] |
| **Thermal infrared** | Records emitted heat - **volcanic eruptions** (APPSC 2023: infrared-sensitive sensors), forest fires, urban heat |

## Orbits for Earth observation → altitude and use | from: S2
- **LEO = Low Earth Orbit:** Most EO satellites fly in **sun-synchronous polar orbits**
  - PSLV built for this: ~**1,600 kg to 620 km SSO**
- **SSO features:**
  - Near-polar; passes each place at **same local solar time** → consistent lighting
  - Polar path covers whole globe
- **Trade-off:**
  - LEO = fine detail but periodic revisit
  - **Geosynchronous imagers** (EOS-05, ~36,000 km) = coarser detail but **priority targets every 5 min** + **all of India every 30 min** (4 Sep 2026; 7 Sep 2026)

| **Orbit** | **Altitude** | **Key use** |
|---|---|---|
| LEO | 200-2,000 km | Earth observation, ISS, Starlink-type constellations |
| MEO | 2,000-30,000 km | Navigation (GPS) |
| GEO (geostationary) | **35,786 km** over the Equator | Communication, weather; satellite appears fixed |
| GSO (geosynchronous) | Same altitude, inclined/elliptical | Traces a figure-8; NavIC's 4 inclined satellites |
| SSPO (sun-synchronous polar) | Polar, ~500-900 km | Passes the same place at the same local time daily, giving uniform lighting for imaging |

- **GEO vs GSO:** Both have period of one sidereal day, about **23 h 56 min**
  - Geostationary = special case with zero inclination + circular orbit

## Chandrayaan-1 and Chandrayaan-2 — payloads, findings, outcome | from: S3, S4
# 1. Chandrayaan-1 (2008)
- **Modules:** Orbiter + Moon Impact Probe (MIP)
- **Specs:** Mass 1,380 kg; **11 instruments** from India, USA, UK, Germany, Sweden, Bulgaria
- **Discovery:** NASA's **M3 (Moon Mineralogy Mapper)** → evidence of **water/hydroxyl**; confirmed **water molecules** on the lunar surface
- **Other payloads:** TMC, HySI, LLRI, HEX, CIXS, SIR-2, SARA, Mini-SAR, RADOM
- **MIP (Moon Impact Probe):** Hit lunar south polar region on 14 Nov 2008; impact site named **Jawahar Point**
  - India = **4th nation** to place its flag on the Moon via impact probe (after USSR, USA, Japan)
- **Life:** **3,400+ orbits**; contact lost **29 Aug 2009** after 312 days
# 2. Chandrayaan-2 (2019)
- **Modules:** Orbiter + lander **Vikram** + rover **Pragyan**; orbiter-based imaging payloads
- **Aim:** Study south polar region (surface, minerals, water ice)
- **Outcome:** **Vikram's** soft landing failed (Sep 2019) - lander crash-landed; crash spot named **Tiranga point**
  - But the **orbiter still works** with all payloads active → highest-resolution lunar images (May 2025; Jun 2026)
- ⚠ Site names: **Jawahar Point** = Ch-1 MIP; **Tiranga point** = Ch-2 crash; **Shiv Shakti** = Ch-3 landing

## Chandrayaan-3 — modules, launch, specs and payloads | from: S5
# 1. Mission
- **Modules:** Propulsion Module + lander **Vikram** + rover **Pragyan**
- **Names:** Kept Chandrayaan-2 names → lander **Vikram**, rover **Pragyan** (APPSC 2022)

## Chandrayaan-3 — landing site, India's rank and findings | from: S6
# 1. Landing site and India's rank
- **Landing site:** **"Shiv Shakti" point (Statio Shiv Shakti)** → about 600 km from the South Pole, on a plain between craters Manzinus C and Simpelius N (Jun 2026)
- **Rank:** India = **4th country** to soft-land on the Moon (after US, Russia, China) + **1st near the lunar south pole**
  - Japan's **SLIM** later made Japan the 5th
- **National Space Day:** **23 August** (Aug 2026)
# 2. Findings
- **Sulphur:** Pragyan confirmed **sulphur** near the south pole (APPSC 2023)
- **Pragyan LIBS:** Also detected Al, Ca, Fe, Cr, Ti, Mn, Si and O besides sulphur
- **APXS:** Shiv Shakti soil rich in iron + magnesium, poor in aluminium; chemically similar to lunar meteorite **ALHA 81005** (Jun 2026)

## Mangalyaan (2013) — FIRST-attempt record, rank, orbit, payloads | from: S7

## Aditya-L1 (2023) — L1 point, payloads, results | from: S8
# 1. Mission basics
- **Status:** India's **first space-based solar observatory**
- **Launch:** **2 September 2023** by **PSLV-C57** from Sriharikota
- **Orbit:** **Halo orbit around Sun-Earth L1**, about **1.5 million km** from Earth
  - Halo-orbit insertion: **6 January 2024** (Jan 2026)
  - Continuous view of the Sun, no occultation/eclipse
- ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes (prep-app) give launch as "25 January 2024" → WRONG; correct = **2 Sep 2023** on PSLV-C57, halo orbit Jan 2024 (APPSC 2022 key)
# 2. Payloads → what they study
- **7 payloads:** 4 remote sensing + 3 in-situ

| **Type** | **Payload** | **Studies** |
|---|---|---|
| Remote sensing | **VELC** (Visible Emission Line Coronagraph) | Corona imaging and spectroscopy; built by **Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA)** |
| Remote sensing | **SUIT** (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope) | Photosphere and chromosphere (near-UV full-disc images); built by IUCAA [GK] |
| Remote sensing | **SoLEXS** | Soft X-ray spectrometer |
| Remote sensing | **HEL1OS** | Hard X-ray spectrometer |
| In-situ | **ASPEX** | Solar wind protons and heavier ions |

- ⚠ **VELC** = IIA (corona); **SUIT** = IUCAA (photosphere, chromosphere)
# 3. Results
- **2025 solar maximum:** VELC recorded the **first-ever spectroscopic observations of CMEs in visible wavelengths** (IIA with NASA)
- **Data:** More than **27 TB** of solar data released (Jun 2026)
