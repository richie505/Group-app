=== ROW 103 | Solid waste, plastic waste, e-waste, biomedical and construction waste - rules and management

## Waste — definitions, categories and biodegradable vs non-biodegradable | from: S0, S19, S23, S24, S30
# 1. Definitions
| **Waste** | **Definition** |
|---|---|
| Solid waste | **Definition:** discarded or abandoned materials - solid, liquid, semi-solid or containerised gaseous |
| E-waste (**Definition (ITU)**) | Discarded electrical and electronic equipment and parts not meant for reuse |
| Biomedical waste | Waste from diagnosis, treatment or immunisation of humans/animals - blood, sharps, anatomical waste, cultures, discarded/expired medicines, chemical waste |
| Construction & demolition | **Composition:** Mostly inert - concrete, bricks, dirt, rocks, debris |

# 2. Categories of solid waste
| **Category** | **What it holds** |
|---|---|
| **Municipal** | household + commercial (organics, plastics, paper, metals) |
| **Industrial** | process by-products, often hazardous |
| **Hazardous** | - |
| **Biomedical** | syringes, gloves |
| **E-waste** | - |
| **Agricultural** | - |
| **Construction & demolition** | inert - concrete, dirt, rocks, debris |
| Radioactive, mining | - |

# 3. Hazardous waste — characteristics
- **Four classic criteria:** **ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity**
  - Some notes add explosive, infectious, radioactive
- **Forms:** solids, liquids, sludges, gases
- **Examples:** lead, mercury, cadmium, chromium, solvents, dyes, pesticides
- **Household examples:** paints, batteries, **fluorescent lamps**, aerosol cans, tyres, fertilisers, pesticides
- **Biomedical composition:** about **85%** general non-hazardous; **~15%** hazardous (infectious, chemical, radioactive) (WHO)
# 4. Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable
- **Biodegradable:** decomposed by microorganisms (mainly **bacteria**) into simpler substances
  - Aerobic decomposition → **compost**
  - Anaerobic decomposition → **methane (CH4)**/biogas
- **Non-biodegradable:** mainly inorganic; not broken down by microbes; accumulates in environment

| **Item** | **Class** |
|---|---|
| Food/kitchen waste, vegetable waste/peels, fruits, green waste, cow-dung, paddy husk | Biodegradable (decomposed by bacteria) |
| Paper, **sewage** | Biodegradable |
| Natural fibres - **wool**, cotton | Biodegradable |
| Metals - **tin**, aluminium foil, metal scrap | Non-biodegradable |
| Plastics (cups, bags, containers), **glass bottles** | Non-biodegradable |
| DDT, heavy metals | Non-biodegradable |
| Synthetic rubber / tyres | Non-biodegradable |

> Exam angle: "which is NOT biodegradable" - tin/metal/glass/plastic; "which IS biodegradable" - wool, peels.

## Terms → meaning: waste hierarchy, 3R/5R and circular economy | from: S2, S7, S19
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **3R** | **3R = Reduce, Reuse, Recycle** |
| **Top priority** | **Reduction at source** - **source reduction** (generating less waste) = top of hierarchy (APPSC-GS 2025: practising 3R reduces waste at source) |
| **Recycling** | converting waste into usable other materials (APPSC-GS 2024) |
| **Reuse** | using the item again in its existing form |
| **5R** | adds **Refuse** and Repurpose/Recover (versions vary) |
| **RRR Centre** | permanent in every city under SBM-U 2.0 (Reduce, Reuse, Recover) (2026) |
| **Circular economy** | biomining of legacy waste supports the **circular economy** (CDCA Jul 2026) |
| **Waste to Wealth Mission** | Swachhta Saarthi Fellowships, Su-Dhara programme |
| **Legacy waste** | Old MSW piled in dumpsites for years; emits methane, pollutes groundwater |
| **Biomining** | Excavating + separating it into recyclables, RDF, bio-soil and inert fill; legacy dumps are cleared by **biomining** |
| **Urban mining** | Iron, steel, copper, aluminium, plastics recoverable from e-waste; India imports **90%** of its lithium and cobalt (Oct 2025) |

- ⚠ **Trap:** "Regain" is not one of the 3R (APPSC-GS 2025)

## Disposal methods → process → waste type | from: S5, S6
| **Method** | **Process** | **Suited to** | **Key point / note** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sanitary landfill | Waste compacted in **cells** and covered with earth daily; liners, leachate collection, methane and groundwater monitoring | Only non-recyclable, non-energy-recoverable and inert (residual) waste | Most common and economic; relies on **containment**, not treatment; landfill gas can be recovered |
| Composting (aerobic) | **Aerobic** microbes oxidise organics; exothermic; end products CO2, nitrite, nitrate; gives manure | Biodegradable organic/wet waste (kitchen, garden, farm waste) | **Indore method** (turning at intervals) |
| Composting (anaerobic pit) | Pits with alternate layers of MSW and night soil; **4-6 months** | - | **Bangalore method** |
| Vermicomposting | **Earthworms** convert organic waste into vermicompost | Biodegradable household/organic waste | - |
| Anaerobic digestion / biomethanation | Organic waste → **methane** biogas + slurry used as manure/fertiliser | Only organic, segregated waste | Odour- and fly-free |
| Incineration | Burning at high temperature in controlled chambers; steam for power (recovers energy) | Hazardous, biomedical, combustible waste | Cuts volume **up to 90%**; air pollution, ash disposal |
| Pyrolysis | Heating in **absence of oxygen** (heat **without oxygen**) → gas, oil, char | Mixed/contaminated waste streams | Costly |
| Plasma gasification | Plasma converts waste into synthetic fuel (syngas) | - | Sweden example |
| Waste-to-energy / RDF | Shredded, dried fuel for cement kilns | High-calorific plastic, paper, textiles | - |

- **Urban India methods:** **composting, incineration and landfilling** - **all** used ("all the above") (APPSC-G2 2025)
  - Uncontrolled dumping is not a viable option
- **Indore/Bangalore composting:** systematised by **Howard**; developed by **Acharya and Subrahmanyam**
- **Refuse-Derived Fuel (RDF):** **Shredding and dehydrating** high-calorific MSW
  - Mainly non-recyclable plastic, paper, textiles, after removing recyclables and biodegradables
  - **Use:** Alternative fuel in **cement kilns** + thermal power plants
- **WtE benefit:** WtE cuts MSW volume by ~90%
- ⚠ **Indore** = aerobic, turning; **Bangalore** = anaerobic pit, 4-6 months

| **Waste** | **Method** |
|---|---|
| E-waste | Recycling |
| Broken glass pieces | Landfilling |
| Biomedical waste | Incineration at CBMWTF |
| Non-recyclable high-calorific dry waste | RDF / co-processing in cement kilns |

> Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2023 match - organic-II (composting), e-waste-IV (recycling), hazardous-I (incineration), glass-III (landfilling).

# 2. Microplastics
- **Size:** Particles **up to 5 mm** (APPSC-GS 2023)
- **Nurdles:** Primary microplastic pellets (1-5 mm)
- **Findings:**
  - **FSSAI study:** Microplastics in **all tested sugar and salt samples**
  - Sundarbans microplastics drive plastic-degrading enzymes in microbes carrying **AMR genes**
- **Curbs:** PWM Rules ban **microbeads** in cosmetics; National Action Plan for Marine Litter (Dec 2025)

## Hazardous constituents → harm: e-waste, PFAS and mercury lamps | from: S19, S24

  - ⚠ **Trap:** NOT heptachlor, NOT plutonium (UPSC 2013 key)
  - Also **PCBs, PBBs** (brominated flame retardants)

| **Constituent** | **Main harm** |
|---|---|
| Lead | Nervous system, kidneys; soil contamination [GK] |
| Mercury | Brain and kidney damage; bioaccumulates in fish [GK] |
| Cadmium | Kidney and bone damage [GK] |
| Brominated flame retardants (PBBs) | Persistent; endocrine effects [GK] |

## Biomedical waste — colour code → waste → treatment | from: S23
- **Scale (2024):** **1.89 lakh** health-care facilities generate **800+ tonnes/day**
- **Colour coding:** **4 colour categories** (**four**)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "10 colour-coded categories" mixes up the 1998 Rules

| **Colour** | **Waste** | **Treatment** |
|---|---|---|
| **Yellow** | Anatomical, soiled, expired medicines, chemical | Incineration / plasma pyrolysis / deep burial |
| **Red** | Contaminated recyclable plastics (tubes, bottles, IV sets, catheters, syringes without needles) | Autoclave / microwave + shredding, then recycling |
| **White** (translucent) | **Sharps** - needles, blades | Autoclave / dry heat, shredding, encapsulation |

- ⚠ **White** = sharps; **Red** = recyclable plastics; **Blue** = glass; **Yellow** = anatomical

## Solid waste rules → year → provisions (MSW 2000, SWM 2016, SWM 2026) | from: S3, S7
| **Rules** | **Key points** |
|---|---|
| MSW (Management and Handling) Rules **2000** | Primary goal: **promoting waste segregation** (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| SWM Rules **2016** (replaced 2000 Rules; from **8 Apr 2016**) | Extended beyond municipal areas to UAs, census towns, industrial townships, railways, airports, SEZs, religious/historical places; **3 streams** (biodegradable, dry, domestic hazardous); user fees and spot fines; EPR for producers/brand owners; no untreated waste to landfill (CDI) |
| **SWM Rules 2026** (replace 2016; in force **1 Apr 2026**) | **4 streams**: **wet, dry, sanitary, special care** (domestic hazardous); landfills only for non-recyclable, non-energy-recoverable and inert waste; landfill fee for unsegregated waste higher than the cost of processing; legacy dumps mapped with time-bound biomining and quarterly reports; **EBWGR** duty on bulk generators (LENS Feb 2026; TH 12 May 2026) |

- **SWM Rules 2026:** notified under **EPA 1986**; in force **1 April 2026**; supersede 2016 Rules
  - Hill and island towns can levy **user fees on tourists**
  - **Environmental Compensation** on polluter-pays principle
  - Legacy waste: map all legacy dumpsites; time-bound biomining/bioremediation with **quarterly** reporting
  - ⚠ **Trap:** draft "SWM Rules 2024, from 1 Oct 2025" proposed a 1 Oct 2025 start - not the notified rules; some 2026 CA notes (Jul, May) still describe the 2016 three-stream rule
- **SC:** pan-India directions to implement; clean environment part of **Art. 21** (Feb 2026)

| **Actor** | **Duty under 2026 Rules** |
|---|---|
| Every generator | Four-stream segregation - wet, dry, sanitary, special care |
| Bulk Waste Generators | Process wet waste on site or get **EBWGR** certificate; thresholds 100 kg/day waste, 5,000 L/day water or 20,000 sq m (CDI) |
| Local bodies | Map legacy dumpsites; biomining; landfill fee for unsegregated waste higher than processing cost |
| Hill/island bodies | User fee on tourists; regulate tourist inflow |

- ⚠ **2000** = segregation goal; **2016** = 3 streams; **2026** = 4 streams

# 2. PWM (Amendment) Rules 2026
- **PWM (Amendment) Rules 2026:** (announced 31 Mar 2026) (Apr 2026; 6 Apr 2026)
  - Plastics classed as **rigid, flexible and multi-layered**
  - Recycled content in rigid plastics: **30% to 60% by 2028-29**
  - Carry-forward of unmet targets up to **3 years**; tradable plastic certificates
  - End-of-life: waste-to-energy (WtE), co-processing, waste-to-oil allowed; plastic waste = advanced-biofuel feedstock (Apr 2026)
  - **QR-code traceability** mandatory from July 2025
- **Reuse targets (rigid packaging, 2025-26):** 10% small containers; **70%** large water packaging (4 Apr 2026)
- **Compostable plastics:** Must meet **IS/ISO 17088**
# 3. Single-use plastic ban — 1 July 2022
- **In force:** **1 July 2022**
- **Scope:** Manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale and use of identified items banned
  - Count: most notes say **19 items**; some notes say **12 SUP items** / group them as 12 heads
- **Items:**
  - Ear buds with plastic sticks, balloon sticks, **plastic flags**, candy and ice-cream sticks
  - **Thermocol for decoration**
  - Plates, cups, glasses, cutlery, straws, trays, stirrers
  - Wrapping film on sweet boxes and cigarette packets
  - **PVC banners under 100 microns** (< 100 microns)
- **Enforcement:** SPCBs/PCCs + local bodies; **National Level Taskforce**; State Special Task Forces
- **Limit:** Ban covers only **11%** of total plastic waste (CSE 2023; Aug 2025)
