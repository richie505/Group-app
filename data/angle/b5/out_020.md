=== ROW 73 | Environment - components; ecology, ecosystem, food chain and food web, energy flow, ecological pyramids

## Environment — meaning, categories and functions | from: S0
- **Meaning:** surroundings and conditions in which organisms live; biotic and abiotic factors interact; **never static**
- **Composite nature:** physical (nutrients, water, air) + biological (organisms) + chemical interactions (carbon, nitrogen cycles)

| **Basis** | **Types** |
|---|---|
| **Categories** | **physical** (lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere); **biotic** (plants, animals, microbes); **cultural** (man-made - social, economic, political) |
| **Primary functions** | provide resources, **assimilate wastes**, habitat for biodiversity |

- ⚠ **Trap:** **"generating synthetic chemicals" is NOT** one (APPSC 2024)
- **Habitat vs environment:** habitat always has life and suits one species; environment may not have life

## Ecology — levels of organisation, habitat and niche | from: S1, S2
# 1. Levels of organisation
- **"Ecology" coined:** **Ernst Haeckel (1869)** (the ecologists table gives 1866)
- **Ecological order:** **organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere**
- **Full hierarchy:** atoms → molecules → protoplasm → cells → tissues → organs → organisms → populations → communities → ecosystems → biomes → biosphere
- **Community:** all populations of different species in an area [GK]
- **Biome:** large region defined by climate and dominant vegetation - rainforest, tundra, desert (UPSC notes)
- **Biosphere:**
  - Absent at the poles, highest peaks and deepest oceans
  - Fungal/bacterial spores above **8,000 m** are dormant
  - Energy from the sun; nutrients recycled within
# 2. Habitat vs niche
- **Habitat:** physical place where an organism lives - its **"address"** [GK]
  - One habitat can hold many species
  - All habitats are environments, not all environments are habitats
- **Niche:** functional role of a species in a habitat - its **"profession/lifestyle"**; the food, space, time, conditions it uses [GK]
  - Set by habitat, food, reproduction and physical-chemical factors
  - **Unique to each species** - no two species occupy the same niche (APPSC 2022: both statements correct)

| **Fundamental niche** | **Realised niche** |
|---|---|
| full range of conditions and resources a species could use **free from other species** (APPSC 2021) | actual range used under competition and other limits |

- **Niche breadth:** specialists (koala - only eucalyptus) vs generalists (most animals)
- ⚠ Habitat = **address**; niche = **profession**

## Ecosystem — concept, structure vs function, open vs closed | from: S4
- **Term "ecosystem":** **Arthur Tansley** (A.G. Tansley; British botanist), **1935**
  - "System resulting from the integration of all living and non-living factors of the environment"
- **Ecosystem:** a biotic community + its non-living surroundings, linked by energy flow and nutrient cycling
- **Status:** **basic functional unit of ecology** (APPSC PYQ)

| **Structure** | **Function** |
|---|---|
| biotic community + abiotic materials, species composition, trophic levels [GK] | **energy flow** (one-way, from sun), **nutrient cycling** (biogeochemical cycles), primary and secondary production, self-regulation (APP) |

- **Flow vs cycle:** nutrients cycle (**biogeochemical cycles**) while energy flows one way
- **Open vs closed:** ecosystems are **open** for energy and matter; the Earth is open for energy but nearly closed for matter
- **Ecosystem components:**

| **Abiotic** | **Biotic** |
|---|---|
| Energy, radiation, temperature, water, gases and wind, fire, gravity, topography, soil | Green plants, non-green plants, decomposers, parasites, symbionts, animals, man |

## Abiotic factors — types and limiting-factor laws | from: S5
- **Abiotic factor:** any non-living component that affects organisms or their environment (APPSC 2018)

| **Type** | **Factors** |
|---|---|
| Climatic | light, temperature, rainfall/water, humidity, wind (APP; UPSC) |
| Edaphic | soil - texture, pH, minerals, substratum [GK] |
| Topographic | altitude, slope, aspect [GK] |

- **Abiotic components (list):** energy, radiation, temperature, water, atmospheric gases and wind, fire, gravity, topography, geologic substratum, soil
- **Role:** can be limiting or enabling for distribution of organisms
- **Limiting-factor laws:**
  - **Liebig's Law of the Minimum:** growth limited by the nutrient in shortest supply
  - **Shelford's Law of Tolerance:** both too little and too much of a factor limit an organism
- **Terrestrial habitants:** plants and animals living on land (UPPSC 2024)
- ⚠ Liebig = **minimum**; Shelford = **tolerance** (too little and too much)

- **Consumers:** macroconsumers (phagotrophs) vs microconsumers (saprotrophs)

| **Component** | **Examples** |

| Primary consumers (herbivores) | cow, rabbit |
| Secondary consumers (carnivores) | wolves |
| Tertiary consumers | lions (eat wolves) |
| Omnivores | humans |
| Decomposers (saprotrophs / osmotrophs) | **bacteria and fungi**; detritivores - earthworms, nematodes, arthropods |

- **APPSC 2023 match:** humans = omnivores; saprotrophs = feed on dead matter; reptiles = cold-blooded; camel = desert-adapted
- **Producers in different ecosystems:**

| **Ecosystem** | **Producers** |
|---|---|
| Terrestrial | Herbs, shrubs, trees [GK] |
| Aquatic | **Phytoplankton**, algae, submerged and floating plants [GK] |
| Deep-sea vents | **Chemosynthetic bacteria** (e.g. sulphur bacteria) use chemical energy, not light [GK] |

- **Autotroph types:**

| **Type** | **Energy source → examples** |
|---|---|
| Photoautotrophs | sunlight - green plants, algae, cyanobacteria [GK] |
| Chemoautotrophs | chemical oxidation - **bacteria in deep ocean trenches using hydrogen sulphide** from sea-floor cracks (APP); nitrifying bacteria [GK] |
| Archaea | many chemoautotrophic archaea (e.g. methanogens) live in extreme habitats [GK] |

## Decomposers and decomposition — steps and factors | from: S7
- **Who:** **bacteria and fungi** break dead matter into **inorganic nutrients reused by producers**; helpers = earthworms, nematodes, arthropods
- **Detritivores:** earthworm fragments dead matter
- **Role:** the **link between biotic and abiotic** parts
- **Steps:**
  - **fragmentation** (detritivores like earthworms) → **leaching** (soluble nutrients into soil) → **catabolism** (enzymes of bacteria and fungi) → **humification** → **mineralisation**

| **Factor** | **Effect on rate** |
|---|---|
| Detritus rich in nitrogen and sugars + **warm and moist** climate | Fast |
| **lignin, chitin**, low temperature, **anaerobic** conditions | Slow |

## Food chains and trophic levels | from: S8
# 1. Grazing vs detritus chains
| **Chain** | **Starts with** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Grazing** | **Living green plants (autotrophs)** → herbivores → carnivores | **Grass → goat → human** (PYQ 2025); grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle (UPSC) |
| **Detritus** | **Dead organic matter** (dead leaves, carcasses, remains) → detritivores/decomposers | Leaf litter → earthworm → bird |

- **PYQ 2025 (A-R) / APPSC-G2 2025:**
  - "Grazing chain begins with dead leaves" = **false**
  - "Autotrophs capture energy and pass (transfer) it to herbivores" = **true**
- **Pond:** phytoplankton → zooplankton → fish; bacteria/fungi as decomposers
- **Grassland:** grass → grasshopper/rabbit → snake/hawk
- **Aquatic sequence:** **phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → large fish**
# 2. Trophic levels
- **Trophic level:** an organism's functional position in the food chain; one species can occupy more than one level
- **Limit:** rarely exceed **4-5 trophic levels** - too little energy remains higher up

| **Trophic level** | **Organisms** | **Role** |
|---|---|---|
| I (T1) | Producers - green plants, phytoplankton, seaweeds; chemosynthetic bacteria (e.g. at hydrothermal vents) | **T1 - producers** (autotrophs); chemosynthetic bacteria make food from chemical energy |
| II (T2) | **Herbivores**, zooplankton | **T2 - primary consumers** |
| III (T3) | Carnivores | T3 - secondary consumers |
| IV/V (T4) | Top carnivores | T4 - tertiary, quaternary consumers |
| - | Detritivores (earthworms), decomposers (bacteria, fungi) | Base of the **detritus** food chain; they recycle all levels, not a single slot |

- **Missing-level question:** "tiger, grass, snake, frog" has **no herbivore (second level)** (PYQ 2025)
> Exam angle: a "missing second trophic level" means no herbivore in the chain (e.g. tiger, grass, snake, frog); "correct grazing chain" means it starts with a producer and moves up in order.

## Food webs and ecosystem stability — terms | from: S9, S15
# 1. Food webs
- **Food web:** network of interlinked food chains
  - Shows all energy paths; gives species alternative food → better survival
  - Removing a link affects the branches after it
- **Stability:** more complex web → more alternative pathways → ecosystem more **stable**
- **Stress:** warming and ocean acidification disrupt food chains through coral bleaching
# 2. Stability terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Resistance** | ability to withstand a disturbance without change [GK] |
| **Resilience** | ability to **recover** after a disturbance (APPSC 2024) |
| Homeostasis | **self-regulation** - every ecosystem resists stress up to a limit through feedback (APP) |

- **Stability:** rises with diversity and web complexity - diverse, complex systems (rainforest, coral reef) more stable than simple ones (tundra, desert, monoculture cropland)
- ⚠ Resistance = **withstand**; resilience = **recover**

## Energy flow — productivity and ten percent law | from: S10
# 1. Nature of energy flow
- **Primary source:** **Sun**
- **Direction:** **unidirectional** - sun → producers → consumers; not cycled (nutrients are)
- **Laws of thermodynamics:** energy flow obeys them
  - First law: energy is transformed, not created or destroyed
  - Second law: every transfer loses energy as heat
# 2. Capture and productivity
- **PAR:** under 50% of incident sunlight is photosynthetically active radiation (PAR); plants fix only about **2-10% of PAR**
- **Total primary production efficiency:** share of incoming **solar energy is captured and used in photosynthesis** (PYQ 2024; APPSC 2024)
  - On average only about 1-2%

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **GPP** (gross primary productivity) | Total rate at which producers fix solar energy / make organic matter in photosynthesis [GK] |
| **NPP** (net primary productivity) | **GPP minus respiration losses (R)** of producers - energy available to consumers (herbivores) [GK] |
| Secondary productivity | Rate of new organic matter formed by consumers (APP) |

# 3. Ten percent law
- **Ten percent law:** only about **10%** passes to the next level; about **90%** lost at each step (respiration, excretion, locomotion, heat)
  - **Lindeman's ten per cent law** - **Raymond Lindeman (1942)**; some notes spell it "Lindemann"
- **Consequence:** chains limited to **4-5 trophic levels**
# 4. Productivity ranking (NPP per unit area)
- **Most productive:** **tropical rainforests** lead on land - standard answer among deserts, rainforests, oceans, estuaries (PYQ 2025; APPSC 2025)
- **Also high:** estuaries, coral reefs, swamps
- **Lowest:** **deserts** and the **open ocean** (deserts and open oceans); ocean's total output still large because of its area
> Exam angle: "most productive ecosystem" = tropical rainforest in APPSC keys.

## Ecological pyramids — types, inverted cases, limitations | from: S11
- **Base vs apex:** base = producers (always); apex = top consumers (top carnivore)
- **Counting:** count **all** organisms at a level; one species may occupy more than one level
- **Types:** **Three** (three types)

| **Pyramid** | **Usually upright** | **Inverted case** |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Upright (grassland, pond) | **Tree ecosystem** - one tree, many insects; **parasitic** food chain |
| Biomass | Upright on land - most terrestrial ecosystems (forest, grassland) | **Aquatic / sea** - small standing crop (biomass) of fast-turning phytoplankton supports larger zooplankton/fish |
| **Energy** | **Always upright** - energy lost as heat at every step | Never |

- **Limitations:**
  - **Simple chain assumed:** rare in nature; food webs not shown
  - **Single level:** a species cannot be placed at two trophic levels
  - **Decomposers ignored:** **Saprophytes / decomposers get no place**, despite their vital role
  - **Practical issues:** numbers hard to count; organism size ignored
> Exam angle: "which pyramid is inverted": pyramid of numbers in a parasitic chain; "which can never be inverted": the energy pyramid.

## Biomagnification vs bioaccumulation — terms and examples | from: S12
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Bioaccumulation** | toxin builds up inside one organism over its life faster than it is excreted |
| **Biomagnification** | toxin's concentration **rises at each higher trophic level**, peaking in top predators |

- **Condition:** only persistent, fat-soluble, non-biodegradable pollutants (DDT, mercury, PCBs) biomagnify

| **Pollutant** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **DDT** | thinning of bird eggshells in fish-eating birds; seabird population crashes |
| **Mercury** | **methylmercury** (methyl mercury) concentrates in apex predators such as dolphins and orcas; caused **Minamata disease** in Japan |
| **Oil spills** | toxic hydrocarbons accumulate up marine food chains |

## Biotic interactions → sign → example | from: S13
- **Intraspecific:** same species (reproduction, help, competition for food/space)
- **Interspecific:** different species - beneficial, harmful or neutral
- **Symbiosis vs antagonism:** symbiosis = one or both partners benefit; **antagonism** = at least one harmed

| **Interaction** | **Sign (A/B)** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Mutualism** | +/+ | **Lichen** (alga + fungus); **fig and fig wasp** (pollination for breeding site); mycorrhiza |
| **Commensalism** | +/0 | Orchid epiphyte on mango (**epiphytes** on trees); **cattle egret** feeding near grazing cattle; barnacles on whales |
| **Parasitism** | +/- | Cuscuta on host (hedge) plants; ticks and lice |
| Predation | +/- | Tiger and deer; sea star Pisaster eating mussels |
| **Competition** | -/- | Species using the same limited resource; Gause's competitive exclusion principle |
| **Amensalism** | -/0 | **Allelopathic** chemicals; large tree shading a small plant; **Penicillium** antibiosis (inhibiting bacteria) |
| Neutralism | 0/0 | - |

- **Resource partitioning:** lets competing species coexist - e.g. insect pests feeding on different parts of the same plant
- **Coevolved plant-pollinator pairs:**
  - **fig - fig wasp**
  - **yucca - yucca moth**
  - **Ophrys orchid - bee** (sexual deceit)
- ⚠ Commensalism = **+/0** (cattle egret); amensalism = **-/0** (Penicillium)

## Species roles → meaning → example | from: S14
| **Role / Species type** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Keystone** | Effect far larger than its abundance (numbers); removal collapses community | Tiger, sea otter, fig; **Pisaster ochraceus** sea star controls mussels in intertidal zones (APPSC 2024; Robert Paine) |
| Foundation | Creates/builds or shapes/defines the habitat itself | Corals, kelp, dominant trees |
| **Flagship** | Charismatic species that draws public support | Tiger, giant panda |
| **Indicator** | Signals environmental health (shows environmental condition) | **Lichens** (SO2 pollution), amphibians |
| **Umbrella** | Wide-ranging; protecting it protects many others | Tiger, snow leopard |

- **Community structure:** shaped by foundation and keystone species (APPSC 2024 match: 1-c, 2-a, 3-b, 4-d)

## Ecotone and edge effect — meaning and places | from: S16
- **Ecotone:** zone of transition where two communities/biomes/ecosystems meet and integrate; conditions intermediate
  - Can be **narrow** (grassland-forest) or **wide** (forest-desert)

| **Ecotone** | **Between** |
|---|---|
| Mangroves | marine and terrestrial |
| Estuary | fresh water and salt water |
| Grassland | forest and desert |
| Riverbank / marshland | dry and wet |

- **Not an ecotone:** a uniform stand of one forest type is a single community, **not** an ecotone
- **Edge effect:** more species (richer species density) + higher density of some species in the ecotone than in either community [GK]
  - Such species = **edge species**
  - e.g. bird density higher in the forest-desert ecotone

## Ecosystem types — classification, forests and deserts | from: S17, S18
# 1. Classification
- **Natural:** mostly free of / grow without much human disturbance (forests, grasslands, oceans, lakes, deserts)
- **Artificial / man-modified:** man-made - crop fields, urban parks, villages, industrial estates; **city** = extreme example
- **Other splits:** freshwater vs marine, biome-based
- **Natural split:** **terrestrial** (tundra, forest, grassland, desert, mountain) vs **aquatic**; aquatic split by salinity:

| **Class** | **Salinity** | **Examples** |
|---|---|---|
| Freshwater | **< 5 ppt** | Lotic: springs, streams, rivers; Lentic: ponds, lakes, bogs, swamps |
| Brackish | **5-35 ppt** | Estuaries, salt marshes, mangrove swamps |
| Marine | **35 ppt or more** | Open ocean, shallow seas, coral reefs |

# 2. Forests
- **Types:** **tropical rainforest**, tropical deciduous (monsoon), temperate deciduous, **taiga / boreal** (evergreen coniferous)
- **Rainforest:** highest species richness on land; fast litter decay → thin, nutrient-poor soil
- **Taiga:** slow decay, thick litter, acidic soil
- **Oxygen vs carbon:** mature forests give little net oxygen (respiration balances photosynthesis) but are vital **carbon sinks**
# 3. Deserts
- **Hot deserts:** the Thar (western Rajasthan, Kutch), Sahara
- **Cold deserts:** Ladakh, the Gobi
- **Common feature:** low precipitation
- **Flora:** cacti and succulents, thorny species (the Indian arid zone, 2026); deep roots, small or spiny leaves, waxy cuticle
- **Fauna:** camels, lizards, snakes, scorpions; mostly nocturnal and water-saving

## Aquatic ecosystems — freshwater, marine, estuaries | from: S20, S21, S22
# 1. Freshwater
- **Extent:** about **0.8%** of Earth's surface (also cited as 1.8%); holds **0.009%** of its water
- **Lentic vs lotic:**
  - **Lentic** = still water (lakes, ponds, pools, ditches, marshes)
  - **Lotic** = flowing water (springs, streams, rivers)

| **Lake zone** | **Features** |
|---|---|
| **Littoral** | Shallow edge; light reaches the bottom; **rooted plants**[GK] |
| Limnetic | Open sunlit water down to where light fades; dominated by plankton [GK] |
| Profundal | Deep dark water below the limnetic zone; no photosynthesis; decomposers [GK] |

- **Trophic status:**
  - **oligotrophic** (nutrient-poor, clear, oxygen-rich)
  - mesotrophic
  - **eutrophic** (nutrient-rich, algal blooms, low oxygen at depth)
- ⚠ **Lentic** = still; **lotic** = flowing
# 2. Marine
| **Zone** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Disphotic | Twilight layer (about 200-1,000 m); too little light for photosynthesis [GK] |
| Aphotic | Permanently dark below about 1,000 m [GK] |
| Pelagic | The open water column; its top layer, the **epipelagic**, holds the most marine habitats [GK] |
| Benthic | The sea floor and its organisms [GK] |

- **Plankton:** phytoplankton = producers of the sea; zooplankton = primary consumers
- **Coral reefs:** the **'rainforests of the sea'** (highest species richness, along with rainforests)
  - Also break storm waves and prevent coastal erosion
  - A-R: both statements true, but II does not explain I
- **Hydrothermal vents:** deep-sea marine habitats; food chains rest on **chemosynthetic bacteria**, not sunlight
> Exam angle: "which does NOT belong to marine ecosystems": rivers and freshwater lakes do not; coral reefs, hydrothermal vents and tidal zones do.
# 3. Estuaries and mangroves
- **Estuary:** where a river meets the sea; an ecotone between freshwater and saltwater
  - Salinity rises from near-fresh upstream to **35 ppt** at the mouth
- **Climate-resilience role:** mangroves buffer cyclones and storm surges, protect coasts from erosion, store carbon
- **Major Indian estuaries:**
  - East coast: Hooghly, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery
  - West coast: Narmada, Tapi

## Plant adaptations to habitat — halophytes, xerophytes, hydrophytes | from: S24
| **Group** | **Habitat** | **Adaptations** [GK] |
|---|---|---|
| **Halophytes** | Saline soils, estuaries, mangroves | **Pneumatophores** (breathing roots), **vivipary** (seeds germinate on the tree), salt-excreting glands, succulent leaves - e.g. Rhizophora |

- ⚠ **Pneumatophores**, **vivipary** = halophytes; **Aerenchyma** = hydrophytes; **CAM** = xerophytes

## Universal angles | from: S27 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - The most-tested static block of Environment - 135 PYQs; statement and sequence questions dominate
  - Ecosystem function (energy flow + nutrient cycling) underlies productivity and ecosystem services
  - The 10% law explains why food chains are short and why a plant-based diet supports more people per hectare

## Biogeochemical cycles — basics, types and human disruption | from: S2, S5
# 1. Basics
- **Recycling:** nutrients recycled indefinitely between living organisms, air and the Earth's crust; energy is not
- **Driver:** energy flow; all cycles linked to the water cycle
- **Body mass:** **C, H, O, N and P** make up about **97%**
- **Common components:** inputs, internal cycling, outputs
- **Decomposers:** removing them would **block mineral movement** - nutrients locked in dead matter never return to the soil (APPSC 2025)
# 2. Types by main reservoir
- **gaseous** (atmosphere and oceans) vs **sedimentary** (Earth's crust)

| **Type** | **Cycles** | **Main reservoir** |
|---|---|---|
| Gaseous | Carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, water | Atmosphere / ocean |
| Sedimentary | **Phosphorus, sulphur, calcium**, potassium | Earth's crust: soil and rocks |

- **Nitrogen cycle:** the one whose main reservoir is NOT the soil (atmosphere, **~78% N2**) (APPSC-G2 2025)
- **Phosphorus** cycle: no atmospheric phase; released by **weathering of rocks**; ends in ocean sediments
# 3. Water cycle regulation - forests and wetlands
- **Role of water:** Carries elements through the ecosystem; biogeochemical cycles would stop if water did not cycle
- **Forests:** Regulate water flow, conserve soil and moisture, recharge groundwater, moderate floods
- **Wetlands:** Act as sponges - store floodwater, recharge aquifers, sustain lean-season flow
# 4. Human disruption - factor → effect
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Fossil fuels** | Burning fossil fuels + deforestation raise atmospheric CO2 → climate change |
| **Fertiliser runoff** | **N and P** → **algal blooms / eutrophication** |
| **Chemical pesticides** | Alter soils and microbial networks, weaken resilience |

## Carbon and nitrogen cycles — processes → agents | from: S3, S4
# 1. Carbon cycle
- **CO2 added by:** **Respiration, decomposition, combustion** (of fossil fuels) + volcanic activity (volcanoes), deforestation
- **CO2 removed by:** **Photosynthesis** (plants, algae, some bacteria) + dissolving in the ocean (dissolution in oceans), carbonate sedimentation, silicate weathering
- **Reservoirs and sinks:**
  - Atmosphere, **oceans**, forests and soils
  - Long-term: carbonate rocks and fossil fuels
  - Forests = vital **carbon sinks**
# 2. Nitrogen cycle
- **N added to soil by:**
  - Biological fixation (enzyme **nitrogenase**)
  - **Lightning** (atmospheric fixation)
  - Industrial fixation (fertilisers)
  - Ammonification of dead matter
  - Nitrification → makes nitrates
- **N removed:** **Denitrification** returns N2 to air

| **Process** | **What happens** | **Agents** [GK] |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixation** | N2 in air becomes ammonia or nitrate | Rhizobium in **legume root nodules**; Azotobacter; cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc); lightning; industrial Haber process |
| **Ammonification** | Organic N in dead matter becomes ammonia | Decomposer bacteria and fungi |
| **Nitrification** | Ammonia becomes nitrite, then nitrate | Nitrosomonas, then Nitrobacter |
| **Denitrification** | Nitrate becomes N2 gas, returned to air | Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus |

- **Net effect:** Fixation, ammonification and nitrification **add** usable N to soil; **denitrification removes** it
- ⚠ Nitrification = **Nitrosomonas → Nitrobacter**; denitrification = **Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus**

## Ecological succession — terms, persons and types | from: S6, S7
# 1. Terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Succession** | gradual, orderly, sequential replacement of one community by another in an area over time; change in species composition |
| **Pioneer species / community** | first hardy colonisers |
| **Seral communities** | intermediate stages; whole sequence = **sere**; each step = seral stage |
| **Climax community** | final stable community, in equilibrium with (set by) climate (forest, grassland, coral reef) |
| **Seasonal succession** | cyclic change driven by environmental rhythms, not by a disturbance (disaster) |

# 2. Persons → theory
| **Person** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| Odum (1971) | Succession = an orderly process of community change within a unit area |
| **Frederic Clements (1916)** | First proposed the sequence of phases; **monoclimax** (one climax per climatic region) |
| Tansley | **polyclimax** |
| Whittaker | **climax-pattern** |

# 3. Primary vs secondary
| **Primary** | **Secondary** |
|---|---|
| Begins with no life, on a new area that never had life (lava, bare rock, new rock, volcanic island) | Follows the removal of an existing community, destroyed by fire, flood, logging |
| No soil | Soil already present |
| Lichens and mosses first | Seeds and roots already present |
| Slower, low biomass | Faster, more common, higher biomass |

- **Grasslands:** do not turn into forest because of **water limits and fire**
# 4. Seres by starting medium
| **Type** | **Starts on** |
|---|---|
| Hydrosere | Water (pond, lake) |
| Xerosere | Dry land (lithosere and psammosere are sub-types) |
| Lithosere | Bare rock |
| Psammosere | Sand |
| Halosere | Saline soil/water [GK] |

- ⚠ Lithosere = **rock**; psammosere = **sand**; halosere = **saline**

## Succession — Clements' phases and sere sequences | from: S6, S7
# 1. Phases (Clements)
| **Phase** | **What happens** |
|---|---|
| 1. **Nudation** | Bare area forms (erosion, landslide, volcanic activity, fire) |
| 2. **Invasion** | Migration of seeds/spores, **ecesis** (successful establishment), **aggregation** |
| 3. **Competition and co-action** | Intra- and inter-specific competition; unfit eliminated |
| 4. **Reaction** | Organisms modify soil, water, light, temperature - the **most important** stage |
| 5. **Stabilisation** | Climax community |

# 2. Xerosere
- **Bare rock pioneers:** **lichens** = symbiotic association of **algae and fungi**
- **Xerosere (bare rock or dry land):** crustose lichens → foliose lichens → mosses → herbs and grasses → shrubs → forest climax
  - Scrub attracts birds whose droppings and seeds speed colonisation
  - Pioneer trees (birch, oak) need high light; shade-tolerant species form the climax
# 3. Hydrosere
- **Hydrosere (lakes):** phytoplankton → submerged rooted plants → floating plants (duckweed Lemna, Pistia; the alga Chara) → reed-swamp → sedge-meadow (marsh) → woodland → forest
  - Sedge peat above water level invaded by alders and willows
  - Ends in wet woodland = **fen carr**
  - Lake sediments keep a permanent record → hydroseres better studied than xeroseres
