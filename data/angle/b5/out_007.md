## Orbits and launch physics — terms → meaning | from: S3, S8
# 1. Orbit terms

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| LEO | Low Earth Orbit (up to ~2,000 km) [GK] |
| SSO | Sun-Synchronous (polar) Orbit - PSLV's speciality |
| GTO | Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit - GSLV, LVM3 |
| MEO | Medium Earth Orbit (e.g., GPS) [GK] |

- **GTO:** Elliptical transfer orbit, apogee near **36,000 km**
- **GSO:** Period = one day
- **GEO:** = GSO over the equator → fixed in the sky
- **Sun-synchronous orbit:** Near-polar; passes each place at the same local solar time (imaging)
- **Strap-on boosters:** Add lift-off thrust to the core
- **Apogee vs perigee:** **apogee** = farthest point; **perigee** = nearest
# 2. Launch direction
- **Eastward launch:** Rockets launched **eastward** gain Earth's rotational speed → saves fuel
  - Sites nearer the **equator** gain the most
- **Sriharikota location:** East coast → rockets fly over the sea, away from populated land
- **Polar/SSO launches:** Go **southward**
  - From Sriharikota → need a dog-leg around Sri Lanka
  - Hence the southern SSLV site at Kulasekarapattinam

=== ROW 36 | Propulsion and future vehicles - cryogenic engine, semi-cryogenic, reusable launch vehicle, scramjet

## Rocket propellants — types → example and feature | from: S0

| **Category** | **Example** | **Feature** |
|---|---|---|
| Solid | **HTPB** (PSLV/GSLV first stage, S200) | High initial thrust; cannot be throttled or stopped; Isp ~260 s |
| Earth-storable liquid | UH25/N2O4; hydrazine, MMH, UDMH | Throttleable, restartable; Isp ~290-320 s |
| Hypergolic | Hydrazine family + N2O4 | Ignite on contact - ideal for spacecraft manoeuvring; highly toxic |
| Cryogenic | LH2 + LOX | Most efficient; complex |
| Semi-cryogenic | Kerosene + LOX | Dense, cheaper, non-toxic |
| Hybrid | Solid fuel + liquid oxidiser | Can be stopped/restarted; rarely built for large thrust |

- ⚠ **Cryogenic** = LH2 + LOX; **Semi-cryogenic** = kerosene + LOX; **Hypergolic** = ignite on contact

## Cryogenic technology — propellants, Indian programme, Russian deal | from: S1
# 1. Propellants and properties
- **Propellants:** Fuel **liquid hydrogen (-253°C)** + oxidiser **liquid oxygen (-183°C)** (UPPSC 2004)
- **Exhaust:** Only water vapour
- **Specific impulse:** ~**450 s** (highest)
- **LH2 density:** Only **0.071 g/ml** → huge tanks; hard to store long → unsuited to military missiles
- **Uses:** **Rocket upper stages**/space shuttle (UPSC 1995, UPPSC 1993)
  - Discovery's tanks held LH2 + LOX (UPPSC 2006)
# 2. Indian programme → year

| **Step** | **Year / fact** |
|---|---|
| **Cryogenic Upper Stage Project (CUSP)** | Begun **1994**, cost **₹300 crore** |
| **Full cryogenic stage test** | **2006** (UPSC 2007 - statement 1 correct) |
| **GSLV Mk II** with indigenous cryo stage (CE-7.5) | Flew on **5 Jan 2014** |

- ⚠ **Trap:** India is not the fourth - Europe/France and Japan also had it
# 3. Russian deal and MTCR
- **Glavkosmos deal (1991):** Agreed to sell engines + technology

- **Outcome:** Russia supplied **7 engines** only

## Scramjet and air-breathing propulsion — ramjet vs scramjet, ATV, HSTDV | from: S5

| **Engine** | **Compression** | **Combustion** | **Speed** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ramjet | Forward speed, no compressor | Subsonic airflow | Above **Mach 2** |

=== ROW 37 | Communication satellites - INSAT and GSAT series, applications, transponder capacity

## Geostationary orbit — terms and features | from: S0
- **Altitude:** About **35,786 km** (~36,000 km) above the **equator**
  - At a **fixed height**, not dependent on the satellite's mass (CGPSC 2018)
- **Period:** **24 hours** (UPPSC 2016)
- **Motion:** **West to east**, in the **equatorial plane** (zero inclination) → looks stationary
- **Placement:** Launchers put satellites into elliptical **GTO** → satellite's own motor raises it to GEO
- ⚠ GEO height does **not** depend on satellite mass
