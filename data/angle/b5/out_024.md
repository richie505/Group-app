=== ROW 84 | Biodiversity - levels, values, measurement; India as a megadiverse country

## Biodiversity — definition, levels and FIRSTs | from: S0, S3
# 1. Definition
- **CBD (1992) definition:** Variability among living organisms from all sources (terrestrial, marine, other aquatic) + ecological complexes they belong to
  - Covers diversity **within species (genetic)**, **between species** and **of ecosystems** (= **within species, between species and of ecosystems**)
- **CBD's three goals:** **Conservation** + **sustainable use** + **fair and equitable sharing** of benefits from genetic resources
# 2. Levels
| **Level** | **Meaning / example** |
|---|---|
| **Genetic** | Variation within a species = basic source of biodiversity; all rice varieties are Oryza sativa but differ in size, aroma, nutrients |
| **Species** | Variety of species in an area (richness + abundance) |
| **Ecosystem** | Variety of ecosystems (rainforest, deciduous, boreal); one ecosystem cannot replace another (boreal conifers ≠ tropical deciduous trees) |
- **Genetic example:** Wild rice Oryza rufipogon (progenitor of cultivated rice) - see Agro-biodiversity
# 3. FIRSTs / richest
| **FIRST / richest** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| Term **"biodiversity"** first coined | **coined by W.G. Rosen** (**Walter G. Rosen**, 1985) (UKPSC 2025) |
| Richest ecosystem on land (PYQs 2017 and 2023) | **tropical rainforests (tropical moist forests)** = **tropical rain / moist forests** |
| Richest ecosystem in the sea | **coral reefs** |
- ⚠ **Trap:** Taiga, deserts and temperate forests are the usual wrong options

## Terms → meaning: measuring biodiversity | from: S1, S3
| **Measure** | **Meaning / scale** |
|---|---|
| **Alpha** | Diversity within one area, community or ecosystem (species richness there) |
| **Beta** | Change (turnover) in species between ecosystems/habitats, along a habitat gradient |
| **Gamma** | Total (overall) diversity across the ecosystems of a whole region or landscape |
| Species richness | Number of species |
| Species evenness | Relative abundance (how equally individuals are spread); low evenness = a few species dominate |
- **Point richness:** Species count at a single point
- **Richness vs evenness example:** Forest A (2 tigers, 6 deer, 7 rabbits) more even than B (1, 6, 8); both 3 species + 15 individuals
- **Indices:** **Shannon-Wiener** index + **Simpson** index (species diversity)
- **Functional diversity:** Range of ecological roles
  - Functional **richness**: niches occupied
  - **Evenness**: abundance spread across niches
  - **Divergence**: dominance of extreme traits
# Species-area relationship
- **Rule:** Species richness rises with area, but only up to a limit
- **Equation:** log S = log C + Z log A
  - **Z is 0.1-0.2** for most areas
  - **0.6-1.2** for very large areas (whole continents)
- ⚠ Alpha = within; Beta = between (turnover); Gamma = whole region

## Species richness patterns — features and causes | from: S3
# Gradients
- **Latitudinal gradient:** Biodiversity **highest in the tropics** → falls from the **tropics toward the poles**
- **Altitude:** Falls from **lower to higher altitudes** on mountains
- **Ant example:** About **10 species** locally near the North Pole vs up to **2,000** at the equator
# Why the tropics are richest
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Evolutionary time** | Long, **undisturbed evolutionary time** (no glaciation) |
| **Climate** | Less seasonal, more stable/predictable → niche specialisation |
| **Energy** | More solar energy → higher **productivity** |
| **Other factors** | Moderate disturbance + habitat heterogeneity also raise diversity |
- **Himalayas are rich:** **Meeting point of different biogeographical zones**
> Exam angle: A/R that butterflies peak in the tropics because they cannot tolerate low temperature: both true, and R explains A (UPPSC 2019).

## Values of biodiversity — types and hypothesis | from: S4
| **Value** | **Meaning / example** |
|---|---|
| Consumptive use [GK] | used directly by local people, not sold: food, fuelwood, fodder, wild food, medicine |
| Productive use [GK] | sold commercially: timber, fibre, medicines, drugs, gums, genetic material for crops |
| Ecosystem services | clean air and water, crop pollination, climate regulation, resilience |
| Social / cultural | sacred species and groves, indigenous knowledge, religious significance, livelihoods |
| Ethical / existence [GK] | every species has a right to exist |
| Aesthetic | natural beauty, recreation, ecotourism |
| Option [GK] | unknown possible future uses, such as undiscovered medicines (new drugs) and crop genes |
- ⚠ Consumptive = used, **not sold**; Productive = **sold commercially**
- **Role in sustainable development (APPSC 2025):** **Supports ecosystem services such as food, water and air quality**
- **Most significant aspect (UPPSC 2015):** **Maintenance of the ecosystem**
# Rivet popper hypothesis (Paul Ehrlich)
- **Analogy:** Ecosystem = aeroplane; species = rivets
  - Losing a few rivets seems harmless but each loss weakens it
  - Losing a key rivet (keystone species) can bring it down

## Biodiversity hotspot — definition, criteria and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
# Definition
- **Definition:** Biogeographic region with large reservoir of biodiversity **threatened with destruction**
- **Proposer:** **Norman Myers**, **1988**
# Criteria (both needed)
- **Criterion 1:** At least **1,500 endemic vascular plant species** (over **0.5%** of world total)
- **Criterion 2:** Must have **lost at least 70%** of its original habitat
- **APPSC-G2 2017 answer:** Hotspots characterised by **endemic flowering plants and threat perception**
# Types
| **Type** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Hottest hotspots** | 8 hotspots with highest endemism under greatest threat; include **Western Ghats-Sri Lanka** |
| **Coldspots** | Areas of relatively **low diversity** losing habitat fast; still matter for rare species, key ecosystem functions, genetic diversity |
| Temperate hotspots | Mediterranean Basin, California Floristic Province, North American Coastal Plain |
- **Distribution:** **Not only tropical**
  - Most lie in tropics; South-East Asian islands among the richest
# FIRSTs and timeline
| **Year** | **Event** |
|---|---|
| **1988** | Norman Myers proposed the concept |
| **1989** | **Conservation International (CI)** adopted it |
| **1999** | Numerical (quantitative) thresholds (published by Myers et al., 2000); **25** hotspots named |
| **2004** | Reworked analysis (current system) |
| - | Count: **25** named in **1999** → 34 → 35 → 36 |
| **2011** | **35th** (Forests of East Australia) |
| **2016** | **36th** (**North American Coastal Plain**) |

## Access and benefit sharing — concept, Kani model, earnings, fund rules | from: S9
# Concept
- **ABS:** Commercial benefits from bioresources shared with providing communities
  - Covers plant, animal, microbial resources + traditional knowledge
  - **Excludes human genetic material**
- **Timeline:** CBD introduced it 1992 → Nagoya Protocol operationalised it 2010
# Kani model (first well-known benefit-sharing model)
| **Item** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| Community | Kani tribe of Kerala |
| Plant | **Arogyapacha** (Trichopus zeylanicus) |
| Institute | TBGRI, Thiruvananthapuram |
| Drug | anti-fatigue drug **"Jeevani"** |
| Sharing | Licence fee + royalty shared with a Kani trust |
# Earnings and rules
- **NBA's ABS has raised over ₹266 crore since 2008:**
  - **Red Sanders** = **45% (₹120 crore)**
  - Seed sector = 32.3%
- **New fund rules (NBA):**
  - Identifiable repository → **25-40%** of ABS funds to it + **60-75%** to local communities via SBBs/UTBCs
  - Untraceable origin → money goes to conservation
- **ABS Regulations 2025:** Cover **Digital Sequence Information**
- **APSBB:**
  - Plans **first-of-its-kind Marine ABS framework** for fishermen
  - Will strengthen about **13,000 BMCs**
  - NBA sanctioned **₹82 lakh** for Red Sanders conservation

## Hotspots vs Key Biodiversity Areas vs Global 200 ecoregions | from: S10
| **Concept** | **Body** | **Basis** |
|---|---|---|
| Biodiversity hotspot | Conservation International (Myers) | endemic plants of 1,500 or more **and** 70% or more of habitat lost; large regions |
| Key Biodiversity Area (KBA) [GK] | IUCN-led partnership (global standard, 2016) | individual **sites** that contribute significantly to the global persistence of biodiversity |
| Global 200 [GK] | **WWF** | priority **ecoregions** (terrestrial, freshwater, marine) chosen for representativeness, not only threat |
