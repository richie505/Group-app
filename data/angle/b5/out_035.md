## Bioremediation — definition, agents, objective and what is NOT bioremediation | from: S7
- **Definition:** Any process using **micro-organisms or their enzymes** to return a contaminated environment to its original condition
  - "Removal of pollutants from the biosphere by biological processes" (APPSC-GS 2019)
  - Microbes (bacteria, fungi) or enzymes degrade contaminants in **soil and groundwater** to less toxic forms
- **Primary objective:** **Neutralising pollutants in contaminated soil and groundwater** using micro-organisms (APPSC-G2 2025)
- **Agents:** **Bacteria, fungi, algae and plants**; microbes can be genetically engineered for the job
- **Pollutants treated:** Soil, water and air - oil spills, heavy metals, pesticides (e.g. chlorinated pesticides), solvents, organic pollutants
- **Eutrophication control:** **Eutrophication control** - excess nutrients and algal blooms in lakes and reservoirs
- **Nodal R&D body:** **CSIR-NEERI** for bioremediation in India (Jun 2026)

| **Method** | **Type** | **Bioremediation?** |
|---|---|---|
| Microbial degradation, composting organic waste | Biological | Yes |
| Phytoremediation, constructed wetlands | Biological | Yes |
| Burning contaminated soil, incineration (thermal destruction) | Thermal | No (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| Chemical treatment (e.g. chemical dust suppressants such as calcium/magnesium chloride) | Chemical | No |
| Desalination, excavation and landfilling [GK] | Physical | No |

- **APPSC-GS 2022:** Breaking down organic waste with bacteria = **bioremediation**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Distractors - desalination, fossilisation, incineration
- ⚠ **Trap:** "Which is NOT bioremediation?" = **burning contaminated soil**; composting and bacterial oil clean-up ARE bioremediation
> Exam angle: "Which is NOT bioremediation?" - burning contaminated soil; composting and bacterial oil clean-up are.

## In-situ vs ex-situ bioremediation — site, techniques, cost | from: S8
| **Basis** | **In-situ** | **Ex-situ** |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Treated **at the contaminated site** | Material **excavated/removed** and treated elsewhere, in beds or reactors |
| Techniques | **Bioventing**, **Biosparging**, **Bioaugmentation **(adding microbes) | Landfarming, composting, biopiles, bioreactors (slurry, aqueous) |
| Cost | Most cost-efficient; relatively passive | Landfarming/biopiles low cost; bioreactors high capital and operating cost (costliest) |
| Limits | Environmental constraints; long treatment time; metals/inorganics complicate | Space needs; abiotic loss; mass-transfer and bioavailability problems; excavation |
| Speed | Slow | Bioreactors fastest (optimised conditions, rapid kinetics) |

- **In-situ:** "Treats contaminated material directly at the site" (APPSC-G2 2025)
> Exam angle: in-situ vs ex-situ; match technique to agent.

## Bioremediation techniques → how → pollutant | from: S9
| **Technique** | **How** | **Typical target** |
|---|---|---|
| Bioaugmentation | Adds specific pollutant-degrading microbes | **Oil spills → bioaugmentation** (APPSC-G2 2025), petroleum hydrocarbons, pesticides, industrial chemicals |
| Biostimulation | Adds nutrients (N, P) and oxygen to boost native microbes | Polluted water bodies, soil |
| Bioventing | Air/nutrients supplied through wells to contaminated soil | Contaminated soil |
| Biosparging | Air injected under pressure below the water table | Groundwater |
| Phytoremediation | Plants absorb, accumulate, detoxify | Heavy metals in soil/water; water hyacinth, duckweed in ponds |
| Mycoremediation | Fungi | Organic pollutants [GK] |
| Rhizofiltration | Plant roots absorb or precipitate contaminants from water [GK] | Metals and radionuclides in water [GK] |
| Rhizoremediation | Roots stimulate microbes in the rhizosphere (root-zone microbes) | Organic pollutants, nutrient imbalance in soil |

- **Group-II table:**

| **Basis** | **Bioaugmentation** | **Biostimulation** |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | **Introduces** specific pollutant-degrading microbes (native consortia or GE strains) | **Stimulates existing** microbes with nutrients and oxygen |
| Example | Oilzapper on oil spills; petroleum hydrocarbons, pesticides | N and P addition to polluted lakes and ponds |

- ⚠ **Bioventing** = soil; **Biosparging** = groundwater (below the water table)

| Plastic | **Ideonella sakaiensis** degrades **PET plastic** (2026) | - |
| Pesticides, solvents | Bacterial degradation (e.g. chlorinated pesticides) | - |
| Sewage, drains, lakes | Biostimulation, aquatic plants, constructed wetlands | Visakhapatnam stormwater drain proposal (TH, 5 Jun 2026) |
| Mine waste, tailings | Rehabilitation of abandoned mines: effluent-treatment monitoring, legal framework, long-term monitoring | - |

## Bioremediation — advantages, limitations and debate vs conventional | from: S14
- **Advantages:**
  - Eco-friendly; few chemicals → no secondary pollution
  - Cheaper than conventional methods
  - In-situ methods cause little site disruption
  - Restores ecosystems
- **Limitations:**
  - **Slow**
  - Results vary with pollutant, organism, site conditions, co-contaminants; pollutant-specific
  - Metals/inorganics complicate
  - Ex-situ needs space + excavation
  - Needs regulation and monitoring

| **Basis** | **Bioremediation** | **Conventional (thermal, chemical, excavation)** |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Low; "fraction of the funds" | High (why conventional clean-up is failing - LENS, Dec 2025) |
| Time | Slow | Faster |
| Scale | Scalable, community-applied | Large infrastructure |
| Side effects | Minimal chemicals, no secondary pollution | Residues, emissions |
| Reliability | Varies with pollutant, organism, site, co-contaminants | Less dependent on site biology [GK] |

=== ROW 110 | Precision agriculture, drip irrigation and other resource-efficient technologies

## Micro-irrigation terms → meaning: drip vs sprinkler, fertigation | from: S0
# 1. Drip vs sprinkler
- **Localized irrigation:** Low-pressure piped water applied as small discharge near each plant - drip, micro-sprinkler/spray, bubbler irrigation

| **Feature** | **Drip** | **Sprinkler** |
|---|---|---|
| How water is applied | Directly to the root zone through emitters on lateral pipes | Under pressure through pipes and nozzles, simulating rainfall |
| Strength | Most efficient; minimises evaporation and runoff | Useful on uneven terrain; minimises wastage |
| Limitation | Higher initial investment | More loss to wind and evaporation than drip [GK] |

- **Drip vs sprinkler:** Both cut evaporation + runoff losses; suit water-scarce areas (PYQ keys: drip irrigation, micro-irrigation)
- **Water-use efficiency (approx.):** Drip **~90%**; sprinkler ~70-80%; flood/furrow ~30-50%
- **Advantages:** Water, labour, power and fertiliser savings; APMIP reports **30-40% higher productivity** (2025-26)
> Exam angle: "which technology suits water-scarce regions?" - drip irrigation (APPSC-G2 2025).
# 2. Fertigation
- **Fertigation:** Fertiliser applied through the irrigation water (delivers nutrients through the drip line)
  - PDMC explicitly promotes drip/sprinkler with fertigation
- **Advantages (UPSC 2020):**
  - Alkalinity of irrigation water can be controlled
  - **More nutrients available** to plants
  - **Less leaching** of chemical nutrients
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Efficient application of rock phosphate is NOT an advantage

## Resource-conserving technologies → benefit | from: S3
| **Technology** | **Benefit tested in exams** |
|---|---|
| Zero tillage | Wheat sown without burning previous residue; paddy seed planted directly without nursery; soil carbon sequestration (UPSC 2020: all three) |
| Reduced/zero tillage + leaving crop residue | Conserve water in agriculture (UPSC 2017); gypsum before irrigation does NOT |
| System of Rice Intensification (alternate wetting and drying) | Less seed, **less methane**, less electricity (UPSC 2022: all three) |
| Direct-seeded rice | No nursery or transplanting; promoted with zero-till wheat under NICRA (CDI) |
| Laser land levelling | Uniform field surface so water spreads evenly; saves irrigation water [GK] |
| Sustainable Sugarcane Initiative | Single-bud chips raised in nursery, wider spacing, less seed cane and water [GK] |

- **Rice GHG cut:** Improved irrigation + optimised fertiliser + residue management + reduced tillage → **~10%** cut in global rice GHG emissions by mid-century (study, 26 Jul 2026)

## Protected cultivation and soilless farming → technique → key point | from: S4
| **Technique** | **What it is** | **Key point** |
|---|---|---|
| Protected cultivation (greenhouse, polytunnel, shade net) | Crops grown in a controlled environment | Protection from weather, pests, disease; year-round crop; tomatoes, cucumbers, flowers |
| Hydroponics | Soilless; roots in nutrient-rich water | Saves water and space; precise nutrient control; leafy greens, herbs |
