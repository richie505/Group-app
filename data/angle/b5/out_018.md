=== ROW 67 | Other renewables - biomass, biofuels (National Policy on Biofuels, ethanol blending), small hydro, geothermal, tidal, waste-to-energy

## Other renewables — definitions, classification and types | from: S0, S1, S4, S5, S6, S10, S11
# 1. MNRE classification
- **MNRE RE:** **Solar, wind, bio-power (biomass, bagasse cogeneration, waste-to-energy), small hydro** + **large hydro** (since 2019)
- **Under development:** Geothermal, tidal, OTEC
- **Nuclear:** Non-fossil but **not renewable** (8.78 GW counted separately)
# 2. Biomass energy and biogas
- **Biomass energy:** Solar energy stored by plants in **photosynthesis**; burned directly or converted to biogas/biofuels
  - **Gobar gas is biomass energy** (APPSC 2019)
- **Agricultural waste - includes:** Crop residue (stubble, paddy-husk), manure, cattle dung, dairy waste - all biodegradable
- !! Not in your sources: annual crop-residue / surplus residue volumes (MNRE estimates).
- **Biogas:** Mixture of **methane + CO2** + traces of other gases, by **anaerobic digestion** of organic matter
  - **Methane share:** Major share, ~**50-75%**
  - **Microbes:** **Methanogenic archaea** / **methanogenic bacteria (methanogens)** do the final methane-forming step, in the absence of oxygen
  - **By-product:** Slurry left behind = good organic manure
- **Gobar gas:** Biogas from cattle dung
# 3. Biofuel generations (1G to 4G)
| **Generation** | **Feedstock** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **1G** | Food crops - sugar, starch, vegetable oils: sugarcane, molasses, corn, cassava (UPSC) | Bioethanol by Saccharomyces cerevisiae; biodiesel |
| **2G** | Non-food lignocellulosic residue - crop residue, straw, husk, wood chips, bamboo, forest waste, non-edible oils (UPSC) | Cellulosic ethanol (2G plants on the places page) |
| **3G** | **Algae** (algal biomass) and micro-organisms (PYQ 2023 answer: algal biomass; APPSC-GS 2023 key) | Algal biodiesel, biobutanol |
| **4G** | **Genetically modified algae/microbes** (genetically engineered) that capture more CO2 (RPSC 2024 key) | Carbon-negative fuels |

- **NPB 2018 categories:**
  - **Basic biofuels** = 1G bioethanol, biodiesel
  - **Advanced biofuels** = 2G ethanol, drop-in fuels from MSW, 3G biofuels, bio-CNG
- ⚠ **3G** = algae; **4G** = genetically modified algae/microbes
# 4. Small hydro - size classes
- **Cut-off:** **Small hydro = up to 25 MW**
  - Micro = **up to 100 kW**
  - Mini = **101 kW-2 MW**
  - Small = **2-25 MW**
# 5. Geothermal - origin
- **Source of heat:** **Earth's internal heat** - heat of the Earth's interior (largely from radioactive decay)
- **Surface expressions:** Hot springs, steam, geysers, hot dry rock, **volcanoes** (APPSC 2018 key: "volcanoes")

## Terms and technologies → meaning | from: S2, S3, S5, S7, S9, S11, S12
# 1. Fuels and processes
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Compressed Biogas (CBG) - definition** | Purified, compressed biogas, **chemically identical to CNG**; renewable, carbon-neutral; promoted under SATAT |
| **Bagasse cogeneration** | Sugar mills burn bagasse (cane residue) → steam + power together |
| Biodiesel (**Definition**) | Methyl or ethyl **ester of fatty acids** from edible/non-edible vegetable oils or animal fat |
| Biodiesel **Process** | **Transesterification** of vegetable oils/animal fats with methanol/ethanol |
| **Gasohol** | Petrol (gasoline) + ethanol blend, typically **~10% ethanol** |
| **Power alcohol** | Fuel-grade (anhydrous) ethanol used as motor fuel; in India mainly from **molasses** (sugar-industry by-product) |
| **Bioethanol** | Fermentation of sugar/starch by yeast **Saccharomyces cerevisiae** |
| **Biobutanol** | By Clostridium acetobutylicum - higher energy density than ethanol |
| **E85** | Used in **Flex-Fuel Vehicles** |

- Sugar mills may now make ethanol directly from **cane juice**
- **1G vs 2G:** 1G = food-fuel conflict; 2G avoids it but needs complex thermochemical/biochemical conversion
# 2. Waste-to-energy routes
| **WtE route / technology** | **Process / principle** |
|---|---|
| Incineration | Direct burning of MSW for heat/power |
| Pyrolysis | Heating in **absence** of oxygen → biochar / char, bio-oil / oil, syngas |
| Gasification | Partial oxidation with **controlled** oxygen → syngas |
| Biomethanation | Microbes convert wet organic waste → biogas |
| Landfill gas recovery | Methane captured from landfills |
| RDF | Refuse-derived fuel = dry combustible part of segregated MSW |

- **Biomass gasification:** Biomass/waste heated with **controlled (limited) oxygen** → **syngas** (CO + H2 + CO2) for engines/power
  - Decentralised; uses farm residue; cuts stubble burning
- **Prerequisite:** Source segregation essential for both gasification and anaerobic digestion
- **Microbial fuel cell:** Bacteria oxidise organic matter + pass electrons to an electrode → electricity while treating wastewater
- ⚠ Pyrolysis = **absence** of oxygen; gasification = **controlled** oxygen
# 3. Geothermal and ocean terms
- **Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS):** Artificially stimulate reservoirs (UPSC notes)
- **OTEC principle:** Temperature difference between warm surface water and cold deep water runs a turbine
  - Needs ~**20 deg C** difference; viable in tropics (**30N-30S**)
  - Gives power + desalination for islands
- **Tidal criterion:** Large tidal range (roughly **5 m** or more) in a narrow gulf/estuary

=== ROW 68 | Green hydrogen - National Green Hydrogen Mission, electrolysers, applications

## Green hydrogen — colour types, definitions and standard | from: S0, S3, S4
# 1. Hydrogen colour codes
| **Colour** | **Production route / method** |
|---|---|
| Grey | Steam methane reforming of natural gas, CO2 released |
| Blue | Fossil route (grey hydrogen) + **carbon capture and storage** |
| **Green** | **Electrolysis of water** using renewable power (PYQ 2023); also biomass gasification |
| Turquoise | **Methane pyrolysis** - gives solid carbon (by-product) |
| Pink | Electrolysis powered by **nuclear** energy |
| White | Naturally occurring (geological) hydrogen |
| Brown / Black | Coal gasification - lignite / bituminous coal |

- **Zero-carbon:** Only **green** hydrogen
- **Cost:** Grey ($1.5-2/kg) still far cheaper than green (~$4/kg) (Jul 2026)
- ⚠ **Pink** = nuclear electrolysis; **Turquoise** = methane pyrolysis; **Blue** = fossil + CCS
# 2. Green Hydrogen Standard and certification
- **Green Hydrogen Standard (MNRE, Aug 2023):** Hydrogen is "green" if well-to-gate emissions **not more than 2 kg CO2-eq per kg H2** (12-month average)
- **Green Hydrogen Certification Scheme of India (GHCI):** Launched **April 2025**
  - **Certification:** **Bureau of Energy Efficiency** (BEE, Ministry of Power) = nodal authority
- **Standards for green ammonia and green methanol:** Announced / notified **Mar 2026**
  - Emission thresholds + eligibility; renewable-powered electrolysis
# 3. Electrolyser - meaning
- **Electrolyser:** Device that splits water into hydrogen + oxygen using electricity

## Technologies — electrolyser types, storage and fuel cells | from: S4, S5, S8
# 1. Electrolyser types
| **Type** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| **Alkaline** | Liquid KOH electrolyte; mature, low cost (cheap) [GK] |
| **PEM** | Solid polymer membrane; compact; quick response to (follows) variable RE [GK] |
| **Solid oxide** | High-temperature steam electrolysis; most efficient [GK] |
| AEM | Anion exchange membrane; avoids precious metals [GK] |

# 2. Storage and transport
| **Method** | **Key fact** |
|---|---|
| Compression | **Compressed gas** in high-pressure composite (Type-IV) cylinders/tanks (LENSD) |
| Liquefaction | **Liquid hydrogen at -253 °C** (cryogenic); very low density **0.071 g/ml** → large, bulky tanks (APP) |
| Metal hydrides | Solid **metal hydrides** - hydrogen absorbed in metal alloys, released on heating [GK] |
| Chemical carriers | **Ammonia**, methanol, LOHC carry hydrogen for export [GK] |

- **Hydrogen train:** Stores hydrogen in roof-mounted **Type-IV** composite cylinders
- **Safety body:** **National Centre for Hydrogen Safety** handles storage, transport and flammability risks
# 3. PEM fuel cell - principle
- **Anode:** H2 splits into protons + electrons - **H2 → 2H+ + 2e-**
- **External circuit:** Electrons flow → **electricity**; protons cross the membrane
- **Cathode:** **1/2 O2 + 2H+ + 2e- → H2O** + heat
- **No burning:** Hydrogen not burnt; chemical energy → electricity
- **Exhaust:** **Only water vapour and warm air** - zero tailpipe emissions

=== ROW 69 | Energy storage - battery technologies, PLI for advanced chemistry cells, grid storage

## Batteries — cell parts and types | from: S0
# 1. Cell parts
- **Cell parts:**
  - **Anode** = oxidation (loses electrons) during discharge
  - **Cathode** = reduction (gains electrons)
  - **Electrolyte** = lets ions flow, keeps electrodes apart (prevents direct contact)
# 2. Battery types → electrodes → uses
| **Chemistry** | **Electrodes / feature** | **Uses** |
|---|---|---|
| Lead-acid | Lead and **lead peroxide (PbO2)**, sulphuric acid electrolyte | Automotive / car starting, UPS, inverters, RE storage |
| **Nickel-Cadmium (Ni-Cd)** | **Nickel and cadmium** electrodes (PYQ 1995; UPSC 1995); rechargeable | **Calculators, cordless appliances, transistors**, torches, shavers (PYQ 2016; UKPSC 2016); now less common / declining - toxic cadmium |
| Nickel-Metal Hydride (NiMH) | Replaces cadmium with metal hydride | Hybrid vehicles, portable electronics / devices |
| Zinc-carbon (dry cell) | Zinc and carbon; **not rechargeable** | Torches, clocks |
| Lithium-ion | Graphite anode, lithium metal-oxide cathode | Phones, laptops, EVs, grid storage |
| Li-ion **NMC** | Nickel-manganese-cobalt cathode; high energy density, critical-mineral heavy (2026) | EVs, phones, laptops |
| Li-ion **LFP** (LiFePO4) | Safe, long cycle life | EVs, RE storage |
| **Sodium-ion** | Na ions; avoids cobalt, nickel, copper; **lower peak temperature in thermal runaway**; volumetric density still below LFP; Li-ion factories adaptable | Storage, low-cost EVs; global capacity ~70 GWh (2025) → ~400 GWh (2030) |
| Solid-state | **Solid electrolyte** - better safety, higher energy density | Emerging, EVs |
| Flow | Liquid electrolytes in tanks; energy and power capacity separate (scaled separately), easy to scale; e.g. vanadium redox | Large grid-scale storage |

- ⚠ Anode = **oxidation**; cathode = **reduction** (during discharge)
- ⚠ Zinc-carbon dry cell = **not rechargeable**; Ni-Cd = rechargeable
