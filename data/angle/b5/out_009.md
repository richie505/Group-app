=== ROW 42 | Human spaceflight - Gaganyaan programme, Bharatiya Antariksh Station roadmap

## Gaganyaan — definitions, modules and FIRSTs | from: S0, S8
# 1. Programme
- **Programme:** India's **first human spaceflight programme**; approved **2018**
- **Aim:** Demonstrate human spaceflight to LEO
- **Goal:** **Crew of 3** to a **400 km LEO** for a **3-day** mission
- **Return:** **Splashdown in Indian sea waters** (10 Sep 2026)
# 2. Spacecraft modules
| **Part** | **Role (TH, 15 Jul 2026)** |
|---|---|
| **Orbital Module (OM)** | Crew Module + Service Module joined by a joint; injected into orbit by HLVM3 |
| **Crew Module** | Crew habitat (double-walled, pressurised, 3 crew)[GK]; survives re-entry, aero-brakes, parachutes and **splashes down in the sea** |
| **Service Module** | On-orbit support; fires thrusters to de-orbit the OM, then separates and **burns up** |

- **Comparison:** Russia's **Soyuz** + China's **Shenzhou** = **three-module** configuration (orbital, descent, service)
  - ⚠ Gaganyaan OM = **two** parts (Crew + Service); Soyuz/Shenzhou = **three** modules
# 3. FIRSTs — own crewed launch
| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| 1st | **USSR/Russia** (Gagarin, Vostok, 1961) |
| 2nd / 3rd | **USA**, **China** |
| Next | Gaganyaan would make India the **4th** |

  - ⚠ **HOPE** = Mars-like habitat station (Tso Kar); **MITRA** = crew behavioural study (with IAM)

## Terms → microgravity effects on the human body | from: S7
| **Condition** | **What happens **[GK] |
|---|---|
| Bone loss (spaceflight osteopenia) | 1-1.5% bone mass lost per month without load |
| Muscle atrophy | Postural muscles weaken - Ax-4 tested **muscle regeneration** |
| Fluid shift | "Puffy face, bird legs" - fluids move to the head |
| Space Adaptation Syndrome | Space motion sickness in first days |
| SANS (Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome) | Optic disc swelling, vision changes |
| Radiation exposure | Cancer and cataract risk |

- **Countermeasures:** Daily resistance + treadmill exercise, diet, medication
- **Research:** Space medicine studied through ISS experiments + ground analogues (Leh, HOPE)
