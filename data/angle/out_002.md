=== ROW 117 | Communalism - meaning, causes, forms and growth in India

## Communalism — meaning, distinction and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1, S8
- **Indian perception:** Communalism most often seen as religious difference between groups → tension and rioting
  - A group promotes its interests **at the expense of others**
| **Religious fundamentalism** | **Communalism** |
|---|---|
| Return to the "fundamentals" and literal scripture; mainly doctrinal | Political mobilisation of religious identity against other communities |
| Directed at reforming one's own community (often anti-modern) | Directed against other communities; seeks power and resources |
| Can exist without inter-group conflict | Always relational - "us vs them" |

| **FIRST / LAST** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| 1909 Act | First legal separate electorate |
| 1906 | First communal political party (Muslim League) |
- ⚠ **1906** = first communal party (Muslim League); **1909** Act = first legal separate electorate

| **Person** | **Definition / statement / role** |
|---|---|
| **Robert Melson and Howard Wolpe** | "The **political assertiveness of a community** to maintain its identity in a plural society undergoing modernisation" (APPSC-G2 2025) |
| **Bipan Chandra**[GK] | The belief that because a group follows a particular religion, it also has **common social, political and economic interests** (APP) |

## Types and stages — Oommen's six dimensions, Bipan Chandra's three stages | from: S2, S3
# 1. Oommen's six dimensions
| **Dimension** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Assimilationist | Small religious groups absorbed into a larger one | Claiming STs, Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are Hindus |
| Welfarist | Works for the welfare of one community only | Christian or Parsi associations |
| Retreatist | Small community keeps away from politics | Bahai community |
| **Retaliatory** | Attempts to harm members of other communities | Riots, revenge cycles |
| **Separatist** | Wants a separate territorial state **within** India for cultural specificity | Gorkhaland, Bodoland |
| **Secessionist** | Wants an independent state **outside** India | Khalistan, "Azad Kashmir" |
- **Source:** CDX Indian Society Pt 1 pp.38-39; UPSC Indian Society p.79
- ⚠ **Separatist** = state **within** India (Gorkhaland, Bodoland); **Secessionist** = state **outside** India (Khalistan)
# 2. Forms
- **Political** = uses religion for votes
- **Economic** = grows from competition for jobs and resources
- **Social** = builds prejudice and social distance
# 3. Bipan Chandra's three stages
| **Stage** | **Features** |
|---|---|
| 1. **Communal nationalism** | Belief that followers of one religion share common **secular** (political, economic) interests; still nationalist in outlook |
| 2. **Liberal communalism** | Secular interests of one religious community are seen as **different from** those of others, but can be reconciled within one nation |
| 3. **Extreme communalism** | Interests seen as **mutually antagonistic**; politics of fear, hatred and violence |
- **After the 1937 elections:** Muslim League turned to **extreme communalism** "based on fear, hatred and violence"
  - Echoed by Hindu Mahasabha and RSS (UPSC Modern History p.300)

## Causes of communalism — factor → cause | from: S4
| **Type** | **Causes** |
|---|---|
| Historical / colonial | British **divide and rule**; separate electorates; caste and religion recorded in the first census (**1871-72**); revivalist movements (APP) |
| Political | Vote-bank and identity politics; religion-based parties (APP; CDX) |
| Economic | Poverty, unemployment, **lopsided development** and relative deprivation (APP) |
| Legal / administrative | Delayed justice, lenient punishment, police inaction, biased intelligence (APP; CDX) |
| Social / media | Fake news and hate messages on social media; media amplifying communal narratives (APP; UPSC) |
| Psychological / international | Prejudices, rumours; foreign funding (CDX) |
- **Revivalism:** Revivalist movements fed communal sentiment in the 19th century (UPSC)
- **1920s movements:** Arya Samaj **Shuddhi and Sangathan** vs Muslim **Tabligh and Tanzim** (UPSC)
- ⚠ **Shuddhi and Sangathan** = Arya Samaj; **Tabligh and Tanzim** = Muslim

=== ROW 119 | Regionalism - meaning, causes, forms and sons-of-the-soil movements

## Regionalism — terms, types, positive vs negative | from: S0
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Region** | Area shaped by history, with its own language, culture, identity + political implications |
| **Regionalism** | Emotional attachment to a particular area; can resist a dominant culture or challenge the nation-state |
| **Supra-state** | Several states sharing interests (e.g., the North-East) |
| **Inter-state** | Conflict between states |
| **Intra-state** | A region inside a state seeking recognition |

| **Positive** | **Negative** |
|---|---|
| Regional parties give backward regions a voice | Parochialism; hostility to migrants (Shiv Sena, MNS) |
| Cultural pride, balanced development, local innovation, accountability | Law-and-order breakdowns, political exploitation, openings for extremists |
| Intergroup solidarity (e.g., Tripura Tribal Autonomous District Council) | Inter-state disputes; new linguistic minorities |

## Causes of regionalism — factor → effect | from: S1
| **Factor** | **Effect / example** |
|---|---|
| **Economic inequality** | Uneven industrialisation: Bombay and West Bengal held **59%** of capital in 1948; infrastructural + social backwardness (low literacy, poor health, few industries) |
| **Cultural dominance / fear of losing identity** | Marginalisation of minority linguistic groups (e.g., Gorkhas in Bengal) |
| **Linguistic identity** | 22 official languages + about **1,635** mother tongues → demands for linguistic states |
| **Historical memory** | Regional kingdoms, British divide-and-rule, autonomy to princely states |
| **Political/administrative neglect** | Sub-regions ignored → new-state demands (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Telangana) |
| **Migration and job competition** | Sons-of-the-soil feeling strongest where migrants and local educated youth compete for middle-class jobs |
> Exam angle: UPPSC 2008 - uneven development, fear of losing cultural identity and political prominence are all causes (answer: "all the above").

| **Full statehood** for UTs | NCT of Delhi; earlier Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim |
