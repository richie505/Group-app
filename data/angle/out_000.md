=== ROW 106 | Family - types, functions and changing structure in India

## Family — definitions and types | from: S0
- **Definitions:**
  - **MacIver:** Group defined by sex relationships precise + enduring enough for procreation and upbringing
  - **Murdock:** **Common residence, economic co-operation, reproduction**
- **Orientation vs procreation:** Family of **orientation** (born into) vs family of **procreation** (set up after marriage)

| **Basis** | **Types** |
|---|---|
| Structure/size | nuclear (conjugal); joint/extended (2+ generations) |
| Residence | patrilocal, matrilocal, **neo-local**, bilocal, **duo-local** (spouses stay with their own natal families) |
| Descent | patrilineal, matrilineal |
| Authority | patriarchal, matriarchal |
| Mates | monogamous; polygamous (polygyny/polyandry) |

- ⚠ **Orientation** = born into; **procreation** = set up after marriage
- ⚠ **Duo-local** = spouses stay with their own natal families; **neo-local** = new home

## Family functions → Murdock, Ogburn, Parsons | from: S1
- **Functions:** Biological, economic, socialisation, psychological
- **Dysfunction:** Persistent failure of these functions (abuse, alcoholism)

| **Scholar** | **Functions** |
|---|---|
| Murdock (250 societies) | 4 universal: sexual, reproductive, economic, educational [GK] |
| Ogburn | 7: economic, protective, religious, recreational, educational, status-giving, affectional; all except affection are passing to outside agencies ("loss of functions") [GK] |
| Talcott Parsons | 2 irreducible: primary socialisation of children, stabilisation of adult personality [GK] |

- ⚠ **4 – 7 – 2** = **Murdock – Ogburn – Parsons**
- ⚠ "**Loss of functions**" = **Ogburn**; only affection stays with the family

## Sociologists → statements on the joint family | from: S2

=== ROW 108 | Kinship - descent, lineage, clan, kinship terminology; North-South Indian kinship contrast

## Kinship — definitions, types and degrees | from: S0
- **Radcliffe-Brown:** Kinship = system of dynamic relations regulated by social usage

| **Type** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Consanguineal** | by blood (parent-child, uncle-nephew) |
| **Affinal** | by marriage (husband-wife, son-in-law and father-in-law) |
| **Fictive** | assumed: ties between people unrelated by birth or marriage |

| **Degree** | **Definition** | **Count** |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | directly related: parents, siblings, spouse | 8 [GK] |
| Secondary | primary kin of primary kin (father's father, wife's brother) | **33** |
| Tertiary | primary kin of secondary kin (father's brother's wife) | **151** |

- ⚠ **8 – 33 – 151** = **primary – secondary – tertiary**
> Exam angle: APPSC 2025. Ravindra-Seema's children are Krishna and Madhavi; Madhavi-Harish's daughter is Jyothi. **Krishna-Jyothi** (maternal uncle-niece) is consanguineous. Ravindra-Seema, Madhavi-Harish and Krishna-Harish are affinal.
- **Ritual kinship examples:** Godparenthood; ritual siblinghood (dharam bhai/behen, rakhi ties)
- **Buddhist monks:** Used kin terms for fellow monks = "spiritual family" (UPSC)

## Descent rules and descent groups → meaning | from: S1, S2
# 1. Descent rules
| **Rule** | **Meaning / example** |
|---|---|
| Unilateral: patrilineal | most of North India; Kondhs, Santhals, Bhils |
| Unilateral: matrilineal | Khasi, Garo, Nair |
| **Bilateral/cognatic** | both sides equal |
| **Double (bilineal)** | different attributes through each parent (movable property via mother, immovable via father) |
| **Parallel descent** | women → daughters, men → sons |
| **Cross descent** | mother → son, father → daughter |

- **Satavahanas:** Kings used **matronymics** (Gautamiputra), yet monarchy patriarchal (APHQ 2011)
# 2. Descent groups
| **Group** | **Definition (CDX)** |
|---|---|
| Lineage | unilineal group descended from a **known** ancestor; exogamous |
| Clan | believes in a common ancestor, but the **link is not demonstrated** |
| Gotra | exogamous Hindu clan; the same-gotra bar dates from the Later Vedic period (CDI) |
| Phratry | two or more related clans |
| Moiety | the society split into **two halves** by descent |

- ⚠ **Lineage** = **known** ancestor; **clan** = link **not demonstrated**
- ⚠ **Parallel** = same sex line; **cross** = opposite sex line

=== ROW 109 | Caste - features, theories of origin, varna-jati distinction

## Caste — etymology, varna vs jati and numbers | from: S0, S2
- **Etymology:** "Caste" from **Portuguese casta** (race, breed); CDX says Spanish

| **Varna** | **Jati (caste)** |
|---|---|
| theoretical 4-fold scheme from the Vedas; the **Chaturvarna** system | the real, local groups; thousands of sub-castes |
| excludes "untouchables" | innumerable regional variations |
| almost no mobility | mobility possible, especially for middle castes |

- **Relationship:** Each varna sub-divided into **jatis**
- **1931 Census:** Recorded **4,147 castes**
- ⚠ Varna = **4**-fold theory; jati = thousands of real groups

## Scholars → definitions of caste | from: S0
| **Scholar** | **Definition / book** |
|---|---|
| S.V. Ketkar | History of Caste in India (1909): membership by birth plus a ban on marrying outside [GK] |

## Theories and periods of origin of caste | from: S1
| **Theory** | **Proponent** | **Core idea (CDX)** |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional / Divine | Rig Veda, **Purusha Sukta** (RV 10.90) | Brahmins from the head, Kshatriyas from the arms, Vaishyas from the thighs, Shudras from the feet |
| Racial | **Herbert Risley** | caste as a by-product of racial difference; Aryans kept apart from the original inhabitants (Pratiloma bar) |
| Occupational | **Nesfield** | hereditary occupations became guilds and then castes |
| Brahminical / Political | **Abbé Dubois** | a device of the Brahmins to keep superiority and purity |
| Cultural | **Sarat Chandra Roy** | restrictions on commensality and intermarriage to preserve culture |

- ⚠ Racial = **Risley**; occupational = **Nesfield**; Brahminical = **Abbé Dubois**
- **Stages of caste:**
  - Early Vedic: **no rigid varna**; Purusha Sukta a late addition
  - **Later Vedic:** four varnas + hereditary divisions crystallise
  - **Post-Maurya/Gupta:** Manu Smriti + rigid caste
  - Medieval: more rigidity, moderated by Bhakti
  - British: caste councils' judicial powers transferred to courts

=== ROW 111 | Tribe - definition, classification, distribution and characteristics

## Tribe and Scheduled Tribe — meaning, Articles and criteria | from: S0
# 1. Meaning
- **Etymology:** 'Tribe' from Latin **'tribus'**
- **Marshall Sahlins:** Segmental organisation of equivalent, unspecialised multi-family groups, clans or bands
# 2. Constitutional provisions
| **Article** | **Provision** |
|---|---|
| **Art. 366(25)** | STs = communities "deemed under **Article 342**"; term took shape under **GoI Act 1935** + Constitution |
| **Art. 342(1)** | **President** notifies after consulting **Governor**; first order: Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950 |
| **Art. 342(2)** | only **Parliament by law** can include/exclude |

- **Lists:** **State/UT-specific**
- ⚠ **342(1)** = President notifies; **342(2)** = only Parliament amends the list
# 3. Criteria (not in the Constitution)
- **Five criteria:** **Primitive traits, distinctive culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact with the community at large, backwardness** (BPSC 2023: all four)
- **Drawn from:** Census 1931, **Kalelkar** (1955), **Lokur Committee (1965)**, **Chanda Committee (1969)**

## Commissions, Acts and schemes on tribes → year | from: S2, S3
# 1. PVTG history and schemes
- **Dhebar Commission (1960-61)** flagged least-developed groups
  - PTG category followed (notes date it to 1973; CDX wrongly dates the Commission itself to 1973)
  - Renamed **PVTGs in 2006**
- **PM-JANMAN / PM PVTG Development Mission** (MoTA):
  - Covers 75 PVTGs
  - Period: **2023-24 to 2027-28**; outlay **₹15,000 crore**
  - Housing, water, health, roads, livelihood
- **Census:** Tribal Affairs Ministry sought **separate PVTG enumeration in Census 2027**
- **Habitat rights:** Under **FRA 2006** (Sec. 3(1)(e)); first PVTG to get them = **Bharia** (Madhya Pradesh)
# 2. Denotified and nomadic tribes
- **Criminal Tribes Act 1871:** Branded ~200 communities 'hereditary criminals' (amended 1924)
  - **Repealed 31 August 1952** on **All India Criminal Tribes Inquiry Committee (1949)** report → **'Denotified'**
  - Replaced by state **Habitual Offenders Acts**
- **Nomadic tribes:** No permanent settlement (pastoralists, hunters, entertainers)
- **Enumeration & commissions:**
  - Last counted in **Census 1931**
  - **Idate Commission (2017)**; earlier Renke Commission (2008)
- **Census 2027:** RGI agreed (30 Jan 2026) to enumerate DNTs; communities demand a "separate column"
  - Centre to Rajya Sabha (Feb 2026): **no proposal** for a separate legal category
- ⚠ **Renke (2008)** came before **Idate (2017)**
- ⚠ First PVTG with habitat rights = **Bharia**
