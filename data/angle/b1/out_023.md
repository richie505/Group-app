=== ROW 115 | Indo-Islamic architecture - arcuate style, Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza, Tughlaqabad, provincial styles
## Arcuate style — terms → meaning | from: S0, S1
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Arcuate** | Arch, dome, vault - brought by the **Turks**; **true arch** held by a **keystone** |
| **Trabeate** | Indian style: lintel, pillar, corbel / corbelled roof - merged with arcuate |
| **Rubble masonry** | Laid in strong **lime mortar**, often with **surkhi** (brick dust); ancient India used **dry-stone** masonry |
| **Squinch / pendentive** | Square room → round dome base |
| **Mihrab** | Prayer niche (new element) |
| **Minbar** | Pulpit (new element) |
| **Minarets** | New element |
| **Arabesque** | Floral vines |
| **Jali** | Pierced stone screens |
| **Double dome** | **Lodis pioneered** it on tombs; **Humayun's Tomb** "introduced" it = first full double dome in Mughal architecture |
- **Decoration:** No human/animal figures
  - **Geometric** patterns, **arabesque**, **Quranic calligraphy**, **jali**
- **Two broad styles:** **Imperial** (Delhi Sultans) vs **Provincial** (regional dynasties)

## Architectural FIRSTs and dynasty → style features | from: S1
# 1. FIRSTs
| **FIRST** | **Monument** |
|---|---|
| **First Indo-Islamic (Muslim) tomb** (mausoleum) in India | **Sultan Ghari** / **Sultan Garhi**, Iltutmish (1231) |
| **First true arch** (keystone) | **Balban's tomb** (Tomb of **Balban**), Delhi |
| **First true dome** / first true application of Islamic principles by Indian craftsmen (first building on fully Islamic principles) | **Alai Darwaza** |
| First **octagonal** tomb | Khan-i-Jahan Tilangani's tomb (Tughlaq period; Firoz Tughlaq's reign) |
- ⚠ **Sultan Garhi – Balban's tomb – Alai Darwaza** = first **tomb – true arch – true dome**
# 2. Dynasty → style features
| **Feature** | **Dynasty / example** |
|---|---|
| **Horse-shoe arch with lotus-bud (spearhead) fringe**, red sandstone and marble inlay | **Khalji**: Alai Darwaza [GK] |
| **Sloping (battered) walls**, pointed domes, false arches; massive, plain, fort-like buildings (because of the Mongol threat) | **Tughlaq**: Tughlaqabad, Ghiyasuddin's tomb; "crisis period of architecture", due to Mongol threat and finances |
| **Octagonal tombs**; **double dome** | **Lodi** (also Sayyid): Sikandar Lodi's tomb, Bara Gumbad, Shisha Gumbad (Lodi Garden) |
| Hindu elements revived: Hindu-style gateways / façades, **plaster ornament**, external **drip-stones** | **Sayyid and Lodi** |
- **Tughlaq:** Domes + false arches show Syrian/Byzantine influence; trabeate elements retained
- ⚠ **Lodis pioneered** the double dome; **Humayun's Tomb** = first full double dome in **Mughal** architecture

## Qutb complex, Delhi — site → features | from: S3
# 1. UNESCO site components
- **"Qutb Minar and its Monuments, Delhi":** **UNESCO World Heritage Site** (inscribed **1993**)
  - **Qutb Minar**, **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque**, **Alai Darwaza** (APPSC-GS 2019), Alai Minar
  - **Iron Pillar**, **tomb of Iltutmish**, Alauddin's tomb and madrasa, Imam Zamin's tomb
# 2. Qutb Minar
- **Dedication:** Sufi saint **Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki** (older notes garble it as "Qutub-ud-Din Aibak")
- **Height:** About **72.5 m** (some notes: **73 m**); tapering, **five** storeys; Quranic calligraphy bands
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Qutb Minar is 180 ft high" is **false** (CGPSC 2023) - actually about **238 ft**
# 3. Iron Pillar
- **Iron Pillar:** Gupta-era (king "Chandra" = Chandragupta II); famous for resisting rust; in the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque court

# Qutb Shahi dynasty — basics
- **Dynasty:** **1518-1687** (some notes give **1512** as start), **7 rulers**, Shia
  - **Qara Qoyunlu (Black Sheep)** Turkmen from Hamadan
  - Official language **Persian**
- **Rule length:** **31 years** accepted for Quli Qutb Shah (APPSC-GS 2023) - counts from **1512**, not 1518

## Qutb Shahi rulers → contributions | from: S3, S8
| **Ruler** | **Key contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk** (1518-43) | **Founder**, a Turkman from Hamadan. Bahmani **tarafdar of Telangana** from 1496; title Qutb-ul-Mulk gave the dynasty its name; **declared independence in 1518**. Defeated by Krishnadevaraya at Goni (1517). Strengthened **Golconda fort** (renamed Muhammadnagar); built the Golconda Jama Masjid and the new city of Muhammad Nagar. Killed by son Jamsheed |
| **Jamsheed / Jamshid** (1543-50) | Took the throne by patricide; won the Battle of Koulan over Bidar (1544); created a cabinet with Wakil, Peshwa and Mir Jumla; died of tuberculosis |
| **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** (1550-80) | Spent **7 years in exile at Vijayanagara**; **"Malkibharama"** / "**Malkibharamudu**" in Telugu literature; chief patron of Telugu poets. Led the **Talikota (1565)** alliance. **Hussain Sagar** (1562) and the Ibrahimpatnam tank date from his reign; first Qutb Shahi coins; Golconda called the "Second Egypt" |
| **Muhammad Quli** (1580-1612) | **Founded Hyderabad (1591)**; built the **Charminar**, the Dar-ul-Shifa (1595) and the Jama Masjid (1598). Dakhni/Urdu poet (pen-name Maani). Allowed the Dutch (1605) and the **English (1611)** to trade at **Machilipatnam** |
| **Muhammad Qutb Shah** (1612-26) | Nephew and son-in-law of Muhammad Quli; began the **Mecca Masjid (1614)**; pen name Zillullah |
| **Abdullah Qutb Shah** (1626-72) | The **longest** reign; ruled with his mother Hayat Bakshi Begum. | **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (1672-87) | **Last** ruler. Relied on **Madanna and Akkanna**; made a treaty with **Shivaji** (1677) |

  - Urdu peaked under Abdullah; some notes call Ibrahim the "Chaucer of Urdu"
- ⚠ Pen names: **Maani** = Muhammad Quli; **Zillullah** = Muhammad Qutb Shah
