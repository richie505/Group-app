## Causes of the Andhra movement → grievances and effects | from: S0
# 1. Grievances in jobs, education and presidency politics
- **Population share:** Telugus about **40% (2 crore)** of Madras Presidency around 1910; lumped together as "Madrasis"

|---|---|---|

  - **8 of 31** colleges in Andhra
  - 163 of 583 secondary schools
  - **No engineering, medical or law college** in Andhra

| **Factor** | **Effect** |

# 2. Deshabhimani
- Anglo-Telugu paper, Guntur; only daily from British Andhra; early paper for the Andhra cause
  - Argued **Telugu culture has no protection without a government of its own** (APPSC 2017)
  - **4 Feb 1910**: first call for a political meeting on Andhra problems
  - **Dec 1911**: English supplement for **George V's Delhi coronation**
  - Editor **Seshagirirao**: distant Madras capital + administrators ignorant of Telugu hurt Andhra
# 3. Andhra Patrika and Krishna Patrika
- **Andhra Patrika:** Founded **9 Sep 1908**, Bombay, by **Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao**
  - Daily from **1914**; moved to Madras
  - Name chosen to popularise the word "Andhra"
- **Krishna Patrika founder:** **Konda Venkatappayya** (with Dasu Narayana Rao), co-founded **1 Feb 1902**; Mutnuri as editor
  - Older notes say Mutnuri Krishna Rao (some notes call Mutnuri the founder)
  - Mocked Montagu-Chelmsford scheme as "digging a mountain to catch a mouse" (13 July 1918)
- **Supporting papers:** **Krishna Patrika** (1902), Andhra Patrika, Desamata, Darbar
- **Swadesamitran:** Tamil daily; backed Telugu-medium resolution of 1915
# 4. Writings → author
- **Jonnavittula Gurunatham:** Youngmen's Literary Association, Guntur
  - Series in **The Hindu**: "On the present position of the Telugu people" — **6 articles in 1911**
  - "Are Telugu people backward?" on **15 April 1911**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC G2 2017 key named "Sitarama Sastri"; notes give Gurunatham

# 4. The province resolution deferred
- **Province demand:** Separate province raised from **1911** onward
- **Mover:** **Vemavarapu Ramadasu Pantulu** (V. Ramadas); supported by V. Satyanarayana
- **Opposed by:** **Nyapati Subba Rao**, **Mocherla Ramachandra Rao**, Ganti Venkataramayya, M. Adinarayanaiah
- **B.N. Sarma's caution:** Separate-province demand might do **"more harm than good"** at that time (premature demand)
- **Loyalty:** Urged loyalty to the British + joint work for Andhra's uplift — **"First we are Indians, then we are Andhras."**
- **Backwardness study:** Alleged government favoured Tamil districts
- **Outcome:** Krishna Rao's amendment passed — collect public opinion, take up at next conference
  - Committee under **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** set up to carry this out
- **Disappointment:** Krishna and Guntur delegates, who had campaigned for two years, disappointed by the deferment
> Exam angle: "The president of the first Mahasabha opposed a separate state. Who?" = B.N. Sarma (APPSC G2 2017).

- **1924:** Harisarvottama Rao's APCC presidency bid defeated
- **Oct 1931, Madras:** Kadapa Kotireddy presiding; Kalluri Subbarao sought separate representation for Rayalaseema
- **Causes of distrust:** 1913 Mahanandi, 1934 and 1935 Mahasabhas + Andhra University seat going to Waltair
# 3. Formation and sessions — sources differ
- **Formed 1934:** Rayalaseema Mahasabha
- **First session:** **Anantapur** (APPSC G2 2008 key); a fragment says Madras
  - First session **28 Jan 1934**, presided by Nemali Pattabhirama Rao; demanded a university at Tirupati
- **1935, Anantapur:** resolved to **oppose** separate Andhra without safeguards (APPSC 2017) → Sribagh Pact
  - **2nd session venue:** **Kadapa** (APPSC G2 2017 key + notes); some notes say Anantapur
  - **2nd Rayalaseema Mahasabha (Kadapa, 1935):** Anti-Andhra; **Pappuri Ramacharyulu**, Koti Reddy, Gadicherla stayed away
# 4. Later stand
- **1937 cabinet:** Rajaji's cabinet had **no Rayalaseema minister** → deepened rift
- **1937 Silver Jubilee session:** Demanded safeguards in **Cabinet, Irrigation and Capital**; committee to examine
- **1938:** R. Suryanarayana Rao + N.M. Sastry formed new Rayalaseema Mahasabha opposing Andhra province

## Congress and the Andhra demand → APCC 1918, linguistic PCCs, INC presidents | from: S5, S6
# 1. Andhras in the early Congress → FIRSTs
- **1885 Bombay session (72 members):** Andhra delegates — P. Anandacharlu, P. Rangaiah Naidu, **Nyapati Subba Rao**, Gutti Kesava Pillai, Venkata Subbarayudu
- **1886 Calcutta session:** **21 Andhras out of 408**
- **P. Anandacharlu (Chittoor):** **7th session, Nagpur, 1891** — first Andhra to preside; earlier Secretary of Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884)
- **1892:** Krishna District Congress Committee (first meeting at Guntur), formed on suggestions of 1885 session
# 2. Reasons for a separate Andhra circle
- **1914 Madras Congress:** 256 Andhra delegates, none on working committee; Tamils blocked an Andhra resolution
- **May 1915:** Madras Provincial Conference refused to discuss province question (pushed by N. Ekambara Aiyer, Nandyal)
- **Precedent:** Andhra leaders cited Bihar and Orissa's separate Congress circles
# 3. Andhra Congress Committee, 1918 — formation, first president
- **1916:** 4th Mahasabha took demand to AICC
  - **Lucknow 1916:** AICC examined it, no decision; some notes say Pattabhi pressed it with Tilak's support
  - Referred to provincial bodies in **December 1916**
- **Calcutta Congress (1917):** Accepted the demand
- **Andhra Congress Committee formed:** **22 January 1918** — through Pattabhi Sitaramayya, Konda Venkatappayya, Tilak
  - First president: **Nyapati Subba Rao**
  - First secretary: **Konda Venkatappayya**
  - First major victory of the Andhra movement
- **First president: Konda Venkatappayya** (APPSC G2 2011 key); some notes give his APCC presidency as **1918-23**
- **Stance timeline:**
  - 1915 provincial conference: refused
  - 1916 Lucknow: no decision
  - 1918: separate committee formed
# 4. Linguistic principle — Nagpur 1920 and Nehru Report 1928
- **PCCs on linguistic lines:** Reorganised at **Nagpur session, 1920**
- **Calcutta special session (September 1920):** Under Lala Lajpat Rai; accepted non-cooperation (vote **1886 to 884**)
  - About 30 Andhra delegates, incl. Konda Venkatappayya, voted against
- **Nagpur, December 1920:** President **C. Vijayaraghavachariar**
  - New constitution: **AICC of 350** + **Congress Working Committee**
  - Membership fee cut to **4 annas**
- **Andhra impact:** Andhra PCC (formed 1918) became a regular linguistic unit (with Karnataka, Maharashtra) → early Congress commitment to linguistic provinces
- **Nehru Report (1928):** Advocated linguistic provinces, in line with 1920 PCC reorganisation
- **Congress commitment:** Linguistic states promised since the 1920s
> Exam angle: "PCCs after 1920 were based on linguistic regions" (APPSC 2016); the RPSC 2018 statement pair on 1920 and 1948 was keyed both true.
# 5. Congress sessions in Andhra and later phase
- **6th session (Kadapa, 1918):** New Andhra Provincial Congress Conference met alongside; separate-state resolution passed unanimously
- **AICC at Bezawada (Vijayawada), 1921:** Tilak Swaraj Fund
- **Kakinada session, 1923:** Maulana Mohammed Ali
- **1921-35:** Movement merged into Gandhian mass struggles ("Swarajya within Swarajya"); moderates (Radhakrishnan, Sir Vijaya, C.R. Reddy) kept Mahasabha going
- **1946 onwards:** Congress, in office in Madras, took over the demand from declining Mahasabha
# 6. Later INC presidents from Andhra
- **Pattabhi Sitaramayya:** **Jaipur session, 1948**
  - Lost **1939 Tripuri** contest to Subhas Bose, despite Gandhi's backing
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC G2 2011 key named **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** as "first Telugu person" to preside — read the wording
- **N. Sanjiva Reddy:** INC president 1960-62

## Commissions → Dar Commission 1948 and JVP Committee 1948–49 | from: S15, S16
# 1. Background, 1947–48

|---|---|---|---|

# 3. Linguistic Provinces Commission (Dhar/Dar)
- **Appointed:** **17 June 1948** by the **Constituent Assembly** (its President, Rajendra Prasad)
- **Composition:**
  - Chairman **Justice S.K. Dhar** (**S.K. Dar**), former judge of Allahabad High Court
  - Members **Dr. Panna Lal** + **Jagat Narayan Lal**
- **Mandate:** Formation of Andhra, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra - boundaries and consequences
- **Evidence (Madras, 1948):**
  - Andhra Mahasabha: all Telugu areas
  - **N. Sanjiva Reddy**: postpone the whole question
  - APCC under **N.G. Ranga**: memorandum on **10 Sep 1948**
  - **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy**, for 20 Rayalaseema MLAs: opposed linguistic states or asked that Sribagh terms be written in
- **Report, December 1948 (10 Dec 1948, published 13 Dec):** Reorganise on **administrative convenience**, not language
  - Criteria: geographical contiguity, financial self-reliance, administrative viability, development potential
  - Warned linguistic states threatened **national unity** = first official rejection of the linguistic basis
> Exam angle: "1948, Justice S.K. Dhar" (APPSC G2 2025); APPSC-GS 2025 key: Linguistic Province Committee chaired by **S.K. Dar**; PYQ "linguistic states a threat to national unity" = **Dar Commission** (G2 2017).

# 4. Comparative note: conferences and commissions
- **Andhra conferences:**
  - **Nidadavolu 1912** = first formal resolution
  - **Bapatla 1913** = first numbered Andhra Conference
  - **Vijayawada 1914** = first session called **"Andhra Mahasabha"**
- **Commissions sequence:** **Dhar Commission** (**1948**, CA-appointed) → **JVP** (**1948-49**, Congress) → **Wanchoo** (**1953**, Andhra only) → **Fazal Ali SRC** (**1953-55**, all India; members **H.N. Kunzru** and **K.M. Panikkar**)
# 5. Criticism & debates: regionalism and delays
- **Regional suspicion:** Rayalaseema and Nellore leaders saw the body as **Circars/Brahmin-led** → district-wise vote of **1917** was a response
- **Leadership splits:** **Circars vs Rayalaseema**; **Prakasam vs Pattabhi** — repeatedly slowed the movement
- **University seat/jurisdiction:** Left lasting Rayalaseema resentment → addressed in the **Sri Bagh Pact**
- **Delay:** Blamed on Centre caution after Partition + Andhra factionalism
- **Losing Madras:** New state "administratively and economically orphaned"; had to use undeveloped **Kurnool** as capital
# 6. AP angle: Rayalaseema and capital
- **Rayalaseema irrigation grievance:** **Telugu Ganga** — meant for **2.75 lakh acres** + Chennai water — barely irrigates **20,000** acres
- **Sri Bagh terms:** Capital, High Court, irrigation — still cited in Rayalaseema debates
- **Rayalaseema higher education:**
  - **Central University of AP, Anantapur** (after the **AP Reorganisation Act 2014**; campus funds released, Mar 2026)
  - **Yogi Vemana University, Kadapa (2006)**
- **Bapatla:** Now HQ of **Bapatla district**
- **AP Reorganisation (Amendment) Act, 2026:** Affirms **Amaravati's** permanent statutory position as capital (Block04)
