=== ROW 173 | Company's constitutional framework - Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784, Act of 1786

## Regulating Act, 1773 — background, committees and FIRSTs | from: S0
- **Background:** after **Buxar (1764)**, **Shah Alam** granted the **Diwani** of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa to the Company in **1765**
- **1772 committees:** Parliament appointed a **Select Committee** (**Burgoyne**) + a **Secret Committee** to inquire into Company affairs → reports led to the **Regulating Act**
- **FIRST - significance:** **First step by the British Parliament to control the Company**; recognised its political and administrative functions
> Exam angle: "incorrect statement" - it did **not** abolish the Court of Directors (APPSC-GS 2019).

## Acts → year → provisions (1773, 1781, 1786, 1793) | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. Regulating Act, 1773
- **Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774):** **1 Chief Justice + 3 judges**; civil + criminal jurisdiction
- **Reporting:** Court of Directors to report on revenue, civil and military affairs to the British Government
# 2. Amending Act, 1781
- **Other name:** **Act of Settlement**
- **Exemption:** **Governor-General, Council and Company servants** exempted from Supreme Court jurisdiction for **acts in their official capacity**
- **Also:**
  - **Revenue** matters excluded from the Court's jurisdiction
  - Court told to respect the **personal laws** of Hindus and Muslims
# 3. Act of 1786
- **Why passed:** to meet **Lord Cornwallis**'s condition for accepting office
- **Powers:** GG could **override his council** in special cases + also be **Commander-in-Chief**
- **Extension:** **Charter Act of 1793** extended the overriding power to the governors of the presidencies ("greater control over Bombay and Madras")
- ⚠ **1781** = Act of Settlement (curbs Supreme Court); **1786** = override power (Cornwallis)

## Defects of 1773 → remedies | from: S0, S1, S2
- **Defects:** of the Regulating Act 1773

| **Defect** | **Remedy** |
|---|---|
| GG had **no veto**; could be outvoted by his council | **Act of 1786** - override power |
| Supreme Court's jurisdiction over the Council and over Indians undefined → Council-Court clashes | **1781** Act of Settlement - official acts and revenue exempted |

- **Context:** the **1786** Act corrected the **1773** defect under which **Hastings** was repeatedly outvoted

## Universal angles | from: S3 | universal
- **Significance:** **1773** began Parliamentary control; **1784** set the dual system that lasted until **1858**, when the **GoI Act** ended **"Double Government"**
- **AP angle - judicial system in Andhra:**
  - **Madras Supreme Court (1801)** for British subjects
  - **Sadar Adalat** and **Sadar Faujdari Adalat** for others
  - **Madras Regulations 1816** set up **Munsiff** and **Panchayat courts**
- **Comparative note:** **Ministry of Law and Justice** (oldest organ of GoI) traced to the **Charter Act 1833** (2026), not to 1773

## Legislature, centralisation and Agra Presidency → Act | from: S1, S2, S3
# 1. Charter Act 1833 — centralisation
- **Title:** **Governor-General of Bengal → Governor-General of India**
- **Legislation:**
  - **GG-in-Council** got **exclusive legislative power** for all British India
  - Presidencies lost law-making powers
  - Laws now called **"Acts"** (earlier **"Regulations"**)
- **Law Member:** added as the **4th** member of the GG's council
- **Agra Presidency:** Act provided for splitting the **Bengal Presidency**
  - Present **UP** separated and placed under Agra Presidency in **1834** (UPPSC 2021 key)
  - Renamed **North-Western Provinces (1836)**
# 2. Charter Act 1853 — separate legislature
- **Separated the legislative and executive functions** of the GG's Council:
  - **6 new legislative members** → separate **Central Legislative Council** (the **"mini-Parliament"**)
  - The 6: **4** members appointed by the provincial governments + the **Chief Justice** and one judge of the **Calcutta Supreme Court**
# 3. Centralisation trend (1773 to 1861)
| **Act** | **Step** |
|---|---|
| 1773 | Governor of Bengal made GG of Bengal; Bombay and Madras subordinated |
| 1784 | Board of Control; stricter supervision of the presidencies |
| 1786 | GG could override his council |
| 1793 | Greater control over Bombay and Madras; override extended to governors |
| **1833** | **GG of India**; **exclusive legislative power** with the GG-in-Council - **peak of centralisation** |
| 1853 | Legislative work separated into a Central Legislative Council with provincial members |

- **Reversal:** **Indian Councils Act 1861** restored legislative power to **Bombay** and **Madras** (decentralisation)
