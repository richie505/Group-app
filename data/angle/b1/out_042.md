## Andhra social reform — origin, leaders and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. Where reform began
- **Origin:** **Rajahmundry (Godavari district)**, today East Godavari = Andhra's first Renaissance centre
- **Led by:** English-educated middle class
- **First guru:** **Atmuri Lakshmi Narasimham**
- **Peak:** **1870-1935**; main centres **Rajahmundry and Kakinada** (the other main centre); Machilipatnam, Bapatla, Guntur followed
# 2. FIRSTs of Andhra reform
| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| First Telugu journal | **Satyaduta** / **Satyadoota** - Bellary Christian Mission, **1835**, from **Bellary** (spelt "Saryudson" in some notes) |
| First organised reform body in Andhra | **Rajahmundry Social Reform Association** (Veeresalingam) |
| First girls' school in Andhra | **1874**, **Dowlaiswaram** near Rajahmundry |
| First Prarthana Samaj in Andhra | **Prarthana Samaj, Rajahmundry**, **1878** |
| First night school + first Theosophical High School | Andhra's **first night school** + **first Theosophical High School** (Veeresalingam) |
| Co-education | Veeresalingam first in Andhra to promote it |
| First widow remarriage in Andhra | **11 Dec 1881**, Rajahmundry |
- ⚠ First Telugu journal = **Satyaduta** (**1835**); first Telugu social-reform journal = **Vivekavardhini** (**1874**)

## Brahmo and Prarthana Samaj in Andhra — samaj → year → founder | from: S7
# 1. Origins
- **Parent body:** Brahmo Samaj founded by **Raja Ram Mohan Roy, 1828, Calcutta** - monotheism, no idol worship
- **Madras root:** **Veda Samajam**; secretary **Chembeti Sridharulu Naidu** renamed it **Dakshina Bharatha Desa Brahmo Samajam** (Dakshina Bharata Brahmo Samaj)
- **Andhra branch:** Rajahmundry (**1878**) per some notes
# 2. Samajs
| **Year** | **Samaj** | **Founder** |
|---|---|---|
| **1878** | **Prarthana Samaj, Rajahmundry** (first in Andhra) | **Veeresalingam** (PYQ) |
| 1882 | Brahmo Samaj, Machilipatnam | Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu |
| 1887 | Brahmo Samaj, Rajahmundry | - |
| 1888 | Kakinada Samaj | Tayi Surya Prakasarao Naidu, Duggirala Surya Prakasarao |
| 1898 | Guntur Samaj | Pillarisetty Sitaramayya |
- **Trinity of Brahmo Samaj:** **Veeresalingam, Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu, Desiraju Peda Bapayya** (Desiraju Pedda Bapayya)
- **Decline:** Splits after Venkataratnam Naidu joined the **Justice Party**
- ⚠ **1878** = Prarthana Samaj, Rajahmundry; **1887** = Brahmo Samaj, Rajahmundry

## Reformers and patrons → contributions | from: S7, S8, S9
# 1. Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu
- **Life:** **1862-1939**
- **Inspiration:** Joined Brahmo Samaj inspired by **Pandit Sivanath Sastri**
- **Spread:** Took reform from the Godavari districts to **North Coastal Andhra** (Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, Srikakulam); centre = **Kakinada**
- **Bodies:**
  - **Samajika Shuddhi Samajam (Social Purity Association), 1891** - most important
  - **Brahmopasana Mandiram**
  - **Andhra Brahma Dharma Pracharak Nidhi**
  - **Karunalayam**, Kakinada (with Pithapuram Maharaja)
- **Anti-nautch (Bhogam / Devadasi) campaign:** **Raghupathi Venkataratnam Naidu** (PYQ 2019) began the **anti-nautch (Devadasi)** movement in Madras
  - Main force behind ending the **Bhogam (Devadasi)** caste custom (APHQ)
  - APPSC 2019: main factor in eliminating the **'Bhogam' caste**
- **Offices:**
  - Principal, **Pithapuram Raja's College, Kakinada (1905-18)**
  - **First Andhra Vice-Chancellor of Madras University**; Andhra University Bill came during his tenure
  - Tenure **1925-1928** (some notes say **1918-1928**)
# 2. Desiraju Pedda Bapayya (Bapatla)
- First practising (Anushtanika) Brahmo in Andhra; discarded sacred thread
- Founded **Temperance and Social Purity Sangham**, Rajahmundry → boycott of Bhogam melas
- Dates differ in some notes: **1877-1903** / **1881-1908**
# 3. Zamindari patrons
- **Pithapuram Raja** (Rao Venkatakumara Mahipati Suryarao, **1885-1964**):
  - **Karunalayam** (**1909**) = only orphanage in Andhra
  - Orphan and vocational schools
  - Brahmopasana Mandiram (Kakinada); Brahma Mandir (Pithapuram)
  - **Rs 1 lakh** to the Brahma Dharma Prachara Nidhi → train preachers + translate Bengali Brahmo books
- **Vizianagaram:**
  - Kanyasulkam (2nd edn **1909**) dedicated to **Ananda Gajapati**
  - First staged by the Vizianagaram troupe (**1892**)

## Vyavaharika vs granthika — terms, bodies and persons | from: S14
# 1. Terms → meaning
|  | **Granthika** | **Vyavaharika** |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Sanskritised classical (grandhika), archaic grammar | Spoken Telugu (also called Gramya / Janapada bhasha) |
| Aim / claim | Only "pure" literary medium | Close the gap between written and spoken Telugu |
| Leaders | Orthodox pandits | **Gidugu** (theory, education), **Gurajada** (literature) |
# 2. Movement and bodies
- **Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy:** "**Father of the Vyavaharika Bhashodyamam**"; led the **spoken Telugu (Vyavaharika Bhasha)** movement with **Gurajada**
  - Founded **Vyavaharika Bhashodyama Samstha (1910)** after a meeting at **Visakhapatnam (1910)**
  - Founded the Varthamana Vyavaharikandhra Bhasha Parivartaka Samaj (APPSC 2017)
- **Navya Sahitya Parishat:** Youth body spreading his cause
- **Outcome:** **Andhra Mahasabha** finally endorsed colloquial Telugu for literature, education and administration
# 3. Persons and books
| **Person** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **J.A. Yates** | Inspired the movement |
| **P.T. Srinivasa Ayyangar** | Wrote Life or Death: A Plea for Vernaculars |
| Malladi Suryanarayana Sastri | Andhra Bhashanusasanam (grammar for vyavaharika) |
| Vadlamudi Gopalakrishnayya | Vyavaharika Bhasha Vyakaranam |
| Vajjala Venkateswarlu | Aadhunikapramanikandhra Vyakaranam |
# 4. Supporters and opponents
- **Supporters:** Gidugu, Gurajada, Tapi Dharma Rao, Navya Sahitya Parishat
- **Opponents:**
  - **Kokkonda Venkataratnam**: orthodox editor of Andhra Bhasha Sanjeevini
  - **Jayanti Ramaiah Pantulu**: led the classical side through the Andhra Sahitya Parishad

## Social evils, methods and opponents | from: S3, S4
# 1. Evils campaigned against
- Prohibition of widow remarriage

# 2. Methods
- Journals and satire; public lectures
- Schools and co-education
- Remarriages at his own cost; widow homes
- Prarthana Samaj worship without idols

## Marriage and consent Acts → ages (1872, 1891, 1929 and later) | from: S3, S4, S8
# 1. Native Marriage Act (Civil Marriage Act), 1872
- **Act III of 1872:** Passed at **Keshab Chandra Sen's** urging for Brahmo marriages - product of his campaign
  - Minimum age **14 for the girl, 18 for the boy** (APPSC-GS 2025 answer)
  - **Inter-caste** marriage allowed
  - **Polygamy** barred
- **Other names:** Native (Civil) Marriage Act, "Brahmo Marriage Act"; listed as "Brahma Act (Anti-Child Marriage Act)"; some notes say it "prohibited child marriage"
- **1878 Brahmo split:** Keshab broke this Act by marrying his minor daughter to the **Cooch Behar** prince
# 2. Age of Consent Act, 1891 (Lansdowne)
- **1891 Act (Lansdowne):** **Age of Consent Act, 1891** raised the age of consent for girls **10 to 12** - from **10 (IPC 1860)** to **12** (p.102)
  - Regulated consummation, not marriage (some notes say it "prohibited marriage below 12")
- **Later steps:** Age of consent **14** (IPC amendment **1925**) → **16** (**1949**) → **18** (**POCSO 2012**)
- **BNS 2023:** **Section 63** keeps **18** (Jan 2026)
# 3. Child Marriage Restraint Act (Sarda Act), 1929
- **Sarda Act:**
  - Passed **1929**; in force from **1 April 1930** → some notes date it 1930
  - Minimum marriage age **girls 14, boys 18**
  - Moved by **Harbilas Sarda**, under **Irwin**
- **Amended 1978:** **18 (girls) / 21 (boys)**
- **Today:** **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006** = **18 for women, 21 for men**
  - A **2021** Union Cabinet proposal to raise women's age to **21** has not become law - still **18** (Jan 2026)
- ⚠ **14/18** = both the **1872** Act and the **1929** Sarda Act; **1891** = consent **12**
