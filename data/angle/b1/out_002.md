=== ROW 12 | IVC religion - Pashupati seal, Mother Goddess, tree and animal worship, fire altars

## Harappan religion — nature and features | from: S0
- **Nature:** IVC **predominantly secular** - religious element present but not dominant (UPSC 2011, keyed correct - true, along with cotton for textiles)
- **Temples:** **No temple** found; worship possibly domestic or open-air
- **Idol worship:** **Idolatry was practised** (figurines, images) - beginnings of idol worship
- **Other practices:**
  - **Great Bath** at Mohenjodaro → ritual purification
  - Yoga and meditation practised
- **Other beliefs:** Spirits, ghosts and evil forces; amulets (see amulets and charms)

## Pashupati seal and Mother Goddess — features and identification | from: S2, S3
# 1. Pashupati seal
| **Feature** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| Site | **Mohenjodaro** |
| Figure | Seated in a **yogic posture**; **three-faced** (CDI); horned headdress [GK] |
| Animals around | **Elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, buffalo**; **deer** below |
| Identification | **Proto-Shiva / Pashupati** (lord of animals), named by John Marshall [GK] |
| Debate | Some read it as a deity of animals and fertility, not Shiva |

- **Horned deity on a terracotta cake:** **Kalibangan** (UPPSC 2008)
- **Clay mask:** Horned god from Kalibangan/Gujarat
> Exam angle: the Indus people worshipped → Shiva (UKPSC 2006).
# 2. Mother Goddess
- **Mother Goddess:** Terracotta female figurines with **fertility** symbolism → fertility cult; most important clay figures
- **Found at:** **Harappa** (Mother Goddess sculpture), **Kot Diji**, Kalibangan, Gujarat sites
- **Both deities:** With male Pashupati figure → worship of **both male and female deities** (UPSC 2013)

=== ROW 13 | Decline of the IVC - competing theories; Late Harappan cultures (Cemetery-H, Jhukar, Rangpur)

## Decline theories → proponent / study | from: S0
| **Theory** | **Proponent / study** |
|---|---|
| Aryan invasion | **Mortimer Wheeler (1947)** - now rejected; also Gordon Childe, Stuart Piggott [GK] |
| Floods | John Marshall, E. Mackay, S.R. Rao [GK]; one list also gives M.R. Sahni [GK] here |
| Tectonic shift / uplift damming the Indus | Robert Raikes (tectonic damming), G.F. Dales, M.R. Sahni [GK] |
| Shift of the Indus course | H.T. Lambrick [GK] |
| Drying of the Ghaggar-Hakra | D.P. Agrawal and Sood [GK] |
| Climate change, prolonged drought | Aurel Stein, A.N. Ghosh [GK]; **IIT Kharagpur study - 900 years of low rainfall**; Deoria Tal pollen study (2026) |
| Ecological imbalance (deforestation, overgrazing, salinity) | W.A. Fairservis [GK] |
| Decline of Mesopotamian trade (c. 1900 BCE) | Fall in Mesopotamian demand - CDI economic explanation |

- **Current consensus:** **Multi-causal** + **gradual** (about 200 years, varying by region); "sudden catastrophe" idea rejected
  - Causes: climate, rivers, ecology, trade collapse, failure of urban management, loss of cohesion
- ⚠ **Trap:** M.R. Sahni appears under both floods and tectonic uplift; Lambrick = shift of Indus course, not floods

## Mortimer Wheeler's invasion theory — evidence and rejection | from: S1
- **Wheeler (1947):** Blamed Aryan invaders
  - Cited **skeletons huddled together at Mohenjodaro** + Indra as "fort-breaker" (Purandara)
- **Rejected:**
  - **No archaeological evidence of invasion**
  - Rig Veda post-dates the decline
  - "Massacre" skeletons = multiple burials of different periods
  - Decline was gradual

## Evidence → theory (floods, rivers, ecology, climate) | from: S2, S3
| **Theory** | **Evidence** |
|---|---|
| Floods | **Indus changed course** → repeatedly flooded, then abandoned settlements such as Mohenjodaro; Chanhudaro flooded several times |
| Dried rivers | **Ghaggar-Hakra** system (densest cluster of settlements) **dried up** - tectonic shifts, less rainfall |
| Ecology | Deforestation, **soil salinity**, overgrazing; exhaustion of copper and timber; failing drainage upkeep |
| Disease | Malaria outbreak detected at Mohenjodaro |
| Palaeoclimate | Weakening monsoon + rising aridity c. 1900 BCE |

- **IIT Kharagpur study:** **Continuous weak monsoon/low rainfall for about 900 years** caused decline (BPSC 2018 key: **900 years**)
> Exam angle: IIT Kharagpur study group - continuous low rainfall for **900 years** (BPSC 2018).
- **Newest evidence - Deoria Tal (Uttarakhand) (30 Jul 2026, PIB):**
  - Lake pollen dated by AMS radiocarbon on Trapa seed cases
  - Abrupt rise in oak-to-pine ratio → summer monsoon weakened sharply about **4,200 years ago** (**"4.2 ka event"**)
  - Cut water in Indus and Ghaggar-Hakra → drought + **eastward migration**
- ⚠ **Trap:** **900 years** = IIT Kharagpur low-rainfall span; **4,200 years ago** = Deoria Tal 4.2 ka event; **200 years** = pace of decline

## De-urbanisation and the Late Harappan phase — features | from: S4, S5
# 1. De-urbanisation
- **Pace:** **Gradual (about 200 years)**; regional differences in timing
  - "Sudden catastrophe" view of early scholars = rejected
- **Outcome:** Cities → small rural settlements, regional cultures; many sites abandoned
- **Migration:** **East and south**
  - **Eastward:** To the **Ganga-Yamuna Doab**
  - **Southward:** To **Gujarat** and the **Deccan**
# 2. Late Harappan phase
- **Nature:** **Primarily Chalcolithic** - tools of **stone and copper**
  - **No metal objects needing complicated casting** (APPSC-G1 2024, statements I and II correct)
- **Period:** **c. 1900-1300 BCE** - post-urban
- **Settlement pattern:** **De-urbanisation** → smaller rural settlements
- **Pottery:** Standard designs → **varied regional styles**
- **Uniformity lost:** Seals, script, weights, bricks decline / lose uniformity
- **Economy:** Long-distance trade weakens; local subsistence farming grows

## Continuity — OCP, Copper Hoards, Harappan traits and Vedic links | from: S7
# 1. OCP and Copper Hoards
- **OCP:** Listed as a Late Harappan culture with Cemetery-H (Jun 2026); Harappan legacy may have shaped OCP
- **Copper Hoards:** Often found with OCP in Ganga-Yamuna Doab → commonly linked
- **Carriers:** Cemetery H + OCP carry traits into later cultures
# 2. Continuity of Harappan traits
- **Religious:** Pipal + Mother Goddess worship, linga-yoni, fire altars, ritual bathing (→ later temple tanks and ghats); burial practices
- **Material:** Bead-making in Gujarat, red ware with black designs, dish-on-stand vessels, bullock carts

=== ROW 16 | Early Vedic polity - rajan, sabha, samiti, vidatha, gana; nature of tribal chiefdom

## Early Vedic polity — nature, units, Early vs Later Vedic | from: S0
- **Nature:** **Tribal polity** (APPSC 2018; not a republic or autocracy) - kinship-based; **no formal bureaucracy**; no fixed territory
- **Basic unit:** **Jana** (tribe) = largest socio-political unit; mentioned **275 times** in Rigveda; term **Janapada** absent
- **Hierarchy:** Jana > Vis (under Vispati) > Grama (Gramani) > Kula (Kulapa)

| **Feature** | **Early Vedic** | **Later Vedic** |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | Jana (tribe) | Janapada / **Rashtra** (territory) |
| Chief | Elective/limited chief | Hereditary king |
| Assemblies | Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha powerful | Vidatha disappears; Samiti weakens |

## Rajan — titles, selection debate, duties | from: S1
- **Rajan:** Tribal chief - protector of tribe and its cattle, not ruler of a fixed territory

| **Title** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Gopati** | Lord of cattle |
| **Gopa** | Protector |
| **Gopajanasya** | Title of the Rajan |
| **Mahisi** | Chief queen |
| **ardhadeva** | Demi-god - title of Puru king **Trasadasyu** (APP notes garble the name) |

- **Election vs hereditary debate:**
  - Tending towards hereditary, not strictly so - traces of **election by the Samiti**; assemblies had a role in selection
  - APP notes + older PYQ keys = **hereditary monarchy** (some notes call kingship hereditary)
  - In practice: monarchy usual, often passing in the family
- **Duties:** Protect tribe and cattle; lead in war; keep order, settle disputes - with advice of assemblies and purohita
- **Divinity:** Kingship not seen as divine / not linked to divinity; exception = Trasadasyu (ardhadeva)

## Vedic assemblies → composition and function | from: S2
| **Assembly** | **Composition** | **Function** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sabha** | Elders, elites; women could attend (Sabhavati) | Advisory, social and **judicial** |
| **Samiti** | General tribal members (folk assembly); broader participation | Tribal business; legislative; **election of the king** (**elective** - choosing the chief) |
| **Vidatha** | Whole tribe; **earliest assembly**; women attended | Secular, religious, economic and military |
| **Gana** | Group/troop of tribesmen; tribal body | Military unit (army divided into Sardha, Vrata, Gana); military and tribal functions |

- **Vidatha:** Assembly most often mentioned in the Rig Veda (**122 times**; Samiti 9, Sabha 8)
- **Atharvaveda:**
  - Sabha and Samiti = **twin daughters of Prajapati**
  - Treated as **separate institutions**
  - Sabha called "**narishta**"
  - Mentions women's participation in Samiti
- **Women in Early Vedic assemblies:** Women attended **Sabha and Vidatha**; women members = **Sabhavati**
- **Later Vedic change:** Vidatha disappeared; Sabha + Samiti survived but under chiefs and nobles (limited to nobles and Brahmanas); **women were excluded**
> Exam angle: "judicial assembly" = Sabha; "earliest/most-mentioned assembly" = Vidatha; "daughters of Prajapati" and "Veda treating Sabha and Samiti as separate" = Atharvaveda (not Rigveda).

## Universal angles | from: S7 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - Assemblies with elective traces = early "democratic" elements in Indian polity
  - Rig Veda = only source for the period; assemblies show a proto-participatory tribal order
- **Comparative note:**
  - Later Vedic = hereditary territorial kingship, ratnins, regular taxes, royal sacrifices (Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha)
  - Rig Vedic Aryans knew the **horse**, spoked-wheel chariot, coat of mail, helmet vs Harappans left little horse evidence (UPSC 2017)
- **FIRST/LAST:** Vidatha = earliest assembly + first to disappear in Later Vedic period
- **AP angle:** "Andhra" first appears later, in the **Aitareya Brahmana** (Sunahshepa story), not in Rig Veda
- **Criticism & debates:** Aryan homeland (external migration vs indigenous) remains debated
