## Gandhi's early life and South Africa — events → dates | from: S0, S1
# 1. Early life (1869-1893)
- **Birth:** **2 October 1869, Porbandar** (Gujarat)
- **Education:** Samaldas College, **Bhavnagar** → law in **London 1888-1891** at the **Inner Temple**
  - Called to the Bar **1891**
- **Practice:** **Rajkot and Bombay** - did not succeed
# 2. South Africa (1893-1914)
- **Duration:** **1893-1914** (about **21 years**)

| **Year** | **Event (CDI unless noted)** |
|---|---|
| **1893** | Went to South Africa for **Dada Abdullah's** case |

=== ROW 229 | Gandhian philosophy - truth, ahimsa, satyagraha, means and ends

## Gandhian terms → meaning | from: S0, S1, S2
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Truth** | The end; non-violence the means |
| **Ahimsa** | In **thought, word and deed**; **not a sign of weakness** but of the strong and morally upright |
| **Satyagraha** | **Satya (truth) + agraha (insistence/eagerness)**; English "**passive resistance**"; "**Soul force**" in place of physical force |
| **Swaraj** | Not only political freedom but **self-rule, self-control** + capacity of masses to govern themselves without coercion |
| **Decentralisation** | Power close to the people; local communities decide |

- **Brave vs weak:** non-violence of the **brave** vs of the **weak**
  - If only choice is **cowardice vs violence** → he would advise **violence**
- **Origin of satyagraha:** born **1906** in **Transvaal**
- ⚠ **Trap:** satyagraha = **insistence on truth**, not "weak passive resistance"

## Gandhism compared — Marxism, socialism, anarchism, individualism | from: S4
| **Point** | **Gandhism** | **Marxism** |
|---|---|---|
| Final goal | **Stateless society** | **Stateless society** - the common ground (UPSC 2020) |
| Method | **Non-violence**, **change of heart** | **Class struggle**, **revolution** |
| Property | **Trusteeship** - rich not expropriated (CDI) | **Abolition of private property** |
| Basis | **Moral/spiritual** | **Economic determinism** |

- **Anarchism:** called a **philosophical anarchist** - ideal of "**enlightened anarchy**"; State = **violence in concentrated form**
- **Socialism:** Indian Preamble socialism = blend of **Marxism + Gandhism**, leaning to **Gandhian socialism**
- **Individualism:** **individual and his conscience** = the unit; self-rule before State rule

## Universal angles | from: S5 | universal
- **Significance:** replaced **physical force** with **moral force** → national movement became a **mass movement**
- **Current relevance:**
  - Gandhian non-violence inspired the **Baltic Way** + **Singing Revolution** → Baltic re-independence in **1991** (1 Sep 2026)
  - **NSE** launched **Nifty 500 Ahimsa index** (companies free of animal cruelty) with **Ahimsagain Foundation**; **BSE** earlier launched **Saatvik 100** index (23 Jul 2026)
- **Comparative note:** Gandhism vs Marxism share only the **stateless end**; differ on **means**

## Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda → year, place, issue, outcome | from: S1, S3, S4, S5
| **Struggle** | **Year, place** | **Issue** | **Outcome / "first"** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Champaran | **1917, Bihar** | Indigo on 3/20th of land (**tinkathia**); illegal dues | **25% refund**; **first civil disobedience** |
| Ahmedabad Mill Strike | **1918, Gujarat** | Plague bonus dispute; workers asked 50% | Gandhi advised **35%**; tribunal gave 35%; **first hunger strike** |
| Kheda | **1918, Gujarat** | Crop failure; revenue remission refused | **Vallabhbhai Patel** field commander; **first non-cooperation** |

# 1. Champaran (1917)
- **Place & year:** **1917, Bihar** - **first mass movement**, but in **Bihar**, not Gujarat
- **Precursor:** **Bengal indigo revolt of 1859** (see Current relevance)
- **Action:** Gandhi **defied order to leave the district** (**Motihari**)
  - Toured villages with lawyers **recording complaints** → forced a government (**official**) **inquiry**
# 2. Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
- **Date:** **Feb-Mar 1918**, Gujarat
- **Dispute:** mill owners withdrew **"plague bonus"**
  - Workers demanded **50%** wage hike (**wartime inflation**); owners offered **20%**
- **Outcome:** Gandhi advised **35%** + **first hunger strike in India** → **tribunal awarded 35%**
# 3. Kheda (1918)
- **Place:** **Kheda district, Gujarat, 1918**
- **Grievance:** **crop failure** (drought/famine) → peasants sought **remission (suspension) of land revenue**; government refused
  - Peasants refused to pay despite threats of **land confiscation**
- **Called off:** government agreed revenue **recovered only from those able to pay** (APPSC 2023 key)
  - Suspended for poor peasants (tax suspended for the year); **confiscated property returned**
# 4. Chronological order

| **Order** | **Event** | **Date** |
|---|---|---|

## Jallianwala Bagh massacre, 13 April 1919 — officials, casualties, renunciations | from: S6
# 1. Arrests and officials
- **Arrested leaders:** **Dr Satyapal and Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew** (**Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr. Satyapal**, Amritsar) - symbols of **Hindu-Muslim unity**, for organising anti-Rowlatt protests

# 2. Massacre
- **Date:** **13 April 1919 (Baisakhi)**
- **Gathering:** peaceful crowd in **walled garden** protesting arrests + **Rowlatt Act**

- **Casualties:**

  - INC estimate: **over 1,000**
  - Some notes: ~400 dead, ~1,000 / 1,200 injured
- **Aftermath:** **Martial law**, **crawling order**, **floggings**
- **Hunter Committee:** condemned Dyer; **House of Lords** backed him

=== ROW 233 | Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh; Hunter Committee

## Rowlatt Act, 1919 → committee, provisions, repeal | from: S0
- **Official name:** **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act**, **1919**
- **Basis:** **Sedition Committee (Rowlatt Committee)** / **Sedition (Rowlatt) Committee** report, headed by Justice **Sidney Rowlatt**
- **Passed:** **10 March 1919**
- **Provisions:** Extended **Defence of India Act (1915)** powers
  - **Arrest and detention without trial** / without warrant (up to **two years**)
  - **In-camera** trials by a **3-judge High Court panel** with **no appeal**; **special tribunals**
  - **Habeas corpus suspended**; **press curbs**
- **Repealed:** Under **Lord Reading**, along with the **Press Act 1910**
- ⚠ **Trap:** passed **10 March 1919**; **repealed** under **Lord Reading** with the **Press Act 1910**

## Committees and inquiries → members and findings | from: S3
# 1. Hunter Committee
- **Appointment:** By the government (**Oct 1919**) to probe the **Jallianwala Bagh massacre and Punjab disturbances**
- **Chairman:** **Lord William Hunter**
- **Indian members:** **Chimanlal Setalvad**, **Jagat Narayan**, **Sultan Ahmed Khan**
  - Wrote a **minority report** more critical of Dyer
- **Findings:** Censured Dyer → **relieved of command**, but no real punishment
  - **House of Lords** backed him
  - Gandhi called the findings a **"whitewash"**
# 2. Congress inquiry committee
- **Body:** Congress's own **non-official** inquiry (**Punjab Sub-Committee**)
- **Members:** **Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji, M.R. Jayakar and Gandhi**
- **Findings:** Dyer's firing = **inhuman**; martial law = **disproportionate**; demanded action against Dyer
- ⚠ **Trap:** **Hunter** = official (Oct 1919); **Punjab Sub-Committee** = Congress, non-official

- **Crawling order:** Indians had to crawl on their bellies through the **Amritsar** lane where an English woman missionary (**Marcella Sherwood**) had been assaulted
- **Gandhi:** Stopped from entering **Punjab**
