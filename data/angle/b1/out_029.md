## Causes of decline → factor and effect | from: S5
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Aurangzeb's policies** | Empire at **greatest extent**: Kashmir to **Jinji**, Hindukush to **Chittagong**; intolerant policies + long **Deccan wars** → weakened foundations, drained treasury |
| **Weak successors and noble factions** | Sayyids' control bred corruption and mismanagement; nobles acted independently; rivalries broke central authority |
| **Jagirdari and agrarian crises** | Shrinking fiscal base → peasant and zamindar revolts |
| **Other causes** | Maratha rise, invasions of Nadir Shah and Abdali, European companies |

## Ashtapradhan → minister, Persian title and portfolio | from: S5
| **Minister** | **Persian / alternative title** | **Portfolio** |
|---|---|---|
| Peshwa | Mukhya Pradhan | **Prime Minister**, general administration |
| Amatya | **Majumdar** | **Finance**: treasury, accounts audit, revenue |
| **Sachiv** | **Shurnavis** (**Surnis**) | **Royal correspondence**, records, internal (home) affairs |
| **Mantri** | **Waqia-navis** (**Waqianavis**, Vakeenavis) | **Intelligence**, the king's diary, royal household and safety |
| Senapati | **Sar-i-Naubat** | **Military commander** (commander-in-chief) |
| Sumant | **Dabir** | **Foreign affairs**; **master of ceremonies** who received envoys |
| **Nyayadhish** | - | **Justice** (chief justice) |
| Panditrao | Danadhyaksha | **Religious affairs and charitable grants** |

- **Model:** Council modelled on the **Deccan Sultanates**
- **Features:**
  - Posts **not hereditary**; ministers could be **transferred or dismissed** at the king's will
  - Each reported directly to the Chhatrapati
  - All except **Panditrao and Nyayadhish** had to lead military campaigns
- **Later change:** Under the later Peshwas the Peshwa post became **hereditary and supreme**
- **PYQ answers:**
  - **Dabir = master of ceremonies** (APPSC-G1 2024)
  - **Sar-i-Naubat = military commander** (APPSC-G2 2023)
  - BPSC 2024: Sachiv-correspondence, Peshwa-PM, Sumant-foreign, Amatya-finance
- ⚠ **Sachiv** = correspondence; **Sumant** = foreign affairs; **Mantri** = intelligence
> Exam angle: title-to-portfolio matching.

## Revenue system → terms, units and officials | from: S6
# 1. Land revenue terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Ryotwari-type direct settlement** | State dealt with the cultivator |
| **Kathi** | Measuring rod; Annaji Datto's survey |
| **Jirayat** | Dry land |
| **Bagayat** | Irrigated land |
| **Saranjam** | Service land grants; returned under Shahu and the Peshwas |

- **Assessment:** On area cultivated and crop
- **State share:** **33%, later 40%** = **one-third to two-fifths** of gross produce
- **Relief:** Remission in famine; illegal cesses abolished; peasant property not seized
# 2. Units and officials
- **Divisions:** Kingdom divided into **prants**

| **Unit** | **Official** |
|---|---|
| Prant (province) | Subedar / Sarsubedar [GK] |
| Pargana, Tarf | Deshmukh, Deshpande [GK] |
| Mauza (village) | **Patil** (head), **Kulkarni** (accountant) [GK] |

# 3. Grants and hereditary rights
- **Jagirs abolished:** Officials paid in **cash**, not land grants
- **Deshmukhs:** Hereditary **deshmukhs and deshpandes** / watandars bypassed or regulated, their rights curbed; they resisted
  - Big **deshmukhs opposed Shivaji** as they did not back an independent Maratha state (UPPSC 2024 - A and R true, R explains A)

## Chauth and sardeshmukhi → rate, nature and recognition | from: S7
| **Levy** | **Rate** | **Nature / justification** |
|---|---|---|
| **Chauth** | **25%** (one-fourth) of land revenue | Levied on territories **outside direct Maratha rule**; protection money against third-party invasion, or to be spared Maratha raids |
| **Sardeshmukhi** | Additional **10%** | Claimed as the king's **hereditary right as Sardeshmukh** (chief headman) of the Deccan |

- **Where collected:** Lands **outside the Maratha realm** - Mughal and Deccan Sultanate territory
- **Purpose:** Financed military expansion + political hegemony **without the cost of direct governance**
- **Recognition:**
  - Formally conceded by the Mughals in **1719**
  - By the Nizam at **Palkhed**; Nizam forced to pay chauth again after the **1795** war
- **Rajputana (18th century):** Tribute and chauth, no Rajput state annexed (20 May 2026)
- ⚠ **Chauth = 25%** (protection); **Sardeshmukhi = 10%** (hereditary right)
> Exam angle: "Maratha revenue claim for protection" = **Chauth** (UPPSC 2018).

## Military organisation → forts, army and navy | from: S9
# 1. Forts
- **Forts:** Over **300 forts**
- **Bases:** Raigad, Pratapgad, Sinhagad = bases for defence and expansion
- **Fort officers:** Three of equal rank = **havaldar, sabnis and sarnaubat**
  - **Havaldar** = garrison commander
  - **Sabnis** = records
  - **Sarnobat / sarnaubat** = military officer
- **Adnyapatra** of Ramachandrapant Amatya: "forts are the core of the state"

| **Fort type** | **Examples** |
|---|---|
| Giri Durg (hill) | Raigad, Pratapgad, Torna, Rajgad, Sinhagad, Panhala |
| Bhu Durg (land) | Vishalgad |
| Jal Durg (sea) | **Sindhudurg**, **Vijaydurg**, Khanderi, Suvarnadurg |

# 2. Army
- **Command:** **Sar-i-Naubat** (see Ashtapradhan)
- **Hill troops:** **Mavali** infantry
- **Cavalry:** **bargirs** (state-equipped) vs **shiledars** (own horses)
- **Smallest unit:** Led by **havildar**
- **Pay:** Standing army **paid in cash**, no jagirs - loyalty direct to the state
- **Battle of Salher (1672):** Mughals beaten in open battle
# 3. Navy
- **Navy:** Shivaji built a navy
- **Sea forts / bases:** **Sindhudurg**, Kolaba, Vijaydurg - guarded the Konkan
- **Title:** **Father of the Indian Navy** (Feb 2026)
- ⚠ **Bargirs** = state horses; **shiledars** = own horses

## Shivaji's successors → years and role (1680-1749) | from: S3, S10, S11
| **Ruler** | **Years** | **Key steps / role** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sambhaji** | 1680-89 | Kept up resistance to the Mughals; captured and **executed by Aurangzeb (1689)**, which turned the conflict into a people's war |
| **Rajaram** | 1689-1700 | Younger son; moved to **Gingee (Jinji)**, directed resistance from there and drew Aurangzeb south; created the post of **Pratinidhi** |
| **Tarabai** (for **Shivaji II**) | 1700-1707 | Rajaram's widow; regent for her young son after Rajaram's death; massive raids into Mughal territory; led Marathas at **Aurangzeb's death (1707)** |
| **Shahu** | 1707-1749 | Sambhaji's son, **released by Bahadur Shah I** (1707); beat Tarabai at **Khed (1707)**; ruled from **Satara**; appointed **Balaji Vishwanath** Peshwa (**1713**) |

- **Jinji:** Became Rajaram's base against Aurangzeb
- **Aurangzeb:** Died in the Deccan in **1707** without subduing the Marathas
- **Battle of Khed (1707):** Shahu defeated Tarabai's forces, took the throne
- **Balaji Vishwanath:** Peshwa **1713-20**, appointed by **Shahu**
  - Backed Shahu in the civil war; rallied chiefs to him
  - Got the 1719 grants (see treaties page)
- **Power shift:** From **1713**, real power moved from the Chhatrapati at Satara → Peshwa
> Exam angle: order is **Sambhaji - Rajaram - Shivaji II - Shahu** (UPPSC 2005); "Maratha woman who fought the Mughals from 1700" = Tarabai.

## Universal angles | from: S12 | universal
# 1. Significance & comparison
- **Significance:**
  - **State model:** Centralised, non-hereditary, cash-paid state
  - **Mughal finances:** Marathas' Deccan war, especially Tarabai's raids, broke them
  - **Hindavi Swarajya:** Marathi and Sanskrit promoted in administration over Persian
  - **Chauth-sardeshmukhi:** Let a regional state become a pan-Indian power without direct administration
- **Comparative note:**
  - Mughal mansabdari rested on jagirs vs Shivaji paid in cash
  - Ashtapradhan advisory vs Mughal wazir system hierarchical
# 2. AP angle
- **AP angle:** Shivaji's **Golconda alliance (1677)** through Akkanna-Madanna; Tana Shah funded the Karnataka campaign
- **Chauth from the Nizam:** Taken after the **1795** war
  - Pushed the Nizam toward the British Subsidiary Alliance (1798)
# 3. Current relevance
- **Current relevance:** **Shiv Jayanti, 19 February**
  - First public celebration by **Jyotirao Phule**, **1870**, **Raigad**; popularised by Tilak
- **UNESCO:** **Maratha Military Landscapes of India** inscribed as World Heritage in **2025**
  - 12 forts incl. Gingee in Tamil Nadu
- **NCERT map row:** Removal of a "Maratha Empire" map from revised textbook → **PIL in the Bombay HC** (20 May 2026)
  - Peak extent: **Attock to Cuttack**; Gulf of Khambhat to beyond the Kaveri

=== ROW 141 | Peshwas and the Maratha confederacy; Third Battle of Panipat (1761); three Anglo-Maratha wars

## Rise of the Peshwa → causes and shift of power | from: S0
- **Shift:** From **1713 (Balaji Vishwanath)** real power moved from the Chhatrapati at **Satara** → Peshwa at **Poona**, the Peshwas' seat of power (APPSC 2018); monarchy became a confederacy

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Shahu owed his throne to Balaji Vishwanath | Peshwa gained real power |
| Office became **hereditary** | Peshwa line entrenched |
| Baji Rao I and Nana Saheb led all expansion | Conquests credited to the Peshwa |

- **Sangola agreement (1750):** After Shahu's death (1749), left the Chhatrapati nominal

## Peshwas → tenure, contribution and treaties (1713-1818) | from: S1, S2
# 1. Peshwas in order
| **Peshwa** | **Tenure** | **Contribution / treaties** |
|---|---|---|
| **Balaji Vishwanath** | 1713-20 | Appointed by **Shahu**; founded Peshwa power; got the **1719** Mughal grant of Shahu's swarajya plus chauth and sardeshmukhi of the Deccan through the Sayyids |
| **Baji Rao I** | 1720-40 | The greatest leader after Shivaji; won **Malwa and Bundelkhand**; raided **Delhi (1737)**; beat the Nizam at **Palkhed (1728)**, then the **Treaty of Mungi-Shevgaon** gave chauth and sardeshmukhi of the Deccan; organised the confederacy |
| **Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb)** | 1740-61 | **Zenith** of Maratha power; took **Gujarat** (APPSC 2025); reached Lahore and Peshawar; defeated the Nizam; lost **Third Panipat (1761)** |
| **Madhav Rao I** | 1761-72 | Revived power after Panipat; defeated the Nizam, Mysore (Haidar Ali), Rohillas, Rajputs and Jats; **Mahadji Scindia** restored Shah Alam II to Delhi (1771) |
| **Narayan Rao** | 1772-73 | Murdered (killed) in a succession struggle with his uncle **Raghunath Rao** |
| **Raghunath Rao** (Raghoba) | 1773-74 | Ousted by the **Barabhai** council; sought British help - First Anglo-Maratha War |
| **Madhav Rao II** | 1774-95 | Infant Peshwa ruling through **Nana Phadnavis**; confirmed by the **Treaty of Salbai**; beat the Nizam at **Kharda (1795)** |
| **Baji Rao II** | 1796-1818 | Signed the **Treaty of Bassein (1802)**; **the last Peshwa**; deposed and pensioned; office abolished |

# 2. Baji Rao I
- **Stature:** Most charismatic Maratha leader after Shivaji
- **Ideology:** **Hindu Pad Padshahi**; "strike the trunk, the branches will fall"
- **Conquests:** **Malwa and Bundelkhand**; raided **Delhi** (1737)
- **Nizam:** Beaten at Palkhed and Bhopal
# 3. Traps and PYQs
- **Madhav Rao II:** Installed as an infant in **1774**; Salbai (1782) recognised him
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes say he was installed after Salbai - wrong
- **Chronology PYQs:**
  - UPPSC 2025: Balaji Vishwanath > Baji Rao I > Balaji Baji Rao > Madhav Rao I
  - UPPSC 2024: Balaji Vishwanath > Balaji Baji Rao > Narayan Rao > Raghunath Rao
- **Name traps:**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Balaji Vishwanath called "Nana Saheb" - wrong; **Nana Saheb = Balaji Baji Rao**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Raghoba called Madhav Rao I's "younger brother" - he was his **uncle**
> Exam angle: UPPSC 2024 order - Balaji Vishwanath, Balaji Baji Rao, Narayan Rao, Raghunath Rao; APPSC 2025 - Gujarat under **Balaji Baji Rao**.

## Maratha leaders beyond the Peshwas → role and title | from: S2, S3, S6
| **Person** | **Role / known as** |
|---|---|
| **Chimaji Appa** | Took **Salsette and Bassein** from the Portuguese (1739) |
| **Nana Phadnavis** | Led the **Barabhai** (12 chiefs) council; regent for infant Madhav Rao II (1774-95), against Raghunath Rao; "**Maratha Machiavelli**"; held the confederacy together in the First Anglo-Maratha War, with Mahadji Scindia as northern arm |
| **Mahadji Scindia / Shinde** | (1771) Recaptured Delhi; Shah Alam II restored under Maratha protection → Marathas became king-makers of the Mughals; modernised army with European-style infantry and artillery |
| Senapati **Dabhade** | Led the older chiefs whom the confederacy had to satisfy (UPSC spells it "Dabodi") |

- **After Nana Phadnavis's death (1800):** Holkar and Scindia fought over the weak Peshwa (1801-02) → Bassein + Second War

## Maratha confederacy → houses, seats and administration | from: S3
| **House** | **Seat / capital** | **Founder** |
|---|---|---|
| **Peshwa** | **Poona (Pune)** | Balaji Vishwanath |
| **Bhonsle** | **Nagpur** | Raghuji Bhonsle |
| **Gaekwad** | **Baroda** | Pilaji Gaekwad |
| **Holkar** | **Indore** | Malhar Rao Holkar |
| **Scindia (Shinde)** | **Gwalior** | Ranoji Scindia |

- **Origin:** **Baji Rao I** built the confederacy
  - To keep Maratha factions together
  - To satisfy older chiefs led by senapati Dabhade
- **Nominal head:** Chiefs worked under authority of **Shahu**
- **Saranjam:** Land assignments to chiefs for military service - base of the confederacy
- **Peshwa Daftar:** At Pune; Peshwa-era state records, largely in Modi script
- **Main weakness:** Internal rivalries + semi-independent chiefs put regional interest first → main cause of decline
- **Later decline:** Corruption + slack central and provincial government
- ⚠ **Holkar** = Indore; **Scindia** = Gwalior; **Gaekwad** = Baroda; **Bhonsle** = Nagpur

## Battles and treaties with the Nizam → year and result (1728-1795) | from: S4
| **Battle / treaty** | **Year** | **Peshwa** | **Result** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Palkhed** | 1728 [GK] | Baji Rao I | Nizam-ul-Mulk defeated; chauth and sardeshmukhi of the Deccan recognised |
| **Bhopal** / Treaty of Doraha Sarai | 1737-38 [GK] | Baji Rao I | Nizam ceded Malwa [GK] |
| Udgir | 1760 [GK] | Balaji Baji Rao | Nizam defeated |
| Rakshasbhuvan | 1763 [GK] | Madhav Rao I | Nizam defeated |

- **Mungi-Shevgaon:** Treaty between the **Marathas and the Nizam** (APPSC 2025), after Palkhed
  - Some notes call it the "Treaty of Palkhed", after the battle
> Exam angle: APPSC 2025 - Mungi-Shevgaon was between the **Marathas and the Nizam**.

## Third Battle of Panipat (1761) → sides, causes and consequences | from: S5
# 1. The battle
| **Item** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| Date | **14 January 1761**[GK] |
| Opponents | **Marathas vs Ahmad Shah Abdali** (APPSC-G1 2018) |
| Maratha commanders | **Sadashiv Rao Bhau** (commander) and **Vishwas Rao** (the Peshwa's son); both killed. Artillery under **Ibrahim Khan Gardi**[GK] |
| Peshwa at the time | **Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb)** |
| Mughal emperor | **Shah Alam II** |
| Abdali's immediate motive | The Marathas had driven his son **Timur Shah** out of the **Punjab** (Lahore, 1758) [GK] |
| Abdali's allies | Rohilla chief **Najib-ud-Daula**, and **Shuja-ud-Daula** of Awadh [GK] |
| Maratha allies | Almost no allies - Jats (Suraj Mal) and Rajputs stayed away [GK] |
| Eyewitness chronicler | **Kashiraj Pandit** (**Kashi Raj Pandit**), Shuja-ud-Daula's official [GK] |

# 2. Causes
| **Cause** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| **Immediate cause** | Maratha push into Punjab threatened Abdali's hold; Raghunath Rao reached Lahore-Attock (1758); Najib invited Abdali |
| **Causes of defeat** | No allies in the north; supply collapse + starvation in camp; heavy baggage and non-combatants; earlier plunder alienated Rajputs and Jats |

# 3. Consequences
- **Northern ambitions:** Halted; many leaders lost
- **Peshwa:** Balaji Baji Rao died soon afterwards (1761)
- **Chiefs:** Peshwa control weakened, especially after Madhav Rao I's death in 1772
- **British gain:** Power vacuum helped the Company (won Plassey 1757, Wandiwash 1760)
- **Not destroyed:** Maratha power recovered **within a decade**
> Exam angle: APPSC 2018 - "Third Battle of Panipat was fought between Ahmad Shah Abdali and the Marathas".

## Anglo-Maratha wars → treaties, years and Governors-General | from: S6, S7
# 1. First Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82)
- **Cause:** Succession dispute after Narayan Rao's murder
  - **Barabhai** (twelve chiefs led by **Nana Phadnavis**) backed infant **Madhav Rao II**
  - **Raghunath Rao** turned to the British; offered **Salsette**, **Bassein** + revenue from Surat and Bharuch

| **Treaty / event** | **Year** | **Point** |
|---|---|---|
| Treaty of **Surat** | **1775** | Bombay government agreed to back Raghunath Rao with 2,500 troops |
| Treaty of **Purandar** | 1776 | Calcutta council annulled Surat |
| **Talegaon** and Convention of **Wadgaon** | **1779** | British defeated and forced to give up gains; **Warren Hastings** rejected **Wadgaon** |
| **Treaty of Salbai** | **1782** | Ended the war; concluded with Mahadji Scindia |

- ⚠ **Surat (1775)** = British back Raghunath Rao; **Purandar (1776)** = annulled; **Salbai (1782)** = end
# 2. Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817-18)
- **Governor-General:** **Lord Hastings** (Marquess of Hastings)
- **Causes:** His imperial designs + **Pindari campaigns** (seen by Marathas as violating sovereignty)
- **Participants:** Nagpur (Bhonsle) and Indore (Holkar) joined
- **Battles:** **Khadki (Kirkee)**, **Koregaon**, **Sitabuldi**, **Mahidpur**
- **Outcome:**
  - **Peshwaship abolished in 1818**
  - **Baji Rao II** deposed + **pensioned off to Bithur** near Kanpur; territory annexed
  - Small Satara state created for Shivaji's descendant
- **Dating:** Some notes say 1817-19; others 1817-18
- ⚠ **Warren Hastings** = First war (rejected Wadgaon); **Lord Hastings** = Third war

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
# 1. Significance & comparison
- **Significance:** British "captured India from the Marathas, not the Mughals"; factionalism let the Company break them in three wars
- **Comparative note:**
  - Salbai (1782) = status quo
  - Bassein (1802) = subsidiary subordination
  - 1818 = extinction of the Peshwaship
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - First Peshwa of Shahu's line: **Balaji Vishwanath**
  - Last Peshwa: **Baji Rao II**
  - Salbai = first Anglo-Maratha peace
# 2. AP angle
- **AP angle:** **1795 war:** Nizam defeated + forced to pay **chauth**
  - Blamed **Sir John Shore**'s neutrality → **Subsidiary Alliance (1798)**
- **Third Mysore War:** Marathas + Nizam joined the British against Tipu
  - **Treaty of Srirangapatnam (1792):** Half of Rayalaseema (Bellary, Gandikota, Kadapa and others) went to the Nizam
- **Repeated defeats of the Nizam:** Palkhed, Udgir, Kharda; chauth from the Nizam's Deccan
# 3. Current relevance
- **Current relevance:** **NCERT map debate:** Extent of Maratha rule debated after map removed; Rajputana held as tribute zone, not annexed

=== ROW 142 | Bhakti Movement - origins, Alvars and Nayanars, Nirguna and Saguna streams, social base

## Bhakti → meaning, scriptural roots, origin and spread | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. Meaning and roots
- **Etymology:** bhakti from the root **"bhaj"** (to share or participate)
- **Roots:** **Upanishads**, **Bhagavad Gita**, **Bhagavata Purana**
- **Gita, Chapter 12 (Canto 12) = Bhakti Yoga:** Both Saguna and Nirguna valid; Saguna upasana easier for ordinary people / embodied beings vs Nirguna harder (17 Aug 2026)
- **Bhagavata Purana:** First to put bhakti at the centre of religion
  - Dating: some notes say 6th c. (6th century AD); most scholars c. 9th-10th c.
  - **Ninefold bhakti** (navadha): shravana, kirtana, smarana, padasevana, archana, vandana, dasya, sakhya, atmanivedana
# 2. Origin and spread
- **Began in the South (Tamil region first):** Alvars and Nayanars (6th-9th c.)
  - **Alvars:** Vishnu devotees, **12**
  - **Nayanars:** Shiva devotees, **63**
- **Spread:** From the Tamil region → reached the northern belt by the **15th c.**
- **Overall span:** **6th to 18th c.**
- ⚠ **Alvars = 12** (Vishnu); **Nayanars = 63** (Shiva)

## Sacred texts → tradition and compiler | from: S1
| **Text** | **Tradition** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nalayira Divya Prabandham** | **Alvars** (Sri Vaishnavism) | **4,000** hymns, compiled by **Nathamuni**[GK]; the "Tamil Veda" [GK] |
| **Tevaram** | **Nayanars** | Hymns of Appar, Sambandar and Sundarar; the first seven books of the Tirumurai [GK] |
| **Tiruvasagam** | Nayanars | **Manikkavachakar**[GK] |
| **Tirumurai** | Shaiva canon (Nayanars) | **12 books** compiled by **Nambi Andar Nambi**[GK]; called the "**Fifth Veda**" (JPSC 2021) |
| **Periya Puranam** | Shaiva hagiography | By **Sekkizhar** under Kulottunga II, on the lives of the **63 Nayanars**; the 12th Tirumurai [GK] |

- ⚠ "Tamil Veda" = **Nalayira Divya Prabandham**; "Fifth Veda" = **Tirumurai**

## Saints and forerunners → contribution | from: S2, S4, S5
| **Saint** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Shankaracharya** | Forerunner; b. **788** at **Kaladi**, d. 820; Advaita |
| **Ramananda** | Forerunner who brought it to the north; first medieval reformer to denounce caste outright; admitted disciples of all castes |
| **Kabir** | Disciple of Ramananda; called the greatest saint of the cult; raised by Muslim weaver **Niru and Nima**; one God, many names (Ram, Rahim, Allah, Hari); condemned ritual on both sides |
| **Guru Nanak** | b. **1469**, Talwandi; opposed idol worship |
| **Dadu Dayal** (Rajasthan) | Founded the **Dadu Panth**, in the Kabir tradition |
| **Palnati Brahmanayudu** | Set up **Chapakudu** (common dining for all castes); treated Mala devotee **Kannama Dasu** as his son |
| **Vemana** and **Potuluri Veerabrahmam** | Condemned caste |

- **Women saints:** Andal, Karaikkal Ammaiyar, **Akkamahadevi** (12th c., Karnataka), Mirabai, **Daya Bai**

## Nirguna, Saguna and Nathpanthi streams → idea and saints | from: S3, S4
# 1. The two streams
| **Stream** | **Idea of God** | **Saints** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nirguna** | Formless and attributeless; rejects idol worship (e.g. the Sikh **Nirankar**) | **Kabir** (APPSC GS 2024), **Guru Nanak**, **Dadu Dayal** [GK for Dadu], **Ravidas (Raidas)** |
| **Saguna** | God with form and attributes: avatars and personal deities | Vaishnavas: **Ramamargis** - **Tulsidas** (Rama); **Krishnamargis** - **Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya**, Vallabha (Krishna) |

- **Common base:** Both streams drew on the Upanishadic doctrine of Advaita; movement essentially monotheistic
- ⚠ **Ramamargi** = Tulsidas; **Krishnamargi** = Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya
# 2. Nathpanthis, Siddhas and Yogis
- **Role:** Made devotional religion popular in **Northern India** (BPSC 2023)
- **Leading figures:** **Gorakhnath**, Matsyendranath
- **Practices:** Hatha yoga, meditation, renunciation
- **Criticised / rejected:** Ritualism, caste system, orthodox religion
- **Influence:** Used vernacular; influenced Kabir's nirguna idiom
- **Siddhacharyas (AP-Karnataka Veerashaiva tradition):** Panchacharyas **Revana (Renuka)**, **Marula**, **Ekorama**, Panditaradhya, Vishwaradhya
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Udbhata** is **not** a Siddhacharya; he was a Sanskrit rhetorician (APPSC-G2 2012)

## Causes, beliefs and limits → factor and effect | from: S2, S5
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Reaction** | Against ritualism, priestly dominance and caste |
| **Hard paths** | **Gyana marga** and **karma marga** too hard for ordinary people |

- **Debate:** Older historians = reaction to Islam vs modern scholars = indigenous (Alvar-Nayanar) roots; Islam shaped only later egalitarianism
- **Core beliefs:** Monotheism; guru essential; salvation through devotion
- **Rejected:** Idol worship, ritual, pilgrimage, fasting, caste
- **Limit:** Only **moderated** caste rigidity to an extent

## Universal angles | from: S7 | universal
# 1. Significance
- **Significance:** Vernacular literature (Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati) flourished
- **Sikhism:** Through Nanak's successors → emerged as an organised religion
- **Society:** Brought Hindus and Muslims closer; weakened caste bondage / attack on caste
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Andal = only female Alvar
  - Ramanuja = first true exponent
  - Ramananda = first to denounce caste outright in the north
# 2. Criticism & debates
- **Criticism & debates:** Did not fully achieve reformation of Hinduism or lasting Hindu-Muslim unity
- **Origins debate:** Arose from Islam vs indigenous roots
# 3. AP angle
- **AP angle:** **Veerashaivism** Reached Andhra through Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya and Palkuriki Somanatha
- **Palnadu:** Veeravaishnavism + the Chapakudu
- **Anti-caste thread:** Vemana and Veerabrahmam
- **Vallabhacharya:** Telugu Brahmin; visited **Krishnadevaraya's** court
# 4. Current relevance
- **Current relevance:** **Ravidassia community** Renewed demand for separate "Ravidassia religion" category in the Census (15 Jul 2026)
  - Guru Ravidas envisioned **Begampura** (city without sorrow)
  - Main seat: **Dera Sachkhand Ballan** (Punjab)
