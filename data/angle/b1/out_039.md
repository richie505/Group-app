## Battle of Padmanabham, 1794 → background, parties, outcome | from: S1
# 1. Background
- **Ruler:** **Chinna Vijayarama Raju** (ruled 1760-94) removed step-brother and Diwan **Sitarama Raju** for misrule (1784)
  - Sitarama Raju bribed Council members → got his post back
  - Peshkash fell into arrears
- **Company demand:** **Rs 3 lakh** / **₹3 lakh** tribute + disbanding of troops → Raja **(Chinna) Vijayarama Raju** refused
- **2 Aug 1793:** British seized the Vizianagaram fort
  - Estate split into **3 parts** (some notes: three collectorates)
  - Raja ordered to live in Machilipatnam on a pension of **Rs 1,200**
# 2. The battle

| **Point** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| Battle | **Padmanabham**, between Bhimunipatnam and Vizianagaram |
| Date | **10 July 1794** (CDX, APHQ; CDI gives 8 July) |
| Parties | English East India Company vs **Vizianagaram zamindar Pusapati (Chinna) Vijayarama Gajapati Raju (II)** |
| British commander | Col. "Fender Gast" in CDX's spelling (Pendergast) |
| Result | British victory; the Raja and about 1,000 Kshatriya warriors (over a thousand) killed; compared to **Culloden** |

# 3. Outcome
- Vizianagaram reduced to an **ordinary zamindari**
- Minor son **Narayana Babu** (hid at **Makkuva**) restored on accepting the **Permanent Settlement (1802)** + **Rs 6 lakh peshkash** / **₹6 lakh peshkash**
- George Russell made special officer over the forts
> Exam angle: "Padmanabham decided whose defeat?" = Vijayarama Raju of Vizianagaram, not Bobbili.

## Limited response in Andhra → reasons; aftermath → repression, rewards, Crown rule | from: S12, S13
# 1. Reasons for the limited response (assertion-reason)
- **Nature:** Only scattered disturbances; Andhra stayed **loyal**
- **Background grievances:** 1800 cession; Wahabi conspiracy of 1839; **1853 Berar treaty** (Berar, Raichur, Osmanabad/Dharashiv)
- **Reasons for loyalty:**
  - **Nizam and Salar Jung loyal**; Madras Presidency quiet
  - Zamindars gained from the Permanent Settlement
  - People valued the stability of Company rule over the "earlier anarchy"
  - Public loyalty meetings at Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Vijayawada
- **Kadiri** (Rayalaseema): Attempt made to incite rebellion (APPSC G2 2008)
- **APPSC-G2 2025 key:** answer **(4)**
  - A ("unaffected because Andhra had **no local leaders**") = **false** - Turrebaz Khan, Subba Reddy, Dandasena etc. existed
  - R (Afzal-ud-Daula pro-British) = **true**
# 2. Repression
- **Executions:** Subba Reddy, Dandasena, Fida Ali hanged; Sheikh Peer Sahib arrested; across India thousands executed after mock trials
# 3. Rewards to loyalists
- **Afzal-ud-Daula** received the "**Star of India**"; Salar Jung (Mir Turab Ali Khan) honoured
- Raichur Doab + Dharashiv (Dharaseo, Osmanabad) restored to Hyderabad; its debt written off

## Universal angles | from: S16 | universal
# 1. Significance
- **Significance / local revolts:** South India's revolts were local and scattered; the Nizam's loyalty kept the Deccan closed to 1857
- **Consolidation:** Took the Company **50-60 years** to consolidate its hold on Andhra (zamindars on the coast + palegars in Rayalaseema)
- **1846 revolt:** Last major palegar rising in Rayalaseema, a decade before 1857
- **Hyderabad:** Its loyalty kept the revolt from spreading in the Deccan
# 2. FIRST/LAST & comparisons
- **FIRST/LAST:** Vellore 1806 - first big sepoy mutiny, half a century before 1857
- **Comparative note:** 1800-01 palegar rising (80 palegars, Munro) vs 1846 (single-leader guerrilla revolt with a peasant and tribal base)
- **Criticism & debates:** Revolt lacked central leadership, a programme and a base in the south
# 3. AP angle & current relevance
- **AP angle:** Telugu valour symbols + new district
- **Bobbili:** Ranga Rao and Tandra Paparayudu remain symbols of Telugu valour
- **Uyyalawada:** Now in Nandyal district, after the 2022 district reorganisation
- **Current relevance:** PM paid tribute to **Mangal Pandey** on his birth anniversary
  - He attacked officers at Barrackpore on **29 March 1857** (21 Jul 2026)

## Brahmo tenets and social programme (statement type) | from: S3
# 1. Religious tenets
- **Monotheism:** Worship of one formless, eternal God
- **Sources:** Rational teachings of the **Upanishads, Bible and Koran**
- **Banned:** **idols, animal sacrifice and sectarian symbols** in its halls
- **Rejected:** Priestly ritualism + caste distinctions
- **Scripture:** None treated as final authority; no belief in avatars
- **Nature:** Theistic (**Astika**) reform within Hinduism, not a new religion
  - No heaven or hell
  - Soul (Jivatma) and Supreme Soul (Paramatma) are not one
  - Collective prayer
# 2. Social programme
- **Against:** **child marriage, polygamy, female infanticide, caste**
- **For:** **widow remarriage, women's education and property rights**
- **UPSC 2025 key:** He respected the traditional philosophies of the East **and** wanted a rational, scientific approach - **both** statements correct

## Paramahansa Mandali and Prarthana Samaj → nature, aims, agenda | from: S2, S3
# 1. Paramahansa Mandali, 1849
- **Nature:** **secret society**; one God; members broke caste rules by eating food cooked by lower castes
- **Aims:** Against caste discrimination; for **women's education** + **widow remarriage**
# 2. Prarthana Samaj, 1867
- **Nature:** **offshoot of the Brahmo Samaj**, reform within Hinduism
- **Inspiration:** **Keshab Chandra Sen**'s visit
- **Four-point social agenda:**
  - (1) Disapproval of the **caste system**
  - (2) **women's education**
  - (3) **widow remarriage**
  - (4) Raising the **age of marriage** for men and women
- **Additional aims:** Inter-dining, inter-marriage, uplift of the depressed classes
- **Method:** **persuasion and social work**, not confrontation
- **Idol worship:** Unlike the Brahmos, did **not outright forbid idol worship or abandon caste**; focus social, not theological (p.84)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** p.83 claims it rejected idol worship - treat p.84 as the exam-safe version

## Arya Samaj, 1875 → founding, base, programme | from: S1
- **Founder:** **Swami Dayananda Saraswati**
- **Founding:** **Bombay in 1875** (April 1875)
  - Some notes give **7 April 1875**; many texts give 10 April 1875
- **Name:** "Society of Nobles"
- **Base:** Strongest in **Punjab**; HQ later shifted to **Lahore**; also strong in Rajasthan + United Provinces

## Arya Samaj movements → Shuddhi and the Hyderabad satyagraha | from: S3, S4
# 1. Shuddhi movement
- **Started by:** **Arya Samaj**
- **Aim:** Ritual "purification" to **reconvert** Hindus who had converted to **Islam or Christianity** + admit outsiders into the Hindu fold
- **Later leader:** **Swami Shraddhanand**
  - Led the 1920s Shuddhi campaign through the **Bharatiya Hindu Shuddhi Sabha (1923)**
  - Assassinated in 1926
- **Impact:** Contributed to the communalisation of social life → later communal political consciousness
> Exam angle: "Shuddhi was started by ___" → Arya Samaj (not Brahmo Samaj, Satyashodhak Samaj or Ramakrishna Mission).

  - Ran alongside the Hyderabad State Congress satyagraha
- **Impact:** Helped build political awakening against Nizam rule in the Telangana region
