## Nayankara system and military organisation — features | from: S2, S3
# 1. Nayankara system
- **Definition:** Military commanders (**Nayakas**) made administrators of **Sthalas**/**Mandalams**; each protected a specific **fort**
- **Who introduced it:** **Prataparudra II** (APPSC 2025 key) → reorganised the kingdom into nayakships
  - Other notes: Rudrama Devi began it in Ganapatideva's time; Prataparudra strengthened it
- **Number:** **72 Nayankaras**, each guarding a fort; other notes: **75 (or 77)** nayakships/principalities
- !! Not in your sources: the number of bastions of the Warangal fort tied to the Nayakas.
- **Land instead of salary:**
  - Villages called **Nayakasthalam**
  - Nayankaras controlled **one-fourth (1/4)** of the kingdom's land
  - Collected revenue → kept a share for a fixed army for the king → paid **fixed annual sum/tribute**, remitting the rest
- **Sources:** Baddena's **Neeti Sastra Muktavali**; Ekamranatha's **Pratapa Charitra**
- **Legacy:** Continued by Vijayanagara as **Amara Nayankara** / **Amara-Nayankara** system
  - Land = **Amaram**; tribute = **Kappam/kist**
  - Its break-up → **Poligars**
> Exam angle: statement questions on "who introduced the Nayankara system" → Prataparudra II.
# 2. Military organisation
- **Chaturanga bala:** Four arms
  - Prataparudra's army = **100 elephants, 20,000 cavalry, 900,000 infantry**
- **Two divisions:** **Mula Balam** (king's standing army) + **Nayankara army**
- **Fort types:**
  - **Giridurga** (hill), **Jaladurga** (water), **Vanadurga** (forest), Sthaladurga (plain)
  - Warangal fort = concentric walls + moat
- ⚠ **Mula Balam** = king's own army; **Nayankara army** = Nayakas' contingents

## Sects → founders, traits and tenets | from: S2, S3, S4
# 1. Jainism
- **Rulers:** **Early Kakatiyas were Jains** (APPSC G2 2011) → Shaivas from **Prola II / Rudradeva**
- **Decline:** Kalamukha + Veerashaiva hostility
  - Kakatiyas credited with final eradication of Jainism in the region
- **Syncretism:** Jain deities absorbed as Hindu goddesses (Kakitamma, Jogulamba)
# 2. Shaiva sects
| **Sect** | **Founder / trait** |
|---|---|
| Pasupata | oldest Shaiva sect; Lakulisa (Nakulisa) |
| Kalamukha | Kalasana/Kalanana, disciple of Lakulisa; temple managers |
| Kapalika | offshoot of **Pasupata** (APPSC G2 2019); skull-bowl ascetics |

- **Patronage:** Kakatiyas patronised the "extremist" **Pasupata and Kalamukha** sects
- **Kalamukha divisions:** **Simha Parishat** (Andhra) vs Shakti Parishat (Karnataka)
- **Pasupata Shaivism:** Popular faith into the **Reddi** period (APPSC G2 2011) → declined under Vijayanagara
> Exam angle: "Kapalikas arose from which doctrine" and "popular religion of the Reddi Rajas" - both answer Pasupata.
# 3. Veerashaivism
- **Founder:** **Basaveshwara**
  - **First social-reform religion** in Andhra
  - Followers = Lingayats; gurus = **Jangamas**; texts = Agamas
- **Tenets:**
  - Rejected Vedas, caste, untouchability, child marriage, animal sacrifice
  - **Ishtalinga** worn on the body; "Kayakave Kailasa" (work is worship)
- **In Andhra:** "Veerashaiva" absent from inscriptions → followers = **Veeravratulu / Asankhyakulu**
- **Aradhya stream:** Brahmin Shaivas → kept Vedic ritual + varna, unlike radical Veerashaivas
- **Kakatiya stance:** Neutral - grants to orthodox Brahmin sects, no persecution of Lingayats
- ⚠ **Simha Parishat** = Andhra; Shakti Parishat = Karnataka
- ⚠ **Aradhyas** kept varna; radical Veerashaivas rejected caste

## Kakatiya style — features, terms and influences | from: S1, S3
# 1. Local innovations
| **Innovation** | **What it is** |
|---|---|
| Stellate (star) plan | raised **star-shaped platform**; more wall surface for sculpture, more light and air |
| Sandbox foundation | deep pits filled with sand, stone chips, earth - shock absorber on black cotton soil; why Ramappa has survived earthquakes |
| Floating bricks | light porous bricks in Ramappa's vimana - float on water; reduce the dead weight of the tower |
| Ekasila carving | monolithic Nandi and pillars in black basalt, as in the Thousand Pillar Nandi; granite |
| Lathe-turned pillars, Kirtimukha motif | polished granite pillars; "face of glory" motif |

- **Brackets:** **Madanika/bracket figures** projecting from outer walls in dance poses
- **Other features:** High adhisthana, jaali parapets, toranas, rangamandapas, swan and lotus motifs
# 2. Influences and phases
- **Influence:** **Western Chalukyas of Kalyani** (carved pillars, star-shaped platforms, ornate doorways, sculptured ceilings)
  - APPSC G2 2025 statement II correct
  - Follows **Chalukyan (Vesara)** style per some notes; others call it a **transitional style** between early Chalukyan and Vijayanagara
- **Jain phase:** **Early Kakatiya kings built Jain temples** (APPSC G2 2025 statement I)
  - e.g. **Padmakshi temple, Hanamkonda** (Prola II), originally a Jain shrine
- ⚠ **Floating bricks** = lighter tower; **sandbox foundation** = earthquake cushion

## Temple components and orders → terms | from: S0, S1
# 1. Basic parts
- **Core:** **Garbhagriha** (sanctum) + **mandapa** (hall)
- **Tower:** **Shikhara** in the north vs **vimana** in the south
- **Axis:** Vahana and dhvaja pillar set axially before the sanctum
- **Nandi mandapa:** Introduced in the Badami Chalukya phase at Pattadakal
- **Trikutalaya:** Three shrines on one plan
- **Kalyana mandapa:** Vijayanagara thousand-pillared hall = peak of the pillared-hall tradition
# 2. Nagara, Dravida and Vesara
| **Order** | **Marker** | **Andhra-region example** |
|---|---|---|
| Nagara | Curvilinear shikhara; sanctum under the tallest tower | Kalinga variant on the north coast - Mukhalingam, Simhachalam [GK] |
| Dravida | Pyramidal vimana, enclosure walls, gopurams | Eastern Chalukya delta temples; Vijayanagara temples |
| Vesara | Blend of both; stepped shikhara | Kakatiya temples (CDX) |

- **Pattadakal:** Nagara (Papanatha) and Dravida (Virupaksha) side by side
- ⚠ **Shikhara** = north (Nagara); **vimana** = south (Dravida); Vesara = blend (Kakatiya)
