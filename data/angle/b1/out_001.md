## IVC chronology → phases and dates | from: S2
# 1. Phases
| **Phase** | **Period** | **Features** |
|---|---|---|
| Early Harappan | **3300-2600 BCE** | Kot Diji, Amri, Sothi-Siswal; villages |
| **Mature** Harappan | **2600-1900 BCE** | Planned cities, 1:2:4 bricks, script, uniform weights |
| Late Harappan | **1900-1300 BCE** | De-urbanisation; OCP and Cemetery-H cultures |

# 2. Dating → scholar / method
| **Scholar / method** | **Date** | **Basis** |
|---|---|---|
| **John Marshall** | **3250-2750 BCE** | **Mesopotamian cross-dating** |
| **Radiocarbon dating** (D. P. Agrawal) | About **2500-1750 BCE** | Some notes also give c. 2500-1750 BC |

- ⚠ **Marshall** (cross-dating) = **3250-2750 BCE**; **Agrawal** (radiocarbon) = **2500-1750 BCE**
# 3. Dholavira dates
- **Dholavira:** Inhabited **3000-1800 BCE** (APPSC 2018 key; ⚠ Sources differ: UNESCO/ASI seven stages run c. 3000-1500 BCE incl. the Late Harappan phase; exams have used 3000-1800 BCE)
  - Habitation span: **Seven stages, c. 3000-1500 BCE** (UNESCO: 3rd to mid-2nd millennium BCE)

=== ROW 6 | IVC town planning - grid layout, citadel and lower town, drainage, Great Bath, granaries

## Town planning features — streets, houses and wells | from: S0, S3
# 1. Hallmarks and streets
- **Hallmark:** Town planning (PYQ APPSC G2 2011); no temples found
- **Grid layout / grid pattern:** Streets run **north-south and east-west**, crossing at **right angles** → city divided into blocks (Mohenjodaro, Harappa)
  - Mohenjodaro shows the first street layout
- **Main streets:** About **30 feet (~10 m)** wide
- **Banawali:** Radial streets (only city)
- ⚠ Grid = Mohenjodaro, Harappa; **radial** = Banawali only
# 2. Houses - features
| **Feature** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| **Plan** | Rooms round a **central courtyard** |
| **Multi-storeyed** | **Staircases** show multi-storey houses |
| **Bathrooms** | Burnt-brick floors connected to the drains |
| **Privacy** | **Doors and windows opened onto side lanes**, not main streets |
| **Social differentiation** | Housing fairly uniform but house size varied |

# 3. Wells → site
- **Wells:**
  - Inside houses or neighbourhoods in the developed stage at **Mohenjodaro** (PYQ UPPSC 2004)
  - **Mohenjodaro has the most wells**
  - Well irrigation at Allahdino (Sindh)

## Sites → layout — citadel, lower town, three-part plan | from: S1, S8
# 1. Divisions
- **Citadel (west)**: **Western** side = raised, fortified **citadel** on a platform with public buildings
- **Lower town (east)**: **Eastern** side = **lower town** for commoners' houses
- Seen at Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Kalibangan
# 2. Site → plan
| **Site** | **Plan** |
|---|---|
| Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Kalibangan | Citadel + lower town |
| Kalibangan | Citadel + **fortified lower town** (lower town also fortified) |
| **Dholavira** | **Three parts**: citadel (castle + bailey), **middle town**, lower town |
| **Chanhudaro** | **No citadel** (only such city) |
| Lothal | Citadel and lower town in one enclosure [GK] |
| Sutkagendor | Citadel + lower city |

- **Three-part plan:** Only IVC city **divided into three parts** (citadel, middle town, lower town); remnants of three townships (CGPSC 2015)
- ⚠ **Chanhudaro** = no citadel; **Dholavira** = three parts; **Kalibangan** = lower town also fortified

## Building materials — bricks, ratio and statement traps | from: S2, S8
- **Common material:** **Burnt (kiln-fired) bricks**, standard ratio **1:2:4** (thickness : width : length)
- **Mud bricks:** Fillings and platforms; at Kalibangan
- **Sizes:** **Not the same for every construction** - same ratio, different size

| **Use** | **Brick size** |
|---|---|
| House bricks | **7x14x28 cm** |
| City-wall bricks | **10x20x40 cm** |

- **Exception - Dholavira:** **Sandstone and limestone** blocks, not baked brick (vs baked brick at Mohenjodaro, Harappa)
- **PYQ (CGPSC 2022):**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Same size of brick for all buildings" = **false** (house and wall bricks differ)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Most houses were single-storeyed" = **false** (staircases prove multi-storey)
  - "Main roads about 10 m wide" = true
> Exam angle: statement questions - "same size bricks for all constructions" and "houses mostly single-storey" are both **false** (CGPSC 2022).

## Drainage — features | from: S4
- **Status:** Most advanced feature of IVC towns
- **Flow:** House drains → street drains → **soak pits**/main channels
# 1. Design
- Drains **covered**, with **manholes** for cleaning
- Built of burnt brick; **terracotta pipes** for wastewater
- **Slope**: streets sloped for natural flow
# 2. Site → drainage
- **Coverage:** Even small settlements had planned drainage
  - **Kalibangan:** **Wooden drains**
  - Banawali lacked a systematic system (no systematic drainage)
- ⚠ **Wooden drains** = Kalibangan; no systematic drainage = Banawali

## Great Bath — site, size, materials, purpose | from: S5
| **Angle** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Site** | **Mohenjodaro** (PYQ UPPSC 2010), in the citadel mound |
| **Size** | About **12 m x 7 m, 2.4 m deep**; flights of steps at north + south ends |
| **Materials** | **Burnt bricks** set in **gypsum mortar**, sealed with a layer of **bitumen** (waterproofing) |

- ⚠ **Gypsum** = mortar; **bitumen** = waterproofing layer
- **Purpose debate:**
  - Most: **ritual bathing**/purification
  - Others: a public water tank
  - Ritual bathing may survive in later temple tanks and ghats

## Dholavira → water harvesting and other features | from: S8, S9
- **Water management (UPSC 2021):**
  - **Giant reservoirs**, partly cut into the rock
  - Series of **bunds (check-dams)** + channels feeding the reservoirs
  - Dams and embankments
  - **16 reservoirs** cover about **10%** of the site (largest ~73 x 29 m)
- **Other features:** **Stadium** (ceremonial ground); **signboard** inscription
- **Rank:** Dholavira = one of the **five largest** Harappan cities (with Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Ganweriwala, Rakhigarhi)
- **Occupation:** Occupied through **seven stages, c. 3000-1500 BCE (APPSC 2018 key: 3000-1800 BCE)**

# 1. Weights
- **Material:** **Chert** (also limestone, steatite); generally **cubical**
- **Standard unit:** About **13.63 g** (the "16" ratio); accuracy within 1-2%
- **Regulation:** Graduated scales + uniform weights across ~8 lakh sq km → central regulation of trade
# 2. Scale find → site

# 3. Unit length

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - Farm surplus + trade networks + craft specialisation + standard weights → urbanisation
  - Standard weights, seals, bricks across the whole area → economic integration + regulation
- **Current relevance:**
  - 2026 current-affairs compilations repeat the Lothal dockyard, Persian Gulf seal and Meluhha-Dilmun-Magan table (Jun 2026)

=== ROW 9 | IVC seals, weights and measures, Harappan script (undeciphered features)

## Seals and sealings — material, shapes, uses | from: S0
# 1. Material and shapes
- **Material:** Mostly **steatite** (soapstone) - easy carving; also terracotta, copper, ivory

| **Shape** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| **Square**/oblong plaque | Standard seal; **square** seals = animal + inscription |
| **Rectangular** | Inscription only |
| Mesopotamian-type **cylinder** | Rare |
| **Button seal** | Lothal |

# 2. Uses and value as a source
- **Sealing:** Bags of goods closed with wet clay and stamped; intact sealing on arrival = safe delivery + **ownership**
- **Identity:** Inscriptions may be names or titles
- **Functions:** Trade regulation + **long-distance communication**
- **Value as source:** Script unread → seals = **most distinctive** IVC artefact; key source on the people
> Exam angle: long-distance communication → "seals and sealings" (APPSC-G1 2017); distinctive feature of IVC → seals (APPSC-GS 2024).

## Weights — material, shape, binary-decimal system | from: S1
- **Material:** Mostly **chert**; also limestone and steatite
- **Shape:** **Cubical**

| **Denomination** | **System** |
|---|---|
| Lower | **binary** (**1:2:4:8:16:32:64**) |
| Higher | **decimal** multiples (160, 320, 640, 1600, 3200) |

- ⚠ Lower = **binary**; higher = **decimal**
- **Uniformity:** Same weights across about **8,00,000 sq km** → central regulation
- **Finds:** Ivory weight balance at Mohenjodaro and Lothal; chert trade weights at Rakhigarhi

## Harappan script — features, numbers and FIRSTs | from: S2, S4
# 1. Nature and direction
| **Feature** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Nature** | Mostly **pictographic** + some abstract signs |
| **Commonest sign** | **Fish** |
| **Direction** | **Right to left** (cramped spacing at the left end) |
| **Boustrophedon** | Some texts (alternate lines reversed), e.g. Kalibangan |

# 2. Numbers
| **Item** | **Number** |
|---|---|
| **Number of signs** | About **400**; estimates range 400-700 |
| **Inscription length** | Average about **5 signs** |
| **Longest known inscription** | **26 signs** |
| **Corpus** | About **5,000** inscribed objects |
| **Dholavira signboard** | **10** large signs - the famous 10-sign inscription |

- **Found on:** Seals, tablets, pottery, copper plates, tools
- ⚠ Average **5** signs; longest **26**; signboard **10**; total signs about **400**
# 3. FIRSTs
- **First notice:** J. Fleet noticed seals with unknown symbols in **1912**
- **FIRST/LAST:** Indus script = among the world's earliest writing systems
# 4. Dholavira
- Excavated by **R.S. Bisht**, 1990
- **Only** Harappan site on the **UNESCO World Heritage** list (**2021**)
- Rakhigarhi = on the Tentative List

## Scholars → decipherment hypotheses | from: S3, S4
- **Status:** **No bilingual text** → **still undeciphered**

| **Hypothesis** | **Scholar** | **Standing** |
|---|---|---|
| **Proto-Dravidian** | **Iravatham Mahadevan, Asko Parpola** | Most widely supported |
| **Indo-Aryan** (early Sanskrit) | **S.R. Rao** | Minority view; Rig Veda post-dates Mature Harappan |
| Proto-Munda | - | Weak linguistic support |
| Isolate | - | Accepts decipherment is impossible for now |

- ⚠ **Mahadevan, Parpola** = Proto-Dravidian; **S.R. Rao** = Indo-Aryan
- **Criticism & debates:** Language of the script (Dravidian vs Indo-Aryan) remains open

## Universal angles | from: S4 | universal
- **Significance:** Uniform seals + weights = main proof of standardisation and regulated trade
- **Current relevance (Jun 2026):** Rakhigarhi - cylindrical steatite seal with alligator motif + five Harappan characters; chert trade weights

=== ROW 10 | IVC society and polity - social stratification debate, burial practices, absence of temples/palaces

## Social stratification — evidence | from: S0
| **Evidence** | **What it shows** |
|---|---|
| **Town layout** | **Citadel** on the **west** (public buildings, elite) vs **lower town** on the **east** (commoners' houses) |
| **Houses** | Vary in size - three building types (residential, larger buildings, public); **single-roomed barracks** at Harappa |
| **Burial goods** | Variation in goods and ornament quality → differentiation; quantity and quality of goods reflect status |

- **Four-fold division:** Scholars, warriors, traders, labourers - **speculative**
- **Caste:** **No textual evidence of a rigid caste system**; caste crystallised only in Later Vedic period

## Views on the polity → basis | from: S1
# 1. View → basis
| **View** | **Basis** |
|---|---|
| Priest rule | Citadel, Great Bath, steatite **Priest-King**; citadel may have held rulers or temples |
| Merchant elites / civic administrators | Trade-driven uniformity; **no kings or palaces** (CDI) |
| Monarchy | Fortified citadel (APP) |
| Municipal governance | Planned cities, advanced drainage |

- **Evidence:** **No clear-cut evidence** of political structure; "appears corporate" - unlike monarchical Later Vedic polity
- **City-state argument:** Several large cities; no single capital identified
- **Centralised-state argument:** Uniformity over a huge area
# 2. Uniformity as evidence of central authority
- **Standardisation:**
  - Bricks in standard **1:2:4** ratio
  - **Binary** weights; uniform seals
  - Grid-plan towns across about **8,00,000 sq km**
- **Labour mobilisation:** Walls, platforms, reservoirs
- **Craft centres:** Lothal beads, Chanhudaro shells
- **Exports:** Quality control

## Absence of palaces and temples; weapons | from: S2
# 1. No monumental temples or palaces
- **Finding:** **No royal palaces and no temples**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Possessed great palaces and temples" is **WRONG** (UPSC 2013)
- **Readings:**
  - Worship domestic or open-air
  - Power not personalised in a king
  - Public structures civic (Great Bath, granary, pillared hall)
# 2. Warfare and weapons
- **Weapons:** Maces, axes, spears, bows and arrows of **copper and bronze**
- **No horse-drawn chariots** (UPSC 2013)
- **Reading:** Weapons few and simple → relatively peaceful society
- **Mohenjodaro skeletons:** "Huddled together" - once read as massacre → now multiple burials

## Daily life — dress, ornaments, cosmetics, pastimes → site | from: S3
# 1. Dress, ornaments and cosmetics
- **Clothing:** **Cotton and wool**; two garments (upper + lower); shawl on Priest-King; no evidence of stitching
- **Ornaments:**
  - Gold, silver, ivory, copper, lapis lazuli, carnelian, faience
  - Bangles of shell, copper, terracotta
- **Cosmetics:** Bronze mirrors, ivory combs, collyrium sticks
- **Hairstyles:** Varied; men with trimmed beards + shaved upper lips
# 2. Object → site
| **Object** | **Site** |
|---|---|
| Necklaces of gold, silver, carnelian | **Allahdino** |
| **Lipstick** | **Chanhudaro** |
| **Dice** - cubical (like modern ones) | Harappa |
| Toy factory | Chanhudaro |

# 3. Pastimes
- **Board games:** Possibly proto-chess
- **Toys:** Terracotta carts, animals, marbles, rattles, whistles, spinning tops
- **Other pastimes:** Music (drums, harps), dance (Dancing Girl), hunting and fishing (speculative)

## Burial types → site | from: S4
# 1. Burial type → site
| **Type** | **Site / feature** |
|---|---|
| Extended (complete) burial with grave goods | Mohenjodaro, Harappa (**cemetery R-37**; coffin burial) |
| Fractional burial (bones buried after exposure) | Mohenjodaro, Harappa |
| Post-cremation burial (ashes with pottery) | Harappa (**Cemetery-H**), Mohenjodaro, Lothal |
| Pot burial | **Surkotada** (four pot burials, stone-covered grave) |
| **Joint/double burial** | **Lothal** |
| Dog buried with human (oval pits) | **Ropar** |

- ⚠ **Double burial** = Lothal; **dog with human** = Ropar; **pot burial** = Surkotada
# 2. Sites → burial features
- **Kalibangan:** Three types
  - Extended burial in rectangular pits
  - Pot burials
  - Circular pits with grave goods but no skeleton
- **Sanauli** (Baghpat district, UP): Large Harappan-period **cemetery of human burials**
- **Harappa R-37:** Extended burials, head generally to the north (some notes: east-west axis)
- **Rakhigarhi** (Mound 7): Burial plot
  - **56 skeletons** recovered, incl. a woman about **4,600 years** old (older notes say 4,500)
  - Standard **north-south** orientation + pots of provisions
  - Female skeletons wore **jasper and agate beads** + **shell bangles**
# 3. Grave goods and beliefs
- **Grave goods:** Pottery, ornaments, mirrors, beads, food → **belief in afterlife**
> Exam angle: double burial → Lothal (UPPSC 2016); Sanauli → human burials (UPPSC 2004).

## Universal angles | from: S5 | universal
- **Current relevance (Jun 2026):**
  - ASI handed Rakhigarhi skeletal remains to the Anthropological Survey of India (Kolkata) under an MoU for scientific study
  - Rakhigarhi = about **550 ha**, largest known Harappan settlement
- **Criticism & debates:** Stratification and polity inferred only from material remains; script gives no names of rulers
- **Comparative note:** Harappan polity corporate vs Later Vedic hereditary monarchy with declining Sabha and Samiti
