## Historians and court writers → origin, patron, contribution | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. Al-Biruni
- **Origin:** **Khwarazm, Central Asia** (present **Uzbekistan**); came with **Mahmud of Ghazni**
- **Period + patron:** **11th century (c. 1030 CE)** under **Mahmud of Ghazni** (who also patronised Firdausi, Utbi)
- **Work:** **Kitab-ul-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind** (Tarikh al-Hind), in Arabic
  - Records **Diwali** on **Kartika Amavasya**
- **Scholarship:** Learned Sanskrit; wrote on Indian religion, philosophy, astronomy, caste system → Indian sciences and society
- **View:** Found Indians unwilling to share learning with outsiders

## Qutbuddin Aibak and Iltutmish → career and contributions | from: S1, S2
# 1. Qutbuddin Aibak (1206-1210)
- **Early life:** Born in Turkestan; sold first to the **Qazi of Nishapur**
  - Then slave of **Muhammad Ghori** (APPSC G1 2004)
  - Rose to **Master of Stables** (Amir-i-Akhur) → general
- **Rise:** After **Tarain II (1192)** = Ghori's representative in India
  - Sultan on Ghori's death; ruled **1206-1210**
  - Capital: **Lahore**
- **Patronage:** **Hasan Nizami**
- **Death:** **1210**, fell from horse playing **chaugan** (polo)
- **Successor:** Son **Aram Shah** → ruled ~8 months
# 2. Iltutmish (1210-36)
- **Background:** **Ilbari** Turk; slave + son-in-law of Aibak
  - Iqtadar of Gwalior → governor of Baran and Badaun
  - Title: **Shamsuddin**
- **Capital:** Moved from **Lahore to Delhi**
- **Rivals:**
  - **Taj-ud-din Yildiz** → defeated at Tarain (**1216**)
  - **Nasiruddin Qubacha**
- **Legitimacy:** Investiture ("mansur") from **Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad, 1229**
- **Institutions:** **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** (the Forty / Chalisa); **Iqta** system (see terms page)
- **Mongols:** Refused shelter to **Jalaluddin Mangbarni** of Khwarazm → kept **Chengiz Khan** out of India
- ⚠ Aibak's capital = **Lahore**; Iltutmish moved it to **Delhi**
- ⚠ **Tarain II (1192)** = Ghori's win; Tarain **1216** = Iltutmish defeats Yildiz

## Razia, Nasiruddin Mahmud and Balban → career and contributions | from: S3, S4, S5
# 1. Razia Sultan (1236-1240)
- **Reign:** **1236-1240** → first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate
- **Conduct:** Discarded purdah, wore male attire, led the army
- **Opposition:**
  - Turkish nobles resented her favour to **Yaqut** (Abyssinian)
  - **1240**: **Altuniya**, governor of **Bhatinda (Sirhind)**, revolted → Yaqut killed, Razia imprisoned
- **End:**
  - Nobles enthroned **Bahram Shah**
  - Razia married Altuniya, marched on Delhi → defeated and killed (1240)
# 2. Nasiruddin Mahmud (1246-66)
- Pious, weak Sultan
- **Balban's role:** Principal minister (naib) + leader of the Forty
  - Briefly displaced by **Imaduddin Raihan** → returned
- **Source:** Minhaj-us-Siraj's **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** named after this Sultan
# 3. Balban (1266-86)
- **Early life:** Born c. 1205 as **Bahauddin**
  - Captured by Mongols → bought by Iltutmish
  - Water-carrier → member of **the Forty**
  - Razia made him **Amir-i-Shikar**; title **Ulugh Khan**
- **Blood and iron policy:**
  - Ruthless suppression of Mewatis + rebellious villages
  - **Broke the power of the Forty** by promoting junior Turks
- **Army:**
  - Reorganised; created military department **Diwan-i-Arz**
  - Outposts + forts vs the **Mongols**
  - Son Prince Muhammad died fighting the Mongols (1285)
- **Assertion-reason (UPPSC 2024):** Centralised authority AND guarded north-west, but second is not the reason for the first
- ⚠ Iltutmish **created** the Forty; Balban **broke** its power

=== ROW 106 | Khalji dynasty - Jalaluddin; Alauddin Khalji's conquests, Deccan campaigns of Malik Kafur

## Khalji Sultans → accession, policy, contribution | from: S0, S2, S5
# 1. Jalaluddin Khalji (1290-1296)
- **Origin:** Khalji from Turkestan; had fought the Mongols
- **Accession:** Crowned himself at **Kilokhari**, **1290**, aged about **70**
- **Policy:**
  - First Sultan with benevolent attitude to Hindus
  - Lenient to rebels, e.g. **Malik Chhajju**
  - Attack on Ranthambore failed
- **End:**
  - Nephew + son-in-law **Alauddin** raided **Devagiri** (1294-96) = **first Turkish invasion of the south**
  - Alauddin murdered Jalaluddin at **Kara (1296)**
# 2. Alauddin Khalji (1296-1316)
- **Earlier name:** **Ali Gurshasp**
- **Ambitions:** World conqueror like **Alexander** (Sikandar-i-Sani) + found a new religion
- **Advice against both:** Kotwal of Delhi **Ala-ul-Mulk** (Barani's uncle)
- **Ulema:** Ruled without the **ulema** → acted in state's interest whatever religious leaders advised
- **Nobles:** **Ulugh Khan, Nusrat Khan, Zafar Khan, Ain-ul-Mulk Multani, Malik Kafur, Ghazi Malik**
- **Court poet:** **Amir Khusrau**
# 3. Delhi Sultans → reign period (revision)

## Mongol invasions → leaders, battles, defence | from: S1
# 1. Under Jalaluddin
- **First Mongol threat:** Under **Iltutmish** (Chengiz Khan, 1221)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "First Mongol invasion under Jalaluddin" is **false** (UPSC 2022)
- **Jalaluddin's response:** Defeated a Mongol invasion
  - Converted + settled Mongols = **"New Mussalmans"**
  - Married his daughter to **Ulugh Khan**, a descendant of Chengiz Khan
# 2. Under Alauddin
- **Battle of Kili:** **Zafar Khan** killed
- **Siege of Delhi:** Mongol army besieged **Delhi** → Alauddin sheltered in **Siri** fort
- **Defence:**
  - Repaired old forts + built new ones on north-west frontier (completing Balban's policy)
  - Large standing army
  - Built **Siri** as new capital
  - **Ghazi Malik** made governor of Punjab to hold the frontier

=== ROW 107 | Alauddin Khalji's market control regulations, revenue and military reforms

## Alauddin Khalji → posts, kingship, titles, Mongol victories | from: S0, S1
# 1. Earlier posts
- **Earlier posts:** Under Jalaluddin, Alauddin was Amir-i-Tuzuk + **Ariz-i-Mamalik**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes call this post "minister of law"; it was head of the army department
# 2. Kingship and religion
- **Ulema:** Openly overrode the **ulema** / refused their guidance → declared he would act in the state's interest; policy decided by state needs, not religious advice
  - = "State interest over Shariat" position (Barani)
- **Model:** Revived **Balban's** harsh, centralised rule, without Balban's dependence on religious sanction
- **Title:** **Sikandar-i-Sani** (second Alexander)
- **Ambitions:** World conquest + new religion → Kotwal **Ala-ul-Mulk** talked him out of both
# 3. Mongol defence
| **Victory over Mongols** | **Year** |
|---|---|
| **Jalandhar** | **1298** |
| **Kili** | **1299** |
| **Amroha** | **1305** |
| **Ravi** | **1306** |
- **Shelter:** During one invasion took shelter in **Siri** fort
- **Forts:** Repaired old frontier forts + built new ones → completed Balban's frontier policy

## Military reforms → features and FIRSTs | from: S2
# 1. Standing army
- **Army type:** **First Sultan to maintain a large permanent standing army**
- **Recruitment:** By the **Ariz-i-Mumalik** (army minister)
- **Payment:** In **cash** from the treasury
- **Size:** Ferishta = about **4,75,000** cavalry

| **Dagh** | Branding of horses |
| **Huliya / chehra** | Descriptive roll of each soldier |

## Market control → aim, markets, enforcement, supply | from: S3
# 1. Aim and plan
- **Objective:** Keep a large standing army on **low, fixed salaries** by lowering + fixing prices of daily goods
  - So soldiers could live comfortably on low pay (APPSC G2 2017 key)
  - Military need directly shaped economic policy
- **Four-point plan:**
  - Fix prices
  - Guarantee supply (state granaries)
  - Control distribution + end monopolies
  - Enforce through officials / a separate administration
# 2. Markets → goods
| **Market** | **Goods** |
|---|---|
| **Mandi** | Food grains (grain) |
| **Sarai Adl** | Cloth, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil |
| Third market | Horses, slaves, cattle |
| Fourth market | Miscellaneous goods |
- ⚠ **Mandi** = grain; **Sarai Adl** = cloth and other goods
# 3. Enforcement
- **Checks:**
  - Daily reports from **barids** (intelligence officers) + **munhiyan** / **munhiyans** (secret spies)
  - Slave boys made test purchases to check prices
  - Cheating merchants punished
# 4. Supply
- **Government granaries:** Held stocks; rationing in scarcity
- **First public distribution system** of the Sultanate (BPSC 2020)
> Exam angle: UPPSC keys call the market control "highly successful"; Barani is the main source.

- **State share (kharaj):** **Half (50%)** of produce (BPSC 2018)
  - Some notes say one-fifth
# 2. Terms → meaning

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal

  - First land measurement for revenue

  - Despotism + heavy spying hollowed out the state → dynasty fell soon after his death in 1316

=== ROW 108 | Tughlaq dynasty - Ghiyasuddin; Muhammad bin Tughlaq's five experiments; Firoz Shah's reforms

## Tughlaq rulers → rise, reign, contribution, end | from: S0, S5, S7
# 1. Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320-25)
- **Earlier name:** **Ghazi Malik**; son of a Turkish slave + a **Jat** woman
- **Under Alauddin:** **Governor of Punjab** against the Mongols
- **Accession:** Killed Khusrau Shah → **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq Shah in 1320**
- **Agrarian policy:**
  - Took back unlawful grants
  - Land revenue rise limited to about one-tenth, raised gradually
  - Remission in drought
  - Waste land brought under the plough
  - **First Sultan to start irrigation/canal works**
- **Postal system:** Improved with runners + horsemen
- **Warangal:**
  - **1321-22:** Prince **Juna Khan** (later MBT) attacked → false news of Sultan's death caused retreat
  - **1323:** Warangal fell after a 5-month siege → renamed **Sultanpur**
  - **Prataparudra II:** Taken towards Delhi; died in the **Narmada**
- **Tughlaqabad:** Built the fort
- **Nizamuddin Auliya:** Strained relations
- **Death (1325):** Wooden pavilion collapsed at Afghanpur → **Ibn Battuta** suspects a plot by Juna Khan
# 2. Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351-88)
- **Parentage:** Son of Ghiyasuddin's younger brother **Rajab**
- **Accession:** Raised to throne at **Thatta** after MBT died there (1351); ruled 1351-88
- **Wazir:** **Khan-i-Jahan Maqbul** → paid compensation to those who suffered under Muhammad bin Tughlaq
# 3. Successors and end
- **Weak successors:** Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq II, Abu Bakr, Nasiruddin Muhammad Shah → fought each other
- **Last Tughlaq:** **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq**, on throne during **Timur's invasion (1398)**
  - Some notes call him "Nasiruddin Muhammad Tughlaq"
- **End:**
  - After Sultan Mahmud's death **Daulat Khan Lodi** briefly recognised
  - **Khizr Khan** took Delhi in **1414**
- **Tughlaq period:** **1320-1414**

## Muhammad bin Tughlaq's five experiments → cause and result | from: S1, S2
| **Experiment** | **Cause / aim** | **Result / outcome** |
|---|---|---|
| **Transfer of capital**, Delhi to **Devagiri**, renamed **Daulatabad**, 1327 [GK] | Central base to control the Deccan | Mass migration, hardship and disruption; the court returned to Delhi (later reversed) |
| **Token currency** of **copper and brass** (c. 1329-30) [GK] | Silver shortage; the idea of a token coin | Forged widely (mass forgery); the coins were recalled and redeemed at a heavy cost to the treasury |
| **Higher taxation / tax rise in the Doab** [GK] | More revenue | Coincided with (came during) a famine; peasants revolted and fled |
| **Khurasan expedition** [GK] | Conquest in Central Asia | A huge army was raised, then disbanded unpaid / unused |
| **Qarachil expedition** (Kumaon hills) [GK] | Securing the hill frontier | Army destroyed in the hills |
- **Famine relief:** During Doab famine, Sultan moved to a **Swarga-dwari** / **Swargadwari** famine-relief camp on the Ganga near Kannauj [GK] → relief and cultivation during the famine
> Exam angle: statement questions ("token currency was copper and brass"; "the Doab tax coincided with famine").
