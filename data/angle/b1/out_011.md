=== ROW 57 | Andhra society and culture in the early period - varna, women, education and festivals

## Varna, caste terms and marriage types | from: S0, S1
# 1. Satavahana varna order
- **Basis:** Ancient Andhra society **predominantly shaped by the Vedic varna system**; **Satavahanas** played a crucial role (APPSC-GS 2024 statement pair)
- **King's duty:** **Duty-bound to enforce varna-dharma**
- **Brahmins:** **Tax-free villages**; **Nanaghat inscription** mentions Brahmins trained in Vedic learning and ritual
- **Gautamiputra Satakarni:** Claimed he **stopped varna-sankara** (mixing of castes)
  - Nasik prasasti: **"Ekabrahmana"** + humbler of Kshatriya pride
- **Vishnukundins:** Followed **varnasramadharma**
  - Vaisyas = internal + external trade
  - Sudras (carpenters, sculptors, handloom weavers) = recognised place
  - **Farmers were "the backbone"**; received land grants
# 2. Kshatriya claims
- **Kshatriya claims by assimilation:**
  - Groups: Greeks, Sakas, Pahlavas, Kushanas (+ Kardhamakas, Western Kshatrapas) → Indian names/faiths → **Kshatriya status**
  - Examples: Saka **Nahapana's daughter Dakshamitra**, son-in-law **Rishabhadatta**, **Rudradaman**
# 3. Occupational castes → term
- **Caste by profession:** Varna-sankara + occupational diversification → many sub-castes (Gathasaptasati, Kamasutra)

| **Term** | **Occupation** |
|---|---|
| Halikas | Farmers (the emerging agricultural group) |
| Sethis | Merchants |
| Selavaddikas | Stone sculptors / masons |
| Swarnakaras | Goldsmiths |
| Gandhikas | Perfumers |
| Tailapishakas | Oil pressers |
| Golikas | Cowherds |

> Exam angle: statement pairs on "Vedic caste system + Satavahana role"; match sub-caste name → occupation.
# 4. Marriage types and customs
- **Nature:** Marriage = **sacred institution**
- **Polygamy:** Among upper classes

| **Type** | **Union** | **Offspring termed** |
|---|---|---|
| **Anuloma** | Higher-caste man + lower-caste woman | **Ugra** |
| **Viloma (Pratiloma)** | Lower-caste man + higher-caste woman | **Chandala** |

- ⚠ **Anuloma → Ugra**; **Viloma → Chandala**
- **Inter-caste marriages:** Mentioned in **Kathasaritsagara** - caste not fully rigid at first
- **Sati:** **Nagarjunakonda sculpture** shows sati; Greek writer **Strabo** also noted it
- **Widows:** Expected **celibacy**

=== ROW 58 | Sangam Age - the three Sangams, sources; Tolkappiyam, Ettuthogai, Pattupattu, Pathinenkilkanakku

## Three Sangams → place, scholar, chronology | from: S0
- **"Sangam":** Assemblies/academies of poets under **Pandya** patronage (all three Sangams)
- **Source of tradition:** Preface of **Iraiyanar Agapporul** (2026)

| **Sangam** | **Place** | **Presiding scholar** | **Survives** |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | **Madurai** (Then Madurai) - submerged | Agastya [GK] | Nothing |
| Second | **Kapatapuram** - submerged | Agastya / Tolkappiyar [GK] | Only **Tolkappiyam** |
| Third | **Madurai** | Nakkirar [GK] | Most of the Sangam corpus (few of many works) |

- ⚠ First and Third both at **Madurai**; Second at **Kapatapuram**
- **Legendary chronology:** Three-Sangam account (two submerged cities) = traditional, not historical
- **Accepted span:** c. **3rd century BCE-3rd century CE (300 BCE-300 CE)**
  - Tamil-Brahmi dating to the 3rd c. BCE supports the early limit
- **Later works:** Tirukkural + twin epics = **post-Sangam**

## Sangam literature — terms, corpus and anthologies | from: S1, S2
# 1. Language and corpus
- **Language:** **Tamil** (old Tamil)
- **Corpus:** About **2,400 poems** by several hundred poets, **including women poets**
  - One of the earliest bodies of **secular** literature in India
# 2. Terms → meaning

| **Type** | **Theme** |
|---|---|
| **Aham** (interior) | Love, personal emotion, nature |
| **Puram** (exterior) | War, valour, deeds of kings, ethics |

- **Division:** Both Ettuthogai and Pattupattu divided into aham and puram
- **Melkanakku:** Ettuthogai + Pattupattu = the **18 major works (Melkanakku)**
- **Pathinenkilkanakku = 18 minor works** on **ethics and morals**, incl. the **Tirukkural** - largely post-Sangam
- ⚠ Melkanakku = 18 major; Pathinenkilkanakku = 18 minor
# 3. Anthologies → works
- **Ettuthogai (8 anthologies):** Short poems
  - Ainkurunuru, Narrinai, Akananuru, Purananuru, Kuruntogai, Kalittogai, Paripadal
  - **Padirruppattu** - Chera kings
- **Pattupattu (10 idylls):** Longer poems on kings, cities, trade
  - Tirumurugarruppadai, Porunarruppadai, Sirupanarruppadai, Perumpanarruppadai, Mullaippattu, Nedunalvadai, **Maduraikkanji**, Kurinjippattu, Malaipadukadam
  - **Pattinappalai** - Puhar port, Karikala
# 4. Content
- **Content:** Polity, trade, cities, wars, economy
  - Rich in **material culture** + **warrior ethic**
  - Sangam poets **knew the varna classification** (UPSC 2022 key)
> Exam angle: statement questions - "devoid of material culture / no warrior ethic" are false.

## Tamil works → authors and subjects | from: S3
| **Work** | **Author** | **Subject** |
|---|---|---|
| **Tolkappiyam** | **Tolkappiyar** | **Grammar** and poetics; earliest extant Tamil work; also society and polity |
| **Tirukkural** | **Tiruvalluvar** | Ethics/philosophy - **1,330 couplets**, 3 books **Aram** (virtue), **Porul** (wealth), **Inbam** (love) |
| **Silappadikaram** | **Ilango Adigal** | First Tamil epic; Kannagi-Kovalan (the anklet) |
| **Manimekalai** | **Sittalai Sattanar** | Buddhist epic, sequel to Silappadikaram |
| Sivaga Sindamani (Jivaka Chintamani) | Tiruttakkadevar [GK] | Jain epic [GK] |

- **Other names of the Kural:** **"Laghuveda"** (UPPSC 2013 key), "Tamil Veda", "Ulaga Podhumarai" (universal Veda)
- **Silappadikaram:** Ilango Adigal = younger brother of **Chera Senguttuvan**
  - Merchant Kovalan, wife **Kannagi**, dancer Madhavi
  - Kovalan executed by the Pandya king → Kannagi burns **Madurai**
- **Manimekalai:** Daughter of Kovalan-Madhavi becomes a Buddhist nun; reflects Buddhist philosophy
- **UPPSC 2014 matching key:**
  - Tirukkural = philosophy
  - Tolkappiyam = grammar
  - Silappadikaram = love story
  - Manimekalai = merchant story

## Foreign writers and inscriptions → what they record | from: S4
| **Writer / source** | **Work** | **Records** |
|---|---|---|
| Anonymous Greek | **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** | Ports, **Muziris** pepper, Arikamedu |
| **Pliny** | Natural History | **Spices from Chera, pearls from Pandya, muslin from Chola**; Roman gold drain |
| **Ptolemy** | Geography | Classical account |

- **Other classical writers:** Also Megasthenes, Strabo
- **Ashokan inscriptions:** Name **Chera, Chola and Pandya** as neighbours south of the Mauryan empire
  - With Satiyaputras, Keralaputras in RE II
- **Indirect sources:** Buddhist-Jain texts; later travellers (Fa Xian, Xuanzang, Marco Polo)

## Archaeological sites → findings | from: S5
| **Site** | **Note** |
|---|---|
| **Arikamedu** (near Puducherry) | Indo-Roman trading settlement - **"market street of the Romans"** (Periplus) |
| **Kaveripattinam (Puhar)** | Chola port; **Pattinappalai** describes it as cosmopolitan with **Yavana settlements** |
| **Keezhadi (Keeladi)** | **Most important Sangam urban excavation** - South Indian urbanisation **contemporary with the Gangetic Second Urbanisation** (NextGen CA 2026) |

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Current relevance:**
  - Feb 2026: **~30 Indian inscriptions, mostly Tamil-Brahmi**, of the 1st-3rd c. CE documented in the Theban tombs of Egypt → Indo-Roman trade mobility
  - Jul 2025: Tamil Nadu excavation with Tamil-Brahmi potsherds, carnelian beads, weaving tools
- **Significance:** Earliest detailed picture of South Indian society, economy and polity
- **Comparative note:** Tamil for literature vs contemporary Satavahanas used Prakrit
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Oldest Tamil work = Tolkappiyam
  - First Tamil epic = Silappadikaram

=== ROW 59 | Sangam kingdoms - Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas; Karikala, Senguttuvan, Nedunjeliyan

## Muvendar → capital, port, emblem, river | from: S0
- **Collective name:** **Muvendar** (three crowned kings)

| **Kingdom** | **Capital** | **Port** | **Emblem** | **Heartland river** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chera** | **Vanji (Karur / Karuvur)** | **Muziris (Musiri)**, Tondi | **Bow** (and arrow) | Periyar [GK] |

## Battles, Kallanai, Pattini cult and Pandya decline | from: S2, S3, S4
# 1. Battles → victor

| **Battle** | **Victor** | **Result** |
|---|---|---|
| **Venni** | **Karikala Chola** | Defeated the **Cheras, Pandyas and 11 chieftains** |
| Vahaipparandalai | Karikala | 9 chieftains submitted |
| **Talaiyalanganam** (Tanjore) | Nedunjeliyan (Pandya) | Control over Tamil country |

# 2. Kallanai and water works
- **Kallanai (Grand Anicut):** Built across the **Kaveri**, attributed to **Karikala Chola**, with irrigation canals
- **Aug 2026 find (15 Aug 2026):** Remains of an ancient **Sangam-period water-management structure** in the **Kollidam** river
  - Kollidam = Kaveri distributary branching off at the **Upper Anicut (Mukkombu)**

- **Pattini cult:** Founded by **Chera Senguttuvan**

# 4. Decline
- **Decline:** Pandya power waned under the **Kalabhra** invasion

=== ROW 60 | Sangam society and economy - tinai concept, Indo-Roman trade, ports, religion; the Kalabhra interregnum

- **Religion:** Patrons of **Jainism and Buddhism** rather than Vedic Brahmanism
- **"Dark age" debate:**

  - Newer view: period of Jain-Buddhist literary activity
- **End:** Late 6th century, by **Pandya Kadungon** and **Pallava Simhavishnu**

## Allahabad Prasasti — Harishena, language, the pillar | from: S1
- **Allahabad Pillar (Prayaga Prashasti):** Other name **Prayaga-prashasti**; inscribed on an Ashokan pillar [GK]
- **Nature:** **Sanskrit** eulogy of **Samudragupta**'s conquests (APPSC-GS 2025 key); mixed prose + verse (champu style)
- **Samudragupta's conquests:** Recorded in the Allahabad Pillar → not Mehrauli, Junagadh or Nasik (APPSC 2025)
- **Composer:** Court poet **Harisena** (**Harishena**), author of the Prayaga prashasti
  - Posts held at once: **Kumaramatya, Sandhivigrahika** + **Mahadandanayaka**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Not the later Jain author **Harisena of the Brihatkatha Kosha** → a different person
- **Same pillar also carries:**
  - **Ashoka's edicts**
  - **Queen's edict** (queen Karuvaki)
  - **Jahangir's inscription** (UPSC Art & Culture)
- **Contrast:** Glorifies war vs Ashoka's message of peace on the same pillar

## Gupta origin and Chandragupta I — FIRSTs | from: S2, S3
# 1. Origin
- **Origin:** **Uncertain and debated**; rose from modest beginnings
- **Caste:**
  - **Originally Vaishyas** → later treated by brahmanas as **Kshatriyas**
  - Brahmana origin also argued from the **Dharana gotra** of Prabhavatigupta
- **Home:**
  - **Sri Gupta** ruled a small part of North Bengal and South Bihar
  - Early territory = **Magadha, Prayag and eastern UP**
- **Line:** **Sri Gupta (c. 240-280) → Ghatotkacha (c. 280-319)**, both "Maharaja"
# 2. Chandragupta I
- **Status:** **Chandragupta I (c. 319-335 CE)** = **first important king** / real founder
- **Capital:** **Pataliputra**
- **Territory:** South Bihar, eastern UP (Saketa, Prayaga), Bengal
- **Marriage:** **Kumaradevi**, a **Lichchhavi** princess (Nepal) → legitimacy, prestige and power
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes say Ghatotkacha married her → wrong; she was Chandragupta I's queen
- **Gupta Era (Gupta Samvat):** Started **319-20 CE** = date of his accession
- **Coins:** First Gupta **gold coins (dinaras)**
  - **King-queen type** showing Chandragupta I + Kumaradevi
  - Issued in the joint names of king, queen and Lichchhavis
- **Saka victory:** He did **not** defeat the Sakas → that was **Chandragupta II**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC-G1 2024 statement I is false; crediting Chandragupta I with silver coins + Saka victory mixes him up with Chandragupta II

## Chandragupta II Vikramaditya → accession, titles, conquests, alliances, court | from: S1
# 1. Ramagupta episode and accession
- **Source:** **Devichandraguptam** of **Vishakhadatta**
- **Story:**
  - Ramagupta (elder brother) agreed to surrender his queen **Dhruvadevi** to a Saka king
  - **Chandragupta II** killed the Saka → then killed **Ramagupta** + married Dhruvadevi
- **Succession:** Chandragupta II took the throne **after killing his elder brother** (BPSC 2022 key)
# 2. Titles and coins
- **Titles:** **Vikramaditya** and **Simhavikrama**
- **Other names:** **Devagupta / Devaraja**
- **Coins:** Lion-slayer type (his father appears as tiger-slayer)
# 3. Sakas and second capital
- **Saka conquest:** Defeated **Rudrasimha III** → annexed **western Malwa and Gujarat**, reached the western sea
- **Second capital:** Made **Ujjain the second capital**
- **Evidence:**
  - **Udayagiri cave inscription of Virasena** records his campaign
  - Mehrauli iron pillar claims north-west India and Bengal
- **Silver coins:** Modelled on Saka silver coins, issued after the Saka conquest for the new region → **strongest evidence** of the Saka victory
  - Weight about **33 grains** (MPPSC 2023, UPPSC 2022 keys)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Gold coins modelled on Saka silver coins" is wrong → the Saka-model coins were **silver**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Crediting Chandragupta I with the first silver coins and Saka victory is wrong
# 4. Alliances

| **Alliance** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| Naga | Married **Kuberanaga**, a Naga princess |
| Vakataka | Daughter **Prabhavati(gupta)** married **Rudrasena II**; indirect control over the Vakataka kingdom |
| Dhruvadevi | Married after killing Ramagupta |

# 5. Fa-Hien and court
- **Fa-Hien:** Visited India in his reign (UPSC 2025 key), c. **399-414 CE**
  - Never named the king → focus was Buddhism
- **Navaratnas (nine gems, a tradition):** Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Dhanvantari, Varahamihira, Vararuchi (Prakrit Prakasha), Shanku, Ghatakarpara, Kshapanaka, Vetalabhatta (Vetala Bhatta)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Fa-Hien listed as a court member → he was a pilgrim, not a courtier

## Kumaragupta I and Skandagupta → titles and contributions | from: S2, S3
# 1. Kumaragupta I
- **Titles:** **Shakraditya** and **Mahendraditya**
- **Institution:** Founded **Nalanda** University
- **Religion:** Devotee of **Kartikeya**; performed **ashvamedha**
- **Coins:** New gold types showing **Kartikeya** + king **feeding a peacock**
- **Threats:** **Pushyamitras** + first **Huna** attack late in his reign (413-455) → repulsed by prince **Skandagupta**
# 2. Skandagupta
- **Reign:** **Skandagupta (455-467)**
- **Invasions faced:**
  - **Pushyamitras** (end of Kumaragupta's reign)
  - **Hunas** → beat back / repulsed twice (twice, per some notes); title **Vikramaditya**
  - UPPSC 2015: the Gupta ruler who defeated the Hunas
- **Gold coins:** Bow and arrow + **Garuda standard** + **Lakshmi**
- **Administration:** Central control weakened → governors became hereditary feudatories
- ⚠ Kumaragupta I coins = **Kartikeya** + peacock; Skandagupta coins = **Garuda** + **Lakshmi**

## Huna invasions → leader, Gupta reign, opponent | from: S4
# 1. Huna waves
- **Origin:** **Hunas (Hephthalites / White Huns)** from Central Asia via the **Oxus** valley → occupied **Gandhara** in the mid-5th century

| **Huna wave / leader** | **Gupta reign** | **Repelled / defeated by** |
|---|---|---|
| First attack (Hephthalites / White Huns via the Oxus, took Gandhara) | Late **Kumaragupta I** | Prince **Skandagupta** |
| Later waves | **Skandagupta** | Skandagupta (repulsed twice; Bhitari pillar) |
| **Toramana** | Budhagupta and after, c. 500 CE [GK] | Occupied Malwa and central India (by 485 CE Hunas held eastern Malwa, APP) |
| **Mihirakula** (son of Toramana, persecuted Buddhism) | Narasimhagupta [GK] | **Yashodharman** of Malwa (APP) and **Narasimhagupta Baladitya**[GK] |

- **Toramana (late 5th c.):** Under weak successors, conquered western India up to **Eran** (near Bhopal)
  - **By 485 CE:** Hunas held Punjab, Rajasthan, Kashmir, eastern Malwa, central India
- **Mihirakula (c. 515 CE):** Toramana's son; tyrant + iconoclast who **persecuted Buddhists**
  - Defeated by **Yashodharman of Malwa**, **Narasimhagupta Baladitya** and the Maukharis
# 2. Yashodharman of Mandasor
- **Role:** Gupta **feudatory in Malwa** who defeated **Mihirakula**
- **Victory pillars:** At **Mandasor (Sondani)**, **c. 532 CE**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes misread this as "532 victory pillars"
- **Effect:** His rise undermined Gupta authority → victory over the Hunas did not revive the empire
> Exam angle: Kumaragupta I = first attack; Skandagupta = repulsed; Yashodharman = defeated Mihirakula (UPPSC 2016).

## Causes of Gupta decline — factor → effect | from: S5
# 1. Causes

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| External | Repeated **Huna** invasions (**Huna invasions** drained treasury and army) + Pushyamitra attack |
| **Weak successors** | **Weak successors** after Skandagupta; last ruler **Vishnugupta** (c. 540-550 CE) |
| Religious policy | Later Guptas' **leaning to Buddhism** listed as a cause in some questions |

- **Feudatory:**
  - Land grants + hereditary governors created feudatories
  - **Feudatories** broke away: Yashodharman; Bihar, Bengal, MP, Valabhi, Gujarat, western Malwa
  - Rise of Maukharis, Maitrakas, Later Guptas, Pushyabhutis
  - Administration rested on feudatories and beneficiaries vs centralised Mauryas
- **Economic:**
  - **Land grants** cut revenue + falling trade revenue → no **professional army** could be kept
  - **Loss of western India** → lost trade income + Roman silk trade
  - Silk weavers migrated from Gujarat to Malwa (**AD 473**), left their craft
  - Towns decayed
- ⚠ **Trap:** The **Arab invasion** was **not** a cause (UKPSC 2012)
# 2. Coinage debasement as evidence
- **Debasement:** Later Gupta gold coins = **less gold, more alloy**
- **Disappearance:** Coins gradually vanish post-Gupta → payments shifted to land
- **Lost territory:** **No Gupta coins or inscriptions** in western Malwa and Saurashtra after Skandagupta
