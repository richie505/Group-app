=== ROW 17 | Early Vedic society and economy - pastoralism, gavishti, varna origins, position of women

## Pastoral economy — cow terms → meaning, food and pastimes | from: S0, S7
- **Economy:** Mainly **pastoral**, agriculture secondary (mixed economy)
- **Cow:** **Most valuable property** + standard unit of exchange
  - Priests paid in cows, horses, gold

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Gau** (cow) | Mentioned **176 times** in Rigveda; cow = standard unit of value |
| **Aghanya** | "Not to be killed" - epithet of the **cow** |
| **Gavishti** | "Search for cows" = **war** |
| **Gomat** | Wealthy/rich man (owner of many cattle) |
| **Gopati / Gopa** | Lord/protector of cattle - title of the chief |
| **Godhuli** | Time measure (dusk, when cattle return) |
| **Gavyuti** | Measure of distance |
| **Duhitri** | Daughter - "one who milks the cow" |

- ⚠ **Aghanya** = cow ("not to be killed"); **Gavishti** = war; **Gavyuti** = distance; **Godhuli** = time
- **Animals kept:** Cattle, sheep, goats, horses, dogs, asses (buffalo not prominent)
- **Animals known:** Lion, elephant, bear
- **Drinks:**
  - **Soma** = sacred drink from a plant brought from the Mujavant mountains
  - **sura** = intoxicating drink
- **Food:** Milk and milk products, barley preparations, meat on occasions
- **Pastimes:** **chariot racing**, **dicing** (gambler's hymn, Rigveda X.34), music, dance, hunting
> Exam angle: "Aghanya refers to - cows"; "most valuable property in early Rigvedic period - cow".

## Social units and varna origins — FIRSTs and features | from: S4, S5
# 1. FIRSTs

| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| **Purusha Sukta** | **Rigveda, Mandala X (hymn 10.90)**, a late addition; first statement of the four-fold varna |
| **varna** (word) | First in the **Rigveda** |
| **Shudra** (word) | First in the **10th Mandala** |
| Gotra (lineage sense) | First in the Atharvaveda (Rigveda word = cow-pen) |

- **Purusha Sukta body parts:** Brahmana = mouth; Kshatriya/Rajanya = arms; Vaishya = thighs; **Shudra (feet)**
- **Vaishya origin:** Grew out of **vis / Vis** (the mass of the people / tribespeople)
# 2. Family and social units
- **Family type:** **Patriarchal, patrilineal joint family**
  - Head = **Grihapati** / **Kulapa**; wife = Sapatni
- **Hierarchy of units:** **Kula > Grama > Vis > Jana** (Kula smallest)

| **Unit** | **Head** |
|---|---|
| **Kula** (family) | kulapa/grihapati |
| **Grama** (village) | gramani |
| **Vis** (clan) | vispati |
| **Jana** (tribe) | rajan |

- **Jana vs Janapada:** **Jana** mentioned **275 times**; Janapada never
- ⚠ **Jana** **275 times** vs **Gau** **176 times**
# 3. Nature of early society
- **Arya varna vs Dasa varna:** Cultural/linguistic (and colour-based) distinction between Vedic groups (conquerors) and conquered local groups; **not rigid caste**
- **Character:** Tribal, largely **egalitarian**
- **Broad groups:** Priests, warriors, common people
- **Occupational mobility:** Occupations not birth-based
  - Hymn: "I am a poet, my father a physician, my mother a grinder of corn"
- **Absent:** Untouchability (none) and gotra
  - **Gotra:** Absent as an institution in the Rigveda (word there = cow-pen; lineage sense first in the Atharvaveda); gotra exogamy = Later Vedic development
- **Slavery:** Existed (Dasa/Dasyu captives; slave women gifted to priests), but not the base of the economy

# 3. Marriage terms

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|

| **Swayamvara** | Practised |
| Age | Adult marriage; **no child marriage** |
| **Widow remarriage** | Allowed |

- **Exclusion:** From Upanayana, rituals, assemblies
- **Aitareya Brahmana:** Calls daughters a source of misery

## Rig Vedic gods → role and epithets | from: S8
# 1. Nature of early Vedic religion
- **Character:** Gods personified **forces of nature** (naturalistic polytheism)
- **Henotheism/Kathenotheism:** Each god praised as supreme in turn
- **Mode of worship:** Hymns + **yajnas in the open air**; **no temples, no idols**
- **Aim:** Mainly material (cattle, sons, victory)
- **Other features:** Male gods dominate; cult of the domestic hearth
- **Rita:** Cosmic and moral order upheld by Varuna
# 2. Major gods

| **God** | **Role** | **Epithet / hymn count** |
|---|---|---|
| **Indra** | War, rain, thunder; most important god | **Purandara** (destroyer of forts); **250 hymns** - most mentioned; the largest hymn is to Indra |
| **Agni** | Fire; **intermediary between gods and men** | Second place; about **200 hymns**[GK] |
| **Varuna** | Water, **Rta** (cosmic and moral order) | Third place; ethically highest god |
| **Dyaus** | Sky/celestial father | Counterpart of Greek **Zeus**/Roman Jupiter |
| **Soma** | God of plants; the soma drink | Entire **Mandala IX** addressed to Soma [GK] |
| Mitra | Solar god, ally of Varuna | Named with Indra, Varuna, Nasatyas at Boghazkoi |
| **Aditi** | **Mother of the gods** | Female deity (others: Usha - dawn) |
| Maruts | Storms | |
| Rudra | Destruction and healing | |
| Parjanya | Rain | |
| Prithvi | Earth, fertility | |
| Aranyani | Forest | |
| Pushan | Cattle, roads | |
| Vishnu | Minor | **3 hymns** |

- ⚠ **250 – 200 – 3** hymns = **Indra – Agni – Vishnu**
> Exam angle: statement sets - "Prithvi was mother of all gods" is wrong (that is **Aditi**); Dyaus = Zeus/Jupiter is right.

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal
- **Significance:** Shift from a cattle-based tribal economy to settled agrarian territorial society → explains later rise of territorial kingship and varna hierarchy
- **Comparative note:**
  - Early Vedic = pastoral, yava, copper/bronze, egalitarian, flexible varna/occupation, women in assemblies
  - Later Vedic = agrarian, rice/wheat, iron, birth-based varna, lower status for women
- **Criticism & debates:** Extent of women's freedom debated
  - Texts praise women seers; education and assembly rights
  - But society stayed patriarchal; son-preference

## Iron — terms, sites and earliest dates | from: S2
# 1. Vedic metal terms

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|

# 2. Iron in North India
- **Dating:** Iron introduced **c. 1000-800 BCE**; common at most PGW sites; smiths and smelters important after **1000 BCE**
- **Sites:** **Atranjikhera, Hastinapur, Kaushambi**, **Taxila/Gandhara** (c. 1000 BC)
- **Uses:**
  - Iron axes + fire cleared the Ganga (Gangetic) forests (Videgha Madhava legend)
  - Iron weapons + horse-drawn chariots helped the eastward push
  - Iron ploughshares, axes, hoes for farming
# 3. Earliest iron — new dates from South India
- **New dates (South India):** South Indian Iron Age Now placed around **2100 BCE**, earlier than the Gangetic Iron Age (reported Jun 2026)

| Thirumalapuram | Tenkasi | Iron Age urn chamber, early-mid 3rd millennium BCE |
| Adichanallur | Thoothukudi | Type-site for urn burials |

- ⚠ **Krishna ayas** = iron; **lohita ayas** = copper; Rigvedic **ayas** = copper/bronze

## Agriculture, settlements, crafts and trade — features | from: S3, S5
# 1. Agriculture
- **Status:** Agriculture becomes the **primary** livelihood
- **Chief crops:** **rice (vrihi)** + **wheat**; barley continues
  - Rice recommended for Vedic rituals
- **Other crops:** Pulses/lentils, millets, **sugarcane, sesame**; double cropping mentioned
- **Ploughing:**
  - Ploughs drawn (yoked with) **6, 8, 12 and even 24 oxen**
  - **buffalo** used for ploughing
- **Land holding:** Held by the clan (vis) in common, with household head (**Grihapati**) holding it
# 2. Settlements
- **Settlements:** **villages** of mud/wattle houses
  - **burnt bricks not prevalent**; Hastinapur's mud structures modest
- **True towns:** Only after the Vedic period, in the NBPW phase ("second urbanisation": Shravasti, Kausambi, Hastinapur)
# 3. Crafts and trade
- **Crafts:** Iron-working, copper, weaving (by women), leather, pottery, carpentry
- **Trade:**
  - **Shresthins** → merchant groups
  - **Nishka** = unit of value; barter still main
  - Ox-drawn wagons

=== ROW 19 | Later Vedic polity - rise of territorial kingship; rajasuya, ashvamedha, vajapeya; the ratnins

## Territorial kingship — terms and titles | from: S0
# 1. From tribe to territory

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **jana** | Tribe |
| **janapada** | Territorial kingdom |
| **rashtra** | Territory/state - word first appears |

# 2. Nature of kingship

- **Functions:** Protects territory + runs administration + patronises ritual

| South | Bhoja [GK] |
| West | Svarat [GK] |
| North | **Virat** (vairajya) [GK] |
| Middle country | **Raja** (hereditary Kshatriya) |

## Kingdoms → region | from: S1

| **Kingdom** | **Region** |
|---|---|
| **Kuru** | Delhi-Meerut / upper Doab; capital **Hastinapur**; Kurukshetra |
| **Panchala** | Central Doab-Rohilkhand; Ahichchhatra [GK] |
| **Kosala** | Eastern UP |
| **Kashi** | Varanasi region [GK] |

## Texts → theory of divine origin of kingship | from: S2

| **Text** | **Statement on kingship** |
|---|---|
| **Aitareya Brahmana** | Earliest account of the **origin of kingship**: gods losing to the Asuras → chose a king to lead them; King = **protector of Brahmins** |
| **Shatapatha Brahmana** | Links the king to **Prajapati** (creator god, now dominant) |

- **Aitareya view:** Kingship arose from need for a war leader; later backed by divine sanction
- **Rites:** Rajasuya + Vajapeya meant to give the king divine qualities
- **Legitimacy:** Brahmanas' ritual monopoly supported royal legitimacy

## Royal sacrifices → purpose and titles | from: S3

| **Sacrifice** | **Purpose** |
|---|---|
| **Rajasuya** | Royal consecration conferring **supreme power**; five major rites incl. **Ratnahavimshi** (offerings at the ratnins' houses) |
| **Ashvamedha** | Horse sacrifice: **supremacy over other kings** and unquestioned control over the area the horse roamed (Aitareya and Shatapatha Brahmanas) |
| **Vajapeya** | "Drink of strength"; includes a **chariot race** won by the king: **superiority over his kinsmen/people** |
| **Punarabhisheka** | Re-consecration (renewed anointing) of the king [GK] |

- **Titles won through yajnas** (Ikshvaku kings):
  - **Vajapeya = Samrat/Virat**
  - **Ashvamedha = Swarat**
- **Sources:** Aitareya and Shatapatha Brahmanas
- **Effect:**
  - Large **Shrauta** sacrifices needed many priests + heavy **dakshina** → raised the power/position of Brahmanas
  - Reinforced royal power
- ⚠ **Ashvamedha** = over other kings; **Vajapeya** = over kinsmen; **Rajasuya** = supreme power

## Assemblies, army and taxes — fate and nature | from: S4, S5
# 1. Assemblies

| **Assembly** | **Later Vedic fate** |
|---|---|
| **Vidatha** | **Vidatha disappeared.** |
| **Sabha** | Kept a **judicial** role; limited to nobles and Brahmanas |
| **Samiti** | Lost power |
| **Village assemblies** | Handled local cases |

- **Sabha and Samiti continued:** Survived but came under control of chiefs, Brahmanas and **wealthy nobles**
- **Women were excluded** from assemblies
# 2. Army (both views in exam books)
- **standing elements** emerged (standing army elements: chariot warfare, iron weapons, professional warriors)
- vs still no standing army; tribal levies raised for war
# 3. Taxes — bali, shulka, bhaga

| **Tax** | **Nature** |
|---|---|
| **Bali** | Earlier voluntary; now a regular tribute |
| **Bhaga** | King's share of produce |
| **Shulka** | Customs/toll |

- **Collection:** Collected by the **Bhagadugha**; managed by the **Sangrahitri** (treasurer)
- **Payers:** **Vaishyas** = main tribute-payers
- **Source conflict on Bali:**
  - Some notes still call Bali "voluntary offerings" in this period
  - Others describe **regular, compulsory taxation** → follow this view

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Significance:** Tribal chiefdom → hereditary territorial monarchy; prepared the way for the Mahajanapadas of the 6th century BCE
- **Comparative note:**
  - Early Vedic = elective chief, voluntary bali, strong assemblies
  - Later Vedic = hereditary king, regular bali-bhaga-shulka, ratnins, assemblies in decline
- **AP angle:** Early AP dynasties (Satavahanas, Ikshvakus, Vishnukundins) performed **Ashvamedha, Rajasuya and Vajapeya** to proclaim sovereignty
  - Ashvamedha altar found at **Nagarjunakonda**

=== ROW 20 | Later Vedic society - four varnas, ashrama system, gotra, decline in the status of women

## Varnas → role; groups outside the varna order | from: S0, S1
# 1. The fourfold order
- **Origin:** Purusha Sukta (**Rigveda, 10th mandala**) = first statement of the four-fold order
  - "varna" and "shudra" words also occur in the Rigveda

| **Varna** | **Role in later Vedic texts** |
|---|---|
| **Brahmana** | Priests and teachers; seeker of livelihood; initially one of 16 classes of priests, became the dominant priestly class; APP notes call them the most powerful class |
| **Kshatriya/Rajanya** | Rulers, warriors; political power and protection |
| **Vaishya** | Farmers, traders, cattle-rearers, some artisans; the **tribute payers** (tribute-payers) |
| **Shudra** | Service to upper three; no Upanayana, no right to study the Vedas |

- **Dvija:** Upper three = **dvija / Dvija** (twice-born, via upanayana)
- **Aitareya Brahmana:** Shudra = "servant of another", could be expelled and beaten/slain/struck at will; Vaishya = tribute payer
# 2. Rigidity and emergence of caste
- **Later Vedic shift:** Birth-based occupation + rigid hierarchy set in during the **later Vedic period**; social mobility fell
- **Jatis:** Proliferation (sub-castes, mixed castes) is post-Vedic
  - Gupta-era diversification; post-Satavahana mixed castes in Andhra
> Exam angle: "caste as a new form of social organisation" questions test that varna rigidified after the Rig Vedic phase.
# 3. Occupational groups, guilds and FIRSTs
- **Specialisation:** Iron-age economy → specialised crafts
- **Money-lending:** Satapatha Brahmana = **first reference to money-lending**
- **Guilds:** Guild-like bodies of Vaishyas (gana, headed by a shreshthin) in later Vedic texts
# 4. Outside the varna order
- **Outside the varna order:** **Vratyas, Nishadas / Vratyas and Nishadas**
  - Rathakaras kept sacred-thread privilege
- **Discrimination:** Against **Shudras and Chandalas** in later Vedic society
- **Chandala:** Named as a despised birth in the Chhandogya Upanishad
- **Formal rule:** Untouchability as a formal rule = post-Vedic Dharmasutras

## Gotra and kinship terms → meaning | from: S2
# 1. Gotra
- **Meaning:** Literally "cow-pen" → **lineage from a common male ancestor**
- **Word:**
  - First occurs in the **Rig Veda** (UPPSC key)
  - **Atharvaveda / Atharva Veda** uses it in the lineage sense
- **Institution:** Established in **Later Vedic** times
- **Rule:** **gotra exogamy** = no marriage within the same gotra
  - + caste endogamy + **sapinda** prohibition ("sasan" rule) → North Indian pattern
# 2. Kinship terms

| **Kula** | Family/household |
| **Vamsa** / vamsha | Lineage/dynasty |
| **Gotra** | Exogamous clan |
| **Kosa** | Treasury |

- ⚠ **Trap:** **Kosa** (treasury) is the odd one out (UPSC 1996)
# 3. Scholar → work

## Ashramas and purusharthas — stages, duties and goals | from: S3, S4

## Women — texts → statements on status | from: S8
# 1. Decline in status
- **Status:** **declined**

  - Excluded from religious rites/rituals
  - Lost **attendance at assemblies (Sabha)**: in Rig Vedic times women attended Sabha; Atharva Veda mentions women in the Samiti

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - Varna + ashrama = **Varna-ashrama-dharma**, normative frame of later Hindu society
  - Later Vedic society fixed varna, gotra, samskaras and ashramas → base of later Dharmashastra society + target of Buddhist/Jain protest
- **Comparative note:**
  - Rig Vedic = relatively fluid, egalitarian; flexible varna; women in assemblies
  - Later Vedic = birth-based varna, gotra exogamy, women excluded
  - Harappan = differentiation but no textual caste
- **AP angle:**
  - Gautamiputra Satakarni opposed varna-sankara and protected varnashrama dharma
  - Post-Satavahana (Ikshvaku) society: mixed castes and sub-castes
  - Satavahana Andhra: **anuloma** offspring = **Ugra**; **pratiloma** offspring = **Chandala**
