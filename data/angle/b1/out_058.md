## Aims, methods and outcomes of the movements | from: S2, S3
# 1. Bhu Poratam
- Aim: occupy and distribute government and surplus land to the landless
- Continues the 1956-69 land-campaign tradition
# 2. Anti-arrack movement (Dubagunta, 1991)
- **Origin:** **1991**, **Dubagunta**, **Nellore**; led by rural women
- **Literacy-campaign link:** Grew out of **"Aksharajyothi"** adult-literacy classes
  - Lessons led women to discuss men's arrack drinking, debt, domestic violence and men missing work
- **Methods:** Women closed arrack shops and stopped arrack auctions using **broomsticks and chilli powder**
- **Spread:** From **Nellore** to the whole State
- **Demand:** Stop the sale and auction of arrack
- **Outcome:**

| **Date** | **Step** |
|---|---|
| **1 Oct 1993** | **Arrack prohibited** in AP (CDX, APP) |
| **1995** | Total prohibition across the State (UPSC) (later abandoned) |
| 1997 | Prohibition lifted [GK] |

- **Significance:** One of India's most successful women-led movements

## Universal angles | from: S4 | universal
- **Significance:** Movements moved AP politics from the national-movement elite → caste, gender and class assertion (the **Mahasabhas**, **MRPS**, the anti-arrack women)
- **Current relevance:** **200th birth anniversary of Jyotirao Phule** commemorated on **11 April 2026**
  - Phule's **Satyashodhak Samaj (1873)** + Periyar's **Self-Respect Movement** inspired AP's Adi-Andhra leaders
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - **1995** prohibition later "largely abandoned" (UPSC)
  - After **Mandal**, OBC and Dalit movements split over **sub-categorisation**
