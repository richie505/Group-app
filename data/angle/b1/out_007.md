=== ROW 37 | Mauryan administration - central, provincial and local; espionage, judiciary, revenue system

## Mauryan state — nature, Saptanga elements and checks | from: S0
- **Nature:** Highly centralised **"paternal despotism"** - king heads executive, legislature, judiciary
- **Checks on the king:** **Mantriparishad**, **paura-janapada** assemblies, public opinion ("a chariot cannot move on one wheel")

**Saptanga theory - seven elements, in Kautilya's standard order**

| **No.** | **Element** |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Swami** (king) [GK] |
| 2 | **Amatya** (ministers) [GK] |
| 3 | **Janapada** (territory and people) |
| 4 | **Durga** (fort) |
| 5 | **Kosha** (treasury) |
| 6 | **Danda/Bala** (army, justice; army, force) |
| 7 | **Mitra** (ally) |

- ⚠ **Trap:** some notes list Durga 6th, Land 7th and add an 8th, "Enemy" ("Shatru") - learn the standard seven

## Judiciary — courts, judges and penalties | from: S7
- **Courts:** Each with three judges and three amatyas

| **Court** | **Cases** | **Presided by** |
|---|---|---|
| **Dharmasthiya** | **Civil** (marriage, contract, inheritance) | 3 dharmasthas/amatyas [GK] |
| **Kantakashodhana** ("removal of thorns") | **Criminal** | 3 pradeshtris/amatyas [GK] |

- ⚠ **Dharmasthiya** = civil; **Kantakashodhana** = criminal
- **Court ladder:** Village (Grama Sabha) → Sangrahana → Dronamukha → Janapada → **king** (final authority, supreme judge)
- **Pradvivaka:** Chief judicial adviser/judge (APPSC G2 2019: **judicial system**)
- **Punishments:** Severe - fines, amputation, mutilation, death
  - **Severity depended on varna**
  - No written code per Megasthenes
- **Civic fines:** Dumping dirt on / littering streets, nuisance
- **Slave rules:** e.g. selling a pregnant slave without arranging maternity is punished

## Revenue terms → meaning and sources | from: S8
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Sita** | Income from **crown (state) land** (**crown lands**) |
| **Bhaga** | King's **share of produce** - normally **1/6** (**one-sixth**); Megasthenes: **1/4 to 1/6** |
| **Bali** | Additional levy/tribute |
| **Pindakara** | Tax assessed on **groups of villages** |
| **Pranaya** | **Emergency tax** ("gift of affection") |
| **Vivita** | Pasture land and its dues |
| **Hiranya** | Tax paid **only in cash** |
| **Setu** | Income from gardens, orchards, root crops (some notes: **Kara** on fruit/flower gardens) |
| **Vana** | Income from **forests** |

- **Land revenue:** Main income; Megasthenes: **king owned all land**, revenue collected directly by royal officials
- **Other income:** Ferries, forests, mines, pastures, licence fees, fines
- **Lumbini:** Exempted from bali; bhaga cut to one-eighth (Rummindei pillar)
- **Evasion:** **Tax evasion on sales punishable by death** (Megasthenes)

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal
- **Significance:** First centralised bureaucratic empire; census and standardised measures
- **AP angle:** **Suvarnagiri** (Dakshinapatha) = southern capital; governed the Deccan, Andhra in its orbit
- **Comparative note:** Later dynasties (Eastern Chalukyas - Ashtadasha Tirthas; Kakatiyas) used the Saptanga and **18 Tirthas** model
- **Criticism & debates:** Arthashastra describes an ideal; how fully it worked is disputed
  - Kautilya: a kingdom cannot run on one wheel

=== ROW 38 | Mauryan economy, society and urban life; Mauryan art and architecture (pillars, Sarnath capital, Barabar caves, Didarganj Yakshi)

## Agriculture, state monopolies and taxes — terms | from: S0
- **Base:** Agriculture

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Sita** | Crown land cultivated directly for/under the state (Sitadhyaksha), often by slaves and hired labourers; large-scale use of slaves in farming |
| **Drona** | Unit for measuring rainfall (~13.2 kg water) near granaries, for six regions |
| **Dronavapa** | Land sown with one drona of seed |
| **Bhaga** (land revenue) | **1/4 to 1/6** of the produce, collected directly by royal officials (Megasthenes) |
| **Pindakara** | Tax on groups of villages |
| **Kara** | Tax on orchards |
| **Hiranya** | Tax paid only in cash |

- **Officials:** **Samaharta**, **Gopa**, **Sthanika**
- **Famine relief:** seed, cattle, tools in drought and famine
- **Rain gauge:** rainfall measured in **drona**
- **Mines and metals:** state monopoly under the Akaradhyaksha; iron from nearby deposits = one basis of Magadhan power
- **State control over:** **mining, forestry, salt, liquor, arms** and metallurgy
- **Tax evasion:** could mean death

## Crafts, guilds, credit and coins | from: S1, S2
- **Growth of crafts:** Good roads + state incentives spread crafts
- **Trade:** Sale of goods state-supervised
- **Credit:** Usury prevailed in absence of banking; interest **15% a year** (Arthashastra); students, poor and sick got debt relief
- **Pottery:** **NBPW** = typical Mauryan pottery
- **Guilds - patronage:** merchants (shreshthis), artisans and **shrenis** financed early Buddhist centres, including in Andhra
- **Guilds - military role:** Arthashastra counts shreni troops among army types
- **Imperial currency:** **Punch-marked silver coins** with symbols such as peacocks and hills
  - Minted in state mints; barter continued alongside
  - Punch-marked coins + NBPW sherds found as far as Bangladesh, Odisha, Andhra and Karnataka
  - Punch-marked silver panas/karshapanas go back to the Mahajanapada age (6th century BCE)

| **Coin (Arthashastra)** | **Metal** |
|---|---|
| **Pana** (karshapana) | Silver |
| **Mashaka** | Copper |
| **Kakani** | Copper, 1/4 mashaka |
| Suvarna | Gold |

- **Coin name:** **Pana** (APPSC G2 2011; official key left blank)
> Exam angle: "Mauryan silver coin was called" - Pana (not Niska, Kakini or Shatamana).

## Trade routes, ports and partners → places | from: S3
# 1. Trade routes
- **Uttarapatha:** Northern highway from **Taxila** through the Ganga valley / Pataliputra to Tamralipti
  - The **royal highway** ran from the north-west to Pataliputra
- **Dakshinapatha:** Great southern trade route; Andhra (Krishna-Godavari delta) a node on it
- **Three northern routes:** Shravasti-Rajgir, Shravasti-Pratishthan, Rajgir-Sindhu-Sauvira
  - Centres: Pataliputra, Takshashila, Kashi, Ujjain, Kaushambi
- **Caravans:** Led by a **Sarthavaha**; state took road tax for protection
# 2. Ports

| **Port** | **Coast** |
|---|---|
| **Tamralipti** | East (main port of the Ganga basin) (APPSC 2012) |
| **Bharukachchha (Broach)** | West |
| **Sopara** | West |
| Bernis (Berenice) | Red Sea port - trade with **Egypt** via the Red Sea |

# 3. Foreign trade
- **Partners:** Greece, Persia, Egypt, Syria, Sri Lanka, Java, Sumatra
- **Exports:** Diamonds, pearls, muslin, fine textiles

## Megasthenes' Indica → observations on society | from: S4
- **Indica:** by **Megasthenes** (Greek; envoy of **Seleucus**)
- **Seven classes (BPSC 2017):** philosophers, farmers, herdsmen/hunters, artisans, soldiers, overseers, councillors
  - Hereditary occupation + endogamy
- **Other observations:** said India had **no slavery** (**no slaves**); theft rare; no written law
- **Andhras:** noted their forts - **30 walled towns** (p.17) (some notes say 80)
- ⚠ Megasthenes: **no slaves**; Kautilya: **nine categories of slaves** - sources differ

## Arthashastra on society — castes, learning, slaves and women | from: S5, S6, S7
# 1. Caste and learning
- **Mixed castes:** five mixed castes called **Antyavasayin**, outside Aryan society
- **Shudras:** **Shudras could own land**
- **Learned men:** called **Sishtas**
- **Centres of learning:** **Taxila** (most famous; UKPSC 2010), **Ujjain**, **Varanasi**
- **Literature:** Bhadrabahu's **Kalpasutra** belongs to this age
# 2. Slavery and labour
- **Categories:** Kautilya lists **nine categories of slaves** (dasas); Ashokan inscriptions also mention dasas
- **Sources of slavery:** birth, sale (self-sale), capture in war, **judicial punishment**
  - No Arya could be made a slave
- **Female slave:** bore her master a son → became **free**; son legitimate, entitled to the **legal status of the master's son**
  - ⚠ **PYQ:** UPSC 2022 set these three statements
- **Protective rules:**
  - Penalty for selling a pregnant slave without arranging maternity
  - Freedom by payment

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Karmakaras** | Wage labour |
| **Vishti** | Unpaid forced labour for the state → later Telugu **Vetti** |
| **Moksha** | Divorce |
| **Stridhana** | A woman's own property (jewellery, maintenance) |
| Anuloma and pratiloma | Inter-varna marriages, which occurred |

# 3. Women
- **Divorce (moksha):** Arthashastra **allows divorce** to a wife deserted by her husband (UPSC 1996); also allowed for mutual enmity in certain marriage forms
- **Remarriage:** **Widow remarriage** permitted for widows and long-deserted wives (some notes say widow remarriage ceased)

## Religions → followers and patrons | from: S8
- **General policy:** Liberal and tolerant - free choice of faith, no persecution; state still protected **varna and ashrama**

| **Faith** | **Patron / evidence** |
|---|---|
| **Jainism** | Chandragupta Maurya followed Bhadrabahu to Shravanabelagola |
| **Ajivikas** | Ashoka and Dasaratha gave Barabar/Nagarjuni caves to them; PE VII names Ajivikas and Nirgranthas |
| **Buddhism** | Ashoka's patronage, stupas, Sangha |
| **Brahmanism** | Continued (Kautilya a Brahmana, 'dvijarshabh') |

- **Untouchables:** Chandalas, Shvapakas grew in number
  - Ashokan edicts mention neither varna nor sati

- **Medium:** Shift from **wood to stone**
- **Two streams:**
  - **Royal (court) art** - palaces, pillars, stupas, caves
  - **Folk (popular) art** - yaksha/yakshi statues, clay/terracotta figurines

| **Object / figure** | **Site / findspot** |
|---|---|
| Rock-cut elephant | **Dhauli** (near Bhubaneswar, Odisha) |

- **Religious spread:** Yakshas revered in Jainism, Buddhism and Hinduism alike (UPSC)

## Pillars — material, parts, capitals → sites, Persian debate | from: S10
# 1. Material and form
- **Shaft:** **Monolithic**, free-standing, circular, smooth shafts of **Chunar sandstone** (buff/grey); **no base**
- **Mauryan polish:** **"Mauryan polish"** gives a lustrous metallic shine, like **NBPW** pottery
- **Pillar sites:** Sarnath, Sanchi, Rampurva, Lauriya Nandangarh, Lauriya Araraj, Vaishali, Kaushambi, Sankisa
# 2. Parts of the capital
- **Sequence:** shaft → bell-shaped inverted lotus (**ghanta**) → abacus → **capital animal**

| **Part** | **Name** |
|---|---|
| Pedestal/abacus | **chowki** |
| **Bell-shaped inverted lotus** base | Baithaki (Ghantaki) / ghantaki |
| Decorative band | Mekhala |
| Crowning **animal** | Shirsha |

- **Animals on capitals:** **lion, bull, elephant**

| **Capital** | **Site** |
|---|---|
| **Four lions** back to back + Dharmachakra (four-lion capital) | **Sarnath** - now the **National Emblem** |
| **Bull** | **Rampurva** |
| Single lion | **Lauriya Nandangarh** (Champaran, Bihar) |
| Elephant | **Sankisa**[GK] |

# 3. Persian influence debate

| **Achaemenid (Persian) similarity** | **Mauryan difference** |
|---|---|
| Polished surface, animal capitals | **No base** (Persian pillars have bases) |
| Edict style like Darius's - Achaemenid edicts resemble Ashoka's in form and words **"lipi/dipi"**, third person turning to first | **Smooth** (not fluted) shaft |
| - | **Inverted lotus** bell of distinct form and round abacus with free-standing round animals |

- **Verdict (against influence):** the differences → an Indian adaptation
> Exam angle: "Persian influence" questions - similar in polish and edict idiom, different in base, shaft and lotus.

## Pataliputra palace and city → excavated sites | from: S12
- **Palace:** Chandragupta's palace and assembly hall
- **Megasthenes:** compared it to the palaces of **Susa and Ecbatana**
- **Fa-Hien (5th c. CE):** admired it

| **Site** | **Finding** |
|---|---|

## Barabar and Nagarjuni caves — donor, sect, caves, inscriptions | from: S13
- **Builders:** **Ashoka** and grandson **Dasharatha** for **Ajivika** ascetics
  - Later used by Buddhist, Jain and Hindu worshippers
- **Status:** **India's oldest surviving rock-cut caves**, 3rd c. BCE (not Badami - UPSC 2013)
- **Location:** Barabar and Nagarjuni hills, **Jehanabad district, Bihar** (near Gaya)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes place the Nagarjuni caves in Guntur, AP - mix-up with Nagarjunakonda

| **Hill** | **Caves** |
|---|---|
| Barabar (4) | **Lomas Rishi**, **Sudama**, Viswamitra, Karna Chopar |
| Nagarjuni (3) | Vahiyaka, **Gopika**, Vadathika |

- **Satghar:** The seven caves together = **Satghar** ("Seven Houses")
- **Material:** Carved from **granite**; mirror polish; vaulted ceilings give an echo
- **Lomas Rishi:** Entrance = **earliest stone ogee-shaped arch** / **earliest stone ogee (horseshoe) arch**, copying a thatched wooden hut (the Parnashala style)
- **Gopi (Gopika):** Largest Nagarjuni cave; has inscriptions
- **Inscriptions:** Dedicatory **Brahmi** inscriptions call Ashoka **"King Piyadasi"**
  - Three Barabar caves carry Ashoka's inscriptions
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Inscriptions are 3rd c. BCE, not 6th c. (UPPSC 2017)

## FIRSTs, oldest structures and chronology | from: S15
| **FIRST / oldest** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Oldest stone structure in India** | **Sanchi Stupa** (APPSC key 2018) - among Ajanta, Sanchi, Konark and Brihadeeswara |
| **Oldest rock-cut** | **Barabar caves** = oldest surviving rock-cut structures |
| **Oldest stupas (some notes)** | Bharhut = most ancient stupa in India; **Bhattiprolu** = most ancient in Andhra |

- **Order:** Dhauli elephant (3rd c. BCE) earlier than Udayagiri Varaha (Gupta, Chandragupta II), Mahabalipuram (Pallava), Lingaraja (UPSC 2015)
> Exam angle: "which was made earliest" and "which of these are Mauryan" sets.

## Universal angles | from: S16 | universal
- **Current relevance:**
  - **Barabar caves** (in the news, 27 May 2026; Jun 2026): unsung "blueprint" of Indian rock-cut architecture; India's oldest rock-cut structures; **4 + 3 = Satghar**; Ajivika dedication
  - **Balirajgarh fort** (Madhubani, Bihar; Apr 2026): excavated by ASI; habitation from Mauryan to Pala period; punch-marked coins
  - **Vaishya Tekri** (Ujjain; 23 Aug 2026): MP to resume excavation after ~90 years; 3rd-century BCE Mauryan stupa, possibly larger than Sanchi; earlier digs found **punch-marked and cast Avanti coins**
- **Significance:**
  - Mauryan stone art began Indian monumental architecture
  - Mauryan rainfall records predate Korea's Cheugugi gauge (1441 CE)
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Earliest/first rock-cut architecture/caves = Barabar
  - Earliest stone ogee arch = Lomas Rishi
  - Sarnath capital = National Emblem
- **AP angle:** Guntupalli chaitya compared with the Barabar caves; Bhattiprolu = Andhra's oldest stupa

=== ROW 39 | Decline of the Mauryan Empire - competing explanations

## Later Mauryan rulers → contributions and division of the empire | from: S0
- **Vishnu Purana:** lists **seven successors** of Ashoka
- **Usual list:** **Kunala, Dasharatha, Samprati, Shalishuka, Devavarman, Shatadhanvan, Brihadratha**
- **Division of the empire:** probably split in two

| **Part** | **Rulers** |
|---|---|
| Western (north-west) | **Kunala**, later **Samprati**; Indo-Greeks virtually replaced them by **180 BC** |
| Eastern (Pataliputra) | Dasharatha ... **Brihadratha** (last) |

| **Ruler** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Dasharatha** | Ashoka's grandson; dedicated the **Nagarjuni caves** to the Ajivikas |
| **Samprati** | Also a grandson; Jain patron; ruled c. **230-215 BCE** |

## End of the dynasty — Brihadratha, Pushyamitra's coup, year | from: S3
- **Last king:** **Brihadratha**, **7th king** in succession after Ashoka; ruled the eastern half from **Pataliputra**
- **Successor dynasty:** **Sungas** came to power in Magadha after the Mauryas (APPSC G2 2003) - immediate successors
- **Founder:** **Pushyamitra Sunga**, Mauryan **commander-in-chief (senani)**
- **Manner:** Military coup - his general Pushyamitra killed (assassinated) Brihadratha at a **military review** of the army (APPSC 2023; BPSC 2008)
  - Army-review detail from Bana's Harshacharita
  - ⚠ **Key:** BPSC 2008 - Brihadratha
- **Year:**
  - **187 BC** (UPSC)
  - Some notes and the AP History PYQ book give **c. 185 BCE**
- **Order in Magadha:** **Mauryas → Sungas → Kanvas** (then Satavahana conquest)

## Foreign invaders and breakaway regions → powers | from: S1, S2

## Historians → theories of decline, and the evidence | from: S4, S5
| **Theory** | **Historian** | **Core argument** |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmanical reaction** | **H.P. Shastri**[GK] | Ashoka's pro-Buddhist policies and the ban on sacrifices angered Brahmins; the Sunga coup was a Brahmin revolt |
| **Ashoka's pacifism** | **H.C. Raychaudhuri**[GK] | After Kalinga, non-violence weakened the army's fighting spirit (dulled military spirit) |
| **Financial crisis** | **D.D. Kosambi**[GK] | Huge army, bureaucracy and Dhamma spending emptied the treasury; debased later coins |
| **Oppressive provincial rule** | Divyavadana evidence [GK] | Revolts at **Taxila** against wicked officials (Bindusara's and Ashoka's reigns) |
| **Over-centralisation, no national consciousness** | **Romila Thapar**[GK] | Administration depended on the king; a weak ruler meant collapse; over-centralised administration, weak successors |

- ⚠ **Shastri** = Brahmanical reaction; **Raychaudhuri** = pacifism; **Kosambi** = finance; **Thapar** = over-centralisation
- **Weak successors:** **oppressive**, "ineffective and oppressive" successors after Ashoka's death in **232 BC** (UPSC)
- **Frontier:** neglect of the **north-west frontier** - Mauryas neglected frontier defence (UPSC); Indo-Greek raids drained resources
- **Against the Brahmanical-reaction theory:** Pushyamitra was a Mauryan general; Sunga period saw Buddhist art thrive
- **Evidence for the financial crisis theory:**
  - **Ashoka's spending:** heavy spending on the **Dhamma mission** + donations to the Buddhist Sangha strained the treasury (UPSC)
  - **Coin debasement:** **Debasement of later punch-marked coins** - more alloy in the silver → sign of fiscal strain
  - **Taxation:** new taxes on everything, even actors and prostitutes (Arthashastra) → revenue hunger; super-heavy taxation (UPSC)
> Exam angle: match theory with historian; "which is NOT a cause" type.

## Successor dynasties — Sungas and Kanvas | from: S6, S7
# 1. Sungas
- **Pushyamitra:** **Brahmana** senapati of the **Bharadvaja gotra**; led a strong Brahmanical revival
- **Rituals:** **two Ashvamedhas** (Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva)
- **Court:** Patanjali linked with his court
- **Buddhism debate:**
  - Buddhist texts (Divyavadana) call him a persecutor of Buddhists
  - Against: Sanchi and Bharhut grew under Sunga rule
- **Territory:** Sunga core = Magadha, Malwa-Vidisha, Narmada valley
  - North-west lost to Indo-Greeks; Deccan to the Satavahanas
- **Indo-Greek wars:** Pushyamitra held the Ganga heartland against **Demetrius** and **Menander**
- **Malavikagnimitram (Kalidasa):** grandson **Vasumitra** defeated a Yavana army on the **Sindhu** river
- **Menander (Milinda):** capital Sakala (Sialkot); debated **Nagasena** in the **Milindapanho**
# 2. Kanvas
- **Founder:** **Vasudeva Kanva**, Brahmana minister; killed the last Sunga **Devabhuti** and seized power (UPSC 2003)
- **Four Kanva kings (c. 73-28 BC):** Vasudeva, Bhumimitra, Narayana, **Susharman**
- **End:** Susharman ended by the Andhras/Satavahanas

## Rise of the Satavahanas — origin, founder, titles | from: S9
- **Origin:** **feudatories of the Mauryas**; independent in the Deccan after the decline
- **Founder:** **Simuka (Srimukha)**
- **Puranas:** call them **Andhra-bhrityas**
- **Achievements:** defeated the Sunga-Kanva line; first to unite the Krishna-Godavari region
- **Feudatory chiefs:** **Bhojas, Maharathis, Rashtrikas** rose in the Deccan

## Universal angles | from: S10 | universal
- **Current relevance:** Samprati (Ashoka's grandson) ruled c. 230-215 BCE (Apr 2026)
- **AP angle:**
  - Ashokan edicts at **Erragudi and Rajula Mandagiri (Kurnool)** → Mauryan rule in Andhra
  - At the end of Mauryan rule Andhra feudatories declared independence → Satavahanas rose from feudatories to rulers of the Deccan and Andhra
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - No single theory accepted; historians stress structural weakness and weak successors over religious reaction
  - Ashoka-blame thesis vs structural causes; Pushyamitra's alleged persecution
- **Significance:** end of the first pan-Indian empire → regional kingdoms + Brahmanical revival

## Pushyamitra and Buddhism — the persecution debate | from: S2
