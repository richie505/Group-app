=== ROW 101 | Telugu language and literature to the 14th century - Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana, Palkuriki Somanatha

## Telugu language — family, names, epithets and literary ages | from: S0, S4
# 1. Family and status
- **Family:** **Dravidian** (CDCA 2026)
- **Sub-branch:** **South-Central Dravidian**; sister languages include Gondi and Kui
- **Status:** Official language of AP; one of the **22 Eighth Schedule languages**
- **Origin:** Grew out of the **Desi bhasha (Paisachi)** of Prakrit; D.C. Sircar found Desi words very close to Telugu
# 2. Names of the language → etymology
| **Name** | **Etymology / note** |
|---|---|
| Telugu | **Trilinga theory**: Andhra = **Trilinga desa** after three Shaiva centres **Srisailam, Kaleshwaram and Draksharama** → "Telugu" derived from Trilinga |
| **Tenugu** | some derive Tenugu from tene (honey) |
| **Andhra / Andhrika** | other names of the language |

# 3. Epithets of Telugu → coined by
| **Epithet** | **Coined by** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|
| **"Italian of the East"** | Venetian traveller **Niccolo de' Conti** (15th c.) | Reason: Telugu's rounded, vowel-ending, highly phonetic character; asked in APPSC GS 2021 (key: Italian of the East) |
| **"Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa"** (Telugu is the finest of the regional languages) | **Sri Krishnadevaraya** | in Amuktamalyada |

- ⚠ **Italian of the East** = a foreign traveller (**Niccolo de' Conti**); **Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa** = a king (**Krishnadevaraya**)
# 4. Periodisation of Telugu literature
| **Group** | **Age** | **Period** |
|---|---|---|
| Early ages | **Pre-Nannaya** | to c. 1000 |
| Early ages | **Nannaya age** | 11th c. |
| Early ages | **Shiva Kavi Yugam** | **1060-1260** |
| Middle ages | **Tikkana age** | 13th c. |
| Middle ages | **Errana age** | 14th c. |
| Middle ages | **Srinatha age** | 15th c. |
| Later ages | **Prabandha (Rayala) age** | 16th c. |
| Later ages | **Dakshinatya (southern Nayaka) age** | 17th-18th c. |
| Later ages | **Modern age** | 19th c. onward |

- **Note:** Boundaries other than Shiva Kavi Yugam and Errana age are approximate

- **Source script:** **Brahmi script** → Telugu script

| **Stage** | **Script** |
|---|---|

## Nannaya and Narayana Bhattu → contributions and the Nandampudi grant | from: S6
# 1. Nannaya → contribution
- **Position:** Court poet, teacher and adviser of **Rajaraja Narendra** (r. **1018-1061 CE**) at **Rajamahendravaram**, 11th century
- **Title as pioneer:** **Adi Kavi** (first poet) of Telugu; began the work + set rules of Telugu grammar
- **Translation:** **2.5 parvas** of the Mahabharata = **Adi, Sabha and part of Aranya**
  - Andhra Mahabharatamu = **first book in Telugu**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "first three parvas and part of the fourth" (one note) is WRONG; all other notes say Adi, Sabha + half of Aranya
- **Style:**
  - Pioneered **Champu** style (mixed prose + verse)
  - Fitted Sanskrit metres to Telugu
  - Used **Akkara** metre borrowed from Kannada
  - Style known for **Akshara Ramyata**
- **Grammar:** **Andhra Shabda Chintamani** = treatise on Telugu grammar **written in Sanskrit** (G2 2017, 2025 keys)
  - Authorship debated by some scholars
- **Other works:** Indra Vijayam, Chamundika Vilasam
- **Titles:** **Adikavi**, **Vagadheeshwara**, **Vidyadvaitudu**, **Shabda Shasanudu**
  - Tikkana praised him as Kavitva Visharadudu
# 2. Narayana Bhattu - assistant in the translation
| **Grant** | **Recipient** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nandampudi** agrahara (Rajaraja Narendra) | **Narayana Bhattu** (Narayana Bhatta), Nannaya's assistant in the translation | The **Nandampudi donation inscription** records the gift and is the evidence of his help (G2 2019 key); **Nannaya wrote it** |

- **Nandampudi grant recipient:**
  - Notes say **Narayana Bhattu**
  - APPSC keys accepted "Nandampudi given to **Nannaya**" (G2 2017 key; G2 2025 statement III marked correct)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** If both names are offered, follow the key's wording of the question
- **Narayana Bhattu:**
  - Polyglot, knew **eight languages**
  - Titles: **Ashtabhasha Kavisekhara**, Kaviraja Shekhara, Karibha Vajrankudu, **Vangmaya Durandharudu** (in the Bharatam)
  - Someshwara I of Kalyani made him his representative at Rajamahendravaram

## Srinatha and the Prabandha age — works, patrons, features | from: S14
# 1. Srinatha - works and patrons
- **Srinatha (c. 1365-1441):**
  - Title: **Kavi Sarvabhauma**
  - Bridge between Kavitraya and Prabandha age
  - Main patron: **Pedakomati Vema Reddi**

| **Work** | **Note** |
|---|---|
| **Sringara Naishadham** | from Sriharsha's Naishadhiyacharita (Nala-Damayanti) |
| **Haravilasam** | dedicated to merchant **Tippayya Setti**; describes the sea trade |
| **Kashikhandam** | Kashi and Shiva; CDI says it was written at **Kataya Vema's** request |
| **Bhimakhandam** (Bhimeswara Puranam) | on Bhimeswara of Draksharama [GK]; CDI links it to the Bhima river |
| **Palnati Veera Charitra** | the Battle of Palnadu; mentions instruments such as Sannai and Mukha Veena |
| Sivaratri Mahatmyam, Marutratcharitra | - |

- **Srinatha's disputed work:** **Palnati Vira Charitra** listed in notes, but APPSC 2025 key treated it as **not** his
  - Other options: Haravilasam, Madanavilasa Bhanam, Sivaratri Mahatmyam
- **Distractor:** **Veerabhadra Vijayam** is by **Bammera Potana**, NOT Srinatha
# 2. Prabandha age - features, first prabandha, patronage
- **Features:**
  - Epic kavya → prabandha
  - Strict metrical rules; elaborate descriptions; strong shringara element
  - Single episode, not a whole epic
  - Set descriptions introduced by Nannechoda's Kumarasambhava
- **First prabandha:** Notes credit **Errana** as first prabandha writer; others call **Srinatha** the pioneer (Sringara Naishadham, Haravilasam)
- **Patronage:**
  - Peak: **Ashtadiggajas** at **Krishnadevaraya**'s court
  - Reddi courts → Srinatha and Errana
  - Vijayanagara Nayaka courts spread the style
- **First influence:** Telugu literature "first influenced" by the **Vijayanagara Empire** (APPSC-GS 2019 key)

## Causes, effects and debates | from: S0, S1, S4
# 1. Arab conquest - causes and effects
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Causes** | Arab maritime interests + expansion + piracy against Arab ships |
| **Religion** | No mass Islamisation; Hindus + Buddhists given **dhimmi** status |
| **Limited reach** | Rule **confined to Sindh**: Thar + Aravallis; strength of Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas, Palas; weak resources |
| **Knowledge transfer** | Indian maths (**decimal and place-value** systems), astronomy, medicine → Islamic world → Europe |

# 2. Mahmud's raids - motives and effects
- **Motives:** More than religion
  - Political expansion; temple wealth to fund the state
  - Control of Punjab; prestige
- **Effect:** Exposed India's military weakness + opened the north-west frontier
# 3. Causes of Turkish (Ghurid) success
- **Political:** No political unity in North India (UPPSC 2024: A true, R the correct explanation)
  - Post-Pratihara collapse → fragmented, rival Rajput states
- **Military:** Superior war technique (APPSC key)
  - Mobile **cavalry and mounted archers** vs elephant-reliant Indian armies
  - Frontiers exposed after Mahmud's raids
- **Social:**
  - R.S. Sharma's "Indian feudalism" → feudal levies, sub-infeudation, closed village economy → weak central armies
  - Caste confined fighting to a few groups
- **Debate:** Historians stress military organisation > religious zeal
> Exam angle: APPSC-G1 2018 asked why Ghori won. Key: **superior war technology of the Turks**.
