## RTCs, Communal Award and Poona Pact → year, persons, provisions | from: S3
# 1. Round Table Conferences
- **Attendance:** Ambedkar represented the **Depressed Classes** at **all three RTCs** (1930, 1931, 1932); Gandhi attended only the **2nd** (1931)
- **Demand:** **separate electorates** for depressed classes → clash with Gandhi at the 2nd RTC
# 2. Communal Award
- **Announced:** By **Ramsay MacDonald**, **16 August 1932**
- **Provision:** Separate electorates for minorities, including **depressed classes** (gave **71** seats)
# 3. Poona Pact - Yerwada fast, signatories, seats, electorate
- **Gandhi's stand:** Fasted against separate electorates, fearing a permanent split in Hindu society
- **Yerwada fast:** Gandhi, jailed in **Yerwada (Pune)** Jail, began a **fast unto death** (20 Sept 1932)
- **Date:** **September 1932** (24 Sept; some notes give 25 Sept)
- **Signatories:** **Dr B.R. Ambedkar** (depressed classes) + **Madan Mohan Malaviya** (caste Hindus), Malaviya mediating
  - Popularly called a Gandhi-Ambedkar pact

| **Feature** | **Communal Award** | **Poona Pact** |
|---|---|---|
| Electorate | Separate (+ general vote) | **Joint** electorate with reserved seats |
| Provincial seats for depressed classes | **71** | **147** (APP: 148) |

- **Terms:**
  - Separate electorates dropped; **joint electorates** retained
  - Reserved seats in provincial legislatures raised from **71 to 148** / **148 seats** (some notes: **147 reserved seats**)
  - Similar principle at the Centre: reservation / depressed-class share in the central legislature
  - Fair representation / fair share in civil services + a share of education grants
- ⚠ **71** = Communal Award; **147 / 148** = Poona Pact
# 4. Aftermath and remarks exchanged
- **Harijan upliftment:** Gandhi made it part of his programme (UPSC 2025)
- **Harijan Sevak Sangh (1932):** Founded by Gandhi against untouchability
- **Ambedkar on Gandhi:**
  - Rejected the term **"Harijan"**
  - Wrote **"What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables"** (1945)

=== ROW 251 | Ambedkar's contribution to the making of the Constitution - Drafting Committee and key interventions

## Drafting Committee → members, dates, numbers | from: S0
# 1. Constituencies Ambedkar represented
- **Before partition (1946):** Elected from **Bengal** (Jessore-Khulna area) with support of Jogendra Nath Mandal
- **After partition:** Districts went to East Pakistan → lost the seat; re-elected from **Bombay** in the seat vacated by M.R. Jayakar (1947)
- ⚠ **Bengal** (1946) → **Bombay** (1947)
# 2. Drafting Committee - set-up and work
- **Set up:** Constituent Assembly resolution, **29 August 1947**; the most important committee
- **Chairman:** **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**
- **Members:** **7 members** (chairman + 6 others)
- **Basis:** B.N. Rau's initial draft (Oct 1947) built on committee reports
- **Work:** Sat for only **141 days**; draft completed in under six months
- **Draft Constitution:** Published **February 1948** (21 Feb 1948) with **315 Articles and 13 Schedules**

## Articles and provisions → Ambedkar's view | from: S1
| **Article / provision** | **Ambedkar's view** |
|---|---|
| **Article 32** (Right to Constitutional Remedies) | The "**heart and soul**" of the Constitution |
| **Art. 17** | Abolition of untouchability (punishable) |
| **Arts. 15-16** | Non-discrimination, equal opportunity |
| **Art. 46** | SC/ST welfare |
| **Arts. 330 and 332** | Reserved seats |
| **DPSP** | Equally important for social and economic justice; a "novel feature" like instruments of instructions |
| **Emergency provisions (Art. 356)** | Hoped they would remain a "**dead letter**" |
| **UCC** | Advocated a Uniform Civil Code but suggested it could be **voluntary** - for those who declare themselves willing (18 Sept 2026) |

- **Fundamental Rights & safeguards:** Universal adult suffrage including women from the start
- **Federalism:**
  - A "**Union of States**" - Union indestructible; states have no right to secede
  - Can work as unitary or federal as times require
  - Favoured a **strong Centre** to protect weaker sections
- ⚠ "Heart and soul" = **Art. 32**; "dead letter" = **Art. 356**

## Ambedkar → statements and warnings in the Assembly | from: S1, S2
| **Statement** | **Context** |
|---|---|
| Did **not need to apologise** for borrowing from the **Government of India Act 1935**; borrowing tested provisions no shame | Charge of copying the 1935 Act (UPPSC 2015; speech of **4 Nov 1948**) |
| **Constitutional morality** "is not a natural sentiment; it has to be cultivated" | Assembly, **4 Nov 1948**; borrowed **George Grote's** term (1 Mar 2026) |
| "the **grammar of anarchy**" | Bloody methods of revolution and civil disobedience |
| "Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it social and economic democracy." | Final speech |

# Final speech - 25 November 1949
- **Date:** **25 November 1949**
- **Three warnings:**
  - Give up bloody methods of revolution and civil disobedience = "the **grammar of anarchy**"
  - Do not lay liberties at the feet of a great man - **bhakti or hero-worship** in politics → dictatorship
  - Turn **political democracy into social democracy** - on 26 Jan 1950 India enters "a life of contradictions" (equality in politics vs inequality in social and economic life)
- ⚠ **4 Nov 1948** = 1935 Act reply + constitutional morality; **25 November 1949** = three warnings

## Universal angles | from: S3 | universal
- **Hindi Diwas (14 Sept):** Recalls the **Munshi-Ayyangar formula** adopted 14 Sept 1949, named after two Drafting Committee members (15 Sept 2026)
- **Criticism & debates:** "Lawyers' paradise" and "copy of the 1935 Act" charges; Ambedkar's reply defended adaptation
- **Significance:** Embedded a "social revolution" - rights, reservation, abolition of untouchability - in the legal framework

=== ROW 252 | Ambedkar's social thought - annihilation of caste, state socialism, conversion to Buddhism

## Ambedkar → statements on caste, village and religion | from: S0, S1, S2, S4
| **Statement** | **Speaker / context** |
|---|---|
| "Nothing can emancipate the outcaste except the **destruction of the caste system**" | **B.R. Ambedkar** (APPSC-G1 2018) |

## Books → theme | from: S0, S1, S2, S3
# 1. Annihilation of Caste (1936) - undelivered speech
- **Origin (1936):** Presidential address for the **Jat-Pat Todak Mandal** of Lahore (Hindu reformist body)
  - Organisers cancelled the session over its contents → Ambedkar **published it himself**
  - Gandhi replied in Harijan; Ambedkar rejoined
- **Critique:**
  - Caste has religious sanction in the **Shastras/Vedas** → reform within Hinduism futile
  - Whole caste system, not just untouchability, must go
  - Direct rebuke of Gandhi's approach
- **Remedy:** **inter-caste marriage**
- **Telugu translation:** **Kula Nirmoolana** - beacon of Andhra Dalit literature
# 2. Other writings

| **Book** | **Year** | **Theme** |
|---|---|---|
| **"What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables"** | 1945 | Criticised Congress's refusal to treat caste as a political issue |
| **Who Were the Shudras?** | 1946; APPSC-GS 2019 | Origin of the Shudras |
| **States and Minorities** | 1947 | Memorandum to the Constituent Assembly on behalf of the **Scheduled Castes Federation** |
| **The Untouchables** | 1948 | Origin of untouchability - "broken men" theory |
| **Thoughts on Linguistic States** | 1955 | Smaller states |
| **The Buddha and His Dhamma** | posthumous, 1957 | Rational, social reading of Buddhism = basis of Navayana |

- **Who Were the Shudras?:** Originally a **Kshatriya** community degraded after conflict with Brahmins, who denied them the **upanayana**
- **Thoughts on Linguistic States:**
  - Warned against huge states
  - Split **UP, Bihar and Madhya Pradesh**; divide Maharashtra into smaller units
  - **Hyderabad** as a second capital of India
- ⚠ Shudras = degraded **Kshatriyas**; Untouchables = "broken men"

## Ambedkar vs Gandhi and Congress — caste, electorates, village | from: S1
# 1. Debate on untouchability and caste

| **Parameter** | **Gandhi** | **Ambedkar** |
|---|---|---|
| Caste | Varna an ideal division of labour; untouchability a corruption | Whole caste system oppressive; annihilate it |
| Term | "**Harijan**" (children of God) | Rejected "Harijan"; **Dalit**/Scheduled Castes |
| Method | Moral persuasion, change of heart, temple entry | Legal, constitutional, political rights; state action |
| Electorate - **Poona Pact (1932)** | Opposed separate electorates; fasted against them (1932) | Demanded them; accepted joint electorates with more reserved seats to save Gandhi's life - settled for **147** reserved seats [GK] |
| Religion | Reform Hinduism from within | Left Hinduism for Buddhism (1956) |
| Governance | Decentralised Gram Swaraj | Strong Centre |

- **Common ground:** Both sought uplift of the marginalised and stressed education
# 2. Separate electorates
- **Ambedkar's view:** **Political power** necessary for the depressed classes to protect themselves (APPSC GS 2016)
- **Later view:** Felt the Pact made Dalits dependent on caste Hindus
# 3. Gandhism and Congress
- **Gandhism:** Rejected **Gram Swaraj** and trusteeship; advocated a **strong, centralised constitutional democracy** to protect minorities
- **Congress:**
  - Criticised its refusal to treat caste as a political issue
  - SCF constitution declared its members distinct from Hindus

  - **State ownership of key industries** + of **agricultural land**

  - **Nationalised insurance**
  - State socialism written into the Constitution so a legislative majority could not undo it

## Universal angles | from: S5 | universal
- **Current relevance - SC ruling (24 March 2026):** Justices P.K. Mishra and Manmohan

- **Nature:** Partly elected, partly nominated
- **Non-members:** Nearly every leader a member except **Gandhi and Jinnah**
- **Women:** **15 women** members (e.g. Durgabai Deshmukh, Sarojini Naidu, Hansa Mehta)
