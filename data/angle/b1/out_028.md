=== ROW 135 | Mughal economy - agriculture, karkhanas, trade, silver influx, Indian Ocean commerce

## Agriculture — crops, marketed share and irrigation | from: S0
# 1. Marketed share
- **Share marketed:** About **15%** of rural agricultural produce (APPSC G2 2017 key)
  - Cash revenue demand pushed peasants to sell
> Exam angle: "approximately what % was marketed?" - 15%.
# 2. Crops → region
| **Crop group** | **Crops** |
|---|---|
| **Commercial crops (jins-i-kamil, "perfect crops")** | **cotton, sugarcane, indigo**, oilseeds, opium |
| Cash crops and staples | rice, wheat, sugar(cane), cotton, indigo, pepper and spices |
| **New World crops** | Via the Portuguese = **tobacco**, **maize** (also chillies) |
- **Regional exports:** Gujarat = **indigo** + food grains; Bengal = **sugar** + rice
- Tobacco, cocoa and rubber domesticated in the New World (UPSC 2019 PYQ)
# 3. Irrigation and condition
- **Shahab Nahr:** Built by restoring **Firuz Shah's Rajabwah** canal (UPPSC 2020 key)
- **18th century:** Agriculture stagnant, technologically backward
  - Peasants paid the state, zamindars, jagirdars and revenue-farmers

## Land revenue terms → meaning — Zabt / Bandobast of Raja Todarmal | from: S1
- **Author:** Akbar's revenue minister **Raja Todarmal**
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Ilahi Gaz** | Measurement: bamboo rod with iron rings |
| **Polaj** | Cultivated annually |
| **Parauti** | Fallow one year |
| **Chachar** | Fallow 3-4 years |
| **Banjar** | Uncultivated |
| **Patta** | Title deed |
| **Qabuliyat** | Deed of agreement |
- **Four land classes:** Polaj, Parauti, Chachar, Banjar
- **State share:** **one-third** of average produce of the **past ten years**
- **Payment:** Mainly in **cash** → cash crops, peasants tied to the market
- ⚠ **Patta** = title deed; **Qabuliyat** = deed of agreement

## Agrarian classes and trading communities → role | from: S2, S4
# 1. Agrarian and social classes
| **Class** | **Features** |
|---|---|
| Nobility (**Umara**) | High mansabdars; mix of **Turanis, Iranis, Hindustanis and Rajputs**; wealthy but dependent on the Emperor's favour |
| Zamindars | Hereditary rural superior-right holders; took a large share with jagirdars and revenue-farmers |
| Peasants (**Raiyats**) | **Khud-kasht**: cultivated land in their **own** village; **Pahi-kasht**: cultivated land in **another** village |
- **18th-century society:** Extreme poverty alongside extreme wealth
# 2. Trader classes
| **Class / community** | **Function / role** |
|---|---|
| **Banjaras** | Carried **bulk goods over long distances** (grain, salt) on **oxen**, in bullock caravans - PYQ answer |
| **Seths and Bohras** | Specialised in **long-distance** trade |
| **Baniks** | **Local** (retail) traders |
| **Beoparis** | Traders/dealers in commodities, often buying produce from villages for wholesale markets [GK] |
| **Mahajans** | Moneylenders-cum-merchants; merchant-bankers [GK] |
| **Sarrafs (shroffs)** | Money-changers and bankers (see credit instruments) |
> Exam angle: "traders who carried bulk goods over long distances" = **Banjaras** (APPSC G2 2017).
- ⚠ **Seths and Bohras** = long-distance; **Baniks** = local
# 3. Trading communities → region
| **Community** | **Region / note** |
|---|---|
| **Marwaris** | Oswals, Maheshwaris and Agarwals of **Rajasthan** |
| **Chettis** | Merchants of the **Coromandel coast** (South India) |
| Muslim merchants | Dominated **Malabar** trade |
| Gujarati merchants | Hindu, Jain and Muslim - communities cut across religion |
- **Nature:** Numerous, organised and highly professional
- **Community head:** **nagarseth**, dealt with officials
- **Merchant strikes:** Recorded (e.g. **Ahmedabad**)
- **Earlier (Sultanate) pattern:**
  - Overseas trade = **Multanis and Khurasanis**
  - Inland trade = Gujarati, Marwari and Muslim Bohra merchants

## Karkhanas — royal workshops and artisans | from: S3
- **Supervision:** **Mir Saman (Khan-i-Saman)**
- **Products for court:** Costly **gold and silver articles**, fine textiles, carpets, arms, jewellery, luxury goods
- **Origin:** Tradition going back to Sultanate karkhanas
- **Artisans:** Often in guilds; worked in state **karkhanas** or private enterprises
  - Artisans and merchants = middle and lower urban strata

## Credit and coin terms → meaning | from: S5, S6
# 1. Credit instruments
| **Instrument** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Hundi** | **Letter of credit** issued by **Sarrafs/Shroffs** to a cash depositor: cash deposited at one place, collected (encashed) later or at another place after a journey (APPSC-GS 2018 key) |
| **Bima** | **Insurance** of goods in transit, underwritten by sarrafs for a premium [GK] |
- **Hundi:** Allowed secure transactions over long distances
- **Shroffs / sarrafs:** Ran the credit network; money-changers and bankers - tested coins, took deposits (deposit keepers), lenders, issued **hundis**
- **EIC link:** Relied on Indian **brokers (dalals)**, **shroffs** and **banians**; fitted into this system
> Exam angle: distractors are Farman (royal order), Parcha, Rokad (cash).
# 2. Coins — metal and name
| **Coin / term** | **Metal / meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Mohur** | **Gold** |
| **Rupiya** | **Silver** (standardised by Sher Shah, **178 grains**; continued by Akbar) |
| **Dam** | **Copper**; **40 dams = 1 rupee** |
| **Chalni (chalani)** | Coins of the **current** reign in circulation |
| **Khazana** | Coins of a **previous reign**, accepted at a discount (JPSC 2023 key) |
| **Sikka** | Right of coinage in the ruler's name - a mark of sovereignty with **khutba** |
- ⚠ **Mohur – Rupiya – Dam** = **Gold – Silver – Copper**
- ⚠ **Chalni** = current reign; **Khazana** = previous reign
- **Golconda:** Mughal silver coins circulated; Golconda minted only copper (Thevenot)

- ⚠ **Masulipatnam:** **Dutch 1605** → **English 1611** → French 1669

## Silver influx and Indian Ocean commerce — causes and effects | from: S8
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| European companies paid in **bullion (gold and silver)** from Europe | Export surplus drew a **large inflow of silver/bullion** (Bernier) |
| **New World (Spanish American)** silver, via Europe and the Pacific | Reached India; **Careri** (c.1690) described (traced) silver's global route, crossing the globe to India |
| Silver inflow | More silver **rupee** coinage + rise in prices |
- India = "**sink of precious metals**"; 17th century = "the **largest producer of industrial goods** in the world"
- **Merchant groups:**
  - **Asian** = Gujaratis, Bohras, Chettis, Malabar Muslims, Armenians
  - **European** = Portuguese, Dutch, English EIC (**1600**), French
- **Trade balance:** Favoured India until **mid-18th century**
  - Decline: Aurangzeb's Deccan wars, insecurity, loss of Indian shipping

## Universal angles | from: S11 | universal

  - Cash revenue under zabt monetised the rural economy, linked peasants to markets
- **Current relevance:** India **more than doubled import duties on gold and silver from 6% to 15%** in May 2026; bullion imports stayed robust (17 Jul 2026) - India's pull for precious metals persists

=== ROW 136 | Mughal society - nobility, peasantry, position of women, urban classes

## Nobility — groups → reign and changing shares | from: S0
- **Umara:** Top mansabdars; wealthy but wholly dependent on the emperor's favour
- **Cosmopolitan body:** **Turanis** (Central Asians), **Iranis** (Persians), **Afghans**, **Hindustanis** (Indian Muslims), **Rajputs**, later **Deccanis**
| **Reign** | **Shift in nobility** |
|---|---|
| Babur-Humayun | Turanis and Iranis dominated |
| **Akbar** | Added **Rajputs** and Hindustanis to balance them |
| **Aurangzeb** | Deccanis and **Marathas** swelled the nobility after Deccan wars; Hindu share about **one-third** (M. Athar Ali) |
- ⚠ **Turanis** = Central Asians; **Iranis** = Persians; **Hindustanis** = Indian Muslims

## Terms → meaning: peasants, court customs and institutions | from: S1, S2, S4
# 1. Peasantry — categories
| **Category** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Khud-kasht** | Resident peasant cultivating **own land in own village** |
| **Pahi-kasht** | Cultivator of land in **another village** (non-resident) |
| **Muzarian** | **Tenants** cultivating land held by others [GK] |
| **Raiyats** | Peasants = backbone of the economy; bore the land revenue |
- ⚠ **Khud** = own village; **Pahi** = another village
# 2. Festivals and court customs — origin
| **Custom** | **Origin / note** |
|---|---|
| **Nauroz** | **Persian New Year**, adopted from the **Zoroastrians** (UPPSC 2001 key); stopped by Aurangzeb [GK] |
| **Jharokha darshan** | Emperor showed himself daily at a balcony; begun by **Akbar**, ended by **Aurangzeb**[GK] |
| **Tuladan** | Weighing the emperor against gold and goods on his birthday, given to the poor - a **Hindu** custom adopted by Akbar [GK] |
| **Sijda / paibos** | Prostration and kissing the feet, introduced by Akbar [GK] |
# 3. Institution
- **Firangi Mahal (Lucknow)** = madrasa of **Muslim jurisprudence** (UPPSC 2016 key)

## Urban classes, slavery and women's position → features | from: S2, S3
# 1. Urban classes
- **Merchants and artisans:** Nagarseths, Marwaris, Bohras, Chettis + **artisans** (often in guilds, state **karkhanas** or private) = middle and lower urban strata
- **Professional groups:** Physicians (hakims), teachers, lawyers, painters, musicians
# 2. Slavery and domestic servants
- **Noble households:** Still full of slaves and large domestic staffs (despite Akbar's 1562 ban on enslaving prisoners of war)
# 3. Position of women
- **Purdah:** Muslim women strictly veiled, carried in covered palanquins; Hindu women moved freely (John Fryer)
- **Social evils:** Sati, child marriage, polygamy (Bernier, Tavernier)
- **Property:**
  - Muslim women = **inheritance shares** + **mehr** under sharia
  - Hindu women controlled **stridhana**
- ⚠ **Mehr** = Muslim women; **stridhana** = Hindu women

## Universal angles | from: S5 | universal
- **Significance:** Travellers' accounts = main source for Mughal social history; read against Persian chronicles
- **Criticism & debates:** Bernier's "no private property" and "bleak India" view reflects European bias; still conceded a prosperous merchant class
- **AP angle (Qutb Shahi Golconda):**
  - Shared Mughal customs: **Nauroz**, Muharram (**Peerla Panduga**)
  - 14 national festivals under Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah
  - Tavernier recorded **20,000** registered prostitutes in Hyderabad under Abdullah Qutb Shah

## Tomb tradition — evolution and FIRSTs | from: S1
# 1. Humayun's tomb and Humayun-nama
- **Humayun-nama:** Written by **Gulbadan Begum**, Humayun's sister
- **Humayun's tomb, Delhi:**
  - Builder: widow **Haji Begum (Bega Begum)** = standard answer; some notes say **Hamida Banu Begum** (Akbar's mother)
  - First **garden tomb** in the subcontinent
  - Introduced the **double dome**
  - Red sandstone with white marble inlay
# 2. Evolution
| **Stage** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| Sayyid-Lodi tombs | Hindu elements - Hindu-style gates and facades, plaster ornament, drip-stones |
| Sher Shah's tomb | Sur transition |
| **Itimad-ud-Daula** ("Baby Taj") | First Mughal tomb in white marble with pietra dura; pietra dura began in Jahangir's reign |
| Taj Mahal | Climax |
| **Bibi ka Maqbara** ("Taj of the Deccan") | Late imitation |
- ⚠ "Baby Taj" = Itimad-ud-Daula; "Taj of the Deccan" = Bibi ka Maqbara

## Architectural terms → meaning and where first seen | from: S5
| **Feature** | **Meaning** | **Key example / where first or best seen** |
|---|---|---|
| Double dome | Inner and outer shells | Introduced at **Humayun's tomb** |
| **Pietra dura** | Inlay of semi-precious stones | Began under **Jahangir**; first on a large scale in **Itimad-ud-Daula**; peak at the **Taj Mahal** |
| Charbagh | Four-quartered garden | Introduced by **Babur** (Ram Bagh, Agra) |

- **Other hallmarks:**
  - Calligraphy, arabesque, **jali (lattice)** work (Humayun's tomb)
  - Mirror work in the **Sheesh Mahal**
  - Red sandstone for structure + marble for decoration (Red Fort)

- **Material:** Mainly **red sandstone**

## Taj Mahal — architect, marble source, historians' views, court case | from: S3
# 1. Basic facts
- **Builder & place:** **Shah Jahan** at **Agra**, on the right bank of the **Yamuna**
- **For:** Mausoleum of **Mumtaz Mahal (Arjumand Banu Begum)**, Shah Jahan's wife
- **Built:** **1632-1648** (older notes give 1653, year the whole complex was finished)
- **Chief architect:** **Ustad Ahmad Lahori**
- **Marble source:** **Makrana** (Rajasthan) (APPSC GS 2016)
- **Status:** UNESCO World Heritage Site
# 2. Style and design
- **Features:** Pinnacle of **pietra dura** and symmetry; white marble
- **Layout:** Tomb at the river end of its charbagh, not at the garden's centre
- **Design ancestry:** Humayun's tomb (garden tomb, double dome, red sandstone with marble) inspired it
# 3. Historians → views on style origin
| **Historian** | **View** |
|---|---|
| **V.A. Smith** | Combination of **European and Asiatic genius** (APPSC-G1 2022 key) |
| James Fergusson, Percy Brown | Product of the Indo-Persian tradition |
- Other options: James Fergusson, Percy Brown, R.C. Majumdar
# 4. Pollution threat and case
- **Marble cancer:** Yellowing from sulphur dioxide (Mathura refinery, industries, traffic)
- **Supreme Court:** **Taj Trapezium Zone (TTZ)** rulings in **M.C. Mehta** case (1996)

=== ROW 138 | Mughal painting, music, language and literature; Persian-Sanskrit translation movement

## Mughal painting — emperor → emphasis | from: S0
- **Humayun:** Brought Persian masters **Mir Sayyid Ali** and **Abdus Samad** → founded the Mughal school (APPSC-G2 2025, statement a)
- **Evolution:** Persian miniature → realistic, colourful Indian style
| **Emperor** | **Emphasis** |
|---|---|
| Humayun | Foundation: the two Persian masters |
| **Akbar** | **Illustrated manuscripts**. A **Tasvir Khana** atelier (karkhana) of **100+ painters** (**Daswant, Basawan, Mukand**) produced the **Hamzanama**, Tutinama, Akbarnama and Razmnama. Features: 3-D figures and realism |
| **Jahangir** | **Zenith / golden age** ("golden period" of Mughal painting): **portraits**, albums, flora and fauna, peak of **naturalism** and precision; European influence; introduced the **halo (nimbus)** behind the emperor's head |
| Shah Jahan | Formal court scenes, **albums (muraqqas)**, floral borders, heavy use of gold |
| **Aurangzeb** | **Withdrew patronage**, so painters moved to Rajasthan, the Punjab Hills and Bihar, fuelling **Rajput and Pahari** schools |
- **Hamzanama:** Tales of Amir Hamza; huge illustrated project of Akbar's atelier
  - Supervised first by Mir Sayyid Ali, then Abdus Samad
> Exam angle: statement sets. "Jahangir's reign = golden period" is correct; statement pairs on Humayun's Persian masters and Jahangir's golden age (APPSC G2 2025).

## Regional painting schools → region and features | from: S1
| **School** | **Region / features** |
|---|---|
| Rajput | Rajput kingdoms of Rajasthan. Hindu epics, love legends, daily life; **bold colours**; flat, 2-D |
| Pahari | Sub-Himalayan belt (Himachal, Jammu, Almora, Garhwal), **17th-19th c.** |
| Deccani | Began in the **Bahmani** court and continued in its successor states. At Golconda the founder was **Mir Hashim**, later invited to the Mughal court. Heavy use of **gold** |

  - Some notes place them in the Tarikh-i-Hussain Badshahi Deccan; others credit a Quli Qutb Shah manuscript

## Languages — Persian, Urdu/Rekhta and Deccani | from: S3
# 1. Persian
- **Court and administration:** **Persian** replaced Sanskrit and Arabic as language of administration, diplomacy, high culture and elite lingua franca
  - Linked Delhi with Isfahan and Central Asia
- **Babur:** Wrote in **Turki**
# 2. Growth of Urdu and regional languages
- **Urdu** (Hindavi → Rekhta, Lashkari, Urdu):
  - Grew out of military camps and bazaars
  - Mix = Persian, Arabic, Turkic, local Prakrits, Braj (also summed up as Persian + Hindi + regional dialects)
  - **Perso-Arabic script** + indigenous grammar
- **Deccani / Dakhni Urdu:** Lingua franca of Deccan camps and bazaars; literary medium patronised by the Qutb Shahis
- **Sufi saints:** Wrote in local vernaculars to reach common people

## Causes of Mughal decline — factor → effect | from: S8
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Aurangzeb's Deccan wars** | Last ~**27 years** camped in the Deccan vs the Marathas → drained treasury |
| Religious policy | Jizya 1679 alienated Rajputs, Marathas and Sikhs |
| Jagirdari crisis | Too many mansabdars for too few revenue-yielding jagirs (be-jagiri) → weakened mansabdari system |
| Sayyid brothers (kingmakers) | **Abdullah Khan** + **Husain Ali Khan** controlled Jahandar Shah and **Farrukhsiyar** (deposed 1719), then puppet successors |
- **Deccan:** Sambhaji executed **1689**; Rajaram at Jinji; Tarabai
- **Sayyid brothers:** Overthrown with **Nizam-ul-Mulk's** help
- **Other causes:** Weak successors, noble factions (Turani, Irani, Hindustani), regional powers, **Marathas**, foreign invasions (Nadir Shah, Abdali)
