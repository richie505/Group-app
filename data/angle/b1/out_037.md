## Railways — guarantee system, motives and effects | from: S0
- **Guarantee system:** Private British companies got a guaranteed **5%** return (interest) on capital, paid from Indian revenues whether or not lines made a profit

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Motive** | Mainly **colonial exploitation** - troop movement, raw materials to ports, British manufactures inland |
| **Drain** | Nationalists counted the guaranteed interest in the **"drain"** of wealth |
| **Famine** | Railways also moved grain for famine relief |
| **Unintended effects (railways, post and telegraph)** | National market (integrated the market); shared public travel across castes; faster nationalist (pan-Indian) mobilisation |

## Vernacular Press Act, 1878 — Lytton's "Gagging Act": provisions, targets, repeal | from: S5
- **Passed by:** **Lord Lytton**, amid public anger over the **1876-77 famine** + the lavish **Delhi Durbar (1877)**
- **Nickname:** **"Gagging Act"** [GK]
- **Provisions:**
  - **District magistrates** could make printers/publishers of vernacular papers sign **bonds** not to publish seditious matter
  - Demand **security deposits** (forfeitable)
  - **Seize presses** for repeat offences
  - **No appeal** to a court of law
  - Exemption only by submitting proofs to a government censor before publication
- **Discriminatory:** Applied to Indian-language papers, not English ones
- **Targets:** **Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash, Samachar**
- **Evasion:** **Amrita Bazar Patrika** became an **English** paper overnight (1878) to escape the VPA [GK]
- **Repeal:** **1882 by Lord Ripon**; pre-censorship clause dropped + press commissioner appointed

## Macaulay's Minute, 1835 — recommendation, remarks, downward filtration | from: S6
- **Recommendation:** Education funds exclusively to **English-medium** teaching of Western science and literature
  - British higher education prioritised over mass primary schooling
- **Famous remark:** "a single shelf of a good European library was worth the whole native literature of India and Arabia" (APPSC-G2 2025)
- **Aim:** A class "Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste, in opinions, in morals, and in intellect" - interpreters between rulers and ruled
- **Downward Filtration Theory:** Educate a few upper-class Indians → knowledge filters down to masses
  - Related to **education** (APPSC-G2 2024)
  - **Wood's Despatch** later asked the Company to take up mass instruction - rejecting downward filtration
- **Madras impact:** 1835 Resolution banned regional-language instruction in Madras district schools
  - Munro's collectorate and tahsildari schools (2 in Kadapa, 1 Nellore, 2 Vizag by 1830) closed in 1836

## Ilbert Bill controversy, 1883 — aim, "White Mutiny", compromise | from: S5
- **Introduced (1883):** Under Viceroy **Lord Ripon**, drafted by Law Member **C.P. Ilbert**
- **Aim:** Let **Indian magistrates** (**district magistrates and sessions judges**) **try European British subjects** - Europeans till then tried only by European judges
- **Opposition:** Europeans protested violently - fierce agitation (the **"White Mutiny"**)
- **Compromise:** Ripon **watered the Bill down** (diluted) - a European accused tried by an Indian judge could claim a **jury of which at least half were Europeans** (1884)
- **Significance:** Showed racial discrimination persisted; exposed British racism + showed Indians the value of organised agitation
- **Indian Association:** **Backed** the Bill against the European agitation
  - ⚠ **Trap:** some notes wrongly say it "opposed" the Bill

=== ROW 181 | Revolt of 1857 - political causes (annexations, Doctrine of Lapse, subsidiary alliance)

## Subsidiary alliance, 1798 — terms, states → year, Hyderabad first | from: S0
- **Devised by:** **Lord Wellesley, 1798**
- **Terms:**
  - Ruler to **disband his own army**
  - Keep a **British subsidiary force** at his cost
  - Accept a **British Resident**
  - Give up independent **foreign policy** (no other Europeans in service)
  - Unpaid subsidy → **ceding territory**
- **Result:** Princes lost sovereignty + foreign-policy independence

  - 1800 treaty ceded the Rayalaseema districts to pay for the force = **"Ceded Districts"**

## Doctrine of Lapse — Dalhousie, meaning and withdrawal | from: S1
- **Meaning:** Policy of **Lord Dalhousie (1848-56)**, from **1848**
  - Ruler of a dependent state dying **without a natural male heir** → adopted son could not inherit the throne → state "lapsed" to the Company
  - Adoption allowed only for private property
  - APPSC-GS 2018 also called it "the policy of annexation devised by the EIC"
- **Withdrawal:** **Queen Victoria's Proclamation** (read by **Canning** at the **Allahabad Durbar, 1 November 1858**)
  - Abolished it + **recognised adoption**; annexation abandoned
  - Princes became "breakwaters to the storm" (Lord Canning)
- ⚠ **Lapse** = no natural male heir (Dalhousie, **1848**); **Awadh** = "misgovernment", not lapse (**1856**)

- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - First subsidiary ally = Hyderabad (1798)

=== ROW 182 | Revolt of 1857 - economic, socio-religious, administrative and military causes

## Political and administrative causes — factor → effect | from: S0
| **Political cause** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Doctrine of Lapse** | Took Satara, Jhansi and Nagpur; Rani Lakshmibai's adopted son refused the throne; Nana Saheb denied his pension |
| **Awadh annexed (1856)** | On grounds of "misgovernance"; **Wajid Ali Shah** deposed → lords, officials, retainers, soldiers lost livelihoods |
| **The Mughal emperor** | Bahadur Shah Zafar told successors would lose the **Red Fort**; **Canning** announced loss of **royal title** too |
| **Other** | Pindaris and irregular soldiers disbanded |

| **Administrative cause** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Exclusion** | Indians **excluded from high civil and military posts** / **shut out of high administrative posts** |
| **Corruption** | Rampant in **lower courts and police**; justice slow and costly, favouring the rich; administration inefficient and prejudiced |
| **Unemployment** | Annexations threw thousands of officials, retainers and soldiers of princely courts out of work |

## Economic causes — peasants, landed classes, artisans | from: S1
| **Class** | **Grievance → result** |
|---|---|
| **Peasants** | **Heavy land revenue / High land revenue** collected rigidly under **Permanent/Zamindari**, **Ryotwari** and **Mahalwari** settlements → debt → land lost to **moneylenders** and traders |
| **Peasants** | **Commercialisation:** forced cash crops under high taxes |
| **Taluqdars of Awadh** | About **21,000** in Awadh dispossessed / lost estates under the **Summary Settlement of 1856** |
| **Estate owners** | **Inam Commission (1852, Bombay):** confiscated some **20,000** estates whose owners could not show title |
| **Artisans and handicrafts** | **Artisans were ruined** by cheap British machine-made goods after the Industrial Revolution, esp. textiles |

- ⚠ **Trap:** Taluqdars were **not** allowed to keep their arms and forts (UPSC 2026 key)
- ⚠ **21,000** = taluqdars of Awadh (1856); **20,000** = estates confiscated by the Inam Commission (1852)
- **Outcome:** **Taluqdars and zamindars lost their estates** → fiercely anti-British landed class
- **Artisans:** British goods entered **duty-free** vs heavy tariffs on Indian goods → **deindustrialisation**
  - Annexations ended princely/court patronage
- **Absentee sovereignty:** Company rule seen as **absentee sovereignty** + **drain of wealth** to Britain

## Socio-religious causes — social laws → year, missionaries | from: S2
| **Measure** | **Year** | **Why resented** |
|---|---|---|
| Abolition of **Sati** (Bentinck) | **1829** | Interference in custom |
| **Lex Loci Act** / Religious (Caste) Disabilities Act / **Caste Disabilities Removal Act** | **1850** | Converts to Christianity could **inherit ancestral property** - seen as a state incentive to convert |
| **Hindu Widow Remarriage Act** | **1856** | Challenged orthodox norms |

- **Laws against child marriage and female infanticide:** Also seen as interference
- ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes date the **Religious Disabilities Act** to 1856; the Act is of **1850**
- **Christian missionaries:** Preached under police protection; attacked Hindu and Islamic practice
- **Modern fears:** Western education, railways and telegraph seemed to threaten caste and religion → fears of mass conversion
- **Other grievances:**
  - **Taxation of temple and mosque lands**
  - **Racial arrogance:** of officials; social discrimination against Indians

## Military grievances — GSEA, bhatta, discrimination, troop ratio | from: S3
- **"Peasants in uniform":** Sepoys' grievances mirrored the village's; carried rural grievances into the army

| **Grievance** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| **General Service Enlistment Act 1856 (Canning)** | New recruits to serve **anywhere, including overseas** → crossing the sea = loss of caste for orthodox Hindus |
| **Bhatta** | Foreign service allowance withdrawn for service in **Sindh and Punjab**, now British territory |
| **Pay and promotion** | Lower pay than British soldiers; racial contempt from officers; Indian could rise **no higher than Subedar** |
| **Postage** | Free postage for sepoys ended by the Post Office Act 1854 |
| **Troop ratio** | About **2.3 lakh sepoys to 45,000 Europeans** (roughly **5:1**); Europeans drawn off for the Crimean and Persian wars |
| **Bengal Army composition** | **Awadh** (annexed 1856) = **"nursery of the Bengal Army"** → annexation angered mostly high-caste sepoys from that region |

- APPSC-GS 2023: Subedar ceiling + greased cartridges both correct
- **Not a cause:**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Officers' **inefficiency** was NOT a cause (BPSC 2018 key)
  - Missionary pressure, the order to travel by ship and the stopping of bhatta WERE causes

## Earlier sepoy mutinies → date and cause | from: S4
| **Mutiny** | **Date** | **Cause** |
|---|---|---|
| **Vellore mutiny** | **July 1806** | New dress code: turban with leather cockade, ban on caste marks and earrings; Tipu's sons lodged in the fort [GK] |
| **Barrackpore mutiny** (47th NI) | **November 1824** | Refusal to go to **Burma by sea** (First Anglo-Burmese War) [GK] |
| Bhatta mutinies | 1840s | e.g. 34th NI 1844, 66th NI 1849-50 |
| 38th NI | 1852 | Refused to go to Burma |
| Santhal revolt (civil, not a sepoy revolt) | 1855-56 | - |

- **UPPSC match key:**
  - Barrackpur = Nov 1824
  - Santhal revolt = 1855-56
  - Vellore = July 1806
- ⚠ **Barrackpore 1824** (47th NI, Burma) ≠ **Barrackpore 1857** (Mangal Pandey, 34th Bengal NI)

## Immediate cause — Enfield rifle, rumours and events of 1857 → date | from: S5
- **Enfield rifle:** New (Pattern **1853** Enfield) rifle introduced in the Army in **December 1856** (BPSC key)
  - Greased-paper cartridges believed to contain **cow and pig fat**; sepoys had to **bite** them open → offended both Hindus and Muslims
  - APPSC-GS 2019 key: immediate cause = introduction of the Enfield rifle
  - Read as final proof of a colonial-missionary plot to defile religion and force conversion
- **Rumours:** Bone dust mixed in Company flour; mysterious **chapatis** passed village to village across North India in early 1857

| **Event** | **Date** |
|---|---|
| **Berhampur**: 19th Native Infantry (19th NI) refused the new cartridges/rifle | **February 1857** |
| **Barrackpore**: **Mangal Pandey** (34th Bengal NI) attacked **Lt Baugh** and **Sergeant-Major Hewson** | **29 March 1857** |
| Mangal Pandey hanged/executed; regiment disbanded | **8 April 1857** |
| **Meerut**: 85 sepoys of the 3rd Cavalry (3rd Native Cavalry) court-martialled, sentenced to 10 years | **9 May** 1857 |
| **Meerut rising**: officers shot, comrades freed, "Delhi Chalo" | **10 May 1857** |
| Rebels reach Delhi; **Bahadur Shah II** proclaimed Shahenshah-e-Hindustan | **11-12 May 1857** |

- **Barrackpore date:** attack on **29 March**; some notes date it to April 1857 (month of the execution)

## Universal angles | from: S7 | universal
- **Current relevance:** On **21 July 2026** the PM paid tribute to **Mangal Pandey** (born **19 July 1827**) on his birth anniversary
  - Recalled the **29 Mar 1857** Barrackpore attack on Lt Baugh and Sergeant-Major Hewson, the 1853 Enfield rifle, and his hanging
- **Significance:**
  - 1857 brought together grievances of rulers, peasants, soldiers and common people
  - After 1858 the Crown promised **religious neutrality** (Queen's Proclamation) + withdrew state patronage from missionary work
- **Criticism & debates:** "sepoy mutiny" (Roberts) vs "first war of independence" (Savarkar); revolt combined peasant, princely and sepoy grievances
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - First Indian-language work on the causes = Syed Ahmed Khan
  - First martyr of 1857 = Mangal Pandey
