=== ROW 21 | Vedic literature - four Vedas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads, Vedangas, Sutras

## Four Vedas — content, priests, Upavedas and FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
| **Veda** | **Content** | **Priest** | **Upaveda** [GK] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rig** | Hymns of praise; oldest | **Hotri** (reciter) | Ayurveda = medicine |
| **Sama** | Rig Vedic hymns set to **melody** (saman); mostly from Mandalas VIII-IX; root of Indian music | **Udgatri** (chanter) | Gandharvaveda = music |
| **Yajur** | Ritual formulas, prose manual; **Shukla (White)** and **Krishna (Black)** | **Adhvaryu** (executor) | Dhanurveda = warfare |
| **Atharva** | **Spells, charms, healing**, medicine, folk beliefs; latest Veda | Brahman (supervisor) | Shilpaveda = architecture (some link Ayurveda here) |

- ⚠ **Hotri – Udgatri – Adhvaryu – Brahman** = **Rig – Sama – Yajur – Atharva**

| **FIRST / LAST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| Oldest Veda | **Rigveda** - "First Testament of Mankind" |
| Latest Veda | **Atharvaveda** |
| **Trayi** | First three Vedas; the Atharvaveda was first excluded from Trayi |
| Term **kshetra-patni** ("mistress of the field") | Originates in the Atharvaveda (UPSC 2026 key) |

- **Shruti:** All Vedic texts = **Shruti** ("heard"); called apaurusheya
  - All four layers (Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka, Upanishad) = Shruti
# Veda → numbers and features
- **Samaveda:** Borrows almost all its verses from the Rig Veda
  - ~1,549 verses, all but 75 from the Rigveda (some notes give 1,603 and 1,063; some notes: all but **99**)
- **Atharvaveda:**
  - **731 hymns, 20 kandas**
  - Sabha-Samiti = daughters of Prajapati; mentions **Pashupati**

## Rigveda — mandala → content, family books, dating | from: S1
- **Size:** **10 mandalas, 1,028 hymns** (1,017 + 11 Valakhilya)
- **Parallel:** Similarities with the Iranian **Avesta**
- **Content:** Hymns = prayers to nature deities for cattle, progeny, victory

| **Mandala** | **Content** |
|---|---|
| **III** | **Gayatri Mantra** (Vishvamitra, to Savitri; 3rd mandala) [GK for mandala] |
| VII | **Battle of Ten Kings** (Dasharajna) |
| **IX** | **Soma mandala** - entirely **Soma** Pavamana; all hymns to Soma |
| **X** | **Purusha Sukta** (four varnas), **Nasadiya Sukta** (creation hymn), Ganga, Rajanya, Shudra |

- ⚠ **III – VII – IX – X** = **Gayatri – Ten Kings – Soma – Purusha/Nasadiya**
- **Family books II-VII** = oldest "**family books**"
  - Seers: Gritsamada (II), Vishvamitra, Vamadeva, Atri, Bharadvaja, Vasishtha
  - Some notes call II-VIII "Saptarishi mandalas"
- **Later additions:** Mandalas I and X (some notes add VIII-IX)
# Dating of the Rigveda
- **Early Vedic:** **c. 1500-1000 BCE** (some notes' comparison table: 1500-1100 BCE)
- **Other dating:**
  - Indigenists = astronomical dating for an earlier Rigveda
  - Michael Witzel = "Long Chronology" of textual layers
- **Sources - archaeological correlates:** Textual rivers, tribes and settlements broadly match archaeological evidence; text must be read critically

## Upanishads — number, teaching, saying → source | from: S4

- **Teaching:** Anti-ritual/anti-ritualistic; Atman = Brahman (atman-brahman identity); Karma, Samsara, Moksha
- **vs Puranas:** Upanishads = Shruti; Puranas = Smriti (later, legend-based)

## Rise of Magadha — causes, rivalries, capitals | from: S2, S3
# 1. Four powers and rivalries
- **Four powers:** **Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti** dominated
- **Rivalries:**
  - Pradyota rivalled Bimbisara and Udayana
  - Kosala annexed **Kashi** → Magadha later took it as dowry and by war
  - **Shishunaga ended Avanti**
- **Outcome:** Magadha emerged supreme (6th-4th c. BCE)
# 2. Reasons for the rise
- **Geography:** Fertile alluvium, ample rainfall, rice

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Iron ore** close by | Tools and weapons |
| **Elephants** from the east | War |
| **Rivers** (Ganga, Son) | Transport + defence |
| Towns + metallic money | Raised revenue; "unorthodox" mixed society |

- **Status:** By the 6th-4th centuries BCE = most powerful Mahajanapada (APPSC 2018)
# 3. Capitals
- **Rajagriha:** Ringed by **five hills** - "almost impregnable"
- **Pataliputra (later):** At the Ganga-Son confluence

## Jain philosophy — terms and doctrines → meaning | from: S2, S3
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Jina | Conqueror (of passions); root of "Jain" |
| Jiva / Ajiva | Soul (consciousness) / matter (non-consciousness) |
| Karma | Material bondage of the soul causing rebirth; clings to the soul |
| Kevala jnana / Kaivalya | Omniscience attained by the soul |
| Siddha | Fully liberated soul |
| **Anekantavada** | Manifold nature / many-sidedness of reality; truth has many (multiple) facets |
| **Syadvada** | Theory of **conditioned predication** / relativity of truth - every statement holds only "from a point of view" (syat = "may be"); seven-fold predication (saptabhangi) [GK] |
| **Nayavada** | Theory of **partial standpoints** |

- **Souls in natural objects:** All objects, animate or inanimate, possess a soul capable of feeling pain → extreme ahimsa
- **No creator god:** World runs by an eternal law of decay and development; Jainism is non-theistic + rejects Vedic authority
- **Karma = material bondage:** Liberation: stop inflow + shed karma (nirjara) through penance → **Kevala Jnana** (omniscience) → Siddha state
- **Tattva (reality):** **origination, decay and permanence** (utpada-vyaya-dhrauvya)
- **Four forms of existence:** **Deva, Manushya, Naraki (hell-beings), Tiryancha (animals/plants)**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Yaksha is not one**
- **Contrast with other schools:**
  - Buddhism = anatta, pratityasamutpada
  - Ajivikas = niyati (fate)
  - Charvaka = materialism
# Triratna (Ratnatraya) - three jewels
- **Jain Triratna (Ratnatraya):** Path to moksha
  - **Right Faith (Samyak Darshana)** (samyag-darshana)
  - **Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnana)** (samyag-jnana)
  - **Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra)** (samyag-charitra)
- **Buddhist Triratna:** **Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha** (refuges, not a path)
> Exam angle: questions test which set belongs to which religion.

## Panchamahavratas, Anuvratas and Jain practices — vows, rituals, Sallekhana | from: S4

- **5th vow:** **Brahmacharya (celibacy)** - the **fifth vow added by Mahavira**
- ⚠ 4 vows = **Parshvanatha**; 5th (**Brahmacharya**) = **Mahavira**
- **Householders:** Milder **Anuvratas** (non-injury, non-stealing, non-adultery, truth, non-possession) + daily charity of cooked food to the needy
- **Ahimsa:** Extends to all life forms → vegetarianism; strict followers avoid agriculture (hence trading base)
# 2. Practices and rituals → meaning

| **Practice** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Paryushana** | Last day = **Samvatsari** (forgiveness day) |
| **Pratikramana** | Introspection ritual |
| **Padmasana or Kayotsarga** | Idol posture of Tirthankara/Jina idols |
| **Sallekhana / Santhara** | Ritual **fast unto death** at end of life (life's end) to purify karma |

- **Sallekhana:** A **Jain** practice (not Buddhist/Sufi/Parsi)
  - Chandragupta Maurya performed it at **Shravanabelagola**
- **Other names:** **Nigranthas** ("free from fetters"), Shramanas

## Jainism and Buddhism compared | from: S5
| **Point** | **Jainism** | **Buddhism** |
|---|---|---|
| Soul | Eternal soul (jiva) in everything / all things | **Anatta** - no permanent soul (self) |
| Karma | Material bondage | Intention-based; shared belief in karma-rebirth |
| Penance | Severe (**extreme asceticism**), self-mortification | **Middle Way** (Middle Path) avoiding extremes |
| Ahimsa | Stricter, even in thought; strict diet | Important but less strict |
| Monks | Digambara monks unclothed | Saffron robes |
| Texts | Agamas (Ardhamagadhi) | Tripitaka (Pali) |
| Goal | Kevala jnana | Nirvana/Buddhahood |
| Spread | Limited to India | Spread abroad |

- **Shared:**
  - Rejection of **Vedic authority**, rituals, sacrifice and Brahmana supremacy; non-theism
  - Accept **karma and rebirth**
  - Renunciation; human effort for salvation
  - Monastic orders for men and women; challenge to caste hierarchy
  - Use of Prakrit/Pali; founders were contemporaries
