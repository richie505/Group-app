=== ROW 98 | Reddi kingdoms of Addanki, Kondavidu and Rajahmundry - Prolaya Vema Reddi, Kumaragiri Reddi

## Reddi dynasty — period, lineage, capitals and forts | from: S0
# 1. Dynasty – period
- **Period:** **c. 1325-1448 CE**
  - Kondaveedu line: 1325-1424
  - Rajamahendravaram line: 1402-1448
- **Reddi lineage:** **Panta Vamsam** (**Deseti kula**)
  - Panta Rashtra = Guduru and Muttembaka areas of Nellore
- **Royal emblem:** **Bull**; family deity: **Mula Guramma**
- **Royal / official language:** **Sanskrit was the official language**; many kings were scholar-poets
# 2. Capitals → place
- **Sequence:** **Addanki** → **Kondavidu** → **Rajahmundry**

| **Capital** | **Place** | **Feature** |
|---|---|---|
| **Addanki** | Prakasam | Prolaya Vema |
| **Kondavidu** (Kondaveedu) | Guntur region | Replaced **Addanki** as the Reddi capital; later a battleground between the Gajapatis and Vijayanagara |
| **Rajahmundry** (Rajamahendravaram) | - | second seat, 1402-1448 |

- ⚠ **Trap:** "near Eluru in West Godavari" for Kondavidu is wrong
- **Who moved Addanki → Kondavidu:**
  - **Anavota (Anapota) Reddi** - by the Manyapuram inscription
  - **Anavema Reddi** - APPSC G2 2012 key
  - ⚠ **Trap:** In the exam, pick Anavema if both appear or if Anavota is not among the options
# 3. Forts → builder
- **Prolaya Vema Reddi:** Built **74 forts**, including **Kondaveedu, Kondapalli and Dharanikota**
- **Kondapalli:** Strategic hilltop fort (Krishna district/region); credited to Prolaya Vema Reddi; later held by the Gajapatis
- **Gap:** Your sources do not describe the architecture of either fort in detail

## Kondavidu Reddi rulers → period → contribution | from: S1, S2, S3
| **Ruler** | **Period** | **Key facts / public works** |
|---|---|---|
| **Prolaya Vema Reddi** (founder) | 1324/1325-1353 | Served in the court of **Prataparudra** earlier; capital **Addanki**; restored **agraharas** to Brahmins; Errana at court; built steps to **Pathala Ganga at Srisailam** (done 1346) and to **Ahobilam** hill |
| **Anavota (Anapota) Reddi** | 1353/54-1364 | Moved the capital to **Kondavidu** (Manyapuram inscription); developed / revived the **Motupalli** dock (port); abolished the tax on gold; "Dweepa Vijetha" |
| **Anavema Reddi** (greatest) | 1364-1386 | Greatest Reddi; widest conquests; Srisailam and Simhachalam temple works; large charities |
| **Kumaragiri Reddi** | 1386-1402 | Patron of dance; Vasantotsavams; carved out the Rajahmundry division under his son Anavota, then **Kataya Vema**; ousted |
| **Pedakomati Vema Reddi** | 1402-1420 | Scholar-king; Sangeeta Chintamani; drove out Kumaragiri; recovered land from Vijayanagara; court of Srinatha and Vamana Bhatta Bana |
| **Racha Vema Reddi** (last) | 1420-1424 | Levied the hated, oppressive **Puriti** tax; killed by his own people |

- **Order (APPSC 2025):** **Anavota → Anavema → Kumaragiri → Pedakomati**
# 1. Prolaya Vema Reddi - rise
- Expelled Tughluq forces from coastal Andhra c. **1325** (some notes: 1324); Panta clan
# 2. Anavema Reddi - donor and officials
- **Donor:** **Anavema Reddi** = most charitable Reddi king
  - Agraharas, Brahmanda, Kalpataru, Kanakadhara, Gosahasra danas (guided by **Vemadri Pandit**)
- **Officials:** Minister Mamidi Peddanamatya; army chief Chennamanayaka
# 3. Kumaragiri Reddi - reign
- **Accession:** Opposed by **Pedakomati Vema** → settled by making him feudatory of Chandolu and heir
- **Eastern conquests:** Brother-in-law and commander **Kataya Vema** conquered the east up to Cuttack
- **Division:** Kumaragiri made **Rajahmundry** a separate division under his son Anavota, then Kataya Vema (1395)
  - **Resentment:** Pedakomati resented this
- **End (civil war):** **1402**: Pedakomati seized Kondavidu and ousted Kumaragiri, who fled to Rajahmundry and died there in refuge

## Rajahmundry (Rajamahendravaram) rulers → period → contribution | from: S4
- **Kingdom of Rajamahendravaram (1402-1448):**
  - Founders: Pakanati Reddis who moved east under Anavota and Anavema
  - Carved out by **Kumaragiri Reddi** from Kataya Vema's eastern conquests

| **Ruler** | **Period** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|---|
| Anavota Reddi (Kumaragiri's son) | to 1395 | first ruler of the division; died young |
| **Kataya Vema Reddi** | 1395-1414 | independent after 1402; patron of poets; killed at the **Battle of Kakaraparthi (1414)** by **Gajaraju Tippa**, an ally of Pedakomati |
| **Allada Reddi** (regent) | 1414-c.1420 | ran the state for **Veerabhadra Reddi** |
| **Veerabhadra Reddi** | c.1414-1448 | nominal king; restored 1423 and 1429 |
| Vema (Veda) Reddi | 1423-1448 | Veerabhadra's brother; **prime minister and de facto ruler** |

- **Rivalry with Kondavidu:** Kataya Vema ruled independently after 1402; spent his whole reign fighting Pedakomati
  - Rivalry ended with Kondavidu's fall (1424); Rajahmundry survived another 24 years as a Vijayanagara feudatory
- **Allada Reddi - opponents:**
  - **Pedakomati Vema Reddi** of Kondavidu - attacked Rajahmundry in 1414; beat Kataya Vema and Deva Raya I's army at Attili-Kakaraparthi
  - **Annadeva** (Annadevu) - captured parts of Vengi with Velama support during Kataya Vema's reign
- **Allada Reddi - ally:** **Vijayanagara** (Deva Raya I had backed Kataya Vema)
- **Allada's helpers in the east:** His sons Vema Reddi, Veerabhadra Reddi and Doddareddi

## Battles, conquests and the end of Reddi rule → defeated / conquered by | from: S2, S5, S6, S7
# 1. Anavema Reddi - conquests
- **Conquests:** Defeated the **Kalinga Gajapatis** (with Padmanayaka and Vijayanagara help)
  - Took **Nidadavolu** (gave it to Choda Bhima), **Rajahmundry, Pithapuram, Simhachalam**, Konaseema
# 2. Wars by opponent
- **Velamas:** Held the north
  - **Battle of Bhimavaram** - Reddis expelled the Velamas from Palnadu (APPSC G2 2019 key)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes place a Battle of Bhimavaram (1367-68) as the Velama victory over Kapaya Nayaka - do not mix the two
- **Bahmanis:**
  - Mallaya Reddy defeated Hasan Gangu at Motupalli (see Motupalli)
  - Senani Mallareddy drove Bahmani forces from **Amaravati** + restored the Amareswara temple
  - **Gajarao Tippa** defeated Firoz Shah's army at **Kambhammetta (1396)**
  - Kandukur attack repelled (1398)
- **Vijayanagara:**
  - Kataya Vema defeated **Harihara II**; took Tripurantakam and Vinukonda (Srisailam later returned)
  - Treaty (Sarpavaram inscription): Harihara married his daughter Vijayalakshmi to Kataya's son
  - Pedakomati recovered lost land (1404)
- **Orissa/Gajapatis / Kalinga:**
  - Anavota halted the Kalinga Gangas on the Krishna; Anavema defeated the Kalinga forces (Gajapatis); Kataya Vema raided up to **Cuttack**
  - Gajapati **Kapileswara** attacked in **1448**; Kondaveedu fell to him around **1450**
# 3. Gajapati and Vijayanagara rivalry over Rajahmundry, 1420-1448

|---|---|

| 1423 | Veerabhadra, with brother Vema, recovered Rajahmundry |

| 1435 | **Kapileswara Gajapati** took Cuttack and pressed on the Reddis |
| 1444 | Draksharama inscription: Vijayanagara commander **Mallappa Odayar** came to help |
| 1446-48 | after Deva Raya II's death, **Kapileswara Gajapati annexed Rajahmundry** |

| **Line** | **Last ruler** | **Ended by** |
|---|---|---|
| **Kondavidu** | **Racha Vema Reddi**, killed by his own people over the **Puriti tax** | **Vijayanagara (Deva Raya II), 1424** |
| **Rajahmundry** | **Veerabhadra Reddi and Vema Reddi** | **Kapileswara Gajapati** of Orissa (**1448**) |
| **Kandukur branch** (Mallareddi's line) | - | **Deva Raya I** annexed the Kandukuru Reddis → reduced to Vijayanagara feudatories |

## Administration and taxes → terms → meaning | from: S8, S9
# 1. Administration
- **Continuity:** Same decentralised pattern continued under the Gajapatis
- **Village - 12 Ayagars** (village servants; Kakatiya pattern inherited):
  - Karnam (accountant), Reddi/Peddakapu (headman), Talari (watchman)
  - Paid in **Manyam** land or a crop share (**Mera**)
  - Karnam, Reddi and Talari held village judicial and police powers
# 2. Taxes → ruler
- **Puriti Pannu:** Tax on the delivery of a child (maternity tax), levied by **Rachavema Reddi** (PYQ 2018, 2019)
  - Rachavema = **last Kondaveedu Reddi ruler**; **killed by the public** for this oppressive tax
  - Quoted as the example of burdensome taxation in the late, weakened Reddi phase
- **Anavota Reddi:** **Abolished the tax on gold** (to encourage trade; see Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam)
# 3. Taxes → meaning
| **Tax** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Puriti Pannu | tax on childbirth (Rachavema) |
| Kanika | presents/gifts due to the king or officials [GK] |
| Pullari | grazing tax on cattle [GK] |
| Illari | house tax [GK] |
| Ari | land tax (Kakatiya usage) |

- **Inherited taxes:** Kanika, Pullari, Pannu, Siddhayam, Sangam, Darshanam, Niruvidi, Ardhayam - from Kakatiya-era tax lists
- **Exemptions:** Temple lands, Brahmin grants and Ayagar lands were **tax-exempt**
> Exam angle: "Which Reddi king levied Puriti Pannu?" - Rachavema (not Kumaragiri or Anavema).

## Motupalli port → trade, Abhaya Sasanam, literary source | from: S10
- **Port:** **Motupalli** = chief Reddi port
- **Capture:** **Mallaya Reddy** (**Mallareddi**), Prolaya Vema's brother, took Motupalli by defeating Bahmani founder **Hasan Gangu**; Anavota developed the dock
- **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam:** Issued by **Somaya**, minister of **Anavota Reddi**, to encourage trade
  - Guaranteed merchants protection from sea pirates
- **Literary source:** **Srinatha's Haravilasam** (APPSC 2019; PYQ 2018, 2019)
  - Describes the sea trade + **imports and exports** of the Reddi age
  - Dedicated to merchant **Tippayya Setti**, famous for overseas trade
- **Earlier trade route (APPSC 2019):** Buddhist text **Vinayavatthu** mentions the 'Andhra Path'

## Society, women, festivals and dance → persons | from: S3, S11
# 1. Castes
- **Warrior-farmers:** **Kammas, Kapus, Reddis and Velamas** - backbone; supplied most soldiers
- **Traders:** **Komatis** ran inland and coastal trade
- **Continuity:** Brahmin-warrior-merchant order of the Kakatiya-Reddi period survived into Gajapati rule
# 2. Festival, dance and music
- **Vasantotsavam:** Spring festival; most popular festival; celebrated grandly by **Kumaragiri**
  - Funded by merchant **Avachi Tippaya Setti** (Avachi Tippayya Setti) - recorded in Srinatha's **Haravilasam**
- **Court dancer:** **Lakumadevi** (APPSC G2 2012), renowned in Bharatanatyam
  - C. Narayana Reddy later wrote Karpura Vasanta Rayalu on him
- **Kumaragiri himself:** Expert in Bharatanatyam (his dance treatise: see Books → authors)
- **Instrument:** **'Jalakaranda'** mentioned in the **Dharmasagara inscription** (PYQ G2 2019)
# 3. Queen Surambika and her tank
- **Queens:** Educated in the arts
- **Who:** **Surambika (Suryambika)**, wife of **Pedakomati Vema Reddi**; dug a tank
- **Tank name - keys differ:**
  - **Govardhana Samudram** - APPSC G2 2019 key
  - **Santana Sagaram** - APPSC GS 2018 key; some notes call it **Santana Payonidhi**
- **Canals:** Their son **Rachavema** cut canals (**'Jaganobbaganda'**) to feed water into the queen's tank
- **Welfare role:** Reddi rulers built tanks, canals and reservoirs; royal women took an active part in such welfare works
> Exam angle: read the options. If only one of Govardhana Samudram or Santana Sagaram is offered, that is the answer.
# 4. Anitalli (Anithalli)
- **Family:** Daughter of **Kataya Vema Reddi**; married **Veerabhadra Reddi**, Allada's second son
  - Was the **rightful heir** after her father
  - Some notes (p.224) call her "Allada Reddi's wife"; most (p.220) say wife of Veerabhadra → followed
- **Record:** issued the Kaluvacheru inscription (see Inscriptions → ruler)
# 5. Temple women
- **Devadasis:** Temple women trained in music, dance and ritual; respected performers at Shiva temples such as the Pancharamas

## Religion and temples → built by | from: S2, S12
# 1. Religion
- **Shaivism:** Kings = **Pashupata Shaivism**; people = **Veerashaivism**; Bhairava and Mailara worship common
- **Ghoderayas:** Propagated Shaivism from **Tripurantakam**; Reddi kings were disciples of Ghoderayacharulu
- **Vaishnavism:**

- **Endowments:** **Srisailam, Simhachalam, Ahobilam**; **Pancharama** Shiva temples renovated and endowed + land grants

## Court poets → patron, titles, contributions | from: S1, S14, S15
# 1. Errana
- **Patron:** Court poet of **Prolaya Vema Reddi** (Addanki) (PYQ G2 2011)
  - APPSC keys say Prolaya Vema Reddi → follow them
  - Harivamsa dedicated to him
- **Titles:** **Prabandha Parameswara**, **Sambhudasa**
- **Mahabharata:** Translated the remaining part (**Seshabhaga**, Aranya Parva shesham) of the **Aranya Parva** → completed the Telugu Mahabharata; third of the **Kavitrayam**
- **Place:** Bridges Nannaya's classical style and Tikkana's style; called the greatest Telugu translator
# 2. Srinatha
- **Title & dates:** **Kavi Sarvabhauma** (c. 1365-1441); court poet of **Pedakomati Vema Reddi** (PYQ 2002)
- **Office:** **Vidyadhikari** (minister of education); composed Pedakomati's Sanskrit inscriptions
- **Later life:**
  - After Kondaveedu fell (**1424**) → joined the Rajahmundry Reddis (patron **Veerabhadra Reddi**) with Bendapudi Annamatya's help
  - Some notes: wrote **Kashikhandam** at Kataya Vema's request; Rajahmundry court under Kataya Vema = competitive literary centre
  - After Rajahmundry fell (**1448**) → poverty in Palnadu
- **Vijayanagara:** Defeated **Gauda Dindima Bhatta**, court poet of **Devaraya II**
  - Honoured with **Kanakabhishekam** + **Ganda Penderam**
- **Era:** **Kavya Yuga**; wrote more kavyas than any other Telugu poet; prepared the way for the Prabandha style
- **PYQ (G2 2012):** Srinatha = poet cited as evidence that **paper** was used for writing in the 15th century

## Universal angles | from: S17 | universal
# 1. Significance
- **Significance:** Reddi rule (c. 1325-1448) = "second golden age" of Telugu culture and literature
  - Peak of Telugu literature before Vijayanagara; kings were themselves authors
  - Errana, Srinatha; royal language Sanskrit; emblem the bull; faith Shaiva (Pasupata)
  - Patronage of literature gave political legitimacy; ardent Shaivas who restored the **Pancharama** shrines
- **Rajahmundry literary hub:**
  - **Srinatha's Kashikhandam** (some notes: at Kataya Vema's request)
  - Sivaleka Kommana's Sivalila Vilasamu
  - Vinnakota Peddana's Kavyalankara Chudamani
- **Comparative note:** One of three successor states after 1323, with the Musunuris and the Recherla Velamas
# 2. AP angle
- **AP angle:** Kondavidu/Kondaveedu hill fort (Guntur/Palnadu region), Motupalli, Srisailam, Ahobilam, Tripurantakam
- **Reddi-era renovations:** Pancharama shrines - Draksharama Bhimesvara, Samarlakota Kumararama (Godavari region); Bikkavolu temples
# 3. FIRST/LAST
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Errana completed the Andhra Mahabharata (last of the Kavitrayam)
  - Srinatha opened the Kavya Yuga

=== ROW 99 | Musunuri Nayaks and the Velama (Racherla) chiefs of Rachakonda-Devarakonda

## Fall of Warangal (1323) and the liberation movement — FIRSTs, leader, chiefs | from: S0
# 1. Background - fall of Warangal
- **Background:** Warangal fell in **1323**; Muhammad bin Tughlaq renamed it **Sultanpur** (some notes: 'Sultanpurti')
- **Administration:** Andhra under Malik Burhanuddin, with **Malik Maqbool** / **Malik Maqbul** as deputy
- **Survivors:** Of Prataparudra's **75 generals**, only three survived: **Prolaya Nayaka, Recherla Singama and Prolaya Vema Reddi** (also spelt Racherla)
  - Kaluvacheru grant (Anitalli, **1423**) names **75 Nayakas** who backed Prolaya
- **Opening:** Tughluq's misrule (from **1326**) + revolts across the empire → Andhra freed within about **2 years**
# 2. Liberation movement
- **Name:** **Vimochanodyamam** - **first organised resistance** to Muslim rule in Andhra
- **Leader of the liberation confederacy:** **Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka** (APPSC GS 2018; G2 2019)
- **Chiefs involved:** **Prolaya Vema Reddi, Recherla Singama, Koppula Prolaya Nayaka**, Kolani Rudra, Vengi Bhupala, Manchikonda Ganapati Nayaka, Kapaya Nayaka
- **Kingdom:** **1325-1368**

## Musunuri rulers → origin, capital, contribution, titles | from: S1, S2
# 1. Prolaya Nayaka - capital Rekapalli
- **Origin:** Son of **Potinayaka**; **Kamma** clan (Chaturthanvayas / **Chaturthanvaya** lineage)
  - Surname from **Musunuru** village near Vuyyuru (Krishna district)
- **Rule:** Freed the land between the Krishna and Godavari; ruled from **Rekapalli** (on the Godavari, forest near Bhadrachalam) from **1325** (capital: APPSC G2 2017)
- **Vilasa copper-plate grant (1330)** (Vilasa Tamra Sasanam, 1330 CE):
  - Sufferings under Tughluq rule + Prolaya's leadership
  - Agrarian reforms/relief + **restoration of agraharas** to Brahmins
- **Succession:** Childless → succeeded by his cousin/nephew Kapaya Nayaka
# 2. Kapaya Nayaka (1336-1368) - recovery of Warangal
- **Relation:** Paternal nephew of Prolaya (some notes: cousin)
- **1336 recovery:** With **Hoysala Veera Ballala III** (+ Prolaya Vema Reddi, Singama) took **Orugallu from Malik Maqbool in 1336** (**Warangal** from **Malik Maqbul**)
  - Ruled the old Kakatiya realm from Orugallu
- **Simultaneous campaigns:**
  - Singama cleared Nalgonda-Mahabubnagar
  - Prolaya Vema Reddi cleared Kammanadu-Pakanadu south of the Krishna
# 3. Kapaya Nayaka - administration
- **Vengi:** Appointed nephew **Anapota Nayaka** (Anapotha Nayaka) as governor at Vengi
- **Polavaram inscription:** Issued by Kapaya; names 14 members of the lineage, only 3 ruled
- **Extent:** Extended power to Kaulas (Bidar)
# 4. Titles → holder
| **Title** | **Holder** |
|---|---|
| **Andhra Suratrana** | **Kapaya Nayaka** - first holder (APPSC GS 2019); later also **Anavema Reddi** (APPSC G2 2011 key) |
