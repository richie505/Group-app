## Agency grievances and earlier rebellions → place, cause, leader (1794-1880) | from: S0, S1
# 1. Agency grievances → effect
| **Grievance** | **Effect on tribals** |
|---|---|
| **Madras Forest Act, 1882** | Reserved forests → cut off tribal rights to **podu** (shifting cultivation) + **minor forest produce**; curbed grazing |
| **Begar** | Tribals forced into unpaid coolie work on roads (road building) |
| **Muttadari** system | oppressive - extortion, forced labour |
| **Tahsildar Bastian** | Local Tahsildar whose excesses were the spark |
- **Tribes:** **Koya** and other hill tribes
# 2. Rampa (1879-80) and Rekapalle (1879)
|  | **Rampa (1879-80)** | **Rekapalle (1879)** |
|---|---|---|
| Place | Rampa, **Rampachodavaram** agency, Godavari district | **Rekapalle, Bhadrachalam taluk**, Godavari district (APPSC 2017 key) |
| Cause | Munsubdar **Madhava Bhupati** (made Munsubdar 1848) exploiting tribals and eroding the muttadars | Axe tax on podu raised from **4 annas to 4-12 annas** in a famine; area joined Madras Presidency in 1874 |

## Rampa / Manyam Rebellion 1922-24 → event, date, place | from: S3, S4
# 1. Span and area
- **Span:** **Rampa / Manyam Rebellion**, **January 1922 - May 1924** (2 years 5 months)
- **Area:** Agency tracts of **East Godavari and Visakhapatnam (Gudem hills)**
- **Causes:** Forest Act, begar, muttadari, Bastian - see Agency grievances
# 2. Event → date
| **Date** | **Event** |
|---|---|
| **22 Aug 1922** | **Chintapalli** PS - the **first police station attacked** (first raid); arms seized |
| Aug 1922 | **Krishnadevipeta** and **Rajavommangi** stations raided; Addateegala [GK] |
| **24 Sept 1922** | **Damanapalli ghat** ambush: officers **Scott Coward and Heiter** killed |
| Sept 1922 | Battle at **Panjerighat** (Damanghat) - the **direct battle** with British forces (APPSC G2 2012; APPSC 2012 key) |
| **6 Dec 1922** | **Peddagaddapalem**: **Malabar Special Police** (crackdown) - Raju's first defeat; wounded at Lingapuram |
| 18 Apr 1923 | Annavaram PS raided |
| **17 Sept 1923** | **Gam Mallu Dora** arrested at Nadimpalem |
| 27 Jan 1924 | **Assam Rifles** [GK] and Gurkhas reach Narsipatnam |
| **7 May 1924** | Raju captured and shot dead at **Koyyuru**[GK] |
| 7 June 1924 | Gam Gantam Dora killed in firing |

- ⚠ **Chintapalli** = first PS attacked (22 Aug 1922); **Panjerighat** = direct battle; **Peddagaddapalem** = first defeat
# 3. Capture, death and tomb
- **Death:** Captured by Jamadar **Kanchu Meenan**; shot on **7 May 1924**, tied to a tree, before **Major Goodall** (also spelt "Gudal", "Goodman")
- **Last rites:** **8 May 1924** at **Krishnadevipeta** near the Tandava river
  - Some notes say cremated, others say buried there
- **Tomb/samadhi at Krishnadevipeta**
- **Nature / impact:** **Outside the Congress** and non-violence, yet troubled the British more than the Non-Cooperation Movement (NCM) in that region

## Chirala-Perala Satyagraha 1921 → cause, exodus, arrest, outcome | from: S7
- **Name:** ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes call it "Chirala-Palleru" - standard name is **Chirala-Perala**
# 1. Cause
- Chirala and Perala (Bapatla taluk; weavers and small farmers, ~15,000 people) made a **municipality** - proposed Nov 1919, formed **Jan 1920** (some notes: **municipality in 1919**)
- Tax rose from **Rs 4,000 to Rs 40,000** a year
# 2. Exodus
- On Gandhi's advice; at midnight on **25 April 1921**, **13,572 of 15,326** residents (~**13,000**) left
- New camp **"Ramnagar" (Ramanagaram)** outside municipal limits
- Parallel all-caste assembly + arbitration court; lasted **11 months**
# 3. Arrest
- Speech at the **Andhra Mahasabha, Berhampur (Sept 1921)** → banned from speaking for 2 months → broke the ban
- Arrested **1 October 1921**; tried by **Rutherford at Srikakulam**; jailed **1 year** (some notes say the arrest was at Trichinopoly)
# 4. Failure
- Lack of funds (donations: Srinivasa Ayyangar Rs 1,000, APCC Rs 3,000, Guntur DCC Rs 250)
- Jealousy of other Congress leaders; no second line of leaders
# 5. Outcome
- People returned
- Municipality **abolished in 1938** by **Bezawada Gopala Reddy** (Local Self-Govt Minister, Rajaji ministry)
- Gandhi wrote in Young India that the municipality only meant more taxes

# 2. Palnad pullari (grass tax) movement
- **Region:** Palnadu (Guntur) villages - **Macherla, Veldurthi, Sirigiripadu, Minchalapadu**
- **Leader:** **Kanneganti Hanumanthu**, killed at **Minchalapadu**; backed by Unnava Lakshmi Narayana
- **Year:** **1921-22** (APPSC 2012 key)
- **See also:** Full detail in M1-A-68
# 3. Pedanandipadu no-tax campaign
- **Place & date:** **Pedanandipadu firka, Guntur district**; **Jan-Feb 1922**
  - !! Not in your sources: the taluka of Pedanandipadu firka.
- **Tax refused:** **January instalment of land revenue** (no-tax)
- **Unique features:**
  - (1) **Mass resignation of village officers** - **18 of 19** in the firka (29 in Guntur district), led by Chandavolu karanam **Machiraju Ramamurthy**
  - (2) **Shanti Sena** - formation decided by Guntur DCC, **Dec 1921**; led in the firka by **Parvataneni Veeraiah Chowdary**
- **Congress split:**
  - Backed it: Konda Venkatappayya, Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri
  - Urged restraint: Nyapathi Subbarao
  - Inquiry committee (**T. Prakasam, Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao, Dantu Narayana Raju**) praised it but advised waiting for Bardoli
- **Withdrawal:** **11 Feb 1922** - Guntur DCC got **Gandhi's message** to Konda Venkatappayya to suspend → suspended; ended with the NCM
# 4. Hyderabad State Congress, 1938
- **Formed:** **1938**; first president **Swami Ramananda Tirtha**
- **Ban:** **Banned by the Nizam in 1938**; ban lifted **1946**
- **Satyagraha (1938):** Against the ban; coincided with the Osmania Vandemataram agitation

## Universal angles | from: S12 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - "Glorious Decade" (1921-31) of Andhra - Chirala-Perala, Palnadu, Pedanandipadu showed mass non-cooperation; Rampa showed tribal armed resistance
  - 1921-22 Guntur saw the NCM's most original methods in Andhra - mass exodus, officials resigning, no-tax campaign
- **Current relevance:**
  - **Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport, Bhogapuram (Vizianagaram)**, named after him; CM invited the PM to inaugurate it on **1 August 2026** (23 Jul 2026)
  - Guinness record for the **largest Dhimsa dance** by **13,000+ tribal women** and girls held there ahead of the PM's visit (July 2026)
- **AP angle:**
  - **30-ft bronze statue** of Alluri unveiled by the PM at **Bhimavaram** for his **125th birth anniversary** (Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav) + rebellion's centenary
  - Agency district named **Alluri Sitarama Raju district**

- **FIRST/LAST:** First woman jailed at Chirala-Perala - Ravuri Alivelu Mangatayaramma
- **Comparative note:** Unlike Gandhian forest satyagrahas, Alluri used armed guerrilla war, yet respected Gandhi

## Garimella Satyanarayana → life, song, imprisonment | from: S4
- **Life:** **14 July 1893 to 18 Dec 1952**; born at Gonedapadu, Narasannapeta taluk (Srikakulam); left studies at Gandhi's call
- **The song:** **"Maakoddee Tella Doratanamu"** - Non-Cooperation era. Grew to 162 lines
  - Cyclostyled a thousand times; **praised by Gandhi**
  - Grievances: taxes, forced labour, untouchability, divide and rule
- **Imprisonment:**
  - Jailed in **1922 for one year** for the song (some notes say 6 months)
  - Later **2.5 years RI**; wife, father and grandfather died while he was in jail
- **Other works:** Harijana Paatalu, anti-liquor songs, Samajika Navala, Palletullalo
- **Legacy:** First people's poet of the freedom struggle; inspired Nazar, Subbarao Panigrahi and Gaddar

## Sri Sri → titles, awards, FIRSTs, works | from: S1

# 2. Terms → meaning
- **Padya Natakam:** Classical Telugu theatre with dialogue mainly in verse

| **Feature** | **Padya natakam** | **Social drama** |
|---|---|---|
| Content | mythological or epic (Keechaka Vadham, Gayopakhyanam, Prahlada Charitamu) | contemporary evils (bride-price, dowry, widowhood) |
| Language | sung verse (padyam) [GK] | prose in spoken Telugu (**vyavaharika bhasha**) |
| Examples | Surabhi repertoire, Gayopakhyanam | Kanyasulkam, Vyavahara Dharmabodhini, Varavikrayam |

- **Gurajada:** Made spoken Telugu the norm for writing → widened the audience

## Nationalist role → Swadeshi troupes and banned plays | from: S5
- **Themes:** Drama companies carried **social-reform themes** (Kanyasulkam, Veeresalingam's drama) before nationalist ones
- **Swadeshi & NCM years:** Troupes such as the **Jatiya Nataka Rangam** promoted swadeshi goods and national education
- **Viplava Parivartanamu:** Published and **banned during the Non-Cooperation Movement**
  - **Author:** **Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri** per the APPSC G2 2019 key vs **Kodali Anjaneyulu** per the APPSC 2018 key
  - ⚠ **Trap:** The two official keys disagree → learn both names with this play
  - Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri supported the NCM boycott of elections in 1920
- **Why banned:** A drama company could put a political idea before **thousands of illiterate people in one evening** → colonial officials watched troupes closely

> Exam angle: "banned during which movement" plus "who wrote it".

## Unnava Lakshmibayamma → Sarada Niketanam and movements | from: S1
- **Founders:** **Unnava Lakshminarayana** with wife **Unnava Lakshmibayamma** (APPSC G2 2019 key: Unnava Lakshminarayana)
- **1902:** The couple ran a widow asylum; she held the **first widow remarriage** there
- **Year:** Women's Gurukulam founded in **1922**
  - The **1902** widow asylum later merged into it
- **Role:** Sheltered widows and destitute girls, taught girls; a "hotbed" of the freedom movement
- **NCM:**
  - Led Guntur hartals after her husband's arrest over the **Palnadu forest (pullari) satyagraha**
  - Palnadu forest satyagraha saw **8 women** arrested with 28 men
  - Took personal responsibility for Guntur women's participation in all Congress movements
- **Salt Satyagraha, 9 April 1930:** Led women in Krishna and Guntur districts, with Rukmini Lakshmipathi
- !! Not in your sources: dates and places of her imprisonment.

# 3. Durgabai Deshmukh - later posts
