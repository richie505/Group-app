## Ikshvaku rulers → period and contributions | from: S0, S2, S3, S4
- **Political status:** **Feudatories of the Satavahanas** (Alluru)
- **Duration:** **c. 75 years** (about **AD 225-300**; some notes say 3rd-4th century CE)
- **Number of kings:** **Puranas list 7 kings**; **inscriptions confirm 4**
  - Some notes (Part 2) say 8 and 5

| **Ruler** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Vasishthiputra (Sri) Chamtamula / Santamula** | **Founder**, first a feudatory of the Satavahanas. Performed Vedic yajnas. Tried to make the **intelligentsia partners in agricultural development** (G2 2012), with gifts of land, ploughs, bullocks and cows |
| **Virapurushadatta** | The **golden age of Buddhism**. Queens were great donors. Issued the **Alluru** and **Nagarjunakonda** inscriptions. Title **Sri Parvatadhipati** |
| **Ehuvala Chamtamula** | Son of Virapurushadatta and Bhatti Mahadevi; ruled about 24 years. **First Sanskrit inscriptions** in the Telugu lands. **Gummadidurru** inscription |

- **Brahmins:** Invited from the north
# Virapurushadatta — matrimonial alliances
- **Virapurushadatta's marriage:** **Rudrabhattarika, the princess of Ujjain** → tie with **Sakas / Western Kshatrapas** (G2 2025)
  - Other wives: Bhattidevi, Bapisri

=== ROW 52 | Ikshvaku religious conditions - Buddhism at Nagarjunakonda, Brahmanical temples

## Ikshvaku kings → religious policy | from: S0
| **King** | **Religious policy** |
|---|---|
| **Vasishthiputra Sri Santamula** (founder) | Vedic: Ashvamedha, Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Agnistoma; worshipped **Virupakshapati (Shiva)** and **Mahasena (Skanda)**; agraharas to northern Brahmins |
| **Virapurushadatta** | The **only Ikshvaku king to adopt Buddhism** (earlier Vedic); his reign was the **golden age of Buddhism**; rebuilt the Maha Chaitya under **Acharya Bhadanta Ananda**; titles "Ashoka of the South", **"Sri Parvatadhipati"**; sculptures at Nagarjunakonda show a Shivalinga trampled underfoot |

  - **Queens, princesses and rich lay women** funded stupas + viharas
  - Kings did not oppose; allowed it

=== ROW 53 | Ikshvaku art, architecture and literature

## Ikshvaku art FIRSTs and sculptures → significance | from: S0, S7
- **School:** Nagarjunakonda = main centre of the **Amaravati School**
  - Reached its **final, most mature phase** under the Ikshvakus
  - Followed **Mahayana** themes
- **Early Ikshvaku style:** Less stylisation + **low relief** = "last flicker" of the Satavahana Amaravati tradition
- **Themes:** Scenes from the **Buddha's life and Jataka stories** on panels
- **Buddha depiction:** **Human form** in this later phase, alongside older symbols

| **Sculpture / feature** | **Significance** |
|---|---|
| **Mandhata sculpture, Jaggayyapeta** | Most important Ikshvaku sculpture; **first depiction of a Chakravarti** in Indian art |
| **Sati** panel | **First depiction of sati** in Indian art |
| Five Aparamahavinaseliya sculptures | Buddhist sculptures of that sect found at Nagarjunakonda |
| Sculptors' names carved | Started the **"Sculptor's Style"** (e.g. **Badantacharya** at the Mahavihara) |
| **Viragallu (hero stones)** | Also began under the Ikshvakus |

- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - First Sanskrit inscription in South India
  - First structural Hindu temples
  - First sculptor signatures

## Stupa and temple terms → meaning | from: S1

- ⚠ **Dhatugarbha** = relics; **Paribhogika** = used objects; **Uddeshika** = nothing

=== ROW 54 | Vishnukundins - political history, Madhava Varma I and II

## Scholars → Vishnukundin origin | from: S0

## Undavalli caves — features | from: S1
- **Structure:** **Four storeys** + 3 main caves
  - Carved **top-down**: upper levels finely detailed; the **ground floor is left crude and unfinished** (29 Jul 2026)
- **Centrepiece:** **Monolithic reclining Vishnu, Ananta Padmanabha Swamy**, about **5 m (16 ft)** long
- **Motifs:** **Purnakumbha** throughout
- **Earlier use:** A **Jain abode, then used by Buddhist monks, then made a Hindu temple** (2026)
  - Reclining (some notes: padmasana) **Buddha image was replaced by Vishnu**
- **Date:** **4th-5th c. CE**; regional form of the **Gupta** rock-cut style
> Exam angle: Aug 2026 test - "built by the **Cholas** as a Hindu temple, later Buddhist" is **false**; the true statements are the Krishna-bank location and the Ananta Padmanabha statue.

## Architecture terms → meaning | from: S4
