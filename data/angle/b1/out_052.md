# 3. Razakars & MIM
- **Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen:** Emerged in **1927** (APPSC G2 2008 key)
- **Razakars:** Militant volunteer force/militia of the **Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen (MIM)**, led by **Kasim (Qasim) Razvi**

- **Peasant revolt (1946-51):** Led by the **Communists and the Andhra Mahasabha** against the Nizam, the **deshmukhs** and later the Razakars
  - Trigger: killing of **Doddi Komaraiah (1946)**
- **Link to integration:**
  - Unrest + Razakar violence → Operation Polo (**1948**)
  - Indian Army then put down the armed squads; struggle withdrawn in **October 1951**
- ⚠ **1927** = AISPC founded **and** Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen emerged; **1938** = Hyderabad State Congress

=== ROW 248 | Wavell Plan and Simla Conference; Cabinet Mission Plan 1946; Interim Government

## League demands, formulas and pacts (1943-45) → author and proposals | from: S0
| **Demand / formula** | **Author / year** | **Proposal** |
|---|---|---|
| **"Divide and Quit"** | Adopted by the **Muslim League** at its **Karachi session (Dec 1943)** (some notes date it 1944) | - |
| **C.R. Formula** | **C. Rajagopalachari**, **1944** | See below |
| **Desai-Liaquat Pact** | **Bhulabhai Desai** (Congress leader in the **Central Legislative Assembly**) + **Liaquat Ali Khan** (League), **1945** | Interim government at the Centre with **equal numbers of Congress and League nominees** + **20%** for minorities; never formally accepted |

# 1. C.R. Formula (1944) - provisions
- League to back the independence demand + cooperate in a provisional (interim) government
- After the war, a **plebiscite** in contiguous Muslim-majority areas (NW and NE) to decide on separation
- If separation: joint agreements on defence, commerce and communications
- Gandhi-Jinnah talks in Bombay (**9-27 Sept 1944**) on its basis failed
- Rejected by both Congress and League; Jinnah wanted only Muslims to vote and no common centre
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes wrongly say **Wavell** proposed it; it was Rajagopalachari's

## Wavell Plan and Simla Conference, 1945 → proposals and failure | from: S1
- **Announced:** **Lord Wavell** (Viceroy **1943-47**), **1945**; discussed at the **Simla Conference** (**June-July 1945**)
# 1. Proposals
- All Executive Council members Indians **except the Viceroy and Commander-in-Chief**
- **Equal representation to caste Hindus and Muslims**
- Indians to frame their own constitution after the war
# 2. Failure
- **Representation:** Congress led by **Maulana Azad**; League by **Jinnah**
- **Cause:** Jinnah demanded **all Muslim members be nominated only by the League** (denying Congress and the **Punjab Unionists** any Muslim nominee)
  - Wavell abruptly closed the conference on **14 July 1945** instead of going ahead without the League
# 3. Result
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Simla closed without the League | League got a **de facto veto** over constitutional progress |
| Multi-communal **Unionist Party** of Punjab weakened | Polarised **1945-46** elections |

## Cabinet Mission, 1946 → members, provisions and grouping | from: S3, S5
# 1. Origin & members
- **Sent by:** British PM **Clement Attlee** after **WWII**; known as the "**Ministerial Trio Mission**"
- **Dates:** Arrived **24 March 1946**; announced its plan **16 May 1946**

| **Member** | **Office held in Britain** |
|---|---|
| **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** (leader) | Secretary of State for India |
| **Sir Stafford Cripps** | President of the Board of Trade |
| **A.V. Alexander** | First Lord of the Admiralty |

- **Purpose (Pethick-Lawrence):** Quickly set up machinery to frame India's constitution + arrange an **Interim Government**
- **Rejected a full Pakistan:**
  - NW and NE zones had large non-Muslim populations (**38%** and **48%**)
  - Partitioning **Punjab/Bengal** would break regional ties → recommended an undivided India
# 2. Three-tier structure
- **Tier 1:** **Union of India** with a **weak Centre** - only **Defence, Foreign Affairs and Communications**
- **Tier 2:** **Groups** of provinces
- **Tier 3:** Provinces holding all residuary subjects

| **Group** | **Provinces** | **Character** |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Madras**, **Bombay**, **UP**, **Bihar**, **CP**, **Orissa** | Hindu-majority |
| B | **Punjab**, **NWFP**, **Sindh** | Muslim-majority (NW) |
| C | **Bengal**, **Assam** | Muslim-majority (NE) |

# 3. Other provisions
- **Interim Cabinet:** All members Indians; minimum Viceroy interference
- **Commonwealth:** Recognised India's right to leave
# 4. Acceptance and the grouping trap
| **Party** | **Stand on the plan** |
|---|---|
| Congress | Accepted the Constituent Assembly scheme; rejected a weak Centre + compulsory groups; held grouping **optional** (so NWFP and Assam could opt out) |
| League | Accepted it (June 1946) holding grouping **compulsory** → stepping stone to Pakistan |

- **Withdrawal trigger:** Nehru's **10 July 1946** statement that Congress could modify the scheme/plan through its Assembly majority → Jinnah withdrew

## Constituent Assembly and Interim Government → composition and portfolios | from: S4, S6
# 1. Constituent Assembly
- **Envisaged by:** **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**; idea accepted in the **August Offer (1940)**
- **Strength:** **389** members = **296** from British India + **93** from princely states
- **Ratio:** About **1 seat per 10 lakh** population; indirectly elected by provincial assemblies on the basis of population
- **Provincial seats:** Divided among **General, Muslim and Sikh** communities; each elected by its own members in the provincial assembly by **proportional representation (single transferable vote)**
- **Princely states:** Members nominated by rulers
- **Elections (July 1946):** Of 296 British India seats = **Congress 208, Muslim League 73, others 15**
> Exam angle: "The Constituent Assembly was formed under which plan?" - **Cabinet Mission Plan** (not Cripps, not Mountbatten). "Not a member" questions list a fake name.
# 2. Interim Government
- **Formed:** **2 September 1946**
- **Envisaged by:** **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**
- **Head:** **Jawaharlal Nehru** as **Vice-President of the Viceroy's Executive Council** (Viceroy **Wavell** remained President)

| **Member** | **Portfolio** |
|---|---|
| Vallabhbhai Patel | Home; Information and Broadcasting [GK] |
| Rajendra Prasad | Food and Agriculture [GK] |
| Baldev Singh | Defence [GK] |
| Jagjivan Ram | Labour [GK] |
| **Liaquat Ali Khan** (League, from Oct 1946) | **Finance** |
| Jogendra Nath Mandal (League nominee) | Law [GK] |

- **League's entry:** **October 1946** with **5** nominees
  - **Sarat Chandra Bose**, **Shafaat Ahmed Khan** and **Syed Ali Zaheer** made way
  - League refused to attend the Constituent Assembly
- ⚠ **389 = 296 + 93**; **296 = 208 + 73 + 15**

- **Significance:**
  - Cabinet Mission = last British attempt to keep India united; its Constituent Assembly scheme survived partition and framed the Constitution
  - Chain: Cripps → Cabinet Mission → Mountbatten turned "freedom" into "freedom with partition"
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - Wavell's yielding at Simla handed Jinnah a veto
  - Ambiguity over compulsory grouping doomed the Mission
  - Hasty advance of the date + Radcliffe's award blamed for the partition violence
- **AP angle:** Tanguturi Prakasam led the Congress ministry in Madras Presidency after the 1946 elections

## Events → date: Direct Action Day and Gandhi, 1946-48 | from: S0, S1

## Plans and schemes of 1947 → author, date, provisions | from: S2, S3
# 1. Attlee's Statement (20 February 1947) (CDX)
- British rule to end by **30 June 1948** (British to leave by **June 1948**), even without agreement
- Power could go to provincial governments (hint of Balkanisation)
- Wavell replaced by Mountbatten
# 2. Alternative schemes
| **Scheme** | **Author** | **Proposal and fate** |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan Balkan / "Dickie Bird Plan" (May 1947)** | Mountbatten's earlier scheme | Transfer power to provinces as successor states; **Nehru rejected** it as Balkanisation of India |
| **United Bengal plan (1947)** | **Sarat Chandra Bose** + **H.S. Suhrawardy** | A sovereign undivided Bengal; rejected by Congress and League |

- Some notes credit Plan Balkan to Wavell; attribution to Mountbatten is the standard one
# 3. Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan), 1947
- **Mountbatten:** Became Viceroy in **March 1947**
- **Announced:** **3 June 1947**
- **Rationale:** Accepted the League's Pakistan demand to avoid civil war + administrative collapse
- **Provisions:**
  - **Punjab and Bengal:** Legislative assemblies to meet in two parts (Hindu-majority vs Muslim-majority districts); vote by simple majority on partition → both voted for partition
  - **Sindh:** To decide on its own
  - **Referendums:** **NWFP** → joined Pakistan despite the **Khudai Khidmatgar** boycott led by Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan; **Sylhet** district of Assam → joined East Pakistan
  - **Boundary Commission:** For Punjab and Bengal, to fix borders
  - **Princely states:** Paramountcy to lapse; join India or Pakistan (no third option)
  - **Governor-General:** Common GG permitted, but Pakistan chose Jinnah
  - **Legal basis:** Power transferred on the basis of the **GoI Act 1935**; armed forces divided
  - **Advanced the transfer date:** **June 1948** → **15 August 1947**
- **Congress's acceptance - reasons:** Avoid Balkanisation + secure a strong Centre + stop communal bloodshed
- ⚠ Attlee deadline **30 June 1948** → Mountbatten advanced it to **15 August 1947**

## Indian Independence Act 1947 → provisions | from: S5
# 1. Dominions and offices
- **Dominions:** Two independent **dominions**, **India and Pakistan**, with the right to **leave the British Commonwealth**
- **Offices abolished:** **Viceroy** + **Secretary of State for India**
- **Governor-General:** Each dominion got one acting on its cabinet's advice = constitutional head (a common GG allowed)
# 2. Constituent Assemblies
- Made **sovereign** in both dominions
  - Could frame any constitution and repeal any British law, including this Act
  - Also acted as dominion legislatures
# 3. Paramountcy and partition
- **Paramountcy:** **British paramountcy lapsed** → the **565** princely states free to accede to India or Pakistan
- **Other:** Armed forces divided; **Boundary Commissions** for **Punjab and Bengal**
  - partition votes in Punjab and Bengal; referendums in **NWFP and Sylhet**
# 4. Other provisions
| **Provision** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| Crown | No veto over dominion bills; "Emperor of India" title dropped [GK] |
| Interim law | Each dominion governed by the **Government of India Act 1935** / **GoI Act 1935** (as adapted) until its new constitution [GK] |
| Civil services | Recruitment by the Secretary of State discontinued |

- **Most distinctive feature (APPSC-G2 2017):** An **Act without a constitution** - transferred power but left constitution-making to Indians

## Partition → boundaries, assets and consequences | from: S6, S7
# 1. Radcliffe Boundary Commissions
- **Two commissions:** **Punjab** + **Bengal**
- **Composition:** Each = 2 Congress + 2 League nominee judges
- **Radcliffe Line award:** Announced only **after independence**
  - Ready on **12 August** but published on **17 August 1947** so that Britain would not be blamed for the violence
# 2. Division of assets
- **Partition Council:** Divided assets and liabilities
  - Pakistan took **17.5%** of the assets and liabilities
- **Division:** Armed forces, services and assets divided between the two dominions
# 3. Consequences
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Migration** | An estimated **10-15 million** crossed the new borders; hundreds of thousands killed in communal violence |
| **Rehabilitation** | New Government of India set up a **Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation** → refugee camps, resettlement, evacuee property |
| **Ceasefire** | UN ceasefire **1 Jan 1949** |

- ⚠ Award ready **12 August**, published **17 August 1947**
