=== ROW 126 | Battle of Talikota (1565), the Aravidu dynasty and the legacy of Vijayanagara

## Talikota 1565 → date, names, place and sides | from: S1
# 1. Date, names and place
- **Date:** **23 January 1565**; some notes give **26 January** (APPSC-GS 2022 answer: **1565**; G1 2018)
- **Place:** North bank of the **Krishna**, between the villages of Rakshasi and Tangadi

| **Name of the battle** | **Used by** |
|---|---|
| **Talikota** | Robert Sewell |
| **Rakshasi-Tangadi** | Fr. Heras, Nilakanta Sastri |
| **Bannihatti** | H.K. Sherwani |

# 2. Sides
- **Sides:** **Vijayanagara Empire** vs **Deccan Sultanates** (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar) (APPSC-G1 2018)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Vijayanagara faced the **Deccan Sultanates**, NOT the Bahmani kingdom (G1 2018; UPPSC 2014)
- **Vijayanagara side:**
  - De-facto ruler: **Aliya Rama Raya**, regent of Tuluva king **Sadasiva Raya**
  - Brothers **Tirumala and Venkatadri** fought alongside him
- **Sultans ("Pancha Padushas"):** Hussain Nizam Shah, Ali Adil Shah, **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** (key organiser of the alliance), Ali Barid

| **Confederacy member** | **Ruler** |
|---|---|
| Bijapur | **Ali Adil Shah** |
| Ahmadnagar | **Hussain Nizam Shah** (he beheaded Rama Raya) |
| Golconda | **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** (led the coalition, per CDX) |
| Bidar | **Ali Barid Shah** |

- **Stayed out:** **Berar** (Imad Shahi) did not take part
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes add Berar's **Burhan Imad-ul-Mulk** to the "Pancha Padushas" list; others (incl. Sherwani) exclude Berar → expect that version too

## Aliya Rama Raya → regency, policy and causes of Talikota | from: S0, S2
# 1. Rama Raya
- **Aliya (son-in-law) Rama Raya:** Married Krishnadevaraya's daughter
  - Earlier served under Golconda and the Bahmanis
  - **Regent** for nominal king **Sadashiva Raya** (1543-)
- **Turka Vada:** Muslim quarter he set up
- **Guru:** **Tatacharya**
# 2. Policy toward the Sultans
- **Policy:** Played the Sultans against each other

| **Years** | **Move** | **Gain** |
|---|---|---|
| **1546-47** | Backed Ahmadnagar vs Bijapur and Bidar | Took the **Raichur doab** |
| **1557-63** | Backed Bijapur | Razed **Ahmadnagar** |

# 3. Causes of Talikota
| **Cause** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| Rama Raya's aggressive **divide-and-rule** diplomacy | United the Sultans |
| Interference in the Sultanates' affairs | Sacked Ahmadnagar, 1557-63 |
| Marriage links | **Bibi Jamal** married Ibrahim; **Chand Bibi** married Ali Adil |
| Shelter to a rebel | Rama Raya sheltered **Jagadeva Rao**, a rebel from Golconda |
| Religion | Ferishta also alleges religious insults |

## Talikota → course and results | from: S2
# 1. Course
- Early Vijayanagara successes → deceptive peace talks + surprise attack
- Muslim contingents in Vijayanagara army withdrew/deserted
- **Rama Raya** **captured and killed**; Venkatadri also killed
# 2. Results
| **Area** | **Consequence** |
|---|---|
| **Capital (Hampi)** | Looted for about **six months**; severe but not total damage (Sewell's "graveyard" account may exaggerate) |
| **Flight** | Sadashiva and Tirumala fled to **Penukonda** |
| **Golconda gains** | **Kondavidu and Machilipatnam** + coastal tracts |
| **Nayakas** | Became independent/autonomous → later zamindars and poligars |
| **Sultanates** | Went back to fighting each other after the victory |

## Aravidu dynasty → origin, span, capitals | from: S3, S4
- **Name:** From **Araviti (Aravidu) village, Kurnool district**
- **Rule:** **1570-1680**; **7** rulers
- **End:** Death of **Sriranga III** in **1680**; some notes date the empire's end to **1646**
- **Sadasiva Raya:** Lived on as nominal king; death **1576** per some notes, while others give his reign as **1542-1570**
- **Capitals:** **Penukonda** (**1570**) → **Chandragiri** (under Venkata II) → **Vellore** (**1600**)
  - ⚠ Order: **Penukonda → Chandragiri → Vellore (1600)**

## Aravidu rulers → period and contribution | from: S3, S4
# 1. Rulers
| **Ruler** | **Key fact / contribution** |
|---|---|
| (Aliya **Rama Raya**, regent 1543-65) | De facto ruler under Sadashiva |
| **Tirumala Raya** (1570-72) | Founder; crowned at **Penukonda** in **1570** (LENS Jun 2026) at the age of **90**; wrote a commentary on the Gita Govinda; Vasucharitra was dedicated to him |
| **Sriranga Raya I** (1572-85) | Lost Kondaveedu to Ibrahim Qutb Shah |
| **Venkatapati Raya II** (1585-1614) | Greatest and longest-ruling ruler, called the "Second Krishnadevaraya"; his **Vilapakam inscription (1601) **is the key source for Tuluva-Aravidu genealogy |
| Sriranga II (1614) | Killed by **Jagga Raya** |
| Ramadeva (1616-30) | **Battle of Toppur (1616)**, the "second Talikota", in which Jagga Raya was killed |
| **Venkatapati III** (1630-43) | Grant of Madras |
| **Sriranga Raya III** (1643-80) | **Last** ruler; defeated at **Vandavasi (1642)** by Mir Jumla of Golconda; later held Penukonda as a chieftain; died **1680**, the end of the empire |

# 2. Venkatapati Raya II (1585-1614)
- **Status:** Greatest and longest-ruling Aravidu king (**29 years**)
  - Titles: "**Second Krishnadevaraya**", "Second Andhra Bhoja"
- **Vilapaka inscription (1601):** Genealogy of the Tuluva and Aravidu dynasties
- **Mughals:** Rejected **Akbar's** demand for submission (suzerainty)
- **Europeans:** Ties/alliance with the **Portuguese and Philip II of Spain**; let the **Dutch** trade at **Pulicat** (Dutch factory)
- **Jesuits:** Allowed churches at **Chandragiri and Nellore** (APPSC-G1 2022 key)
- **Muslims:** Villages to the saint **Babanathu** (Penukonda inscription)
- **Coins:** "Om Venkateshwaraya Namah"
- **Court:** **Appayya Dikshita** (**Shaivarka Mani Dipika**)
- **Military:** Defeated Veerappa Nayaka of Madurai at **Vallankota**
- ⚠ **Vilapaka** = Venkatapati II's (**1601**); **Siddhavatam** fort inscription = Venkatapati II's (**1605**)

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal
- **Current relevance:** Revisited (Jun 2026): Sadasiva Raya (**1542-1570**), Rama Raya's death at Talikota (Raksasa Tangadi), Caesar Frederick's **1567** visit, Tirumala's **1570** shift of capital to Penukonda
- **AP angle:** After **1565** empire's centre moved into AP (Penukonda, Chandragiri); Aravidus from Kurnool; Madras grant from a Chandragiri-era Nayaka
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Last Tuluva ruler = **Sadasiva**
  - First Aravidu ruler = **Tirumala** (crowned at about age **90**); founded the line in **1570** + moved capital to Penukonda
  - Last Aravidu = **Sriranga III**
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - Sadashiva's death: some notes give both **1570** and **1576**; Tirumala's accession dated **1570** (2026), after Sadashiva died
  - Sack as a "graveyard" (Sewell) → may be exaggerated
- **Significance:** After Talikota, centre of gravity shifted to the Telugu country (Penukonda, Chandragiri)

=== ROW 127 | Mughal Empire - Babur: Panipat, Khanwa, Chanderi, Ghaghra; Tuzuk-i-Baburi

## Babur → birth, descent, career and invitations | from: S0
- **Birth:** **1483**, **Farghana** (Central Asia)
- **Descent:** **Timur** (father's side) + **Chingiz Khan** (mother's side)

| **Year** | **Career step** |
|---|---|
| **1494** | Ruler of Farghana as a boy |
| - | Won and lost **Samarkand** |
| **1504** | Took **Kabul** |
| **1519** | First Indian raid (Bajaur, Bhera); some notes give **1522** |

| **Invited by** | **Who** |
|---|---|
| **Daulat Khan Lodi** | Governor of Punjab |
| **Alam Khan** | Ibrahim's uncle |
| **Rana Sanga** | Mewar |

## Panipat I (1526) → tactics, terms and FIRSTs | from: S1, S2
# 1. Terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Tulughma** | Flanking units wheel round to encircle the enemy; **Uzbek** origin |
| **Rumi method / araba** | Carts chained as a barrier for matchlockmen and guns = **Ottoman** field-defence system |

- ⚠ **Trap:** Common error says it used war elephants (Apr 2026 correction)
- **Artillery:** Commanded by Ottoman gunners **Ustad Ali** and **Mustafa**
- **Panipat numbers:** Babur's **12,000** vs about **1,00,000** of Ibrahim's troops
# 2. Gunpowder debate
- Panipat = first large-scale use of **field artillery** in North Indian warfare
- Firearms not wholly unknown in India before Babur
# 3. Gains
- **Founder:** **Babur**, after **Panipat I (1526)** → displaced the Lodis
- **Gains from Panipat:** **Delhi and Agra** (APPSC-GS 2023) + the treasure of the Lodis
- **Treasure:** Shared out lavishly → nickname **"Qalandar"**; treasury left depleted for Humayun

## Khanwa (1527) → coalition, religious appeal, title | from: S3
- **Coalition:** Rana Sanga's Rajput confederacy + Afghan **Mahmud Lodi** + **Hasan Khan Mewati**
- **Tactics:** Trenches and barricades vs Sanga's elephants; artillery decisive again
  - Sanga wounded → retreated
- **Religious appeal (before battle):** Declared a **jihad**; renounced wine; remitted the **tamgha** for Muslims
- **Title (after victory):** **"Ghazi"**
- **Result:** Khanwa broke Rajput power
- ⚠ Babur's titles: **"Qalandar"** = for sharing the Lodi treasure; **"Ghazi"** = after Khanwa

## Babur's death and empire → extent and limits | from: S4
- **Extent at death:** **Kabul** and Badakhshan → Punjab, Delhi, Agra → borders of **Bihar** (after Ghaghra)
- **Death:** **1530**, at Agra
  - Humayun took the throne on **29 Dec 1530**
  - Buried at **Kabul** (Bagh-e-Babur)
- **Limits of Babur's 4-year rule:**
  - Mughal authority had **shallow roots**
  - **Bengal** unconquered; Afghans still claimed Delhi
  - **Gujarat-Malwa** united under Bahadur Shah
  - No administrative system; treasury empty
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Mansabdari system** = **Akbar's**, not Babur's

## Books, buildings and coins → Tuzuk-i-Baburi, gardens, mosques | from: S5, S6
# 1. Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama)
- **Language:** **Chagatai Turki**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Mughal court language = **Persian**, not Turki (UPPSC 2025: A true, R false)
- **English translators:** **Leyden and Erskine** (**1826**); **Annette Beveridge**
- **On India:** **Five Muslim and two Hindu powers** → Hindu ones = **Vijayanagara and Mewar**
  - Praised **Krishnadevaraya** as the most powerful ruler
# 2. Buildings and coins
| **Work** | **Place** | **Feature** |
|---|---|---|
| **Ram Bagh (Aram Bagh)** | Agra | First Mughal garden |
| **Kabuli Bagh mosque** | **Panipat** | Commemorating the **1526** victory |
| Mosque | **Agra** | - |
| Silver **Shahrukhi** | Coinage | Timurid style |

## Universal angles | from: S7 | universal
- **Practice MCQ themes:** Tulughma, Rumi method, start of gunpowder artillery, Kabuli Bagh mosque
- **Significance:** Panipat I ended the Delhi Sultanate (Lodis = last Sultanate dynasty) → began Mughal rule
- **AP angle:** Babur contemporary of **Krishnadevaraya** + Golconda's **Sultan Quli**; Mughals did not interfere in the Deccan in his time
- **FIRST/LAST:**
  - Ibrahim Lodi = **last Delhi Sultan**
  - Panipat I = first major field-artillery battle in North India

=== ROW 128 | Humayun and Sher Shah Suri - Sur administration, land revenue, sarais, currency, Grand Trunk Road

## Babur → origin, memoir, tactics, buildings | from: S0
- **Origin:** Born in **Farghana** (Central Asia)
  - Descended from **Timur** (father's side) + **Genghis Khan** (mother's side)
  - Had held **Samarkand** and **Kabul**
- **Memoir:** **Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama)** in **Chaghatai Turkish**; praises Krishnadevaraya
- **After Panipat:** Occupied **Delhi and Agra** (APPSC 2023)

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Tulughma** | Rapid cavalry flanking and encircling |
| **Rumi (Ottoman) method** | Carts linked into a barrier sheltering matchlockmen and artillery |

- **FIRST:** First large-scale use of **gunpowder artillery** in Indian warfare
- **Buildings:**
  - **Kabuli Bagh Mosque** (Panipat) to mark 1526
  - Introduced the **Charbagh**
  - **Ram Bagh**, Agra (first Mughal garden)

## Humayun → accession, battles, exile, return, death | from: S1, S3
# 1. Accession and problems
- **Accession:** **29 Dec 1530**, three days after Babur's death
- **Problems:**
  - Rival brothers **Kamran, Askari, Hindal**
  - Afghans
  - **Bahadur Shah** of Gujarat-Malwa
  - Empty treasury
# 2. Battles in order
| **Battle / event** | **Outcome** |
|---|---|
| **Dourah** | Beat the Afghans; drove **Mahmud Lodi** from **Jaunpur**; lifted siege of **Kalinjar** |
| **Gujarat (1535)** | Pressed by Humayun, Bahadur Shah let the **Portuguese** fortify **Diu** |
| Sher Khan's rise | Won at **Surajgarh**; took **Gaur** (**1538**) |
| **Chausa (1539)** | Defeated by Sher Khan |
| **Kannauj / Bilgram (1540)** | Decisive defeat → exile |

- Kannauj and Bilgram = one battle (1540), a Sher Khan victory
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes list "Bilgram (1540)" as a Humayun victory and a "Battle of Samugarh (1555) Humayun vs Hemu" - both WRONG; **Samugarh was 1658** (Aurangzeb vs Dara)
# 3. Exile
- Married **Hamida Banu Begum**; **Akbar** born at **Amarkot** (**1542**)
- Went to **Persia**; Safavid **Shah Tahmasp of Persia** helped him retake **Kandahar and Kabul**; alliance with the Safavids
# 4. Return and death
- **Return:** **July 1555**, after Sher Shah's death, exploiting quarrels among his successors
  - Defeated **Sikandar Sur at Machhiwara and Sirhind**, then took **Delhi and Agra**
- **Death:** **January 1556**, fall from the stairs of the **Sher Mandal** library in the **Purana Qila**
- **Humayun-nama:** By his sister **Gulbadan Begum**

## Sher Shah and the Sur successors → rise, titles, conquests | from: S2, S3
# 1. Sher Shah
- **Origin:** Born **Farid**, of the Afghan **Sur** tribe; studied at **Jaunpur**; managed father's jagir from **1511**
- **Title "Sher Khan":** Given by **Bahar Khan** of Bihar for slaying a tiger
- **Early career:** Served Babur → deputy regent (de facto ruler) of Bihar

| **Step** | **Event** |
|---|---|
| **Surajgarh** | Turning point; took Gaur (1538) |
| **Chausa (1539)** | Assumed title **Sher Shah** |
| **Kannauj (1540)** | Took the throne |

- **Conquests:**
  - **Malwa**
  - **Raisen** (Puran Mal)
  - **Marwar:** **Maldeo of Marwar** resisted; hard-fought battle of **Samel** (**Sammel/Giri-Sumel**, **1544**) → Maldeo's generals **Jaita and Kumpa** fought so hard that Sher Shah said he nearly lost Hindustan "for a handful of bajra"
- **Death:** **1545**, gunpowder explosion during siege of **Kalinjar**
- ⚠ Titles: **"Sher Khan"** = from Bahar Khan (tiger); **"Sher Shah"** = assumed after **Chausa (1539)**
# 2. Sur successors and Hemu
- **Islam Shah:** **Jalal Khan**, **1545-53**
- **Later rivals:** **Muhammad Adil Shah** (east) and **Sikandar Sur**, fighting each other
- **Hemu:**
  - Adil Shah's Hindu minister-general
  - Seized **Agra and Delhi** (1556); title **"Vikramaditya"**
  - Lost **Panipat II (5 Nov 1556)**: arrow struck his eye → captured; Afghan power ended

## Sher Shah's land revenue → measurement, terms, legacy | from: S5
# 1. Measurement and assessment
- **Measurement:** Land **measured** under a **uniform system** of survey
  - Yard = **gaz-i-Sikandari** (sikandari gaz); rope/jarib
- **Classification:** **Three categories** (good, middling, bad) by produce
- **State share:** **One-third** of average produce; payment in **cash or grain**
  - Some notes say "one-fourth to one-third"
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC-G1 2024 key → statements I (three categories) and II (uniform measurement) true; "one-fourth" false
# 2. Documents, officials and fees
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Patta** | Title deed stating each peasant's revenue/dues; given to the cultivator |
| **Qabuliyat** | Peasant's deed of agreement/acceptance of the demand; signed by him |
| **Amin** | Assessment official (with shiqdar, qanungo, patwari) |
| **Jaribana** | Surveyor's fee |
| **Muhasilana** | Collector's fee |

- ⚠ **Patta** = given by the state; **Qabuliyat** = signed by the peasant
# 3. Nature and legacy
- **Direct settlement:** State-peasant settlement with cultivators; illegal demands removed; tenant rights recognised
  - Some notes call Sher Shah the first to introduce a **ryotwari**-type system
- **Legacy:** Model for **Todar Mal's Bandobast**

## Sher Shah's currency, roads and sarais → coins, routes, dak-chowkis | from: S6, S7
# 1. Currency
| **Coin** | **Metal** | **Note** |
|---|---|---|
| **Rupiya** | **Silver** | About **178 grains**[GK]; introduced **1540-45** (APPSC-GS 2019; PYQ); remained standard for centuries (APPSC-G2 2017) |
| **Dam** | Copper | Introduced copper coinage (APP) |
| Mohur / ashrafi | Gold | Pure gold coins (APP) |

- **Currency reform:** Minted **pure** gold and silver coins; reform outlived the **Sur** dynasty
# 2. Roads
- **Sadak-e-Azam / Grand Trunk Road:** **Restored/rebuilt** by Sher Shah; **Sonargaon (Bengal) to the Indus**; still exists
  - APPSC-G1 2022 answer: "Indus to Sonargaon"; APPSC-GS 2018: he restored it
  - Some notes say Chittagong-Kabul, about **1,500 miles**
- **Other roads:** Agra-Burhanpur; Agra-Jodhpur-Chittor; Lahore-Multan
# 3. Sarais
- **Location:** Built along roads, with **shady trees** planted, for travellers
- **Lodging:** Separate for Hindus and Muslims
- **Dak-chowkis:** Sarais served as **dak-chowkis** (post stations; news and mail relays, with horses)
- **Effect:** Market towns grew around them

## Universal angles | from: S10 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - Sher Shah's short reign (**1540-45**) set the pattern for Mughal land revenue (via **Todar Mal**), coinage (the **rupee**) and roads
  - Sher Shah's rupiya, roads and revenue system = base for Akbar's administration
- **Comparative note:**
  - Sher Shah = **1/3** of average produce + **three** land classes
  - Akbar-Todar Mal = kept **1/3** but used **ten-year averages** + **four** land classes (**polaj, parauti, chachar, banjar**)
- **FIRST/LAST:** **Rupiya** began with Sher Shah; Surs = last Afghan dynasty to rule Delhi
- **Current relevance:** PMF IAS practice item (**Apr 2026**) tested First Panipat: tulughma, Rumi method, gunpowder artillery, Kabuli Bagh Mosque
- **AP angle:** **Sultan Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda (**1518-43**) contemporary with Babur, Humayun and Sher Shah; Mughals did not interfere in the Deccan then

## Revenue systems → terms and land classes | from: S3
- **Raja Todar Mal:** Akbar's revenue minister; earlier served Sher Shah
  - Built the **Zabti / Bandobast** (dahsala) system, modelled on Sher Shah's survey-based system

| **System** | **Features** |
|---|---|
| **Zabti / Bandobast / Dahsala** | Designed by **Raja Todar Mal** (1580); land measured with the bamboo **Gaz-i-Ilahi** (about 82 cm); revenue = **one-third** of **average produce and prices of the last 10 years**, paid in cash |
| **Batai / Ghallabakshi** | Crop-sharing: harvest divided between state and peasant |
| **Kankut** | Revenue fixed by **estimation** of the standing crop by eye/sample |
| **Nasaq** | Rough assessment from the peasant's past payments |

| **Land class under Zabti** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Polaj | Cultivated every year |
| Parauti | Fallow 1-2 years |
| Chachar | Fallow 3-4 years |
| Banjar | Uncultivated 5 years or more |

- **Relief:** **Taccavi** loans + remissions in famine or floods
- ⚠ **Gaz-i-Ilahi** = bamboo measure of Todar Mal's Zabti (1580), about 82 cm

## Universal angles | from: S13 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - **Sulh-i-Kul** + Rajput partnership turned a conquest state into a durable empire; underpinned Rajput and Hindu support
  - Aurangzeb's reversal (jizya **1679**) alienated Rajputs, Marathas, Sikhs; cited among causes of decline
  - **Mansabdari-jagirdari** and **Zabti** = core of Mughal governance for **150 years**
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - Din-i-Ilahi: new religion or a discipleship order? Only a handful joined; lapsed with Akbar
  - Under Aurangzeb, too many mansabdars chased too few jagirs (**jagirdari crisis**) → pressed the peasantry
- **Comparative note:** Akbar abolished jizya **1564**; Aurangzeb reimposed it **1679**
- **AP angle:**
  - Golconda's **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** kept friendly relations with Akbar (who sent **Masud Beg** with gifts)
  - **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (Golconda): religious tolerance; appointed **Madanna and Akkanna** to high office; **Bhakta Ramadasu** was their nephew
- **Current relevance:**
  - **Sirhind Fateh Diwas (12-14 May)** commemorates Banda Singh Bahadur's **1710** conquest (May 2026)
  - Guru Tegh Bahadur's martyrdom = **Shaheedi Divas, 24 Nov** (Apr 2026)
  - **Baisakhi** recalled as Khalsa foundation day, **13 April 1699** (14 Apr 2026)
