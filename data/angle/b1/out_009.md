=== ROW 47 | Satavahana administration, economy, coinage and Prakrit literature (Hala's Gathasaptasati)

## Satavahana kings and queens → titles and contributions | from: S0, S14, S15, S18, S19
# 1. Nature of kingship
- **Ideal:** King = **upholder of dharma** (Dharmashastra ideal); king upheld Dharmashastras
- **Titles:** **Maharaja, Raja-Raja**
- **Nature:** Hereditary + patriarchal; decentralised vs the Mauryas
- **Polity:** **semi-feudal** (see feudatories)
- **Dynasty's caste:** **Brahmins**
# 2. Ruler → title / contribution
| **Ruler / queen** | **Title / contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Gautamiputra Satakarni** | **matronymics** (maternal gotra names) from him; titles **Ekabrahmana** + **Varnasankarya nirodhaka** (stopper of the mixing of varnas) → stopped **varna-sankara** (mixing) |
| **Kuntala Satakarni** (13th king) | Made **Sanskrit** the royal language; court of Gunadhya and Sarvavarma; **Katantra** composed for him |
| **Hala** (17th king) | Title **"Kavivatsala"** (G2 2008); compiled the Gathasaptasati in **Maharashtri Prakrit**; married Sinhala princess **Lilavati** |
| Yajna Sri | **Harshacharita** (Banabhatta): calls Yajna Sri **"Trisamudradhipati"** (lord of three seas) |
- **Gautamiputra's titles:** show Brahmanical ideology amid mobility of Sakas/Yavanas absorbed into society
- **Hala's court:** Honoured poets **Kumarila and Sripalita**; his reign = **golden age of Prakrit**
- **Hala's marriage:** Sinhala princess **Lilavati** at the **Bhimeswara temple, Draksharamam** (Saptagodavari)
  - Recorded in **Lilavati Parinayam** (Kutuhala, Prakrit), which also says a Satavahana king patronised Nagarjuna
# 3. Queen mothers
- **Naganika** → regent for her minor sons; issued the Naneghat inscription
- **Gautami Balasri** → issued the Nasik inscription in her grandson Pulumavi's reign
- ⚠ **Kavivatsala** = **Hala**; **Ekabrahmana** = **Gautamiputra**; **Trisamudradhipati** = **Yajna Sri**

## Feudatories → titles and grading | from: S4
- **Polity:** Semi-feudal → **Samanta rajyas (Janapadas)** paid annual tribute + sent troops
  - Examples: **Chutus in Mysore, Kuras in Kolhapur, Ikshvakus in Vijayapuri**

| **Feudatory title** | **Feature** |
|---|---|
| **Raja** | Highest feudatory - could **issue coins** |
| **Maharathis** | Feudatory title; hereditary; collected and remitted taxes; western Deccan |
| **Mahabhojas** | Powerful local feudatory chiefs; hereditary; Konkan region; inscriptions omit the emperor's name → near-independence |
| **Mahatalavaras / Mahasenadhipatis** | Generals in charge of border provinces (later phase) → grew into feudal lords |

- **Maharathi marriage ties:** **Naganika** = daughter of Maharathi **Tranakayiro**
- **Maharathi coins:** Maharathi **Sadakana Chutukadananda** issued his own coins
- **Grading:** **Raja** (could issue coins) > **Mahabhoja** > **Senapati**
- **After the fall:** Many feudatories became independent

## Taxes and tax-free land grants → terms and FIRSTs | from: S6, S7
# 1. Tax terms
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Bhaga** | Land revenue - king's share of the harvest - **1/6th** (**1/6** of produce) |
| Deya-meya (APP: "Deevameeya") / Bhodga | King's share of produce, collected in kind |
| Kara | General tax / levy [GK] |
| **Karukara** | Tax paid by artisans / professionals |

- **Chief income:** **Land revenue**
- **Other income:** Tribute from feudatories + trade taxes
- **Collection:** in **cash and kind**
# 2. FIRST — tax-free land grants
- **First:** Satavahanas = **first to make tax-free land grants (agraharas) to Brahmanas and Buddhist monks** (NextGen CA 2026; UPSC)
  - **earliest land grants** in India: **tax-free villages to Brahmins and Buddhist monks**
- **Significance:** Root of the later early-medieval feudal order
- **Examples:**
  - **Naneghat**: Satakarni I gave land, cows (godhana) and gold to Brahmins
  - **Gautamiputra**: village at **Tirasmri** to monks + **100 nivartanas** of his own land
- **Immunities (Gautamiputra's Nasik grants):**
  - Royal officers and soldiers could not enter
  - No digging for salt
  - Free of district police and royal dues
- ⚠ **Bhaga** = land revenue (**1/6**); **Karukara** = artisans' tax

## Agriculture and guilds → crops, crafts and banking | from: S8, S9
# 1. Agriculture and crops
- **Expansion:** Cultivation in the **Krishna-Godavari basin**
- **Technique:** **Paddy transplantation**
- **Main crops:** **rice and cotton**
- **Irrigation:** **ghatiyantras** → made by the Udayantrika guild
- **Impact:** Farm surplus fed urban growth, trade and crafts
# 2. Guilds — definition and functions
- **Definition:** Shreni = organisation of **traders and artisans** of one craft; guilds = **shrenis (Srenulu)**
- **Head:** **jetthaka** (Pali); Satavahana-era notes → president **Shreshthi** (also **Sethi / Shreshthi**, **Shetti**)
- **Functions:** Fixed **rules of work, quality standards, prices and wages**; controlled members' conduct
- **Registration:** **not registered with a central state authority** (UPSC 2012: statements 2 and 3 correct)
- **Spread:** Across India, **not only in the north** (RPSC 2018)
# 3. Guild → craft
| **Guild** | **Craft** |
|---|---|
| **Kolika** | **Weavers** (textiles) |
| **Padhika** | **Butchers** (Katikavaru) |
| Tailika / Tilapishaka [GK] | Oil pressers |
| Udayantrika / Adyantrika | Water-machines (ghatiyantras) |
| Gandhika | Perfumers (later: traders in general) |
| Kulara / Kularika [GK] | Potters |
| Malakara | Florists |
| Sarthavaha | Long-distance / foreign traders |

- **Vadhika:** work of carpenters (some notes)
# 4. Guild banking
- **Banking role:** Acted as **banks** → took deposits, paid interest to monasteries; interest on deposits **12%** (APPSC 2019)
- **Nasik inscription of Ushavadata** → deposits with the **Govardhana weavers' guild**:
  - **2,000 karshapanas at 1% a month = 12% a year** → interest bought robes for monks (12 karshapanas per monk)
  - 1,000 karshapanas at 3/4% a month → monks' other needs
> Exam angle: rate of interest (12%), which inscription (Nasik), which guild (Kolika = weavers; Padhika = butchers).

## Coins → metals, issuers, motifs and FIRSTs | from: S13
# 1. Metals and denominations
- **Metals:** **lead (most common)**, **potin** (copper-tin-lead alloy), **copper, bronze** and **silver** (NextGen CA 2026 adds silver)
- **Silver coin:** **Silver coin = karshapana**
- **Gold:** **No gold coins** issued by the Satavahanas; gold used only as bullion
  - Some notes list gold "Suvarnas" among their coins
- **Exchange:** **1 gold coin = 35 karshapanas** (Rishabhadatta inscription)
- **Not Satavahana:** Iron coins with copper coating → **Shalankayanas** (guide keyword); not in your passages, so check the Salankayana notes
# 2. Coin type → issuer
| **Coin type** | **Issuer / fact** |
|---|---|
| Ship-motif coins | **Vasishthiputra Pulumavi** (NextGen CA 2026) |
| **ship-with-anchor coins of Yajnasri Satakarni** | **Yajna Sri Satakarni** → maritime trade with Siam, Java, Borneo |
| **Portrait coins** | Indigenous innovation (UPSC); began with **Gautamiputra Satakarni**; silver portrait coins common after him |
| Restruck coins | GPS **restruck Nahapana's silver coins** → Jogalthambi hoard, **13,270** coins |
| **bilingual coins (Prakrit + Dravidian)** (G2 2025 key) | Silver portrait coins of later rulers, Prakrit on one side, Dravidian language on the other |

- **Ship coins:** Some notes credit Pulumavi, others name coins of both kings
- **Last:** No later ruler issued ship coins (G2 2025)
- **Legends:** in **Prakrit, Brahmi script**
# 3. Motifs, shapes, technique and mint
- **Motifs:** elephant, horse, lion, camel, **chaitya, dharmachakra**
  - Reverse → **Ujjain symbol** (a cross with four circles)
  - Other symbols: bull, ship, swastika, triratna
- **Shapes:** round, square, rectangular
- **Technique:** Punch-marked coins first → then die-struck
- **Mint:** **Kondapur (Medak)** → legend "Siri Chimuka Sata"
- ⚠ **Lead** = most common; **No gold coins**; iron coins with copper coating = **Shalankayanas**, not Satavahanas

## Society and women → evidence | from: S15, S16, S18

- **Status:** Women **respected**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Women were looked down upon" = incorrect option (G2 2008)

  - Queens **Naganika** and **Gautami Balasri** issued inscriptions

  - Independent donations at **Bharhut, Sanchi and Amaravati** without naming husbands or fathers

## Gathasaptasati, Brihatkatha and Katantra → features | from: S18, S19
# 1. Gathasaptasati
- **Nature:** An anthology of **700 Prakrit gathas** (verses), mainly **love and romance**, rural life and nature (G2 2025)
  - Most important literary source for Satavahana society
  - Opens with a prayer to Shiva
- **Women contributors:** **Anulakshmi, Anupalabdha, Reva (Revati), Madhavi**
  - Answer to "which text has contributions by women" (APPSC 2017 key; G2 2017)
- **Navarasas:** First literary work covering all **Navarasas**
- ⚠ **Trap:** Gathasaptasati is **not** a source for early Satavahana political history (APPSC 2017)
# 2. Brihatkatha
- **Brihatkatha language:** **Paishachi** (not Sanskrit, G2 2025)
- **Size:** **7,000 verses in 7 stories**; original lost
- **Content:** Mentions slavery
- **Legend:**
  - Gunadhya = court poet of **Kuntala Satakarni**
  - Quarrelled with Sarvavarma over the king learning Sanskrit → lost, gave up Sanskrit, wrote in Paishachi
  - King ignored the work → he began **burning it**
# 3. Katantra
- **Katantra — nature:** **short Sanskrit grammar**
- **Katantra — patron:** **Kuntala Satakarni**
- **Katantra — purpose:** So the king could learn Sanskrit quickly
- ⚠ **700** gathas = Gathasaptasati; **7,000** verses in **7** stories = Brihatkatha

# 3. Chaitya, vihara, arama
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Chaitya** | A prayer hall, rectangular or apsidal, with pillars + stupa at the far end |
| **Vihara** | Monastery with rooms/cells around a central hall (Madhyashala) → fortnightly **Uposatha** meetings (every 15 days) |
| **Arama** | Complex with stupa, vihara, chaitya and school together |

- **Chaitya plan:** **Rectangular hall ending in an apse** (horseshoe plan)
  - **Rows of pillars on both sides** forming aisles
  - **Stupa at the apse end**

  - **Barrel-vaulted roof** copying wooden ribs
  - **Horseshoe (chaitya) window** on the facade
  - Screen walls shield the interior (UPSC)
- **Guntupalli:** "house viharas"
- ⚠ **4** platforms × **5** pillars; **Horse** = Great Departure; **Wheel** = first sermon; **Stupa** = Mahaparinirvana

## Amaravati Mahachaitya → patrons, phases, size and themes | from: S0, S2

## Art schools → region, material, phases and comparison | from: S9, S11
# 1. Amaravati school — region, period and material
- **Region:** Developed on its own in the **lower Krishna-Godavari valley** (Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda)
- **Period:** **2nd century BCE to the 3rd century CE**, under **Satavahana** patronage
  - Final, most mature phase under the **Ikshvakus**
- **Material:** **white marble-like limestone**; Ikshvaku phase also used green stone
# 2. Style and themes
- **Style:**
  - **narrative reliefs** of the Buddha's life and the **Jatakas** → movement and emotion
  - Creepers, vines, flowers; Buddha on a lotus in padmasana
- **Themes:** Jataka panels, Dharmachakra Parivartana, Buddha at Gaya
- **Influence:** Amaravati school → Sri Lanka and SE Asia
# 3. Phases of depiction
- **Early phase:** Buddha shown aniconically, only through **symbols** (see the Ayaka symbol table) → bodhi tree, empty throne, footprints, wheel, stupa
- **Later phase:** Buddha in **human form**, seated on a lotus with dancing Gandharvas and Yakshas
# 4. Ivory and wall paintings
- **Ivory:**
  - Guilds of **ivory workers** donated at Amaravati
  - Ivory carvers of **Vidisha** carved the Sanchi gateway
- **Wall paintings:** Gathasaptasati says people painted mythological scenes on house walls
