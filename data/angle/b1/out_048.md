=== ROW 224 | Government of India Act 1919 - dyarchy, Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms; Rowlatt Act

## Montagu Declaration and GoI Act 1919 → date, provisions | from: S0, S2
# 1. Montagu Declaration, 1917
- **Date:** **20 August 1917**, in Parliament
- **Text:** "Gradual development of self-governing institutions with a view to the **progressive realisation of responsible government** in India as an integral part of the British Empire"
- **Significance:** First official recognition of self-government as an aim; prompted by **Home Rule Leagues**
- **Tour:** Montagu + Chelmsford toured India in **Dec 1917**
# 2. GoI Act 1919 — basics
- **Named after:** **Edwin Montagu** (Secretary of State) + **Lord Chelmsford** (Viceroy)
- **Report:** Announced **July 1918**; Act of the UK Parliament
# 3. Centre and franchise
- **Centre:** **Bicameral** - **Council of State** (upper) + **Legislative Assembly** (lower); mostly elected
  - Assembly = 145 members (104 elected); Council of State = 60
- **Executive Council:** **3 of 6** members (other than Commander-in-Chief/C-in-C) Indians
- **Franchise:** limited, based on **property, tax and education**
- **Communal representation:** Extended (Sikhs etc.)
- **Review clause:** Act required a **statutory commission after 10 years** to review its working

## Terms → meaning: dyarchy, reserved and transferred subjects | from: S2
- **Dyarchy:** Dual government in the **provinces**
  - Subjects split into central and provincial lists
  - **Provincial dyarchy**: **transferred** subjects (e.g. local self-government) run by Governor with ministers
  - **Provincial budgets separated** from the central budget
| **Provincial subjects** | **Run by** | **Answerable to** |
|---|---|---|
| **Reserved** (e.g. law and order, finance, land revenue [GK]) | Governor + Executive Council | Not to the legislature |
| **Transferred** (e.g. education, health, local self-government [GK]) | Governor + **Ministers** from the elected legislature | Legislature |
- ⚠ **Trap:** Dyarchy = **dual (not single) government** by **Montagu and Chelmsford** (APPSC 2019: "dyarchy = single government" false; only statement B correct)
- ⚠ **Trap:** UPSC notes wrongly call this "provincial autonomy" - that came with the 1935 Act

## FIRSTs and bodies created under the 1919 Act | from: S2, S3
- **Direct elections:** **GoI Act 1919** was the first Act to introduce **direct elections**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** APPSC GS 2018 key gave **Indian Councils Act 1909**
  - 1909 introduced **elected** non-official members, largely via indirect and separate electorates
  - If 1919 is not among the options, mark 1909
| **Body / office** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Public Service Commission** | Provided in **Section 96(C)** (APPSC GS 2022); set up **1 October 1926** after the Lee Commission |
| **High Commissioner for India** | Office created, in **London** |
| **Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal)** | Created **1921** under the 1919 reforms by royal proclamation; consultative body of rulers |
| **ICS exams** | Held simultaneously in India from **1922** (Allahabad, later Delhi) |
| **Legislature officers** | **President** + **Deputy President** |
- **First President:** **Frederick Whyte** (1921)
- **First Deputy President:** **Sachchidananda Sinha**

## GoI Act 1935 → structure and provisions | from: S7
# 1. Structure & basis
- **Size:** Longest Act = **321 sections and 10 schedules** (some notes: "14 parts, 321 Articles")
- **Basis:** Simon Report + three RTCs + **White Paper**
# 2. Key provisions
- **All-India Federation:** provinces + princely states; optional for states → never came into being
- **Dyarchy at the Centre:** never operated
- **Provincial autonomy:** provincial dyarchy abolished; began **April 1937**
  - Bicameral legislatures (**Vidhan Parishads**) in some provinces
  - **India Council** abolished
- **Federal Court:** at Delhi (1937)
- **PSCs:** **Federal PSC and Provincial PSCs**
- **Local self-government:** became a **provincial subject** (1919: transferred subject)
- **RBI:**
  - RBI Act **1934** (Hilton Young Commission, 1926)
  - Started **1 April 1935**; nationalised 1 Jan 1949
- ⚠ **Dyarchy:** 1919 = in the provinces; 1935 = provincial dyarchy abolished, Centre dyarchy never operated

=== ROW 227 | Growth of communal politics - separate electorates, Two-Nation theory, Lahore Resolution 1940

## Acts → separate electorates, 1909 to 1935 | from: S0
| **Act/event** | **Step / communities given separate representation** |
|---|---|
| **Indian Councils Act 1909** (Morley-Minto) | Separate electorates for **Muslims** - only Muslims voted for Muslim candidates, plus **weightage** (more seats than their population share); legalised communalism; Minto "Father of Communal Electorate" |
| **Lucknow Pact 1916** | Congress accepts them, plus **weightage** and a communal veto (three-fourths rule) |
| **GoI Act 1919** | Extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans (UPSC: "extended") |
| **Communal Award 1932** | Extended to depressed classes; Poona Pact replaced this with reserved seats in joint electorates |
| **GoI Act 1935** | Further extended to **depressed classes (SCs), women and labour**; Muslims given **33%** of seats in the Federal Legislature; continued till the Constituent Assembly |
- **Jinnah's Point 5 (1929):** separate electorates to continue; any community free to opt for joint electorates
- **1909 impact:** "legally institutionalised communalism" → seed of the Two-Nation Theory
- **Muslim League (1906):** came up in the run-up to 1909
> Exam angle: APPSC-G2 2025 - "Which Act introduced the communal electorate?" = **Morley-Minto Reforms**.
