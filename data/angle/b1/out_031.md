=== ROW 146 | Sufism - doctrines, khanqah, sama; silsilas - Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi

## Sufism — meaning, origin, FIRSTs and Sufis vs Ulema | from: S0, S4
# 1. Meaning and origin
- **Origin of 'Sufi':** From **Suf** (coarse wool), Safa (purity) or Greek Sophia
- **Beliefs:** Accepted the Quran and the Prophet; absorbed Vedanta, Yoga, Neo-Platonism
# 2. FIRSTs
| **FIRST** | **FACT** |
|---|---|
| First prominent Sufi in India | **Ali Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh)** - author of **Kashf-ul-Mahjub** |
| Most popular order | **Chishti** |
# 3. Sufis vs Ulema
- **Ulema contrast:**
  - Ulema = legalistic orthodoxy + the Shariat
  - Sufis = inner experience over ritual; freely used music, which orthodox Islam did not generally sanction
# 4. Ba-shara vs be-shara orders
- **Ba-shara:** Followed Islamic law (Sharia) - Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, Firdausi
- **Be-shara:** Not bound by it - wandering **Qalandars**, Madaris
- **Coexistence:** Both coexisted in India

## Sufi terms → meaning | from: S1, S2, S7
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Khanqah** | Sufi hospice where disciples lived and trained; it also gave food and shelter to the poor (training and service) |
| **Sama** | Devotional music meant to produce ecstasy; raqs = ritual dance ('Majlis-i-Sama' of Chishtis and Suhrawardis) |
| **Pir / Murshid** | Spiritual master / guide; the pir-murid bond (preceptorship) is called **Piri-Muridi** |
| **Murid** | Disciple pledged in absolute submission to the pir |
| **Wali** | Friend of God, a saint [GK] |
| **Dargah** | Shrine at a saint's tomb [GK] |
| **Urs** | Death anniversary of a saint, seen as the soul's union with God [GK] |
| **Zikr** | Continuous remembrance and repetition of God's name |
| **Fana / Baqa** | Annihilation of the self (the ego) in God / subsistence in God |
| **Ba-shara / Be-shara** | Sufis who follow the Shariat / Sufis outside it (e.g. qalandars) [GK] |
| **Tariqa** | Spiritual path through stages; also means an order (Silsila / Tariqa = order / path) |
| **Chilla** | Solitude or retreat |
| **Malfuzat** | Recorded conversations |
| **Maktubat** | Letters |
| **Tazkiras** | Biographies of saints |
- ⚠ **malfuzat** = conversations; **maktubat** = letters; **tazkiras** = biographies

## Doctrines, stages and practices — Wahdat-ul-Wujud, maqamat, yogic borrowings | from: S2, S3
# 1. Doctrines
| **Doctrine** | **Meaning** | **Associated with** |
|---|---|---|
| **Wahdat-ul-Wujud** | "**Unity of Being**": Creator (Haq) and creation (Khalq) are one | Ibn Arabi [GK]; followed by the Qadiris and Chishtis |
| **Wahdat-ush-Shuhud** | "**Unity of Appearance/Witness**": creation only reflects God | **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi** (Naqshbandi) - his counter-doctrine |
- ⚠ **Wujud** = Being (Ibn Arabi, Qadiri, Chishti); **Shuhud** = Appearance (Sirhindi, Naqshbandi)
# 2. Stages
- **10 stages (maqamat):** From **Tauba** (repentance) → Wara, Zuhd, Faqr, Sabr, Shukr, Khauf, Raja, Tawakkul → **Riza** (submission)
# 3. Practices and yogic borrowings
- **Practices:** zikr, meditation, fasting, asceticism, sama, solitude or retreat (chilla)
- **Amritakund:** Hatha-yogic text translated into Arabic and Persian in the **13th c.**
  - Afterwards **breath control** vital in almost every order **except the Naqshbandis**
- **Nath contact:** Sufis met the **Kanphata (Nath) yogis** of Gorakhnath's tradition
- **Alberuni:** Noted similarities between Sufi ideas and **Patanjali's Yoga Sutra** + the **Gita**

=== ROW 148 | Interaction and impact of the Bhakti and Sufi movements on society and language

## Bhakti and Sufi — common principles | from: S0
| **Principle** | **Bhakti** | **Sufi** | **Common?** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monotheism** (one God, many names) | One God (Kabir: Ram, Rahim, Allah are one); Nirguna saints | **Tawhid**; Wahdat-ul-Wujud | Yes |
| **Asceticism**, simplicity, self-purification | Renunciation, simple life | Self-purification; the maqamat of Tauba, Faqr and Zuhd | Yes |
| **Religious tolerance**, universal love | Kabir and Nanak criticised both Hindu and Muslim orthodoxy | Love and the unity of humanity | Yes |
| Guru / pir (guide-disciple bond) | Guru is central | **Pir-murid** bond is central | Yes |
| Devotion over ritual | Bhakti over Vedic ritual and priests | Inner experience over the formal Shariat | Yes |
| Idol worship | Bhakti saints (Kabir, Nanak) criticised it | Sufis rejected it | **No** |
- **Common principles:** **I, II and III only** (monotheism, asceticism, tolerance) (APPSC-G1 2022)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** **Idol worship was NOT common** - Sufis and Nirguna saints rejected it
- **Also shared:** Vernacular preaching, spiritual equality of all castes
> Exam angle: APPSC 2022 and 2023 both keyed **I, II and III only** (monotheism, asceticism, tolerance) - "accepted idol worship" is the trap.

## Outcomes, limitations and debates | from: S2, S9
# 1. Outcomes
- **Shared shrines:** Sufi shrines = common religious and cultural centres; rulers sought Sufi blessings for legitimacy
- **Result:** Composite / syncretic **Indo-Islamic culture**
- **Political echo:** Early Sikh and Maratha awakening
# 2. Limitations
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Caste** | **Caste inequalities could not be eradicated** despite Srivaishnavism (Ramanuja) and Veerashaivism |
| **Veerashaiva violence** | At times turned to **physical violence (Himsa)**; spread by the "Sivapasa" (sword) |
| **Sectarianisation** | Later hardened into separate sects and panths |
| **Orthodox pushback** | Naqshbandi orthodoxy against Sufi-Bhakti liberalism |

- Medieval norms grew more restrictive for women and lower castes: purdah, limited education and work

## Universal angles | from: S10 | universal
- **Current relevance:** Profile of Punjabi Sufi poet **Bulleh Shah** (Feb 2026)
  - Put Tariqat over rigid Shariat; followed Wahdat-al-Wujud
  - Wrote in the **Kafi** style blending Sufism with Bhakti-Vedantic ideas
  - Guide: lower-caste saint **Shah Inayat Qadiri**
- **Significance:**
  - Early feelings of national awakening among the Marathas (Tukaram, Ramdas and Shivaji) and the Sikhs
  - Revived Hinduism around devotion; weakened priestly dominance; built a composite culture
- **Criticism & debates:** Egalitarian preaching did not dismantle caste; sects often hardened into new communities
- **AP angle:** Pre-colonial reform line - Veerashaivism / Veerasaivism, Srivaishnavism, Brahmanayudu (Brahmanaidu's Chaapakudu), Vemana, Veerabrahmam, Nasaraiah, Molla

=== ROW 150 | Age of discovery and the sea route to India - Vasco da Gama; motives of European expansion

## Motives of European expansion → factor and effect | from: S0
- **Pre-15th century route:** Europe → Constantinople → Central Asia → India = most important

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Commodities** | **Spices** (pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, ginger, cloves, nutmeg), silk; Indian textiles in huge demand |
| **Monopoly to break** | Asian trade controlled by **Arabs**; **Italy** (Venice, Genoa) the distribution centre |
| **Fall of Constantinople (1453)** | Ottoman **Mehmed II** captured **Constantinople** → main overland route closed (some notes say 1452) |
| Other motives | Religion (spread of Christianity), wealth and prestige, rivalry among Spain, Portugal, Netherlands, England, France; curiosity |
- **Quote:** "the commerce of India is the commerce of the world" = **Peter the Great** of Russia (APPSC-G2 2017)

## Explorers, navigation and treaties → sponsor, year and provision | from: S1
# 1. Navigational advances
- **Prince Henry "the Navigator":** Portugal; set up naval training centres; first European to actively promote navigation
- **Ships and instruments:** **Caravel** + **astrolabe** made long voyages safer (UPSC)
# 2. Explorers → discovery and sponsoring country
| **Explorer** | **Sponsor** | **Achievement** |
|---|---|---|

| John Cabot | England [GK] | North America (1497) [GK] |
| Vasco da Gama | Portugal | Sea route to India 1498 |

## Vasco da Gama — voyage, arrival at Calicut, Zamorin, later voyages | from: S2
# 1. Voyage
- **Sponsor:** **King Manuel I**
- **Departure:** **Lisbon on 8 July 1497**, four ships (São Gabriel, São Rafael, Berrio + supply ship)
- **Route:** **Lisbon - Cape of Good Hope - East African coast - Calicut**
  - Guided by trader **Abdul Majid** (some notes say Najit) to **Calicut (Kozhikode)**
- **Arrival ships:** **three ships** - São Gabriel (Vasco), São Rafael (Paulo da Gama), Berrio (Nicolau Coelho)
# 2. Arrival and the Zamorin
- **Arrival:** Reached **Calicut (Kozhikode) on 17 May 1498**; APPSC-GS 2019 key uses **20 May 1498** (landing date; many books)
  - Both appear; year **1498** / voyage **1497-98**
- **Reception:** By the Hindu ruler **Zamorin** (Samuthiri) of Calicut
- **Profit:** Spices sold in Europe at **60 times** the cost
- **Return:** To Portugal **1499**
- **Contemporaries in 1498:** Manuel I (Portugal), Zamorin (Calicut), **Sikandar Lodi** (Delhi), **Immadi Narasimha Raya** (Vijayanagara)
# 3. Three voyages
| **Voyage** | **Outcome** |
|---|---|
| 1st (1497-99) | Calicut; Zamorin's welcome |
| 2nd (1502) | Zamorin refused trade; war, Calicut occupied, settled at Kochi |
| 3rd (1524) | Came as Viceroy [GK]; died at **Kochi, 1524** |
> Exam angle: CGPSC 2022 - only "Zamorin ruled Calicut" was correct; he did not stay a year in India.
