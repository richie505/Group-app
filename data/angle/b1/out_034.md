=== ROW 165 | Dual Government in Bengal and its consequences; the Bengal Famine of 1770

## Dual Government — basis, structure and terms | from: S0, S1, S2
# 1. Basis and duration
- **Duration:** **1765-1772**
- **Basis:** **Second Treaty of Allahabad (1765)**
  - Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II** granted the **Diwani** of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the **EIC**
  - Annual tribute = **₹26 lakh**
# 2. Diwani vs Nizamat

| **Diwani (with EIC)** | **Nizamat (with Nawab)** |
|---|---|
| Revenue collection and finance (plus civil justice) | Police, criminal justice, general administration |
| Real power, no responsibility | Responsibility, but no power or funds |

- **Method:** Company did not collect revenue itself; worked through Indian deputies (**Naib Diwans**, **Naib Nazim**)
# 3. Why "dual"
- Hide imperial ambitions from Indian rulers
- Avoid **British Parliament**'s scrutiny
- Keep other European companies paying duties to the "Nawab"
- Lack of British manpower for civil administration
- **Verdict:** "a sham" → economic ruin of Bengal + **famine of 1770**
# 4. Terms → meaning

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Diwani** | Revenue collection and finance (plus civil justice) |
| **Nizamat** | Police, criminal justice, general administration |
| Naib Diwan | Deputy Diwan |
| **dastak** | Duty-free trade pass; misused by Company servants for inland trade |
| **Mercantilist phase (1757-1813)** | Phase of **R.P. Dutt**'s three stages; Bengal's surplus revenue used to buy Indian goods for export |

- ⚠ **Diwani** = revenue (with EIC); **Nizamat** = police and criminal justice (with Nawab)

## Persons → roles in Dual Government | from: S0, S1, S2, S3, S4
# 1. Persons → role

| **Person** | **Role** |
|---|---|
| **Robert Clive** | **Architect** of Dual Government; Governor of Bengal, 2nd term **1765-67** |
| **Shah Alam II** | Mughal Emperor; granted the Diwani (1765); lost the ₹26 lakh payment in 1772 |
| **John Cartier (1769-72)** | **Governor** during the 1770 famine |
| **Warren Hastings** | **Abolished** Dual Government, **1772** |
| **Mir Qasim** | Had earlier abolished internal duties for everyone in protest against dastak misuse |
| **R.P. Dutt** | Three stages; **Mercantilist phase (1757-1813)** |

> Exam angle: "The dual system was enforced by" - Robert Clive (not Warren Hastings, who abolished it).
# 2. Naib Diwans and Naib Nazim

| **Deputy** | **Post** | **Region** |
|---|---|---|
| **Muhammad Reza Khan** | Naib Diwan (Deputy Diwan) at Murshidabad; also Naib Nazim for the minor Nawab [GK] | Bengal |
| **Raja Shitab Rai** | Naib Diwan [GK] | Bihar (Patna) |

- **Mohd. Reza Khan:** Appointed by **Clive** as Deputy Diwan of **Murshidabad** after the **Treaty of Allahabad** (BPSC 2001 key)
- **End:** Both removed + tried when the dual system ended in **1772**
- ⚠ **Reza Khan** = Bengal (Murshidabad); **Shitab Rai** = Bihar (Patna)

## Abolition (1772), FIRSTs and LASTs | from: S4, S6
# 1. Abolition of Dual Government (1772)
- **Abolished by:** **Warren Hastings**, **1772** (on becoming Governor of Bengal)
- **Approach:** "Tore the mask of Mughal sovereignty"; ruled Bengal by right of conquest
- **Revenue:** Company collected directly through its own servants
- **Treasury:** Shifted **Murshidabad → Calcutta**
- **Shah Alam II:** Stopped the **₹26 lakh** annual payment; took back **Allahabad** and **Kora** → sold to the **Nawab of Awadh**
> Exam angle: BPSC 1996 asked "who abolished Dual Government" - Warren Hastings.
# 2. FIRST / LAST

| **FIRST / LAST** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| First Governor of Bengal | **Clive** (**1758**) |
| Last Governor of Bengal | **Warren Hastings** (**1772-73**) |
| First Governor-General of Bengal | **Warren Hastings** (**1773**) |

- ⚠ **Clive** enforced (1765); **Hastings** abolished (1772)

## Consequences of Dual Government — factor → effect | from: S2
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Revenue extortion** | Heavy taxation + exploitative collection ruined the Bengal economy; poverty and distress spread |
| **Conflict of authority** | **Nizamat** vs **Diwani**; local nobility sidelined → resentment |
| **Wealth drain** | Bengal's surplus revenue used to buy Indian goods for export (= **Mercantilist phase (1757-1813)** of **R.P. Dutt**'s three stages) |
| **Weavers** | Forced to sell to Company agents at low prices; handicrafts declined |
| **Private trade** | Company servants misused duty-free **dastak** (trade pass) for inland trade; **Mir Qasim** had earlier abolished internal duties for everyone in protest |

- **Outcome:** In under two decades Bengal, India's richest province → acute poverty, famines, epidemics

## Bengal Famine of 1770 — areas, causes, mortality, Company response | from: S3
# 1. Areas and causes
- **Areas:** **Bengal and Bihar** (lower Gangetic plain)
- **Causes:**
  - Failure of the monsoon
  - **High land revenue collected in cash** → peasants forced to sell grain
  - Grain exported out of Bengal
- **Governor:** **John Cartier (1769-72)**
# 2. Mortality and response
- **Deaths:** Recorded differently in different sources

| **Source** | **Estimate** |
|---|---|
| UPSC notes | about **10 million** (one of the deadliest famines) |
| APP (Governors' table) | "about two million lives" |
| Common textbook figure | about **one-third** of Bengal's population [GK] |

- **Company response:** Little or no relief; revenue collection continued (squeezed even as famine spread)
  - Hurt Company's reputation + increased local opposition
> Exam angle: "Governor during the 1770 famine" - John Cartier; "Famine and Dual Government" - direct cause-effect.

## Sanyasi-Fakir rebellion and Anandamath — revolt, leaders, book | from: S5
- **Sanyasi (Fakir) Revolt (1763-1800):**
  - Causes: **famine of 1770** + Company insensitivity + restrictions on pilgrims visiting holy places
  - Participants: Hindu Sanyasis, Muslim Fakirs, dispossessed zamindars, peasants
  - Leaders: **Majnum Shah, Bhawani Pathak, Devi Chaudhurani**

| **Book** | **Author** | **Theme / year** |
|---|---|---|
| **Anandamath** | **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** | **1882**; set against the 1770 famine + Sanyasi revolt; contains the song **Vande Mataram** |

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Significance:** First formal Company control of revenue in India; scholars trace the **drain of wealth** + **deindustrialisation** to it
- **Criticism & debates:** "power without responsibility"; 1770 mortality figures differ widely between sources (see table)
- **Comparative note:** Andhra famines were separate - don't confuse with 1770
  - **Guntur (Dokkala) famine 1832-33** (about **2 lakh** deaths)
  - **Kurnool famine of 1866**

=== ROW 166 | Anglo-Mysore Wars I-IV - Haidar Ali, Tipu Sultan, Treaty of Mangalore and Srirangapatnam

## Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan → contributions | from: S0, S1
# 1. Haidar Ali
- **Rise:** Under the **Wodeyar** kingdom of Mysore; **Faujdar of Dindigul in 1755** under minister **Nanjaraja**
- **Military:** Adopted Western techniques; modern arsenal at **Dindigul** with French help
- **De facto ruler:** **1761**
- **Alliances (1st war):** Won over the **Marathas** + detached the **Nizam** from the British
# 2. Tipu Sultan - capital, embassies, reforms
- **Capital:** **Seringapatam (Srirangapatna)**
- **Foreign embassies:**
  - Ambassadors to **France** for European technology → built a **navy** (APPSC-G1 2018 key)
  - Envoys to **Turkey (Constantinople)**, **Afghanistan (Zaman Shah)**, **Mauritius**
- **Jacobin Club:** Helped French soldiers set up a **Jacobin Club at Seringapatam (1797)**; became a member
  - Called himself **"Citizen Tipu"**; planted a **Tree of Liberty**
- **Reforms:**
  - Introduced **sericulture** in Mysore
  - New calendar, new coinage, new weights and measures; used war rockets
> Exam angle: statement questions on "French embassy + navy" point to Tipu, not Haidar or the Wodeyars.
# 3. Tipu's titles
- **Titles:** **"Tiger of Mysore"**; "Citizen Tipu"; **Padshah**
- ⚠ **Dindigul** arsenal = Haidar; **French embassy + navy + Jacobin Club** = Tipu

## Treaties of the Mysore wars — year and terms | from: S3, S4, S6
# 1. Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
- **Terms:** Tipu ceded about **half his territory** + **₹3 crore** war indemnity + **two sons as hostages**

| **Gainer** | **Share** |
|---|---|
| **Nizam** | Areas from the **Krishna to beyond the Pennar** (UPSC). This included half of Rayalaseema: Bellary, Gurramkonda, Gandikota, Kambham, Kadapa (CDX p.367) |
| **Marathas** | Areas around the **Tungabhadra** and its tributaries (UPSC) |
| **British** | Malabar, Coorg (as protectorate), Dindigul and the **Bara Mahal** (Salem region) [GK]. Bara Mahal is where Read and Munro began the Ryotwari system (APP) |

- **Payment:** Half the indemnity paid at once; sons returned after the rest was paid
# 2. Settlement of Mysore (1799) and the Ceded Districts (1800)
- **Settlement:** Mysore **partitioned**
  - Hindu **Wodeyar** dynasty restored (boy ruler **Krishnaraja Wodeyar III**) under a **Subsidiary Alliance**
- **Ceded Districts:** Nizam's share of Mysore lands → handed to the Company in **1800** to pay for the subsidiary force (Block11)
# 3. Treaty traps
- ⚠ **Trap:** **Treaty of Madras (1769)** = with Haidar vs **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)** = with Tipu

## Subsidiary Alliance — aims, terms and effects | from: S2
# 1. Origin and aims
- **Introduced by:** **Lord Wellesley**, **1798**
- **Aims / objectives:**
  - Counter French influence - keep the **French** out (Nizam's French battalions under **Raymond**; Tipu's French links)
  - Establish **British paramountcy** without the cost of direct administration - make the Company paramount
  - Maintain a large army **at the Indian states' cost**
# 2. **Terms:** (p.367-368)
- **Foreign policy:** Ruler surrendered **foreign policy** / **control of foreign affairs** to the **EIC** - no dealings with other states without Company consent
- **Resident + army:** A **British Resident** at his court + a permanent **British subsidiary force** stationed in the state
- **Cost:** Ruler **paid for its upkeep**, in cash or by **ceding territory** (**ceded territory** in lieu of cash if he could not pay on time)
- **Europeans:** No other Europeans employed without British consent
- **Return:** Company protected the state's borders
# 3. Effects on the states

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Sovereignty** | Lost sovereignty + control of foreign policy |
| **Finances** | Drained by the subsidy → territorial cessions (e.g. Nizam's **Ceded Districts**) |
| **Government** | Rulers dependent, neglected government → later pretext for annexation |
| **Soldiers** | Disbanded soldiers left without work |

## Policies towards Indian states → author | from: S6, S8
# 1. Revision table

| **Policy** | **Author** |
|---|---|
| **Ring-fence** | **Warren Hastings**[GK] |
| **Subsidiary Alliance** | **Lord Wellesley** (1798) |
| **Subordinate isolation** / paramountcy | **Lord Hastings** (1813-23) [GK] |
| **Doctrine of Lapse** | **Lord Dalhousie** (1848) |
| Annexation for misgovernment | Lord Dalhousie (Awadh, 1856) |
| Subordinate union (after 1858) | Crown (Queen's Proclamation) [GK] |

- **Name:** Annexation policy = **Doctrine of Lapse** (APPSC-GS 2018 key)
- ⚠ **Warren Hastings** = ring-fence; **Lord Hastings** = subordinate isolation
# 2. Ring-fence policy
- **Author:** **Warren Hastings** - guard Company frontiers by defending borders of neighbouring states
- **Awadh = classic buffer:**
  - Set up after **Buxar (1764)**; kept up by Hastings, who sold **Kora and Allahabad** to the Nawab of Awadh
  - Shielded Bengal from the **Marathas** and the **Afghans**
# 3. Subordinate isolation and the Rajput alliances (Lord Hastings)
- **Subordinate isolation (Lord Hastings):** Each state bound separately to the paramount Company; cut off from others
- **Treaties:** **Jodhpur, Jaipur, Bundi** + other Rajput states, **1818**, under **Lord Hastings**
- **Negotiator:** **Charles Metcalfe**
- **Aims:**
  - Protect the states from the **Marathas and Pindaris**
  - Bring them under British paramountcy
  - Isolate them from the Maratha powers
# 4. Paramountcy - grounds for intervention
- **Grounds for intervention:**
  - States ran internal affairs but had **no external or international life**
  - British stepped in **to give effect to their international commitments** (APPSC-G2 2017)
  - Also over succession disputes + misgovernment
- **After 1858 (subordinate union):**
  - Annexation stopped; **right of adoption** recognised; borders guaranteed
  - Princes = "**breakwaters to the storm**"

## Regions → how they came to the Company — Circars, Ceded Districts, Carnatic | from: S0, S2, S3
# 1. Northern Circars and the Mughal grant
- **Five Circars:** **Chicacole, Rajahmundry, Ellore, Mustafanagar (Kondapalli), Guntur (Murtazanagar)**
- **Modern districts:** Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, East and West Godavari, Krishna + Guntur
- **Mughal grant:** Firman of **Shah Alam II** secured by **Robert Clive**, dated **1765** (some notes: **1766**)
  - Nizam at first refused it → Company negotiated a lease from him in **1766**
- ⚠ **Trap:** Some notes call the **1753** grant to the French a Mughal grant + date **Forde**'s capture to **1765-66**; Forde's campaign was **1758-59** (see M1-A-52)
# 2. Ceded Districts - region and background
- **Districts:** **Kadapa, Kurnool, Anantapur** (AP) + **Bellary** (now Karnataka) = **Kadapa (Cuddapah), Kurnool, Anantapur and Bellary** = today's **Rayalaseema** (APPSC GS 2024; APHQ 2025)
- **Background:** **Hyder Ali** and **Tipu Sultan** took these lands from Hyderabad
  - **Nizam Ali** regained them as British ally in Mysore wars of **1790-92** and **1799**
- **Cession:** Under the **Subsidiary Alliance** (**1798**) Nizam could not pay in cash → **ceded** them in **1800**
# 3. Ceded Districts - names

| **Name** | **Fact** |
|---|---|
| **Dattamandalam / Dattamandalalu** | Old name ("ceded lands") - used until **1928** |
| **Rayalaseema** | Suggested by **Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao** (APPSC G2 2012 key); adopted at the **Andhra Mahasabha, Nandyal, 1928** (p.367), presided over by **S. Radhakrishnan** (some notes say **1927**, p.357); other notes credit **Chilukuri Narayana Rao** |

# 4. Carnatic districts (Nellore, Prakasam area, part of Chittoor), 1801
- **Earlier rule:** **Nellore** and **Chittoor** under Aravidu **Sriranga Raya III** → then part of the **Carnatic**
- **Muhammad Ali:** Installed as Nawab by the British after the **Second Carnatic War** (secure only after **1763**)
  - Shielded from **Najibullah** of Nellore, the **Marathas**, **Hyder Ali**
- **Annexation:** After Muhammad Ali's death, Company annexed the Carnatic districts in **1801**
  - **Carnatic Treaty** signed with Nawab **Azim-ud-Daula**

## Early administration — Chiefs and Councils, Circuit Committee, Collector, Board of Revenue | from: S2, S4, S5
# 1. Factories and the Chiefs and Councils
- **Before acquisition:** Company factories + godowns at **Machilipatnam** and **Visakhapatnam**; a supervising revenue body set over them
  - Some notes call this body the "**Board of Revenue**", abolished **1794**
- **Chiefs and Councils:** Provincial Chiefs and Councils at these stations governed the Circars until **1794** → replaced by district **Collectors**
  - **Chief-in-Council** at **Masulipatnam** governed the Circars until then
# 2. Circuit Committee (Committee of Circuit)
- **Purpose:** Reviewed Circars' revenue; examined revenue administration
- **Finding:** Recommended against the **auction (highest-bidder) system** of revenue rights - hurt both government and ryots (p.417)
  - **Munro** then abolished auctions in **Rayalaseema**
- !! Not in your sources: the year of appointment and the full findings on the Northern Circars (check the CDX Circars chapter).
# 3. Collector system
- **From 1794:** Each district had a **Collector** - land revenue (main duty) + law and order + magistrate (District Magistrate/Judge)
  - Judicial powers taken away in **1816** (separation)
  - Under ryotwari, some magisterial powers returned to Collectors; civil justice stayed with the **District Judge**
- **Exam key:** "Collector's administration in place of the Revenue Board" = **1786** (APPSC G2 2012)
  - Notes date the Collector system in coastal Andhra to **1794**; in the exact PYQ wording, answer **1786**
# 4. Board of Revenue and the hierarchy
- **Madras Board of Revenue:** Set up **1786**; state-level apex of the revenue hierarchy

| District | Collector |
| Division | Sub-Collector / Revenue Officer |
| Taluk | Tahsildar |
| Firka | Deputy Tahsildar / Firka Revenue Inspector |
| Village | Karanam, Munsab (hereditary village officers) |

## Revenue systems — Permanent Settlement areas vs Ryotwari areas | from: S8
- **Before 1802:** Zamindaris given by **auction**
- **Permanent Settlement:** **Cornwallis**, **1793** - replaced auctions with a fixed demand; applied in Andhra in **1802**

| **Feature** | **Zamindari (Permanent Settlement)** | **Ryotwari** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Estates in the Northern Circars: **Vizianagaram, Bobbili, Pithapuram** (CDI) | **Rayalaseema** first; later most of Coastal Andhra (CDI; APP) |
| Settled with | Zamindar (intermediary) | Ryot (cultivator) directly |
| Effect | Absentee landlordism; tenants impoverished (CDI) | Over-assessment (often **50%+** of produce); moneylenders; land alienation (CDI) |

- **Mahalwari** (village headmen collect): placed in **Nellore**
- **Ryotwari:**
  - First region: **Baramahal (1792)** (now **Salem/Krishnagiri**, Tamil Nadu), under **Captain Alexander Read** with **Munro**
  - In Andhra: began in the **Ceded Districts in 1802**
- **Zamindari abolition:** **Madras Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948**; in force **7 Sep 1949** (p.486; APHQ)
  - Some notes say "**1952**" (p.358), but exam keys use the **1948** Act

# 3. Munro's judicial reforms
- **Judicial Commission:** Munro = **President (1814)**; report submitted **April 1816**, mostly accepted
- **Madras Regulations of 1816:**
  - **Munsiff courts** + **Panchayat courts** in every large village and district = courts of first instance
  - Village officials handled petty thefts

- **Cornwallis:** Created **thanas** under **darogas** (Indian officers) + constables; darogas known for abuse of power

## Universal angles | from: S12 | universal
# 1. Significance and debates
- **Significance:**
  - Today's Andhra took shape via the **Circars (1766)**, **Guntur (1788)**, **Ceded Districts (1800)**, **Nellore-Chittoor (1801)**
  - Fertile, educated Circars vs arid, neglected Ceded Districts grew apart → later shaped the **Sri Bagh Pact** debate
  - **District Collector** (revenue + magisterial + police powers) = pivot of British rule; ended village autonomy
- **Criticism & debates:** Collectors who did not know Telugu depended on corrupt **dubashis** / native subordinates
- **AP angle:**
  - **Munro** = Telugu-speaking Collector, needed no interpreters (with **Brown** the exceptions - rare Telugu learners)
  - Munro brought **Ryotwari** from the **Bara Mahals** to the Ceded Districts; died as Governor at **Pattikonda (Kurnool), 1827**
- **Comparative note:** **Telangana** stayed under the Nizam; coast + Rayalaseema passed to the Company
# 2. Current relevance
- **Sericulture in Rayalaseema (20 Feb 2026):** Traced to British promotion in the **"Ceded Districts"** in late 19th-early 20th centuries
  - Silk hubs today: **Dharmavaram** and **Madanapalle**
- **Revenue judicial service (1 May 2026):** Supreme Court (CJI **Surya Kant**) issued notice on a plea for a separate "revenue judicial service" cadre
  - Argues revenue officers without legal training should not decide land-title disputes, under **Art. 14**
