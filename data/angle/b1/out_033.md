=== ROW 159 | First Carnatic War - causes, Battle of St. Thome, Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle

## First Carnatic War — belligerents, causes and European link | from: S0
- **Belligerents:** **British and the French** East India Companies (APPSC 2018)
- **European link:** Direct extension of the **War of Austrian Succession**
- **Immediate cause:** English navy seized French ships → France retaliated
  - French fleet from **Mauritius** captured Madras in **1746**
- **Andhra:** Not directly affected

## Persons → role in the First war | from: S1
| **Person** | **Role** |
|---|---|
| **Nizam-ul-Mulk** | Sent Anwaruddin as guardian of minor Nawab **Syed Ali** |
| **Anwaruddin** | Killed Syed Ali → made himself **Nawab of Arcot (1744)**; asked both Companies not to fight in his territory; marched to support the English against the French |
| **Captain Paradis** | Led the small **French** force at St. Thome |
| **Mahfuz Khan** | Anwaruddin's son; led the Nawab's army at St. Thome |

- **Anwaruddin's death:** Killed at **Ambur (1749)** in the Second War

## Battle of St. Thome and Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle → date, terms, results | from: S1, S2
# 1. Battle of St. Thome / Adyar (1746)
- **Belligerents:** **French** (Captain Paradis) vs Nawab **Anwaruddin's** army (Mahfuz Khan)
- **Numbers:** About **930** French defeated about **10,000**
- **Significance / military lesson:** Small disciplined European infantry + artillery beat far larger Indian cavalry armies → opened the door to European interference
- ⚠ **930** French beat **10,000** at St. Thome (**1746**); Anwaruddin died later at **Ambur (1749)**
# 2. Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)
- **Signed:** **18 September 1748**
- **Ended:** War of Austrian Succession + First Carnatic War
- **Exchange:** **Madras restored to the English** ↔ **Louisbourg** (North America) to the French
> Exam angle: UPPSC 2020/2025 - "war ended" and "English got back Madras" are both correct.
# 3. Results
- **Naval lesson:** **naval power** decided the Anglo-French contest on the Coromandel coast
- **Political result:** Nil despite Dupleix's victories; both sides now eyed Indian politics

## Universal angles | from: S3 | universal
- **Significance:** First showed Indian rulers' military weakness against European-trained troops
- **Comparative note:** **Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)** restored the pre-war position vs **Paris (1763)** – ended French political power

=== ROW 164 | Mir Jafar, Mir Qasim and the Battle of Buxar (1764); Treaty of Allahabad (1765) and Diwani

## Terms → meaning (farman, dastak, Diwani, Nizamat) | from: S0, S7
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Farman (1717)** | Mughal emperor **Farrukhsiyar** let the Company trade in Bengal without customs duties for a fixed **Rs 3,000** a year; called the **"Magna Carta" of the Company** |
| **Dastak** | Free pass for duty-free trade |
| **Diwani** | **Revenue** + civil justice |
| **Nizamat** | Police, criminal justice / law and order |
| **Naib Subahdar** | Company-nominated officer handling defence + administration (**Muhammad Reza Khan**) |

- **Dastak misuse:** By Company servants for private trade → drained the Nawab's revenue
  - Caused quarrels with **Siraj** and later **Mir Qasim**

## Battle of Plassey (1757) — year, location, commanders, conspirators | from: S1
- **Date/place:** **23 June 1757**, **Plassey (Palashi)**, on the **Bhagirathi**, present-day **West Bengal** (**Nadia** district)
- **Commanders:** **Clive** (about 3,000) defeated **Siraj-ud-Daulah** (about 50,000)
  - Siraj = last independent Nawab; had French support
- **Causes:**
  - Company servants misused **dastaks**
  - English fortified Calcutta without consent (French obeyed his order to stop; English did not)
  - Company sheltered his enemies
- **Black Hole (June 1756):** Siraj took Qasim Bazar → Calcutta (Fort William)
  - Governor **Drake** fled to Fulta
  - European prisoners incl. **Holwell** shut in a small cell; many died
  - British used it as a pretext for war
- **Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757):** Restored privileges of the **1717 farman** + duty-free trade; Company still plotted to remove Siraj

| **Side** | **Persons** |
|---|---|
| **Conspirators** | **Mir Jafar** (Mir Bakshi), Rai Durlabh, **Jagat Seth**, Amir Chand (Omichund), Manik Chand |
| **Loyal to Siraj** | **Mir Madan** + **Mohan Lal** |

- ⚠ **Trap:** A statement listing Mir Madan among the traitors is WRONG (Jul 2026 practice question)
- **Results:**
  - Siraj fled, was captured and executed
  - Mir Jafar → Nawab; paid **Rs 1.77 crore**
  - Company got **zamindari of the 24 Parganas** + duty-free trade in Bengal, Bihar, Orissa
  - Resources to beat the French

## Nawabs of Bengal → tenure and acts (Mir Jafar, Mir Qasim, Najm-ud-Daula) | from: S2, S3, S7, S9
# 1. Nawabs of Bengal in sequence
| **Nawab** | **Note** |
|---|---|
| **Murshid Quli Khan** (1717-27 [GK]) | The last Subahdar of Bengal appointed by the Mughal emperor (UPPSC 2021 key); asked the English to pay customs duties |
| Shuja-ud-din (1727-39 [GK]) | Too powerful to heed court orders that favoured the English (1735) |
| Sarfaraz Khan | Killed by Alivardi |
| **Alivardi Khan** (1740-56 [GK]) | Deputy Governor of Bihar; stopped both companies fortifying Calcutta and Chandannagar |
| **Siraj-ud-Daulah** (1756-57) | Alivardi's grandson; killed Shaukat Jang at Manihari |
| **Mir Jafar** (1757-60; again 1763-65) | Puppet; deposed by Vansittart, restored after 1763 |
| **Mir Qasim** (1760-63) | Reformer; fought at Buxar |

# 2. Mir Jafar — two tenures, Dutch intrigue, removal
- **Tenures:**
  - 1: **1757-1760** (after Plassey); concessions = the Rs 1.77 crore payment and the 24 Parganas zamindari
  - 2: **1763-1765** (restored after Mir Qasim's fall; died soon after Buxar)
- **Dutch intrigue:** Plotted with the **Dutch** against the English
  - Clive defeated the Dutch at **Chinsura/Bidara (1759)** → Dutch left India
- **Removal (1760):** Could not meet Company demands
  - Pension Rs 15,000 a month; some notes print "Rs 1,500 annually"
- **Second term:** Accepted limits on the size of his army
# 3. Mir Qasim — accession, reforms, trade duties and conflict
- **Accession:** Son-in-law of Mir Jafar; put on the throne in **1760** by Governor **Vansittart**
- **Treaty of 1760:**
  - Ceded **Burdwan, Midnapur and Chittagong**
  - Half share of **Sylhet** chunam trade
  - Rs 5 lakh for the Company's southern wars
- **Reforms:**
  - Capital: **Murshidabad to Monghyr (Munger), Bihar**
  - Modernised the army; improved Bengal's finances
- **Trade duties:** **Abolished all internal trade duties** (inland) (APPSC G2 2017)
  - Indian merchants paid the same as the English → dastak benefit gone
- **Conflict:** Company servants, angry at lost private-trade profits, went to war
  - **Defeated in 1763** → fled to **Awadh**
# 4. Najm-ud-Daula - Company control
- **Identity:** Son of **Mir Jafar**; Nawab from **1765**
- **1765 treaty:** Handed defence + administration to a Company-nominated **Naib Subahdar** (Muhammad Reza Khan)
  - Left with the **Nizamat** in name only

## Battle of Buxar (1764) — allies, commander, outcome; Buxar vs Plassey | from: S4, S5
# 1. Buxar - allies, British commander, Nawab at the time
- **Date/place:** **October 1764** (22-23 Oct), **Buxar** (Bihar)

| **Ally** | **Position** |
|---|---|
| **Mir Qasim** | Deposed Nawab of Bengal (ex-Nawab) |
| **Shuja-ud-Daulah** | Nawab of Awadh |

- **British commander:** **Major Hector Munro**

# 2. Buxar vs Plassey — "most decisive battle"
| **Point** | **Plassey (1757)** | **Buxar (1764)** |
|---|---|---|

## Treaty of Allahabad (1765), Diwani and treaty chronology | from: S6, S9
# 1. Treaty of Allahabad - parties and terms
- **Signed:** **1765**, after Buxar, by **Clive** (returned as Governor of Bengal for second time, May 1765)
  - Other parties: **Shah Alam II** + **Shuja-ud-Daulah**

| **Party** | **Term** |
|---|---|
| Shah Alam II | Granted the Company the **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** in return for **Rs 26 lakh a year**; kept (got) **Kora and Allahabad** → Company **pensioner** |
| Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh) | Got his dominions back except Kora and Allahabad (gave them to the Emperor); paid a war indemnity of **Rs 50 lakh**[GK]; Awadh became a **buffer state** under Company protection |
| Balwant Singh | Zamindar of Banaras; confirmed in full possession of his estate |
| Nawab of Bengal | Kept the Nizamat (defence, police, justice), funded by **Rs 53 lakh** a year from the Company [GK] (some notes say the Company received the Rs 53 lakh) |

# 2. Grant of Diwani
- **Date:** **1765** (APPSC G2 2017; APPSC-G2 2017 key); formally **12 August 1765**
- **Grantor:** Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II** under the **Treaty of Allahabad**
- **Recipient:** Company under **Robert Clive** (Governor of Bengal for the 2nd time, from May 1765)
- **Terms:** Diwani (**revenue collection and civil justice**) of **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**
  - **Annual tribute of Rs 26 lakh** to the Emperor
> Exam angle: "Which treaty followed Buxar?" The answer is Allahabad, not Salbai, Bassein or Sugauli.

## Dual Government (1765-72) → division, criticism and end | from: S7
- **Period:** **1765-72**
- **Division:**
  - Company = **Diwani** (revenue + civil justice)
  - Nawab = **Nizamat** (police, criminal justice / law and order)
  - Mughal emperor = nominal sovereign
- **Criticism:** Power without responsibility
- **Naib diwans:** **Muhammad Reza Khan** (Bengal) + **Raja Shitab Rai** (Bihar) – collected revenue for the Company
- **End:** **Warren Hastings (1772)** ended it
  - "Tore the mask of Mughal sovereignty" (ended Mughal pretence) → ruled by right of conquest
  - Stopped the tribute to Shah Alam

- **Governor of Bengal:** **1758-60 and 1765-67**

- ⚠ Clive's two governorships = **1758-60** and **1765-67**; Treaty of Allahabad signed in his second term
