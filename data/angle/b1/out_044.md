## Partition of Bengal 1905 — dates, division, motives and annulment | from: S0
# 1. Event → date
| **Event** | **Date** |
|---|---|
| Partition announced (Viceroy **Lord Curzon**) | **19/20 July 1905** (some notes give 19 July, others 20 July) |
| Partition took effect | **took effect 16 Oct 1905** (**16 October 1905**) - day of mourning, hartal, Ganga bath, **Rakhi-bandhan** |
| Partition annulled | **annulled on 12 Dec 1911** at the **Delhi Durbar** of **King George V** (Viceroy **Hardinge II**) |

- Annulment → **Capital moved from Calcutta to Delhi**
> Exam angle: APPSC 2022 A-R - 1911 Durbar true; Edward VII's coronation (celebrated at the 1903 Durbar [GK]) is not its explanation.

# 2. Division → population
| **Part** | **Population** |
|---|---|
| **Eastern Bengal and Assam** | **31 million** |
| Rest of Bengal | **54 million** (**18 million** Bengalis, **36 million** Biharis and Oriyas) → Bengalis a minority in their own province |

# 3. Motives
- **Stated motive:** Administrative convenience (province "too big")
- **Real motive:** Divide **Bengali Hindus from Muslims** + weaken Bengali nationalism

## Moderates vs Extremists — classification and ideology | from: S0, S1
# 1. Classification
- **Moderates:** Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gokhale, S.N. Banerjee, Ranade, W.C. Bonnerjee, R.C. Dutt
- **Extremists:** Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Pal, Aurobindo
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Ranade, Gokhale, G.V. Joshi are **not** militant (APPSC-GS 2018: militant = Tilak)
| **Parameter** | **Moderates (1885-1905)** | **Extremists (1905-1916)** |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Self-government within the Empire | **Swaraj** (complete freedom) |
| View of British | Faith in British justice | British rule exploitative |
| Methods | **3 Ps**: prayers, petitions, protests | **3 Ss**: self-reliance, self-sacrifice, strong will; mass agitation |
| Base | Urban educated elite | Students, women, workers, peasants |
| Social reform | Pro-reform | Orthodox (Tilak opposed the Age of Consent Bill) |

- ⚠ **3 Ps** = Moderates; **3 Ss** = Extremists
# 2. Extremist ideology — terms → meaning
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Swaraj** | Complete freedom from British control, not colonial self-government |
| **Attitude** | **No faith in British generosity.** |
| **Passive resistance** | Methods: passive resistance, mass agitation, boycott, Swadeshi, national education, self-sacrifice |
| **Spiritual nationalism** | Nation as mother goddess (Bande Mataram, Bhavani Mandir); Hindu festivals for mass contact; pride in India's past |
| **Self-reliance (Atmashakti)** | Constructive work such as cooperatives |

## Indian Councils Act 1909 — name, aim and provisions | from: S2
# 1. Background and name
- **Popular name:** **Morley-Minto (Minto-Morley) Reforms**
- **Introduced by:** **John Morley** (Secretary of State) + **Lord Minto** (Viceroy)
- **Aim:** Rally the Moderates + reward Muslim loyalty
# 2. Provisions
- **Councils:** Central Legislative Council enlarged (additional members **to 60**); provincial councils enlarged
- **Majorities:**
  - **Official majority retained at the Centre**
  - Non-official majority in provinces, but Governor's veto left them powerless
- **Electorates:** **Separate electorates for Muslims** with **weightage** (more seats than population share) - most critical feature; indirect, multi-tier elections
- **Powers:** Councils could debate the budget - discuss budget, ask supplementary questions, move resolutions
  - **Could not vote on the budget** or question military affairs
- **Executive Council:** Indians in the Executive Council - S.P. Sinha (Law Member)

## Lucknow session and Lucknow Pact 1916 — terms | from: S3
# 1. League's younger leadership
- **1912-24:** League dominated by **younger Muslim nationalists**; their nationalism took a communal view of politics
# 2. Lucknow session (Dec 1916) — historic for
- **Reunion of Moderates and Extremists** after the 1907 Surat Split (Tilak's return)
- **Lucknow Pact** - Congress-League alliance; League led by the **"Young Party"** (Jinnah, Ali Brothers)
# 3. Lucknow Pact - terms
| **Key clause** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| Goal | **Self-government** accepted by both as common goal |
| Separate electorates | Congress **accepted** Muslim separate electorates - a major concession |
| Weightage | Fixed proportion of seats for Muslims above their population ratio |
| Communal veto | No bill on a community passed if **three-fourths** of that community's members opposed |
| Others | Separation of executive and judiciary; elected majority in provincial legislatures |

- **Limit:** Congress and League still held separate annual sessions with distinct agendas
