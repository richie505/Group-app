=== ROW 198 | Growth of nationalism - factors; pre-Congress political associations

## Factors of nationalism — causes, reactions and Lytton's measures | from: S0, S1
# 1. Unintended results of British rule
- **Unification:** Political, administrative, economic (railways, telegraph, post, uniform laws)
- **Western liberal ideas** through English education
- **English** as a link language
- **New educated middle class** (lawyers, journalists, teachers)
# 2. Other factors
- **Socio-religious reform:** Fostered awakening
- **Press and literature:** Telugu papers e.g. Veeresalingam's Vivekavardhani **1874**
- **History-writing:** Rediscovery of India's past → self-confidence
# 3. Reactions
- **Economic exploitation:** drain, ruin of artisans
- **Racial arrogance:** **Lytton**'s policies; **Ilbert Bill**
- **Foreign inspiration:** the **American**, **French** and **Italian** movements
# 4. Lytton's resented measures (1876-80)

| **Measure** | **Year** | **Why resented** |
|---|---|---|
| **Delhi Durbar** | **1877** | Proclaimed **Queen Victoria** Empress while millions starved in the **1876-78** famine |
| **Vernacular Press Act** | **1878** | Gagged the Indian-language press |
| **Arms Act** | **1878** | Disarmed Indians but not Europeans |
| **Second Afghan War** | **1878-80** | Drained Indian revenue |

> Exam angle: "Which was NOT a measure of Lytton?" (the Ilbert Bill was Ripon's).
- ⚠ **Lytton** = Durbar, Vernacular Press Act, Arms Act, Afghan War; **Ilbert Bill** = **Ripon**

## Founding of the INC, 1885 — origins, first session, demands | from: S3
# 1. Origins
- Private meeting of **17** people after the **Theosophical Convention** at **Madras** (**Dec 1884**)
- Hume's **Indian (National) Union**
# 2. First session
- **Date & venue:** **28-31 December 1885**, **Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay**
  - Shifted from **Poona** because of a **cholera** outbreak
- **Delegates:** **72 delegates** (**54** Hindus, **2** Muslims, plus Jains and Parsis) - mostly lawyers, journalists, teachers
- **Office-bearers:** President **W.C. Bonnerjee**; **A.O. Hume** = General Secretary
- **Four demands of the 1st session:**
  - Simultaneous **ICS** exams in England and India
  - Council reform - election in place of nomination (elected legislative councils)
  - Opposition to the annexation of **Upper Burma**
  - Cuts in **military expenditure**
# 3. Second session
- **2nd session:** Calcutta **1886** - **436** delegates; some notes give **408** in total with **21 from Andhra**
- ⚠ Planned at **Poona** → held at **Bombay** (cholera); **72** delegates in 1885 vs **436** in 1886

## Scholars → statements on the safety-valve theory | from: S4
- **Claim:** **A.O. Hume** and **Lord Dufferin** set up the INC as a safe outlet for educated Indians' anger → to prevent another **1857**
- **Basis:**
  - Seven volumes of secret reports Hume read at **Simla** in **1878**
  - First mentioned in **William Wedderburn**'s biography of Hume (**1913**)

| **Person** | **View** |
|---|---|
| **Lala Lajpat Rai** | **First user** (Young India, **1916**) - used it to attack the Moderates |
| **R.P. Dutt (Marxist)** | "Conspiracy" theory - INC created to head off a popular uprising, bourgeois leaders as partners |
| **Bipan Chandra** | Critic - a colonial myth; Indians were already organising (e.g. **Indian Association**); coined the **"lightning conductor"** phrase |
| **G.K. Gokhale** | Made the point that no Indian could have started it (lightning-conductor view also credited to him) |

- **Lightning-conductor view:** Indian leaders used Hume, a Briton, to shield the infant body from official suppression/hostility
- **Dufferin's role:** Likely suggested an annual meeting of educated Indians; no proof that he proposed the INC
- ⚠ **Wedderburn (1913)** = first mention; **Lajpat Rai (1916)** = first user; **Bipan Chandra** = lightning conductor

## Moderate methods — beliefs, 3 Ps, limitations and official attitude | from: S4
# 1. Beliefs and methods
- **Belief:** British rule "providential"; British just but ill-informed → faith in **British justice and fair play**
- **Methods 3 Ps:** **PPP - Prayers, Petitions, Protests**; resolutions, meetings, pamphlets; strictly constitutional agitation
- **Two-pronged strategy:** Build public opinion in India + persuade the British public and government
# 2. Limitations
- **Session length:** Met **only 3 days a year** (**three days**); no machinery to work between sessions
- **Narrow social base:** **narrow urban, educated base**; little trust in / distrust of mass action
- **Naive faith:** Mild methods; faith in British justice exposed by the **Partition of Bengal (1905)**
# 3. Official attitude to the Moderates

| **Official** | **Statement** |
|---|---|
| **Dufferin** | Congress represents a **"microscopic minority"** |
| **Curzon (1900)** | "The Congress is tottering to its fall"; hoped to assist it to "a peaceful demise" |
