=== ROW 63 | Gupta administration - feudalism debate, land grants, agraharas, revenue and local bodies

## Gupta kingship — titles, divine claims and decentralisation | from: S0
# 1. Titles and divine claims
- **Royal titles:** **Parameshvara, Maharajadhiraja, Paramabhattaraka**

| **King** | **Divine claim** |
|---|---|
| Gupta kings | Likened to **Vishnu** |
| Samudragupta | Equated with **Kubera (Dhanada), Varuna, Indra and Yama (Antaka)** |

- **Succession:** Hereditary, but **primogeniture was not firm**
# 2. Decentralised vs the Mauryas
- **Feudatories:** Large areas held by **feudatories (samantas)**, some reinstated by Samudragupta; grantees ran much of the empire
- **Hereditary offices:** Many offices **hereditary** (e.g. Harisena inherited his post)
- **Pay:** Salaries often in land or kind
- ⚠ **Mauryas** = centralised; **Guptas** = **decentralised**, run through **samantas** and grantees

## Officials → functions; judicial system | from: S1
# 1. Officials

| **Official** | **Function / role** |
|---|---|
| **Kumaramatya** | High / senior cadre officer (ranked with princes); ministers and commanders (generals) chosen from them; own office (**adhikarana**); often paid in cash |
| Sandhivigrahika | Minister of **peace and war** |
| **Mahadandanayaka** | Chief judicial officer / general (chief judicial/military officer) |
| Dandapashika | District police officer |
| Akshapataladhikrita | Keeper of royal records |
| Pustapala | Land-transfer record keeper (land records) |
| Dutaka / Dutakas | Spies and messengers |

- **Harishena:** **Harishena** held all three top titles → kumaramatya, sandhivigrahika, mahadandanayaka
- ⚠ **Akshapataladhikrita** = royal records; **Pustapala** = land records; **Dandapashika** = police, not judge
# 2. Judicial system
- **Development:** More developed than earlier → **civil and criminal law clearly separated**
  - Criminal: theft, adultery
  - Civil: property disputes
- **Basis:** Laws followed varna hierarchy

## Land grants → grantee, rights, tenures and effects | from: S6
# 1. Grant → grantee → rights

| **Grant** | **Grantee** | **Rights** |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmadeya** | Brahmanas, individually or collectively (singly or jointly) | Tax exemption + administrative rights |
| **Agrahara** | **Brahmanas** (religious functionaries) - tax-free land / village | Tax-free, hereditary, often with administrative rights |
| **Devagrahara / Devadana** | Institutions - **temples and monasteries** | Revenue for worship |
| Secular grants | Officials, feudatories | Revenue in lieu of salary |

- ⚠ **Term clash:** One set of notes (Block01) reverses the terms ("Agrahara for Brahmanas, Brahmadeya for religious institutions"); the glossary above is the usual meaning
  - MPPSC 2014: tax-free land went to **Brahmins** → brahmadeya usually = grant to Brahmanas
# 2. Tenure terms → meaning

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Nivi dharma** | Trust |
| **Nivi dharma akshayana** | Perpetual endowment |
| **Aprada dharma** | Enjoy income, cannot alienate |
| **Bhumichchhidranyaya** | First reclaimer of waste owns it, tax-free |

# 3. Rights and effects
- **Rights transferred:** **Revenue plus administrative, judicial and police rights**; grantees could collect taxes from peasants
  - Royal officials barred from entering → led to **sub-infeudation**
- **Tribal frontiers:** Grants in tribal and forest areas brought **virgin land under cultivation** → absorbed tribes into the caste order as new sub-castes (APPSC-GS 2023 key)
- **Peasantry:** Reduced to (sank to) **semi-servile status**
  - Tenant farming, sharecropping, **tenants-at-will**, forced labour (**vishti**), many taxes
# 4. FIRST
- **Earliest land grants in India:** The **Satavahanas**
  - 100 nivartanas to Buddhist monks + agrahara-type grants to Brahmanas

## Historians → positions on Indian feudalism | from: S7
# 1. Definition and elements
- **Definition:** Feudalism = grant of land by an overlord to a donee **with financial, administrative and judicial rights**
- **Essential elements (UPSC 2015):**
  - Administrative structure based on **control and possession of land**
  - **Lord-vassal** relationship
  - **Weak** central authority
  - ⚠ **Trap:** A strong centre with weak local authority is **not** a feudal feature
# 2. Historian → position

| **Historian** | **Position** |
|---|---|
| **R.S. Sharma** | Proponent - Indian feudalism crystallised through **land grants**, sub-infeudation, **vishti**, decline of trade, **urban decay** (CDI) and fewer gold coins |
| **D.C. Sircar** | Critic - grants did not create feudal lords; transferred **revenue rights, not ownership**; no feudalism in the European sense [GK] |
| **Harbans Mukhia** | Critic - "Was there feudalism in Indian history?" - **no serfdom**, a largely free peasantry; peasants kept autonomy [GK] |
| B.D. Chattopadhyaya | **Integrative / processual** model - grants spread state society into new areas; rejects decay as the full story [GK] |

- **Evidence cited:** 7th-century fall in long-distance trade + gold coinage; shrinking towns; closed village economy
- ⚠ **Sharma** = proponent; **Sircar** and **Mukhia** = critics; **Chattopadhyaya** = integrative model

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
- **Criticism & debates:** Feudalism debate → R.S. Sharma's land-grant thesis vs critics who see no serfdom
- **Significance:** Gupta land grants anchor the "Indian feudalism" debate
- **Comparative note:** Mauryan state centrally regulated vs Gupta administration relying on feudatories and grantees
- **AP angle:**
  - Satavahana grants to Buddhist monks (e.g. **100 nivartanas**, 1 nivartana = about 1.5 acres) = earliest land grants
  - Satavahana and Ikshvaku brahmadeya grants barred royal officials
  - Andhra vishayas continued under later dynasties

## Trade decline and urban decay — causes, effects, timing | from: S8
# 1. Causes → effect

| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire** | **Eastern Roman Empire** trade in silk and spices continued until about **550 CE**; fell once the Romans learnt to **rear silkworms** → Indian silk exports suffered |
| Western Rome | **Collapse of the West Roman Empire** = standard cause of North India's trade decline (7th-10th c.) (APPSC-G2 2017 key) |
| Sassanid decline | After the rise of Islam → hurt overland trade |
| Hunas | **Hunas disrupted the north-western route** + loss of Western India → cut trade revenue; less gold and silver inflow |
| Dharmashastras | Banned sea travel ("salt seas") as polluting |

# 2. New directions
- **Shift to Southeast Asia:** Trade with **South-East Asia** grew via **Tamralipti** → did not revive the northern economy
- **China:** Trade grew; Chinese silk = **'Chinansuka'**
# 3. Urban decay and timing
- **Urban decay (R.S. Sharma):** Old commercial towns shrank; economy turned rural and self-sufficient; **gold coins became scarce**
- **Decline of towns:** Sites → **Sanghol, Hastinapur, Mathura, Shravasti, Kaushambi** (APPSC-GS 2023 key)
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Pronounced only by the mid-9th century" was marked false
- **Timing:**
  - Decline visible in the **post-Gupta period / 7th century**
  - c. **750-1000 CE** = stagnation in North India
  - Recovery with the Arab empire from the 10th century
> Exam angle: statement-type questions on the post-Gupta decline of trade and towns.

=== ROW 65 | Gupta religion - Bhagavatism, Vaishnavism, Saivism; beginnings of temple architecture (Deogarh, Bhitargaon)

## Bhagavatism and Vaishnavism — origin, persons, royal titles, FIRSTs | from: S0, S1
# 1. Origin and doctrine
- **Centre of worship:** **Vasudeva-Krishna** of the **Vrishni (Satvata)** clan; his followers were its first worshippers
  - **FIRST:** The **Bhagavatas** first worshipped Vasudeva-Krishna (UPPSC 1997); Panini refers to Vasudeva worship
- **Other names:** **Pancharatras** also called Bhagavatas or Sattvatas
- **Doctrine:** **Avatara** theory of Vishnu (ten incarnations) preached; foundations = **karma, bhakti and ahimsa**
# 2. Persons → contribution

| **Person** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Heliodorus** | Greek envoy of Indo-Greek king **Antialcidas** at the court of Sunga **Bhagabhadra**; set up the **Garuda pillar at Besnagar (Vidisha)** to Vasudeva; called himself a **Bhagavata** |
| **Guptas** | Patronised **Bhagavatism** - worship of Bhagavat (Vishnu); contributed most to the sect (BPSC 1994) |
| **Chandragupta II** | Many notes credit him as first to assume **'Parama Bhagavata'** (his coins carry it); colossal **Varaha (boar) Vishnu** panel at Udayagiri carved under him |
| **Samudragupta** | UPPSC keys (2009, 2015) name him as the first Gupta to take the title Parama Bhagavata |
| **Skandagupta** | Garuda standard on his coins |

- ⚠ **Trap - first 'Parama Bhagavata':** many notes = **Chandragupta II**; UPPSC keys (2009, 2015) = **Samudragupta**
- **Dynastic emblem:** **Garuda**, Vishnu's mount
# 3. Varaha panel at Udayagiri
- **Panel:** Varaha Vishnu lifting the earth
- **Cave status:** His **personal temple**
- **Symbolism:** Usually read as his rescue of the land, e.g. from the **Sakas**

## Puranic Hinduism, Shaktism and Tantrism — texts and practices | from: S2, S4
# 1. Puranas
- **Number:** **18 Mahapuranas** (APPSC-G2 2011 key)
- **Composition:** Compiled over centuries; final shape / present form in the **Gupta period** (c. 4th-6th c. CE) → Puranas and epics **expanded** then
- **Principal Puranas:** **Vishnu, Bhagavata, Matsya, Vayu, Brahmanda, Markandeya, Shiva, Kurma**
- **Five topics (pancha-lakshana):** creation, re-creation, genealogies, manvantaras, dynastic history
# 2. Nature of religion and worship
- **Nature of religion:** **Theistic and devotional**, replacing sacrificial Brahmanism
  - Supreme deities = **Brahma, Vishnu, Mahesh**
- **Worship:** **Idol worship in temples** common → shift from Vedic fire altar to temple
- **Purusharthas:** Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha (first three = **trivarga**)
# 3. Texts and schools
- **Smriti texts:** **Yajnavalkya, Narada, Brihaspati, Katyayana**
- **Smarta** practices spread
- Six schools of philosophy took final shape
# 4. Shaktism, Tantrism and Surya
- **Mother goddesses:** **Durga, Amba, Kali, Chandi** worshipped; **Gaja-Lakshmi** appears on seals
- **Tantrism:**
  - Spread from the **5th c.**
  - Rituals, shakti (feminine energy); open to women and shudras
  - Touched Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Buddhism and Jainism
- **Early mention:** Surya and Shakti already in **Hala**'s **Gathasaptasati**

## Temple architecture — stages → examples, features, FIRSTs | from: S8
| **Stage** | **Examples** | **Features** |
|---|---|---|
| First | **Sanchi Temple 17, Tigawa** | Square sanctum, **flat roof**, shallow 4-pillar portico, low platform |
| Second | **Nachna (Parvati), Bhumara (Shiva)** | Roofed **ambulatory** around the sanctum, raised platform, two storeys; Bhumara adds 2 flanking shrines |
| Third | **Deogarh (Dashavatara), Bhitargaon** | Flat roof gone; low square **shikhara**; **Panchayatana** plan |

- **Pre-Gupta:** No free-standing Hindu temple survives from before the Guptas
- **Panchayatana:** Main shrine + **four subsidiary shrines** at the corners; **FIRST - Deogarh** = earliest example
- **Basic elements fixed in this age:**
  - **garbhagriha** (square sanctum)
  - **pillared porch / mandapa**
  - **pradakshina path**
  - Raised **jagati** platform
  - → beginning of the **Nagara style**
- ⚠ **Flat roof** = first stage; **shikhara** = third stage (Deogarh)

## Universal angles | from: S10 | universal
- **Significance:** Gupta age = **Golden Age of art and architecture**; iconography of Brahmanical, Jain and Buddhist deities standardised
- **AP angle:**
  - **Gudimallam** (Tirupati district) = oldest Shiva linga
  - **Kalamukha** and **Pashupata** sects later flourished under the **Kakatiyas**
  - Salankayanas followed **Vaishnavism** (APHQ 2011 key); some notes stress their Shaiva **Parama Mahesvara** titles too
  - **Nandivarman II**'s **Pedavegi** inscription = first in AP on Vishnu temples
- **Current relevance - Sirpur:**
  - **Mahasamund, Chhattisgarh**; capital of **Dakshina Kosala** (**5th-12th c.**)
  - Being readied for **UNESCO** nomination (Dec 2025)
  - Centre of **Buddhism, Shaivism, Vaishnavism and Jainism**
  - ~**6th-c.** brick **Lakshmana temple**
- **Comparative note:** Mauryan religion = Vedic + Shramana; Gupta change = sacrifice → temple and bhakti
