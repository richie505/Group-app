=== ROW 109 | Timur's invasion; Sayyid and Lodi dynasties; the First Battle of Panipat, 1526

## Sayyid and Lodi dynasties → period, founder, succession | from: S1, S2
# 1. Sayyid dynasty (1414-51)
- **Founder:** **Khizr Khan (1414-21)** = **Timur's viceroy**
  - Arrested Daulat Khan Lodi + took Delhi in 1414
  - Ruled as Timur's deputy, without the title of Sultan
- **Order:** **Khizr Khan - Mubarak Shah (1421-34) - Muhammad Shah (1434-45) - Alauddin Alam Shah** (UPPSC 2023: 1, 3, 2, 4)
- **End:** Alam Shah gave up the throne voluntarily to **Bahlol Lodi** (1451)
# 2. Lodi dynasty (1451-1526)

| **Ruler** | **Reign** | **Key points** |
|---|---|---|
| **Bahlol Lodi** | **1451-1489** | Governor of Punjab; received the throne from Alam Shah; conquered **Jaunpur**; ruled longest of these (BPSC 2025) |
| **Sikandar Lodi** | 1489-1517 | Agra founded; Gulrukhi; Portuguese arrival in 1498 |
| **Ibrahim Lodi** | 1517-1526 | Arrogant to nobles; killed at Panipat |

- **Order:** **Bahlol, then Sikandar, then Ibrahim** (son follows father each time)
- **Kingship:** King = "**first among equals**" among the Afghan nobles
- ⚠ **Khizr Khan** ruled without the title of Sultan; **Bahlol** got the throne from **Alam Shah** without a battle

## Sayyid and Lodi rulers → contributions | from: S1, S3, S4
# 1. Mubarak Shah (Sayyid, 1421-34)
- **Military:** Failed against the Khokhars
- **Literature and building:** **Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi** written in his reign; **Octagonal tomb** (see the next page)
# 2. Sikandar Lodi (1489-1517)
- **Reign:** **1489-1517**
- **Capital:** Founded **Agra** (1504) → moved capital there from Delhi (CGPSC 2018)
- **Measurement unit:** **Gaz-i-Sikandari** = yard used to measure land
- **Tax remissions:** **Abolished several taxes and trade restrictions** → necessities cheaper
- **Control:** Systematic audits + spy network
- **Extent:** Defeated Bengal rulers + Rajput chiefs → rule from Punjab to Bihar
- **Jaunpur:** Subdued its ruler
- **Pen name:** **Gulrukhi** (Persian poetry)
- **Bigotry:** Destroyed Jwalamukhi (Nagarkot) images + Mathura temples; imposed jizya
- **Translation:** Yet had a Sanskrit medical treatise translated into Persian
- **Buildings:** **Moth ki Masjid** + royal tombs of the **Lodi Gardens**
# 3. Ibrahim Lodi (1517-26)
- **Plot:** Nobles plotted to enthrone his brother **Jalal Khan**
- **Gwalior:** Seized **Gwalior** (had sheltered Jalal Khan) → ruler **Vikramajit** made a vassal
- **Cause of Babur's invitation:** Arrogance alienated the Afghan nobles

## Invasions and battles → year, route, tactics, result | from: S0, S4, S5
# 1. Timur's invasion, 1398
- **Route:** Crossed the Indus → Punjab (Multan, Bhatnair) → sacked **Delhi** in December 1398
- **Effects:**
  - Delhi plundered + people massacred
  - Tughlaq state broke up into regional kingdoms (Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat)
  - His nominee **Khizr Khan** later founded the Sayyid dynasty
# 2. Khatauli (1517-18)
- **Rana Sanga:** Fought **Rana Sanga** of Mewar at Khatauli (1517-18) → Ibrahim defeated
# 3. First Battle of Panipat, 1526
- **Date:** **21 April 1526**; older notes say 20 April
- **Place:** Panipat (Haryana)
- **Forces:** **Babur** (about **12,000**) defeated **Ibrahim Lodi** (about **1,00,000**)
- **Tactics:**
  - Field **artillery** + muskets = decisive factor
  - **Tulughma** flanking manoeuvre
  - **Araba** carts chained as a defensive line ("Rumi" / Ottoman device)
  - Gunners **Ustad Ali** and **Mustafa**
- **Consequences:**
  - Ibrahim died on the battlefield = only Delhi Sultan to do so
  - Babur captured **Delhi and Agra** + the Lodi treasure
  - End of Delhi Sultanate → start of the **Mughal Empire**
- **What followed:** **Kanwah (1527)** vs **Rana Sanga** broke Rajput power → **Chanderi (1528)**
- ⚠ **Khatauli** = Ibrahim defeated by **Rana Sanga**; **Panipat** = Ibrahim killed by **Babur**

=== ROW 111 | Sultanate central administration - Sultan, wazir, the diwans, nobility, theory of kingship

## Sultans → theory of kingship and relations with the Caliph | from: S0, S1
# 1. The Sultan
- **Basis:** Sultans saw themselves as the **Caliph's representatives**
- **Powers:** Supreme in military, legal + political matters; **highest court of appeal**
- **Succession:** No fixed rule → rested on backing of nobles, ulema + army; whoever nobles + ulema accepted, or military strength
# 2. Theory of kingship

| **Sultan** | **Theory** |
|---|---|
| **Iltutmish** | Legitimacy through caliphal investiture |
| **Balban** | **Zil-i-Ilahi** (Shadow of God / God's shadow) with **Niyabat-i-Khudai** (divine deputyship); **sijda and paibos**; **Nauroz**; "**blood and iron**"; no low-born men in office [GK] |
| **Alauddin** | State interest above ulema and law; "kingship knows no kinship"; **Sikandar-i-Sani**[GK] (some notes: Sikander-i-Azam) |
| **MBT** | Rationalist absolutism; appointed non-Turks and low-born men [GK] |

# 3. Relations with the Caliphate

| **Sultan** | **Relation with the Caliph** |
|---|---|
| **Iltutmish** | Letter of investiture (**mansur**) from the **Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad in c.1229** |
| **Balban** | Some notes give "Nasir-amir-ul-momin" for Balban |
| **Alauddin Khalji** | Titles **Yamin-ul-Khilafat Nasir-i-Amir-ul-Mominin** (UPPSC 2023 key); did not seek investiture |
| **Mubarak Shah Khalji** | **Proclaimed himself Caliph** |
| **MBT** | Sought investiture from the Abbasid Caliph in **Egypt** |
| **Firoz** | **Firoz** respected the Caliph |

- ⚠ **Baghdad** Caliph = Iltutmish's investiture; **Egypt** Caliph = MBT's; **Mubarak Shah Khalji** = himself Caliph
> Exam angle: BPSC 2022 asked who "opposed the Khalifa's power". The key was "more than one of the above".

## Features of the state — legal basis and nature | from: S2
# 1. Legal basis
- **Sharia:** Covered Muslims' civil matters
- **Hindus:** **Hindus** kept their own personal law
- **Criminal law:** **Criminal law** followed the Sultans' rules
- **Zawabit:** Royal decrees supplemented the sharia
# 2. Nature of the state
- **Composite:** **Composite of Perso-Arabic, Turko-Mongol and Indian** elements (JPSC 2021)
  - Perso-Arabic: title Sultan, Caliph's nominal authority, Persian court ritual (sijda, paibos, Nauroz)
  - Turko-Mongol: army in decimal units (khan, malik, amir, sipahsalar); horse-based warfare with mounted archers
- **Centralisation:** Comparatively **centralised** / essentially a **military, centralised state**
  - Sultan = supreme commander + highest court of appeal
  - **Barids** = intelligence network

=== ROW 112 | Sultanate provincial and military administration - iqta system, dagh and huliya, army organisation

## Iqta system — definition, origin and FIRSTs | from: S0
- **Definition:** **Iqta** = territory whose **land revenue** was assigned to an officer instead of a cash salary
  - Holder = **iqtadar/muqti** (or **wali** for a province)
  - **Not ownership of land**
- **Muqti duties:** Kept troops, collected revenue, kept law and order, sent the **surplus (fawazil)** to the centre
- **Fawazil:** Surplus revenue left after the muqti's costs → had to go to the treasury
- **Origin:**
  - **First iqta grant:** **Hansi**, by **Muhammad Ghori** to **Qutbuddin Aibak**; some notes say Kuhram
  - Some notes credit Aibak with introducing it
  - **Iltutmish** institutionalised it / built it into a system (UPPSC 2010 key: **Iltutmish**)
- **Nature:** **Turkish, non-indigenous** institution
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "ancient indigenous institution" is false (UPSC 2019)
- **Transferability:** Iqtas **transferable** → muqtis moved periodically until Firoz made them hereditary

## Sultans → changes in the iqta and army pay | from: S1

| **Sultan** | **Change in the iqta system / mode of army pay** |
|---|---|
| Iltutmish | Organised the system; iqtas granted to Turkish officers (Shamsi) [GK for Shamsi]; iqtas to officers and troopers [GK] |
| **Balban** | Iqtas to officers and troopers [GK]; tried to resume the iqtas of old soldiers; appointed **khwajas** (auditors) to check muqtis' accounts [GK] |
| **Alauddin Khalji** | **First large permanent standing army paid in cash** from the royal treasury; market control kept that pay adequate; enlarged the **khalisa**; tightened audit of muqtis [GK for audit] |
| Ghiyasuddin Tughluq | Inquiry into grants/jagirs; unlawful grants seized |
| **Firoz Tughlaq** | Back to assignments: reinstated iqtas and made them **hereditary**; the revival of the **jagir** let nobles control recruitment, promotion and discipline, which hurt army efficiency |

- ⚠ **Alauddin** = cash pay; **Firoz** = hereditary iqtas

## Army organisation and military technology | from: S2, S3
# 1. Ariz's department, dagh and huliya
- **Ariz's department:** Under the **Ariz-i-Mumalik** → recruited, inspected + paid the army
- **Introducer:** **Dagh** (branding of horses) + **huliya** (descriptive roll of each soldier) introduced by **Alauddin Khalji**
- **Purpose:** Stopped substandard horses or proxies at the muster
- **Revival:** **Sher Shah** later revived the dagh
  - ⚠ **Trap:** "Balban introduced the branding of horses" is **false** (UPSC 2002)
# 2. Ranks and strength
- **Ranks:** Followed a **decimal** pattern

| **Rank** | **Commanded** |
|---|---|
| **Sar-i-khail** | 10 horsemen |
| **Sipah-salar** | 10 sar-i-khails |
| **Amir** | 10 sipah-salars |
| **Malik** | 10 amirs |
| **Khan** | 10 maliks (highest) |

- **Arms:** Cavalry = core arm; also infantry + elephants
- **Alauddin's cavalry:** Ferishta = about **4,75,000**
# 3. Military technology
- **Turkish innovations:** **Horse-based warfare**, **mounted archers**, the **iron stirrup**, **armour** + stronger fortification
- **Horse trade:** **Horse** became a key military + economic resource → imported from West Asia
- **War elephants:** A royal preserve

| **Term** | **Meaning** | **Dynasty / region** |
|---|---|---|
| **Iqta** | Assignment of **land revenue** (UPPSC 2023); transferable revenue assignment for service | Delhi Sultanate (also Bahmani, where it is used as a synonym for jagir) |
| **Khalisa** | **Land under direct state (crown) administration**; its revenue went to the royal treasury, court and household | Sultanate; Bahmani Khalisa paid the central army |
| **Mahrousa** | **Protected town/territory** (UPPSC 2023) | Delhi Sultanate |
| **Shiq** | **A portion**: a division of a province, under a **shiqdar** | Delhi Sultanate |
| Inam | Land granted to religious leaders/institutions | Sultanate |
| **Jagir** | Revenue assignment to a noble/officer; Mughal jagir tied to mansab rank, transferable [GK] | Bahmani, Qutb Shahi, Mughal |
| **Amaram** (nayankara) | Land given to **Amaranayakas** for keeping troops [GK] and paying **kappam** | **Vijayanagara** |
| Mokasa | Revenue assignment for military service [GK] | Deccan (Bahmani and later) |

- **UPPSC 2025:** Sultanate's territory broadly **Khalisa and Iqta**, not "Khalsa and Jagirs" (A true, R false in the key)

## Universal angles | from: S6 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - **Iqta** financed the army + integrated distant provinces
  - Ancestor of the Mughal **jagir/mansab** system
- **AP angle:**
  - Vijayanagara: standing **Siddha Sainyam** (Nuniz: about **50,000**; paid every **4 months**) + feudal **Amara Nayankara** army
  - Qutb Shahis: **Siddha Sainyam** + **Jagirdar Sainyam**; **Ain-ul-Mulk** headed the army (Thevenot)
  - Kakatiya posts: Gajasahini, Ashwasahini; royal bodyguards = **Lenkas**
- **Comparative note:** Shivaji kept a **standing army paid in cash**, avoided jagir grants, set up **Sindhudurg and Vijaydurg** naval bases

=== ROW 113 | Sultanate economy - kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams; agriculture, urbanisation, trade and craft

## Taxes and cesses → nature, payer, Sultan | from: S0, S1
# 1. Sharia taxes and booty

| **Tax** | **Nature** | **Payer** |
|---|---|---|
| **Kharaj** | Land tax: usually **1/3** of produce, varying from 1/10 to 1/2 by crop and region (one-third to one-half of produce) | Non-Muslim cultivators (mainly Hindu peasants) |
| **Ushr** | Land tax at **1/10** on Muslim-held land (less on irrigated land); a lower rate (5-10%) | Muslim landholders |
| **Jizya** | Poll or "protection" tax on non-Muslims; **Firoz Tughlaq** extended it to **Brahmins**; sometimes waived | Non-Muslims |
| **Zakat** | Religious tax (about **2.5%** of wealth) | **Muslims** only |
| **Khams** | **One-fifth (1/5) of war booty** and treasure-troves, due to the state | State's share of booty (APPSC-G1 2012) |

- **Khams (booty share):**
  - Sharia: state = **1/5**, soldiers = **4/5**
  - **Alauddin and MBT** took **4/5**; **Firoz** restored **1/5**
- **Firoz:** Kept the Sharia taxes, dropped the others: abolished many illegal cesses; kept only the four sharia taxes (kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams)
> Exam angle: "Khams = 1/5 of war booty" and "Zakat = tax on Muslims only" are the standard traps.
# 2. Land revenue and land categories
- **Land revenue:** Peasants paid **1/3** (**one-third**) of produce, sometimes **1/2** (**one-half**); Alauddin raised it to **1/2**

| **Category** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Iqta** | Assigned to officials in place of salary |
| **Inam** | Granted to religious men and institutions |

# 3. Additional cesses

| **Cess** | **Nature** | **Sultan** |
|---|---|---|
| **Ghari** | House tax | **Alauddin Khalji**[GK] |
| **Charai** | Grazing tax on milch cattle (cattle and sheep) | **Alauddin Khalji**[GK] |
| **Haq-i-sharb** (Sharb) | Irrigation tax, one-tenth (about 1/10 of produce) on canal-watered land; paid by users of canal water | **Firoz Tughlaq**[GK] |

- **Other levies:** Octroi + customs duties
- ⚠ **Kharaj** = non-Muslims' land tax; **Ushr** = Muslims' land tax at 1/10

## Agriculture — crops, irrigation, agrarian measures | from: S2
# 1. Crops
- **Crops grown:** Wheat, barley, gram, rice, sugarcane, cotton, oilseeds
- **Harvests:** Ibn Battuta saw two a year (**rabi** and **kharif**); rice sown three times a year
- **Not grown in the 13th-14th centuries: maize** (UPPSC 2011) + tobacco
  - Came later with the Portuguese / Europeans
# 2. Irrigation
- **Araghatta:** **Water-wheel used for irrigation**
- **Persian wheel (saqiya):** Popularised by the Turks
- **Ghiyasuddin Tughluq:**
  - **First canal-builder of the 14th century: Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** (UPPSC 2017)
  - Remission in droughts; wasteland brought under the plough
- **Firoz Tughlaq:** Built the most canals = extensive **canal network**
  - Longest: Yamuna → Hisar
  - Irrigation cess = **haq-i-sharb**
- ⚠ **First** canal-builder = **Ghiyasuddin**; **most** canals = **Firoz**
# 3. Agrarian measures of MBT
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq:** **Diwan-i-Kohi** (**Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi**) + **taccavi (sondhar) loans** / taqavi loans to extend cultivation
- **MBT + Firoz:** Both pushed irrigation and "superior crops"

## Crafts, industries and Turkish technologies | from: S3
- **Karkhanas:** Royal **karkhanas** made luxury goods; Firoz ran about **36 karkhanas**
- **Turkish technologies:**
  - **Paper-making** (strengthened bureaucratic record-keeping), glass-making
  - **Spinning wheel**, better looms
  - Iron stirrup, armour
  - Superior **mortar** for arches and domes
- **Crafts flourished:** Textiles, carpets / carpet-weaving, leather, metal-work
- **Sericulture:** Reduced dependence on / the need to import raw silk

|---|---|
| Siri | Alauddin Khalji |
| **Tughlaqabad** | Ghiyasuddin Tughluq [GK] |

## Trade — routes, imports, post and trading groups | from: S5
# 1. Exports, imports and routes
- **Exports:** To the Persian Gulf, West Asia, South-East Asia
- **Imports:**
  - Textiles, glassware, **horses** from West Asia
  - Silk, porcelain from China
- **Overland route:** Khorasan → Kabul → Multan and Lahore
- **Coastal trade:** Gujarat ports (**Cambay, Broach**)
- **World economy:** India tied into the Islamic world economy (Central Asia, Persia, Arabia, East Africa, South-East Asia)
# 2. Transport and post
- **Caravans:** Used roads + sarais; road building helped transport and communication
- **Ibn Battuta's two kinds of post:**
  - **Horse post (uluq/ulaq):** Royal horses about every **4 miles**; the **horse-post system** brought Khorasan fruit to Delhi (APPSC-G1 2017 key)
  - **Foot post (dawa):** Three stations to every mile = one about every third of a mile; some notes say "every mile"
# 3. Trading groups

| **Trading group** | **Role** |
|---|---|
| **Multanis and Khurasanis** | Controlled **overseas** trade (and financing) |
| **Gujarati, Marwari, Muslim Bohra** merchants | Dominated **inland** trade; Gujarati merchants included Hindus, Jains and Muslims |
| **Banjaras** | Bulk carriers (grain) using oxen over long distances |

- ⚠ **Multanis and Khurasanis** = overseas; **Gujarati, Marwari, Bohra** = inland

## Coins → metal and Sultan | from: S6
- **Pre-Sultanate:** Delhi minted the **dehliwal** under the Tomaras and Chauhans
- **Iltutmish:** Introduced the two basic Sultanate coins = silver **tanka** + copper **jital** (UPPSC 2001; APPSC G1 2018)
  - Purely Arabic / standard Arabic coinage
  - Tanka = ancestor of the rupee
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq:** **Token currency** (c. **1329-30**)
  - Brass/copper at par with the silver tanka
  - Failed due to widespread forgery → withdrawn
- **Firoz Tughlaq:** Smaller coins for the poor in copper-silver alloy
  - **Adha (1/2 jital)**, **bikh (1/4 jital)** (some notes print "bitch = 23% of jital", an OCR slip)
  - **Shashgani (6 jitals)**

| **Coin** | **Metal** | **Sultan / note** |
|---|---|---|
| **Tanka** | Silver | Iltutmish; **175 grains** [GK] (some notes print "175 grams") |
| **Jital** | Copper | Iltutmish; copper coins were few and undated |
| Dinar | Gold | **Gold dinars** common/popular under Alauddin, after the southern loot (South Indian conquests); copper coins fewer and undated |
| **Shashgani** | Silver-based coin worth 6 jitals [GK] | Muhammad bin Tughlaq |
| Token coins | Copper and brass | Muhammad bin Tughlaq |
| Bahloli coins | Copper | Bahlol Lodi |

- ⚠ Sources differ on **Shashgani**: listed under Firoz's small coins and, in the table, under Muhammad bin Tughlaq

| **Class** | **Role** |
|---|---|
| **Nobles** | Turkish, later Indian Muslim and Afghan; held iqtas |
| **Ulema** | Keepers of religious law |
| **Peasants** | Bore the revenue burden; collected through khuts and muqaddams |
| **Slaves** | Traded in slave markets (Ibn Battuta); Firoz had a separate department |

- **Hindu society:** Caste order kept; **Sati** and **purdah** spread

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - **Price control:** Alauddin's price-control markets = military needs shaping economic policy
  - **Technology:** Paper + Persian wheel = lasting gains
  - **Economy:** Money economy, town growth, new crafts → base of the medieval economy
- **AP angle:**
  - Kakatiya land = **Maagani** (irrigated) vs **Metta** (rain-fed); land tax = **Ari**
  - Big tanks (**Samudrams**) linked in chains
  - Bahmani kharaj normally **1/3**
  - Kakatiya Warangal's diamonds and wealth (noted by Amir Khusrau) drew the Delhi invasions
  - Ibn Battuta described the Malabar pepper trade with China and Persia
- **Comparative note:** Sher Shah's revenue = **1/4 to 1/3** of produce, based on survey → model for Todar Mal's bandobast

=== ROW 114 | Sultanate society - nobility, ulema, position of women, slavery; conversion and syncretism

## Social groups → terms and meaning | from: S0

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Kulah-daran** | **Sayyids** (wearers of the pointed cap, kulah) (UPSC 2022); Sayyids = descendants of the Prophet [GK] |
| **Dastar-bandan** | **Ulema**, the turban-wearers (UPPSC 2014); scholars of Islamic law |
| **Ashraf** | Muslims of **foreign descent**: the elite (Turks, Persians, Afghans, Sayyids) |
| **Ajlaf** | **Local converts**, stratified by occupation |
| **Rais, Rana, Rawat** | **Hindu chiefs** and landed warrior aristocracy in Barani's vocabulary [GK] |
| Qalandars | Wandering dervishes who rejected convention [GK] |

- **Hindu society:** Little structural change; caste order with Brahmins at the top continued
- **Muslim society:** Own **ashraf-ajlaf** stratification despite the Quranic ideal of equality
- ⚠ **Kulah** (cap) = **Sayyids**; **Dastar** (turban) = **Ulema**

## Sultans and the ulema → religious policy | from: S1, S2
# 1. Ulema, qazis and the Islamic character of the state
- **Ulema:** Interpreted the sharia; wanted a say in state policy
  - Relied on by some (Firoz); shut out by others (Alauddin)
- **Sadr-us-Sudur:** Chief Sadr; headed Diwan-i-Risalat (religious affairs, grants to mosques and madrasas) + **Chief Qazi**; controlled religious grants
- **Qazis:** Administered Sharia law in Muslims' civil cases; **Qazi-ul-Quzat** = chief judge
- **Barani's view:** Sultanate **not truly Islamic** (UPSC 2002 key)
  - Reason: **the Sultan supplemented Muslim law by framing his own regulations (zawabit)**
  - Ideal set out in *Fatawa-i-Jahandari*
# 2. Religious policy by Sultan

| **Sultan** | **Approach** |
|---|---|
| Iltutmish | Tolerant; gave non-Muslims posts |
| Jalaluddin Khalji | **First Sultan with a benevolent attitude towards Hindus** |
| Alauddin Khalji | Kept the ulema out of state affairs; reduced **ulema interference in state matters**; told Qazi Mughisuddin he decreed whatever he judged good for the state; acted in the state's interest without ulema guidance → state not a true theocracy |
| Nasiruddin Khusrau Shah (1320) | **Hindu gods** and goddesses **worshipped in the royal palace** (UPPSC 2021) |
| Muhammad bin Tughlaq | Leaned on the Sufis against the ulema; visited the Ajmer shrine |
| **Firoz Tughlaq** | Followed the ulema's advice (ulema counsel; **theocratic** principles); respected the Caliph of Egypt; ordered temples destroyed, **prohibited public idol worship**, penalised Hindus and Shias; **jizya on Hindus**, extended to **Brahmins** [GK] |
| **Sikandar Lodi** | Orthodox: destroyed Mathura temples and broke the Jwalamukhi images at Nagarkot; imposed **jizya** |

- ⚠ **Alauddin** = ulema shut out; **Firoz** = ulema followed

## Jizya and temple destruction — features | from: S2
# 1. Temple destruction
- **Motives beyond fanaticism:** Political + economic motives as well as religious
  - Rival **royal legitimacy (political)**
  - **Accumulated wealth (economic)**: temple wealth
# 2. Jizya
- **Nature:** Protection tax on non-Muslims in place of military service; collected unevenly / irregularly, sometimes waived
- **Exempt:** Women, children, the old and disabled, and at first Brahmins
- **Imposed by:**
  - **Firoz** extended it to Brahmins (exempt earlier)
  - **Sikandar Lodi** enforced it
- **Golconda:** Qutb Shahis **never imposed** jizya

## Position of women — purdah, sati, jauhar, women rulers | from: S3
- **Purdah:** Brought by Arabs and Turks; spread among the elite + child marriage
- **Islamic law:** Women's rights in **property, inheritance and dower (mahr)**
- **Sati:** Prevalent, documented cases; described by Ibn Battuta
- **Jauhar:** At **Ranthambhor (1301)** and **Chittor (1303)** against Alauddin
- **Razia Sultana (1236-40):** **Only woman to rule the Delhi Sultanate**; resisted due to gender norms
- **Female saints:** **Lal Ded** (Kashmir) found a voice through Bhakti and Sufism

## Slavery — sources, markets, Diwan-i-Bandagan | from: S4
- **Sources:** **War captives, purchase in markets, and defaulters**
- **Use:** Households + skilled work; markets dealt in men and women
- **Alauddin:** Third regulated market = **horses, slaves and cattle** at fixed prices
- **Firoz Tughlaq:** About **1,80,000 slaves**; created **Diwan-i-Bandagan** (also weakened the army)
- **Slave dynasty:** Aibak, Iltutmish, Balban were themselves slaves (bandagan) → "**Slave (Mamluk) dynasty**"

## Conversion — theories, causes and debates | from: S5

| **Theory** | **Status in current scholarship** |
|---|---|
| **Sword** (forced mass conversion) | **Rejected** as a colonial trope; coercion was isolated |
| **Political patronage** / state employment | A contributing factor (tax relief, office) |
| **Social liberation** (escape from caste) | Partial: egalitarian ideals appealed, but caste-like stratification survived among converts |
| **Sufi influence** | Major factor, especially in **Bengal and Punjab**, together with **agrarian expansion**; **Sufi saints** spread Islam among lower castes + farming communities via pirs and dargahs |

- **Nature:** **Multi-causal**, gradual process, including genuine spiritual conviction

## Sufis and scholars → contributions to syncretism | from: S6, S7

| **Person / text** | **Contribution** |
|---|---|
| **Al-Biruni** | Noted parallels: Sufi idea of soul vs **Patanjali's Yoga Sutra**; Sufi divine love vs **Gita** |
| **Amritakund** | Hatha-yoga text translated into Arabic and Persian (13th c.) |
| **Nizamuddin Auliya** | Struck by Nath-yogic body theory |
| **Hamiduddin Nagori** | Hindi verses equating **Alakh Niranjan** with **Khuda** |
| **Sheikh Nuruddin** | Founded Kashmir's **Rishi** movement (echoing **Lalla**); **vegetarianism**; served all castes |
| **Chishti Sufis** | Muinuddin, Nizamuddin Auliya, Baba Farid |
| Amir Khusrau | Traditionally credited with sitar + qawwali |
| Pir Bhodan | Sufi musician |
| Raja Man Singh of Gwalior | *Man Kautuhal* |
| Jayasi | *Padmavat*: human love as metaphor for divine love |

- **Breath control:** Entered almost every Sufi order except the **Naqshbandis**
- ⚠ **Rishi** movement = **Sheikh Nuruddin** (Kashmir); **Alakh Niranjan = Khuda** = **Hamiduddin Nagori**

## Bhakti-Sufi interaction — features, shared rituals, music | from: S7
- **Chishti Sufis:**
  - Kept away from the court; **khanqahs** = centres of charity
  - Spoke to people in **Hindavi**
  - Khanqahs + dargahs drew Hindus and Muslims, alongside Bhakti and Nath traditions
- **New shared rituals:** **Shrine (dargah) worship, Urs festivals, qawwali**
- **Bhakti:** Indigenous roots (Alvars and Nayanars); Islam reinforced equality + rejection of ritual and shaped later Bhakti's **egalitarianism** (Kabir, Nanak, Ravidas)
- **Sufi poetry:** Human love as metaphor for divine love (Jayasi's *Padmavat*); in the Deccan written in **Dakhani** (early Urdu)
- **Music:**
  - **Sitar** = classic Hindu-Muslim fusion instrument (RPSC key)
- **Deccan:** Mixed culture; Hindu Nayakas served Sultans, Sultans patronised temples

## Universal angles | from: S8 | universal
- **Current relevance:**
  - Conversion politics (6 Apr 2026) recalls **Meenakshipuram (Feb 1981)**: **558 Dalits** embraced Islam to escape caste oppression → "social liberation" argument
  - (23 Jan 2026) "**paro**" bride-slavery persists in Haryana, Rajasthan and UP
- **AP angle:**
  - Islam arrived with **Malik Kafur (1310)** and MBT
  - **Gesu Daraz** (Bandanawaz): moved to Gulbarga under Firoz Shah Bahmani; wrote in Persian + early Dakhni (*Mirqat-ul-Ashyaq*)
  - Golconda: foreign Muslims = **Afaqis**; Brahmins got agraharas + **Mustajir** (tax-farming) rights
  - Qutb Shahis = Shia rulers who patronised Hindu temples; Abul Hasan Tana Shah granted villages to **Bhadrachalam** temple
  - **Akkanna and Madanna** held top posts; Telugu poets called them "Andhra Sultans"
  - Vijayanagara slavery = **Benabaga**
- **Criticism & debates:**
  - Conversion: "destruction" vs "liberation" framings; forced vs social and Sufi-driven; current scholarship stresses regional variation
  - State: theocracy (Barani) vs military state adjusting to local realities
