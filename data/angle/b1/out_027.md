## Revenue systems → meaning and region | from: S2
| **System** | **Meaning** | **Where / when** |
|---|---|---|
| **Zabti** (bandobast) | Land measured; cash rates fixed per crop per bigha | Core provinces of North India (Lahore to Allahabad, Malwa, Gujarat) [GK] |
| **Dahsala** (Ain-i-Dahsala) | Revenue fixed on the **average produce and prices of the last ten years**, then commuted to cash | Introduced **1580**[GK]; APPSC-G2 2017 answer |
| **Batai / Ghalla-bakhshi** | **Crop-sharing** between state and peasant | Areas such as Thatta, Kabul, Kashmir [GK] |
| **Nasaq** | Rough assessment from past revenue records, without fresh measurement | Mainly **Bengal**[GK] |
| **Kankut** | Yield estimated on the standing crop | Used where measurement was not practical [GK] |
| Plough assessment | Assessment by **number of ploughs** | **Deccan**, in parts (UPPSC 2017 key) |

- **Bandobast:** "**Bandobast** system" = **Dahsala** (JPSC 2013 key); some notes use "Zabti or Bandobast" for the same Todar Mal reform
- ⚠ **Batai** = crop-sharing; **Kankut** = standing-crop estimate; **Nasaq** = past records (Bengal)

=== ROW 133 | Mughal administration - mansab (zat and sawar), jagirdari system and the jagirdari crisis

## Mansabdari system → introduction, purpose and PYQ features | from: S0
- **Introduced by:** **Akbar** (some notes date it **1571**; APPSC-GS 2018); backbone of both civil and military administration
- **Meaning:** "Mansab" = Arabic for **rank/position**
- **Model / origin:** From **Central Asia** (Mongol decimal grading)
- **Purpose:** Fuse **nobility, army and bureaucracy** into one graded service - one grading for **both army and civil service**; every civil and military officer held a **mansab** (rank) → backbone of army + administration; many mansabdars non-military
- **Administration:** **Mir Bakhshi** ran the system (JPSC 2023)
- **Nature (PYQ statements):**
  - Mansabdars = **official nobility** of the state
  - **Not hereditary**: rank and jagir ended with the holder
  - **Not all were military officers**: high civil post-holders also mansabdars, graded the same way
- **Military state:** Mughal state essentially a **military state**; central system rested on the military-cum-civil mansab (UPPSC A-R)
  - **UPPSC 2021 key:** "Mughal Empire was originally a military state" - A and R both true, R explains A
> Exam angle: statement sets on "hereditary?", "introduced by Akbar?", "three classes?", "civil officers also mansabdars?".
> Exam angle: the system served both army recruitment and revenue/civil administration (BPSC 2019 - "more than one").

## Mansab terms → meaning (zat, sawar, classes) | from: S0, S1
| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Zat** | Personal rank - status, **salary** and prestige |
| **Sawar** | Number of **cavalrymen (horses)** the officer had to maintain |
| Range | Mansabs from **10 to 10,000** (decimal) under Akbar; highest reserved for princes; **66** grades in theory, **33** in practice |
| Three classes | Sawar = zat - **first**; sawar at least half of zat (half or more) - **second**; less than half - **third** |
| Payment | In cash (**naqd**) or by assignment of land revenue (**jagir**) |

- **Horse rule:** Standard **10-20-30** norm; every **10** sawars ("10 troopers") to bring **20** horses
- ⚠ **Zat** = personal rank and pay; **Sawar** = cavalry to maintain

## Emperors → changes in the mansab system | from: S1
| **Change** | **Emperor** | **What it did** |
|---|---|---|
| **Du-aspa sih-aspa** | **Jahangir** | For trusted mansabdars: part of the sawar rank counted **double** (troopers with 2-3 horses) without raising zat; those troopers paid double |
| **Month-scale** | **Shah Jahan** | Jagir yield (hasil) below estimate (**jama**): half-yield = **shashmaha**, quarter-yield = **sihmaha**; pay scaled down |
| Reduced obligation | **Shah Jahan** | Mansabdar need maintain only **1/5 to 1/3** of sawar strength without cut in claims |
| **Mashrut** (conditional) | **Aurangzeb** | Temporary extra increase in sawar rank tied to a specific assignment |

- **Du-aspa sih-aspa worked example:** sawar **4,000**, of which **1,000** du-aspa sih-aspa → (**3,000** x **8,000**) + (**1,000** x **8,000** x **2**) = **4 crore dams** (**40,000,000 dams**) vs **3.2 crore dams** (**32,000,000**) without it
- ⚠ **Du-aspa sih-aspa** = Jahangir; **month-scale** = Shah Jahan; **mashrut** = Aurangzeb
- ⚠ **Shashmaha** = half-yield; **sihmaha** = quarter-yield

## Jagir types → features; jagirdar vs zamindar | from: S6
| **Type** | **Holder / feature** |
|---|---|
| **Tankhwah jagir** (**Tankha**) | Given to mansabdars **in lieu of salary**; transferable [GK] |
| **Watan jagir** | Hereditary home territory (homeland) of zamindars / **Rajput chiefs**; not transferred [GK] |
| **Mashrut jagir** | Given on conditions of service [GK] |
| **In'am jagir** | Free of any service obligation [GK] |
| **Altamgha** | Perpetual grant |
| **Khalisa** | **Crown land** - revenue went to the imperial treasury; not a jagir |

- **Madad-i-maash:** Revenue-free grants to scholars and religious men, under the **Sadr**
- **Nature:** A jagir was **not a property right**, only the right to collect revenue; strict checks to protect the peasantry

| **Jagirdar** | **Zamindar** |
|---|---|
| Mansabdar holding a **jagir** = right to collect revenue in lieu of salary | Holder of **hereditary** rights over land revenue and local power [GK] |
| **Not hereditary**, frequently **transferred** | Hereditary; not transferred by the state [GK] |
| Not property in land | Local intermediary between state and peasants [GK] |

- **UPSC 2019 key:** Neither statement correct; jagirs = revenue assignments in lieu of salary and military service, not judicial/police duties
- ⚠ **Tankhwah** = transferable; **Watan** = hereditary, not transferred

## Jagir transfers and jagirdari crisis → causes and historians | from: S7, S8
- **Frequent transfers:** About every **3-4** years; to stop mansabdars striking local roots and becoming independent feudal lords
- **Effects:**
  - Short-term jagirdars squeezed peasants (no stake in long-term prosperity)
  - **Bejagiri**: mansabdars kept waiting without jagirs → discontent and faction at court
- **Crisis:** Under **Aurangzeb** number of mansabdars grew sharply while profitable/paying land for assignment (**paibaqi**) shrank → peasants squeezed by ruthless extraction, agrarian ruin, decline

| **Historian** | **Explanation** |
|---|---|
| **Satish Chandra** | Shortage of jagirs relative to claimants - the system could no longer distribute surplus [GK] |
| **Irfan Habib** | Agrarian crisis: jagirdars over-exploited peasants, provoking flight and revolts (Jat, Satnami, Maratha) [GK] |
| **M. Athar Ali** | Influx of **Deccani and Maratha** nobles after the Deccan wars swelled the nobility beyond available jagirs [GK] |

- ⚠ **Irfan Habib** = agrarian crisis; **Satish Chandra** = jagir shortage; **Athar Ali** = Deccani influx

## Universal angles | from: S9 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - Mansab-jagir system bound the nobility to the emperor; its breakdown (**jagirdari crisis**) = core explanation of Mughal decline
  - Sher Shah's pargana set-up (**Amin, Shiqdar**) + Sultanate ministries (**Wazir, Diwan-i-Arz**) fed the Mughal model
- **Comparative note:**
  - Sultanate **iqta** and Kakatiya **Nayankara** compared with the Mughal jagir
  - Mansabdars mixed civil + military duty, paid in jagirs vs British **ICS**: purely civil, fixed cash salaries, merit-recruited
- **AP angle:**
  - **Asaf Jahi** (Hyderabad) state kept a Mughal-style **jagirdari** administration
  - Qutb Shahi Golconda: Peshwa, **Mir Jumla** (finance), **Ain-ul-Mulk** (military), **Majumdar** (auditor)
  - Under Tana Shah: **37 sarkars and 517 parganas**; village **Mukaddam** and **Kulkarni**

## Mansabdar-jagirdar-zamindar linkage → flow and zamindar categories | from: S2
- **Flow:**
  - Mansabdar gets rank → paid by a **tankhwah jagir**
  - Jagirdar's agents collect the assessed revenue (**jama**) → through **zamindars** (keep a hereditary share)
  - Peasant pays about **one-third** of produce

| **Jagirdar** | **Zamindar** |
|---|---|
| Assignee of revenue **in lieu of salary** for state (mainly military) service | Holder of **hereditary** rights over land/revenue in a locality |
| **Not hereditary**; transferred often | Hereditary, saleable rights [GK] |
| Outsider to the area | Local, with armed retainers; helped police and collection [GK] |

| **Category** | **Rights and role** |
|---|---|
| **Autonomous chiefs** | Rajas ruling their own territory; paid tribute, served as mansabdars [GK] |
| **Intermediary zamindars** | Collected revenue from primary zamindars for the state; got a commission [GK] |
| **Primary zamindars** | Proprietary, hereditary rights over cultivated land [GK] |

- **Conflicts (local face of the jagirdari crisis):**
  - Short-tenure jagirdars pressed for more
  - Zamindars resisted or rebelled
  - Peasants fled
> Exam angle: UPSC 2019 - "jagirs in lieu of judicial/police duties" and "jagirs hereditary" are both **wrong** (answer: neither).
