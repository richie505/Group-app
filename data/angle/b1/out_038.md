## Nature of the revolt → statement-type judgments | from: S5
- **Nature:** Began as a **military mutiny** → quickly became a **popular rebellion** (rulers, peasants, soldiers, common people)
- **Not a modern nationalist movement:** Idea of one Indian nation-state not yet emerged
  - Traditional uprising of classes defending local, economic and religious interests against a common foreign enemy
- **Why not a "first war of independence" (UPSC):**
  - No common goal; modern nationalism yet to emerge
  - Confined largely to **North India**
  - Elite and urban classes uninvolved; most of India neutral
- **Outcome:** Failed, but shook British rule
- **Legacy:** Heroes (Lakshmibai, Kunwar Singh, Tantia Tope, Zafar) invoked later by Bose and Bhagat Singh (Quit India, INA trials)

## Acts and commissions → year → provision | from: S0, S1, S3, S5
# 1. Government of India Act, 1858
- **Title:** **Act for the Good Government of India**
- **Core change:** Passed after the 1857 Revolt; **abolished Company rule** / **ended East India Company rule** → government, territories and revenues of India to the **British Crown**
  - The **1858** answer in APPSC-GS 2018, 2019; UPPSC 2010
> Exam angle: options mix Charter Acts 1833/1853, Pitt's Act and Indian Councils Act 1861 (ICA 1861) - only the 1858 Act transferred power to the Crown.
# 2. Act → what it did
| **Act / commission** | **Year** | **What it did** |
|---|---|---|
| Charter Act | 1833 | **Governor-General of India** (Bentinck); Company made purely administrative |

| **Peel Commission** | **1859** | Reshaped the army (see army page) |

## Queen's Proclamation, 1858 → promises, kept vs broken | from: S3
# 1. Basics
- **Reader & venue:** **Lord Canning** at a **Durbar at Allahabad** / **Durbar in Allahabad** on **1 November 1858** (UPPSC 2009)
- **Called:** "**Magna Carta of the Indian people**"
# 2. Promises
- **Princes:** No further **annexation** of Indian territory; rights, dignity and honour of native **princes** respected; Company treaties honoured; **Doctrine of Lapse abolished**, right of **adoption** accepted (see the princes page)
- **Religious neutrality:** **No interference in religion** and social customs; no forced conversions
- **Equal opportunity in public service:** Appointments by merit and qualifications, without discrimination of race or creed
- **Laws & property:** Ancient rights, usages and customs respected; protection of property and succession; peasants' land rights while revenue was paid
- **Concluding line:** "The well-being of our Indian subjects is our strength. Their satisfaction is our security. Their gratitude is our reward."
- **Objects (UPSC 2014):** Disclaim annexation of Indian States + place administration under the Crown → "1 and 2 only"
  - ⚠ **Trap:** Regulating Company trade was NOT an object
# 3. Kept vs broken
| **Kept** | **Broken / Not kept** |
|---|---|
| No annexation, adoption (promises to princes), religious neutrality; state withdrew patronage from missionary conversion | **Not kept** - Equal employment → ICS stayed European-dominated |
- **SoS Kimberley:** an adequate number of civil servants "shall always be Europeans"
- **1858-1885** = **"Era of Broken Promises"**
# 4. Empress of India
- **Victoria:** Became **Empress of India** only in **1877** - Royal Titles Act 1876, proclaimed at Delhi Durbar of 1 Jan 1877 under Lytton
  - Some notes place it at the Allahabad Durbar of 1858
- ⚠ **Allahabad 1858** = Proclamation (Canning); **Delhi 1877** = Empress of India (Lytton)

## Policy towards princes → end of annexation, adoption, paramountcy | from: S4
- **Annexation:** Policy of Dalhousie officially abandoned/ended
- **Doctrine of Lapse:** **Canning withdrew the Doctrine of Lapse**; **right of adoption** by childless rulers fully recognised
- **Paramountcy:** Territorial integrity guaranteed if princes accepted **British paramountcy** (**British Crown as the ultimate sovereign**)
  - Became subordinate allies and props of British rule, having stayed loyal in 1857
  - **"Breakwaters to the storm"** (Lord Canning; some notes credit Lord Elgin)

## Army reorganisation → Peel and Eden Commissions | from: S5
# 1. Background
- **Presidency armies:** Three - Bengal, Madras, Bombay - under the Company
# 2. Peel Commission (1859) → measures
| **Measure** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| European : Indian ratio | Bengal Army from **1:5 to 1:2** (**1:2**, earlier 1:5); Madras and Bombay armies **2:5** (some notes say 1:3); European share raised |
| Artillery | **Exclusively European**; Indians barred from heavy weapons; key positions reserved for Europeans |
| Regiments / organisation | Organised by **caste, community and region** (Sikh, Gurkha, Rajput regiments) to stop cross-regional fraternising (prevent fraternisation); **"Divide and counterpoise"** - regiments mixed by caste/region |
| Recruitment | Shift to **Gurkhas, Sikhs and Pathans** ("martial races": Punjab, Gorkhas, NWFP) - communities that stayed loyal in 1857, rather than Awadh and Bihar |
> Exam angle: "divide and rule" inside the army - the counterpoise principle.
- ⚠ **Bengal 1:2**; **Madras and Bombay 2:5**
# 3. Eden Commission (1879)
- **Eden Commission (1879):** Recommended abolishing separate presidency armies → merged into a single Indian Army with four commands in **1895**

## Changes after 1857 → end of Mughals and Company, divide and rule, racial attitudes | from: S2, S6
# 1. End of the Mughal dynasty and the East India Company
- **Bahadur Shah II:** Proclaimed Shahenshah-e-Hindustan by the rebels; tried and **exiled to Rangoon**; died there in **1862** → Mughal dynasty/rule formally ended
- **East India Company:** Chartered by Elizabeth I on **31 December 1600**
  - Lost its rule in **1858** (by the **1858 Act**)
  - Finally dissolved in **1874**
# 2. Policy change → effect
| **Factor** | **Effect** |
|---|---|
| **Divide and rule** | British held **Muslims chiefly responsible** → first **anti-Muslim policies**; later cultivated Muslims against the rising educated Hindu middle class; Hindus and Muslims played against each other |
| **Loyal landed class** | Policies now **protected zamindars and landlords**; annexation of large estates reversed; zamindars and taluqdars given proprietary rights → conservative, loyalist class (Awadh especially) |
| **Non-interference** | No further interference in social customs; reforming zeal abandoned; state stepped back from missionary activity; English-educated "Babus" viewed with suspicion |
| **Racial arrogance** | Hardened; atrocities; rebels executed publicly after mock trials; Indians kept out of higher posts |

## Universal angles | from: S7 | universal
- **Significance:**
  - 1858 = watershed between Company rule and Crown rule → start of Crown rule (1858-1947)
  - Administration controlled from London (submarine cable, 1870)
  - Proclamation fixed British policy on princes and religion till 1947
- **Current relevance:**
  - Gandhi cited Queen Victoria's 1858 Proclamation, which ended the Company's power over India, as a "Magna Carta" (12 May 2026, on Magna Carta's legacy)
  - Rani Avantibai Lodhi of Ramgarh, who fought the Doctrine of Lapse and won the Battle of Kheri (1857), remembered on her martyrdom day (1 Sep 2026)
- **AP angle:** Andhra came under direct Crown rule
  - Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema stayed in the **Madras Presidency**; Telangana under the Nizam (British suzerainty) till independence
  - **Gazula Lakshmi Narasu Chetty**'s **Madras Native Association** (1852) = first to petition over non-implementation of the Allahabad Proclamation
- **LAST:** Bahadur Shah II - last Mughal emperor
