RecapEmergency Provisions – National Emergency (352), President's Rule (356), Financial Emergency (360); S.r. Bommai Case

Recap

  • National Emergency (Article 352) – Grounds, Proclamation, Approval — President · imminent danger · 44th Amendment, 1978 · armed rebellion · internal disturbance · no longer a ground · External Emergency
  • President's Rule (Article 356) – State or Constitutional Emergency — on the Governor's report or otherwise · fails to comply with Union directions · 365 · State Emergency · Constitutional Emergency · President's Rule / Governor's Rule · No consent
  • Financial Emergency (Article 360) — financial stability or credit of India · President · two months · simple majority · indefinitely – no maximum period · Effects · canons of financial propriety
  • Effects of National Emergency – Fundamental Rights, Terms, State List — only during a National Emergency · President · Art. 250 · Parliament · State List · 6 months · Lok Sabha term (Art. 83(2))
  • Scheme of Emergency Provisions – Three Emergencies Compared — Part XVIII · 352-360 · Government of India Act, 1935 · Weimar Constitution · three · no "President's Rule at Union level" · false
  • Instances of National Emergency and the 1975 Emergency — Trigger of 1975 · Allahabad High Court · Twenty Point Programme · MoSPI · 25 June · Samvidhan Hatya Diwas · Revocations
  • President's Rule in Andhra Pradesh and Other States — First PR in India · 1951 · never under PR · Most times · Manipur – 11 · Uttar Pradesh – 10 · Longest total period
  • S.R. Bommai v Union of India (1994) and Other Cases — 9-judge bench · Art. 356 · Centre-State relations · judicial review · floor of the Assembly · suspended · restore
