# Trace APPSC Group 2 questions to their sources

APPSC examiners do not copy from one guide book. They write their own questions from a small set of original sources, and the actual papers show which ones. **Polity questions come mostly from M. Laxmikanth's *Indian Polity*: about 44% of the 2025 Mains polity questions share exact word runs with it.** **Geography in the 2024 Prelims reads like an NCERT question bank: about 20 of 30 questions match NCERT Class 6 to 12 books, some word for word.** **Current affairs and science policy questions come from PIB press releases.** The examiner often copies a PIB sentence and changes one name to make it false. **AP economy figures come from the AP Socio-Economic Survey (SES)**, usually the edition out at exam time, plus AP and Union budgets. **AP history repeats facts from earlier APPSC papers.** In 2025 several AP history statements were also taken word for word from English Wikipedia. Indian Society has no single book. It mixes university sociology, welfare schemes from PIB and Laxmikanth's chapters on constitutional safeguards. The weakest area of evidence is the Telugu Academy books. Every coaching list recommends them, but none could be tested word for word, so their role is likely but not proven. The practical result is that a good plan is built on past papers, Laxmikanth, NCERT, the latest AP SES and budget, and 6 to 12 months of PIB. That works better than piling up guide books.

## How the evidence was tested, and how much to trust it

The research used two kinds of evidence. The first was web book lists and paper analyses. These turned out to be weak: they come from coaching and sales sites (Testbook, Adda247, Freshersnow, Embibe), copy one another, and include no topper interviews and no statement from APPSC ([Testbook](https://testbook.com/appsc-group-2/books-for-preparation); [Embibe](https://www.embibe.com/exams/appsc-group-2-books/)). The second, much stronger kind was the official question papers themselves. These were 2008 Papers I to III, 2012 Paper I, 2017 and 2019 Prelims and Mains, 2024 Prelims and 2025 Mains, held in [`/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/`](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/). Their questions were compared against a local library of books and notes, and checked by hand against PIB, Wikipedia, NCERT and government reports. Each match got one of three labels. **Verbatim** means a clause matches word for word. **Clear fact match** means the same distinctive fact appears in different words. **Likely** means the topic fits but other sources carry it too.

Three limits matter. First, some coaching notes (NextGen, Powerplay, CDI) are compilations of old APPSC questions. A match with them only shows that questions repeat. It does not show where the examiner read the fact. Second, no Telugu Academy, D.D. Basu, Ram Ahuja or original NCERT PDF was available as searchable text, so those books could not be string-tested. Third, matching was done on the English papers only. If questions are first drafted in Telugu, some links could be missed.

## History: AP history repeats old papers, and 2025 borrowed from Wikipedia

**AP history (Mains Paper I Section A)** is the most repetitive subject. In the 2008, 2017 and 2019 Mains, AP history questions match the NextGen and Powerplay files almost word for word, often 10 to 26 five-word runs per question. On inspection, though, those files are lists of earlier APPSC questions with answers. For example, "P. Anandacharyulu was the first Andhra who became the President of the INC... 1891" (2008 Paper 2 Q19) and the Kolleru lake in the Pithapuram inscription (2017 Mains Paper 2 Q1) both sit in those lists ([ap-history-appsc-07.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/ap-history/ap-history-appsc-07.txt)). The same facts keep coming back. These include the **Battle of Padmanabham** (2008, 2025), **Vijayaditya II and his 108 temples** (2008, 2019, 2025), **Veeresalingam and widow remarriage** (2008, 2019, 2025), **Machilipatnam English and Dutch factories** (2008, 2017, 2025), **Chirala-Perala and Duggirala** (2019, 2025), and the **Andhra State formation, Gentlemen's Agreement and Sribagh Pact** (every Mains year). So the single best AP-history source is the past papers themselves.

The **2025 Mains** changed format. More than 40 of its 75 AP-history questions were match-the-list, assertion-reason, statement or chronology items. Some of its statements were lifted from English Wikipedia. Q10 says Nannaya "was a Telugu poet and the author of 'Andhra Mahabharatam', a Telugu retelling of the Sanskrit-language Mahabharata". That is the Wikipedia opening sentence, word for word (verbatim) ([Wikipedia: Nannaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nannaya)). Q34 on Gurram Jashuva copies Wikipedia's lines about the 1964 Legislative Council seat and the Sahitya Akademi award. The examiner made the false option by swapping *Kreesthu Charitra* for *Gabbilam* (verbatim) ([Wikipedia: Gurram Jashuva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurram_Jashuva)). Q13 on Draksharama and the Eastern Chalukyan style is a clear phrase match ([Wikipedia: Eastern Chalukyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Chalukyas)), and so is Q17 on the triple-shrine Thousand Pillar Temple ([Wikipedia: Thousand Pillar Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Pillar_Temple)). "Abhinava Nannaya" for Rayaprolu Subba Rao (Q32) is a clear fact match, and that phrase does not appear in any local coaching note ([Wikipedia: Rayaprolu Subba Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayaprolu_Subba_Rao)). Several other 2025 facts are missing from every coaching note in the library: Nedumba Vasati, the Potnuru victory pillar, Jananathapura, *Marichi Parinayam* and *Grandhalaya Sarvasvam*. The examiner is clearly reading beyond coaching material.

The older AP social-history questions (2019 Mains) go into very fine detail. Examples are the foundation cream used by Satavahana dancers, the Kolika guild and the most popular festival under the Reddis. Detail at this level usually comes from Telugu-medium AP history texts such as **Telugu Academy, B.S.L. Hanumantha Rao and P. Raghunadha Rao**. These are also the books coaching lists name most, along with the Vijeta *Social and Cultural History of AP* ([Testbook](https://testbook.com/appsc-group-2/books-for-preparation)). None of these books could be tested, so their link stays at **likely**.

**Indian history (Prelims)** is not copied from coaching notes. In 2019, none of the 30 history questions shared even two word runs with any library file. In 2024, 20 of 30 had no such match. The best 2024 matches were topic matches with Spectrum and PMF modern history. Examples are the AITUC first session under Lala Lajpat Rai (Q11), the Dhar Commission (Q79) and Macaulay's "single shelf" quote (Q23). Other items, such as the **Lottery Committee (1817), Lex Loci and the Taiyuni movement**, appear in no local text. Q30 on the Caste Disabilities Removal Act 1850 is a clear fact match with Wikipedia ([Wikipedia: Caste Disabilities Removal Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_Disabilities_Removal_Act,_1850)). The 2024 ancient-history items (Rummindei, Boghazkoi, Pautavadhyaksha) are UPSC-style. This suggests standard UPSC-level books (Spectrum or Bipan Chandra for modern; R.S. Sharma or NCERT for ancient) and UPSC past papers, but no wording proof was found.

| History source | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Past APPSC Group 2 (and Group 1) papers | Same facts repeat across 2008, 2017, 2019, 2025 | Strong |
| English Wikipedia (AP dynasties, poets, temples) | 2025 Mains Q10, Q34 word for word; Q13, Q17, Q32 | Strong (2025 only) |
| Telugu Academy / B.S.L. Hanumantha Rao / P. Raghunadha Rao | Fine AP social-history detail; named in every list | Likely, untested |
| Spectrum / Bipan Chandra / PMF modern India | 2024 Q11, Q23, Q79 topic matches | Moderate (topic, not wording) |
| Nitin Singhania / art and culture notes | Stupa parts, Mughal painting topics | Weak to moderate |

## Polity and Indian Society: Laxmikanth for polity, three layers for society

**Polity (Mains Paper I Section B)** has the clearest single source of any subject. Laxmikanth is the one book every list names, in English or in McGraw Hill's Telugu edition ([Flipkart, Indian Polity Telugu](https://www.flipkart.com/indian-polity-telugu-7e-m-laxmikanth-upsc-appsc-tspsc-2025-exclusive-access-mcgraw-hill-edge-online-platform/p/itm22c83525a185f)). The papers confirm it. Counting questions that share at least three six-word runs with Laxmikanth 8th edition gives **32 of 73 in 2025 Mains (about 44%)**, 19 of 60 in 2008 (about 32%), 6 of 18 in 2019 Prelims, and about 10 to 17% in 2017 and 2019 Mains. The 2017 and 2019 questions are short one-liners, which keeps word matches low even when the fact is in the book ([ng2.py output, local](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/polity/)).

The 2025 examples are striking. **Q93 on EVMs** copies Laxmikanth's line that EVMs were "used for the first time in 1998... Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Delhi". The examiner swapped Delhi for Gujarat to make the statement false (verbatim, with one fact changed). **Q92** repeats "In 1924, the Cantonments Act was passed by the Central legislature" (verbatim). **Q115** on PIL repeats "PIL is also known as Social Action Litigation" and names Krishna Iyer and Bhagwati as its pioneers (verbatim). **Q113** reuses Laxmikanth's Inter-State Council sentence with Sarkaria changed to Rajamannar. **Q85** asks for the first chairpersons of the national commissions for STs, Minorities, SCs and BCs. Those names appear only in Laxmikanth's tables, not in any coaching note (clear fact match). **Q109 (UPSC) and Q110 (73rd Amendment)** match questions in Laxmikanth's own practice-question appendix. There is one caution here: that appendix is in the 8th edition, printed after the exam, so the book may have borrowed these from APPSC rather than the other way round. In the 2024 Prelims, **Q83 on the 82nd Amendment** closely paraphrases Laxmikanth's sentence on "relaxation in qualifying marks or lowering the standards of evaluation". The local Laxmikanth files are at [laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt).

Beyond Laxmikanth, the **bare text of the Constitution** is a direct source. 2019 Prelims Q54 quotes Article 25 and Q55 quotes the Article 46 heading. 2008 Q121 quotes Article 14 and Q137 quotes Article 40. About 10 to 27% of polity items per paper name an Article ([prelims-2019.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/prelims-2019.json)). **Acts** are quoted too: RTI Act section 15(3) in 2025 Q102 and RP Act section 81 in Q146. The 2008 paper even asked who wrote D.D. Basu's *Introduction to the Constitution of India* and Granville Austin's book ([group2-2008-paper2.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/group2-2008-paper2.json)). Older papers (2017, 2019) also had an **AP layer**: the AP Legislative Council, AP's first Lokayukta, the number of AP municipalities and the gram panchayat secretary as marriage officer. In 2025 there were none. AP Reorganisation Act 2014 questions follow the gazette sections word for word, but they sit under AP history and economy, not polity ([apra.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/ap-reorganisation/apra.txt)).

**Indian Society (Prelims, 30 marks)** has no standard book. Lists name only Telugu market guides, such as Chintha Ganesh's *APPSC Indian Society* ([Flipkart](https://www.flipkart.com/appsc-indian-society-social-structure-issues-policies-welfare-programmes-includes-chapter-wise-practice-bits-telugu-medium/p/itmddd5e4ae9d79a)), and Adda247 bit banks ([Adda247](https://adda247.com/product-ebooks/51282/indian-society-bit-bank-ebook-for-group-2-ap-grama-sachivalayam-and-other-appsc-exams-by-adda247)). The 28 society questions in 2024 fall into three layers. About 11 are **welfare schemes, portals and recent news, in PIB style**: ALIVA, the PENCIL portal, the Bandicoot robot, Mission Vatsalya, PM CARES for Children and the Women's Reservation Act assent date. About 8 are **sociology theory and customs** from university-level texts: W.P. Scott's definition of divorce, "poor man's divorce", Melson and Wolpe on communalism ([Cambridge Core](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/modernization-and-the-politics-of-communalism-a-theoretical-perspectve1/FB6ED70F624365352EA920CEB7719993)), Malinowski, Sambandham, Niyoga and *Diku Andi*. None of these terms appears in the local coaching or NCERT society notes. About 6 to 8 are **laws and constitutional safeguards** of the Laxmikanth type (82nd Amendment, Article 164 proviso, Dowry Prohibition Act). Committee reports also appear. **Q67 (Sachar Committee, Ajlaf)** and **Q72 (Xaxa Committee 2013)** are clear fact matches to those reports ([prelims-2024.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/prelims-2024.json)). Ram Ahuja is the obvious textbook guess for the theory layer, but a search could not tie these exact definitions to his books, so it stays at likely.

## Economy: the AP Socio-Economic Survey and budgets drive the figure questions

Book lists name **Ramesh Singh's *Indian Economy***, the **Union Economic Survey** and a commercial AP economy guide such as Vijeta ([Testbook](https://testbook.com/appsc-group-2/books-for-preparation); [Embibe](https://www.embibe.com/exams/appsc-group-2-books/)). Telugu coaching also runs separate courses just on the AP Socio-Economic Survey ([Unacademy](https://unacademy.com/a/ap-economic-survey-by-vaddiboina.html)). The papers back the official documents more than any book. No question matched Mishra & Puri, Datt & Sundharam or Uma Kapila.

The strongest proof is **2025 Mains Q57**, which names "Socio-Economic Survey of Andhra Pradesh 2023-24" in the question itself. Its keyed answer, 10,778 Rythu Bharosa Kendras, uses the 2023-24 name; the 2024-25 edition renames them Rythu Seva Kendralu ([mains-2025-paper2.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/mains-2025-paper2.json)). **Q54's sector growth figures (1.69%, 8.02%, 5.21%)** match the 2023-24 advance estimates, which NABARD confirms ([NABARD](https://www.nabard.org/auth/writereaddata/tender/pub_2208250520151800.pdf)). **Q58's GVA shares (36.19/23.36/40.45)** come from the older SES 2022-23, about two years before the exam ([SES 2022-23 highlights](https://adda247.com/jobs/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2023/03/26145923/SES-2022-23-Highlights_-15.3.2023.pdf)). Other 2025 items name their own source. **Q59** cites the EAC-PM paper on states' economic performance (AP 4.7% of GDP). **Q61** on the Kopparthy and Orvakal industrial nodes comes from the Union Budget of July 2024. **Q73 to Q75** name AP Industrial Policy 4.0 and Electronics Policy 4.0. **Q12's fiscal deficit of 4.8%** comes from the Union Budget released just 22 days before the exam. **Q60 on low capital spending** fits CAG state finance reports ([CAG Report 2 of 2018](https://www.cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2018/Chapter_1_Finances_of_the_State_Government_of_Report_No_2_of_2018_-_State_Finances_Government_of_Andhra_Pradesh.pdf)).

Earlier papers follow the same rule. **2017 Mains** (July 2017) used SES 2016-17 from four months before. Its 11.18% GVA growth and 31.77/22.23/46.0 sector shares match AP's official March 2017 figures ([AP FRBM March 2017](https://apfinance.gov.in/uploads/frbmreport/FRBM_March_2017.pdf)). At least 26 of its 55 AP-economy questions carry a dated SES-style figure: district land use, milk and meat, irrigation. **2019 Mains** (August 2019) mostly used SES 2017-18, one edition behind. Its "agriculture rose to 34.4%" and "industry about 22%" match 2017-18 figures ([AP FRBM 2018](https://apfinance.gov.in/uploads/frbmreport/FRBM_2018_%20Fiscal%20Policy.pdf)). It also used the 14th Finance Commission revenue deficit grant of Rs 22,113 crore.

The mix has shifted. In 2025, about **43% of economy questions were national theory** (national income methods, high-powered money, SDR, zero-base budgeting, IPR 1956). These are textbook items with no fingerprint of one book. About 27% were **current national data** (Union Budget, Economic Survey 2023-24, RBI's I-CRR, LPI 2023). About 30% were **AP**, now more policy documents and headline figures than the district statistics of 2017. Ramesh Singh covers the theory part well, but it is a fit, not a proven source.

| Economy source | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| AP Socio-Economic Survey (current and previous edition) | 2025 Q57 names it; Q54, Q55, Q58; 2017 Q91, Q99; 2019 Q140, Q146 | Strong |
| AP Budget, AP policies, CAG, Finance Commission | 2025 Q60, Q62, Q73-75; 2019 Q70 | Strong to likely |
| Union Budget, Economic Survey, RBI, EAC-PM | 2025 Q12, Q27, Q50, Q59, Q61 | Strong |
| AP Reorganisation Act 2014 | 2025 Q63; 2019 Q71 | Clear |
| Ramesh Singh / standard macro text | About 29 theory questions in 2025 | Topic fit only |
| Telugu Academy economy books | 2017 Q144-147 on AP plans and irrigation history | Likely, untested |

## Geography: NCERT sets most of the paper, AP lists come from AP sources

Book lists name **Telugu Academy** AP, Indian and World Geography, **NCERT Class 11**, and Telugu guides such as Raman Raju ([Testbook APPSC](https://testbook.com/appsc/best-books-for-preparation); [Adda247 AP Geography](https://www.adda247.com/product-ebooks/48618/ap-geography-ebook-for-appsc-group-sachivalayam-and-other-exams-by-adda247)). The papers strongly confirm NCERT. In the **2024 Prelims, about 20 of 30 geography questions match NCERT**. **Q39** puts lake salinity in NCERT's exact order (Lake Van 330, Dead Sea 238, Great Salt Lake 220), verbatim with NCERT XI *Fundamentals of Physical Geography*. **Q38's** ocean-floor options ("associated with active volcanoes and strong earthquakes") are word for word from the same chapter ([onlyias-ncert-physical-geography-08.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/geography/onlyias-ncert-physical-geography-08.txt)). **Q36 (advection is horizontal heat transfer)**, **Q45 (Mango Showers, Karnataka)**, **Q42 (Satluj to Kali is the Kumaon Himalaya)**, **Q52 (town functions: Ankleshwar mining, Udhampur garrison)** and **Q57 (HVJ pipeline)** are clear fact matches with NCERT XI and XII ([onlyias-ncert-indian-geography-05.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/geography/onlyias-ncert-indian-geography-05.txt)). Pairing Ankleshwar with mining rather than industry is a giveaway that the NCERT example list is the source. Several more follow NCERT VI, VII, IX and X wording: the epicentre "first one to experience the waves", meteorites burning in the mesosphere, and the first cement plant at Madras in 1904. These come from memory of the NCERT text rather than a local copy, so they count as likely to verbatim. Two questions with loose wording were deleted from the key, which also points to textbook-sentence framing.

The **five AP questions in 2024** were all match-the-list items: river origins, minerals by district, APIIC clusters and beaches. They come from AP-specific material. **Q48** spells the Vamsadhara's source "Jayapur hills", as Telugu-medium notes do ([rocket-ap-geography.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/geography/rocket-ap-geography.txt)). That points to a Telugu source such as Telugu Academy. "Mudugal hills" (Q48), "Garividi" (Q59) and "Gandrajupalle" (Q55) appear in no coaching note, so the examiner used an AP text, the SES or APIIC and AP Tourism material ([ap-socio-economic-survey-2024-25.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/economy/ap-socio-economic-survey-2024-25.txt)).

The **2019 Prelims** leaned on reports. **Q119's forest-plus-tree cover of 24.39%** is the India State of Forest Report 2017 figure, verbatim, and Shankar Environment reproduces it ([shankar-environment-11.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/environment/shankar-environment-11.txt)). **Q101 and Q102** use the Global MPI 2018 (271 million out of poverty). **Q130** uses UNDP's line that India's HDI "increased by nearly 50 percent". **Q116 and Q136** use Census 2011 ([prelims-2019.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/prelims-2019.json)). Older papers differ again. **2012 Paper I** had 19 disaster-management questions from government documents (High Powered Committee 1999, BMTPC Vulnerability Atlas) and 9 atlas place facts (Lop Nor, Takla Makan, Cardamom Mountains). **2008 Paper III** used AP Census 2001 and the National Human Development Report 2001 ([group2-2012-paper1.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/group2-2012-paper1.json); [group2-2008-paper3.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/group2-2008-paper3.json)). Nothing in 2019 or 2024 needed Majid Husain, Khullar or G.C. Leong. The ceiling is NCERT level. The current split is about one third world and physical, one half India and one sixth AP.

## Science, technology and current affairs: PIB with one fact changed

Lists name The Hindu, PIB, AP government websites, the current AP Budget and SES, Telugu monthlies, and **Hari Krishna's *Science & Technology*** for static S&T ([CollegeDekho Telugu](https://www.collegedekho.com/te/news/appsc-group-2-prelims-preparation-strategy-2024-49639/); [Testbook](https://testbook.com/appsc-group-2/books-for-preparation)). The papers point most clearly to **PIB press releases**. **2024 Prelims Q98** copies the PIB and CSIR-CRRI release on India's first steel slag highway on NH-66. The examiner wrote "IGBC-CRRI" instead of "CSIR-CRRI" to make the statement false (verbatim) ([CSIR-CRRI news, from PIB](https://crridom.gov.in/en/node/1534)). **Q106** repeats the Global River Cities Alliance release ("275+ global river cities in 11 countries") but swaps Dubai for New Delhi ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1984100)). **Q100 on MILAN 2024** changes Eastern Naval Command to Southern and shifts the dates, so every statement is false ([Indian Navy](https://www.indiannavy.nic.in/content/harbour-phase-highlights-milan-2024)). In **2025 Mains Q132**, the biofuel assertion-reason pair copies the 2018 PIB Cabinet release, including "Farmers are at a risk of not getting appropriate price for their produce during the surplus production phase" (verbatim, from a source 7 years old) ([PM India / PIB](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/?p=3477801)). **Q30** on the SDG India Index ("score of 71 in 2023-24", Uttarakhand and Kerala on top) follows the NITI Aayog release closely ([PIB PRID 2032857](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2032857)).

Static S&T items match **agency "about" pages**: ISRO's launcher descriptions for GSLV and PSLV in Q117 ([ISRO GSLV](https://www.isro.gov.in/GSLV_CON.html)), BARC's "India's largest research reactor" for DHRUVA in Q131, and MNRE's mission list in Q115 ([Wikiwand MNRE](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Ministry_of_New_and_Renewable_Energy)). There are also ISA, DBT and ABDM programme names. About **60 of the 78 S&T questions in 2025** are general ecology, pollution, waste and health concepts with no single source. Their terms ("stenothermal", "marble cancer", "bioaugmentation") are standard in Shankar and PMF environment notes, but not word for word ([pmf-environment-04.txt](/home/user/Group-app/data/notes2026/src/environment/pmf-environment-04.txt)).

**AP current affairs** follow the AP Budget and SES. **2019 Mains Q42 (Agri Gold, Rs 1,150 crore)** and **Q141 (Palasa kidney centre, Rs 50 crore)** come from the AP Budget presented seven weeks before the exam ([The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/andhra-pradesh/highlights-of-ap-budget-2019-20-546051)). Items such as the Isha "Happy classroom" in 3,000 Chittoor schools fit the SES "initiatives and awards" boxes ([Isha](https://isha.sadhguru.org/en/sadhguru/mission/government-school-adoption-program)). The 2024 AP items (Pepper Motion at Punganur, Jagananna scheme amounts, Jonnagiri gold mine) look like Eenadu, Sakshi or Hans India news and scheme portals. In one case (Q109), the official key seems to follow a newspaper error.

The **time window is short**. In 2024 Prelims, the 30 current-affairs questions split 10 international, 14 national and 6 AP. All fell within about **5.5 months of the exam, mostly the last 0 to 3 months**. MILAN 2024 was still going on when the exam was held ([prelims-2024.json](/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/prelims-2024.json)). In 2017 Mains, the PM's Israel visit was asked just 10 days after it happened. Coaching advice to cover 12 months is a safe outer limit ([rmconnection](https://rmconnection.com/?p=102663)).

## Conclusion

The papers show one common habit across subjects. The examiner takes a sentence from an official or standard source and changes one name, date or number to make it false. This happens with Laxmikanth (Delhi to Gujarat), PIB (CSIR to IGBC, Dubai to New Delhi), Wikipedia (*Kreesthu Charitra* to *Gabbilam*) and NCERT. So reading the original source exactly is worth more than covering many guides, which paraphrase and lose the precise detail being tested. The 2025 Mains also used more current official documents and more assertion-reason items than earlier years. This favours aspirants who read the source itself over those who rely on one-line bit banks.

The open question is the Telugu Academy books. Every list recommends them, and the fine AP history and AP geography details fit them, but no one has yet compared them word for word with the papers. Until that is done, treat them as a strong support source for AP topics, not as the proven base. Old papers, Laxmikanth, NCERT, the AP SES and PIB have direct proof behind them.
