# Where APPSC Group 2 Science & Technology and Current Affairs questions come from

Scope: APPSC (Andhra Pradesh) Group 2 only. Mains Paper II Section B (S&T, 75 marks, syllabus leaves M2B.*) and the Prelims/Screening Current Affairs section (30 marks, leaves P4.1.1 International / P4.1.2 National / P4.1.3 AP). Evidence comes from the official papers in `/home/user/Group-app/data/g2papers/*.json` (question text "s", options "o", key "a", explanation "x"), the syllabus-leaf maps (`*.leaves.json`), live checks of primary sources (PIB, CSIR-CRRI, ISRO, NITI Aayog), and coaching and strategy pages. The question extracts I worked from are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/4666e051-48dd-5910-831c-f2c172f98923/scratchpad/dr-sci/*.txt`.

Match strength used below:
- **VERBATIM**: the stem or a statement copies a sentence of the source, sometimes with one fact swapped to make it false.
- **CLEAR FACT MATCH**: the question uses the same facts, figures and terms as one identifiable source, but the wording is the examiner's own.
- **LIKELY**: the facts fit the source type, but more than one outlet carried them, or I could not open the primary page.

Local files are cited as `local:` paths. Dates of events not confirmed on the web come from the "x" explanations in the official-paper JSON and are marked "(per x-field)".

## Which sources do toppers, coaching institutes and analyses name for Group 2 S&T and current affairs? Which are most consistent?

### Takeaway
I found no named-topper interviews. What exists is coaching and test-prep advice, and it is thin and repetitive. It consistently names four kinds of source: The Hindu and PIB; AP government websites; the current-year AP Budget and AP Socio-Economic Survey; and Telugu monthly magazines and compilations (Sakshi Bhavita, Udyoga Kiranam and similar). For static S&T the only book named again and again is Hari Krishna's *Science & Technology, Life Science, Environment*. The papers themselves fit this advice: PIB/ministry text, NITI Aayog reports and AP budget or survey facts show up repeatedly. No source names India Year Book, Yojana/Kurukshetra or Spectrum/Vision compilations specifically for Group 2.

## Matching actual PYQs to specific sources (2017/2019 mains, 2024 prelims, 2025 mains)

### Takeaway
Distinctive current-affairs and policy questions can be traced to **PIB press releases and ministry or agency websites**, with near-verbatim sentences. The examiner often copies a PIB sentence and swaps one entity (CSIR-CRRI becomes "IGBC-CRRI"; Eastern Naval Command becomes "Southern Naval Command"; Dubai becomes "New Delhi") to make a statement false. NITI Aayog report releases, the AP Budget speech and AP Socio-Economic Survey facts, and agency sites (MNRE, ISRO, BARC, DBT, ISA, ABDM) supply the rest. About 60 of the 78 S&T questions in Mains 2025 are generic textbook concepts (ecology, pollution, waste, climate) with no single traceable source.

## Patterns: share of AP-specific, national and international current affairs; time window

### Takeaway
In Prelims 2024 the current-affairs block split 10 international, 14 national and 6 AP-specific (out of 30). Nearly all events fell within about 6 months before the exam, most within 0–3 months, and some (MILAN 2024) were still happening on exam day. Older papers had a similar mix with fewer AP items in prelims. Mains questions mix very recent events (days to weeks) with static policy texts up to about 7 years old (the Biofuel Policy 2018).

### Inferences
- In practice the window is about the **6 months before the exam, weighted heavily to the last 2–3 months**, with some questions set days before the exam. Twelve months is a safe outer bound. For Mains S&T, add static scheme and policy texts from any year (official PIB and ministry wording).
- AP-specific current affairs make up about 20% of the prelims CA block (6/30 in 2024), about 25% in Mains 2017 P-I, and more in Mains 2019 P-III (6 of 11). These items lean on AP Budget and Socio-Economic Survey facts and on AP scheme parameters.
- Examiners like **one-fact distortion** of official text (agency, command, city or figure swapped). Exact PIB wording therefore matters more than gist.
