# APPSC Group 2: sources behind the POLITY (Mains Paper I Section B) and INDIAN SOCIETY (Prelims) questions

Method note: the evidence comes from two places. (1) Web searches of coaching and book-list pages. These sources are weak: they are mostly sales pages, and analysis pages blocked fetching (Sakshi 403, BYJU'S 503). (2) A local comparison of the official papers in `data/g2papers/*.json` with the local text library `data/notes2026/src/`. For every question mapped to a syllabus leaf M1B.* (polity) or P3.* (Indian Society), a script (`/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/4666e051-48dd-5910-831c-f2c172f98923/scratchpad/dr-pol/ng.py`, `ng2.py`, `pq.py`) counted the 6-word sequences the question stem shares with each source. It then checked distinctive phrases by grep. Local file citations use the form `local:path:line`. The Laxmikanth file is the **8th edition (McGraw Hill, copyright 2026, ISBN 978-93-6444-767-6)**, from `local:data/notes2026/src/polity/laxmikanth-indian-polity-01.txt:1-25`.

Caveats on the local match:
- NextGen, Rocket and PMF notes are coaching notes. Some were written after the exams and include PYQs, so a match with them proves nothing about direction. Only Laxmikanth, the APRA gazette text and the NCERT compilations are treated as independent sources.
- No bare-Constitution text, D.D. Basu, Telugu Academy, Ram Ahuja or government-portal text exists in the local library. Matches to those are inferred from question content, not tested by string matching.
- The 2017 and 2019 mains JSON files lack answer keys (`a` is null) for many items.

## Which books do toppers, coaching institutes and analyses name for Group 2 polity and society? Which are cited most consistently?

### Takeaway
For polity, M. Laxmikanth's *Indian Polity* (English or the McGraw Hill Telugu edition) is the one book every list names. Telugu Academy textbooks and Vijeta guides are the Telugu-medium alternatives. For Indian Society no standard book emerges. Lists name Telugu-medium market guides (Chintha Ganesh / MC Reddy Publications; Sravan Sreeram) and coaching bit-banks (Adda247). I found no web source naming a Telugu Academy "Indian Society" title for Group 2.

### Inferences
- Laxmikanth is the only polity source named everywhere, and it is the only one the PYQs confirm independently (next section). Telugu Academy and Vijeta are recommended mainly as translation-friendly versions of the same content.
- For Indian Society, the recommended market guides are compilations. The PYQs (2024) show examiners drawing on university sociology texts and current welfare news rather than any one Group 2 guide.

### Gaps
- No topper interview or verifiable topper strategy for APPSC Group 2 specifically was found. Search results were dominated by sales pages.
- No source confirmed a Telugu Academy *Bharatiya Samajam* / Indian Society title as recommended for Group 2. Telugu searches returned only Adda247 and Flipkart pages.
- The BYJU'S Exam Prep booklist returned HTTP 503, and the full Sakshi analysis returned HTTP 403. Their contents are unverified.

## Matching actual PYQs to sources: which source does each year's polity and society question come from?

### Takeaway
The 2025 Mains Paper I polity section is the most traceable paper. Many of its items reproduce Laxmikanth 8e sentences almost word for word, including two items that match questions in Laxmikanth's own "Practice Questions" appendix. 2008 also leans heavily on Laxmikanth-type and classic-commentary (D.D. Basu / Austin) facts. The 2017 and 2019 polity questions are mostly bare-Article recall plus AP-specific facts. The 2024 Indian Society questions come from (a) sociology textbooks of the Ram Ahuja / university type, (b) PIB, ministry and current-affairs items, and (c) Laxmikanth-type constitutional provisions.

### Inferences
- Polity, by year: 2025 Mains P-I is plainly written from Laxmikanth 8e. About 44% of its stems share real word sequences with the book. The new Assertion-Reason items reuse Laxmikanth sentences and, in two cases, Laxmikanth practice questions. The pre-2023 papers (2017, 2019) are shorter Article- and fact-recall items, closer to Telugu-medium guide or Telugu Academy style, with a strong AP-local layer (AP Legislative Council, Lokayukta, municipalities, gram panchayat officers). 2008 mixes Laxmikanth/Basu-type facts with name-the-author questions on Basu and Austin.
- Indian Society (2024) has three layers in roughly equal parts: (1) sociology theory and definitions from university texts (Ram Ahuja / Ahuja-like, tribal ethnography); (2) welfare schemes, portals and recent judgments from PIB / ministry sites; (3) constitutional and statutory safeguards, Laxmikanth-type. NCERT sociology covers only a small part (Malinowski, kinship).
- Patterns: about 15-20% of polity items in any year are bare-Article based. Committees and amendments make up about 40% of the 2025 items. AP Reorganisation Act questions sit outside the polity section in the current syllabus. Welfare-scheme portal questions dominate the old welfare items (2017-2019 Mains) and roughly 40% of 2024 Indian Society.

### Gaps
- Direction of the Laxmikanth match for 2025 Q109/Q110: the 8th edition is copyright 2026, later than the 23 Feb 2025 exam. Its practice bank could have taken these from APPSC, or from a common UPSC/other-PSC source, rather than the other way round. The Statement-I/II format suggests a UPSC-style original. I could not check whether the 7th edition (2023/24) already had them, and a web search for the 73rd-Amendment statement did not find its origin ([search results, e.g. Vajiram](https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-exam/73rd-constitutional-amendment-act/)). The chapter-text matches (EVM, Cantonments Act, PIL, Inter-State Council, 82nd Amendment) are not affected: those sentences predate the 8th edition in Laxmikanth's text.
- No Telugu Academy polity or society text, D.D. Basu, Ram Ahuja or bare-Constitution file exists in the local library, so matches to those could not be string-tested.
- The 2017 and 2019 Mains JSON files lack answer keys for many items, which limits fact-level checks.
- Prelims 2017/2019 predate the 2023 syllabus, which created a separate Indian Society section. Their "P3" items are mapped retrospectively.

## Patterns: how often are questions bare-Article based, Laxmikanth-type, AP Reorganisation Act based, or drawn from welfare-scheme portals?

### Takeaway
Laxmikanth-type content dominates polity, and most strongly in 2025 (about 44% of stems share text with the book). Bare-Article questions make up about 10-27% depending on the year. AP Reorganisation Act questions are a separate AP-economy/history cluster, not polity. Welfare-scheme and portal questions dominate the society and welfare items: about 60% of the 2019 welfare items and about 40% of 2024 Indian Society.

For Indian Society: a university sociology text (Ram Ahuja-type), the Sachar and Xaxa reports, the safeguards chapters of Laxmikanth, and about 18 months of PIB / Ministry of WCD / Social Justice / Labour releases.
