## Q1. Which books and reports do toppers, coaching institutes and analyses name, and which are most consistent?

### Takeaway
Booklists found online consistently name three things:
- Ramesh Singh's *Indian Economy*
- the Union Economic Survey
- a commercial AP-economy guide (Vijeta, Adda247 and similar)

The AP Socio-Economic Survey (SES) is taught as its own course by Telugu coaching. No source found online names a specific Telugu Academy economy title for Group 2, and none tied Mishra & Puri, Datt & Sundharam or Uma Kapila to Group 2. The actual papers point more strongly to official documents (AP SES, budgets, RBI reports, state policies) than to any one textbook.

## Q2. Matching actual PYQs to specific sources (with edition and lag)

### Takeaway
AP-economy figure questions come from the AP Socio-Economic Survey, usually the edition in force at exam time. The main editions are:
- **2017 Mains (16 Jul 2017):** SES 2016-17 (March 2017), about 4 months' lag.
- **2019 Mains (29 Aug 2019):** mostly SES 2017-18 (March 2018) figures, about 17 months' lag, one edition behind the latest.
- **2025 Mains (23 Feb 2025):** SES 2023-24, named outright in Q57. It also has an older 2022-23 figure set (SES 2022-23), plus documents from 2024 to January 2025: Union Budget July 2024, AP Budget Nov 2024, AP Industrial Policy 4.0 2024-29, the EAC-PM state paper of Sept 2024 and Union Cabinet/PIB releases.

On the national side, 2025 drew on Union Budget 2025-26 (released 3 weeks before the exam), Economic Survey 2023-24 and 2022-23, and RBI publications for FY24.

### Inferences
- **Edition and lag rule.** Examiners take the AP figures mainly from the SES edition current at exam time, but they mix in older editions. Approximate lags:
  - 2017: about 4 months, latest edition.
  - 2019: about 17 months. SES 2018-19 (Jan/Feb 2019) was available, but the figures match 2017-18 data and labels.
  - 2025: about 3 months for SES 2023-24, plus about 23 months for the SES 2022-23 share figures.
- **SES 2023-24 release date.** It most likely appeared with the full AP Budget on 11 Nov 2024. The 2024 vote-on-account had no full SES. An India 2023-24 PE figure (published by NSO end-May 2024) sitting beside AP 2023-24 AE fits a post-May-2024 release. This date is an inference, not verified.
- **What to study for a 2026-27 exam.** Candidates should study the latest SES (2025-26, the edition after the local 2024-25 copy) and also keep the previous edition. Exams repeat a figure set one edition old.
- **Recurring structural sources:** APRA 2014 (Schedule XIII institutions, special assistance for the capital and Polavaram), Finance Commission reports (14th FC revenue deficit grant) and current AP policy documents (Industrial Policy 4.0, Electronics Policy 4.0). In 2025 the stems named EAC-PM and Economic Survey reports outright.
- **National economy.** Current-data questions track the Union Budget (as recent as 22 days before the exam), the Economic Survey (the previous July's edition) and RBI annual and monetary-policy publications. Theory questions are generic, with no fingerprint of a single book.

## Q3. Patterns: AP SES-based vs national theory vs current budget/scheme facts

### Takeaway
The weight of AP-economy questions varies a lot:
- **2017 Mains:** 55 of 120 economy questions (46%), mostly SES 2016-17 district and sector figures.
- **2019 Mains:** 19 of 112 (17%).
- **2025 Mains:** 20 of 67 (30%).

In 2025, about 70% of the AP questions carry a dated figure or name a document. Only about 40% of the national-economy questions do; the rest are textbook theory.

### Inferences
- **2025 rough mix** across the 67 economy questions:
  - About 43% national theory (textbook level: Ramesh Singh or NCERT/university macro).
  - About 27% current national data (Union Budget, Economic Survey, RBI reports).
  - About 10% AP SES figures.
  - About 20% other AP official documents (budget, policies, APRA, CAG, central releases).
- **Shift since 2017.** The 2017 paper was heavily SES-district-statistics based (land use, milk and meat, irrigation by district). By 2025, AP questions moved towards policy documents and headline macro figures.
